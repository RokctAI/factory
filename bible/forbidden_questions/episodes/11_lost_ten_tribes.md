# The Lost Ten Tribes — Who Are the Real Israelites?

## Core Argument
The modern nation of Israel and people who identify as Jewish today descend from only two of the original twelve tribes — Judah and Benjamin, with some Levites. The other ten tribes were scattered by Assyria in 722 BC and assimilated into surrounding nations. They never returned. Their descendants walk the earth today without knowing who they are — possibly including people on both sides of the current Middle East conflict.

---

## The Kingdom Split

**1 Kings 11:11-13** (NASB) — God announces the division because of Solomon's idolatry:
> "So the LORD said to Solomon, 'Because you have done this, and you have not kept My covenant and My statutes, which I have commanded you, I will surely tear the kingdom from you, and will give it to your servant. Nevertheless I will not do it in your days for the sake of your father David, but I will tear it out of the hand of your son. However, I will not tear away all the kingdom, but I will give one tribe to your son for the sake of My servant David.'"

**1 Kings 12:19-20** — After Solomon dies, ten tribes follow Jeroboam and form the Northern Kingdom. Judah and Benjamin remain under Rehoboam in the south.

## The Northern Kingdom — Gone

**2 Kings 17:6** (NASB):
> "In the ninth year of Hoshea, the king of Assyria captured Samaria and carried Israel away into exile to Assyria, and settled them in Halah and Habor, on the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes."

**2 Kings 17:23** (NASB):
> "Until the LORD removed Israel from His sight, as He spoke through all His servants the prophets. So Israel was carried away into exile from their own land to Assyria until this day."

The Assyrian policy was deliberate ethnic displacement — move conquered peoples into foreign territories, bring foreign peoples into the vacated land. The ten tribes were scattered across Media and Assyria and did not return as a coherent group.

**2 Kings 17:24** (NASB) — What replaced them:
> "The king of Assyria brought men from Babylon and from Cuthah and from Avva and from Hamath and Sepharvaim, and settled them in the cities of Samaria in place of the sons of Israel."

These resettled peoples intermingled with remaining Israelites and became the **Samaritans** — despised by later Judeans as half-breeds. Jesus speaking to the Samaritan woman in John 4 was theologically and socially explosive for this reason.

## The Southern Kingdom — Survives

**2 Chronicles 36:20-23** (NASB) — Babylon takes Judah:
> "Those who had escaped from the sword he carried away to Babylon; and they were servants to him and to his sons until the rule of the kingdom of Persia."

But unlike the northern tribes:
- **Ezra and Nehemiah** record the return
- Genealogies were maintained (Ezra 2, Nehemiah 7)
- Temple was rebuilt
- Identity preserved

**The Jewish people today descend from this remnant** — primarily from Judah and Benjamin, with Levitical priestly lines preserved separately.

## The Land Claim Problem

When modern Israel claims the full Abrahamic covenant and all twelve-tribe land rights:

**The reality:** They represent two tribes. The covenant was made with all twelve.

The northern ten tribes' territories — the bulk of what is now called the West Bank and northern Israel — were allocated to tribes whose modern descendants are unidentified, possibly assimilated into Arab, Kurdish, Persian, and other Middle Eastern populations over 2700 years.

**This creates an uncomfortable question:** Some people currently being displaced from that land may carry Israelite blood from the lost northern kingdom — completely unaware of it — mixed with Canaanite, Assyrian, and other bloodlines across 100 generations.

## Where Did the Ten Tribes Go?

Various traditions and research propose:

- **Pashtun/Afghan tribes** — maintain oral traditions of Israelite descent, practice circumcision, observe Saturday rest, have tribal names echoing Hebrew tribes
- **Beta Israel (Ethiopian Jews)** — ancient Jewish community in Ethiopia, possibly descended from Dan
- **Igbo people of Nigeria** — traditions, practices, and oral history pointing to Israelite origin
- **Some Kashmiri populations** — local traditions of descent from Israelites brought by Assyria
- **Scattered throughout the Middle East** — absorbed into populations now identifying as Arab or Muslim

None of these are definitively proven. All are documented traditions that existing populations hold about themselves.

**Isaiah 11:11-12** (NASB) — prophesied second regathering:
> "Then it will happen on that day that the Lord will again recover the second time with His hand the remnant of His people, who will remain, from Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, and from the islands of the sea. And He will lift up a standard for the nations and assemble the banished ones of Israel, and will gather the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth."

The first regathering was from Babylon. The second regathering — which this prophecy references — is still future and involves all twelve tribes from across the entire earth.

## The Edomite Factor — Who Became Jewish

**John Hyrcanus** (c. 125 BC) — Hasmonean ruler — forcibly converted the Edomites (descendants of Esau) to Judaism. They were absorbed into the Jewish population and ceased to exist as a separate people.

**Herod the Great was Idumean (Edomite).** He was not ethnically from Judah. Yet he sat on Israel's throne and rebuilt the temple.

**The irony:** Jacob stole Esau's birthright. Two thousand years later Esau's descendants were sitting in Jacob's seat ruling Jacob's people.

This does not invalidate Jewish identity today. But it complicates any claim that modern Jewish genetic identity maps cleanly onto biblical Israelite identity.

## Translation Notes
- **שְׁבָטִים (shevatim)** — tribes — literally "rods" or "staffs," each tribe associated with a patriarch's staff
- **גָּלוּת (galut)** — exile/diaspora — the condition of being scattered from the land, a recurring theme in Hebrew theology
- **יְהוּדִים (Yehudim)** — Jews — literally "people of Judah." The term only covers one tribe by origin. Its broader application to all Israel developed during and after the Babylonian exile.

## Episode Angle
Modern Jewish identity traces to two tribes. Ten are missing. They are somewhere in the world — in Arab families, African families, Asian families, Middle Eastern families — carrying Israelite DNA without knowing it. The conflict in the Middle East today involves people who may be far more related to each other than their religious or national identities suggest. And the prophecy of the second regathering requires all twelve tribes to be identified and restored — not just the two who kept their records. God is still looking for the ten.
