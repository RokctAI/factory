# God's Glory Departs — When Darkness Is Invited In

## Core Argument
God does not get defeated and leave. He withdraws when darkness is deliberately invited into His space. Ezekiel 8-11 records this departure in precise, almost painfully slow detail — as if God wants the reader to understand He gave every opportunity before leaving. The same principle runs from the garden to the temple to the Third Temple prophecy.

---

## The Principle — Darkness Cannot Be Forced In

**Revelation 3:20** (NASB) — God Himself states the door principle in reverse:
> "Behold, I stand at the door and knock; if anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and will dine with him, and he with Me."

Even God knocks. He does not force entry. The inverse is equally true: darkness must be invited. It cannot occupy sacred space without an open door.

This principle traces directly from Eden:
- Lucifer could not enter the garden as himself — he needed the serpent (invited resident)
- He could not approach Job without divine permission — Job 1:6-12
- He entered Judas through a willing yielding — Luke 22:3 ("Satan entered into Judas")

The temple followed the same law.

---

## What God Saw in the Temple — Ezekiel 8

**Ezekiel 8:3-6** (NASB):
> "He stretched out the form of a hand and caught me by a lock of my head; and the Spirit lifted me up between earth and heaven and brought me in the visions of God to Jerusalem, to the entrance of the north gate of the inner court, where the seat of the idol of jealousy, which provokes to jealousy, was located."

God shows Ezekiel four escalating abominations inside the temple:

1. **The idol of jealousy** at the north gate — a pagan image at the entrance of the inner court (8:5)

2. **70 elders of Israel** burning incense to carved images in secret chambers (8:7-12):
> "Son of man, do you see what the elders of the house of Israel are committing in the dark, each man in the room of his carved images? For they say, 'The LORD does not see us; the LORD has forsaken the land.'"

3. **Women weeping for Tammuz** at the north gate (8:14) — a Babylonian fertility deity, worship involving ritual mourning

4. **25 men** at the entrance to the temple with their backs to the LORD, faces toward the east, bowing down to the **sun** (8:16)

All four abominations were happening simultaneously inside the temple complex. The leadership of Israel was running a dual worship system — maintaining external temple ritual while practicing pagan religion in private.

**Ezekiel 8:17** (NASB) — God's response:
> "Is it too light a thing for the house of Judah to commit the abominations which they have committed here, that they have filled the land with violence and provoked Me repeatedly?"

## The Departure — Step by Step

**Ezekiel 10:1-4** (NASB) — The glory begins to move:
> "Then I looked, and behold, in the expanse that was over the heads of the cherubim something like a sapphire stone, in appearance resembling a throne, appeared above them. And He spoke to the man clothed in linen and said, 'Enter between the whirling wheels under the cherubim and fill your hands with coals of fire from between the cherubim and scatter them over the city.' And he entered in my sight. Now the cherubim were standing on the right side of the temple when the man entered, and the cloud filled the inner court. Then the glory of the LORD went up from the cherub to the threshold of the temple."

The glory lifts from the Holy of Holies to the **threshold**. It is leaving but not yet gone. God pauses at the door.

**Ezekiel 10:18-19** (NASB):
> "Then the glory of the LORD departed from the threshold of the temple and stood over the cherubim. When the cherubim departed, they lifted their wings and rose up from the earth in my sight; with the wheels beside them as they departed, they stood still at the doorway of the east gate of the LORD's house. And the glory of the God of Israel hovered over them."

The glory moves to the **east gate**. Still pausing. Still visible.

**Ezekiel 11:22-23** (NASB) — The final departure:
> "Then the cherubim lifted up their wings with the wheels beside them, and the glory of the God of Israel hovered over them. The glory of the LORD went up from the midst of the city and stood over the mountain which is east of the city."

The **Mount of Olives**. The glory settles there and is gone from the city.

Three stages. Three pauses. God departed slowly, deliberately, and visibly — as if announcing what was happening at each step.

## The Shekinah Never Returned to the Second Temple

The **Shekinah** — God's manifest presence — departed with the first temple's destruction (586 BC by Babylon). When the exiles returned and built the second temple:

**Haggai 2:3** (NASB):
> "Who is left among you who saw this temple in its former glory? And how do you see it now? Does it not seem to you like nothing in comparison?"

**The Ark of the Covenant was gone.** The Holy of Holies of the second temple was empty. Ancient Jewish sources (Talmud, Yoma 21b) explicitly state that five things present in the first temple were absent from the second:
1. The Ark of the Covenant
2. The sacred fire
3. The Shekinah
4. The Holy Spirit (ruach hakodesh in its manifest form)
5. The Urim and Thummim

Jesus' famous statement in Matthew 23:38 (NASB):
> "Behold, your house is being left to you desolate!"

He was not predicting something new. He was confirming that the Shekinah had never returned. The building was there. God was not.

## The Pattern — Darkness Invited, Presence Withdrawn

| Event | What Was Invited | What Withdrew |
|---|---|---|
| Eden | Lucifer via serpent | Direct fellowship with God |
| Temple (Ezekiel 8) | Pagan worship in the inner court | Shekinah glory — stepwise departure |
| Solomon's later reign | Foreign wives and their gods | Kingdom divided, protection reduced |
| Israel's cycles in Judges | Canaanite gods | Deliverance and rest |
| Third Temple (coming) | The Antichrist himself | The restrainer (2 Thessalonians 2:7) |

---

## 2 Thessalonians 2:3-4 (NASB) — The Final Invitation:
> "Let no one in any way deceive you, for it will not come unless the apostasy comes first, and the man of lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction, who opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god or object of worship, so that he takes his seat in the temple of God, displaying himself as being God."

The temple must be rebuilt. The Antichrist must be invited in — or at minimum, his covenant must enable the building (Daniel 9:27). Once he sits in it he declares himself God. The ultimate inversion: the space built to invite God's return becomes the throne of the one who has been trying to replace Him since before the garden.

## Translation Notes
- **שְׁכִינָה (Shekinah)** — not a biblical word itself but a rabbinical term derived from the Hebrew **שָׁכַן (shakan)** — to dwell. It describes God's manifest, tangible presence dwelling among His people.
- **כָּבוֹד (kavod)** — glory — literally "heaviness" or "weight." God's glory has physical presence and weight in Hebrew understanding.
- **Tammuz** in Ezekiel 8:14 — a Sumerian/Babylonian deity whose annual death and resurrection was mourned ritually. The women mourning Tammuz inside the temple court is the specific image that prompted one of God's strongest responses in the vision.

---

## Episode Angle
God did not abandon the temple suddenly or in anger. He left the way a grieved person leaves a relationship — slowly, giving every signal, pausing at each threshold. By the time He reached the Mount of Olives the temple was already functionally empty even though the rituals continued. Eight hundred years later Jesus would weep over Jerusalem on that same Mount of Olives. The departure and the mourning happened at the same location. The same door that glory left through is the door the Messiah entered and will return through — Ezekiel 43:1-5 says the glory returns from the east.
