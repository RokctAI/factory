# Israel's Failure — Interbreeding and the Absorbed Curse

## Core Argument
God's command to completely destroy the Canaanite nations was not carried out. What followed was not just religious compromise — it was the gradual absorption of the very bloodline and corruption the flood had tried to eliminate. By the time the prophets were screaming about Israel's state, the Canaanite influence was not just cultural or spiritual. It had been bred into the covenant people across generations.

---

## The Command That Was Not Kept

**Deuteronomy 7:1-3** (NASB):
> "You shall utterly destroy them. You shall make no covenant with them and show no favor to them. Furthermore, you shall not intermarry with them; you shall not give your daughters to their sons, nor shall you take their daughters for your sons."

**Deuteronomy 20:16-17** (NASB) — even more specific:
> "Only in the cities of these peoples that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, you shall not leave alive anything that breathes. But you shall utterly destroy them."

**חֵרֶם (herem)** — total consecrated destruction. No exceptions, no survivors, no covenant, no marriage. The command was absolute.

---

## What Actually Happened

**Judges 1:19-33** (NASB) — tribe by tribe failure:

- **Judah** — could not drive out inhabitants of the valley because they had iron chariots (1:19)
- **Benjamin** — did not drive out Jebusites; they live in Jerusalem to this day (1:21)
- **Manasseh** — did not take possession of Beth-shean, Taanach, Dor, Ibleam, Megiddo (1:27)
- **Ephraim** — did not drive out Canaanites in Gezer (1:29)
- **Zebulun** — did not drive out inhabitants of Kitron or Nahalol (1:30)
- **Asher** — did not drive out inhabitants of Acco, Sidon, Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik, Rehob (1:31-32)
- **Naphtali** — did not drive out inhabitants of Beth-mesh or Beth-anath (1:33)

The pattern is consistent and nationwide. Every tribe chose partial obedience. Every tribe left populations that were supposed to be removed.

**Judges 2:1-3** (NASB) — God's response:
> "I said, 'I will never break My covenant with you, and as for you, you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall tear down their altars.' But you have not obeyed Me... Therefore I also said, 'I will not drive them out before you; but they will become as thorns in your sides and their gods will be a snare to you.'"

---

## The Interbreeding That Followed

**Judges 3:5-6** (NASB):
> "The sons of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites; and they took their daughters for themselves as wives, and gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods."

The sequence is deliberate in the text: live among → intermarry → worship their gods. You could not take the wife and leave her religion. The marriages carried the worship. The worship carried the corruption.

---

## Solomon — The Most Devastating Example

**1 Kings 11:1-4** (NASB):
> "Now King Solomon loved many foreign women along with the daughter of Pharaoh: Moabite, Ammonite, Edomite, Sidonian, and Hittite women, from the nations concerning which the LORD had said to the sons of Israel, 'You shall not associate with them, nor shall they associate with you, for they will surely turn your heart away after their gods.' Solomon held fast to these in love. He had seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines, and his wives turned his heart away."

**1 Kings 11:7** (NASB):
> "Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh the detestable idol of Moab, on the mountain which is east of Jerusalem, and for Molech the detestable idol of the sons of Ammon."

The wisest man who ever lived — **child sacrifice altars** on the Mount of Olives. Built by his own hands. For his foreign wives.

The Canaanite corruption operating at the highest level of Israel's power, not through invasion but through marriage and accommodation.

---

## Child Sacrifice Returns

Solomon opened the door. Later kings walked through it:

**2 Kings 16:3** (NASB) — King Ahaz:
> "He even made his son pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had driven out from before the sons of Israel."

**2 Kings 21:6** (NASB) — Manasseh:
> "He made his son pass through the fire, practiced witchcraft and used divination, and dealt with mediums and spiritists. He did much evil in the sight of the LORD provoking Him to anger."

The specific practices God cited as the reason for destroying the Canaanites were now being practiced by Israel's own kings — a direct result of the interbreeding and accommodation that had been building for centuries.

---

## The Bloodline Question

By the time of the later prophets, after generations of intermarriage with Canaanite populations, the question becomes uncomfortable: how much of the corruption Israel was displaying was spiritual adoption versus biological expression of what had been bred in?

This is not answerable definitively. But the pattern God warned about in Deuteronomy 7:4 — "they will turn your sons away" — was not just about religion. It was about the transmission of something through family lines that the text treats as running deeper than choice.

**Nehemiah 13:23-25** (NASB) — even after the exile and return:
> "In those days I also saw that the Jews had married women from Ashdod, Ammon and Moab. As for their children, half spoke in the language of Ashdod, and none of them was able to speak the language of Judah... So I contended with them and cursed them and struck some of them and pulled out their hair, and made them swear by God: 'You shall not give your daughters to their sons, nor take of their daughters for your sons or for yourselves.'"

After exile, after return, after the temple was rebuilt — the same interbreeding was still happening. The same warning was still being ignored.

## Translation Notes
- **חֵרֶם (herem)** — appears in Joshua and Deuteronomy for total destruction commanded by God — same word root used for things set apart as sacred/consecrated. To herem something is to remove it entirely from human use and devote it to God — either as an offering or as something destroyed for His purposes.
- **בָּמוֹת (bamot)** — high places — not just geographic features but established worship sites that Israel repeatedly failed to remove. Solomon's high places on the Mount of Olives persisted for over 300 years until Josiah's reforms (2 Kings 23:13).
- **כְּמוֹשׁ (Chemosh)** and **מֹלֶךְ (Molech)** — Canaanite/Ammonite deities associated with child sacrifice. Their altars on Israel's sacred mountain represent the full inversion of what that land was supposed to become.

---

## Episode Angle
Israel did not fall away from God in a single dramatic moment. The compromise was incremental, relational, and domestic. It happened in marriages. In households. In the private worship of a wife's gods while maintaining public temple ritual. By the time the prophets were writing, the Canaanite corruption was not foreign anymore — it was family. The very thing the flood tried to eliminate, the curse of Canaan, had been voluntarily absorbed into the covenant bloodline through three generations of disobedience. And the land — still waiting for the curse to be removed so it could produce its promise — was still defiled.
