## Subtopic: Review of Algebraic Expressions

Before we tackle the equation itself, we must ensure our foundation is solid. An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase that can contain ordinary numbers, variables—like x or y—and operators, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

In our equation, 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0, the left side, 2x² + 5x + 3, is an algebraic expression. It is a trinomial, meaning it has three terms.

Notice the structure. The '2x²' is the quadratic term, giving the equation its name. The '5x' is the linear term. The '3' is the constant term. The '= 0' transforms this expression into an equation. We are searching for the values of x that make this statement true.

Factoring is the process of breaking down this expression into simpler components, or factors, that, when multiplied together, return the original expression. Think of it as reverse-engineering. If multiplication is building the structure, factoring is carefully taking it apart to examine its foundation.

## Subtopic: Principles of the Factoring Method

The factoring method we will use is sometimes called "factoring by grouping" or the "ac method." It is precise and systematic.

Our standard form is ax² + bx + c = 0.
In our problem:
a equals 2.
b equals 5.
c equals 3.

The central principle of this method is to split the middle term, the linear term 'bx', into two separate terms. We do this so that we can then factor the four-term expression by grouping it into pairs.

But we cannot split '5x' arbitrarily. We must find two specific numbers. Let us call them 'p' and 'q'.
These two numbers must satisfy two conditions simultaneously:
Condition one: When multiplied together, p times q, they must equal the product of 'a' and 'c'.
Condition two: When added together, p plus q, they must equal 'b'.

This is the lock. We must find the combination to open it.

## Subtopic: Step-by-Step Example (2x² + 5x + 3 = 0)

Let us apply these principles to our equation: 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0.

Step one: Calculate the product of 'a' and 'c'.
a is 2, c is 3.
Therefore, a times c equals 2 multiplied by 3, which is 6.

Step two: Identify our 'b' value.
b is 5.

Now, we need our two numbers, 'p' and 'q'.
They must multiply to 6.
They must add to 5.

Let us consider the factors of 6.
1 and 6. 1 plus 6 is 7. This is incorrect.
2 and 3. 2 multiplied by 3 is 6. 2 plus 3 is 5.
We have found our numbers: 2 and 3.

Step three: Rewrite the equation, splitting the middle term '5x' using our two numbers.
We rewrite 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0 as:
2x² + 2x + 3x + 3 = 0.

Observe how 5x has become 2x + 3x. The value remains unchanged, but the structure is now open for factoring.

Step four: Factor by grouping. We group the four terms into two pairs.
Pair one: (2x² + 2x)
Pair two: (3x + 3)

Now, we factor out the greatest common factor from each pair.
From the first pair, (2x² + 2x), the greatest common factor is 2x.
Factoring this out leaves us with: 2x(x + 1).

From the second pair, (3x + 3), the greatest common factor is 3.
Factoring this out leaves us with: + 3(x + 1).

Let us write the complete equation now:
2x(x + 1) + 3(x + 1) = 0.

Notice the crucial detail. The expression inside the parentheses, (x + 1), is identical for both pairs. This is the indicator that our method is successful.

Step five: Factor out the common binomial. We now have two terms, and both share the factor (x + 1).
We factor out (x + 1), which leaves the outside terms, 2x and +3, to form our second binomial.

The factored form is:
(x + 1)(2x + 3) = 0.

We have successfully reverse-engineered the quadratic expression into the product of two binomials.

## Subtopic: The Zero-Product Property

We have reached the final stage. Our equation is (x + 1)(2x + 3) = 0.
To solve for x, we utilize a fundamental rule of mathematics: The Zero-Product Property.

This property states a simple but profound truth. If the product of two quantities is zero, then at least one of those quantities must be zero. If A times B equals 0, then either A equals 0, or B equals 0.

In our equation, our two quantities are the binomials (x + 1) and (2x + 3).
Therefore, we set each binomial equal to zero and solve for x.

First equation:
x + 1 = 0
Subtract 1 from both sides.
x = -1.

Second equation:
2x + 3 = 0
Subtract 3 from both sides.
2x = -3
Divide by 2.
x = -3/2.

We have found our solutions. x equals -1, and x equals -3/2.

These are the roots of the quadratic equation. They are the exact values that, if substituted back into the original equation 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0, will make the statement true.
