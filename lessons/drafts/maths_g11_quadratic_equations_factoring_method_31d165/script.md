# Maths Grade 11: Quadratic Equations - Factoring method

## Subtopic: Reviewing Algebraic Expressions
Welcome. I am the Grandmaster. Today, we will conquer the factoring method for quadratic equations.
Before we begin, we must ensure our foundational knowledge is sharp. An algebraic expression represents a mathematical phrase with variables, numbers, and operations. When you multiply two binomials, say (x + 1) and (2x + 3), you produce a quadratic expression: 2x² + 5x + 3.
To solve equations, we often need to reverse this process. We must break a quadratic expression down into its binomial factors. This is the art of factoring.

## Subtopic: Identifying the Coefficients
Let us examine our primary subject: the equation 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0.
A standard quadratic equation is written in the form ax² + bx + c = 0.
Our first maneuver is to identify the coefficients a, b, and c.
Observe our equation carefully.
Here, 'a', the coefficient of the x² term, is 2.
'b', the coefficient of the x term, is 5.
And 'c', the constant term, is 3.
Recognizing these values with absolute certainty is non-negotiable. It is the starting position of our factoring strategy.

## Subtopic: Finding the Magic Numbers
We now seek two specific values, which we shall call our 'magic numbers'.
These numbers must satisfy two absolute conditions simultaneously.
First, their product must equal the product of 'a' and 'c'. That is a multiplied by c.
In our equation, a times c is 2 multiplied by 3, which equals 6.
Second, the sum of these same two numbers must equal 'b', which is 5.
We need two numbers that multiply to 6, and add to 5.
Let us consider the factors of 6: 1 and 6, or 2 and 3.
1 plus 6 is 7. This fails.
2 plus 3 is 5. This succeeds.
Our magic numbers are precisely 2 and 3.

## Subtopic: Factoring by Grouping
We will now execute a technique known as factoring by grouping. We use our magic numbers to split the middle term, the 5x.
We rewrite our equation 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0 as:
2x² + 2x + 3x + 3 = 0.
Notice that the equation remains mathematically identical. We have simply expanded the middle term.
Now, we group the terms into pairs: (2x² + 2x) + (3x + 3) = 0.
Next, we extract the greatest common factor from each pair.
From the first group, (2x² + 2x), we factor out 2x, leaving 2x(x + 1).
From the second group, (3x + 3), we factor out 3, leaving 3(x + 1).
Our equation now stands as: 2x(x + 1) + 3(x + 1) = 0.
Observe that the binomial (x + 1) is common to both parts. We can factor this binomial out entirely.
This yields our final factored form: (x + 1)(2x + 3) = 0.

## Subtopic: Applying the Zero-Product Property
We arrive at the final phase: the zero-product property.
This fundamental mathematical law states that if the product of two factors is zero, then at least one of the factors must be zero.
Therefore, if (x + 1) multiplied by (2x + 3) equals 0, then either:
x + 1 = 0, or
2x + 3 = 0.
We solve each linear equation independently.
If x + 1 = 0, then x = -1.
If 2x + 3 = 0, then 2x = -3, which means x = -3/2.
These are the solutions to our quadratic equation. We have successfully employed the factoring method. Remember this procedure; precision is your greatest ally. Until next time.
