### subtopic_1 — What Is Production?
**Assistant:** Is teaching really production if nothing physical is made?

**Tutor:** Yes. Production means creating anything that satisfies needs and wants, and that includes services as well as goods. A teacher uses inputs, such as her knowledge, time, a classroom, textbooks and a board, and through the process of teaching produces an output: learners who understand more and can do more than before. That output has real value, both to the learners, whose future earnings and life chances improve, and to society, which needs educated citizens and workers. The same is true of doctors, nurses, hairdressers, bus drivers, bankers and software developers. In South Africa, as in most modern economies, more people work in producing services than in producing physical goods. Services are measured as part of a country's total production, called gross domestic product, alongside farming, mining and manufacturing. What makes something production is that resources are used to create something of value that people need or want and, usually, are willing to pay for, whether the output can be touched or not. In an exam, you can describe a service with the same three parts: inputs, process and outputs.

### subtopic_2 — The Factors of Production and Their Rewards
**Assistant:** Why is the entrepreneur paid last?

**Tutor:** Because the entrepreneur takes the risk, and profit is what remains after everyone else has been paid. When a business produces and sells its goods, it must pay the landlord the agreed rent, the workers their agreed wages, and the lenders their agreed interest, whether the business does well or badly; these are contracts. The entrepreneur has no guaranteed payment. If sales are strong and costs are controlled, there is a lot left over and the profit can be large. If sales are weak, there may be nothing left at all, and the entrepreneur bears the loss, perhaps losing the capital invested. This is why profit is called the reward for risk-taking. It also explains why entrepreneurs work so hard to control costs and attract customers: their own income depends on it. Some people argue that profits are too high in some industries; others point out that without the chance of profit, nobody would take the risk of starting businesses, and there would be fewer jobs and products. In an exam, explain that the entrepreneur is a residual claimant: paid from what is left, which is why profit can be high, low or negative.

### subtopic_3 — The Three Sectors of Production
**Assistant:** If services are the biggest sector, why do mining and farming still matter so much?

**Tutor:** Because they matter in ways that a share of output does not fully show. First, exports: minerals such as platinum, gold, coal, iron ore and chrome, and farm products such as citrus, wine, grapes and maize, earn a large share of South Africa's foreign currency, which pays for imports such as fuel, machinery and medicines. Second, jobs in particular places: mining and farming employ many people in rural areas and small towns where there are few other employers, and many of those jobs suit people without tertiary education. Third, linkages: the primary sector supplies the raw materials for the secondary sector, such as steel mills, food factories and power stations, and creates demand for tertiary services such as transport, banking and ports. Fourth, food security: the country depends on its own farmers for much of its food. When mining or farming struggle, because of falling commodity prices, drought or electricity problems, the effects spread through the whole economy. So in an exam, explain that each sector depends on the others, and that the primary sector remains important for exports, rural jobs, raw materials and food.
