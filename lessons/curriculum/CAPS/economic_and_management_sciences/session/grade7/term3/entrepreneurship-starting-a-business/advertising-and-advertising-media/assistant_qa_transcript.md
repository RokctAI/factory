### subtopic_1 — What Advertising Is and Why Businesses Advertise
**Assistant:** Is advertising good or bad for consumers?

**Tutor:** It can be both, and a balanced view recognises this. On the good side, advertising informs consumers about products, prices, special offers and new inventions, which helps them make choices. It increases competition, as businesses try to attract customers, which can lead to better quality and lower prices. It pays for many free services, such as radio stations, free-to-air television and many websites and apps. It helps new and small businesses become known, which creates jobs. On the negative side, advertising can persuade people to buy things they do not need or cannot afford, encouraging debt. It can create unrealistic images of beauty or success that affect self-esteem, especially among young people. Some adverts are misleading, exaggerating benefits or hiding costs. Advertising of unhealthy products, such as sugary drinks and junk food aimed at children, is a concern for health experts. In South Africa, the Consumer Protection Act prohibits false and misleading advertising, and the Advertising Regulatory Board handles complaints. As a consumer, the best protection is to think critically: ask what the advert is not telling you, compare prices and ask whether you really need the product.

### subtopic_2 — Advertising Media
**Assistant:** Which advertising medium is best?

**Tutor:** There is no single best medium; the right choice depends on the business, its target market, its message and its budget. A national cellphone company launching a new package might use television, radio, billboards and social media together, because it wants to reach millions of people of all ages and can afford it. A new restaurant in a small town might use the local community newspaper, a sign outside, flyers in nearby offices and a Facebook page, because its customers live nearby. A plumber might rely on word of mouth, a listing on a website and a vehicle sign. A clothing brand aimed at teenagers would probably focus on Instagram and TikTok, where its target market spends time. A business must also consider reach, meaning how many people will see the advert; frequency, how often they will see it; cost per person reached; and whether the medium suits the message. Food and fashion look good in pictures and videos; a detailed offer may need print. Many businesses use a mix of media and track which bring in the most customers, for example by asking new customers how they heard about the business.

### subtopic_3 — Choosing Media for a Small Business
**Assistant:** How can a stall with no money advertise effectively?

**Tutor:** By using free and low-cost methods cleverly and making the product itself do some of the advertising. Start with posters made by hand: bright colours, a clear picture, the product name, the price and the time and place of the stall. Put them where your target market will see them, with permission. Ask your teacher if you can make a short announcement at assembly or in class. Share a simple digital poster in class WhatsApp groups if the school allows it. Then use word of mouth, the most trusted medium of all: ask friends and family to tell others, and give a few free samples early in the day so that people who enjoy them tell their friends. Make the stall itself attractive: a tidy table, a banner, a catchy slogan, team members wearing matching colours and friendly greetings. The smell of freshly made popcorn or vetkoek is advertising too. Offer a small special, such as two for R15, to encourage people to buy more. These methods cost very little but can be very effective, which is exactly how many real small businesses start. The next lesson, on AIDA, will help you design the message itself.
