# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the concept of advertising and the media used to carry advertisements. The governing idea is that advertising is a paid, persuasive communication that informs potential customers about a product and encourages them to buy, and that a business chooses its media by considering its target market, budget, reach and the kind of message it needs to send. The definition and purposes of advertising, the types of advertising media with their advantages and disadvantages, choosing media for a small business, and responsible advertising are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Advertising Is and Why Businesses Advertise

Advertising is a form of communication, usually paid for by a business, that informs people about a product or service and tries to persuade them to buy it. Advertising is one part of marketing, which includes everything a business does to understand its customers and sell to them, such as choosing the product, the price and the place where it is sold.

Businesses advertise for several reasons. To create awareness: customers cannot buy a product they do not know exists, so a new business or product must be introduced. To inform: adverts tell customers what the product is, what it does, how much it costs, where to buy it and any special offers. To persuade: adverts give reasons to buy this product rather than a competitor's, by showing its benefits, quality, price or style. To remind: well-known businesses keep advertising so that customers do not forget them. To build a brand: over time, advertising creates an image and a reputation that customers recognise and trust.

The target market is the group of people the business most wants to reach, described by things such as age, location, income and interests. An advert for school shoes targets parents of school-going children; an advert for a gaming app targets teenagers and young adults. Good advertising is designed for its target market: it uses language, pictures and media that those people respond to.

Advertising costs money, so a business must decide how much it can spend, its advertising budget, and use that money where it will reach the most potential customers.

## Subtopic: Advertising Media

Advertising media are the channels used to carry adverts to the public. Each has advantages and disadvantages.

Print media include newspapers, magazines, flyers, pamphlets and posters. Local community newspapers reach people in one area cheaply. Magazines target specific interests, such as sport or fashion. Flyers can be handed out where customers are. Print adverts can be kept and read again, but newspaper readership has fallen, and print cannot show movement or sound.

Broadcast media are radio and television. Radio is very important in South Africa, because it reaches millions of people in every language, including in rural areas, and community radio stations are affordable for local businesses. Television reaches huge audiences and combines pictures, sound and movement, which makes adverts memorable, but it is very expensive to produce and air adverts.

Outdoor media include billboards along roads, signs on buildings, adverts on and inside taxis and buses, and posters at taxi ranks, malls and stadiums. They are seen by many people repeatedly, but the message must be very short because people pass quickly.

Digital media include social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp, websites, search engines, email and SMS. They can target specific groups precisely, allow customers to respond immediately, and can be cheap or even free for a small business with a WhatsApp group or social media page. But there is a lot of competition for attention, and they reach only people with phones and data.

Other forms include word of mouth, when happy customers recommend a business to others, which is free and very trusted; sponsorships of sports teams and events; displays in shop windows; branded items such as T-shirts and caps; and samples or tastings.

## Subtopic: Choosing Media for a Small Business

A small business or an Entrepreneur's Day stall usually has a tiny advertising budget, so it must choose media carefully.

First, identify the target market. For a school stall, it is learners, teachers and perhaps parents visiting on the day.

Second, ask where those people will see or hear the message. Learners see notice boards, classroom walls and the area around the tuck shop; many are in class and school WhatsApp groups; they listen to announcements at assembly.

Third, consider the cost. Hand-drawn posters cost only paper and markers. A short announcement at assembly may be free with permission. A message in a WhatsApp group is free. Flyers cost a little for printing. Samples, such as small tasting pieces, cost some product but can be very persuasive.

Fourth, plan the message: the name of the business or product, what it is, why it is good, the price and where and when to buy. Keep it short, clear and eye-catching, with a picture or bright colours and a catchy slogan.

Responsible advertising matters even for a small stall. The message must be honest: do not claim the muffins are sugar-free if they are not. Prices must be clear and correct. Adverts should not be offensive, should not make fun of people and should respect the school's rules. South Africa's Consumer Protection Act requires that advertising not be false or misleading, and these values apply to everyone.

Finally, measure what works. Ask customers how they heard about the stall. Next time, spend more effort on the media that brought the most customers.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again through one popcorn stall and a week of almost-free advertising, in plain words with the same facts.

## Subtopic: Who Needs to Know?

Lindo and Musa have the best popcorn in the school, but nobody knows yet. They need to advertise.

First they ask: who needs to know? Their target market is learners and teachers at their school.

Then: where do those people look and listen? Learners walk past the tuck shop every break. Everybody attends assembly on Monday. Most classes have a WhatsApp group run by the class rep.

So they choose three almost-free media. Bright hand-made posters near the tuck shop and on classroom doors: print media. A thirty-second announcement at Monday's assembly, with the principal's permission: like radio, but live. A digital poster shared in class WhatsApp groups: digital media.

They do not choose television or a billboard. Those reach millions of people they do not need, and cost far more than their whole budget.

## Subtopic: The Best Advert Is a Happy Customer

On market day, Lindo and Musa add two more kinds of advertising.

At the start of first break they hand out small free samples. People taste the popcorn, love it and tell their friends. That is word of mouth, and it is free and trusted.

Their stall is tidy, with a big banner, and the smell of fresh popcorn drifts across the playground. The stall itself is advertising.

They stay honest. Their poster says R10 a bag, and that is what they charge. They do not claim it is healthy or sugar-free, because the caramel kind is not. Honest adverts build trust; misleading adverts break the law and lose customers.

Every time someone buys, Musa asks: how did you hear about us? At the end of the day they count: most said a friend told them, then the posters, then WhatsApp. Next time they will give out more samples early.

Remember: advertising informs and persuades; choose media your target market actually sees; keep it honest; and check what works.
