### subtopic_1 — Forms of Electronic Banking
**Assistant:** If my money is only numbers on a computer, is it really there?

**Tutor:** Yes, in the sense that matters. Your bank balance is a legal record that the bank owes you that amount, and you can turn it into cash at an ATM or spend it with a card or EFT. Banks do not keep all deposits as cash in a vault; they lend much of it to other customers and keep enough on hand for normal withdrawals. This works because, on an ordinary day, only a small share of customers want cash at the same time. To protect depositors, banks in South Africa are registered and supervised by the Prudential Authority, which is part of the South African Reserve Bank, and since 2024 a deposit insurance scheme protects ordinary deposits up to a set limit if a bank fails. So the money is real, but in a different form from coins and notes: it is a promise from a supervised bank, backed by law. That is the same idea of trust you met with promissory notes, now in electronic form. The practical lesson is to keep your banking details and PIN secret, because anyone who has them can move those numbers out of your account.

### subtopic_2 — Advantages and Risks of Electronic Banking
**Assistant:** Why would a bank never ask for my PIN on the phone?

**Tutor:** Because the bank does not need it. Your PIN and your one-time PINs are secret codes that prove to the bank's computer that you, and only you, are approving a transaction. The bank's own systems can already see your account; they never need you to read out the code. So anyone who phones, SMSs or messages you asking for your PIN, password or one-time PIN is almost certainly a criminal trying to approve a payment from your account. These criminals can sound very convincing. They may know your name, say there is a problem with your account and create a feeling of panic so that you act quickly. The safe response is always the same: do not share the code, end the call, and phone the bank yourself using the number on the back of your card or on the official website. South African banks and the South African Banking Risk Information Centre repeat this warning often because so many people lose money to these tricks. Teaching older family members this one rule may protect their savings.

### subtopic_3 — The Role and Functions of Money
**Assistant:** Does money itself make a country rich?

**Tutor:** No, and this is one of the most important ideas in economics. A country is rich when it produces many goods and services that people need and want: food, houses, medicines, schooling, transport and so on. Money is a tool that helps those goods and services change hands; it is not the goods and services themselves. If a government simply printed more money without more goods being produced, there would be more rand chasing the same number of goods, and prices would rise. Each rand would buy less. This is what happened in Zimbabwe in the late 2000s, when prices rose so fast that notes of one hundred trillion Zimbabwe dollars were printed and still bought very little. That is why the South African Reserve Bank's main job is to protect the value of the rand by keeping inflation low and stable. For your answers, say that money is a medium of exchange, a measure of value, a store of value and a standard for deferred payment, and that real wealth comes from producing goods and services. You will study production in Term 4.
