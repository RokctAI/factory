### subtopic_1 — Differentiating Between Needs and Wants
**Assistant:** Can something be a need for one person and a want for another?

**Tutor:** Yes, and recognising this shows real understanding. Needs and wants depend partly on a person's circumstances. Spectacles are a need for a learner who cannot read the board without them, and a fashion accessory for someone with perfect eyesight who wears plain-glass frames. A wheelchair is a need for a person who cannot walk. A car is a want for most families in a city with taxis and buses, but for a district nurse who must reach patients on farms far from public transport it may be a need for her work. Insulin is a need for a person with diabetes but not for others. Even food can differ: a person with an allergy needs special food. However, there is a core of needs that every human being shares: enough nutritious food, clean water, shelter, clothing, health care and safety. In an exam, when a question asks you to classify an item, give your classification and a short reason linked to the person's situation. Markers reward the reasoning, for example: a cellphone is a need for a job-seeker who receives interview calls, because without it she could miss work opportunities.

### subtopic_2 — Basic Needs of Individuals and Families
**Assistant:** Why is education counted as a need and not a want?

**Tutor:** Because in a modern society a person cannot live a decent, independent life without it. In a traditional society, children learned the skills they needed, such as farming, herding, building or cooking, from their families. In a modern economy most work requires reading, writing, calculating and using technology, and many jobs require a qualification. A person without basic education is far more likely to be unemployed or stuck in low-paid work, and is less able to understand contracts, medicine instructions, bank statements or election information. That is why the South African Constitution gives everyone the right to a basic education, including adult basic education, and why schooling is compulsory from Grade 1 to Grade 9, or until a child turns fifteen. Government spends more on education than on almost any other function for the same reason. Education also benefits the whole community, not only the individual: educated people earn more, pay more tax, look after their health better and can build businesses that employ others. So in an exam you can explain that education is a need because it allows people to meet all their other needs through work and income.

### subtopic_3 — Needs of Communities and the Country
**Assistant:** Why do some communities protest about service delivery?

**Tutor:** Because the services they lack are basic needs, and families cannot meet them on their own. A household can buy food and clothing with its income, but it cannot build a water pipeline, a sewerage system, a clinic or a tarred road. Those are provided collectively, mainly by municipalities and provinces, using taxes, rates and grants from the national budget. When services fail, for example taps run dry for weeks, sewage spills into streets, refuse is not collected or electricity is cut, people's health, safety and dignity are directly affected. Communities have the right to protest peacefully and to hold elected officials accountable; the Constitution protects the right to assemble and demonstrate peacefully. Problems arise when protests turn violent and destroy clinics, libraries or schools, because that damages the very services the community needs. There are also other ways to be heard: ward committees, public meetings on the municipality's integrated development plan, petitions and voting. In your answer, explain that service delivery meets community needs, that its failure affects every family, and that communities have peaceful channels to demand improvement.
