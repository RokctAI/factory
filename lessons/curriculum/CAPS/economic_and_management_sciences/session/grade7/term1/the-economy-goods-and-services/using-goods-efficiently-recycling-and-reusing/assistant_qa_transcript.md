### subtopic_1 — Using Goods and Services Efficiently and Effectively
**Assistant:** Can something be efficient but not effective?

**Tutor:** Yes. Efficiency is about not wasting resources, while effectiveness is about achieving the right goal. You can do something with very little waste and still not achieve what you needed. Imagine a learner who studies for two hours using every minute well, with no distractions, but studies the wrong chapter for tomorrow's test. Her study was efficient but not effective. Or think of a municipality that builds a clinic very cheaply and quickly, which is efficient, but in a place where few people live and with no road to reach it, so it does not meet the community's health needs; it is not effective. The opposite also happens: something can be effective but wasteful, such as a family that keeps the house warm in winter by running three heaters in an empty room. The ideal is to be both: do the right thing, and do it with as little waste as possible. In an exam, define both terms separately and give an example that shows the difference, because markers look for that understanding.

### subtopic_2 — Reduce, Reuse and Recycle
**Assistant:** Why is reduce better than recycle?

**Tutor:** Because recycling still uses resources, while reducing avoids using them in the first place. When a plastic bottle is recycled, it must be collected, transported to a depot, sorted, cleaned, shredded, melted and remade, and every step uses energy, water, fuel and labour. Some of the material is lost along the way, and some plastics can only be recycled a few times. If the bottle was never needed, for example because you carried a reusable water bottle, none of those resources were used and no waste was created. Reusing sits in the middle: the item is used again without being broken down and remade, which saves most of the resources. So the order of the waste hierarchy reflects how much each option saves. This does not mean recycling is unimportant. For things we really need, such as cans and glass jars, recycling is far better than sending them to landfill, and it creates jobs. But when you plan how a household or school can cut waste, start by asking what you can stop using, then what you can use again, and only then what you can recycle.

### subtopic_3 — Recycling as an Economic Opportunity
**Assistant:** Are waste pickers treated fairly?

**Tutor:** There are different views, and the honest answer is that their situation has often been difficult, though it is slowly improving in some places. Waste pickers provide a valuable service: they collect a large share of the material that is recycled in South Africa, keep it out of landfills and save municipalities money. Yet most work informally, without contracts, protective clothing, medical cover or a guaranteed income, and they earn only what buy-back centres pay per kilogram, which can fall when prices for recyclables drop. Some face harassment or are chased away from landfill sites and streets. On the other hand, waste picker organisations, researchers and the national government have worked on guidelines that recognise waste pickers as part of the recycling system, and some municipalities and packaging companies now register them, provide equipment and pay service fees. Households can help too by separating recyclables and leaving them clean and accessible, and by treating waste pickers with respect. In an exam, a balanced answer recognises both their economic and environmental contribution and the challenges they face.
