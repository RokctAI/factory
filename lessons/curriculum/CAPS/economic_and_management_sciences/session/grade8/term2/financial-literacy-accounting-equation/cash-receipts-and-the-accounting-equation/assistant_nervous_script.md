Hey, if cash receipts in the accounting equation felt tricky, here is the calm version: three kinds of money in.

Every receipt makes the bank go up. The question is what else moves.

Kind one: the owner's money or earned money. Capital, fees for services, rent income, interest. The owner's share, owner's equity, goes up too.

Kind two: borrowed money. A loan. The amount owed, liabilities, goes up. The owner's share does not change. A loan is never income.

Kind three: swapped money. Selling an asset at its book value, or a debtor paying what they owed. Bank goes up, another asset goes down. Nothing else moves.

The scale always stays level: assets equal owner's equity plus liabilities.

And remember: when owner's equity changes, give the reason, capital or income.

A quick test for any receipt: did the business earn something new, borrow it, or simply turn something it already owned into money? Only owner's money and earned money make the owner's share bigger.
