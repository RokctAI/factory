Today we watch the bank account of a new photography studio and ask one question every time money lands in it: what is this money? The owner deposits capital, a bank lends thirty thousand rand, families pay for photo shoots, a neighbour rents a corner, the bank pays interest, an old camera is sold and a client who owed money finally pays. Every cash receipt increases the asset bank, but the other half of the transaction can increase owner's equity, increase liabilities, or reduce another asset. We analyse all eight receipts in the accounting equation, give the reason for each change in owner's equity, total the effects to prove the equation still balances, and link each receipt to the column and source document it will use in the cash receipts journal.
