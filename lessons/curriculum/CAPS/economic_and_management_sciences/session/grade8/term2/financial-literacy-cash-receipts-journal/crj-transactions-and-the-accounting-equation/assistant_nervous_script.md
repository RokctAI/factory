Hey, if linking the CRJ to the accounting equation felt like two topics at once, here is the calm version: two questions for every line.

Question one: did the bank go up? In the CRJ, always yes. Assets increase every time.

Question two: what kind of money was it?
Current income column: earned, so owner's equity goes up, reason income.
Sundry capital: the owner's money, owner's equity up, reason capital.
Sundry rent or interest income: earned, owner's equity up, reason income.
Sundry loan: borrowed, liabilities up, owner's equity unchanged.
Sundry debtors: a swap, one asset up and another down.

For a whole month: the bank total is the increase in assets, and it must equal the increase in owner's equity plus liabilities.

Every debit posted from the CRJ is the left of the equation going up; every credit is the right going up.

Then split owner's equity into capital and income, so you know how much was really earned.

And remember: the CRJ is only money in, so it cannot show profit by itself.

Open the practice set and analyse a few CRJ lines. You've got this!
