Today we read two closed cash receipts journals of an appliance repair business as if they were statements about the accounting equation. Every line in a CRJ increases the asset bank, and the column and account name tell us whether owner's equity rose through capital or income, whether liabilities rose through a loan, or whether another asset was simply turned into cash. We analyse individual entries, then summarise March, the first month, with its capital and bank loan, and April, an ordinary month of repairs, proving that each month's bank total equals the increase in owner's equity plus liabilities. We separate capital from income to see how much the business really earned, see why the CRJ alone cannot show profit, and finish with an exam-style analysis table.
