Today we open the cash receipts journal of an appliance repair business and learn its layout lane by lane. At the end of each day the bookkeeper faces a small pile of paper, duplicate receipts, the cash register roll and a deposit slip, and at month-end the bank statement, and every rand in that pile must be entered in the CRJ. We set out the purpose of the CRJ, then the job of each column: document, day, details, folio, analysis of receipts, bank, current income and the sundry accounts section with its amount, folio and details. We match each source document to its entries, see how the deposit slip and bank statement act as checks, and practise analysing real receipts, from capital and loans to combined deposits and interest, into the correct columns.
