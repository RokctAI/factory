# Part 1 — Expert

This session takes a full month of receipts for a service business, enters each one in the cash receipts journal, and closes the journal off at the end of the month. The anchor is April at the appliance repair business: eight days on which money arrived, from cash register rolls and customer receipts to an extra capital contribution, storeroom rent, a customer's EFT and interest from the bank. By the end of the session the April CRJ is complete, its columns are totalled and cross-checked, and its totals are ready to post to the general ledger. The governing idea is that entering a CRJ is a disciplined routine, applied in date order to each source document, and that closing it off proves the journal is internally consistent, because the bank column total must equal the sum of the current income and sundry column totals. Recording transactions in the correct columns, handling combined deposits, totalling and cross-casting, ruling off and posting are examinable, and the routine is the same in every cash journal you will meet up to Grade 12.

## Subtopic: The Entering Routine

Every receipt is entered with the same five-step routine.

Step one: identify the source document and enter its number in the document column: the receipt number, CRR for a cash register roll, or B/S for the bank statement.

Step two: enter the day of the month in the day column.

Step three: in the details column, enter who the money was received from: the customer's name, the owner, the tenant, the bank, or cash sales for the cash register roll.

Step four: enter the amount in the bank column, or, if several receipts were deposited together on the same day, enter each amount in the analysis of receipts column and the total deposited in the bank column on the last line of the group.

Step five: enter the amount again in the column that names the account to be credited: current income for fees earned from services; otherwise the sundry accounts amount column, with the account name in the sundry details column.

Two habits prevent most errors. First, ask before every entry: what kind of money is this? Earned from services goes to current income; anything else goes to sundry. Second, work strictly in date order, and enter bank statement items, such as EFTs and interest, on the date shown on the statement.

The repair business's April source documents are as follows. On the first, the cash register roll showed three thousand nine hundred and fifty rand. On the fourth, receipt 005 for one thousand two hundred and fifty rand was issued to a customer for a washing machine repair, and the cash register roll showed two thousand one hundred rand; both were deposited together. On the eighth, the owner contributed additional capital of ten thousand rand, receipt 006. On the twelfth, the cash register roll showed four thousand four hundred and eighty rand. On the eighteenth, the tenant paid rent of two thousand five hundred rand for the storeroom, receipt 007. On the twenty-second, the bank statement showed an EFT of one thousand seven hundred and fifty rand from a customer for a stove repair. On the twenty-ninth, the cash register roll showed five thousand one hundred and twenty rand. On the thirtieth, the bank statement showed interest earned of one hundred and eighty rand.

The questions for this section are with you now: the five steps of the routine, and the two habits that prevent errors.

## Subtopic: Entering the April Receipts

Apply the routine to each April document.

First of April: document CRR, day one, details cash sales, bank three thousand nine hundred and fifty, current income three thousand nine hundred and fifty.

Fourth of April, a combined deposit. Line one: document 005, day four, details the customer's name, analysis of receipts one thousand two hundred and fifty, current income one thousand two hundred and fifty. Line two: document CRR, details cash sales, analysis of receipts two thousand one hundred, bank three thousand three hundred and fifty, current income two thousand one hundred. The bank column shows the total deposited, one thousand two hundred and fifty plus two thousand one hundred, which is three thousand three hundred and fifty, on the last line of the group.

Eighth of April: document 006, details the owner, bank ten thousand, sundry accounts ten thousand, sundry details capital.

Twelfth of April: document CRR, cash sales, bank and current income four thousand four hundred and eighty.

Eighteenth of April: document 007, details the tenant, bank two thousand five hundred, sundry two thousand five hundred, details rent income.

Twenty-second of April: document B/S, details the customer, bank one thousand seven hundred and fifty, current income one thousand seven hundred and fifty. This is current income even though it arrived by EFT, because it was earned from a repair.

Twenty-ninth of April: document CRR, cash sales, bank and current income five thousand one hundred and twenty.

Thirtieth of April: document B/S, details the bank, bank one hundred and eighty, sundry one hundred and eighty, details interest income.

Notice the judgement calls. The EFT from a customer is current income, not sundry, because the method of payment does not change the kind of money. Additional capital is sundry, because the owner did not pay for a service. Interest is sundry, because it is income of a different kind from services, with its own ledger account.

The questions for this section are with you now: entering each April receipt, the combined deposit on the fourth, and why the EFT from a customer is current income.

## Subtopic: Closing Off the CRJ

At the end of the month the CRJ is closed off. This means totalling each money column, checking the totals, and ruling off.

First, total the current income column: three thousand nine hundred and fifty plus one thousand two hundred and fifty plus two thousand one hundred plus four thousand four hundred and eighty plus one thousand seven hundred and fifty plus five thousand one hundred and twenty, which is eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty rand.

Second, total the sundry accounts column: ten thousand plus two thousand five hundred plus one hundred and eighty, which is twelve thousand six hundred and eighty rand.

Third, total the bank column: three thousand nine hundred and fifty plus three thousand three hundred and fifty plus ten thousand plus four thousand four hundred and eighty plus two thousand five hundred plus one thousand seven hundred and fifty plus five thousand one hundred and twenty plus one hundred and eighty, which is thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty rand.

Fourth, cross-cast. The bank total must equal the current income total plus the sundry total: eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty plus twelve thousand six hundred and eighty is thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty. It agrees, so the journal is internally consistent. If the analysis of receipts column is used, its total is also checked against the bank amounts on the days it was used, but it is not part of the cross-cast.

Fifth, rule off: a single line above the totals and a double line below them, across all the money columns, with the totals on one line next to the date, the thirtieth of April.

If the cross-cast does not agree, look for common causes: an amount entered in the bank column but not in current income or sundry, or the reverse; a combined deposit where the bank total was entered as well as each individual amount; an addition error; or a transposed figure, which creates a difference divisible by nine.

The questions for this section are with you now: totalling each column, cross-casting, ruling off, and the causes of a cross-cast that does not agree.

## Subtopic: Posting the CRJ to the General Ledger

After closing off, the CRJ totals are posted to the general ledger, completing the double entry.

The bank column total of thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty rand is posted to the debit side of the bank account, with the details total receipts, the date thirtieth April and the folio CRJ 4. That records the increase in the asset bank.

The current income total of eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty rand is posted to the credit side of the current income account, with details bank and folio CRJ 4.

Each sundry amount is posted individually to the credit side of its own account: capital ten thousand rand, rent income two thousand five hundred rand and interest income one hundred and eighty rand, each with details bank and folio CRJ 4, and the date of the transaction or the month-end date, according to the format being used.

After posting, the ledger folio numbers are written in the folio column of the CRJ, under the totals and in the sundry folio column, to show that posting is complete and where each amount went.

Check the double entry: debits posted total thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty rand; credits posted total eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty plus ten thousand plus two thousand five hundred plus one hundred and eighty, also thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty rand. Debits equal credits.

Posting connects the CRJ to the rest of the accounting cycle. In Term 3 you will post the CPJ in the same way, and in Term 4 you will balance the ledger accounts and draw up the trial balance.

The questions for this section are with you now: which side each total and sundry amount is posted to, the details and folio entries, and why debits posted equal credits posted.

## Subtopic: Practice with a Second Business

A second CRJ, for a dance studio owned by a sole trader, practises the whole routine at once. Its May source documents are these. On the second, the owner contributed capital of twenty thousand rand, receipt 001. On the fifth, a parent paid class fees of one thousand eight hundred rand, receipt 002, and the cash register roll showed nine hundred and fifty rand; both were deposited together. On the tenth, the bank statement showed a loan of fifteen thousand rand from a bank. On the sixteenth, the cash register roll showed two thousand six hundred rand. On the twenty-fifth, the bank statement showed an EFT of one thousand four hundred rand from a parent for class fees. On the thirty-first, the bank statement showed interest earned of forty-five rand.

Entries: the capital goes to bank and sundry, capital. On the fifth, the analysis of receipts column shows one thousand eight hundred and nine hundred and fifty on two lines, the bank column shows two thousand seven hundred and fifty once, and current income shows each amount. The loan goes to bank and sundry, loan, document B/S. The cash register roll on the sixteenth goes to bank and current income. The EFT for class fees goes to bank and current income, document B/S. The interest goes to bank and sundry, interest income.

Closing off: current income is one thousand eight hundred plus nine hundred and fifty plus two thousand six hundred plus one thousand four hundred, which is six thousand seven hundred and fifty rand. Sundry accounts are twenty thousand plus fifteen thousand plus forty-five, thirty-five thousand and forty-five rand. Bank is twenty thousand plus two thousand seven hundred and fifty plus fifteen thousand plus two thousand six hundred plus one thousand four hundred plus forty-five, forty-one thousand seven hundred and ninety-five rand. Cross-cast: six thousand seven hundred and fifty plus thirty-five thousand and forty-five equals forty-one thousand seven hundred and ninety-five. The journal agrees.

Notice how different the two businesses look. In the repair business's April, most money came from current income. In the dance studio's first month, most came from capital and a loan, which is normal for a new business. The CRJ shows the owner at a glance where the money came from, and a business that relies on sundry receipts month after month, rather than on current income, is not yet paying its own way.

The questions for this section are with you now: entering and closing a second CRJ, and what the balance between current income and sundry receipts tells the owner.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again through one month of a repair shop's money and one adding-up check, plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Filling In April, Day by Day

April at the repair shop, eight days with money coming in. Take each paper in date order and put the money in its two lanes.

Day one, the till roll: three thousand nine hundred and fifty. Bank lane and current income lane, because it was earned fixing things.

Day four, two papers banked together: a receipt for a washing machine, one thousand two hundred and fifty, and the till roll, two thousand one hundred. Each one gets its own line in the analysis lane and its own line in current income. The bank lane gets the total that went to the bank once: three thousand three hundred and fifty.

Day eight, the owner puts in ten thousand more. Bank lane, and sundry lane with the tag capital. The owner did not pay for a repair, so it is not current income.

Day twelve, the till: four thousand four hundred and eighty. Bank and current income.

Day eighteen, rent for the storeroom: two thousand five hundred. Bank, and sundry tagged rent income.

Day twenty-two, a customer paid one thousand seven hundred and fifty by EFT for a stove. It came through the bank statement, but it was still earned fixing a stove, so: bank and current income. How the money travelled does not change what kind of money it is.

Day twenty-nine, the till: five thousand one hundred and twenty. Bank and current income.

Day thirty, the bank paid a little interest: one hundred and eighty. Bank, and sundry tagged interest income.

Keep April in mind and try the questions for this part: which lane, and why the EFT is still current income.

## Subtopic: Does It Add Up?

Month-end. Time to add each lane and check.

Current income lane: three thousand nine hundred and fifty, one thousand two hundred and fifty, two thousand one hundred, four thousand four hundred and eighty, one thousand seven hundred and fifty, five thousand one hundred and twenty. Total: eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty.

Sundry lane: ten thousand, two thousand five hundred, one hundred and eighty. Total: twelve thousand six hundred and eighty.

Bank lane: total thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty.

Now the magic check. Every rand in the bank lane also ran in one of the other two lanes. So current income plus sundry must equal bank. Eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty plus twelve thousand six hundred and eighty: thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty. It matches. The journal agrees with itself.

If it did not match, you would hunt for a rand that ran in only one lane, or a deposit counted twice, or two digits swapped.

Then draw the lines: one line above the totals, which means add up, and two lines below, which means finished.

Last step: move the totals into the ledger folders. The bank total goes into the bank folder on the left. The current income total goes into the current income folder on the right. The three sundry amounts go one by one into capital, rent income and interest income, each on the right. Left total thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty, right total thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty. Balanced.

Keep the check in mind and try the questions for this part: add the lanes, check the match, and post the totals.
