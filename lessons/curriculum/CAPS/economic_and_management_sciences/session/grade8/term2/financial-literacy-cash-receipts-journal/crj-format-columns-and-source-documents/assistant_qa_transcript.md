### subtopic_1 — The Purpose of the Cash Receipts Journal
**Assistant:** Why does the CRJ need an analysis of receipts column if the bank column already shows the money?

**Tutor:** Because the bank column must match what the bank actually receives, and the bank receives one deposit, not several separate amounts. On the fifteenth of March the repair business took in three thousand six hundred rand on the till and nine hundred rand from a customer who was given receipt 003. The bookkeeper deposited both together, so the deposit slip shows four thousand five hundred rand, and that single amount is what will appear on the bank statement. If the CRJ showed three thousand six hundred and nine hundred in the bank column as two separate amounts, comparing the CRJ with the bank statement at month-end would be harder, because the statement has one line of four thousand five hundred rand. The analysis of receipts column solves this. It lists each individual receipt on its own line, so each source document is still visible and can be checked, and the bank column shows only the total deposited for that day, on the last line of the group. The current income or sundry columns still show each amount separately against its own document. The analysis column is a working column: its total is not posted to the ledger, because the bank column total already represents the same money. In some textbooks and exam formats, when only one receipt is deposited on a day, the amount is written straight into the bank column and the analysis column is left blank. Check the format a question uses and follow it.

### subtopic_2 — The Columns of the CRJ
**Assistant:** Why must every sundry amount have the account name written next to it?

**Tutor:** Because the sundry column collects amounts belonging to many different accounts, and without the account name the bookkeeper would not know where to post each one. The current income column needs no names: everything in it goes to the current income account, so its total can be posted in one entry. The sundry column might contain capital of sixty thousand rand, a loan of twenty-five thousand rand, rent income of two thousand five hundred rand and interest income of one hundred and fifty rand in the same month. Each of those must be credited to a different ledger account. If the bookkeeper wrote only the amounts, then at month-end there would be four numbers and no way of telling, without going back to the source documents, which belonged where. Worse, someone might post the whole sundry total of eighty-seven thousand six hundred and fifty rand to one account, which would make the ledger badly wrong. So the sundry section has its own details sub-column for the account name and its own folio sub-column, where the ledger folio number is written when each amount is posted, showing that it has been posted and where. Note the difference between the two details columns in a CRJ. The main details column, near the left, names the person or business the money came from. The sundry details column names the ledger account to be credited. For a loan, the main details might name the bank, while the sundry details says loan.

### subtopic_3 — Source Documents for the CRJ
**Assistant:** Why is the bank statement used as a source document instead of waiting for a receipt?

**Tutor:** Because for some receipts no receipt is ever issued, and the bank statement is the first and only evidence that the money arrived. When a customer pays in cash at the counter, the business issues a receipt or rings up the till, so it creates its own evidence. But more and more money now arrives by electronic funds transfer directly into the bank account. The customer sends it from their own banking app; the business may get an SMS notification but issues no receipt book entry. Similarly, when the bank grants a loan and pays it into the business account, or credits interest earned, the business issues nothing. In each case the bank's record, the bank statement, is the evidence. That is why the bookkeeper compares the bank statement with the CRJ when the statement arrives, usually at month-end, and enters any amounts not yet recorded, with B/S in the document column. The comparison also works as a control in the other direction. Every deposit the business made from receipts and the cash register roll should appear on the statement on the same day or the next. If a deposit recorded in the CRJ is missing from the statement, either the money was not banked, which may mean theft, or the bank is still processing it. Today many businesses can download their statements daily or link their accounting software to the bank, so EFTs can be recorded quickly instead of waiting for month-end.

### subtopic_4 — Analysing a Receipt into Columns
**Assistant:** Could a business add more analysis columns to its CRJ?

**Tutor:** Yes, and it should, if a type of receipt happens often enough. The CAPS format for a Grade 8 service business uses one analysis column, current income, because for most service businesses fee income is by far the most frequent receipt. But the rule is practical, not fixed: a business designs its journal to suit its own transactions. If the repair business let out several storerooms and received rent every week, it could add a rent income column, so that rent amounts are totalled and posted once instead of being posted individually from the sundry column. A business with two very different services, such as repairs and installations, might want separate columns for each type of income, so that the owner can see how much each part of the business earns. A trading business, which you will meet in Grade 9 and 10, adds columns for sales and cost of sales and for receipts from debtors. More columns make posting quicker and give more detailed information, but they make the journal wider and harder to manage, so businesses use analysis columns only for the accounts that appear most often and leave the rest to the sundry column. In an exam, use exactly the columns given in the question. If a question includes an extra column, such as rent income, then rent must be entered there, not in sundry, and its total is posted in one entry to the rent income account.
