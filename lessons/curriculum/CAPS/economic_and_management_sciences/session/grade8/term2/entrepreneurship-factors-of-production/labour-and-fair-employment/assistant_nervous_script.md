Hey, if labour and employment law felt like a lot of rules, here is the calm version: one restaurant.

Labour is the work people do for wages or salaries.
Unskilled: little training, like the dishwasher.
Semi-skilled: some training, like the waiters.
Skilled: long training or a qualification, like the chef and the bookkeeper.

Workers have duties: be on time, work honestly, follow safety rules, treat customers well. And they have rights.

The main rules: at most forty-five ordinary hours a week; overtime at one and a half times the normal rate; at least the national minimum wage of twenty-eight rand seventy-nine an hour; paid annual, sick and maternity leave; a payslip; no unfair discrimination; a fair hearing before dismissal; a safe workplace; and UIF contributions of one percent each from worker and employer.

Overtime example: forty rand an hour times one and a half is sixty rand per extra hour.

Fair treatment is also good business: happy workers stay and work harder.
