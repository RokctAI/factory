Today we walk through a busy restaurant on a Friday night and meet labour, the human factor of production. The dishwasher is unskilled, the waiters are semi-skilled, and the chef and bookkeeper are skilled, and the difference shows up in their training and their pay. We look at the roles and responsibilities of workers, productivity and trade unions, then read the poster that the law requires on the wall: the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, the national minimum wage of twenty-eight rand seventy-nine cents an hour, the Labour Relations Act and the CCMA, employment equity, health and safety and the UIF. We calculate weekly pay, overtime at one and a half times and net pay after UIF, and see why fair employment is also good business.
