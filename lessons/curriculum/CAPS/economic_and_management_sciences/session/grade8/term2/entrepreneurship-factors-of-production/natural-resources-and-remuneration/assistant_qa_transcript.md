### subtopic_1 — Natural Resources
**Assistant:** If land cannot be made by people, why does the price of land change?

**Tutor:** Because the supply of land is fixed, its price is driven almost entirely by changes in demand and in how useful a particular piece of land is. The total area of South Africa cannot grow, and the amount of land in a particular place, such as a city centre or a fertile valley, is limited. When more people and businesses want to use land in a place, the rent and the selling price rise, because they compete for something that cannot be increased. That is why a small plot in a busy part of Johannesburg or Cape Town can cost far more than a large farm in the Karoo. The usefulness of land also changes. When government builds a new road, a railway station or a water pipeline, nearby land becomes more valuable because it is easier to use. When a mine closes or a river is polluted, land in the area can lose value. Farmland rises in value when crop prices are high and falls in a long drought. Zoning rules matter too: land that a municipality allows to be used for shops or housing is often worth more than land that can only be farmed. Economists call the payment for land rent, whether it is actually paid to a landlord or not. Because land is fixed, rising rents mostly reward landowners, which is one reason land ownership and land reform are such important questions for equality in South Africa, where land was taken from the majority under colonial and apartheid laws.

### subtopic_2 — Using Natural Resources Sustainably
**Assistant:** Why does the entrepreneur get paid last instead of first?

**Tutor:** Because that is the bargain the entrepreneur makes in exchange for keeping all the reward. The other factors of production agree fixed or contracted payments in advance. The neighbour leases his field for a set rent, the workers agree a wage, and the bank lends at a set interest rate. They provide their resources on the condition that they are paid whatever happens to the harvest. The entrepreneur is the one who says: I will guarantee all of you your payment, and I will keep whatever is left. That makes the entrepreneur the residual claimant. In a good year, the residual is large: the farmer earns one hundred and ninety thousand rand after everyone else has been paid. In a bad year, the residual can be negative: in the drought example, income of two hundred and eighty thousand rand does not cover three hundred and ten thousand rand of costs, and the farmer loses thirty thousand rand. Being paid last is therefore not a punishment; it is the source of both the risk and the opportunity. The arrangement is also fair to the other factors, because workers and lenders usually cannot afford to bear large risks; they need predictable income. It also gives the entrepreneur the strongest reason to manage the business well, because every saving and every extra sale increases his profit directly. In Grade 10 you will see that in a company, the shareholders play this role: they receive dividends from what remains after all other claims are paid.

### subtopic_3 — Remuneration of the Factors of Production
**Assistant:** Why does South Africa have so much mineral wealth but so much poverty?

**Tutor:** Because natural resources create wealth only when the income from them is widely shared and used to build other parts of the economy, and for much of South Africa's history neither happened. The discovery of diamonds at Kimberley in 1867 and gold on the Witwatersrand in 1886 transformed the economy. Mining created cities, railways and banks, and made enormous fortunes. But the system was built on cheap migrant black labour, controlled by laws that restricted where black South Africans could live, work and own land, while the profits went mainly to mine owners and shareholders, many of them abroad, and to a small white population. The majority received low wages, were denied quality education and skilled jobs, and were dispossessed of land. That pattern of unequal ownership of capital and land, and unequal skills, did not end in 1994 and still shapes incomes today. Economists also describe a resource curse in some countries: dependence on mining can make the economy vulnerable to swings in world mineral prices, discourage the growth of manufacturing and other industries, and create opportunities for corruption over mining rights. South Africa has tried to respond through mining charters that require broader ownership and community benefits, royalties paid to the state, and policies to process more minerals locally instead of exporting raw ore. The lesson for this topic is that the factor of production itself, the minerals, is only one ingredient; who owns it, who works it and how the remuneration is shared decide whether it reduces poverty.

### subtopic_4 — Remuneration in the Circular Flow
**Assistant:** Is interest unfair, since the lender does no work?

**Tutor:** Interest is a payment for a real contribution, even though it is not work: the use of money that the lender could have used in another way, and the risk that it will not be repaid. When a saver deposits money in a bank, she gives up the chance to spend it now. The bank lends that money to the farmer, who uses it to buy a tractor that makes his farm far more productive. Interest compensates the saver, through the bank, for waiting and for the risk that the farmer might not repay. Without interest, few people would save, and businesses would struggle to find capital to invest. Interest also allocates capital: businesses with productive plans are willing to pay interest because the capital will earn more than it costs. That said, interest can be unfair when it is excessive or exploitative. Unregistered moneylenders who charge extremely high rates to desperate borrowers, or lenders who give loans that people clearly cannot afford, cause serious harm. That is why the National Credit Act limits interest rates and fees and prohibits reckless lending. Some religious and ethical traditions, such as Islamic finance, avoid charging fixed interest and instead share profits and losses between the lender and the business, which shows that there are different ways of rewarding capital. In economics, the key idea is that capital is a factor of production that must be supplied by someone, and interest is the price that persuades them to supply it.
