Today we follow one month at an appliance repair business down the road every rand must travel. The owner deposits capital, borrows from a bank, fixes fridges and washing machines, sublets a storeroom, buys equipment and pays wages, and each event becomes a source document, then a line in the cash receipts or cash payments journal, then a total posted to the general ledger. We set out the six steps of the accounting cycle, trace each March transaction to its document and journal, and total the journal columns with a cross-check. Then we open the general ledger, with its balance sheet and nominal sections and folio numbers, post the totals using the double-entry principle, and balance the bank account at the end of the month.
