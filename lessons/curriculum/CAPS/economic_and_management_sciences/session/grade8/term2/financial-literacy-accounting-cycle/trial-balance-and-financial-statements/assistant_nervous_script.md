Hey, if the trial balance and financial statements felt like three big tables, here is the calm version: a checklist, a report card and a photograph.

The trial balance is the checklist: every ledger balance on one page, debits on the left, credits on the right. Assets, expenses and drawings on the left; capital, liabilities and income on the right. If both totals match, the adding is right. That does not prove there are no errors.

The income statement is the report card for a period: income minus expenses equals profit. In March, eighteen thousand five hundred and fifty minus eight thousand three hundred and seventy gives ten thousand one hundred and eighty rand.

The balance sheet is a photograph on one date: what the business owns on one side, owner's equity plus liabilities on the other. Owner's equity is capital plus profit minus drawings.

The profit is the bridge from the report card to the photograph.
