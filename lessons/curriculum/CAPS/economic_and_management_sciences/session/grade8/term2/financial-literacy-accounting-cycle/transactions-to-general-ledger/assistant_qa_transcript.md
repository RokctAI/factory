### subtopic_1 — The Accounting Cycle
**Assistant:** Why is the journal called the book of first entry and the ledger the book of final entry?

**Tutor:** Because of where each one sits on the road from transaction to financial statements. The journal is the first place a transaction is written in the accounting records, straight from the source document, in date order. That is why it is called the book of first entry, or the book of original entry. It captures every transaction individually, with the document number, the date, the name of the other party and the amount, so it is the detailed, chronological record. The ledger is where those transactions finally come to rest, grouped by account. Nothing is recorded directly in the ledger from a source document; everything arrives there through posting from a journal. That is why it is called the book of final entry. Once amounts are in the ledger, they are balanced and used to prepare the trial balance and financial statements, but they are not copied into any further book. The two books do different jobs. The journal answers questions about time: what happened on a given day, and in what order. The ledger answers questions about items: how much is in the bank, what the total wages were, how much is owed on the loan. Accounting software keeps the same idea even if you never see a separate book: you capture a transaction once, and the software both stores it in the journal and posts it to the ledger accounts automatically. The folio references link the two, so you can always trace a ledger amount back to its journal and document.

### subtopic_2 — From Source Document to Journal
**Assistant:** Why are analysis columns posted as totals but sundry amounts posted one by one?

**Tutor:** Because every amount in an analysis column belongs to the same ledger account, while each sundry amount belongs to a different account. In the repair shop's CRJ, the current income column contains five amounts during March: four thousand two hundred, one thousand eight hundred, three thousand six hundred, nine hundred and five thousand four hundred rand. All of them belong in the current income account. Posting them one by one would put five entries in that account and take five times as long, with five chances to make an error. Posting the total of fifteen thousand nine hundred rand gives exactly the same balance with one entry. The sundry accounts column is different. Its four amounts belong to four different accounts: capital, loan, rent income and interest income. Adding them up gives eighty-seven thousand six hundred and fifty rand, but there is no ledger account called sundry, so that total cannot be posted anywhere. Each sundry amount must be posted separately to its own account, which is why the sundry column has its own details and folio columns showing the account name and where it was posted. The sundry total is still useful, though: it is needed for the cross-check that the bank column total equals the sum of the analysis and sundry column totals. That cross-casting check confirms the journal was added correctly before anything is posted. Businesses decide which items deserve their own analysis column based on how often they occur.

### subtopic_3 — The General Ledger
**Assistant:** Why are income and expense accounts called nominal accounts?

**Tutor:** Nominal means in name only, and the word reflects the fact that these accounts are temporary. Income and expense accounts exist to collect the details of how the business earned and spent during one financial period, so that the owner can see where profit came from. But at the end of the year, the balances of all the income and expense accounts are transferred, or closed off, to work out the net profit or loss, and that profit is then added to owner's equity. After closing, the nominal accounts start the new year at zero, ready to collect the new year's income and expenses. In that sense, they never hold a lasting balance of their own; they are a name under which a period's activity is gathered. Balance sheet accounts are different. Assets, liabilities and capital carry their balances forward from one year to the next: the bank balance at the end of February is the opening balance on the first of March, and a loan stays on the books until it is repaid. That is why they are sometimes called permanent or real accounts. The division in the ledger into the balance sheet accounts section and the nominal accounts section matches the two financial statements: balance sheet accounts feed the balance sheet, and nominal accounts feed the income statement. Drawings is a balance sheet account, because it reduces owner's equity, even though it is closed off to capital at the year-end. You will perform the closing transfers in Grade 9 and 10.

### subtopic_4 — Posting from the Journals to the Ledger
**Assistant:** What does the contra account in the ledger tell you?

**Tutor:** The contra account tells you where the other half of the double entry was recorded, so that every ledger entry points to its partner. Every transaction has a debit and a credit of equal amounts in two different accounts. When you post the CRJ current income total of fifteen thousand nine hundred rand to the credit side of the current income account, you write bank in the details column, because the matching debit is in the bank account. When you post the CRJ bank total to the debit side of the bank account, you write total receipts, because the matching credits are spread over current income and several sundry accounts. When you post wages of six thousand rand to the debit side of the wages account, you write bank, because the matching credit is in the bank account. This gives three benefits. First, it makes the ledger readable: anyone looking at the wages account can see that wages were paid from the bank, not owed to the workers. Second, it creates an audit trail: together with the folio reference, the contra account allows an auditor or the owner to follow any amount from the ledger back to the journal and the source document, or forward to the financial statements. Third, it helps find errors: if an account has an entry whose contra account has no matching entry, the double entry is incomplete. In Grade 10, when more journals and accounts are used, reading contra accounts becomes an important skill for interpreting the ledger and answering analysis questions in exams.
