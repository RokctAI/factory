Today we finish the accounting cycle for one month at an appliance repair business. With every ledger account posted and balanced, we list the balances on one page, the trial balance, and check that one hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty rand of debits matches the same total of credits. From it we build the income statement, which shows a net profit of ten thousand one hundred and eighty rand for March, and the balance sheet, which shows ninety-two thousand one hundred and eighty rand of assets financed by owner's equity and a loan. We see why one statement covers a period and the other a single date, how net profit bridges the two, who reads the statements and why, and the common errors examiners hide in them.
