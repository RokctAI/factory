Today we meet two learners born on the same day, one in a suburb with a fee-paying school and one in a village that depends on a child support grant, and ask what the national budget can do about the gap between them. We define economic growth and GDP, see why the difference between one and three percent growth decides whether living standards double in a generation, and list the five ways the budget promotes growth, from infrastructure to tax incentives. Then we measure inequality with the Gini coefficient, separate inequality from poverty, and see how progressive taxes, grants and the social wage redress it. We finish with the tension between growing the pie and sharing it, and why a job is the most lasting redistribution of all.
