Hey, if development, pollution and unemployment felt like three separate worries, here is the calm version: one coal town.

The mine and power station brought jobs and electricity. That is development. But they also brought dirty air, acid water and scarred land. That is the environment paying the bill.

When the mine closes, workers lose jobs because their skills do not match the new jobs. That is structural unemployment. The unemployment rate is the unemployed divided by the labour force, times one hundred.

The fix is not to stop developing. It is sustainable development: meet today's needs without stealing from tomorrow.

That means using resources productively. Save water and electricity, fix leaks, and climb the waste ladder: reduce, reuse, recycle, and only then throw away.

And it creates green jobs: recycling, solar and wind energy, clearing alien trees. A cleaner environment and more jobs can go together.

Government sets the rules, like the carbon tax, businesses produce more with less, and households use water and power carefully.
