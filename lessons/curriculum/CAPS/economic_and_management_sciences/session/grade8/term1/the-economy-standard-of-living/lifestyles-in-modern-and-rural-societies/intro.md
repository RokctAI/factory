Today we compare two fourteen-year-old cousins, one in a city flat with a hot shower, a bus to school and home internet, and one in a village who fetches water, feeds the chickens and climbs a hill for a cellphone signal. We define standard of living as material well-being, measured by income and access to goods and services, and separate it from the broader quality of life, which includes health, safety, community and a clean environment. We list the indicators economists use, from income per person to the Human Development Index, then compare modern urban and rural lifestyles under headings an exam expects. We finish with urbanisation, its push and pull factors, remittances, and what the movement to the cities means for both places.
