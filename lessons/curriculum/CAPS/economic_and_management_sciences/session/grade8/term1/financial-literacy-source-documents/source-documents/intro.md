Today we empty the shoebox on a plumber's passenger seat and sort one day's paper trail. A cash invoice for a geyser repair, a receipt for a customer who paid in notes, the till roll from the workshop counter, a deposit slip from the bank, a proof of payment for pipes paid by EFT, and at month-end a bank statement that lists everything, including bank charges nobody handed over on paper. These are the source documents CAPS asks for, the first written evidence of every transaction. We learn what each one contains, who keeps the original and who keeps the duplicate, why SARS requires five years of records, what makes a valid tax invoice, and which cash journal each document belongs in.
