### subtopic_1 — Why Businesses Need Source Documents
**Assistant:** Why does the business record from the duplicate when it issued the document?

**Tutor:** Because the original has left the business, and the duplicate is the business's own copy of exactly the same information. When the plumber writes out a receipt for a customer who pays in notes, the customer needs proof that they paid, so they receive the original. The plumber still needs a record of the money received, so the receipt book is designed with a carbon or carbonless copy underneath, or the computer system saves a copy, which shows the same number, date, amount and details. The bookkeeper records the cash receipt in the cash receipts journal from that duplicate, and files it in number order. This system has an important control built in. Because receipts are pre-numbered, the duplicates in the book must run in an unbroken sequence. If receipt number 47 is followed by number 49, someone must explain what happened to 48. Perhaps it was spoiled and should have been kept marked cancelled; perhaps money was received and not recorded, which could mean theft. That is why spoiled receipts are never torn out and thrown away. The same applies to invoices the business issues. When the business receives a document from someone else, such as a supplier's cash invoice, it is the receiving party, so it keeps the original, and the supplier keeps the duplicate. A useful summary: issuers keep duplicates, receivers keep originals, and each records from the copy it holds.

### subtopic_2 — Receipts and Cash Register Slips
**Assistant:** Why should a business not trust a customer's proof of payment message?

**Tutor:** Because a proof of payment is created by the payer's bank or app, it can be faked or the payment can be cancelled, and only the business's own bank account shows whether the money really arrived. A common fraud in South Africa works like this. A buyer collects goods or a service, then shows or forwards a screenshot or email that looks exactly like a bank's proof of payment. Some are edited images; some come from fake websites; some are genuine instructions that were reversed after the goods were collected, or that will only clear days later. If the seller releases goods on the strength of the message, the money may never arrive. The safe rule is to check the business's own banking app or statement and confirm that the amount is available before releasing goods or treating the account as paid. Banks offer immediate payment services, such as instant EFT and PayShap, which reduce the waiting time, but the check should still be made on the receiving account. From the accounting point of view, this is why the bank statement or the business's own EFT notification is the correct source document for recording money received by EFT in the cash receipts journal, not the customer's proof of payment. The customer's document is their proof that they sent the payment; the business's bank record is its proof that it received the payment. This connects to internal control: good records protect the business against both errors and deliberate deception.

### subtopic_3 — Deposit Slips, EFTs and Bank Statements
**Assistant:** Why are bank charges recorded from the bank statement instead of an invoice?

**Tutor:** Because the bank takes the charges directly from the account and the business does not receive a separate document at the time, so the bank statement is the first place the transaction is evidenced. Banks charge a monthly fee, transaction fees for payments and deposits, cash handling fees and card machine fees. Instead of sending an invoice for each one and waiting for payment, the bank simply debits the account and lists the charges on the statement. The same is true for some other transactions: interest earned on a positive balance, interest charged on an overdraft, debit orders taken by suppliers such as insurers or telephone companies, and direct deposits or EFTs from customers that the business did not know about in advance. When the bookkeeper receives the bank statement at month-end, they compare it line by line with the cash journals. Any amount on the statement that is not yet in the journals is entered from the bank statement: bank charges and debit orders in the cash payments journal, interest earned and direct deposits in the cash receipts journal. Only then are the journals closed off and posted. This process is the start of the bank reconciliation you will do in later grades. It also catches errors: if the bank has charged a fee in error, or a debit order has been taken that the business never authorised, the comparison reveals it and the business can query it with the bank. Remember that the statement shows the bank's view, so a charge appears as a debit on the statement but is a payment, a credit to bank, in the business's books.

### subtopic_4 — Cash Invoices and Tax Invoices
**Assistant:** What happens if a business loses its source documents?

**Tutor:** It faces problems with its own records, with SARS and with anyone who questions its transactions. First, the bookkeeper may be unable to record transactions accurately, because the details of dates, amounts and parties are missing, so the journals, ledger and financial statements may be incomplete or wrong. Second, the business cannot prove its claims. If a customer disputes whether they paid, or a supplier claims an invoice is unpaid, the business has no evidence. Third, there are tax consequences. The Tax Administration Act requires records to be kept for five years from the date of submission of the tax return. If SARS audits the business and the documents are missing, it can disallow expenses that cannot be supported, which increases the taxable profit and the tax payable, and it can impose penalties and interest. A VAT vendor without valid tax invoices cannot claim input tax, so it pays more VAT. Fourth, it weakens internal control, because missing documents make it impossible to check whether all money received was banked or whether payments were authorised. Businesses therefore protect their documents: they file them in numerical order, store them safely away from damp and fire, and increasingly scan and back them up digitally. Accounting software that captures documents electronically makes this easier, but backups must be made and passwords protected. For a small business owner, keeping a simple, orderly filing system from the first day is one of the most valuable habits there is.
