Hey, if source documents felt like a pile of paper with confusing names, here is the calm version: a plumber's shoebox.

A receipt is written when money comes in. The payer gets the top copy; the business keeps the bottom copy.

A till slip comes from the cash register. The register keeps a roll of every sale, and the day's total goes into the books.

A deposit slip proves money was paid into the bank.

An EFT proof of payment shows money sent electronically.

A bank statement comes from the bank every month and lists everything, including bank charges and debit orders.

A cash invoice is a bill paid on the spot. The buyer gets the original; the seller keeps the copy.

Money in goes into the cash receipts journal. Money out goes into the cash payments journal.

And keep it all for five years, because SARS can ask.
