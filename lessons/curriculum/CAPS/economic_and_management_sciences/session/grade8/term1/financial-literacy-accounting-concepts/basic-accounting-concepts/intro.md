Today we stand on one busy corner and build the whole vocabulary of accounting from a single car wash. Its owner is a sole trader who put fifty thousand rand of her savings into the business as capital, bought a high-pressure washer as an asset, and borrowed money that became a liability. Every car washed brings in income; wages, water and soap are expenses; and what is left over is profit, which grows her owner's equity unless she takes it home as drawings. We keep the business separate from the owner, work out profit and owner's equity with real figures, and learn that debit and credit simply mean left and right, with the DEAD rule to remember which side goes up. We finish by classifying every item on the corner.
