Today we open a driving school and watch a scale that never tips. Its owner deposits eighty thousand rand, borrows forty thousand rand from a bank and buys a dual-control car for one hundred thousand rand, and after every step what the business owns still equals the owner's share plus what it owes to others. That is the accounting equation, assets equal owner's equity plus liabilities, the rule underneath all of accounting. We learn why it always balances, rearrange it to find missing figures, and sort every transaction into one of five patterns. Then we fill in a full analysis table for the school's first month, link profit and drawings to owner's equity, and name the three errors examiners see most often.
