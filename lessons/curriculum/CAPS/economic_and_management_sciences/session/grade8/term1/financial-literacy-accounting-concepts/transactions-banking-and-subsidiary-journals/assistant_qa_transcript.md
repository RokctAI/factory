### subtopic_1 — Transactions
**Assistant:** Why does accounting call a card payment cash when no notes or coins change hands?

**Tutor:** Because in accounting the word cash describes the timing of payment, not its physical form. A cash transaction is one where the payment happens at the same time as the transaction. When a customer taps her card for braids, the money is transferred from her bank account to the salon's bank account straight away, so the salon has been paid in full at the moment the service is delivered. That is economically the same as being paid in notes, and it is recorded in the same way: in the cash receipts journal, increasing the bank and current income. The opposite is a credit transaction, where the service is delivered now but payment is made later, for example a company that has its staff's hair done and is invoiced at the end of the month. That creates a debtor, an amount owed to the business, and in Grade 9 it is recorded in the debtors journal. The distinction matters because the two types of transaction affect the business differently. A cash transaction increases the bank immediately. A credit transaction increases income immediately but the bank only later, when the debtor pays, so a business with many credit sales can be profitable on paper and still struggle to pay its wages if debtors pay late. Note also that in Grade 8 the bank account is used for all cash receipts and payments, because businesses are expected to deposit their takings daily. The word cash in cash receipts journal therefore really means bank.

### subtopic_2 — The Bank and Banking
**Assistant:** Why did South African banks stop using cheques?

**Tutor:** Because cheques had become slow, expensive and risky compared with electronic payments, and very few people still used them. A cheque is a written instruction to a bank to pay a stated amount from the writer's account to the person named. It had to be physically delivered, deposited, sent through a clearing process between banks and checked, which took days. During that time the person receiving it could not be sure the money was really there; if the writer's account was empty, the cheque would bounce. Cheques were also easy targets for fraud: thieves altered amounts or names, or forged signatures. Meanwhile electronic funds transfers became faster, cheaper and available on cellphones, so the number of cheques written fell sharply. The Payments Association of South Africa, with the Reserve Bank and the banks, decided to phase cheques out, and by 2021 banks no longer issued or accepted them. In accounting, this changed the source documents used. Where a business once recorded payments from cheque counterfoils, the stubs left in the cheque book, it now records them from EFT payment confirmations, debit orders on the bank statement and card slips. Many textbooks and past exam papers were written before the change, so you will still see columns headed cheque number in cash payments journals and questions about counterfoils. Read them as the document number of the payment. The principles of recording are the same; only the document changed.

### subtopic_3 — Cash Receipts and Cash Payments
**Assistant:** Why is a loan received a cash receipt but not income?

**Tutor:** Because income must be earned, and a loan is borrowed money that must be repaid. When the salon receives a loan of twenty thousand rand from a bank to buy new chairs, money comes into the bank account, so it is a cash receipt and it is recorded in the cash receipts journal. But the salon has not earned that money by doing hair. It has taken on a debt, a liability, which it must pay back with interest. If the loan were recorded as income, profit would look twenty thousand rand higher than it really is, and the owner might think the salon had a wonderful month when in fact it had simply borrowed. So in the CRJ the loan is entered in the sundry accounts column, not the current income column, with the account name loan. Its effect on the accounting equation is: assets, bank, increase by twenty thousand; liabilities, loan, increase by twenty thousand; owner's equity is unchanged. The same reasoning applies to capital contributed by the owner: it is a cash receipt, but it increases owner's equity through capital, not through income. And on the payments side, repaying part of the loan is a cash payment but not an expense; it reduces the liability. Only the interest on the loan is an expense. Learning to separate cash movements from income and expenses is one of the most important skills in accounting, and it will appear in almost every journal and equation question you answer.

### subtopic_4 — Subsidiary Journals
**Assistant:** Why not record every transaction straight into the ledger and skip the journals?

**Tutor:** Because journals make the recording faster, more organised and easier to check, especially as a business grows. Think about the salon's current income. In a busy month it might receive four hundred separate payments for haircuts, braids and treatments. If each one were posted directly to the ledger, the current income account and the bank account would each need four hundred entries, and finding a mistake would mean searching through all of them. With a cash receipts journal, each payment is entered once, in date order, in a column for current income. At month-end the column is totalled, and only that one total is posted to the current income account in the ledger. The bank account receives one total too. Journals also keep a full chronological record of what happened each day, with the document number, so anyone checking can match each entry to its source document. That is an important control against fraud and error: if receipt number 104 is missing from the journal, someone must explain why. In larger businesses, journals allow work to be divided, with one clerk on receipts and another on payments. And because each journal holds one type of transaction, a manager can quickly see, for example, all payments made this month. Modern accounting software still uses the same structure behind the screen: transactions are captured from documents into journals and then posted to the ledger automatically. Learning it by hand helps you understand what the software is doing.
