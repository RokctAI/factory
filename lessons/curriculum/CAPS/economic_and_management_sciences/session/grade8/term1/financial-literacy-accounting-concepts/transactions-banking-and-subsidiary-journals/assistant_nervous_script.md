Hey, if transactions and journals sounded technical, here is the calm version: two boxes on a counter.

A transaction is anything that changes the business's money and can be counted in rand: a haircut paid for, rent paid, a loan received.

Money coming in is a cash receipt. Money going out is a cash payment. In accounting, cash means paid now, whether by notes, card or EFT. Paid later is credit.

Every transaction needs a document as proof: a receipt, a till slip, an EFT confirmation or the bank statement. No document, no entry.

The bank keeps the money safe, lets the business pay by EFT and debit order, and sends a statement every month. Cheques are no longer used in South Africa.

Money-in documents go into the cash receipts journal, the CRJ. Money-out documents go into the cash payments journal, the CPJ. These are the books of first entry.

Keep money safe too: deposit every day, pay by EFT and always keep the proof.
