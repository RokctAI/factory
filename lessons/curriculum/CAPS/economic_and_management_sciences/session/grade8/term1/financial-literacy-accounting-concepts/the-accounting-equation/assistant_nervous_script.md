Hey, if the accounting equation looked like algebra you did not sign up for, here is the calm version: a kitchen scale.

Left pan: everything the business owns, its assets, like money in the bank and the car.
Right pan: where it all came from, the owner's share, called owner's equity, and what is owed to others, called liabilities.

The scale is always level. Assets equal owner's equity plus liabilities.

When the owner puts money in, both sides go up. When the business borrows, both sides go up.
When it earns income, the bank and the owner's share go up. When it pays expenses or the owner takes drawings, both go down.
When it buys a car with cash, only the left side changes: bank down, car up. Still level.

To find a missing number, rearrange: owner's equity is assets minus liabilities.

If something you own is bought with a loan, both sides go up; if it is bought with cash, the left side just swaps.

Open the practice set and try a few transactions on the scale. You've got this!
