Hey, if accounting words felt like a new language, here is the calm version: one car wash.

The owner is a sole trader. The money she puts in is capital.

Assets are what the business owns: the washer, the vacuum cleaners, money in the bank. Liabilities are what it owes to others, like a bank loan.

Income is what it earns, the fees for washing cars. Expenses are what it uses up to earn that income: wages, water, soap.

Income minus expenses is profit. If expenses are bigger, it is a loss.

Drawings are what the owner takes home for private use. They are not an expense, because they do not help the business earn anything; they just reduce her share.

Owner's equity is the owner's share of the business: capital plus profit minus drawings. In the car wash, fifty thousand plus twenty-two thousand minus six thousand is sixty-six thousand rand.

Debit means left, credit means right. Remember DEAD: debits increase expenses, assets and drawings. Capital, liabilities and income increase on the credit side.
