### subtopic_1 — What Government Is
**Assistant:** If businesses can supply almost anything people pay for, why can a business not supply street lights?

**Tutor:** Because a street light is a classic public good, and public goods break the normal way a business earns money. A business sells something to a customer, the customer pays, and anyone who does not pay is left out. That works for bread, airtime and taxi rides. A street light does not work like that. Once it is switched on, it lights the road for every person who walks or drives past, whether they paid or not. There is no gate and no ticket, and it would cost far more than the light itself to try to charge each passer-by. Economists say the good is non-excludable, meaning you cannot keep non-payers out, and non-rival, meaning one more person using the light does not reduce the light for anyone else. A business that put up street lights would find that almost everybody waits for someone else to pay. This is called the free-rider problem. So the light would simply not be supplied, even though everyone wants it. Government solves the problem by paying for street lights from taxes and rates that everyone must pay. The same reasoning explains why government provides national defence, the police service, the courts and most public roads. It is also part of why, in Grade 10 Economics, you will study market failure: cases where the market on its own produces too little of something society needs.

### subtopic_2 — The Three Spheres of Government
**Assistant:** Why does the Constitution call them spheres of government instead of levels of government?

**Tutor:** The choice of word was deliberate. The word levels suggests a ladder, with national government at the top giving orders and local government at the bottom simply obeying. The writers of the 1996 Constitution wanted something different. Section 40 says the national, provincial and local spheres are distinctive, interdependent and interrelated. Distinctive means each sphere has its own powers written into the Constitution, which another sphere cannot simply take away. A municipality has its own elected council and its own power to charge rates and pass by-laws; a province has its own legislature and Premier. Interdependent means they rely on each other: a municipality cannot build a clinic without provincial health budgets, and the national government cannot deliver water to every house without the municipalities. Interrelated means they must cooperate, and Chapter 3 of the Constitution sets out principles of cooperative government that require the spheres to support each other, share information and avoid legal battles with each other where they can. In practice, national government still makes the major laws and divides most of the tax money, so there is a real sense in which it carries the most weight, and that is why most textbooks and exam papers keep using the word levels. In your answers you may use either word. For a high mark, you could add that the Constitution prefers spheres because the three are meant to work as partners.

### subtopic_3 — Who Does What
**Assistant:** Who pays for the services a municipality provides, such as water and refuse removal?

**Tutor:** Mostly the residents and businesses of that municipality, with help from national government. A municipality has three main sources of money. First, service charges: every household with a metered connection receives a monthly account for the water and electricity it uses, and a charge for refuse removal and sewerage. Second, property rates: the owner of every property pays a yearly amount, usually billed monthly, based on the municipal valuation of the property, and this money pays for things that cannot be charged per use, such as street lights, parks and roads. Third, transfers from national government: the Constitution says each sphere is entitled to an equitable share of the revenue raised nationally, so every year the national budget sends a share of tax money to municipalities. Poorer, mainly rural municipalities depend heavily on these transfers because they collect very few rates. Government also funds free basic services for poor households, often six thousand litres of water a month and a small amount of free electricity. This connects to two later sessions: the national budget, where we see how the equitable share is divided, and the role of government towards households, where the municipality acts as a producer of services. One practical point: when residents do not pay their accounts, the municipality has less money to maintain pipes and roads, so service delivery and payment depend on each other.

### subtopic_4 — Government as a Participant in the Economy
**Assistant:** Why is it a problem if a cabinet minister is also a judge?

**Tutor:** Because the separation of powers exists to stop any one person or group from controlling the whole state. Each arm of government checks the others. Parliament makes laws, but the courts can declare a law invalid if it goes against the Constitution. The executive runs the government, but Parliament can question ministers, refuse to pass their budgets and, in extreme cases, remove the President through a motion of no confidence. The courts decide disputes, including disputes between citizens and the government itself. Now imagine a minister who is also the judge. If a citizen sues that minister's department for failing to deliver textbooks, the minister would be deciding a case against himself. Nobody could trust the outcome. The same problem appears in the soccer example from the lesson: a player who is also the referee will never award a penalty against his own team. That is why the Constitution keeps the arms apart and protects the independence of the courts. South Africa has had real cases where this mattered: the Constitutional Court has ruled against the government on matters such as the provision of medicines and the use of public money, and the government had to comply. For an exam answer, name the three arms, state the function of each and then explain that the separation prevents abuse of power and protects the rights of citizens.
