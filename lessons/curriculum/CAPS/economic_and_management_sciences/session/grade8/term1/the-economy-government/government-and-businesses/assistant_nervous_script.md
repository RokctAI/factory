Hey, if government and businesses felt like a tangle of tenders and tax words, here is the calm version: three words, customer, producer and marshal.

Customer: government buys from businesses. Textbooks, medicines, police cars, new classrooms. Because it is taxpayers' money, it must use a fair tender process where businesses bid and the best offer wins.

Producer: government supplies what every business needs. Roads, ports, electricity, water, registering companies, courts that enforce contracts, and educated, healthy workers.

Marshal: government sets the rules, like a taxi rank marshal. Consumer protection, fair wages, no price-fixing, clean environment.

And businesses pay back: company tax of twenty-seven percent on profit, VAT of fifteen percent which they collect and pay over, and the PAYE they deduct from workers' wages.

Government also helps small businesses with preference points in tenders, training and finance, and it must pay suppliers within thirty days.

One quick VAT trick: a business pays SARS the VAT it charged minus the VAT it paid.
