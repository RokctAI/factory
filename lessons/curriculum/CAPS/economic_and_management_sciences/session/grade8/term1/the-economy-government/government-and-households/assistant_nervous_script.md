Hey, if the flows between government and households felt like a tangle of arrows, here is the calm version: two hats and one gift.

Hat one, the customer hat. When government employs a nurse, a teacher or a police officer, it buys their labour. The household sells, the salary is the price.

Hat two, the shop hat. When government runs schools, clinics, libraries and the water network, it produces services for households, often free or cheap at the counter.

The gift is the grant. Nothing is bought or sold, money just moves from people who have more to people who have less. That is called a transfer.

Households give back too: they pay PAYE and VAT to SARS, pay their municipal accounts, save water and vote.

All of this together, services plus grants, is called the social wage, and it is government's biggest tool against poverty.

Remember the arrows: labour goes one way and wages come back; services go one way and taxes come back.
