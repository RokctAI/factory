Today we walk down one ordinary South African street and count three governments on it. The police officer on the corner is paid by the national government, the clinic and the school are run by the province, and the water in the taps and the refuse truck on Tuesday belong to the municipality. We define government as the people and institutions with the authority to make, carry out and judge the laws of the country, and we see why public goods like street lights and defence need a government at all. Then we name the three spheres, their leaders and the nine provinces, and we finish by matching everyday services to the sphere that is responsible for them, ready for the roles government plays towards households and businesses.
