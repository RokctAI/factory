# Part 1 — Expert

This session carries the appliance repair business's April cash journals into the general ledger. You closed off the April cash receipts journal in Term 2 and the April cash payments journal in Term 3. Now every total and every sundry amount is posted to the correct side of the correct ledger account, the accounts are balanced, and the balances are carried down to the first of May. The ledger already contains the balances at the end of March, brought down to the first of April, so you will see how an account that started with seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty rand in the bank ends April with eighty thousand one hundred and eighty-five rand. The governing idea is that posting completes the double entry begun in the journals: the bank column totals go to the bank account, the analysis column totals go to their own accounts on the opposite side, and each sundry amount is posted individually; that every posting records the date, the contra account and the journal folio; and that balancing an account shows how much is in it at the end of the month. Opening accounts, posting the CRJ and CPJ, completing the details and folio columns, balancing accounts and checking that debits posted equal credits posted are examinable, and they lead directly to the trial balance.

## Subtopic: Opening Ledger Accounts

Before any April entries can be posted, each ledger account must exist with its correct starting point.

An account is opened by drawing the T-account, writing the account name across the top, and giving it a folio number: B for balance sheet accounts and N for nominal accounts. At the repair business, the balance sheet accounts are capital B1, drawings B2, equipment B3, loan B4 and bank B5. The nominal accounts are current income N1, rent income N2, interest income N3, wages N4, stationery N5, water and electricity N6, bank charges N7, and three new accounts opened in April for items not used in March: repair parts N8, rent expense N9 and insurance N10.

The repair business's financial year began on the first of March, so every account that was used in March has a balance at the end of March. That balance is brought down, abbreviated b/d, to the first of April, on the side where the account normally has its balance.

Debit balances brought down on the first of April: bank seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty rand, equipment eighteen thousand, drawings three thousand, wages six thousand, stationery seven hundred and fifty, water and electricity one thousand four hundred, and bank charges two hundred and twenty. Credit balances brought down: capital sixty thousand, loan twenty-five thousand, current income fifteen thousand nine hundred, rent income two thousand five hundred and interest income one hundred and fifty.

Each opening entry shows the date, the first of April, the details balance, the folio b/d, and the amount. For example, in the bank account, on the debit side: April, day one, balance, b/d, seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty.

The new accounts, repair parts, rent expense and insurance, have no opening balance. They are opened with only a name and folio number, ready to receive their first posting.

## Subtopic: Posting the Cash Receipts Journal

The April CRJ was closed off with a bank total of thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty rand, a current income total of eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty rand, and sundry amounts of capital ten thousand, rent income two thousand five hundred and interest income one hundred and eighty.

The bank total is posted to the debit side of the bank account, because receipts increase the asset bank. Date: the thirtieth of April. Details: total receipts, because the amount is a total of many receipts credited to several accounts. Folio: CRJ 4. Amount: thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty.

The current income total is posted to the credit side of the current income account, because income increases owner's equity. Date: the thirtieth. Details: bank, the contra account. Folio: CRJ 4. Amount: eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty.

Each sundry amount is posted individually to the credit side of its own account, with details bank and folio CRJ 4. Capital is credited with ten thousand rand, dated the eighth. Rent income is credited with two thousand five hundred rand, dated the eighteenth. Interest income is credited with one hundred and eighty rand, dated the thirtieth. Here the sundry amounts carry the date of each transaction; some textbooks date every posting at month-end, and either convention is acceptable if it is used consistently.

After posting, the ledger folio of each account is written in the folio column of the CRJ: under the bank and current income totals, and next to each sundry amount. This shows that the posting has been done and where to find it.

Check the double entry: debit posted, thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty; credits posted, eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty plus ten thousand plus two thousand five hundred plus one hundred and eighty, which is also thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty.

The questions for this section are with you now: posting the CRJ bank total, analysis total and sundry amounts, with the correct side, details and folio.

## Subtopic: Posting the Cash Payments Journal

The April CPJ was closed off with a bank total of twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five rand, a wages total of six thousand rand, a repair parts total of four thousand two hundred rand, and sundry amounts totalling fifteen thousand one hundred and twenty-five rand.

The bank total is posted to the credit side of the bank account, because payments decrease the asset bank. Date: the thirtieth. Details: total payments. Folio: CPJ 4. Amount: twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five.

The analysis totals are posted to the debit side of their accounts, because expenses are debited. Wages is debited with six thousand rand and repair parts with four thousand two hundred rand, each with details bank and folio CPJ 4.

Each sundry amount is posted individually to the debit side of its own account, with details bank and folio CPJ 4: rent expense four thousand five hundred on the fifth; stationery six hundred and eighty on the tenth; drawings two thousand five hundred on the twenty-fourth; water and electricity one thousand three hundred and eighty on the twenty-fifth; equipment five thousand two hundred on the twenty-eighth; and bank charges two hundred and forty-five and insurance six hundred and twenty on the thirtieth.

Notice that the sundry postings go to three kinds of account: expense accounts, such as rent expense and insurance; the asset account equipment; and the drawings account. All of them are debited, because all of them increased.

Check the double entry: credit posted to bank, twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five; debits posted, six thousand plus four thousand two hundred plus fifteen thousand one hundred and twenty-five, which is also twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five.

The bank account now holds the whole month in three lines: the opening balance and total receipts on the debit side, and total payments on the credit side.

## Subtopic: Balancing Ledger Accounts

Balancing an account means finding the difference between its two sides, so that the account shows a single balance at the end of the month.

The method has five steps, shown with the bank account. Step one: add the bigger side. The debit side has seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty plus thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty, which is one hundred and five thousand five hundred and ten rand. Step two: add the smaller side. The credit side has twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five. Step three: calculate the difference, one hundred and five thousand five hundred and ten minus twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five, which is eighty thousand one hundred and eighty-five rand. Step four: write the difference on the smaller side, the credit side, dated the thirtieth, with the details balance and folio c/d, carried down, so that both sides now total one hundred and five thousand five hundred and ten. Write the totals on the same line on both sides, with a single line above and a double line below. Step five: bring the balance down on the opposite side, the debit side, dated the first of May, with details balance and folio b/d.

The bank account has a debit balance of eighty thousand one hundred and eighty-five rand, which is a favourable balance: the business has that much money in the bank.

If payments had been greater than the opening balance plus receipts, the bank account would have a credit balance. That is an unfavourable balance, or overdraft: the business owes the bank money, so the bank account behaves like a liability. The balancing method is the same; only the side of the balance changes.

Accounts with entries on one side only do not need the full balancing procedure; their balance is simply the total of that side. Capital, for example, has sixty thousand and ten thousand on the credit side, a credit balance of seventy thousand rand. Wages has six thousand and six thousand on the debit side, a debit balance of twelve thousand. Equipment has eighteen thousand and five thousand two hundred, a debit balance of twenty-three thousand two hundred. Drawings has three thousand and two thousand five hundred, five thousand five hundred. Current income has fifteen thousand nine hundred and eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty, a credit balance of thirty-four thousand five hundred and fifty. The loan account has no April entries, so its balance stays twenty-five thousand rand.

The questions for this section are with you now: the steps in balancing an account, c/d and b/d, and the balances of the April accounts.

## Subtopic: Checking the Posting and Reading the Ledger

After posting and balancing, the bookkeeper checks the work before preparing the trial balance.

First, check that every journal amount has a ledger folio next to it. A missing folio means a posting was missed.

Second, check that each journal's postings balance: one debit total equal to the credits for the CRJ, and one credit total equal to the debits for the CPJ, as calculated earlier.

Third, check that each account's balance is on the expected side. Assets, expenses and drawings should have debit balances; capital, liabilities and income should have credit balances. A wages account with a credit balance would signal a posting on the wrong side.

Fourth, check that the bank balance agrees with the bank statement at the end of the month, after allowing for any items not yet recorded.

The balanced ledger is a source of useful information. The owner can see that eighty thousand one hundred and eighty-five rand is in the bank; that the loan of twenty-five thousand rand is still owed; that equipment now cost twenty-three thousand two hundred rand in total; and that, since the start of the financial year on the first of March, the business has earned thirty-four thousand five hundred and fifty rand in current income and spent twelve thousand rand on wages. The nominal accounts show the year so far, not just April, because they accumulate until the end of the financial year.

The questions for this section are with you now: the checks after posting, and what the balanced ledger tells the owner.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Posting is filing. The journals are the month's diary; the ledger is the filing cabinet, with one folder per item.

## Subtopic: Filing the Folders

On the first of April, every folder already has something in it from March. The bank folder starts with seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty rand. The capital folder starts with sixty thousand. Those are the opening balances, brought down.

Three folders are brand new: repair parts, rent expense and insurance. They start empty.

Now file the money-in diary. The bank lane total, thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty, goes on the left of the bank folder: money in. The current income total, eighteen thousand six hundred and fifty, goes on the right of the current income folder. The sundry amounts each go to their own folders on the right: capital ten thousand, rent income two thousand five hundred, interest income one hundred and eighty.

Then file the money-out diary. The bank lane total, twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five, goes on the right of the bank folder: money out. Wages six thousand and repair parts four thousand two hundred go on the left of their folders. Each sundry amount goes on the left of its own folder.

In every folder, write the date, the name of the other folder, and where it came from: CRJ 4 or CPJ 4. That way you can always find your way back.

## Subtopic: What Is Left in Each Folder?

At month-end, look inside each folder and ask: how much is in here now?

The bank folder: on the left, seventy-four thousand one hundred and eighty plus thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty, one hundred and five thousand five hundred and ten. On the right, twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five. The difference is eighty thousand one hundred and eighty-five rand. Write it on the right as balance c/d so both sides match, draw the lines, and then bring it down on the left for the first of May.

Simpler folders just get added up. Capital: sixty thousand plus ten thousand, seventy thousand. Wages: six thousand plus six thousand, twelve thousand. Equipment: eighteen thousand plus five thousand two hundred, twenty-three thousand two hundred.

A quick test: the money-in diary put thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty on the left and thirty-one thousand three hundred and thirty on the right. The money-out diary put twenty-five thousand three hundred and twenty-five on each side too. Left equals right.

Now the cabinet is tidy, and every folder has one number on its front. Next, we list all those numbers on one page. That page is the trial balance.
