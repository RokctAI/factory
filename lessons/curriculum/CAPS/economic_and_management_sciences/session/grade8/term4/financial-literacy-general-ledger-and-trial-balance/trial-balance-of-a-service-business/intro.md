Today we finish the accounting cycle for the appliance repair business by drawing up its trial balance on the thirtieth of April. We list every ledger balance in folio order, balance sheet accounts first and nominal accounts second, placing assets, expenses and drawings in the debit column and capital, liabilities and income in the credit column. Both columns total one hundred and thirty-four thousand eight hundred and eighty rand. Then we look at the errors a trial balance can reveal and the ones it hides, and use the balances to calculate a profit of thirteen thousand eight hundred and eighty-five rand and check the accounting equation.
