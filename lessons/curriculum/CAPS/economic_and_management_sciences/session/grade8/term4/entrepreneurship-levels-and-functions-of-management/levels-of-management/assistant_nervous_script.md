Hey, if levels of management sounds like a lot of titles, here is the calm version.
Management means getting work done through other people, without wasting resources.
Picture a pyramid. Few people at the top, more in the middle, most at the bottom.
Top management, like the owner and general manager, makes big, long-term, strategic decisions for the whole business.
Middle management, the department managers, turns those plans into tactical plans for one department, usually for about a year.
Lower management, the supervisors, makes daily operational decisions on the shop floor.
Instructions travel down; reports and problems travel up.
Quick test: how long does the decision last, and how much of the business does it touch?
In the supermarket, two plus six plus ten makes eighteen managers leading forty-two workers.
Schools, municipalities and big companies have the same three levels, sometimes with extra layers.
