Hey, if good management sounds like a list to memorise, here is the calm version.
A good manager is honest, fair, clear, calm and helps people grow.
Three skills: technical, knowing the work; human, working with people; conceptual, seeing the big picture.
Supervisors need lots of technical skill. Top managers need lots of conceptual skill. Everyone needs human skill.
Three styles: autocratic tells, democratic asks, laissez-faire lets go. Pick the one that fits the moment.
Decisions: define the problem, gather facts, list options, choose, act, then check.
Ethics: do what is right, not just what is legal.
Results show good management. Team A lost one of eight, twelve and a half percent. Team B lost four of eight, fifty percent.
And remember, none of this is magic. Every one of these qualities and skills can be learned with practice, starting with the next group project you lead at school.
Open the practice set and try a few. You've got this!
