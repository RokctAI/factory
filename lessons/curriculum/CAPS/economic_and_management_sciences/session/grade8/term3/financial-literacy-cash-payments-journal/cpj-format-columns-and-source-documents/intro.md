Today the repair business's bookkeeper turns from money in to money out. On her desk is the April payment pile: EFT confirmations for spare parts, wages, rent and a new diagnostic tool, a till slip for stationery, and a bank statement showing bank charges and an insurance debit order. All of it belongs in the cash payments journal, the CPJ. We learn why the CPJ exists, what every column is for, from the document number and payee to the bank, wages, repair parts and sundry columns, and which source documents support each entry. Then we enter all of April's payments, avoid the drawings and equipment traps, and total and cross-check the journal.
