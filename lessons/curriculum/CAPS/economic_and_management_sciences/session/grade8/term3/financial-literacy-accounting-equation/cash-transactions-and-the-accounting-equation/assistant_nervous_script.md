Hey, if the accounting equation with payments feels like juggling, here is the calm version: a balance scale.
Assets on the left; owner's equity plus liabilities on the right. Always balanced.
Every payment makes bank go down. Then ask what else changed.
An expense or drawings: owner's equity goes down by the same amount.
Buying an asset like equipment: one asset up, bank down. A swap, no change to owner's equity.
Repaying a loan: bank down, loan down. No change to owner's equity.
Reasons matter: owner's equity down because of an expense, or because of drawings.
Watch the traps: drawings are not expenses, and a tool that lasts for years is an asset.
April: assets up eleven thousand two hundred and five rand, owner's equity up the same. Profit: three thousand seven hundred and five rand.
Open the practice set and put a few payments on the scale. You've got this!
