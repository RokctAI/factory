Today we bring the two cash journals together, the way a real bookkeeper meets them: a mixed pile of source documents for one month. A new garden service opens in June with the owner's capital, a lawnmower, a bank loan, customers paying by cash and EFT, wages, fuel, advertising, drawings, a loan repayment and the bank's charges and interest. We sort every document into the cash receipts journal or the cash payments journal, enter each one in the correct columns, close off both journals with totals and cross-casts, and calculate the bank balance of sixteen thousand two hundred and forty-five rand. Then we put the whole month on the accounting equation and see why a profit of only five hundred and forty-five rand sits behind a much bigger bank balance.
