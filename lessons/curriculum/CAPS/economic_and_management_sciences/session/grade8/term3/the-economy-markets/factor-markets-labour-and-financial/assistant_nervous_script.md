Hey, if markets sound like big economics, here is the calm version.
A market is any meeting of buyers and sellers, and it does not need a building.
Goods and services markets sell bread and haircuts. Factor markets sell labour, money, land and enterprise.
In the labour market, employers buy labour, workers sell it, and the price is the wage.
Employers want workers because customers want products. That is derived demand.
Trade unions, labour laws and the CCMA help keep the labour market fair.
In the financial market, savers lend and borrowers borrow, and the price is interest.
The money market is short-term, less than a year. The capital market is long-term, like an oven loan or shares on the JSE.
Banks are in the middle, paying savers less interest than they charge borrowers.
Remember the circle: the same households sell their work and savings, then spend their income on bread.
