Hey, if comparing four forms of ownership feels like a lot to remember, here is the calm version: one table.
Line them up: sole trader, partnership, private company, public company.
Owners: one; two or more; one or more shareholders; usually many shareholders.
Separate legal person: no, no, yes, yes.
Liability: unlimited, unlimited, limited, limited.
Continuity: no, no, yes, yes.
Capital: least for the sole trader, most for the public company, which can sell shares to the public.
As you move up, you gain protection, money and continuity, but lose simplicity and privacy and gain rules and costs.
To choose a form, name the business's needs and link each to a characteristic.
Advantages and disadvantages always come from these characteristics, so name the characteristic when you give one.
The same feature can help one business and hurt another: privacy suits a family firm, but banks may trust it less.
