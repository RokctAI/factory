Hey, if forms of ownership sound like legal language, here is the calm version: one baker, then two.
A sole trader is one owner. Easy to start, makes every decision, keeps all the profit.
A partnership is two or more owners who share the capital, the work and the profit.
Both have unlimited liability: if the business cannot pay its debts, the owners' own possessions can be taken.
Neither is a separate person in law, so neither has legal personality.
Neither has continuity: if the owner dies, or a partner leaves, the business or partnership ends.
Partners should sign a written partnership agreement: who puts in what, how profit is shared, and what happens if someone leaves.
Profit can be shared in a ratio: one hundred and sixty thousand rand shared three to five gives sixty thousand and one hundred thousand.
