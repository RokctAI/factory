Today we fit credit transactions into the accounting cycle and the accounting equation. The cycle runs from source documents, through the journals, including the new debtors journal, to the general ledger and a new debtors ledger with a page for each customer, then to the trial balance and, in Grade 10, the financial statements. A credit sale has two moments. When the goods go, debtors increase, and sales and cost of sales affect owner's equity exactly as in a cash sale, so the profit is earned on that day. When the debtor pays, bank goes up and debtors go down, with no effect on owner's equity. We analyse Ridgeview Hardware's April transactions and prove that the equation balances.
