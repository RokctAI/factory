Hey, a credit sale is just a cash sale where the money arrives later. Here is the calm version.

The cycle: source document, journal, ledger, trial balance, financial statements, then repeat. New this term: the debtors journal and the debtors ledger.

Moment one, the sale on credit: debtors up, owner's equity up for sales. Stock down, owner's equity down for cost of sales. Hilltop's R6 000 sale that cost R4 000 gives a profit of R2 000, made on the day of the sale.

Moment two, the debtor pays: bank up, debtors down. Owner's equity does not change. It is just a swap. Never count the sale twice.

Part payment: Ace owes 3 600, pays 2 000, still owes 1 600.

Cost price at 50 percent mark-up: divide by 150, times 100.

Cash or credit, the profit is the same; only bank versus debtors changes.

Open the practice set and split every sale into its two halves first. You've got this!
