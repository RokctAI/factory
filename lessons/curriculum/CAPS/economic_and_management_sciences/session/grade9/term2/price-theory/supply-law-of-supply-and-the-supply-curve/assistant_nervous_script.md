Hey, supply is just demand seen from the seller's side, and it goes the opposite way. Here is the calm version.

Supply means being willing and able to sell, at each price, over a period of time.

The law of supply: price up, quantity supplied up. Price down, quantity supplied down. Other things staying the same.

Why? More profit per unit, making more costs more, and new producers join when the price is high.

Supply schedule for muffins: R4, 30; R8, 80; R12, 120; R16, 160; R20, 200.

Supply curve: same axes as demand, price up the side. It climbs from bottom left to top right, like a ladder. Label it S.

Own price changes: move along the curve, a change in quantity supplied. Anything else changes: the whole curve shifts. Cheaper or easier to produce: shift right. More expensive or harder, like higher flour prices, drought or a tax: shift left.

Open the practice set and ask: own price, or something else? You've got this!
