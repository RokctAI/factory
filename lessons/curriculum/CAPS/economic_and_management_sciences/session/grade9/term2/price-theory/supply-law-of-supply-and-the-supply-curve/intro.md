Today we look at the other side of the market: supply, the quantity producers are willing and able to sell at each price. The law of supply says that when the price rises, producers offer more, and when it falls, they offer less, other things remaining the same, because of profit, rising costs and new producers entering. Using the bakers who supply a tuck shop with muffins, we build a supply schedule and draw a supply curve that climbs upward from left to right. Then we separate a movement along the curve, caused by the good's own price, from a shift of the whole curve, caused by costs, technology, weather, the number of producers or government policy. This completes the tools for equilibrium and Grade 10 Economics.
