Today we start price theory with demand: the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at each price. The law of demand says that when the price rises, people buy less, and when it falls, they buy more, other things remaining the same, and we see three reasons why. Using a school tuck shop's muffin sales, we build a demand schedule and draw a demand curve that slopes downward from left to right, with price on the vertical axis. Then we separate a slide along the curve, caused by the good's own price, from a shift of the whole curve, caused by income, related goods, tastes, population or expectations. These tools are central to Grade 10 Economics.
