Hey, three economic systems sounds like a lot, but it all comes down to one question: who decides? Here is the calm version.

The problem first. Wants are unlimited, resources are scarce, so every country must answer three questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and for whom.

Planned economy: the state owns the land, factories and mines, and the state decides. The idea came from Karl Marx in 1848, and the Soviet Union tried it from 1917. Examples today: North Korea and Cuba. Strength: equality. Weakness: shortages and little choice.

Market economy: private people own the resources, and buyers and sellers decide through prices. The idea is linked to Adam Smith in 1776 and his invisible hand. Strength: choice and efficiency. Weakness: the poor can be left behind.

Mixed economy: both. Private businesses do most of the producing, the state runs some enterprises like Eskom and Transnet, provides schools and clinics, and makes rules. South Africa is mixed, and mixed does not mean fifty-fifty.

Open the practice set and ask, for each example: who owns it and who decides? You've got this!
