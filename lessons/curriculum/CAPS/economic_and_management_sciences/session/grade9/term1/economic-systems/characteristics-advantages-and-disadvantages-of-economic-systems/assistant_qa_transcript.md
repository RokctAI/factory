### subtopic_1 — Characteristics of the Planned Economy
**Assistant:** If a planned economy keeps prices low and fixed, why is that a disadvantage and not just good for consumers?

**Tutor:** Because a low, fixed price stops doing the job that a price is supposed to do. In a market, a price is a signal. When a good becomes scarce, its price rises, and that rise does three useful things at once. It tells consumers to use less of the good or to switch to something else. It tells producers that the good is worth producing, so they make more. And it shares the available goods out among those who value them most. When the state fixes the price below the level that would balance demand and supply, none of this happens. More people want to buy the good than there are goods available, so there is a shortage. Because the price cannot ration the good, something else must, and that something is usually the queue: whoever arrives first or waits longest gets the bread. In the Soviet Union people would join a queue without knowing what was being sold, simply because a queue meant goods had arrived. Fixed prices can also hide the true cost of producing something, so resources are wasted, and they remove the incentive for producers to increase supply. So, for the consumer who gets the cheap loaf, a low price is good. For the economy as a whole, a price that cannot move means shortages, queues, wasted time and no signal to produce more. That is why, in an exam, you write: a planned economy suffers from shortages, because prices are fixed by the state and cannot rise to signal scarcity.

### subtopic_2 — Characteristics of the Market Economy
**Assistant:** Why would a pure market economy fail to provide street lighting, even if everyone wants it?

**Tutor:** Because street lighting is a public good, and public goods have two special features. The first is that they are non-excludable: once a street light is switched on, a business cannot stop anyone who did not pay from walking under it and enjoying the light. The second is that they are non-rival: one person using the light does not leave less light for anyone else. Now think like a private business in a pure market economy. A business produces in order to make a profit, which means it must be able to charge the people who benefit. With a street light, it cannot. If it asked every household on the street to pay, many would refuse, knowing that the light will shine on them anyway if their neighbours pay. Economists call this the free-rider problem. Because the business cannot collect enough revenue, it will not install the lights, even though the whole street would be better off with them. National defence, lighthouses, flood barriers and most roads have the same problem. That is why governments provide public goods and pay for them through taxes, which everyone must pay. This is one of the strongest arguments against a pure market economy and in favour of the mixed economy. In South Africa, municipalities provide street lighting and are funded partly by property rates, and the state provides national defence through the South African National Defence Force. Merit goods such as education and health care are different: a market would supply them, but too little, so the state provides them as well.

### subtopic_3 — Characteristics of the Mixed Economy
**Assistant:** How does a progressive tax help a mixed economy reduce inequality?

**Tutor:** A progressive tax is one in which the percentage of income paid in tax rises as income rises. South Africa's personal income tax works this way: income is divided into brackets, and the rate rises from 18 percent on the lowest taxable bracket up to 45 percent on the highest. Below a certain income threshold, no income tax is paid at all. This means that a person earning a very high salary pays not only more rand in tax, but a larger share of their income, than a person earning a modest salary. That is the first step, collecting a larger share from those who can afford it most. The second step is spending. The government uses the revenue to pay for things that benefit lower-income households most: social grants such as the child support grant and the old age grant, which reach millions of people each month, no-fee schools, free primary health care at clinics, and subsidised housing. The combined effect is to move resources from higher-income households towards lower-income households, which narrows the gap between rich and poor compared with what the market alone would produce. This is exactly what a mixed economy is designed to do: let the market produce efficiently, then use the state to correct the inequality that the market creates. The trade-off is that if taxes become very high, some people may work less, invest less or move their money elsewhere, so governments have to judge the balance carefully. South Africa remains one of the most unequal countries in the world, which shows how hard that balance is.

### subtopic_7 — Sorting Statements into Boxes
**Assistant:** What makes the difference between a one-mark answer and a full-mark answer on advantages and disadvantages?

**Tutor:** The reason. An examiner reading the word choice cannot tell whether you understand why the market economy offers choice, or whether you have simply memorised a list. A full-mark answer names the feature and then explains it with a cause or a consequence, usually joined by because, so or which means. Compare these. Weak: equality. Strong: in a planned economy incomes are more equal, because the state sets wages and distributes goods according to need. Weak: shortages. Strong: a planned economy often suffers shortages, because prices are fixed and cannot rise to signal that a good is scarce, so producers have no reason to make more. Weak: monopolies. Strong: in a market economy a firm may grow into a monopoly, which means it faces no competition and can raise prices and lower quality. A good way to practise is to use the comparison table. Take a row, for example who sets prices, and write one sentence for each system that explains what follows from it. Because the advantages of one system tend to be the disadvantages of another, one well-understood row gives you several answers. Make sure you also name the right system: a sentence about consumer sovereignty belongs to the market economy, and a sentence about central planning belongs to the planned economy. Finally, when a question asks you to evaluate or discuss, give both sides and then a conclusion, for example that South Africa's mixed economy aims to keep the efficiency of markets while using the state to reduce inequality.
