Hey, a lot of advantages and disadvantages in one lesson, but there is one pattern that holds it all together. Here is the calm version.

Planned: the state owns, the state decides, the state sets prices. Good: more equality, basic needs for all, low unemployment. Bad: shortages and queues, little choice, no profit motive so little innovation, a big slow bureaucracy.

Market: private owners, buyers and sellers decide, prices from demand and supply, profit motive, competition. Good: efficiency, innovation, lots of choice, economic freedom. Bad: big inequality, poor people left out, no public goods like street lights, monopolies, pollution, recessions.

Mixed: both. Good: choice and efficiency plus grants, schools and clinics. Bad: state firms can be inefficient, taxes and red tape can slow business.

The pattern: the market's strengths are the planned economy's weaknesses, and the other way round.

Exam habit: write a full sentence with a because in it, for example: a market economy offers choice, because firms compete for customers. Open the practice set and do one table row at a time. You've got this!
