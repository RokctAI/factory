Hey, the CPJ is the CRJ's twin, just for money going out. Here is the calm version.

Every payment: document number, day, who was paid, and the amount in the bank column.

Then one more column. Stock bought to sell goes in trading stock. Pay for employees goes in wages. Everything else goes in sundry with its name: rent expense, equipment, drawings, telephone, bank charges.

Two questions: will a customer buy it? Trading stock. Is it pay for an employee? Wages. Otherwise, sundry.

Watch the traps: shelves for the shop are equipment, not stock. Money the owner takes is drawings, never wages.

Documents: EFT proof of payment, and the bank statement for bank charges.

Cross-cast: trading stock plus wages plus sundry equals bank. For Ridgeview: 29 000 + 12 000 + 15 590 = 56 590.

Bank balance: receipts minus payments, 140 250 - 56 590 = 83 660.

Open the practice set and ask the two questions on every line. You've got this!
