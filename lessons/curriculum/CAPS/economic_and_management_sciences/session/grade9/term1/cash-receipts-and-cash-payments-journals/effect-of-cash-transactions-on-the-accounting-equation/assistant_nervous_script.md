Hey, the accounting equation is just one line, and once you trust it, it checks your work for you. Here is the calm version.

A = OE + L. Assets are what the business has. Owner's equity is what the owner put in, plus income, minus expenses and drawings. Liabilities are debts, like a bank loan.

For every transaction, find two things that change. Then plus or minus each one.

Owner puts in money: assets up, owner's equity up. Loan received: assets up, liabilities up. Buy equipment for cash: one asset up, another asset down. Repay loan: assets down, liabilities down.

Income: assets up, owner's equity up. Expense: assets down, owner's equity down. Drawings: assets down, owner's equity down, and the reason is drawings, never expense.

At the end: R62 500 of assets equals R47 500 of owner's equity plus R15 000 of liabilities.

If it does not balance, check row by row. Open the practice set and say the two accounts out loud for each one. You've got this!
