Today we sort every account of a trading business into its place in the general ledger. The ledger has two sections: balance sheet accounts, for assets, owner's equity and liabilities, numbered with a B, and nominal accounts, for income and expenses, numbered with an N. We classify assets as non-current or current, and see why trading stock is an asset, drawings is owner's equity and a loan is never income. Then we learn the debit and credit rules from the accounting equation: assets, expenses and drawings grow on the debit side; liabilities, capital and income grow on the credit side. We finish by building Ridgeview Hardware's list of fourteen accounts. This classification is the backbone of Grade 10 Accounting.
