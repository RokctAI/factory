Hey, debits and credits scare a lot of people, but they are just left and right. Here is the calm version.

Two sections in the ledger. Balance sheet accounts, folio B: assets, capital, drawings and liabilities. Nominal accounts, folio N: income and expenses.

Assets: non-current, kept longer than a year, like equipment; current, like trading stock and bank.

Two families. Debit family, grows on the left: assets, expenses and drawings. Remember them together, because they always behave the same way. Credit family, grows on the right: liabilities, capital and income.

To make an account smaller, write on the other side. Paying money out credits bank.

Every transaction: one debit, one credit, same amount.

Traps: trading stock is an asset, not an expense. Drawings is owner's equity with a debit balance. A loan is a liability, not income. Rent income and rent expense are two different accounts.

Open the practice set and sort each account into its drawer first, then its family. You've got this!
