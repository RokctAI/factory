# Part 1 — Expert

In the previous lesson we saw how credit purchases affect the accounting equation. In this lesson we record them in the books: the creditors journal, the creditors ledger and the general ledger. We continue with Ridgeview Hardware, a sole trader selling building supplies, and its credit purchases for September.

## Subtopic: The Creditors Journal and Its Columns

The creditors journal, abbreviated CJ, is the subsidiary journal in which a business records all its credit purchases. Unlike the debtors journal, which records only sales of trading stock, the CJ records anything bought on credit: trading stock, stationery, equipment, repairs, advertising and so on. Cash purchases do not go in the CJ; they go in the cash payments journal, because money leaves the bank at once.

The source document is the original invoice received from the supplier. Because the invoices come from many different suppliers, their numbers are not in sequence, so Ridgeview records the supplier's own invoice number. Many businesses also stamp each invoice with their own number when it arrives, so that they can check that every invoice received has been recorded.

The CJ has these columns. First, the document column, for the invoice number. Then the day. Then the creditor, the name of the supplier. Then the folio column, in which the creditors ledger folio is written when the line is posted. Then the money columns. Creditors control shows the total amount of each invoice, the amount owed to the supplier. Trading stock, Stationery and Equipment are analysis columns for the items bought most often. Sundry accounts is for anything else, with space for the amount, the folio and the name of the account.

Every invoice is entered twice on its line: once in Creditors control, for the full amount owed, and once in the analysis column that describes what was bought. That is why the CJ can be cross-cast: the total of the Creditors control column must equal the sum of the totals of the analysis columns.

The CJ has no cost of sales column. Cost of sales arises only when goods are sold, and the CJ records goods bought. When Ridgeview buys trading stock, the cost is added to the Trading stock account; it becomes cost of sales later, when the stock is sold and recorded in the CRJ or DJ.

Which items have their own column depends on the business. A hardware shop buys trading stock very often, stationery every month and equipment now and then, so these three have columns. A business that often buys packing material on credit might add a Packing material column. Anything without its own column goes into Sundry accounts with its account name.

## Subtopic: Recording Ridgeview's September Credit Purchases

Here are Ridgeview's credit purchases for September. On 3 September, trading stock from BuildMart Wholesalers, invoice BM331, R8 000. On 8 September, stationery from Office Hub, invoice OH77, R600. On 12 September, a tile cutter for the workshop from Toolquip, invoice TQ512, R3 500. On 17 September, timber as trading stock from Highveld Timber Suppliers, invoice HT908, R4 200. On 23 September, more trading stock from BuildMart Wholesalers, invoice BM356, R2 800. On 26 September, an advertising board from Glow Signs, invoice GS45, R900.

The heading reads: Creditors journal of Ridgeview Hardware, September 20.., page CJ9. Each invoice takes one line. On the first line: BM331, 3, BuildMart Wholesalers, Creditors control 8 000, Trading stock 8 000. On the second line: OH77, 8, Office Hub, Creditors control 600, Stationery 600. On the third: TQ512, 12, Toolquip, Creditors control 3 500, Equipment 3 500. On the fourth: HT908, 17, Highveld Timber Suppliers, Creditors control 4 200, Trading stock 4 200. On the fifth: BM356, 23, BuildMart Wholesalers, Creditors control 2 800, Trading stock 2 800. On the sixth: GS45, 26, Glow Signs, Creditors control 900, Sundry accounts 900, details Advertising.

Advertising is an expense, and Ridgeview does not buy it often enough to need a column, so it goes in Sundry accounts with its account name. Note that the advertising board is treated as advertising, an expense, because it is a promotional item used up quickly, not equipment.

Now total the columns. Creditors control: 8 000 + 600 + 3 500 + 4 200 + 2 800 + 900 = R20 000. Trading stock: 8 000 + 4 200 + 2 800 = R15 000. Stationery: R600. Equipment: R3 500. Sundry accounts: R900.

Cross-cast: 15 000 + 600 + 3 500 + 900 = 20 000, which agrees with the Creditors control total. The totals are double ruled.

A common error is to record the credit purchase of trading stock in the CPJ because it involves stock. Ask the money question: did money leave the bank today? No, so it belongs in the CJ. Another common error is to put the cost of stock purchased in a Cost of sales column; the CJ has none.

## Subtopic: The Creditors Ledger and Posting to It

The creditors ledger is a subsidiary ledger with a separate account for every creditor. It shows exactly how much Ridgeview owes each supplier, which the general ledger account Creditors control, a single total, cannot show. Its folios begin with C: BuildMart Wholesalers C1, Office Hub C2, Toolquip C3, Highveld Timber Suppliers C4 and Glow Signs C5.

Creditors ledger accounts are usually kept in the three-column format with a running balance, like the debtors ledger, but the sides are reversed, because creditors are liabilities. An invoice increases what Ridgeview owes, so it is posted to the credit side of the creditor's account. A payment decreases what Ridgeview owes, so it is posted to the debit side. The balance is a credit balance.

Each line of the CJ is posted individually, using the Creditors control amount, because that is what Ridgeview owes the supplier. For invoice BM331, we go to BuildMart Wholesalers, C1, and write: date 3 September, details Invoice BM331, folio CJ9, credit 8 000, balance 8 000. Then we write C1 in the folio column of the CJ next to that line. For invoice BM356 on 23 September, BuildMart's account shows a further credit of 2 800.

In September, Ridgeview also made one payment to a creditor: on 20 September it paid BuildMart R5 000 by EFT. That payment is recorded in the CPJ and posted to the debit side of BuildMart's account. So BuildMart's account shows: 3 September invoice BM331 credit 8 000, balance 8 000; 20 September EFT debit 5 000, balance 3 000; 23 September invoice BM356 credit 2 800, balance 5 800. Entries are in date order, mixing CJ and CPJ entries, so that the balance is correct on every date.

The other accounts each have one line: Office Hub credit 600, balance 600; Toolquip credit 3 500, balance 3 500; Highveld Timber credit 4 200, balance 4 200; Glow Signs credit 900, balance 900.

Notice that the creditors ledger shows the total amount of each invoice, regardless of what was bought. The supplier does not care whether Ridgeview recorded the purchase as stock, equipment or advertising; it cares only about the amount owed. The analysis columns go to the general ledger.

A creditors ledger account can be checked against the supplier's statement, which the supplier sends each month. If Ridgeview's account for BuildMart shows R5 800 and BuildMart's statement shows R5 800, the records agree.

## Subtopic: Posting to the General Ledger and the Creditors List

The general ledger receives the CJ totals at the end of the month. The rule is: the Creditors control total is posted to the credit side of Creditors control, and each analysis column total is posted to the debit side of its account.

Creditors control is credited with R20 000, because the liability increases. The details column reads Total purchases, because the contra entries are several different accounts, just as the Bank account shows Total receipts and Total payments for the cash journals. The folio is CJ9.

Trading stock is debited with R15 000, because the asset increases; details Creditors control, folio CJ9. Stationery is debited with R600, an expense increasing; details Creditors control. Equipment is debited with R3 500, an asset increasing; details Creditors control. The Sundry accounts amount is posted individually: Advertising is debited with R900, details Creditors control. The double entry is complete: total debits of 15 000 + 600 + 3 500 + 900 = 20 000 equal the credit of 20 000.

The payment to BuildMart is posted from the CPJ: its Creditors control column total, R5 000 for September, is posted to the debit side of Creditors control, with the details Bank. So Creditors control shows a credit of 20 000 and a debit of 5 000, and its balance is 20 000 - 5 000 = R15 000, a credit balance, because it is a liability.

Now prepare the creditors list at 30 September: BuildMart Wholesalers R5 800, Office Hub R600, Toolquip R3 500, Highveld Timber Suppliers R4 200 and Glow Signs R900. Total 5 800 + 600 + 3 500 + 4 200 + 900 = R15 000. This agrees with the balance of Creditors control, so the creditors records are in order.

If the totals disagree, the bookkeeper looks for the same kinds of error as with debtors: a line not posted, an amount copied incorrectly, or an entry on the wrong side. An invoice of R4 200 posted to the debit side of a creditor's account instead of the credit side makes the list wrong by double the amount, R8 400.

The creditors list is also a management tool. It shows Ridgeview how much it must pay, to whom and when. Combined with the debtors list, which shows how much is coming in, it helps the owner plan the bank balance for the coming month and pay every supplier on time.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Let's make this simple. Buying on credit needs three records, just like selling on credit: a list of everything bought on credit, a page for every supplier, and a total of everything owed.

## Subtopic: A Notebook for Everything Bought on Credit

The creditors journal is a notebook for everything Ridgeview buys on credit. Stock, stationery, equipment, advertising: if it was bought on credit, it goes in the CJ. Each invoice from a supplier takes one line.

On each line, write the full amount in the Creditors control column, because that is what Ridgeview owes. Then write the same amount again in the column that says what was bought: Trading stock, Stationery, Equipment, or Sundry accounts for anything else.

In September, Ridgeview bought R20 000 on credit: R15 000 of stock, R600 of stationery, R3 500 of equipment and R900 of advertising. Add the analysis columns: 20 000. It matches the Creditors control column.

## Subtopic: A Page for Every Supplier

The creditors ledger has a page for every supplier, so Ridgeview knows exactly whom it owes. This time the sides are flipped. A new invoice goes on the credit side, because Ridgeview owes more. A payment goes on the debit side, because it owes less.

BuildMart's page: owed 8 000, Ridgeview paid 5 000, so owed 3 000, then bought 2 800 more, so owed 5 800. Office Hub 600. Toolquip 3 500. Highveld Timber 4 200. Glow Signs 900.

The supplier's page only shows the amount owed, not what was bought.

## Subtopic: Do the Totals Agree?

In the general ledger, Creditors control gets the CJ total of R20 000 on the credit side and the payment of R5 000 on the debit side. Balance: R15 000.

Now add the supplier pages: 5 800 + 600 + 3 500 + 4 200 + 900 = 15 000. Same answer. The records are correct.

Remember the general ledger postings: Creditors control is credited with the total, and each item account is debited: Trading stock 15 000, Stationery 600, Equipment 3 500 and Advertising 900. Practise until you can record, post and check a creditors journal without your notes.
