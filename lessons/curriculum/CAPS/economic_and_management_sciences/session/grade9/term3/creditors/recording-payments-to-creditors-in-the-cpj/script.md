# Part 1 — Expert

In the previous lessons we recorded credit purchases in the creditors journal and posted them to the creditors ledger. Now Ridgeview Hardware must pay its suppliers. In this lesson we record payments to creditors in the cash payments journal, post them to the creditors ledger and the general ledger, and reconcile the creditors list with Creditors control at the end of October.

## Subtopic: Paying Creditors in the Cash Payments Journal

When Ridgeview pays a creditor, money leaves the bank, so the payment is recorded in the cash payments journal, the CPJ. It does not go in the creditors journal, which records only purchases on credit. This follows the rule you already know: did money go out of the bank today? Then it belongs in the CPJ.

The source document is the proof of the payment. Today most businesses pay suppliers by electronic funds transfer, EFT, and the source document is the EFT proof of payment, a printout or electronic record showing the date, the amount, the recipient and a reference number. Some businesses still pay by cheque, in which case the source document is the cheque counterfoil. Payments by debit order appear on the bank statement, which then serves as the source document.

To record payments to creditors, the CPJ has a special analysis column headed Creditors control. Its full set of columns for Ridgeview is now: document, day, name of payee, folio, Bank, Trading stock, Wages, Creditors control and Sundry accounts. The payee is the person or business receiving the money.

A payment to a creditor is entered in two money columns on the same line: the amount in the Bank column, because money left the bank, and the same amount in the Creditors control column, because it reduces what Ridgeview owes. Nothing is entered in the Trading stock column, even if the creditor supplied trading stock, because the stock was recorded when it was bought, in the CJ. Entering it again would count the purchase twice.

Distinguish clearly between two kinds of payment to suppliers. A cash purchase is a purchase paid for immediately: Ridgeview buys stock and pays at once. That goes in the CPJ in the Trading stock column. A payment to a creditor settles an earlier credit purchase: that goes in the CPJ in the Creditors control column. Both involve the bank and a supplier, but the second one does not involve new stock.

Before paying, Ridgeview checks the creditor's monthly statement against its own creditors ledger account, and decides how much to pay and when, based on the credit terms. Paying on time keeps the supplier's trust; paying too early may leave the business short of money for other needs.

## Subtopic: Recording Ridgeview's October Payments

On 1 October, Ridgeview's creditors list, carried forward from September, showed: BuildMart Wholesalers R5 800, Office Hub R600, Toolquip R3 500, Highveld Timber Suppliers R4 200 and Glow Signs R900, a total of R15 000. This was also the credit balance of Creditors control.

During October Ridgeview made these payments, all by EFT. On 2 October, EFT 220, Office Hub, R600, settling its account. On 5 October, EFT 221, BuildMart Wholesalers, R5 800, settling its account. On 10 October, EFT 222, wages, R2 800. On 14 October, EFT 223, Toolquip, R2 000 on account; the rest will be paid in November by agreement. On 18 October, EFT 224, Cash and Carry Wholesale, R3 300 for trading stock bought and paid for on the same day. On 22 October, EFT 225, Highveld Timber Suppliers, R4 200, settling its account. On 29 October, EFT 226, Glow Signs, R900, settling its account.

Sort them. Payments to creditors, in the Creditors control column: Office Hub 600, BuildMart 5 800, Toolquip 2 000, Highveld Timber 4 200 and Glow Signs 900. Wages, in the Wages column: 2 800. The Cash and Carry purchase is a cash purchase of stock, in the Trading stock column: 3 300.

The CPJ for October, page CPJ10, has seven lines in date order. Each line shows the EFT number, the day, the payee, the amount in Bank, and the same amount in the correct analysis column.

The totals are: Bank 600 + 5 800 + 2 800 + 2 000 + 3 300 + 4 200 + 900 = R19 600; Trading stock R3 300; Wages R2 800; Creditors control 600 + 5 800 + 2 000 + 4 200 + 900 = R13 500; Sundry accounts nil.

Cross-cast: 3 300 + 2 800 + 13 500 = 19 600, which agrees with the Bank total.

During October Ridgeview also bought on credit, recorded in the CJ for October, page CJ10: on 6 October, trading stock from BuildMart Wholesalers, invoice BM402, R6 500; and on 20 October, trading stock from Highveld Timber Suppliers, invoice HT955, R3 100. CJ total: 6 500 + 3 100 = R9 600, all in the Trading stock column.

Notice that Ridgeview paid BuildMart's September balance of R5 800 on 5 October and then bought R6 500 more on credit on 6 October. Paying promptly keeps the account open for new purchases.

## Subtopic: Posting Payments to the Creditors Ledger

Each payment to a creditor is posted individually from the CPJ to the debit side of the creditor's account in the creditors ledger, because it decreases what Ridgeview owes. The details show the EFT number, and the folio is CPJ10. After posting, the creditor's folio is written in the folio column of the CPJ.

Purchases from the CJ are posted to the credit side, as before, and all entries are made in date order, so that the running balance is correct on every date.

BuildMart Wholesalers, C1: 1 October balance 5 800; 5 October EFT 221 debit 5 800, balance 0; 6 October invoice BM402 credit 6 500, balance 6 500.

Office Hub, C2: 1 October balance 600; 2 October EFT 220 debit 600, balance 0.

Toolquip, C3: 1 October balance 3 500; 14 October EFT 223 debit 2 000, balance 1 500.

Highveld Timber Suppliers, C4: 1 October balance 4 200; 20 October invoice HT955 credit 3 100, balance 7 300; 22 October EFT 225 debit 4 200, balance 3 100.

Glow Signs, C5: 1 October balance 900; 29 October EFT 226 debit 900, balance 0.

The creditors list at 31 October is therefore: BuildMart R6 500, Office Hub nil, Toolquip R1 500, Highveld Timber R3 100 and Glow Signs nil. Total 6 500 + 1 500 + 3 100 = R11 100.

Notice that the payments of wages and of the Cash and Carry purchase are not posted to the creditors ledger at all. Wages were not owed to a creditor, and the Cash and Carry purchase was paid immediately, so Cash and Carry was never a creditor. Only amounts in the Creditors control column are posted to the creditors ledger.

At the end of each month, suppliers send statements. Toolquip's statement for October should show the opening balance of R3 500, the payment of R2 000 and a balance of R1 500. If Toolquip's statement still showed R3 500, perhaps because the EFT reached Toolquip's bank after its statement date, Ridgeview would contact Toolquip with the EFT proof of payment to resolve the difference. Comparing statements with the creditors ledger in this way is an important control.

## Subtopic: Posting to the General Ledger and Reconciling

At the end of October, the CPJ totals are posted to the general ledger. The Bank account is credited with R19 600, the total payments, with the details Total payments and the folio CPJ10. Each analysis column total is debited to its account, with the details Bank: Trading stock debit 3 300, Wages debit 2 800, and Creditors control debit 13 500.

The CJ total for October is posted as in the previous lesson: Creditors control credit 9 600, details Total purchases; Trading stock debit 9 600, details Creditors control.

Creditors control now shows: credit side, balance b/d 15 000 and total purchases 9 600; debit side, bank 13 500. The balance is 15 000 + 9 600 - 13 500 = R11 100, a credit balance carried down to November.

The creditors list also totals R11 100. The two agree, so the creditors records for October are in order.

Look at what the reconciliation tells the owner. Ridgeview paid R13 500 to creditors in October, more than the R9 600 it bought on credit, so its total debt to suppliers fell from R15 000 to R11 100. That is a sign of a business paying its suppliers on time. Toolquip agreed to a part payment, so R1 500 is still owed for the tile cutter, and BuildMart and Highveld are owed for October's purchases, which are not yet due.

Also notice the total stock bought in October: R3 300 for cash plus R9 600 on credit, which is R12 900. The cash purchase appears in the CPJ Trading stock column; the credit purchases in the CJ Trading stock column. Both are debited to Trading stock in the general ledger. The payments to creditors are not debited to Trading stock, because the stock was already recorded when it was bought on credit.

Common errors in tests are to record a payment to a creditor in the Trading stock column of the CPJ, which counts the stock twice; to record it in the CJ; to post it to the credit side of the creditor's account; and to post wages or cash purchases to the creditors ledger. If the creditors list does not agree with Creditors control, these are the first things to check.

The full creditors cycle is now complete: original invoice, CJ, credit the creditor; EFT proof of payment, CPJ, debit the creditor; at month end, creditors list equals Creditors control. Together with the debtors cycle, this gives Ridgeview complete control over the money it is owed and the money it owes.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Let's make this simple. Buying on credit is a promise. Paying the creditor keeps the promise. When money goes out of the bank to a supplier, it goes in the money-out book, and the supplier's page goes down.

## Subtopic: Keeping the Promise

Ridgeview bought on credit in September. In October it pays. Money goes out of the bank, so the payment goes in the CPJ, the money-out book.

On the line, write the amount twice: once in Bank and once in Creditors control. Do not write it under Trading stock. The stock was already written down when it was bought, in the creditors journal.

There are two kinds of stock payments, and they look alike. Paying for stock today, at the counter: Trading stock column. Paying a supplier for stock bought last month: Creditors control column.

In October, Ridgeview paid out R19 600 in total. R13 500 went to creditors, R2 800 to wages and R3 300 for stock bought for cash.

## Subtopic: The Supplier's Page Goes Down

On each supplier's page, a payment goes on the debit side, because Ridgeview owes less.

BuildMart: owed 5 800, paid 5 800, owed nothing, then bought 6 500 more. Now owed 6 500.

Toolquip: owed 3 500, paid 2 000. Now owed 1 500.

Highveld: owed 4 200, bought 3 100 more, then paid 4 200. Now owed 3 100.

Office Hub and Glow Signs: paid in full. Owed nothing.

Wages and the Cash and Carry stock never go on a supplier's page, because nobody was owed that money for later.

## Subtopic: Checking the Total

Creditors control started October at 15 000. New credit purchases added 9 600. Payments took away 13 500. 15 000 + 9 600 - 13 500 = 11 100.

Add the suppliers' pages: 6 500 + 1 500 + 3 100 = 11 100. Same answer.

And here is the good news: Ridgeview owes less at the end of October than at the start, because it paid more than it bought on credit. A business that pays its suppliers on time keeps their trust. Practise by recording the October payments yourself and checking the total at the end.
