### subtopic_1 — The Creditors Journal and Its Columns
**Assistant:** Why does the creditors journal record all credit purchases, while the debtors journal records only credit sales of stock?

**Tutor:** Because of the different things a business does with credit on each side. On the selling side, a trading business sells one thing on credit: its trading stock. So the debtors journal needs only a Sales column and a Cost of sales column. On the buying side, a business buys many different things on credit: trading stock for resale, stationery for the office, equipment for the workshop, repairs, advertising and services. It would be impractical to have a separate journal for each, so all credit purchases go in one journal, and the CJ uses analysis columns to show what was bought. The columns are chosen to suit the business. Ridgeview buys stock very often, stationery monthly and equipment now and then, so these three have columns; anything else, such as the advertising from Glow Signs, goes into Sundry accounts with its account name. The Creditors control column holds the full amount of each invoice, because whatever was bought, the business owes that amount to the supplier. This design allows the journal to be cross-cast, which catches many errors before posting.

### subtopic_2 — Recording Ridgeview's September Credit Purchases
**Assistant:** Why is the supplier's own invoice number recorded in the CJ, and how does a business know no invoice was missed?

**Tutor:** When Ridgeview sells, it numbers its own invoices in sequence, so a missing number in the DJ immediately shows that something is wrong. When Ridgeview buys, the invoices are written by many different suppliers, each with its own numbering, so the numbers in the CJ are in no particular order and a gap cannot be seen. Recording the supplier's own invoice number still matters, because it links the entry to the document, so that anyone checking the books, or the supplier querying an amount, can find the exact invoice. To make sure that no invoice is lost, many businesses stamp each incoming invoice with their own sequential number when it arrives and keep a register of invoices received. The monthly statement from each supplier is another important control. It lists every invoice the supplier has issued to Ridgeview. If the statement shows an invoice that does not appear in Ridgeview's creditors ledger, either the invoice was lost, the goods were never received, or the supplier made an error, and the matter is followed up before payment.

### subtopic_3 — The Creditors Ledger and Posting to It
**Assistant:** Why is the advertising board recorded as advertising and not as equipment?

**Tutor:** Because of how it is used and how long it lasts. Equipment is something the business keeps and uses for several years to carry on its operations, such as the tile cutter in the workshop. It is a non-current asset. The Glow Signs purchase is a promotional item, an advertising board for the shop, which in this example is treated as a cost of promoting the business, used up over a short period like a printed flyer or a poster. Advertising is an expense, so it reduces owner's equity, and since Ridgeview has no Advertising column, the amount goes into Sundry accounts with the account name Advertising. In real businesses, a large, permanent sign might well be treated as an asset, so the decision depends on the facts given. In a test, the question will make the nature of the item clear, and you should follow it. The general principle is the same one used in the accounting equation: does the business still own something of lasting value after the purchase? If yes, it is an asset; if it is used up quickly, it is an expense.

### subtopic_4 — Posting to the General Ledger and the Creditors List
**Assistant:** Why are the sides of the creditors ledger the reverse of the debtors ledger?

**Tutor:** Because creditors and debtors are on opposite sides of the accounting equation. Debtors are assets, and assets increase on the debit side and decrease on the credit side. So in the debtors ledger, an invoice to a customer is a debit, increasing what the customer owes, and a receipt from the customer is a credit. Creditors are liabilities, and liabilities increase on the credit side and decrease on the debit side. So in the creditors ledger, an invoice from a supplier is a credit, increasing what Ridgeview owes, and a payment to the supplier is a debit. The balance of a debtor's account is normally a debit balance, and the balance of a creditor's account is normally a credit balance. If a creditor's account ever showed a debit balance, it would mean Ridgeview had paid more than it owed, which is unusual and should be checked. A simple way to remember this is to start from the equation: assets on the left are debits, owner's equity and liabilities on the right are credits, and every account grows on its own side.

### subtopic_5 — A Notebook for Everything Bought on Credit
**Assistant:** Why does Creditors control show the details Total purchases?

**Tutor:** Because the details column of a ledger account normally names the contra account, the other account in the double entry, but the CJ total is matched by several different accounts. When the R20 000 total of the CJ is posted to the credit side of Creditors control, the corresponding debits go to Trading stock for R15 000, Stationery for R600, Equipment for R3 500 and Advertising for R900. Writing all four names in one details column would be clumsy, so the entry simply reads Total purchases, in the same way that the Bank account reads Total receipts for the CRJ total and Total payments for the CPJ total. In each of the debit accounts, the details column does name the contra account: Creditors control. The folio column in all of them shows CJ9, so anyone can trace the entries back to the journal. The double entry is complete because the total of the debits, 15 000 plus 600 plus 3 500 plus 900, which is 20 000, equals the credit of 20 000. This is exactly what the cross-cast proved before posting.

### subtopic_6 — A Page for Every Supplier
**Assistant:** Why is the payment to BuildMart part of this lesson about the creditors journal?

**Tutor:** Because the creditors ledger and Creditors control can only be completed if both kinds of creditor transactions are included. Credit purchases, from the CJ, increase what Ridgeview owes. Payments, from the CPJ, decrease it. If we posted only the CJ, BuildMart's account would show R10 800, which is wrong, because R5 000 has already been paid. In a real business, the bookkeeper posts both journals in date order, so that each creditor's running balance is always correct. BuildMart's account therefore reads: 3 September invoice BM331, credit 8 000, balance 8 000; 20 September EFT, debit 5 000, balance 3 000; 23 September invoice BM356, credit 2 800, balance 5 800. In the general ledger, Creditors control receives the CJ total of 20 000 on the credit side and the CPJ's Creditors control column total of 5 000 on the debit side, giving 15 000. The next lesson looks at recording payments in the CPJ in detail. Here, the key point is that the creditors list must reflect all activity, purchases and payments, to agree with the control account.

### subtopic_7 — Do the Totals Agree?
**Assistant:** How does the creditors list help the owner manage the business?

**Tutor:** It tells the owner exactly how much the business must pay, to whom and, together with the invoice dates, by when. At 30 September, Ridgeview owes R15 000 to five suppliers. If BuildMart's terms are 30 days, invoice BM331 from 3 September is due by 3 October, so the owner knows that the balance of that invoice must be paid in the first days of October. Combined with the debtors list, which shows how much customers are expected to pay, the owner can plan the bank balance: will enough money come in from debtors to pay the creditors on time, as well as wages and rent? If not, the owner can take action early, for example by following up slow debtors, delaying a non-urgent purchase or arranging an overdraft. Paying on time keeps suppliers willing to give credit and may earn better prices. The creditors list also allows the owner to check each supplier's monthly statement against Ridgeview's own records before paying, so that the business pays exactly what it owes, no more and no less.
