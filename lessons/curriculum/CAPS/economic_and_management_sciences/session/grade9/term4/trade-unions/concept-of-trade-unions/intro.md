Today we meet trade unions: organisations of workers who join together to protect and improve their wages and working conditions. One worker alone has little power to bargain with an employer, but thousands together can be heard. We look at what unions do, from negotiating wages to representing members in disputes, and at their history in South Africa, from the 1973 Durban strikes to federations such as COSATU, FEDUSA, NACTU and SAFTU. Then we see how collective bargaining works under the Labour Relations Act of 1995, how a wage deal of 7 percent turns R8 000 into R8 560, and when a strike is protected by law.
