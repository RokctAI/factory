# Part 1 — Expert

In the previous lesson we analysed the effect of Kasi Cycles' November transactions on the accounting equation. Now we record exactly the same fourteen transactions in the books of first entry: the two cash journals, the cash receipts journal and the cash payments journal, and the two credit journals, the debtors journal and the creditors journal. Kasi Cycles is a sole trader in Soweto selling bicycles, parts and accessories at a mark-up of 50 percent on cost.

## Subtopic: The Four Subsidiary Journals

A subsidiary journal is a book of first entry in which similar transactions are recorded together in date order, from source documents, before they are posted to the ledgers. Kasi Cycles uses four.

The cash receipts journal, CRJ, records all money received. Its source documents are the cash register roll for cash sales and duplicate receipts for other money received, such as payments from debtors. Its money columns are Bank, Sales, Cost of sales, Debtors control and Sundry accounts.

The cash payments journal, CPJ, records all money paid out. Its source documents are EFT proofs of payment, cheque counterfoils and, for bank charges, the bank statement. Its money columns are Bank, Trading stock, Wages, Creditors control and Sundry accounts.

The debtors journal, DJ, records sales of trading stock on credit. Its source document is the duplicate invoice, the copy kept by the seller. Its money columns are Sales and Cost of sales.

The creditors journal, CJ, records purchases on credit. Its source document is the original invoice, received from the supplier. Kasi Cycles' CJ has a Creditors control column and a Trading stock column, because all its credit purchases are of trading stock; a business that bought other items on credit would add columns such as Stationery or Equipment, or a Sundry accounts column.

The two cash journals deal with money that has actually moved through the bank. The two credit journals deal with promises: in the DJ, a customer's promise to pay Kasi Cycles later; in the CJ, Kasi Cycles' promise to pay a supplier later. When those promises are kept, the money is recorded in the cash journals: a debtor's payment in the CRJ, Debtors control column, and a payment to a creditor in the CPJ, Creditors control column.

Every journal has the same first columns: the document number, the day, the name of the debtor, creditor or payee, or a short description, and a folio column. The folio column is left empty when the transaction is recorded. It is filled in during posting, with the number of the ledger account to which the line was posted, so that anyone checking the books can follow each amount from the journal to the ledger and back. Each journal page also carries its own number, such as CRJ11 for the cash receipts journal of November, the eleventh month of the calendar year, which is written in the ledger accounts as the folio reference.

The most important rule is that each transaction is recorded in exactly one journal. Recording the same transaction in two journals would count it twice when the journals are posted.

## Subtopic: Sorting November's Transactions

Here are Kasi Cycles' November transactions. On 2 November, cash sales according to the cash register roll, R9 000. On 4 November, sold goods on credit to Mzansi Couriers, invoice 301, R4 500. On 6 November, bought trading stock on credit from Bike Parts Wholesale, invoice BP77, R7 000. On 8 November, paid wages by EFT 501, R3 500. On 10 November, received R2 400 from Greenway School in settlement of its account, receipt 088. On 12 November, paid Trail Supplies R3 000 by EFT 502, settling its account. On 15 November, sold goods on credit to Greenway School, invoice 302, R3 000. On 18 November, bought trading stock from Gauteng Cycle Traders and paid immediately by EFT 503, R2 000. On 20 November, received R3 600 from Mzansi Couriers, receipt 089. On 22 November, bought trading stock on credit from Trail Supplies, invoice TS210, R4 000. On 25 November, paid Bike Parts Wholesale R5 000 by EFT 504. On 26 November, paid rent to Orlando Properties by EFT 505, R3 000. On 27 November, cash sales, R6 000. On 30 November, the bank statement showed bank charges of R200.

Sort them with four questions. Did money come into the bank? CRJ: the cash sales of 2 and 27 November and the receipts from Greenway School and Mzansi Couriers, four transactions. Did money go out of the bank? CPJ: wages, the payments to Trail Supplies and Bike Parts Wholesale, the cash purchase from Gauteng Cycle Traders, the rent and the bank charges, six transactions. Was trading stock sold on credit? DJ: invoices 301 and 302, two transactions. Was something bought on credit? CJ: invoices BP77 and TS210, two transactions. That is 4 + 6 + 2 + 2 = 14, every transaction recorded once.

Watch for the traps. The purchase of 18 November involves trading stock and a supplier, but it was paid immediately, so it is a cash purchase, in the CPJ, Trading stock column, not the CJ. Gauteng Cycle Traders never becomes a creditor. The payments of 12 and 25 November are to suppliers, but they settle earlier credit purchases, so they go in the CPJ, Creditors control column, not the Trading stock column. The receipts of 10 and 20 November are from customers, but they settle earlier credit sales, so they go in the CRJ, Debtors control column, with no Sales or Cost of sales.

The cost prices are calculated by multiplying the selling price by 100 and dividing by 150. Cash sales of R9 000 cost R6 000 and of R6 000 cost R4 000. Invoice 301 for R4 500 cost R3 000 and invoice 302 for R3 000 cost R2 000.

## Subtopic: The CRJ and CPJ for November

The CRJ for November, page CRJ11, has four lines. On 2 November: cash register roll, details Cash sales, Bank 9 000, Sales 9 000, Cost of sales 6 000. On 10 November: receipt 088, Greenway School, Bank 2 400, Debtors control 2 400. On 20 November: receipt 089, Mzansi Couriers, Bank 3 600, Debtors control 3 600. On 27 November: cash register roll, Cash sales, Bank 6 000, Sales 6 000, Cost of sales 4 000.

The totals are: Bank 9 000 + 2 400 + 3 600 + 6 000 = R21 000; Sales 9 000 + 6 000 = R15 000; Cost of sales 6 000 + 4 000 = R10 000; Debtors control 2 400 + 3 600 = R6 000; Sundry accounts nil.

Cross-cast: Sales plus Debtors control plus Sundry accounts must equal Bank. 15 000 + 6 000 = 21 000. It agrees. Cost of sales is not part of the cross-cast, because it is not money received; it records the cost of the goods sold.

The CPJ for November, page CPJ11, has six lines. On 8 November: EFT 501, Wages, Bank 3 500, Wages 3 500. On 12 November: EFT 502, Trail Supplies, Bank 3 000, Creditors control 3 000. On 18 November: EFT 503, Gauteng Cycle Traders, Bank 2 000, Trading stock 2 000. On 25 November: EFT 504, Bike Parts Wholesale, Bank 5 000, Creditors control 5 000. On 26 November: EFT 505, Orlando Properties, Bank 3 000, Sundry accounts 3 000, details Rent expense. On 30 November: bank statement, details Bank charges, Bank 200, Sundry accounts 200, details Bank charges.

The totals are: Bank 3 500 + 3 000 + 2 000 + 5 000 + 3 000 + 200 = R16 700; Trading stock R2 000; Wages R3 500; Creditors control 3 000 + 5 000 = R8 000; Sundry accounts 3 000 + 200 = R3 200.

Cross-cast: 2 000 + 3 500 + 8 000 + 3 200 = 16 700. It agrees with the Bank total.

In the Sundry accounts column, the details column next to the amount names the account to which it will be posted, here Rent expense and Bank charges. That is how the bookkeeper knows where each sundry amount belongs when posting.

## Subtopic: The DJ and CJ and Checking the Totals

The DJ for November, page DJ11, has two lines. On 4 November: invoice 301, Mzansi Couriers, Sales 4 500, Cost of sales 3 000. On 15 November: invoice 302, Greenway School, Sales 3 000, Cost of sales 2 000. Totals: Sales 4 500 + 3 000 = R7 500; Cost of sales 3 000 + 2 000 = R5 000. The DJ has no Bank column, because no money moved.

The CJ for November, page CJ11, has two lines. On 6 November: invoice BP77, Bike Parts Wholesale, Creditors control 7 000, Trading stock 7 000. On 22 November: invoice TS210, Trail Supplies, Creditors control 4 000, Trading stock 4 000. Totals: Creditors control 7 000 + 4 000 = R11 000; Trading stock R11 000. Because every purchase was trading stock, the two columns are equal.

Now check the month as a whole, using the analysis from the previous lesson.

Total sales are the cash sales in the CRJ plus the credit sales in the DJ: 15 000 + 7 500 = R22 500. Total cost of sales is 10 000 + 5 000 = R15 000. Those are exactly the figures we found when analysing the accounting equation.

Total trading stock bought is the cash purchase in the CPJ plus the credit purchases in the CJ: 2 000 + 11 000 = R13 000.

The Debtors control column in the CRJ, R6 000, is the money collected from debtors; the DJ total, R7 500, is the new credit given. The Creditors control column in the CPJ, R8 000, is the money paid to creditors; the CJ total, R11 000, is the new credit received. These four totals will update the Debtors control and Creditors control accounts in the general ledger.

Before posting, every journal must be totalled, every cross-cast checked and every line checked against its source document. A common error is a transposition, for example writing 4 500 as 5 400; another is recording a payment to a creditor under Trading stock. Finding errors in the journals is far easier than finding them later in the ledger or the trial balance.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Let's make this simple. A business has four notebooks. Two are for real money: money in and money out. Two are for promises: customers who will pay us later, and suppliers we will pay later.

## Subtopic: Four Books, Four Questions

Ask four questions about each transaction. Money came in? CRJ. Money went out? CPJ. Sold stock on credit? DJ. Bought on credit? CJ.

Each transaction goes in one book only. Never two.

Kasi Cycles had 14 transactions in November: 4 in the CRJ, 6 in the CPJ, 2 in the DJ and 2 in the CJ.

## Subtopic: Money In and Money Out

Money in, the CRJ: two days of cash sales, R9 000 and R6 000, and two debtors paying, R2 400 and R3 600. Total R21 000.

Money out, the CPJ: wages R3 500, two suppliers paid R3 000 and R5 000, stock bought for cash R2 000, rent R3 000 and bank charges R200. Total R16 700.

Careful: paying a supplier for an old credit purchase goes under Creditors control, not Trading stock. The stock was already recorded when we bought it.

## Subtopic: Promises in Both Directions

The DJ: Mzansi Couriers bought for R4 500, and Greenway School for R3 000. They promise to pay later. Total R7 500.

The CJ: Kasi Cycles bought from Bike Parts Wholesale for R7 000 and Trail Supplies for R4 000. Kasi Cycles promises to pay later. Total R11 000.

Add the cash and credit books together. Sales: 15 000 + 7 500 = 22 500. Stock bought: 2 000 + 11 000 = 13 000.

Four books, one month, every transaction once. Practise by recording November yourself, then totalling and cross-casting each journal.
