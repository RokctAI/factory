Today we bring cash and credit transactions together in one accounting equation. We follow Kasi Cycles, a bicycle shop in Soweto that sells at a mark-up of 50 percent on cost, through fourteen transactions in November: cash and credit sales, cash and credit purchases of stock, receipts from debtors, payments to creditors, and expenses. For each one we ask which accounts change, whether each is an asset, liability or owner's equity, and whether it increases or decreases. We follow the totals from 70 000 = 62 000 + 8 000 to 73 800 = 62 800 + 11 000, and check that the R800 increase in owner's equity is exactly the profit for the month.
