# Part 1 — Expert

In the previous lesson we recorded Kasi Cycles' November transactions in the four subsidiary journals and totalled them. Now we complete the accounting cycle for the month: we post the journals to the general ledger, post the individual lines to the debtors ledger and the creditors ledger, check the control accounts against the lists of debtors and creditors, and prepare a trial balance on 30 November. Kasi Cycles is a sole trader in Soweto selling bicycles and parts at a mark-up of 50 percent on cost, and its financial year started on 1 November.

## Subtopic: Posting the Cash Journals to the General Ledger

Posting means transferring amounts from the journals to the ledger accounts. The general ledger contains an account for every asset, liability, owner's equity item, income and expense. Kasi Cycles numbers its balance sheet accounts with a B and its nominal accounts, the income and expense accounts, with an N: Capital B1, Equipment B2, Trading stock B3, Debtors control B4, Bank B5 and Creditors control B6; Sales N1, Cost of sales N2, Wages N3, Rent expense N4 and Bank charges N5.

Two rules guide every posting. Every amount is posted to two accounts, once on the debit side and once on the credit side. And in the details column of each account, we write the name of the other account involved, with the journal page as the folio.

The opening balances on 1 November are written first, as balance b/d: Equipment R30 000, Trading stock R20 000, Debtors control R6 000 and Bank R14 000 on the debit side; Capital R62 000 and Creditors control R8 000 on the credit side.

From the CRJ, page CRJ11, the Bank total of R21 000 is debited to the Bank account, details Total receipts. Then each analysis column is posted to the credit side of its account, with details Bank: Sales is credited with R15 000, and Debtors control is credited with R6 000. The Cost of sales column is posted separately, because it records a different double entry: Cost of sales is debited with R10 000, details Trading stock, and Trading stock is credited with R10 000, details Cost of sales.

From the CPJ, page CPJ11, the Bank total of R16 700 is credited to the Bank account, details Total payments. Each analysis column is debited to its account, details Bank: Trading stock R2 000, Wages R3 500 and Creditors control R8 000. The Sundry accounts column is not posted as a total, because it contains amounts for different accounts. Each sundry amount is posted individually to the account named in the details: Rent expense is debited with R3 000 and Bank charges with R200, both with the details Bank.

After posting the cash journals, the Bank account shows a debit balance of 14 000 + 21 000 - 16 700 = R18 300, which is the balance carried down on 30 November and brought down on 1 December.

## Subtopic: Posting the Credit Journals to the General Ledger

From the DJ, page DJ11, the Sales total of R7 500 is debited to Debtors control, details Sales, and credited to Sales, details Debtors control. The Cost of sales total of R5 000 is debited to Cost of sales, details Trading stock, and credited to Trading stock, details Cost of sales.

From the CJ, page CJ11, the total of R11 000 is debited to Trading stock, details Creditors control, and credited to Creditors control, details Trading stock, or Total purchases, as some textbooks write.

Now the general ledger accounts can be balanced on 30 November.

Trading stock: debit side, balance 20 000, Bank 2 000 and Creditors control 11 000; credit side, Cost of sales 10 000 and Cost of sales 5 000. Balance: 20 000 + 2 000 + 11 000 - 10 000 - 5 000 = R18 000, a debit balance.

Debtors control: debit side, balance 6 000 and Sales 7 500; credit side, Bank 6 000. Balance: 6 000 + 7 500 - 6 000 = R7 500 debit.

Creditors control: credit side, balance 8 000 and Trading stock 11 000; debit side, Bank 8 000. Balance: 8 000 + 11 000 - 8 000 = R11 000 credit.

Sales: credit side, Bank 15 000 and Debtors control 7 500, total R22 500. Cost of sales: debit side, Trading stock 10 000 and Trading stock 5 000, total R15 000. Wages R3 500, Rent expense R3 000 and Bank charges R200, all debit balances. Equipment R30 000 debit and Capital R62 000 credit are unchanged.

Notice that every balance agrees with what we calculated in the accounting equation lesson. That is no coincidence: the journals and the ledger are a formal way of recording exactly the same effects.

## Subtopic: The Debtors and Creditors Ledgers

The control accounts show the total owed by all debtors and to all creditors, but not who owes what. That detail is kept in two subsidiary ledgers, with an account for each debtor and each creditor. Individual lines, not totals, are posted to them, in date order, so that the running balance is correct on every date.

In the debtors ledger, a credit sale from the DJ is debited to the debtor's account, because the debtor owes more, and a receipt from the CRJ is credited, because the debtor owes less.

Mzansi Couriers, D1: 1 November balance 3 600; 4 November invoice 301 debit 4 500, balance 8 100; 20 November receipt 089 credit 3 600, balance 4 500.

Greenway School, D2: 1 November balance 2 400; 10 November receipt 088 credit 2 400, balance nil; 15 November invoice 302 debit 3 000, balance 3 000.

The debtors list at 30 November: Mzansi Couriers R4 500 and Greenway School R3 000, total 4 500 + 3 000 = R7 500. This agrees with Debtors control.

In the creditors ledger, a credit purchase from the CJ is credited to the creditor's account, because Kasi Cycles owes more, and a payment from the CPJ is debited, because it owes less.

Bike Parts Wholesale, C1: 1 November balance 5 000; 6 November invoice BP77 credit 7 000, balance 12 000; 25 November EFT 504 debit 5 000, balance 7 000.

Trail Supplies, C2: 1 November balance 3 000; 12 November EFT 502 debit 3 000, balance nil; 22 November invoice TS210 credit 4 000, balance 4 000.

The creditors list at 30 November: Bike Parts Wholesale R7 000 and Trail Supplies R4 000, total 7 000 + 4 000 = R11 000. This agrees with Creditors control.

After posting each line, its ledger folio, such as D1 or C2, is written in the folio column of the journal, which shows that the line has been posted.

Notice what is not posted to these ledgers: cash sales, wages, rent, bank charges and the cash purchase from Gauteng Cycle Traders. Only amounts in the Debtors control and Creditors control columns, and the lines of the DJ and CJ, involve individual debtors or creditors.

## Subtopic: The Trial Balance

A trial balance is a list of the balances of all the general ledger accounts on a certain date, with debit balances in one column and credit balances in the other. If the double entry has been completed correctly, the two totals are equal. Balance sheet accounts are listed first, then nominal accounts.

Kasi Cycles' trial balance on 30 November:

Balance sheet accounts: Capital, credit 62 000; Equipment, debit 30 000; Trading stock, debit 18 000; Debtors control, debit 7 500; Bank, debit 18 300; Creditors control, credit 11 000.

Nominal accounts: Sales, credit 22 500; Cost of sales, debit 15 000; Wages, debit 3 500; Rent expense, debit 3 000; Bank charges, debit 200.

Debit total: 30 000 + 18 000 + 7 500 + 18 300 + 15 000 + 3 500 + 3 000 + 200 = R95 500. Credit total: 62 000 + 11 000 + 22 500 = R95 500. The totals agree.

The trial balance also lets us check the profit: Sales 22 500, less Cost of sales 15 000, less expenses 3 500 + 3 000 + 200 = 6 700, gives a net profit of R800, the same profit found by analysing the accounting equation.

A trial balance that balances does not prove that there are no errors. A transaction left out completely, or posted to the wrong account on the correct side, or recorded with the same wrong amount on both sides, will not make the totals differ. But a trial balance that does not balance certainly contains an error. Look at the difference. If Wages of R3 500 had been posted only to the Wages account and not to Bank, the debit side would show an extra R3 500 compared with the credit side, so the difference itself points to the transaction. If the difference divides exactly by 9, two digits may have been swapped: for example, R3 600 written as R6 300 gives a difference of 6 300 - 3 600 = R2 700, and 2 700 ÷ 9 = 300.

At the end of the month, each general ledger account is balanced. The two sides are added, the difference is written on the smaller side as balance c/d on the last day of the month, both sides are totalled and double-underlined, and the balance is brought down on the opposite side as balance b/d on the first day of the next month. Nominal accounts such as Sales and Wages are simply totalled during the year, because they will be closed off to the Profit and loss account at the end of the financial year, which you will learn in Grade 10 Accounting. Only the balances, not the individual entries, appear in the trial balance.

Together, the journals, the general ledger, the debtors and creditors ledgers and the trial balance form the complete accounting cycle you have built up through Grade 9.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Let's make this simple. The journals are like notebooks where we write things down each day. Posting copies the totals onto the pages of a big file, the general ledger, one page for each account. Then we list every page's balance and check that both sides agree.

## Subtopic: From Notebooks to Pages

Every amount goes onto two pages: once on the left, the debit side, and once on the right, the credit side.

Money in, R21 000, goes on the left of the Bank page. Money out, R16 700, goes on the right. The bank started at R14 000, so now it has 14 000 + 21 000 - 16 700 = R18 300.

Sales from the cash book and the credit book add up: 15 000 + 7 500 = R22 500.

Stock: started at 20 000, bought 2 000 for cash and 11 000 on credit, sold stock that cost 15 000. Left: R18 000.

## Subtopic: Every Customer and Supplier

Each customer has a page. Mzansi Couriers owed 3 600, bought 4 500 more, paid 3 600. Now owes 4 500. Greenway School paid its 2 400, then bought 3 000. Now owes 3 000. Together: R7 500, the same as Debtors control.

Each supplier has a page. Bike Parts Wholesale: owed 5 000, sold us 7 000 more, we paid 5 000. Now 7 000. Trail Supplies: paid 3 000, then sold us 4 000. Now 4 000. Together: R11 000, the same as Creditors control.

## Subtopic: Do Both Sides Agree?

List every page. Left side: equipment, stock, debtors, bank, cost of sales, wages, rent and bank charges. Total R95 500.

Right side: capital, creditors and sales. Total R95 500.

Both sides agree, so the double entry is complete. And sales minus cost of sales minus expenses leaves a profit of R800.

If they do not agree, look at the difference: it often points straight to the mistake. Practise by posting November yourself and drawing up the trial balance.
