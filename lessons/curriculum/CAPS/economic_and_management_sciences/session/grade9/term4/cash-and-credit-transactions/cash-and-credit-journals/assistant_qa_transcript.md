### subtopic_1 — The Four Subsidiary Journals
**Assistant:** Why does a business use four separate journals instead of one big list?

**Tutor:** Because grouping similar transactions saves a great deal of work and makes errors easier to find. Imagine Kasi Cycles recording all its transactions in one list. At the end of the month, every single transaction would have to be posted separately to the general ledger: every cash sale, every receipt, every payment. In a busy shop with hundreds of transactions a month, that is a huge amount of work, and every posting is a chance to make a mistake. With subsidiary journals, similar transactions are recorded together, in columns. All the sales in the CRJ are in one column, so at the end of the month only the column total is posted to the Sales account. The same applies to Cost of sales, Wages, Trading stock, Debtors control and Creditors control. Instead of hundreds of postings, there are a few totals. Separating the journals also matches the source documents and the people who handle them. Receipts and the cash register roll go to the person who records the CRJ, EFT proofs of payment to the person who records the CPJ, duplicate invoices to the DJ and original invoices from suppliers to the CJ. Each journal has the columns that suit its transactions: the CRJ has a Debtors control column for receipts from debtors, the CPJ a Creditors control column for payments to creditors. Finally, separate journals make the business easier to understand and to control. The owner can look at the DJ and see exactly how much credit was given this month, or at the CJ and see how much was bought on credit. Those are useful management figures that a single long list would hide.

### subtopic_2 — Sorting November's Transactions
**Assistant:** How do I tell the difference between a cash purchase of stock and a payment to a creditor?

**Tutor:** Ask whether the business is paying for stock it receives now, or paying a debt for stock it received earlier. On 18 November, Kasi Cycles went to Gauteng Cycle Traders, chose stock worth R2 000 and paid immediately by EFT. The stock arrived and the money left on the same day. That is a cash purchase. It goes in the CPJ, with R2 000 in the Bank column and R2 000 in the Trading stock column, because the stock is being recorded for the first time. Gauteng Cycle Traders never becomes a creditor, because Kasi Cycles never owed it anything. On 25 November, Kasi Cycles paid Bike Parts Wholesale R5 000. That payment is for stock bought on credit in October, which was recorded in October's CJ when the original invoice arrived. Recording it again under Trading stock would mean the same stock was recorded twice, and the Trading stock account would be overstated by R5 000. So the payment goes in the CPJ with R5 000 in Bank and R5 000 in Creditors control, which reduces the debt. A useful test is to look at the source document. For a cash purchase, the supplier's invoice and the proof of payment carry the same date, and the invoice usually says paid or cash. For a payment to a creditor, the proof of payment refers to an account, a statement or an earlier invoice number, and the creditors ledger shows an amount owing. If a supplier appears in the creditors ledger with an amount owing, a payment to them almost certainly goes in the Creditors control column.

### subtopic_3 — The CRJ and CPJ for November
**Assistant:** What is a cross-cast, and why is Cost of sales left out of it in the CRJ?

**Tutor:** A cross-cast is a check that the column totals of a journal agree with each other. In the cash receipts journal, every rand received is entered in the Bank column and also in one of the columns that explain where it came from: Sales, Debtors control or Sundry accounts. So, if every line has been entered correctly, the total of those explanation columns must equal the Bank total. For Kasi Cycles in November, Sales R15 000 plus Debtors control R6 000 plus Sundry accounts nil gives R21 000, which equals the Bank total. If the figures did not agree, a line would have been entered wrongly, for example an amount written in Bank but not in an analysis column, or a column added incorrectly. Cost of sales is left out because it is not part of the money received. When Kasi Cycles sells goods for R9 000 cash, R9 000 comes into the bank, and that R9 000 is explained by the Sales column. The R6 000 in the Cost of sales column records something different: the cost of the bicycles and parts that left the shop. It is written in the CRJ only because the cost is known at the moment of the sale, so it is convenient to record it on the same line. If it were included in the cross-cast, the explanation columns would add up to more than the money received, and the check would never work. In the CPJ, the cross-cast is similar: Trading stock R2 000, Wages R3 500, Creditors control R8 000 and Sundry accounts R3 200 add up to R16 700, the Bank total. The CPJ has no memorandum column, so every analysis column is included.

### subtopic_4 — The DJ and CJ and Checking the Totals
**Assistant:** Why does the debtors journal have no Bank column?

**Tutor:** Because no money moves when goods are sold on credit. When Kasi Cycles sold goods to Mzansi Couriers on 4 November for R4 500, Mzansi took the goods and received an invoice, but paid nothing at that moment. Kasi Cycles' bank balance did not change. What changed was that Kasi Cycles earned income, so Sales increased, gave up stock, so Cost of sales increased, and gained a right to receive R4 500 later, so Debtors control increased. The DJ therefore has a Sales column and a Cost of sales column, and the debtor's name on each line shows who owes the money. The Sales total of the DJ is later posted to the credit of the Sales account and to the debit of Debtors control in the general ledger, and each line is posted individually to the debtor's account in the debtors ledger. The same reasoning applies to the creditors journal. When Kasi Cycles bought stock on credit from Bike Parts Wholesale for R7 000, no money left the bank. Stock increased and a debt to the supplier was created, so the CJ has a Creditors control column and a Trading stock column. Money moves only later, when the debtor pays or Kasi Cycles pays the creditor, and those transactions are recorded in the cash journals. Keeping the credit journals free of a Bank column is important, because it makes sure the Bank account in the ledger is updated only from the cash journals, where real money is recorded. If a credit sale were also entered in the CRJ, the Bank account would show money that never arrived, and the bank reconciliation at the end of the month would fail.
