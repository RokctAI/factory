Today we sort structures by how they are built. A shell structure is a thin skin that gets its strength from its curved shape, like an egg or a car body. A frame structure is a skeleton of members joined together, like a cell phone tower or a roof truss, with the spaces between left open or covered later. A solid structure is strong because it is thick and heavy all the way through, like a dam wall or a brick. We handle an egg, a wire coat hanger and a brick, visit a stadium, a pylon and the Gariep Dam, and learn to tell the three types apart and to say why each one is used.
