Hey — if stability, strength and rigidity feel like three words for the same thing, here is the calm version: does not fall over, does not break, does not bend. Three different things.

Then the trick. A flat sheet of paper bends because all of it is in one thin layer. Roll it into a tube or fold it into pleats and the paper moves away from the middle, where it has to stretch and squash to bend. So it gets stiff. Same paper, no extra weight.

Try it: flat sheet, one coin, collapse. Tube, dozens of coins. Folded, dozens too, as long as the folds run along the gap.

Straws, bones, corrugated roofs and cardboard all use it. So should your tower legs.

Open the practice set and picture the three sheets. You've got this!
