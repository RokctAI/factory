Hey — if triangulation and tension and compression sound like engineering school, here is the calm version: pin four strips into a square and push it. It falls flat. Pin one strip across. It stops.

That is the whole idea. A triangle cannot change shape unless a side changes length, so cut every shape into triangles.

Push one way and the diagonal is pulled: tension, fine. Push the other way and it is squashed: compression, and a thin strip bows. So use two diagonals in an X, and one is always pulled. Even string works.

Then remember twist: braced faces can still twist, so put one diagonal through the inside.

Open the practice set and picture the square, the X and the cube. You've got this!
