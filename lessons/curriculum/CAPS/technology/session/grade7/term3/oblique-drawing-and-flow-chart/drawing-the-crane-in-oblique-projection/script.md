# Part 1 — Expert

This session produces the working drawing of the chosen crane in oblique projection on squared paper. The anchor is a classroom in Pietermaritzburg where each team has its chosen sketch, its list of sizes, an A4 sheet of squared paper in landscape, a 45 degree set square, two pencils and an eraser, and ninety minutes. The governing idea is that a working drawing is a scaled, dimensioned, conventionally drawn representation from which the object can be made, that the crane is built up in oblique from the base, which is drawn as a flat rectangle receding at 45 degrees, through the mast and arm to the winch and magnet, that hidden parts such as the drum axle are dashed and axes are chain lines, that the drawing is dimensioned once with real sizes, and that a detail view at a larger scale shows small parts. The working drawing is a marked PAT product and oblique drawing is examinable.

## Subtopic: Setting Out the Sheet

Turn the squared paper to landscape, because the crane is tall and the oblique depth runs off to the right, and a landscape sheet gives room for both plus dimensions. Draw a border 10 millimetres in from the edge and a title block 30 millimetres high along the bottom, divided into boxes for title, team, name, date, scale and units. Fill in what you know now: Model Crane with Electromagnet, the team, your name, the date, SCALE 1:2, mm. Deciding the scale first fixes every number that follows.

Make the conversion table in a corner of a rough page, not on the drawing. Base 297 by 210 becomes 148.5 by 105, and the receding 105 becomes 52.5 in cabinet oblique. Mast 350 becomes 175. Arm 250 becomes 125. Mast tube diameter 20 becomes 10. Drum 24 diameter becomes 12. Handle 75 becomes 37.5. Magnet nail 100 becomes 50. Write every size you will draw, because the crane has more lengths than the box did and a missing one will stop you mid-line.

Choose the front face. The crane's most informative view is from the side, with the mast vertical, the arm running to one side and the winch visible at the base, so that is the front face, drawn true. The depth of the base, 210 real, runs back at 45 degrees. Place the front face so that the base's near corner sits about 30 millimetres above the title block and 40 millimetres from the left border, leaving space below and left for dimensions and space right for the receding base.

The questions for this section are with you now: landscape, border and title block, the conversion table and choosing the front face.

## Subtopic: Building the Crane in Oblique

Draw the base first, lightly. Its front edge is a horizontal line 148.5 long. From each end, a 45 degree line 52.5 long up and to the right, then join the far ends: the base is a parallelogram lying flat. If the base is double card 4 millimetres thick, give it a thickness of 2 on the drawing, a second parallelogram just above the first joined by short verticals, so the maker sees a slab, not a sheet.

Find the mast's position on the base from your sketch, for example 100 real from the front edge and central across the width, convert, and mark it. Draw the mast as a vertical rectangle 10 wide and 175 tall if it is a tube seen from the side, with a short 45 degree top to show its thickness in oblique, or as two vertical lines with a dashed inner line if it is a dowel inside a tube. The arm starts at the mast top: a horizontal rectangle 125 long and perhaps 8 high running to the left, with its own oblique thickness. If your design has a tie string from arm tip to mast top, draw it as a thin line. Draw the pulley at the arm tip as a small circle with chain centre lines; draw the string as a thin line down from it; draw the electromagnet as a vertical rectangle 50 tall and 4 wide for the nail with a wider band for the coil, hanging below.

The winch sits at the base of the mast. Draw the drum as a small rectangle 12 by 15 if seen edge on, or a circle 12 across with centre lines if its axis points at you; draw the axle through it as a dashed line where it passes inside the mast or supports, and the handle as a line 37.5 long with a small circle for the grip. Draw the string from the drum up the mast to the pulley as a thin line. Mark the cells and switch at the back of the base as simple rectangles, labelled, since they are the counterweight. Finally, go over every visible edge with the 2B, firm and dark, leaving construction lines faint. The drawing should now read as a solid object at a glance.

The questions for this section are with you now: the base parallelogram, mast, arm, pulley and magnet, and the winch with hidden and chain lines.

## Subtopic: Dimensions, Detail and Checking

Dimension the front face first. Overall height from base to mast top, 350. Arm length, 250. Mast position from the front edge of the base, 100. Handle length, 75. Nail length, 100. Each with a thin dimension line clear of the outline, arrowheads touching extension lines, the real number above. Then the receding dimension: base depth 210, written along a 45 degree dimension line parallel to the base's side. Then base length 297 along the front edge. Pulley diameter and drum diameter with the diameter symbol. Check that no two dimension lines cross and that no number is repeated.

Small parts need a detail. Draw a circle round the winch on the main drawing, label it A, and in a free corner of the sheet draw DETAIL A at 1:1 or 2:1: the drum, its axle through the two support cheeks, the handle at right angles, the string leaving the top of the drum, the stop peg, each dimensioned. Write the detail's own scale beneath it, because a detail at a different scale from the main drawing must say so. If the pulley fixing is unusual, give it DETAIL B. The maker in week eight will build from these details more than from the overall view.

Now check. Against the brief and specifications: does the drawn crane fit the A4 base and stay under 400 tall; does the arm reach 200 sideways; is there a switch in the circuit and a bulb; is the counterweight behind the mast. Against the sketch: has anything been changed, and if so, is the sketch annotated to say why. Against the drawing conventions: four line types used correctly, scale stated, units stated, title block complete, every size once. Have a team mate read the drawing back to you as if building it, and fix anything they have to ask about.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: the base drawn as a flat rectangle with no 45 degree depth, so the crane floats on a line. Two: the mast placed on the drawing without a dimension locating it on the base, so the maker guesses. Three: a detail drawn at a different scale with no scale written under it. Four: construction lines never darkened, so outline and guide are the same grey and the drawing reads as a tangle.

The questions for this section are with you now: which dimensions go where, the detail view and its scale, and the three checks.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with one sheet of squared paper and the crane growing on it from the base up — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Get the Sheet Ready

Landscape. Border 10 millimetres in. Title block 30 millimetres high along the bottom: title, team, name, date, SCALE 1:2, mm.

Rough table on scrap paper: every real size and its half. Base 297 by 210 becomes 148.5 by 105, and the depth 52.5 on the slant. Mast 350 becomes 175. Arm 250 becomes 125.

Front face is the side view: mast up, arm to the side, winch at the base. The base depth goes back at 45 degrees. Leave room left and below for dimensions.

Keep the landscape sheet in mind and try the questions for this part: the title block, the table and the front face.

## Subtopic: Grow the Crane

Base first: a flat parallelogram with its sides at 45 degrees, with a little thickness. Mark where the mast stands. Mast up, a tall thin rectangle. Arm across from the top. Pulley at the tip, a small circle with centre lines. String down. Magnet hanging: a nail with a coil band.

Winch at the mast foot: drum, axle dashed where it hides inside, handle with a grip. String up the mast. Cells and switch as labelled boxes at the back.

Light lines first. Then darken every edge you can see with the soft pencil.

Hold the crane growing from the base in mind and try this part's questions: order of drawing, hidden and centre lines, dark edges.

## Subtopic: Sizes, Detail, Check

Height 350, arm 250, mast 100 from the front edge, handle 75, nail 100: on the front face. Depth 210 along a slanted dimension line. Diameters with the symbol. Once each, no crossing.

Circle the winch, call it A, draw DETAIL A bigger in a corner with its own scale written under it.

Check against the brief, the sketch and the conventions. Let a team mate read it as a maker and fix every question.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Landscape sheet, border, title block, SCALE 1:2. Table of sizes. Side view at the front, base receding at 45 degrees. Base, mast, arm, pulley, magnet, winch, counterweight. Dashed for hidden, chain for centres, dark edges. Dimensions once, real sizes, detail A with its scale. Three checks.

Hold DETAIL A, and take the final questions of the lesson: dimensions, the detail and the summary.
