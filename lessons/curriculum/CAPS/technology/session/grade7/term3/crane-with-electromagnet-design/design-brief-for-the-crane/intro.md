Today we stop investigating and start designing. The scrap-metal dealer needs a crane with an electromagnet that lifts steel out of a mixed pile, swings it across and drops it in a bin, and today you write the design brief that says exactly what the model must do, how well it must do it and what you are not allowed to use. We separate the specifications, which the crane must achieve, from the constraints, which limit how you may build it, and we practise writing both so sharply that a stranger could test your model against them. By the end you will have a one-page brief for your crane that your team can design against for the rest of the term.
