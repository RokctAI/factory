Today we put magnets on the bench and find out what they actually do. A bar magnet and a horseshoe magnet, a handful of paper clips, some iron filings on a sheet of paper and a compass are enough to discover where a magnet is strongest, why two magnets sometimes snap together and sometimes push apart, and what the invisible field around a magnet looks like. By the end you will be able to name the poles, state the rule of attraction and repulsion, draw the field of a bar magnet and explain why these facts matter for a crane that sorts scrap.
