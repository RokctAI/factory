Hey — if magnets feel like a mystery, here is the calm version: a magnet has two ends called poles, and that is where it pulls. Clips stick to the ends, not the middle.

One end points north when the magnet hangs free: that is N. The other is S. N and S snap together. N and N push apart. That is the whole law.

Iron filings show the field as curved lines from N to S, bunched at the ends. Cut a magnet and each piece gets its own N and S.

Open the practice set and picture the clips on the ends. You've got this!
