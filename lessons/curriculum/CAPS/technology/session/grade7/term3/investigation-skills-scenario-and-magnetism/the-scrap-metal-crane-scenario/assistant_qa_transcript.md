### subtopic_1 — Reading the Scenario for the Real Need
**Assistant:** Why does the dealer not simply use a big permanent magnet on a rope instead of an electromagnet?

**Tutor:** A permanent magnet would pick up the steel beautifully, and then it would never let go. Scrap sorting depends on the release as much as the lift: the crane swings the steel over the ferrous pile or the truck and must drop it there, and a permanent magnet would have to be prised off every piece by hand, which puts the worker right back under a dangling load. An electromagnet solves this because its magnetism exists only while current flows through its coil; open the switch and the field collapses, the steel falls, and the crane swings back for more. There are two further advantages. The strength of an electromagnet can be adjusted by changing the current or the number of turns, so a yard can tune it to lift what it needs. And an electromagnet that is switched off is safe to work near, whereas a large permanent magnet is always live and will snatch tools, belt buckles and loose steel from across a bench. Real scrap magnets are electromagnets a metre or more across, powered by the crane's own generator, and the operator has a switch in the cab. Your model will do the same with a nail, a coil of wire, a cell and a switch, and the moment you see paper clips drop when you open the switch you will understand why the scenario insists on it.

### subtopic_2 — A Case Study of Cranes
**Assistant:** How much of the case study do I actually need to write down for the PAT, and what are examiners looking for?

**Tutor:** The investigation stage of PAT 2 carries fifteen marks, and the marks reward evidence of thinking, not volume. A good record has four parts. First, a short restatement of the scenario in your own words that names the user, the need, the situation and at least two constraints such as cost and safety; this shows you understood the problem rather than the proposed answer. Second, a case study of at least three different cranes, each with a labelled sketch or an annotated picture and a sentence on what you could borrow; the examiner wants to see that you looked at real products and extracted ideas rather than copying one. Third, the results of the practical investigations into magnets and materials, which come in the next two sessions, recorded as tables with a conclusion. Fourth, a safety note. A page or two of neat, labelled, honest work scores higher than ten pages of printed pictures with no comments. Write in the first person about what you observed and decided, date each entry, and keep it in your PAT folder, because the design and making stages will refer back to it and the whole folder is marked together at the end.

### subtopic_3 — From Pictures to Design Ideas
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a jib crane and a tower crane, and which is better as a model for a scrap yard?

**Tutor:** A jib crane is the simpler of the two: a fixed vertical post or wall bracket with a horizontal or slightly raised arm, the jib, that can swing round the post; the load hangs from a trolley or the tip of the jib and is lifted by a hoist. Jib cranes are used where loads are moved over a small circular area, such as a workshop or a loading bay, and they are cheap and robust. A tower crane is a tall lattice mast with a long horizontal jib at the top balanced by a counterweight; a trolley runs along the jib to change the reach, and the whole top slews on a slewing ring. Tower cranes are for great height and long reach on building sites. A scrap yard usually uses a third type, a mobile crane with a hydraulic boom and a magnet, because it must drive about the yard. For a model, the jib crane is the best starting point: a post set in a wide, heavy base, an arm pivoted on the post so it can slew, a crank-driven drum at the base of the post winding a string over a pulley at the tip of the jib, and the electromagnet on the string. Add a counterweight on the short end of the arm, borrowed from the tower crane, and you have a stable, simple model that does everything the scenario asks.
