# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the second and third making periods of the crane PAT, in which the model crane is built and the electromagnet integrated. The anchor is a classroom in George where each team's bench holds its working drawing and flow chart, its tested electromagnet, recycled card, skewers, string, a cotton reel, a bead, glue, tape, scissors, a craft knife on a mat, a ruler and a shared hot-glue gun operated by the teacher. The governing idea is that the model is made to the dimensions of the working drawing and in the sequence of the flow chart, that card is stiffened by rolling and folding and the frame made rigid by triangulation and gussets, that the crank and drum are mounted so the string runs straight to the tip pulley, that the base is weighted behind the mast for stability, and that the finished crane is tested against every specification with results recorded. Making skills, use of tools, accuracy to the drawing and testing are marked in the PAT.

## Subtopic: Base, Mast and Arm

Open the flow chart and tick off what is done: the electromagnet is tested, the materials are collected. The chart says the glued parts come first, so start with the base. Measure the A4 outline on double-thickness card using the drawing's dimensions, cut it on the mat with the knife against the ruler, blade away from the body, and glue the two layers with glue spread to the edges. Weight it under a book to dry flat. Mark the mast position from the drawing, 100 millimetres from the front edge and central, with a pencil cross, before the glue sets so the base is not flexed later.

The mast is a tube. Cut the card to the drawing's length, 350 millimetres, roll it tightly round a pencil or a dowel so the rolled diameter matches the drawing, 20 millimetres, glue the final edge and tape it while it dries. A tightly rolled tube resists bending far better than the flat card did, which is the term-two lesson in your hands. If the design is a lattice mast of four skewers, glue them at the corners of a 20 millimetre square to card rungs at the base, middle and top, then add a diagonal skewer or a card gusset to every face so no face is left a parallelogram. Test the mast by holding its base and pushing the top sideways in both directions; it should not sway.

The arm is a second tube or a triangular section of three card strips folded and glued into a triangle, 250 millimetres long, and it is the member most likely to bend under load, so make it stiff first and light second. If the drawing has a tie from the arm's tip back to the mast top, cut the string for it now. While these three parts dry, which the flow chart marks as a gap, the team's free hands make the winch and pulley from the next section.

The questions for this section are with you now: cutting and gluing the base, rolling the mast tube or triangulating a lattice, and making the arm stiff.

## Subtopic: Winch, Pulley and Assembly

The winch is the crank and drum from the detail drawing. Push a skewer through the centre of a cotton reel and glue it so the reel cannot spin on the skewer. Fix the handle at right angles 75 millimetres from the axle: a strip of double card with the skewer passed through two holes and glued both sides, or a stiff wire bent at right angles and bound to the skewer with thread and glue, with a short card tube over its end as a grip. Make two card cheeks, each with a hole just larger than the skewer, to stand either side of the drum, and glue a small bead on the skewer between cheek and drum so the drum turns freely without rubbing. Cut a notch in one cheek for a peg, a short piece of skewer, that the handle can rest against so the load cannot run back down.

The pulley for the arm's tip is a bead or a grooved card disc on a straightened paper clip. Push the wire through the arm's tip at right angles, 10 millimetres from the end, with the bead in the middle and a card cheek either side, and bend the wire ends down and tape them along the arm. Spin the bead with a finger; it must turn freely.

Now assemble, in the chart's order. Glue the mast upright on the base at the pencil cross, with four card gussets round its foot so it cannot lean. Fit the arm to the mast top with a sleeve of card wrapped round both, or two gussets, and tie the string tie from the arm's tip to the mast top if drawn, pulling the arm level. Glue the winch cheeks to the base at the mast's foot, so the drum sits directly below the mast and the string will run straight up. Tie the string to the drum, wind on a few turns, run it up the mast through a small card guide at the top, along the arm and over the pulley, and tie the electromagnet's loop to its end. Fix the cell holder and switch at the back edge of the base with tape, run the coil's leads down the string and the arm to them, wire the loop exactly as on the circuit diagram, and press the switch to see the indicator light.

Use the tools as you were taught. Scissors for straight cuts in single card, the knife on the mat for double card and tubes, glue spread thin and clamped with pegs or tape while it dries, the hot-glue gun only at the teacher's station for joints that must hold at once such as the mast foot. Keep the bench clear of offcuts so nothing is glued to the wrong thing, and keep the electromagnet's leads away from the glue.

The questions for this section are with you now: the drum and crank with cheeks and a stop, the tip pulley, the assembly order and tool use.

## Subtopic: The Whole-Crane Test

Set up the test as the specifications describe. Mark the pile position, a dish of mixed clips, aluminium foil balls, a copper coin and a brass screw if available, under the arm's tip. Mark the bin position 200 millimetres to the side. Press the switch and lower the magnet into the pile by unwinding the crank; lift by winding; read the height of the magnet above the base with a ruler at the top of the lift, 150 millimetres required. Swing the arm or tip it to the bin; release the switch; watch the load drop and the indicator go out. Check what came across: steel clips only, with no foil, coin or screw, is the sorting specification met. Watch the back edge of the base throughout; if it lifts, the crane fails the stability specification.

Repeat three times and record each result in a table against the specification list: clips lifted, height reached, distance moved, time to release, non-ferrous pieces carried, tipping yes or no, bulb on when magnet on. Then fix what fails, one thing at a time. The arm bends: add a tie string or a second tube alongside. The crane tips: move the cells further back, add a stone to the back of the base if the constraints allow, or shorten the arm. The winch runs back: fit or improve the stop peg. The string jumps off the pulley: deepen the groove or add a card cheek. The magnet twists and tangles its leads: hang it from a swivel of a bent paper clip. Retest after each fix and record.

When the crane passes, finish: trim and tape loose string, label the base with the team name and the project, and tick the final box on the flow chart. Photograph or sketch the finished crane for the presentation, and note on the flow chart which steps took longer than planned and why, because next week's evaluation asks exactly that.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: a flat card mast or arm that folds the first time the load swings, when a rolled tube was in the drawing. Two: the drum mounted off to one side so the string rubs up the mast and the crank is stiff. Three: cells taped to the mast for tidiness, raising the centre of gravity until the loaded crane tips. Four: the finished crane never tested with mixed metals, so the sorting specification is unproven at the presentation.

The questions for this section are with you now: setting up and running the test, the fix for each failure and the error museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with card, skewers, string and a cotton reel, in the order the chart says — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Build the Frame

Chart open. Base first: double card, cut on the mat, glued to the edges, pressed under a book. Mark the mast spot before it dries.

Mast: roll 350 millimetres of card tight round a pencil, glue, tape. Or four skewers with rungs and a diagonal on every face. Push the top: no sway.

Arm: a tube or a folded triangle, 250 long, stiff before light. While glue dries, make the winch and pulley.

Keep the tube under your thumb in mind and try the questions for this part: base, mast and arm.

## Subtopic: Winch, Pulley, Put It Together

Cotton reel glued on a skewer, handle at right angles 75 millimetres out, grip on the end. Two card cheeks with holes, a bead to stop rubbing, a peg so the load cannot run back.

Bead on a straightened paper clip through the arm's tip, cheeks either side, wire ends taped down. Spin it: free.

Mast on the cross with four gussets. Arm on the mast with a sleeve, tie string pulled level. Winch under the mast. String up, along, over the pulley, magnet on. Cells and switch at the back, wired like the diagram. Press: light on.

Hold the string running straight up the mast in mind and try this part's questions: the winch, the pulley and the order.

## Subtopic: Test the Whole Crane

Mixed pile under the tip, bin 200 millimetres away. Press, lower, lift to 150, swing, let go. Steel only in the bin? Base stayed down? Light went out?

Three times, write it all down against the specifications. Arm bends: tie it. Tips: cells further back. Runs back: fix the peg. String jumps: deeper groove. Fix one thing, test again.

Trim, label, tick the last box. Note what took longer than planned.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Build in the chart's order from the drawing's sizes: glued base and tubes first, winch and pulley while drying, then assemble with gussets and sleeves, string over the pulley, magnet on, circuit at the back. Test three times against every specification, fix one thing at a time, record and label.

Hold the base's back edge staying down, and take the final questions of the lesson: the test, the fixes and the summary.
