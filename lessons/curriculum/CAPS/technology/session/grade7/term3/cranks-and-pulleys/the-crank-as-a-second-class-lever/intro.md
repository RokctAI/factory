Today we meet the crank, the handle that turns a push into a turn, and we discover that it is a lever bent into a circle. We look at a bicycle pedal, a windlass over a well, a pencil sharpener and a fishing reel, and in each one we find the same thing: a handle on an arm, an axle at the centre, and the load on the axle between them, which makes the crank a second-class lever like a wheelbarrow. We measure why a longer handle makes winding easier, and we design the crank that will wind your crane's string. By the end you will be able to identify a crank, explain why it is a second-class lever, calculate its mechanical advantage and choose the handle length for your model.
