# Part 1 — Expert

This session is a revision lesson that ties the mechanical systems of term three to the mechanical advantage of term one and the structures of term two, in service of the crane. The anchor is a classroom in Welkom where a team's chosen crane sketch is pinned to the board beside two revision sheets, one headed Mechanical Advantage and one headed Strengthening Frames, and the lesson works through the sketch applying each sheet in turn. The governing idea is that mechanical advantage is load divided by effort, that it is gained by levers, cranks, wheels and axles and pulleys at the cost of distance, that advantages in a chain multiply, and that frame structures are strengthened by stiffening members through shaping, by triangulation, by bracing joints and by widening and weighting the base, so that the crane's arm, mast and base can carry the load the mechanism lifts. Calculating mechanical advantage and describing ways to strengthen a frame are examinable in the end-of-year paper.

## Subtopic: Mechanical Advantage Revisited

Mechanical advantage is the number of times a mechanism multiplies the effort: load divided by effort, with both measured in the same units, so it has no unit itself. If a 60 gram load is lifted with a 10 gram effort, the mechanical advantage is six. The law behind it is that no mechanism gives more work out than went in, so a mechanism that multiplies force by six makes the effort move six times as far. Force up, distance down; distance up, force down. Every machine in the year obeys it.

Where it comes from, mechanism by mechanism. A lever: effort distance from the pivot divided by load distance from the pivot. First class with the pivot nearer the load multiplies force; second class always multiplies force; third class always multiplies distance and speed. A crank: handle radius over drum radius, a second-class lever bent round. A wheel and axle: wheel radius over axle radius when the effort is on the wheel, which is why a big steering wheel turns a stiff column. A pulley system: the number of rope sections supporting the load. A gear pair: teeth on the driven gear over teeth on the driver. Hydraulics, from term one: area of the large piston over area of the small one.

Now the crane, link by link. The hand turns a 75 millimetre handle on a 12 millimetre drum: advantage about six. The string runs over a fixed pulley at the tip: advantage one, direction only. The magnet hangs straight from the string: no further mechanism. Combined advantage six times one, six. A magnet and load of 60 grams needs 10 grams at the handle, and the finger does not notice. If you add a movable pulley at the magnet: six times two, twelve, and two turns of the crank become four for the same lift. Write the chain on your sketch as a line of numbers with a total, because the examination asks for exactly that calculation from a labelled drawing.

The questions for this section are with you now: the definition and the trade, advantage for each mechanism and the crane's chain.

## Subtopic: Strengthening Frames Revisited

A frame structure, from term two, is made of members joined at their ends, with the spaces between left open: a tower crane, a pylon, a roof truss, a bicycle frame. Frames fail in three ways. Members bend or buckle. Joints rotate and the frame folds into a parallelogram. The whole thing tips over. Each failure has its own cure, and you learned them all at the cell phone tower.

Against bending: shape the member. A flat strip of card droops; the same card rolled into a tube, folded into an L, a U or a box, resists bending many times better, because the material is moved away from the middle of the section where it does little and out to the edges where it works. The crane's mast should be a tight tube or a square box section; the arm a tube or a triangular section of three strips; skewers are already round sections, which is why they are stiff for their weight. Against buckling, the sudden sideways bowing of a long thin member in compression, keep compression members short and brace them halfway.

Against folding: triangulate. A rectangle of four members pinned at the corners collapses sideways with a push; add one diagonal and it becomes two triangles, which cannot change shape without a member changing length. Every rectangular panel in the crane's frame, the sides of a lattice mast, the gap between the mast and the arm, the uprights that carry a hinged boom, gets a diagonal or a gusset, a triangle of card glued across the corner. Against tipping: a wide base, a counterweight opposite the load and a low centre of gravity, so put the cells and switch at the back of the base, not up the mast. The brief's A4 base is wide enough if the mast is near its centre and the weight sits behind.

Joints deserve a sentence of their own. A butt joint of card to card on its edge is weak; overlap, fold a tab, or add a gusset so the glue works in shear across an area rather than in a thin line. Where a skewer meets card, pass it through two layers and glue both. Where the arm meets the mast, the most loaded joint in the crane, use a sleeve or a pair of gussets, and triangulate from the arm back up to the mast top with a string or a card strut, as a tower crane does with its tie bars.

The questions for this section are with you now: the three ways frames fail and the cures, shaping, triangulating and the base.

## Subtopic: Applying Both to the Chosen Sketch

Take the chosen crane sketch and walk the load path backwards from the magnet. The magnet pulls the string; the string pulls down on the tip of the arm; the arm pushes down on the mast at the joint and tries to rotate about it; the mast pushes on the base; the base presses on the table and tries to lift at the back. At each step ask two questions: does the mechanism here give or lose advantage, and does the structure here resist its failure? At the tip: light grooved pulley, advantage one, little friction. Along the arm: a tube or triangular section, not a flat strip, braced back to the mast top. At the joint: gussets or a sleeve. Down the mast: a tube or a triangulated lattice of four skewers. At the base: double card, mast near the centre, cells as counterweight at the back, and a measured check that the loaded crane does not tip.

Then walk the effort path forward from the hand. Handle 75 millimetres, right angles, firm; drum 12 millimetres, string in one layer; string straight up the mast, over the pulley, down. Total advantage six, written on the sketch. Effort needed, load over six, written beside it. If your group's magnet and load weigh 80 grams, the finger supplies about 13 grams plus friction. If the winch is stiff on the day, the diagnosis is on the sketch: friction at the drum axle, string rubbing the mast or a pulley that does not turn.

Mark the improvements on the sketch in a second colour and keep the sketch in the design folder. The PAT rubric for the design stage gives marks for a design that shows understanding of mechanical advantage and of structural strengthening, and the quickest way to show both is a sketch with the numbers of the mechanism chain on one side and the stiffening labels, tube, gusset, diagonal, counterweight, on the other.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: claiming the tip pulley multiplies the crank's advantage; it is a fixed pulley, advantage one. Two: a mast made from a flat strip of card, which folds the moment the load swings. Three: a rectangular frame with no diagonals, which leans and collapses sideways. Four: cells mounted up the mast for neatness, raising the centre of gravity and tipping the loaded crane.

The questions for this section are with you now: the load path and effort path through the sketch, what to mark, and the error museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with one crane sketch and two checklists — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Advantage, Again

Mechanical advantage is load divided by effort. Lift 60 grams with 10 grams and it is six. The catch never changes: six times the force means six times the distance.

Lever: effort distance over load distance. Crank: handle over drum. Wheel and axle: wheel over axle. Pulleys: count the ropes holding the load. Gears: teeth on the driven over teeth on the driver.

The crane: 75 millimetre handle on a 12 millimetre drum is about six. Tip pulley is one. Total six. Add a movable pulley at the magnet and it is twelve. Write the chain on the sketch.

Keep the 60 grams and 10 grams in mind and try the questions for this part: the definition, each mechanism and the crane's total.

## Subtopic: Frames, Again

Frames fail three ways: members bend, joints fold, the whole thing tips.

Against bending, shape the card: tube, L, U or box. Against folding, triangulate: one diagonal turns a floppy rectangle into two stiff triangles, or glue a gusset across the corner. Against tipping: wide base, mast near the middle, cells at the back as a counterweight, nothing heavy up high.

Joints: overlap, tab or gusset, never a thin edge of glue. The arm-to-mast joint carries the most, so sleeve it and tie it back to the mast top.

Hold the diagonal across the rectangle in mind and try this part's questions: the three failures and their cures.

## Subtopic: Walk the Sketch

Start at the magnet and walk backwards: string, pulley, arm, joint, mast, base. At each stop ask: does it keep the advantage, and will it hold? Light pulley, tube arm braced to the mast, gusseted joint, tube or lattice mast, double base with weight at the back.

Start at the hand and walk forward: handle, drum, string, pulley, magnet. Total advantage six, effort equals load over six. Write both on the sketch.

Mark every stiffener in a second colour and keep the sketch in the folder.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Advantage is load over effort, traded for distance, and multiplies along a chain; the crane's is about six from the crank and one from the pulley. Frames are strengthened by shaped members, triangulation, good joints and a wide, weighted base. The crane is both, so check both on the sketch.

Hold the two walks, and take the final questions of the lesson: the paths, the marks and the summary.
