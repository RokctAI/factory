Today we hang a load on a string, pass the string over a wheel, and discover that pulling down is easier than lifting up even though the force is the same. A pulley is a grooved wheel on an axle with a rope in the groove, and it is a wheel and axle used to guide a rope. We test a single fixed pulley, which changes only the direction of the pull, and a movable pulley, which halves the effort, and we count the ropes to work out the advantage of any pulley system. Then we fit a pulley to the tip of the crane's arm. By the end you will be able to explain why a pulley is a wheel and axle, tell fixed from movable, and calculate what a block of pulleys does to the effort.
