Today we pull two threads from earlier in the year back into the crane. From term one, mechanical advantage: load over effort, the trade of distance for force, and the levers, cranks and pulleys that make it. From term two, strengthening frame structures: tubes, folds, triangulation, a wide base and a low centre of gravity. We put them side by side because a crane is both a mechanism and a structure, and a model that lifts well but folds under the load fails as surely as one that stands firm but cannot wind. By the end you will be able to calculate the crane's advantage from crank to magnet, and show on your sketch exactly where and how the frame will be stiffened.
