Hey — if fibres and fabrics sound like a list to memorise, here is the calm version: start with the danger, then ask what property beats it. Flame wants a fabric that chars but does not melt, an aramid. Cold water wants insulation that works wet, wool or fleece, plus a life jacket. Rain wants a coating or a membrane. Dark wants reflective tape.

Cotton burns and soaks, polyester melts, wool chars and warms, nylon is tough, aramids do not melt.

Then stack the layers: outer, middle, inner.

Open the practice set and picture the three layers of a fire jacket. You've got this!
