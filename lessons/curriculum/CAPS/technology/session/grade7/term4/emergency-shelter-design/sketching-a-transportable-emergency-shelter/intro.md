Today the shelter gets a shape. With the requirement list, the health and safety list and the textile brief in front of us, we sketch design ideas for a transportable emergency shelter for a family of six: a frame that stands in wind and packs flat, a cover of the specified textile that sheds rain and ties down, a floor, a door, vents and a stove place, all on one labelled freehand drawing per idea. We generate at least two ideas that differ in structure, a bent-rod dome and an A-frame or ridge tent for instance, borrowing from the hoop tent, the beehive hut and the tower crane's triangulation, we check each against the lists with ticks and crosses, and we choose one with reasons. By the end you will have the design sketches that the model shelter, the term's last practical, will be built from.
