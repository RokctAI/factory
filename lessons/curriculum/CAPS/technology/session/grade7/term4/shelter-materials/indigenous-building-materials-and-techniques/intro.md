Today we learn from the builders who have housed South African families for centuries without a hardware shop. The Zulu beehive hut, the Xhosa and Sotho rondavel, the Venda and Tsonga homestead and the Nama matjieshuis each solve the same problems your shelter must solve, rain, wind, heat, cold and fire, using what is within walking distance: grass, reeds, saplings, mud, dung, stone and rushes. We look at the materials, the techniques of framing, thatching, plastering and tying, and the reasons they work, and we ask what an emergency shelter designer can borrow. By the end you will be able to describe indigenous housing materials and methods, say why they are readily available, appropriate and environmentally friendly, and name ideas worth using in your own design.
