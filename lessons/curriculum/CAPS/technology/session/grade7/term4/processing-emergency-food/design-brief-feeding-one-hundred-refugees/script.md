# Part 1 — Expert

This session is the design stage of the emergency food task: a design brief with specifications for the types and quantities of food for one hundred refugees, and the ingredient list for a nutritious, tasty meal preparable in camp conditions. The anchor is a classroom in Kimberley where teams have the needs ranking, the camp food list, a supermarket price list and a calculator, and produce a one-page brief and a meal plan scaled to one hundred portions. The governing idea is that a design brief states what is to be provided, for whom and why, that specifications quantify energy, food groups, portions and adjustments for the mix of people, that constraints limit cost, fuel, water, storage and time, that scaling a recipe multiplies each ingredient by the number of portions and checks against bulk pack sizes, and that a meal is judged on nutrition, flavour, texture and practicality. Writing the brief and planning the meal are examinable and the meal is cooked in the next session.

## Subtopic: The Brief and Its Specifications

Begin with the brief, one or two sentences built as in term three: what, for whom, why. "Plan and supply one day's food for one hundred people displaced by flooding and housed in a community hall, including babies, children, adults and elderly people, providing a nutritious balanced diet that can be stored without refrigeration and cooked on gas burners in large pots with limited water, so that no one goes hungry or becomes ill from the food." Every phrase is traceable: displaced by flooding and the hall come from the scenario, the mix from week one, no refrigeration and gas burners from the camp conditions, nutritious and balanced from the food investigation.

Now the specifications, measurable and in order of importance. Energy: about 2 100 kilocalories per person per day, 210 000 for the camp, divided across a breakfast, a main meal and a lighter evening meal. Balance: every day includes a staple, a protein, a fat and a vegetable or fruit. Quantities, from the standard daily ration per person: 400 grams of cereal staple, 60 grams of beans or lentils or equivalent protein, 25 grams of oil, 15 grams of sugar, 5 grams of salt, with vegetables of 100 grams or more where available; for one hundred people that is 40 kilograms of maize meal or rice, 6 kilograms of pulses or equivalent in tinned fish, 2.5 litres of oil, 1.5 kilograms of sugar, half a kilogram of salt and 10 kilograms of vegetables. Adjustments: soft porridge enriched with milk powder and oil for the estimated ten children under five and five elderly people; rehydration salts available; no pork; rice available as an alternative staple for ten people. Safety: all food cooked or opened within two hours of serving; cooking and drinking water boiled or treated; hand washing before cooking and eating.

Then the constraints. Cost: a budget of, for example, R20 per person per day, R2 000 for the camp, which is the figure to check against the price list. Fuel: two gas burners and one open fire, so no more than two pots cooking at once and no dish longer than an hour. Water: 400 litres for cooking and washing up. Storage: a dry corner of the hall, no fridge, so nothing fresh kept beyond two days. Time: breakfast ready by seven, main meal by one, with four volunteer cooks. Equipment: two 50 litre pots, one 20 litre pot, ladles, a tin opener, knives, boards and bowls. Write specifications and constraints in two columns, as before, and check each against who it is about: the food, or the kitchen.

The questions for this section are with you now: the brief's three parts, the quantified specifications and the constraints.

## Subtopic: Planning One Nutritious, Tasty Meal

Choose the main meal of the day and build it from the four groups so that it is both nutritious and something people will want to eat. A good South African camp meal: pap, a tinned pilchard and tomato relish with onion, and a side of cabbage cooked with a little oil. Staple, protein, fat and two vegetables, familiar to most, no pork, quick, two pots. Alternatives: rice with a lentil, tomato and onion stew and cabbage; samp and beans with pumpkin; soya mince chakalaka with pap. For the soft-food group, take a portion of the pap thinner and stir in milk powder and a spoon of oil, and mash the pumpkin or cabbage.

Write the recipe for ten people first, because that is a size you can test at home or in a school kitchen: 1.2 kilograms of maize meal and 4 litres of water for stiff pap; 2 tins of pilchards in tomato sauce, 1 tin of tomatoes, 2 onions, a tablespoon of oil, salt and a pinch of curry powder for the relish; half a cabbage, an onion and a tablespoon of oil for the side. Then scale by ten for one hundred: 12 kilograms of maize meal, 40 litres of water, 20 tins of pilchards, 10 tins of tomatoes, 20 onions, 100 millilitres of oil, 5 cabbages. Check the scaled list against the pots: 40 litres of pap fills one 50 litre pot, the relish fits the 20 litre pot, the cabbage can follow in it. Check against time: pap 30 minutes once the water boils, relish 20 minutes, cabbage 10; the whole meal in under an hour on two burners. Check against water: 40 litres in the pap plus 20 for washing, well under the budget.

Judge the meal on three things, because that is how it will be evaluated next week. Nutrition: does each plate carry a staple, a protein, a fat and a vegetable, and roughly 700 kilocalories, a third of the day; pap 250 grams, relish 150 grams and cabbage 100 grams does. Flavour: onion, tomato, salt and a little spice turn plain pilchards into a relish people ask for again; bland food is left and wasted. Texture: stiff pap for adults, soft for children and the elderly, relish with some body, cabbage tender not slimy. Write these three as the evaluation criteria on the plan, so that next week's tasting is a test and not a chat.

The questions for this section are with you now: a four-group meal, scaling from ten to one hundred and the three judging criteria.

## Subtopic: Costing, Checking and Presenting the Plan

Cost the meal from the price list. Maize meal at about R10 a kilogram, 12 kilograms, R120. Pilchards at about R20 a tin, 20 tins, R400. Tinned tomatoes at R15, 10 tins, R150. Onions at R3 each, 20, R60. Cabbage at R15 a head, 5, R75. Oil, salt and spice, R30. Total about R835 for the main meal of one hundred people, R8.35 a plate. Add a breakfast of soft porridge with sugar and milk powder, roughly R3 a person, and an evening meal of bread or rice with lentil soup, roughly R6, and the day comes to about R17 to R18 a person, inside the R20 constraint with a little left for tea, fuel and the rehydration salts. If the total had come to R25, the design would change: more beans and less tinned fish, cabbage in place of some tomato, or a smaller breakfast.

Check the plan once more against the mix. Children under five: soft enriched porridge at breakfast, thin pap and mashed cabbage at lunch. Elderly: the same soft options. Pregnant and breastfeeding women: an extra tin of pilchards shared among them. The sick: rehydration drink and soft porridge. Muslim families: the meal has no pork. Rice eaters: 1 kilogram of rice cooked alongside for ten. Then check it against the kitchen: two pots at once, under an hour, four cooks, water in budget, nothing stored fresh beyond two days, pilchards and tomatoes opened just before cooking.

Present the plan on one page in the design folder: the brief at the top; the two columns of specifications and constraints; the meal with its recipe for ten and its scaled list for one hundred; the cost table; the three evaluation criteria; and the adjustments for the mix. This page is the design product the PAT marks, and it is the shopping list and recipe card for next week's cooking. A second team member reads it as if they were the cook arriving at the hall at six in the morning: can they buy it, store it, cook it in time and serve everyone? Every question they cannot answer is a gap to fill.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: a brief that says "feed the refugees" with no numbers, so nothing can be checked. Two: a meal that is delicious but needs an oven, a fridge and three hours. Three: scaling the recipe for one hundred by guessing rather than multiplying, so the pap runs out at plate seventy. Four: costing only the main meal and claiming the day is within budget.

The questions for this section are with you now: costing against the budget, the final checks and the one-page plan.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with a one-page brief, a recipe and a calculator — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Write the Brief

One or two sentences: feed one hundred flood-displaced people in a hall, babies to grandparents, a balanced diet with no fridge, on gas, with little water, so nobody is hungry or ill.

Specifications with numbers: 2 100 kilocalories each; staple, protein, fat and vegetable every day; 400 grams of staple, 60 grams of protein, 25 grams of oil, 100 grams of vegetables each; soft food for the young and old; no pork; rice for some; everything cooked within two hours of serving.

Constraints: R20 a head, two burners and a fire, 400 litres of water, no fridge, meals by seven and one, four cooks, two big pots. Two columns.

Keep the two columns in mind and try the questions for this part: the brief, the numbers and the limits.

## Subtopic: Plan the Meal

Pap, pilchard and tomato relish with onion, cabbage on the side. Staple, protein, fat, two vegetables, no pork, two pots, under an hour.

Write it for ten: 1.2 kilograms of meal, 2 tins of pilchards, 1 tin of tomatoes, 2 onions, half a cabbage, oil, salt, spice. Multiply by ten for a hundred: 12 kilograms, 20 tins, 10 tins, 20 onions, 5 cabbages. Check it fits the pots and the hour.

Judge it on nutrition, flavour and texture. Stiff pap for adults, soft with milk powder for children and the old.

Hold the recipe for ten in mind and try this part's questions: the meal, the scaling and the three criteria.

## Subtopic: Cost It and Check It

Meal: R120 for meal, R400 for pilchards, R150 for tomatoes, R60 onions, R75 cabbage, R30 extras, about R835, R8.35 a plate. With breakfast and supper, about R18 a day, inside R20. Over budget? More beans, less fish.

Check the mix one more time: soft food, extra protein for mothers, rehydration for the sick, no pork, rice for ten. Check the kitchen: two pots, an hour, four cooks, water.

One page: brief, two columns, recipe for ten and for a hundred, cost table, three criteria, adjustments. Let a team mate read it as the morning cook.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The brief says what, for whom and why. Specifications give the energy, groups, grams and adjustments; constraints give money, fuel, water, storage, time and pots. Plan a four-group meal, write it for ten, multiply for one hundred, check pots, time and water, cost it against the budget, and judge it on nutrition, flavour and texture.

Hold the one-page plan, and take the final questions of the lesson: costing, checks and the summary.
