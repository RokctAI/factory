Today we investigate what food can actually be supplied to a refugee camp. Not what we would like to eat, but what can be bought in bulk, moved on a truck, stored in a tent for weeks without a fridge, cooked in a big pot on a fire with little water, and eaten by a baby, a teenager and a grandmother. We sort foods into those that keep and those that spoil, we see how processing, drying, milling, tinning, fermenting, turns a fresh food into one that travels, and we build the list of staples, proteins, fats and vegetables that a camp kitchen can rely on. By the end you will be able to say which foods suit a camp and why, and name the processing that makes them suitable.
