Today we set the task that the rest of the term builds toward: design and make a model of a sturdy, waterproof, easy-to-erect shelter for a family of six for a month. We take the scenarios from last lesson and the needs from week one and turn them into the requirements the shelter must meet, space, weather, structure, erection, transport and duration, and then we spend the second half of the lesson on the requirements that are easiest to forget and most dangerous to miss: health and safety. Fire inside and between shelters, ventilation and smoke, water and sanitation around the shelter, security and privacy, and the hazards of the site itself. By the end you will have the requirement list that your shelter brief and your sketches must satisfy.
