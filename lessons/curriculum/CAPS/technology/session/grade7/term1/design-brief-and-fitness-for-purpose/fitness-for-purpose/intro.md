Today we ask the designer's hardest question: is it fit for its purpose? A product can work and still fail, because it costs too much, hurts the user, looks wrong or harms the people around it. We take a real rescue tool, a school chair and a paraffin stove and judge each one against eight design considerations: users, purpose, function, cost-effectiveness, safety, ergonomics, aesthetics, and impact on society and the environment. We finish by applying all eight to our own rescue device so that the brief we wrote covers more than just whether the jaws open.
