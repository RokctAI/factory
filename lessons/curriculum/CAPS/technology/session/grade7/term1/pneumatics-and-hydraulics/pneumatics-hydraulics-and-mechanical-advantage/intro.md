Today we learn how a push can travel along a tube. Pneumatics uses air, hydraulics uses a liquid, and both move force from one place to another and can multiply it on the way. We start at a taxi rank, where the bus doors hiss open on compressed air and a mechanic lifts a whole minibus on a hydraulic jack. We find out why air is springy and liquid is not, what pressure means, and how a narrow piston pushing on a wide one gives a mechanical advantage without a single lever. By the end you will know why the Jaws of Life is hydraulic and not pneumatic.
