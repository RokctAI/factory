Hey — if load, effort, pivot and mechanical advantage feel like a tangle, here is the calm version: picture one bar lifting one rock.

The stone under the bar is the pivot. The rock is the load, the thing you move. Your push is the effort.

Mechanical advantage is just load divided by effort. If the rock is 800 newtons and you push with 100, the MA is 8. The lever made you eight times stronger.

Why? Your push is far from the pivot and the rock is close to it. Far from the pivot means more help. That is all.

The catch: your hands move far and the rock moves a little. Force for distance. No free energy.

Open the practice set and try two MA sums; they are just division. You've got this!
