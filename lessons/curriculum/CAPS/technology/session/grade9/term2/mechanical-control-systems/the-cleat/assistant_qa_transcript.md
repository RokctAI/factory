### subtopic_1 — What a Cleat Is and How It Holds a Rope Without a Knot
**Assistant:** Why does wrapping a rope round a cleat hold it when the same rope would slide straight off a smooth post?

**Tutor:** It holds because friction is not a one-off thing; every part of the rope that touches the cleat adds its own share, and the figure-of-eight makes the rope touch a lot of cleat while bending sharply. Each time the rope passes round a horn, friction takes up a fraction of the tension, so the load in the rope drops with every turn. After a round turn and two figure-of-eights the tension in the free end can be a small fraction of the load on the boat, small enough to hold with two fingers or to tuck under the last horn. A smooth post gives only one straight contact with no bends, so friction takes up almost nothing and the rope slides. The cleat's horns force the rope into bends and the bends are what make friction work.

### subtopic_2 — Types of Cleat: Horn, Cam and Jam Cleats
**Assistant:** The cam cleat sounds like the ratchet from the last lesson. Is it really the same idea?

**Tutor:** It is the same principle wearing a different shape. In a ratchet, a pawl rides over sloping tooth faces one way and jams against steep faces the other way. In a cam cleat, each cam is a toothed piece pivoted so that when the rope moves inward the cam swings away and lets it pass, and when the rope tries to move outward the teeth catch the rope and the cam swings toward it, pinching harder. The spring holds the cams against the rope just as the ratchet spring holds the pawl on the wheel. The difference is that the rope is soft and the cams grip it directly, so the rope must be the right diameter and in good condition; a thin or glazed rope slips through the teeth, which a steel ratchet wheel never does.

### subtopic_3 — Where Cleats Are Used and How to Evaluate Them
**Assistant:** Why not just tie a good knot instead of fitting a cleat?

**Tutor:** A knot works, and sailors use knots all the time, but under a changing load a knot is the wrong tool for three reasons. First, speed: securing a rope on a horn cleat takes two seconds and releasing it takes one, while tying and untying a bowline or a round turn with hitches takes much longer, and on a boat coming alongside in wind those seconds matter. Second, release under load: a knot that has been pulled tight by a boat surging in a swell, especially a wet one, can be nearly impossible to untie until the load comes off, which may not be possible; a cleat releases under full load. Third, adjustment: a cleat lets you take in or let out a few centimetres and re-secure instantly, which a knot does not. Knots are for joining and for loads that will not change; cleats are for loads that must be held and released repeatedly.
