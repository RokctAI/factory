Today we begin mechanical control systems, the parts of a machine that do not create movement but govern it: letting it happen one way, stopping it, or holding it. The first is the ratchet and pawl. A ratchet is a wheel with sloped teeth; a pawl is a small hinged arm that drops into the teeth. The wheel can turn one way, lifting the pawl over each tooth with a click, but when it tries to turn back the pawl jams against a tooth face and holds it. This is what stops a winch handle from spinning back, holds a car jack up and lets a socket spanner tighten a nut without being lifted off. We look at how it works, where it is used, and how to draw and evaluate one.
