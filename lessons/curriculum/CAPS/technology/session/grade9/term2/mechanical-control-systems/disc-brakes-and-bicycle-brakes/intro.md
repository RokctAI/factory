Today's control systems stop things. A brake takes a moving wheel and slows it by friction, turning the energy of motion into heat. The disc brake on a car or motorbike does this with a steel disc that spins with the wheel and a pair of pads that squeeze it, pushed by hydraulic pressure from the brake pedal, which brings back Pascal's principle from earlier in the term. The bicycle brake does it with a lever, a cable and two rubber blocks that pinch the wheel rim, a lever system from Grade 7. We see how each works, why brakes get hot, and how to judge a brake for safety and fitness for purpose.
