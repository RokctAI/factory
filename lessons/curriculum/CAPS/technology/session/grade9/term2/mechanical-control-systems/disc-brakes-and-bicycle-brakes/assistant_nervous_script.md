Hey — if brakes feel like car mechanic territory, here is the calm version: a brake is friction on purpose.

Disc brake: a steel plate spins with the wheel. Press the pedal, fluid pushes two pads onto the plate. Friction slows it. Small piston at the pedal, big pistons at the pads, so your foot's push gets multiplied. That is Pascal from a few weeks ago.

Bicycle brake: squeeze the lever, pull a cable, two rubber blocks pinch the rim. Levers multiply your squeeze.

All the stopping becomes heat. That is why discs glow.

To judge a brake: does it stop every time, wet or dry, can it fail quietly, can you reach it, what does it cost.

Draw the chain from your hand all the way to the heat. That is the lesson.
