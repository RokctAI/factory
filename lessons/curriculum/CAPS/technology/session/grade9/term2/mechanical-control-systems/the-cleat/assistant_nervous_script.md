Hey — if cleats feel like something only sailors know, here is the calm version: a cleat is a rope holder you can let go of in a second.

Wrap the rope round it, or pinch it in jaws, or wedge it in a slot. Friction holds. The harder the rope pulls, the harder it holds. Flick it off and the rope runs.

It adds no force. The boat or the wind pulls. The cleat just holds.

Three kinds: horn, with figure-of-eight wraps; cam, with toothed jaws like a ratchet; jam, with a tapered slot.

Judge it: holds at the load, right rope size, no accidental release, one hand, price.

Look at the flagpole at school. There is a cleat on it.
