The last control system of the week is the cleat, a fitting that holds a rope under load and lets it go again in a moment. On a boat the cleat holds the mooring line to the jetty; on a flagpole it holds the halyard that keeps the flag up; on a washing line post it holds the line taut. The cleat works by friction and jamming, not by knots, which is why a sailor can release a loaded rope with one hand in a second. We look at the horn cleat, the cam cleat and the jam cleat, see how each grips, and judge them for fitness for purpose and safety.
