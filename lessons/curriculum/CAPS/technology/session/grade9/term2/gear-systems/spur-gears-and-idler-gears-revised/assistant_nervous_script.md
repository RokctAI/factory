Hey — if gear ratios feel like a trap, here is the calm version: count teeth, divide, and say which way.

Two gears meshing turn opposite ways. Always.

Ratio is driven teeth over driver teeth. 10 drives 30: 3 to 1. Output turns slower, a third of the speed, with three times the turning force. Flip it, 30 drives 10: three times faster, a third of the force.

Speed and force trade. You never get both.

An idler in the middle flips the direction back and changes nothing else. Its teeth cancel.

Two gears on one shaft is a compound train, and then ratios multiply.

Draw it, arrow every gear, count the teeth. That is all of it.
