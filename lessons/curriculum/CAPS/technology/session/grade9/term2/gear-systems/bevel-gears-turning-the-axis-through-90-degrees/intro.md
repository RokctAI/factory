Spur gears pass motion between parallel shafts. Today's gears pass it round a corner. Bevel gears have teeth cut on a cone instead of a cylinder, so two of them mesh with their shafts at right angles: a horizontal shaft turning a vertical one, or the other way round. Everything from spur gears still applies, teeth counting, ratios, speed and force trading, but now the axis of rotation is turned through 90 degrees. We meet them in the hand drill, the eggbeater, the differential of every bakkie and the mechanism of a windmill, and we practise drawing and calculating with them.
