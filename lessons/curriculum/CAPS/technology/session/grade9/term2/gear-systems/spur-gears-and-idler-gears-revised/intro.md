This week is gears, and we start by revising what Grade 8 established about spur gears, the plain toothed wheels on parallel shafts. Two meshing gears turn in opposite directions; the ratio of their teeth sets how speed and force change between them; a small gear driving a large one slows the output and increases its turning force, and the reverse speeds it up and reduces force. Then we add the idler, a third gear placed between two others, which restores the original direction without changing the ratio. We count teeth, calculate ratios and build trains with cardboard gears or a kit.
