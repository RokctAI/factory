Today we combine the fixed and the movable pulley into the block and tackle, the lifting machine found on cranes, sailing boats, workshops and tow trucks across South Africa. A block and tackle has a fixed block above, a movable block on the load, and one rope threaded back and forth between them. The rule is simple and powerful: the mechanical advantage equals the number of rope sections that hold the load up. Two sections give 2, three give 3, four give 4, and each extra section means pulling that much more rope for the same lift. We build tackles, count ropes, measure effort and see how far the measured value falls below the ideal.
