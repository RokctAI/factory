Today the pulley moves. Instead of fixing the pulley to the beam, we fix one end of the rope to the beam, hang the load from the pulley itself, and pull up on the free end. Now the load is held by two sections of rope, one on each side of the pulley, and each carries only half the weight, so the effort is halved: the mechanical advantage is 2. The price, as always, is distance, since the rope must be pulled twice as far as the load rises. We measure it, explain it by counting rope sections, and see the awkward direction of pull that the next session will fix.
