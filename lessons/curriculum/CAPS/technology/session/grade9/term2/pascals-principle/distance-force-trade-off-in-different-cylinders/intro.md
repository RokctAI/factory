Today we make the trade-off between force and distance exact. When a master plunger moves, it pushes out a volume of liquid, and that same volume must arrive in the slave cylinder. In a wide cylinder that volume is a short extension; in a narrow one it is a long extension. We learn to calculate the extension from the areas, see that the distance ratio is the inverse of the force ratio, and tie both to mechanical advantage: a mechanical advantage above 1 means strong and slow, below 1 means fast and weak, and never both at once.
