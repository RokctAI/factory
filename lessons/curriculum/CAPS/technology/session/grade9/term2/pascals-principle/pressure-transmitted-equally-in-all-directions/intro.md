Today we meet the law behind every hydraulic machine: Pascal's principle. First we pin down what pressure is, a force spread over an area, measured in pascals, and see why a sharp point hurts and a flat palm does not. Then we state the principle: pressure applied to a confined liquid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the liquid and to the walls of its container, in all directions. We see it in a bottle with holes, a squeezed balloon and the brake system of a car, where one pedal pushes four wheels equally.
