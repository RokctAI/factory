Hey — if the exam has you worried about hydraulics, here is the calm version: three facts and one sum.

Fact one: gases squash, liquids do not. That explains spongy brakes and why jacks use oil.

Fact two: pressure in a trapped liquid is the same everywhere. So force over area is the same both sides. Big area, big force.

Fact three: the volume moved is the same both sides. So the big piston moves less. Force up, distance down, work the same.

The sum: pressure from the known side, apply it to the other side, check the big area has the big force.

The jack is that sum with a pump handle and valves. The systems diagram is boxes and arrows, not a picture.
