### subtopic_1 — Revising Pulleys: Fixed, Movable and Block and Tackle
**Assistant:** The exam might describe a mechanism without naming it. How do I work out which one it is?

**Tutor:** Ask three questions of the description, in order, and the answer usually falls out. First, does it supply or change motion, or does it govern motion? If it allows, stops or holds, it is a control: one direction only is a ratchet, slows by friction is a brake, holds a rope and releases quickly is a cleat. If it changes speed, force or the kind of motion, it is a mechanism. Second, for mechanisms, is the medium rope, liquid or teeth? Rope means pulleys; liquid means hydraulics; teeth means gears. Third, for gears, what happens to the axis and the motion? Parallel shafts, spur; axis turned 90 degrees, bevel; straight-line output, rack and pinion; huge reduction and cannot be driven backwards, worm. Underline the clue words in the question, hold, one way, stops, cone, straight, right angle, and match them. Then name the mechanism, state its rule and give one application, which is usually the three parts of the marks.

### subtopic_2 — Revising Control Systems: Ratchet, Brakes and Cleat
**Assistant:** Why is the pulley rule about ropes and the gear rule about teeth? Is there one idea behind both?

**Tutor:** Yes: in both, the input and the output are connected by something that cannot stretch or slip, so what goes in must come out, and the rule is just counting how the connection shares the work. In a pulley system the connection is one rope with one tension; the load is shared by however many sections hold it, so effort is load divided by the section count, and the rope must shorten in every section, so distance is multiplied by the same count. In gears the connection is teeth passing the meshing point; every tooth of the driver moves one tooth of the driven, so turns are shared in the ratio of the tooth counts, and torque is shared the other way round. Both are really the same statement: work in equals work out, so force times distance is fixed, and the count tells you how the trade is split. Hydraulics does it with volume, pulleys with rope sections, gears with teeth.

### subtopic_3 — Revising Gears: Spur, Idler, Bevel, Rack and Worm
**Assistant:** How should I split my time between explaining and calculating in the exam for this section?

**Tutor:** Read the mark allocation and the verb. A one or two mark question that says name, state or identify wants a single term or a one-line rule, nothing more: ratchet and pawl; driven over driver; advantage equals load-bearing ropes. A three or four mark question that says explain or describe wants the rule plus the reason plus an example, so three short sentences, not a paragraph. A calculation question wants the formula written, the numbers substituted, the answer with its unit, and for pulleys and gears a statement of direction or of what happened to speed and force; show the working, because a correct method with a slip in arithmetic still earns most of the marks and a bare wrong number earns none. Do the short recall questions first to bank marks, then the calculations, then the longer explanations. Draw a quick labelled sketch whenever the question allows it, because an arrowed sketch of a tackle or gear train often earns a mark on its own and keeps your counting honest.
