Hey — if the transistor feels like the point where electronics gets hard, here is the calm version: it is a switch that a trickle of current closes.

Three legs. Collector to the load and positive. Emitter to negative. Base is the control.

A tiny current into the base lets a big current through collector to emitter. No base current, nothing flows.

Always put a resistor on the base. Tens of kilohms.

Hang a sensor on the base, an LDR or probes, and the sensor decides when the LED lights.

As an amplifier it does the same thing gently: small wobble in, big wobble out, same shape.

Check the legs on the card. That is the one thing that goes wrong.
