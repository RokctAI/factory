A sensor is a component whose electrical behaviour changes with something in the world: light, temperature, touch, wetness. Today's sensors are all resistors of a kind. The light-dependent resistor, LDR, has a resistance that falls as light increases, from about a megohm in darkness to a few hundred ohms in sunlight. The thermistor's resistance changes with temperature, falling as it warms for the common negative type and rising for the positive type. Touch and moisture detectors are simply two contacts bridged by skin or damp soil, which conduct a little. Each of these feeds the base of a transistor, and each gives a PAT device its senses. We learn how they behave, how to test them and how to choose.
