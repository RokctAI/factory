The last new component of the term stores energy for a moment and gives it back. A capacitor is two metal plates separated by an insulator; connect it to a battery and charge builds up on the plates until the capacitor's voltage matches the battery's; disconnect the battery and connect a lamp or LED and the stored charge flows out, lighting it briefly. The bigger the capacitor and the bigger the resistor it charges through, the longer the charging and discharging take, which is how a capacitor makes a timer. We learn the symbol, the unit, the farad and its tiny fractions, the difference between small ceramic and large electrolytic types, and the polarity rule that keeps the big ones from bursting.
