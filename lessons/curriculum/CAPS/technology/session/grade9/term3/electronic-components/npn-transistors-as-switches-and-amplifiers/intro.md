Until now every switch has been closed by a hand, a float or a magnet. Today's component is a switch closed by a tiny electric current: the npn transistor. It has three legs, the base, the collector and the emitter, and a small current flowing into the base lets a much larger current flow from collector to emitter. Feed the base from a sensor whose output is too weak to light an LED or sound a buzzer, and the transistor does the heavy work. The same property, small current controlling large, makes the transistor an amplifier, the heart of every radio and hearing aid. We learn the symbol, the legs, the switch circuit and the amplifier idea, and the precautions that keep transistors alive.
