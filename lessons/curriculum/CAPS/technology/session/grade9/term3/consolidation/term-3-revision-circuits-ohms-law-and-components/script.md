# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the electrical and electronic systems content of Term 3 for the examination. The anchor is a revision wall in a classroom in Mahikeng covered with the term's diagrams in order: the symbol chart, the series and parallel lamps, the AND and OR switch circuits, the Ohm's law graph, the LED with its resistor, the resistor colour strip, the four switches, the transistor switch, the sensor divider, the capacitor and the two standard circuits, each with its one-line rule written beneath. The governing idea is that the term's content rests on a small set of rules, that current needs a complete loop, that series shares voltage and parallel shares current, that V equals I times R for a resistor, that each component has one job and one rule, and that these rules are applied by drawing, tracing, calculating and explaining. Recalling and applying the term's rules in examination questions is the purpose of this session.

## Subtopic: Revising Circuits: Symbols, Series, Parallel and Logic

Circuits need a complete path from the positive terminal through components to the negative terminal; a gap anywhere stops the current. Diagrams use standard symbols so anyone can build them: cell, battery, lamp, switch, resistor, variable resistor, LED, diode, buzzer, motor, transistor, LDR, thermistor, capacitor, with wires straight and junctions dotted. Conventional current is drawn from positive to negative, and polarised components, the battery, diode, LED, electrolytic capacitor and transistor, are drawn the right way round.

Series: one path, the same current through every component, cells adding voltage, lamps and resistors sharing the supply voltage in proportion to resistance, one failure stopping all. Parallel: several paths, each branch getting the full voltage, branch currents adding, cells in parallel sharing current without adding voltage, branches independent. Switches in series give AND logic, output only when all are closed, the safety interlock; switches in parallel give OR logic, output when any is closed, the alarm. Two switches give a four-row truth table.

Examination questions on this strand ask for a diagram drawn from a description, a prediction of brightness or behaviour, a truth table, or an explanation of why house lights are parallel and torch cells are series. The method is the same each time: draw with symbols, trace the loop with a finger, count the paths, and say what happens. The common errors are pictures instead of symbols, missing junction dots, switches drawn closed, and expecting two series lamps to be as bright as one.

The questions for this section are with you now: the complete loop and symbols, series and parallel rules, and AND and OR logic.

## Subtopic: Revising Ohm's Law: Measuring, Graphing and Calculating

Three quantities: voltage in volts, the push, measured across a component with a voltmeter in parallel; current in amperes, the flow, measured in the loop with an ammeter in series, never across a supply; resistance in ohms, the opposition, read from colour bands or found as volts over amps. Ohm's law: at constant temperature the current through a resistor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, V equals I times R. The three forms, I equals V over R and R equals V over I, with milliamperes converted to amperes before substituting.

The investigation: a 100 ohm resistor with one, two and three cells, voltmeter across and ammeter in line, readings about 1.45 volts and 14.5 milliamperes, 2.9 and 29, 4.4 and 44, repeated and tabulated with a volts over amps column near 100. The graph: voltage vertical, current horizontal, labelled axes, even scales from zero, points as crosses, one straight line of best fit through the origin, gradient as rise over run giving the resistance, compared with the marked value, and a three-sentence conclusion. The straight line through the origin is direct proportionality; a lamp curves, a diode is offset.

Calculations: five lines each. Series resistors add and share voltage; parallel branches take the full voltage and their currents add, with the combined resistance below the smallest. The LED resistor: supply minus 2 volts, over the current, 250 ohms for 10 milliamperes on 4.5 volts, so 270 or the kit's 470. Battery life: capacity over current, and a device that draws nothing in standby lasts for months. Common errors: unconverted milliamperes, the battery voltage used for the resistor when an LED is present, Ohm's law applied to the LED itself, and the law stated without constant temperature.

The questions for this section are with you now: the quantities and meters, the investigation and its graph, and the calculations with their errors.

## Subtopic: Revising Components: Diodes, LEDs, Resistors, Switches, Transistors, Sensors and Capacitors

Diode: conducts one way, anode toward positive, blocks reversed, 0.7 volts drop, used in rectifiers and for protection; symbol a triangle pointing at a bar. LED: a diode that lights, 2 volts drop, long leg anode, always with a series resistor of a few hundred ohms, its own resistor if in parallel. Resistor colour code: two digits and a number of zeros from black 0 to white 9, tolerance band gold 5 percent or silver 10; yellow violet brown is 470. Switches: push-to-make for a momentary test button, SPST for on-off, SPDT to change over between two paths, DPDT to reverse a motor; chosen by poles, throws, action and rating.

Transistor: npn with collector, base and emitter, a small base current letting a current about a hundred times larger flow from collector to emitter, the load in the collector circuit, the emitter to negative, always a base resistor; a switch for sensors and, driven gently, an amplifier that enlarges a signal using the supply's energy. Sensors: the LDR's resistance falls as light rises; the NTC thermistor's falls as it warms and the PTC's rises; touch and moisture detectors are two contacts bridged by skin or water; each in a divider feeding the base, sensor above for on-when-active, below for the inverse. Capacitor: two plates storing charge, farads in micro, nano and pico, charging and discharging through a resistor in a time of resistance times capacitance, electrolytics polarised with a stripe on the negative lead.

The two standard circuits: a 4.5 volt battery, switch, 470 ohm resistor and LED in series, about 5 milliamperes; an LDR and resistor divider feeding a transistor that drives a buzzer on 3 volts, sounding when light falls. And the full circuit: thermistor and variable resistor divider, base resistor, transistor, LED and resistor, capacitor from base to negative for smoothing and delay, switch in the supply. The error museum, four exhibits for the term. One: an LED or electrolytic capacitor connected backwards. Two: a transistor with no base resistor or legs in the wrong order. Three: a sensor expected to drive a load directly. Four: a circuit explained as a whole without naming each component's job.

The questions for this section are with you now: diodes, LEDs, codes and switches in a line each, the transistor, sensors and capacitor, and the standard circuits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again walking along the revision wall in Mahikeng, one diagram at a time — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: One Loop or Many, Both or Either

Current needs a complete loop from plus to minus. Standard symbols so anyone can build it. Dots where wires join. Current drawn plus to minus. Polarised parts the right way round.

Series: one path, same current, cells add, lamps share the volts, one fails and all go out. Parallel: many paths, full volts each, currents add, branches independent. Switches in series: AND, both needed. In parallel: OR, either will do. Four-row table.

Exam method: draw with symbols, trace the loop, count the paths, say what happens. Avoid pictures, missing dots, closed switches, bright series lamps.

Keep one loop or many, both or either in mind and try the questions for this part: circuits and logic.

## Subtopic: Volts, Amps, Ohms and the Straight Line

Volts across with a voltmeter. Amps in the loop with an ammeter, never across the battery. Ohms from the bands or volts over amps. Ohm's law: V equals I times R, if nothing heats up. Convert milliamps first.

The practical: 100 ohms, one, two, three cells, about 1.45 and 14.5, 2.9 and 29, 4.4 and 44, volts over amps near 100. The graph: volts up, amps across, crosses, one straight line through zero, gradient is the resistance, three-sentence conclusion.

Sums in five lines. Series adds and shares volts. Parallel takes full volts and adds currents. LED resistor: supply minus 2, over the current. Battery life: capacity over current; zero in standby means months.

Hold volts, amps, ohms and the straight line in mind and try this part's questions: Ohm's law.

## Subtopic: Every Part and Its One Job

Diode: one way, triangle to bar. LED: a diode that lights, 2 volts, long leg to plus, always its own resistor. Colour code: two digits and zeros, yellow violet brown is 470, gold is 5 percent. Switches: push-to-make, SPST, SPDT, DPDT; count poles and throws.

Transistor: small base current, big collector current, load on the collector, emitter to minus, always a base resistor; a switch for sensors, an amplifier when driven gently. Sensors: LDR drops in light, NTC drops in heat, PTC rises, probes bridged by water; sensor above or below in the divider. Capacitor: a bucket for charge, ohms times farads is seconds, stripe on minus.

Two standard circuits: battery, switch, 470, LED. LDR divider, transistor, buzzer on 3 volts. The full one adds a thermistor, a knob, and a capacitor on the base.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Current needs a complete loop drawn with standard symbols; series shares voltage and parallel shares current; series switches are AND and parallel switches are OR. Ohm's law gives V equals I times R at constant temperature, measured with a voltmeter across and an ammeter in line, graphed as a straight line through the origin whose gradient is the resistance, and calculated in five lines with units converted. Each component has one job: the diode passes one way, the LED lights and needs a resistor, the transistor lets a small current control a large one, sensors change resistance with the world, the capacitor stores charge for a time.

Hold every part and its one job, and take the final questions of the lesson: components and circuits.
