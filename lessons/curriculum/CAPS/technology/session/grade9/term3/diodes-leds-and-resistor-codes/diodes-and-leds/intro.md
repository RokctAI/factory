Today we meet the first component that cares which way round it goes. A diode lets current pass in one direction and blocks it in the other, like a one-way valve in a pipe, which is why a diode in a battery charger stops the battery discharging back and why a diode across a motor protects a circuit. The light-emitting diode, the LED, is a diode that glows when current passes the right way, and it is the indicator in almost every modern device, including the PAT tank warning. LEDs are efficient and long-lived but fragile: they need a resistor in series to limit the current, or they burn out in a flash. We learn the symbol, the legs, the polarity and the reason for the resistor.
