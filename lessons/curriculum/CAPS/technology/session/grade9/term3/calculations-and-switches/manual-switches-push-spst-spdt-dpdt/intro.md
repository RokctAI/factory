Every circuit this term has a switch, and today we sort the manual switches out by what they can do. A switch is a set of metal contacts that are either touching or apart. A push switch makes contact only while pressed; a single-pole single-throw toggle, SPST, simply opens or closes one path; a single-pole double-throw, SPDT, connects one wire to either of two others, so it can choose between two lamps or two circuits; and a double-pole double-throw, DPDT, does that for two wires at once, which is how a motor is reversed. We learn the symbols, the names, where each is used, and which one the PAT device needs for its silence switch and test button.
