### subtopic_1 — Planning a Five-Minute Presentation: Story, Roles and Timing
**Assistant:** What do we do if the LED does not light during the demonstration?

**Tutor:** You say so, calmly, and you keep going, because a presentation is judged on how the team handles reality as much as on the model. The presenter says plainly that the LED is not lighting now, shows the test record page where it lit yesterday at ten metres outdoors, and offers the likely cause: a battery clip loosened in the bag, a dry joint, a float that has stuck. If a quick check is possible without eating the time, reseating the battery clip for example, do it once; if it works, continue, and if not, move on to the costing and evaluation. The audience and the examiner have seen the drawings, the circuit traced on the diagram and the written tests, so they know the device was designed and built properly; what they learn from the failure is whether the team understands its own device well enough to say what went wrong. A team that hides it, fiddles for two minutes, or blames each other loses far more than a team that explains it in three sentences and carries on.

### subtopic_2 — Showing the Sketches, Impressions, Plans, Costing and Model
**Assistant:** How specific does my contribution record really need to be?

**Tutor:** Specific enough that a teacher who was not in the room could find your work in the portfolio and see that it is yours. The test is whether each sentence points at a page or a part. I drew Sketch B, the scissor platform, in week 3 points at a named sketch. I built and tested the LDR divider on the breadboard and recorded the three LDR readings in week 6 points at a results table with your name. I drilled the 5 millimetre LED hole and the switch hole in the lid points at the model. I compiled the comparison table and wrote the justification paragraph points at the Design pages. Each of those can be checked. I helped with the design cannot be checked and earns almost nothing. The record is not the place for modesty or for exaggeration; it is a plain list of what you did, when, and what you learnt from it, and the team reading each other's records before handing in is how the plain truth gets agreed.

### subtopic_3 — Delivering It Well and Recording Each Learner's Contribution
**Assistant:** Why does the presentation include the cost when the model is already built?

**Tutor:** Because the device is meant for a real household with a real budget, and the brief made cost a constraint, so the audience needs to know whether the team met it. The costing is the parts list with the price of each item and the total, compared with the budget written in the brief. If the total is under, say so and say what the margin was spent on or saved. If it is over, say by how much and why, for instance a high-brightness LED cost more than planned to pass the visibility specification, and what the team would do about it in a real product. Showing the cost honestly also connects the presentation back to Term 1, where the tender for the ramp was judged partly on its budget, and it tells the examiner that the team understands that a design is not finished when it works; it is finished when it works at a price the user can pay. One slide or page, thirty seconds, and the story is complete.
