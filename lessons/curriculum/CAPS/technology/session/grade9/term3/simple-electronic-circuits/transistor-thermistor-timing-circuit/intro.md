Today's circuit brings the term's components together: a thermistor senses temperature, a variable resistor sets the temperature at which the circuit acts, a transistor does the switching, an LED shows the result, a capacitor adds a delay so the LED does not flicker as the temperature hovers, and a switch turns the whole thing on. It is a temperature alarm, a frost warning or an overheating indicator depending on how the sensor is arranged. We draw it to standard with every value, trace it to understand each part's job, build it on a breadboard, set its threshold with warm fingers and a cold cloth, and see how the same circuit becomes a light alarm or a moisture alarm by swapping the sensor.
