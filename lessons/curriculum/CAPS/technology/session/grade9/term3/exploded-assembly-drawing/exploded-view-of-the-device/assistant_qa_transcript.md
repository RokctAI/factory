### subtopic_1 — What an Exploded View Is and Why Assembly Needs One
**Assistant:** Why keep the parts in a straight line? It would be easier to spread them out to fit the page.

**Tutor:** Because the line is the information. An exploded view is not a picture of the parts; it is a picture of how they go together, and the straight axis with its alignment lines is what tells the reader that the board drops into the base, the lid closes onto the board, and this screw goes into that hole. Spread the parts around the page and you have a parts catalogue, which shows what exists but not where it goes, and the reader has to guess the assembly from the shapes. If the device is tall and will not fit, reduce the scale, shorten the gaps between parts to about half a part height, or let a part that comes off sideways explode along a second horizontal axis from its hole so it uses the width of the page. The rule is that every part sits on an axis that leads back to its place, so that following the lines reassembles the device in the reader's mind.

### subtopic_2 — Drawing the Device Exploded: Axis, Order and Alignment Lines
**Assistant:** How detailed should the circuit board be? Do I draw every resistor?

**Tutor:** Draw enough to identify the parts the assembler must handle, and no more. The board itself is a flat rectangle in isometric. On it, show the components that matter for assembly: the transistor as a small half-cylinder, the capacitor as a small can, the resistors as small cylinders, the connector or wires where the battery and sensor attach. They need not be accurate in detail, and their values are not written here, because the circuit diagram already carries them; the exploded view only needs the reader to see that the board carries those parts and which way up it sits. Give the board one balloon number and, if the components are bought separately and need listing, give the populated board a sub-list or list the components under the board's number in the parts list. What must not be forgotten are the parts that mount in the lid or the box rather than on the board, the LED, the switch, the cable gland, because they are assembled separately and each needs a balloon.

### subtopic_3 — Labelling, Parts List and Checking the Exploded Drawing
**Assistant:** Our device has a sensor unit on the tank as well as the indoor box. One exploded view or two?

**Tutor:** Two, on the same sheet if they fit, each with its own axis and its own balloon numbers continuing from one to the other. The indoor box and the tank sensor housing are assembled separately, in different places, from different parts, and connected only by the cable. Forcing them onto one axis would put the sensor housing floating oddly above or beside the box with an alignment line that does not represent a real fit. Draw the indicator box exploded vertically on the left, parts 1 to 8. Draw the sensor housing exploded on the right, a short pipe fitting with its cap, the reed switch inside, the cable gland and the magnet float on its guide, parts 9 to 13. Draw the cable between them as a simple line with its length noted. One parts list covers both. This is exactly how manuals for products with several assemblies are arranged, and it shows the examiner that the team has thought through the whole device, not just the part on the windowsill.
