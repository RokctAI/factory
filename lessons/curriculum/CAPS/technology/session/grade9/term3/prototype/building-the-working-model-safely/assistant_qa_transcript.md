### subtopic_1 — Planning the Build: Parts, Tools, Order and Safety Rules
**Assistant:** Why is a dry joint so dangerous if the circuit works on the bench?

**Tutor:** Because a dry joint is a connection held by luck rather than by metal, and luck runs out at the worst time. When solder is fed to the iron instead of to the heated joint, or the part moves while the solder sets, the solder cools as a ball sitting on the lead without flowing over the pad, and the lead merely touches the pad inside it. On the bench, with the board flat and still, that touch conducts and everything works. Carry the model to the presentation, press the button, warm the box in a hand, and the touch opens; the LED goes dark in front of the examiner, and the fault is almost invisible because the joint looks soldered. Dry joints are found by their appearance, dull and lumpy or ball-shaped, and by gently wiggling each lead with a fingernail while watching the joint; a good one does not move. Reflowing with the iron touching lead and pad together for two seconds, and a little fresh solder, fixes it in a moment, which is why the board is inspected joint by joint before it is boxed.

### subtopic_2 — Soldering and Assembling the Circuit Neatly
**Assistant:** Why drill the box before the circuit goes in when the holes have to line up with the LED and switch on the board?

**Tutor:** Because the drawing, not the board, decides where the holes go, and drilling a box with a circuit inside ruins the circuit. The orthographic drawing fixes the LED and switch positions on the lid; the exploded view shows the LED and switch mounted in the lid, not on the board, with wires to the board. So the lid is drilled from the drawing's dimensions while it is empty, the LED and switch are fitted into their holes, and short wires connect them to the board when it is fitted; the board does not have to line up with anything except its own fixing points. If the design does have a board-mounted LED poking through the lid, the position is still taken from the drawing, and the hole is drilled before the board goes in, with the board offered up to check alignment before any glue. Drilling after assembly throws plastic swarf and metal filings across the board, where a filing bridging two tracks is a short circuit that may never be found.

### subtopic_3 — Fitting the Enclosure, Testing to Scale and Finishing
**Assistant:** How do we test visible from ten metres in daylight in a classroom?

**Tutor:** By taking the test where the specification says, and by writing down what was actually done. The brief said the LED must be visible from the kitchen window at ten metres in daylight, so the honest test is outside: one team member stands ten metres away in the school yard in daylight and says whether the LED, lit by the test button, is clearly seen. If it is, the specification passes and the note says tested at ten metres outdoors at eleven in the morning, clear. If it is not, the team has learnt something before the grandmother does, and the fix is a brighter LED, a flashing one, or a small reflector or hood around it to cut the glare on the lens. A test done indoors at two metres and recorded as a pass would be the kind of self-chosen test the brief's evaluation criteria were written to prevent. The examiner marks the honesty of the testing as well as the result, and a recorded fail with a fix is worth more than an unsupported pass.
