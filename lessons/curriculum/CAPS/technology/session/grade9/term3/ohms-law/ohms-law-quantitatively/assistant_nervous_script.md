Hey — if Ohm's law looks like the maths part you dread, here is the calm version: one multiplication.

Volts equals amps times ohms. That is it.

Want amps? Volts over ohms. Want ohms? Volts over amps. Draw a triangle, V on top, I and R below, cover what you want.

Turn milliamperes into amperes first: 10 milliamperes is 0.01.

The law says double the volts, double the amps, as long as the part does not heat up.

For the LED resistor: battery volts minus 2 for the LED, divided by the amps you want. 2.5 over 0.01 is 250. Use 470 and you get about 5 milliamperes. Safe.

Write the formula, put the numbers in, write the unit. Done.
