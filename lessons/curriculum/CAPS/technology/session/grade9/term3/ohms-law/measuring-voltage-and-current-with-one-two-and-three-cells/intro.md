Today is the practical that gives Ohm's law its evidence. A fixed resistor is connected to one cell, then two, then three in series, and at each step the voltage across the resistor and the current through it are measured. The expectation is that each extra cell adds about 1.5 volts and the current rises by the same step, so that voltage over current stays equal to the resistor's value. We learn to connect the meters correctly, read them to a sensible precision, record every reading in a table as it was, repeat readings to check them, and note anything odd, such as a tired cell that gives less than 1.5 volts. The table feeds next session's graph.
