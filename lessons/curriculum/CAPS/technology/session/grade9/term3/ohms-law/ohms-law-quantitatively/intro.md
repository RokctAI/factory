Grade 8 described Ohm's law in words: more voltage, more current; more resistance, less current. Grade 9 puts numbers to it. Voltage in volts is the push, current in amperes is the flow, resistance in ohms is the opposition, and for a resistor at constant temperature the current is exactly proportional to the voltage: double the volts, double the amps. The constant that links them is the resistance, so voltage equals current times resistance, and the law can be turned round to find any one from the other two. Today we define the three quantities, state the law in numbers, and do the first worked examples, including the LED resistor from the PAT.
