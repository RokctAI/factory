Today we look at how materials respond to the forces we met last session. A member can be pulled, which is tension, or pushed, which is compression, and most materials are good at one and poor at the other. A beam carrying a load across a gap is bent, which puts its top in compression and its bottom in tension at the same time, and that tells us where to put reinforcing. A structure can also be twisted, which is torsion, and we learn why a diagonal cross-brace is the standard way to resist it. These ideas explain why the ramp slab has mesh near its bottom and why the handrail has diagonal braces.
