Today we look at the forces a structure must carry. A static load is a weight that stays still, like the structure's own concrete or a parked wheelchair. A dynamic load changes or moves, like a crowd running down the stairs at the bell or the wind on a tall rail. We learn why dynamic loads are more dangerous than their size suggests, and then we look at how a load can be spread evenly over a slab or concentrated in one place, and what that does to the structure. These ideas decide how thick the slab is and how strong the rails must be.
