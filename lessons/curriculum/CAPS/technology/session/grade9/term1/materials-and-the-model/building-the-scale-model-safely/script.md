# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the Make stage of PAT 1: building a neat scale model of a viable solution using safe working practices. The anchor is a Technology room in Mahikeng on a Monday morning with base boards, sheets of corrugated card and mounting board, dowel, balsa, PVA, cutting mats, craft knives and a first aid box laid out, and a team's working drawing and flow chart pinned above their bench. The governing idea is that a model is a scaled translation of the working drawing, so accuracy comes from the conversion table and from checking at the points the flow chart marks, that neatness comes from sharp tools, clean cuts and patience with glue, and that safety is a set of specific habits for specific tools rather than a general warning. Building to the drawing with safe practice is the Make stage worth 35 marks.

## Subtopic: Model Materials, Tools and the Conversion Table

A model is built from materials that stand in for the real ones at a scale the hands can manage. At 1:20 the ramp is 360 millimetres long and 60 wide, the risers 7.5 high and the rails 45 above the surface. Concrete is represented by thick card, foam board or mounting board, which cut cleanly and hold a flat surface; the fill under the ramp by blocks of polystyrene or stacked card; the rails and posts by wooden dowel of 3 or 4 millimetres or by stiff wire; the kerb by a strip of card; the ground by a base board of hardboard or thick card about 700 by 300. Surfaces that are brushed concrete in reality can be textured with fine sandpaper glued on or with a thin coat of paint mixed with sand. Colour is kept realistic and restrained: grey for concrete, a metal colour for rails.

Tools are few and should be sharp: a steel rule, a set square, a pencil, a craft knife with spare blades, a cutting mat, a small saw for dowel, a sanding block, PVA glue for card and timber and a hot glue gun for quick joints that need to hold while PVA dries, masking tape to hold parts while glue sets, and paint with brushes. A drill or awl makes post holes. Everything the team needs should be gathered before cutting begins, which is the first box of the flow chart.

The conversion table is the model's foundation. Every dimension on the working drawing is listed and divided by the model scale, 20, to give the cutting size: rise 600 becomes 30; riser 150 becomes 7.5; tread 250 becomes 12.5; width 1 200 becomes 60; ramp leg 7 200 becomes 360; landing 1 200 becomes 60; rail height 900 becomes 45; post spacing 1 500 becomes 75; kerb 75 becomes about 4. The table is checked by a second team member against the drawing and pinned beside the bench. Parts are marked out from the table, not from memory, and labelled in pencil before they are cut.

The questions for this section are with you now: suitable model materials for each part, the tools needed, and how the conversion table is made and used.

## Subtopic: Safe Working Practices with Knives, Glue and Dust

Safe working practice is a set of habits, each tied to a tool. The craft knife: always cut on a cutting mat, never on the bench or on your lap; hold the rule with fingers well back from the edge; cut away from the body with several light passes rather than one heavy one, because a blade that is forced slips; retract or cap the blade whenever it is put down; change blades when they drag, because a blunt blade needs more force and is more dangerous than a sharp one; dispose of old blades in a marked container, never in the bin. The saw: clamp or hold the dowel firmly in a bench hook, keep the free hand behind the blade, and cut with the saw doing the work.

The hot glue gun: it is the most common cause of burns in a Technology room, because the glue stays hot for several seconds and sticks to skin. Rest the gun on its stand, never on its side; hold parts with tweezers or a scrap of card, not with fingers, for the first few seconds; keep a bowl of cold water on the bench and plunge any burn into it at once; switch off and unplug when it is not in use. PVA is safe but slow; use it for structural joints and leave them taped overnight. Spray adhesives and solvent glues are used only in a ventilated space and with the teacher's permission.

Dust and mess: sanding foam board and card makes fine dust that should not be breathed, so sand gently, in a ventilated room, and wipe the bench rather than blowing the dust about. Paint is water-based; brushes are washed immediately. The bench is kept tidy at all times: a knife lost under a pile of offcuts is found by a hand. At the end of each session tools go back to their places, blades are counted, and the floor is swept, because the next class works here too. Anyone cut or burnt tells the teacher at once, however small the injury. These rules are not an interruption to the making; they are part of the Make marks, and a team that works safely also works more accurately, because a careful cut is both.

The questions for this section are with you now: the rules for the craft knife, the rules for the hot glue gun, and how dust, mess and injuries are handled.

## Subtopic: Building Neatly to the Drawing and Checking as You Go

Building follows the flow chart. Mark out the base: draw the plan of the stairs, ramp and landing on the base board at model scale from the table, so that every part has a place to go. Cut the ramp legs and landing from mounting board, with the kerb strips, and check them against the table before gluing: leg length 360 for a rise of 30, which is 1 in 12. Build up the fill under the ramp from polystyrene cut to the slope, and glue the ramp surface onto it with PVA, taped flat until dry. Cut the step blocks, four risers of 7.5, four treads of 12.5, 60 wide; stack and check that the risers are equal with the rule before gluing. Fit the landing level with the top step and the top of the ramp, and check that it is 60 long and sits flat.

Rails come after the structure is firm. Mark post positions at 75 spacing along both edges of the ramp and stairs and at the landing; drill or push holes; cut dowel posts to 45 plus 10 for the fixing; glue them in, checking with a set square that each is vertical and with a scrap of card cut to 45 that each is the right height. Glue the handrail dowel along the tops, following the slope of the ramp and the pitch of the stairs, extending 15 beyond the ends for the real 300. Add knee braces of dowel or card at the rail ends if the design calls for them. Finish: paint the concrete grey, texture the walking surfaces, paint the rails, and add small details that help the tender board read the model, such as a door outline on a card wall and a figure in a wheelchair at the scale of 1:20, about 90 millimetres tall.

Neatness comes from four habits: sharp blades and light cuts so edges are clean, parts cut slightly oversize and sanded to the line rather than cut short, glue applied thinly with a scrap of card so it does not ooze, and parts held square with tape or a set square until set. The model is checked against the specification list at the end, exactly as the drawing was, and the check sheet goes in the file with photographs of the model from the same directions as the drawing's views, front, top and side, which lets the marker compare model to drawing directly. The error museum, four exhibits. One: cutting from memory instead of the table, so the ramp comes out at 1 in 10. Two: gluing the ramp before checking its length; the check belongs before the glue. Three: thick blobs of hot glue at every joint, which are ugly and weak. Four: no photographs or check sheet, so the Make evidence is incomplete when the model is taken home.

The questions for this section are with you now: the order of building the model, how posts and rails are fitted, and the habits and records that earn Make marks.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again at a bench with card, dowel, glue and a sharp knife — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: What You Build It From and How Big

Scale 1:20. The ramp is 360 long and 60 wide. Risers 7.5. Rails 45 high. Small enough for hands, big enough to see.

Materials: thick card or mounting board for concrete. Polystyrene blocks for the fill. Dowel or stiff wire for rails and posts. A card strip for the kerb. A hardboard base. Sandpaper or sandy paint for the rough surface. Grey for concrete.

Tools: steel rule, set square, pencil, craft knife, cutting mat, small saw, sanding block, PVA, hot glue gun, tape, paint.

First make the conversion table: every drawing size divided by 20. Check it with a teammate. Pin it up. Cut from the table, never from memory.

Keep the table in mind and try the questions for this part: what you build from and how big.

## Subtopic: Sharp Things, Sticky Things, Tidy Benches

Knife: on a mat, fingers back, cut away from you, light passes, cap it when you put it down, change blunt blades, old blades in the blade tin.

Saw: hold the dowel in a bench hook, free hand behind the blade.

Hot glue: it burns. Gun on its stand. Hold parts with tweezers or card for the first seconds. Cold water bowl on the bench. Unplug when done. PVA for strong joints, taped overnight.

Dust: sand gently, wipe, do not blow. Tidy bench always. Tools back, blades counted, floor swept. Any cut or burn, tell the teacher.

Hold the capped knife and the cold water in mind and try this part's questions: sharp things and sticky things.

## Subtopic: Follow the Chart, Check the Sizes

Follow the flow chart. Draw the plan on the base. Cut the ramp pieces and check: 360 long for 30 up. Glue the fill and the ramp surface, tape flat. Cut the steps, check all risers are 7.5, glue. Fit the landing level and flat.

Then rails. Posts every 75, holes, dowel cut to 45 plus 10, check each is upright and the right height. Handrail along the tops, 15 past the ends. Braces if the design has them. Paint, texture, add a door and a 90 millimetre wheelchair figure.

Neat means: sharp blade, cut a bit big and sand to the line, thin glue, hold square till dry. At the end, tick the specification list and photograph front, top and side.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Build at 1:20 from card, polystyrene and dowel, cutting every part from a checked conversion table. Work safely: knife on a mat and cut away, glue gun on its stand with cold water ready, dust wiped, bench tidy, injuries reported. Build in flow chart order, checking gradient, risers and rail height before gluing, finish neatly, and record the model with a check sheet and photographs.

Hold the check sheet and the three photographs, and take the final questions of the lesson: follow the chart, check the sizes.
