# Part 1 — Expert

This session applies first angle orthographic projection to the stairs and wheelchair ramp of the Term 1 design brief. The anchor is the brief from the KwaZulu-Natal school: a rise of 600 millimetres, four equal risers of 150, treads of 250, a stair 1 200 wide, and a ramp 1 200 wide at 1 in 12, 7 200 long, with a 1 200 landing at the top, handrails on both sides at 900. The governing idea is that a working drawing is produced in a fixed order, views and scale first, then the front view set out from the brief's numbers, then the plan projected down and the side view projected across, then line types corrected and dimensions added, and that the drawing is finished only when every number a builder needs can be read from it. Producing such a drawing is the Design part of PAT 1 and is assessed.

## Subtopic: From Brief to Drawing: Choosing Views and Scale

The drawing begins with two decisions. First, which views are needed. For a staircase and ramp side by side, the front view, looking at the face a person approaches, shows the risers of the stairs as horizontal lines and the ramp as a sloping surface seen end-on, which is not very informative; the side view shows the step profile and the ramp's gradient clearly; the top view, the plan, shows the widths, the treads and the landing. All three are needed, and the side view is the one that carries the most information, so some drawers choose to make the side the front. Whatever is chosen, the view that faces the reader is called the front view, and the pattern is applied from there.

Second, the scale. The overall length including the ramp, the landing and the stairs is about 10 metres; the overall height is 600 millimetres plus a handrail of 900, so 1 500. On an A4 landscape sheet of 297 by 210, a scale of 1:50 gives 200 millimetres for the length, 30 for the height, and 24 for the width in plan, which leaves room for dimensions and the title block. At 1:50 a riser of 150 is 3 millimetres and a tread of 250 is 5 millimetres: small but drawable with a sharp pencil. 1:50 is chosen and written in the title block with the first angle symbol.

Before any line is drawn, the brief's numbers are converted to drawing sizes in a short table: rise 600 becomes 12; riser 150 becomes 3; tread 250 becomes 5; width 1 200 becomes 24; ramp 7 200 becomes 144; landing 1 200 becomes 24; rail 900 becomes 18. This table is the single place where arithmetic happens; the dimensions written later are the real sizes from the brief.

The questions for this section are with you now: choosing views for the stairs and ramp, choosing and justifying a scale, and preparing the conversion table.

## Subtopic: Setting Out the Front View and Projecting the Plan

Set out the front view with the stairs on the left and the ramp on the right, viewed from the direction a person approaches. Draw a feint horizontal ground line. For the stairs, measure 24 millimetres of width and draw the four risers as dark horizontal lines at 3, 6, 9 and 12 above the ground, each the full width of the stair; the vertical faces are seen head-on, so the front view of the stairs is a rectangle 24 wide by 12 high crossed by three internal horizontal lines. For the ramp, the front view looking up the slope shows a rectangle 24 wide rising to 12 at the top, with the sloping surface seen as a plain face. The handrails appear as vertical posts with a horizontal rail 18 above the surface they protect, drawn with dark lines.

Project the plan directly below. Drop feint vertical construction lines from every width in the front view. The stairs in plan are four treads of 5 millimetres each, drawn as a rectangle 24 wide and 20 deep with three internal lines, plus the top landing 24 deep behind them. The ramp in plan is a rectangle 24 wide and 144 long, which may need a wavy break line if space is short, and the same landing of 24 deep at its top; the ramp and stairs share the landing at the door. Handrails in plan are two thin parallel lines along each edge. The plan must sit exactly below the front view with widths matching, which the projection lines guarantee.

Check the plan against the brief: are the treads 250 real, that is 5 at scale; is the landing 1 200 real, 24 at scale; does the ramp length give 1 in 12 against a rise of 600? If the plan passes, the two views already contain widths, depths and heights, and the side view will confirm the profile.

The questions for this section are with you now: how the stairs and ramp appear in the front view, what the plan shows, and how projection keeps the two aligned.

## Subtopic: The Side View, Hidden Detail and Dimensions

The side view, in first angle, goes to the right of the front view if it shows the left end. Project the heights across with feint horizontal lines: ground, 3, 6, 9, 12 and the rail at 18 above each surface. Transfer the depths from the plan with a 45 degree line. The stairs in side view are the step profile: four risers of 3 and four treads of 5, the familiar sawtooth, finishing at the landing 24 deep at height 12. The ramp in side view, if it lies behind the stairs from this direction, is drawn as hidden detail: a dashed sloping line from ground to 12 over a length of 144. If the side view is taken from the other end, the ramp is visible as a dark sloping line and the stairs are hidden. The drawer states which end is viewed in the label.

Hidden detail also includes the foundation of the stairs below ground level, shown dashed, and the fixings of the handrail posts into the concrete. The handrail in side view is a line 18 above the tread line, following the slope of the stairs, with posts at each end and at the landing.

Now correct the line types: outlines dark, construction and projection feint, hidden dashed, the ramp break wavy if used, centre lines chain where a post is symmetrical. Then dimension. In the side view: the risers, given once as 4 at 150 equals 600, the treads as 4 at 250 equals 1 000, the overall rise 600, the rail height 900. In the plan: the stair width 1 200, the ramp width 1 200, the ramp length 7 200, the landing 1 200, the overall length. In the front view: only what the others do not show, which may be nothing. Finally the title block: project name, scale 1:50, first angle symbol, units in millimetres, drawn by, date. The error museum, four exhibits. One: dimensioning the scaled sizes, 144 instead of 7 200. Two: forgetting the landing, so the ramp meets the door on a slope. Three: drawing the hidden ramp with dark lines. Four: a ramp length that does not match the stated gradient.

The questions for this section are with you now: the side view profile, what appears as hidden detail, and which dimensions go in which view.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the brief in one hand and a sharp pencil in the other — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Which Views and What Scale

Two decisions first. Which views? Front, top and side. The side view shows the steps and the slope best. The top view, the plan, shows widths and the landing.

What scale? The whole thing is about 10 metres long. At 1:50 that is 200 millimetres on the page. Fits on A4. A riser of 150 becomes 3. A tread of 250 becomes 5. Write 1:50 in the title block.

Make a little table: every real size from the brief, divided by 50. Do the maths once, here.

Keep the table in mind and try the questions for this part: views and scale.

## Subtopic: Steps Across, Then Down to the Plan

Front view: stairs on the left, ramp on the right. Stairs are a rectangle 24 wide and 12 high with lines at 3, 6, 9. Those are the step edges seen head-on. The ramp is a plain face 24 wide. Rails are posts with a line 18 up.

Now drop feint lines straight down for the plan. Stairs in plan: four treads, 5 each, then the landing 24 deep. Ramp in plan: 24 wide, 144 long, same landing at the top. Rails are two thin lines along each edge.

Check: does the ramp length give 1 in 12? 144 times 50 is 7 200, twelve times 600. Yes.

Hold the sawtooth and the long rectangle in mind and try this part's questions: front view and plan.

## Subtopic: The Side View and the Sizes

Side view on the right. Project heights across. Transfer depths round the 45 degree line. Draw the step profile: four up, four along, landing at the top. The ramp behind the stairs is dashed, a sloping hidden line from ground to 12 over 144.

Fix the lines: dark, feint, dashed. Then write the real sizes, once each: risers 4 at 150, treads 4 at 250, rise 600, rail 900 in the side view; widths 1 200, ramp 7 200, landing 1 200 in the plan.

Title block: name, 1:50, first angle symbol, millimetres.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Choose three views and a scale of 1:50, convert the brief's numbers once in a table, set out the front view, project the plan below and the side view to the right, show the hidden ramp dashed, correct the line types, dimension real sizes once each, and fill in the title block.

Hold the dashed ramp and the title block, and take the final questions of the lesson: side view and sizes.
