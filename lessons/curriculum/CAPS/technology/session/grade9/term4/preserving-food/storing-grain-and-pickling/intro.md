Metals are preserved by keeping air and water away; food is preserved by keeping away the living things that eat it before we do. Bacteria, moulds, yeasts, insects and rodents all spoil food, and each needs moisture, warmth, air or access to do so. This week covers four ways of denying them, two in theory today and two in a practical next session. Storing grain, from the clay-sealed pits and raised baskets of indigenous South African farmers to the steel silos at the co-op, keeps maize and sorghum dry, cool and closed. Pickling preserves vegetables in vinegar or in the acid made by fermentation, which bacteria cannot grow in. We look at how each works and why it has lasted.
