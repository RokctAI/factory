Two of the oldest food preservation methods are done today with our own hands. Drying removes the water that bacteria and moulds need, which is how biltong, dried fruit, dried fish and dried maize have kept South Africans fed for centuries. Salting draws water out of the food and out of any organism on it, which is how fish and meat were preserved for long journeys before refrigeration. In the practical we take slices of apple, tomato or meat, dry some in the sun or a warm oven and salt others, weigh them before and after, watch them over days beside untreated slices, and record what happens. The results show why these methods have lasted and where their limits are.
