The third way to coat a metal is to grow a thin layer of another metal onto it with electricity. Electroplating puts chrome on taps and bumpers, nickel on coins and cutlery, gold on jewellery and connectors, and zinc on small bolts and screws. The object to be coated is made the negative electrode in a bath of a metal salt solution, a piece of the coating metal is the positive electrode, and a current carries metal atoms from the positive electrode through the solution onto the object. We learn the parts of the plating cell, why it works, and then do it: copper plating a steel key with a cell, copper sulphate solution and a strip of copper.
