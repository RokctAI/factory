### subtopic_1 — Term 3 Recalled: Electrical Systems and the Device
**Assistant:** How do I know whether a question wants describe or explain, and what is the difference in marks?

**Tutor:** The command word is printed, and the marks beside the question confirm it. Describe asks what happens or what something is like, in order: the bottles are sorted, shredded, washed, dried and extruded. Each correct step is a mark. Explain asks why, so each point needs a cause attached: the flake is dried because water in the melt breaks the polymer chains and weakens the pellet. That is a point and a reason, usually two marks, and a bare description scores only the first. A quick test: if your sentence could have the word because inserted and completed, the question wanted explain. Compare is explain for two things with a link word, whereas or but, and the mark scheme gives marks for the link, not just for two separate facts. If a four-mark question says explain, plan two points each with a reason; if a four-mark question says describe, plan four steps. Reading the command word and the mark allocation before writing is the single highest-scoring habit in the exam.

### subtopic_2 — Term 4 Recalled: Preserving, Plastics and Recycling
**Assistant:** What is the fastest way to revise two terms of content in the time left?

**Tutor:** Rebuild each term as a one-page map and test yourself from it, rather than rereading notes. Take a blank page for Term 3 and draw the circuit diagram with every symbol, write Ohm's law with units and one worked example, list the six components with one line each, draw a truth table for AND and OR, and write three safety rules and three energy facts. Take a page for Term 4 and draw the systems diagram of the recycling plant, list the preservation methods with the one sentence that explains each, write the plastics codes 1 to 7 with one use each, list the four moulding processes with a product each and the four design rules, and sketch three views in first angle positions. That takes an evening. Then cover the page and redraw it from memory the next day; what you cannot redraw is what to revise. Finish with past questions done against the clock, marking your own answers against the command word. Diagrams redrawn from memory stick far better than paragraphs reread.

### subtopic_3 — Exam Technique: Reading, Drawing and Explaining
**Assistant:** Why does the exam keep setting questions in a scenario instead of just asking for the facts?

**Tutor:** Because Technology is a subject about using knowledge to solve problems, and a scenario is the only way to test that on paper. Knowing that zinc is more reactive than iron is a science fact; choosing galvanising for a coastal fence and explaining why it beats paint there is technology. So the paper describes a farm, a shop, a car or a school and asks what you would do and why. The marks are still for the same facts, but you earn them by selecting the right ones and tying them to the situation, which is what a designer does. The method is to read the scenario twice, underline the clues, coastal, outdoors, food contact, large numbers, cheap, and then let each clue call up the fact it points to: coastal means salt means faster corrosion means thick zinc or paint over zinc; food contact means polypropylene not polystyrene; large numbers means injection moulding. Write the clue, the fact and the link in one sentence each. That is also the thinking the design project asked for all year, which is why the scenario questions feel familiar once you look for the clues.
