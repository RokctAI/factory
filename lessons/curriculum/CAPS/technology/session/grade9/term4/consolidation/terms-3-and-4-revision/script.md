# Part 1 — Expert

This session consolidates Terms 3 and 4 for the end-of-year examination. The anchor is a revision wall in a Durban classroom: on the left a circuit diagram with a battery, switch, resistor and LED in series and a parallel branch with a buzzer, with Ohm's law beside it; on the right a systems diagram of a recycling plant and a three-view drawing of a plastic cup holder. The governing idea is that the examination tests recall of facts, interpretation and drawing of diagrams, and explanation of choices in context, across electrical systems and control from Term 3 and processing from Term 4, and that marks are earned by reading the command word, using the correct terms, showing working and labelling drawings. The content of both terms is examinable.

## Subtopic: Term 3 Recalled: Electrical Systems and the Device

Term 3 began with the circuit. A circuit needs a source, a conductor, a load and usually a control; a switch opens or closes it. In series, components lie in one path, the same current flows through each, the voltage divides across them, and one break stops everything. In parallel, components lie on separate branches, each gets the full voltage, the current divides, and a break in one branch leaves the others working, which is why a house is wired in parallel. Ohm's law links voltage, current and resistance: V equals I times R, so a 9 volt battery through a 450 ohm resistor gives 20 milliamps. Remember the units, volts, amps, ohms, and the circuit symbols: cell and battery, switch, resistor, variable resistor, lamp, LED with its arrows, buzzer, motor, LDR and thermistor.

The electronic components: a resistor limits current and is read by colour bands; an LED lights only one way and needs a series resistor; an LDR's resistance falls in light and a thermistor's falls with heat, which makes them sensors; a transistor acts as an electronic switch, turning a larger current on when a small current reaches its base; a capacitor stores charge for a time delay. The device project put these together: a sensor, a transistor switch and an output, built on a board, tested with a multimeter, with a circuit diagram, a flow chart or logic description, and an evaluation against specifications.

Control and logic: an input, a process and an output; AND means both inputs needed, OR means either, NOT inverts; a truth table lists every combination. Safety: insulation, fuses and earth, the dangers of mains compared with battery circuits, and the rules of the project workshop. Electricity in South Africa: generation from coal, nuclear and renewables, transmission at high voltage, load shedding and energy saving, and the environmental cost of coal.

The questions for this section are with you now: series and parallel and Ohm's law with units, the components and sensors and the device, and logic, safety and the energy context.

## Subtopic: Term 4 Recalled: Preserving, Plastics and Recycling

Term 4 began with preserving. Iron rusts when oxygen and water both reach it; paint is a barrier that fails at chips; galvanising is a zinc coat that also sacrifices itself at scratches; electroplating deposits a thin metal layer by electrolysis, with the object as the cathode and the plating metal as the anode. Food spoils through microbes, enzymes and moisture; grain is stored dry and cool, pickling uses acid and salt, drying removes water and salting draws it out, and the indigenous methods of biltong and dried maize are the same science as the factory's.

Plastics are thermoplastics, which soften and can be recycled, or thermosets, which char; their profile is light, tough, flexible, insulating, water-resistant and mouldable, weak in heat, sunlight and fire; the recycling codes 1 to 7 identify PET, HDPE, PVC, LDPE, polypropylene, polystyrene and other. Reduce, reuse and recycle are a hierarchy in that order. A recycling plant sorts, strips, shreds, washes, float-sinks, dries, extrudes and pelletises, described by a systems diagram of input, process, output and feedback. Pellets become products by injection, blow, extrusion or rotational moulding, and parts are designed with even walls, draft, no undercuts and rounded corners.

The case studies, cars and homes, taught the method: name the material, state the property that chose it, ask about safety and end of life. The design task ran from an observed need, through a problem statement, brief and measurable specifications, to a choice of recycled plastic and method and a first angle orthographic drawing with a section. The exam can ask for any step of that process, and often asks you to apply it to a new item.

The questions for this section are with you now: preserving metals and food, plastics, the three Rs, the plant and moulding, and the case study and design process.

## Subtopic: Exam Technique: Reading, Drawing and Explaining

Exam technique starts with the command word. Name or list wants the term only, one mark each. Describe wants what happens in order. Explain wants why, with a reason for each step, usually two marks per point. Compare wants both sides and a link, such as paint fails at chips whereas galvanising protects them. Draw wants a diagram with symbols, labels and, for orthographic drawing, correct positions, line types and dimensions. Calculate wants the formula, the substitution with units, and the answer with a unit; working earns marks even if the final figure slips.

Diagrams and drawings score by their parts. A circuit diagram scores for correct symbols, a closed loop, the right series and parallel arrangement and labels. A systems diagram scores for the three boxes in order, the arrows, the feedback loop and the labelled resources and waste. An orthographic drawing scores for first angle positions, alignment, line types, dimensions once each and a title block. A truth table scores for all combinations and the correct outputs. Practise each one on blank paper until the layout is automatic.

Context questions give a scenario, a farm, a township shop, a car, a school, and ask you to apply the term's ideas: which preservation method and why, which plastic and why, what the hierarchy says, where the risk is. Answer in the scenario's own terms and use the technical words; an answer that says it will not rust because zinc is more reactive than iron and corrodes first earns more than it is protected. Manage time by marks: a mark a minute, with the drawing left enough time. The error museum, four exhibits. One: describing when asked to explain, with no reasons. Two: a calculation without units or working. Three: a diagram without labels. Four: spending so long on the first section that the drawing is rushed.

The questions for this section are with you now: the command words and what each wants, how diagrams and drawings are marked, and applying the ideas in a scenario within the time.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Durban revision wall, the circuit on the left and the recycling plant and drawing on the right — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Circuit Half of the Year

Circuit: source, conductor, load, control. Series: one path, same current, voltage shares, one break kills all. Parallel: branches, full voltage each, current shares, one break leaves the rest working, like a house. V equals I times R: 9 volts over 450 ohms is 20 milliamps. Know the symbols.

Components: resistor limits current, LED one way with a resistor, LDR and thermistor are sensors whose resistance changes, transistor is an electronic switch, capacitor gives a delay. The device: sensor, transistor, output, built, tested, drawn, evaluated.

Logic: input, process, output; AND, OR, NOT; truth tables. Safety: insulation, fuses, earth, mains versus battery. Energy: coal, nuclear, renewables, load shedding, saving.

Keep the circuit half of the year in mind and try the questions for this part: Term 3.

## Subtopic: The Processing Half of the Year

Preserving: rust needs air and water; paint is a barrier; zinc is a barrier that sacrifices itself; electroplating puts metal on by electrolysis, object at the cathode. Food: microbes, enzymes, moisture; store grain dry, pickle in acid and salt, dry and salt to remove water; biltong is the same science.

Plastics: thermoplastics soften and recycle, thermosets char. Light, tough, bendy, insulating, waterproof, mouldable; weak in heat, sun, fire. Codes 1 to 7. Reduce, reuse, recycle in that order. Plant: sort, strip, shred, wash, float-sink, dry, extrude, pelletise; systems diagram with feedback. Moulding: injection, blow, extrusion, rotational; even walls, draft, no undercuts, round corners.

Case studies: material, property, safety, end of life. Design: need, problem statement, brief, measurable specs, plastic and method, first angle drawing with a section.

Hold the processing half of the year in mind and try this part's questions: Term 4.

## Subtopic: How to Earn the Marks

Command words: name gives the term; describe gives what happens; explain gives why; compare gives both sides and a link; draw gives symbols, labels, positions; calculate gives formula, substitution with units, answer with unit.

Diagrams score by parts: circuit symbols and loop; systems boxes, arrows, feedback, resources; orthographic positions, lines, dimensions, title block; truth tables complete. Practise the layouts until automatic.

Scenario questions: answer in their terms with the technical words. Zinc is more reactive than iron beats it is protected. A mark a minute; leave time for the drawing.
