# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers identifying plastics by their codes and sorting them for recycling. The anchor is a sorting table at a buy-back centre in Soweto, where waste pickers tip out bags collected from the suburbs and sort by eye and by the code on the base: clear cooldrink bottles in one cage, milk bottles in another, margarine tubs in a third, yoghurt cups and polystyrene trays set aside because nobody will pay for them. The governing idea is that plastics are a family of different polymers whose properties and melting behaviour differ, that recycling requires them to be separated by type, that the resin identification code moulded into an item names its polymer, and that the market for each code determines what is actually recycled. Identifying the seven codes and their common items, sorting plastic correctly and explaining why sorting matters are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Plastics Are and Why They Must Be Sorted

Plastics are materials made of polymers, very long chain molecules built by joining thousands of small molecules, mostly from oil and gas. Different small molecules give different polymers, and each polymer has its own properties: polyethylene is soft and waxy, polypropylene is tougher and takes heat, polystyrene is stiff and brittle, PVC is strong and can be made rigid or flexible, PET is clear and strong. Plastics are shaped by heating until they soften and then moulding, which is why most of them can be reshaped again and recycled: the thermoplastics. A smaller group, the thermosets, set hard once and cannot be remelted; melamine cups and bakelite plugs are thermosets.

Sorting matters because plastics do not mix. Each polymer melts at its own temperature and flows in its own way, and when two are melted together they do not blend but form weak layers and lumps, like oil and water, so the recycled product cracks or cannot be moulded at all. A single PVC bottle in a batch of PET bottles can ruin the whole batch, because PVC breaks down and discolours at PET's melting temperature. Recyclers therefore pay only for plastic sorted by type and clean, and a mixed bag is worth very little. Sorting is the first process in recycling and the one the public can do.

South Africa recycles a large share of some plastics and almost none of others. Clear PET cooldrink bottles and HDPE milk and detergent bottles have strong markets and are collected by thousands of waste pickers who earn a living from them. Polystyrene and mixed or unmarked plastics have weak markets and mostly go to landfill or are burnt in the open, which releases harmful smoke. Knowing the codes lets a household put the valuable plastics where they will be collected and avoid contaminating them with the rest.

The questions for this section are with you now: plastics as a family of polymers and thermoplastics versus thermosets, why mixed plastics cannot be recycled together, and recycling in South Africa.

## Subtopic: The Seven Recycling Codes and the Plastics They Name

The resin identification code is a triangle of three chasing arrows with a number inside and often letters beneath, moulded into the base or side of a plastic item. Code 1, PET or PETE, polyethylene terephthalate: clear cooldrink and water bottles, some food jars; strong, clear, widely recycled into fibre for clothing and new bottles. Code 2, HDPE, high-density polyethylene: milk bottles, detergent and shampoo bottles, buckets, crates, some bags; opaque, tough, widely recycled into pipes, crates, bins and new bottles. Code 3, PVC, polyvinyl chloride: pipes, gutters, window frames, some cling film, some bottles; rigid or flexible, rarely recycled from household waste and a contaminant in other streams.

Code 4, LDPE, low-density polyethylene: shopping bags, bread bags, cling film, squeezable bottles; soft and flexible, recycled where collected into bin liners and irrigation pipe. Code 5, PP, polypropylene: margarine and yoghurt tubs, bottle caps, straws, lunch boxes, car bumpers; tough, takes heat, increasingly recycled into crates, pots and car parts. Code 6, PS, polystyrene: yoghurt cups, disposable cutlery, CD cases and, as expanded polystyrene, foam trays, cups and packaging; stiff, brittle, light, rarely recycled from households because it is bulky and dirty. Code 7, Other: everything else, including polycarbonate, nylon, acrylic, biodegradable plastics and multi-layer packaging; not recycled with the others.

Learning the codes is easier with the items in hand. A collection of a dozen clean containers from home, each turned over to find its triangle, sorted into seven piles and labelled, fixes them better than a list. The codes 1, 2 and 5 are the ones with value in South Africa; 4 has some value if clean and dry; 3, 6 and 7 are the ones to avoid buying where possible and to keep out of the recycling bag. Some items carry no code, especially older or imported ones, and those are treated as 7 unless they can be identified by feel, which the next session covers.

The questions for this section are with you now: codes 1 to 3 with their items and recyclability, codes 4 to 7 with theirs, and which codes have value in South Africa.

## Subtopic: Sorting a Bag of Household Plastic for Recycling

Sorting a household bag follows the depot's method. First, empty and rinse: food residue attracts pests, smells and contaminates the melt, so containers are rinsed and shaken dry; labels can stay on for most, caps come off bottles because caps are usually a different plastic, code 5, from the bottle, code 1. Second, find the code on each item and sort into piles: PET bottles, HDPE bottles and tubs, PP tubs and caps, LDPE bags and film, and a pile for 3, 6, 7 and unmarked items, which go to general waste or to a specialist collector. Third, squash bottles flat to save space, since a bag of air is expensive to transport. Fourth, bag each type separately, or follow the local scheme's rule if it accepts mixed recyclables for sorting at a plant.

Common traps: a clear bottle is not always PET, some are PVC, so the code is checked; a black plastic tray is often PP or PS but the optical sorters at large plants cannot read black, so black trays are poorly recycled whatever their code; a crisp packet is multi-layer code 7 and goes to general waste however shiny; a polystyrene tray is code 6 and goes to general waste unless there is a specific collector; a bottle with its cap on jams the baler and mixes two plastics. The weighing of each sorted pile, on a kitchen scale, shows the household what its week of plastic amounts to and which codes dominate.

The sorted plastic goes to a buy-back centre, a kerbside collection, a school recycling scheme or a waste picker, who will be paid by the kilogram for codes 1, 2 and 5. A week's sorted plastic from a family might be two kilograms of PET, one of HDPE and half a kilogram of PP, worth a small amount to a picker and a great deal more to the landfill it does not reach. The error museum, four exhibits. One: putting unrinsed yoghurt tubs in the bag, contaminating the lot. Two: leaving caps on bottles. Three: assuming all clear plastic is PET without checking the code. Four: putting polystyrene and crisp packets in the recycling because they are plastic.

The questions for this section are with you now: rinse, code, sort, squash and bag, the common sorting traps, and where sorted plastic goes and what it is worth.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again at the sorting table at the buy-back centre in Soweto — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: A Family of Materials, Not One

Plastics are long chain molecules from oil and gas. Different chains, different plastics: soft, tough, stiff, clear. Most soften with heat and can be reshaped: thermoplastics. A few set hard forever: thermosets, like melamine cups.

They do not mix when melted. Two plastics together make weak lumps, like oil and water. One PVC bottle ruins a batch of PET. Recyclers only pay for sorted, clean plastic.

South Africa recycles a lot of PET bottles and HDPE milk bottles, because people buy them. Polystyrene and mixed plastic mostly go to landfill or get burnt. Knowing the codes puts the valuable stuff where it gets collected.

Keep a family of materials, not one in mind and try the questions for this part: what plastics are and why sort.

## Subtopic: Look for the Triangle and the Number

The triangle with a number. 1 PET: clear cooldrink and water bottles, widely recycled. 2 HDPE: milk and detergent bottles, buckets, crates, widely recycled. 3 PVC: pipes, gutters, some film and bottles, rarely recycled, a contaminant.

4 LDPE: shopping and bread bags, cling film, soft, recycled if clean. 5 PP: margarine and yoghurt tubs, caps, straws, lunch boxes, bumpers, increasingly recycled. 6 PS: yoghurt cups, cutlery, foam trays and cups, rarely recycled. 7 Other: everything else and multi-layer packs, not recycled with the rest.

1, 2 and 5 have value here. 4 some, if clean. 3, 6, 7: avoid and keep out of the bag. No code: treat as 7.

Hold look for the triangle and the number in mind and try this part's questions: the codes.

## Subtopic: Rinse, Squash, Sort, Bag

Empty and rinse. Caps off, they are a different plastic. Find the code, sort into piles: PET, HDPE, PP, LDPE, and a pile for 3, 6, 7 and unmarked to general waste. Squash the bottles. Bag each type.

Traps: not all clear bottles are PET, check. Black trays do not get sorted by machines. Crisp packets are 7. Polystyrene trays are 6. Caps on bottles jam the baler.

Weigh the piles to see what a week looks like. Sorted 1, 2 and 5 go to a buy-back centre or a waste picker, who is paid by the kilogram. Two kilograms of PET, one of HDPE, half of PP: a small sum to the picker, a big saving for the landfill.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Plastics are a family of polymers with different properties that cannot be recycled together, so they are sorted by the resin code moulded into each item: 1 PET bottles, 2 HDPE bottles and tubs, 3 PVC pipes, 4 LDPE bags, 5 PP tubs and caps, 6 PS cups and foam, 7 other. In South Africa codes 1, 2 and 5 have strong markets. Household sorting means rinse, caps off, code, sort, squash and bag, keeping 3, 6, 7 and unmarked items out.
