# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers drawing the plastic item in first angle orthographic projection. The anchor is the tap cup holder specified in the Polokwane class two weeks earlier: a polypropylene clip-on holder, 120 by 80 by 60 millimetres, with a tapered cup socket, a snap clip for a 20 millimetre pipe, a drain hole and two stiffening ribs. The governing idea is that first angle projection places the top view below the front view and the right side view to the left, that views are aligned and drawn to a stated scale with standard line types and dimensioning, and that a drawing of a moulded item shows wall thickness, draft and ribs, often with a sectional view. Producing a correct, dimensioned three-view drawing is examinable.

## Subtopic: First Angle Orthographic Projection Recalled

Orthographic projection shows a three-dimensional object as a set of flat views, each looking straight at one face, so that true lengths and angles can be measured. South Africa and most of the world outside North America use first angle projection, and the exam expects it. The rule: the object is imagined between the viewer and the drawing plane, so each view is drawn on the far side of the object. The front view is drawn first. The top view, the plan, goes directly below the front view. The view from the left goes on the right, and the view from the right goes on the left. The symbol for first angle, a truncated cone with its small end to the left of its circle view, is drawn in the title block.

The views must line up. Every width in the front view projects straight down into the top view; every height in the front view projects straight across into the side view; and depth is carried from the top view to the side view with a 45 degree mitre line or with a compass. Choose as the front view the face that shows the most about the shape, usually the longest, most detailed side. Draw to a stated scale, 1:1 if the item fits on the page, 1:2 or 2:1 otherwise, and write the scale in the title block.

Line types carry meaning. Outlines and visible edges are continuous thick lines. Hidden edges, inside or behind, are short dashes, thin. Centre lines of holes and symmetrical features are long-short chain lines, thin. Dimension and projection lines are thin continuous, with the projection lines starting a small gap from the outline and the dimension line between them with arrowheads touching the projection lines. Section planes are chain lines with thick ends and arrows, lettered.

The questions for this section are with you now: the first angle rule and the position of each view, aligning the views and choosing scale, and the line types.

## Subtopic: Drawing the Plastic Item: Views, Lines and Dimensions

Draw the cup holder. Start with a sketch and decide the front view: looking at the clip side, which shows the pipe clip and the socket profile. Draw a light frame of boxes for the three views at the chosen scale, the front view 120 wide by 60 high, the top view 120 by 80 below it, the side view 80 by 60 to the left, with projection lines linking them. Add the outline, the clip opening, the socket as a tapered shape, the drain hole as a circle in the top view with its centre line and as hidden dashes in the front and side views, and the ribs as thin raised lines. Firm up the visible edges, then the hidden ones, then the centre lines.

Dimension the drawing. Give each dimension once, on the view that shows the feature most clearly, never repeated and never on the object. Overall length, width and height first, then positions of holes from a datum edge, then sizes of holes with a diameter symbol, then radii with an R, then angles in degrees for the draft or taper. Dimension lines sit outside the view, the smallest nearest the outline and the overall outside, so that lines do not cross. All dimensions in millimetres, with the unit stated once in the title block, figures written above the line and readable from the bottom or the right of the page.

The title block in the bottom right corner carries the name of the item, your name and class, the date, the scale, the unit, the material, polypropylene, recycled, and the first angle symbol. A drawing without a title block is not complete, because nobody knows what it is of or how big it is.

The questions for this section are with you now: setting out the three views with projection lines, the dimensioning rules, and the contents of the title block.

## Subtopic: Showing Moulding Features and Checking the Drawing

A moulded part has features that a plain box does not, and the drawing must show them. Wall thickness is the first: a cup holder is a shell, not a solid, so a sectional view is drawn, cutting through the socket on a plane marked A-A in the top view, with the cut material hatched at 45 degrees, to show the 2.5 millimetre wall and the ribs. Draft is the second: the socket's sides taper by a degree or two so that it will release from the mould, shown as a slightly non-vertical line with the angle dimensioned, or noted as draft 2 degrees all vertical faces. Rounded corners are the third: outside and inside corners carry a radius, dimensioned as R3 or noted as all unspecified radii R2. Ribs and holes are the fourth: ribs drawn in section with their thickness, no more than two thirds of the wall so they do not sink, and the drain hole with its diameter.

Then check the drawing against the specification and against the mould rules before handing it in. The checklist: are the three views in first angle positions and aligned; is the scale stated and used; are the line types correct, thick outlines, dashed hidden, chain centres; is every necessary dimension given once, from a datum, with diameters and radii marked; is there a section showing wall thickness; are draft and radii shown or noted; is the title block complete with material and symbol; and do the overall dimensions match the size specification. A second learner reading the drawing should be able to describe the item without seeing it.

A clean drawing is also the input to the next stage: making the model, and for a factory, machining the mould. The error museum, four exhibits. One: putting the top view above the front view, which is third angle. Two: views that do not line up, so the widths differ between front and top. Three: dimensions repeated on two views or missing altogether, so the maker must guess. Four: a solid section with no wall thickness, which shows a lump that would sink and warp.

The questions for this section are with you now: showing wall thickness in section, draft, radii and ribs; the checking list; and why the drawing must be readable without the object.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the tap cup holder from Polokwane drawn on a sheet of A3 — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Three Views in Their Places

Orthographic: flat views looking straight at each face, true sizes. First angle, which South Africa uses: front view first, top view straight below it, view from the right on the left side. Draw the cone symbol in the title block.

Views line up: widths drop from front to top, heights go across from front to side, depth carried across with a 45 degree line. Front view is the most detailed face. Pick a scale, 1:1 if it fits, and write it down.

Lines: thick continuous for edges you see, thin dashes for edges you cannot, chain lines for centres, thin lines with arrowheads for dimensions.

Keep three views in their places in mind and try the questions for this part: the rules.

## Subtopic: Lines, Numbers and a Title Block

Set out light boxes: front 120 by 60, top 120 by 80 below, side 80 by 60 to the left, linked with projection lines. Add the clip opening, the tapered socket, the drain hole as a circle on top and dashes elsewhere, the ribs. Firm up seen edges, then hidden, then centres.

Dimensions: each once, on the clearest view, outside the outline, smallest nearest. Overalls first, then hole positions from one edge, then diameters with the symbol, radii with R, angles in degrees. Millimetres, stated once.

Title block bottom right: name of item, your name, class, date, scale, unit, material, first angle symbol.

Hold lines, numbers and a title block in mind and try this part's questions: drawing and dimensioning.

## Subtopic: Draw What the Mould Needs

Moulded features: a section A-A through the socket, hatched at 45 degrees, showing the 2.5 millimetre wall and the ribs. Draft: sides lean a degree or two, dimensioned or noted. Corners: R3, or a note for all. Ribs no thicker than two thirds of the wall. Drain hole with its diameter.

Check before handing in: first angle positions, aligned, scale stated, right line types, every dimension once from a datum, a section with wall thickness, draft and radii, full title block, overall sizes matching the spec. A friend should be able to describe it from the drawing alone.

The drawing feeds the model, and in a factory, the mould.

Pocket summary of the lesson. In first angle orthographic projection the front view is drawn first, the top view directly below it and the right side view on the left, aligned by projection lines at a stated scale, using thick lines for visible edges, dashes for hidden edges and chain lines for centres. Each dimension is given once in millimetres outside the view, with diameters, radii and angles marked, and a title block carries name, scale, unit, material and the first angle symbol. A moulded item adds a hatched section showing wall thickness and ribs, draft angles and corner radii, and the drawing is checked against the specification and mould rules before it is handed in.
