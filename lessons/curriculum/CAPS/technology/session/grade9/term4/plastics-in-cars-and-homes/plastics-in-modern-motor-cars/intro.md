Open the door of any modern car and almost everything you touch is plastic: the dashboard, the door trim, the seats' foam, the steering wheel skin, the switches. Underneath, the bumpers, the fuel tank, the lamp lenses, the wiring insulation and the engine's air intake are plastic too. This case study walks around a car assembled in South Africa and names the plastics part by part, explains why each was chosen over steel, glass or rubber, and looks at what happens when the car is scrapped. It also shows why plastics have made cars lighter, safer and cheaper to run, and why a car body is still steel.
