Today we see how people adapt a material so that it withstands forces it would otherwise fail under. Last session showed that concrete is weak in tension and that timber is weak across its grain and splits along it. Today's two case studies, reinforced concrete and plywood, are the classic answers: combine two materials, or layers of one material, so that each covers the other's weakness. We trace where the steel goes in a concrete beam and why, how plywood's layers are arranged, and what both have to do with the Term 1 truss and the forces you tested on the bench. By the end you will be able to explain why a six-storey block of flats and a skateboard deck are both composites.
