### subtopic_1 — Equitable Sharing of Power: What Fair Access to Electricity Means
**Assistant:** Is it really fair that someone who uses more electricity pays more per unit? They are just using what they need too.

**Tutor:** It is a defensible kind of fairness, though not the only kind, and your report could present both views. The inclining block tariff rests on two arguments. First, heavy use is correlated with wealth: a household running a geyser, a pool pump, air conditioning and a tumble dryer is almost always better off than one running a two-plate stove and three lights, so charging more per unit for heavy use is a way of asking those who can pay more to pay more, without the paperwork of means-testing every household. Second, electricity is scarce and mostly coal-generated, so a price that rises with use discourages waste and spreads the limited supply further. Against this, a large poor family in one house uses more than a wealthy single person in a flat, so the tariff is a rough tool that sometimes charges need as if it were luxury; and a business that uses a great deal of power employs people, so punishing use can punish jobs, which is why industry is on separate tariffs. The honest conclusion is that the inclining tariff is an imperfect proxy for ability to pay that most people accept because the alternatives, a flat price that hurts the poor or means-testing that is slow and intrusive, are worse. Saying that, with the arguments on each side, is exactly what a balanced report does.

### subtopic_2 — Gathering Evidence: Facts, Figures and Voices From Both Sides
**Assistant:** What if everyone I interview says the same thing? Then my report is not balanced, but I did not make it up.

**Tutor:** Then you have learned something about your sample, and the fix is to widen it rather than to invent disagreement. If five residents of the same street all say the municipality has failed them, that is real evidence of a shared view and you should report it, but it tells you about one street, and a balanced report needs to seek out the people who would see it differently: the official who knows why the project is delayed, the paying customer two blocks away who lost a freezer of meat when the transformer failed, the electrician who removes connections and has been threatened, the environmentalist who worries about more coal power. If you genuinely cannot reach such people, you can still represent their side from documents, the municipality's published plan, a newspaper interview with the utility, a court judgement, and you must say in your report that you did so and why. What you must not do is present a one-sided sample as if it were the whole picture, or pretend to balance by softening what your interviewees actually said. Balance is about seeking out every side in good faith and reporting what you found; if one side turns out to have the stronger evidence, your conclusion is allowed to say so.

### subtopic_3 — Writing the Balanced Report: Structure, Tone and a Reasoned Conclusion
**Assistant:** Why does it matter whether I write 'residents complain' or 'residents report'? It means the same thing.

**Tutor:** It does not quite mean the same thing, and the small difference is where bias hides. "Complain" tells the reader that the residents are the kind of people who grumble, and that their statement is an emotional reaction rather than a description of fact; "report" tells the reader only that they said it. Likewise "the municipality claims" plants the suggestion that the municipality is probably lying, while "the municipality states" leaves the reader to judge. Readers absorb these signals without noticing them, which is exactly why journalists and report writers are trained to use neutral attributive verbs: said, stated, reported, argued, according to. If you want to show that a statement is doubtful, you do it with evidence, "the municipality states that the project will begin next year; its 2022 plan gave the same date for 2023", not with a loaded verb. The test is to swap the verbs between sides: if "residents claim and the municipality reports" reads as biased to you, then "residents report and the municipality claims" is equally biased the other way. Writing that passes this swap test is writing a reader on either side can trust, which is the whole point of a balanced report.
