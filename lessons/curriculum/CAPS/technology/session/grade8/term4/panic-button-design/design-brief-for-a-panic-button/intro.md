Today we begin the term's design project. A panic button is a device that lets a person in danger call for help by pressing a button that sounds an alarm or alerts someone, and it is needed by elderly people living alone, by families in homes that have been broken into, by shopkeepers, by learners walking home, and by many others across South Africa. We investigate a scenario, identify who the users are and what they need, and then write the three parts of a formal design brief: the problem, the users and the purpose, followed by a list of specifications the device must meet and constraints it must work within. By the end you will have a written brief and specification for a panic button system that next lesson's circuit and truth table must satisfy.
