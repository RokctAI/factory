Today we meet the simplest form of decision a circuit can make. A logic gate takes inputs that are either on or off and produces an output that is on or off according to a rule: an AND gate switches on only when all its inputs are on, and an OR gate switches on when any input is on. We build both from switches and a lamp, state their rules in words, and find them in daily life, from the car that will not start unless the clutch is pressed and the key turned, to the burglar alarm that sounds if any door or window opens. By the end you will be able to describe what a logic gate is, build and explain AND and OR gates from switches, and name where each is used, which is the foundation for next lesson's truth tables and the panic button design that follows.
