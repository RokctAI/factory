Today we open the battery. An electrochemical cell turns chemical energy into electrical energy through a reaction between two different metals and a conducting paste or liquid, and a battery is two or more cells joined together. We study how a cell works and why it runs flat, then the two ways of joining cells: in series, end to end, which adds the voltages, and in parallel, side by side, which keeps the voltage but adds the capacity. We weigh the advantages and disadvantages of each, from the torch that needs three volts to the solar home system that needs to run all night. By the end you will be able to explain what a cell does, predict the voltage and running time of any arrangement of cells, and choose the right arrangement for a job.
