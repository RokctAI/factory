Today we work with the component whose whole job is to resist. A resistor is a small striped tube that adds a known resistance to a circuit, and how resistors behave together depends on how they are connected: in series, one after another, their resistances add and the current falls; in parallel, side by side, the current has more paths and the total resistance falls, so the current rises. We read resistor values from their colour bands, draw series and parallel resistor circuits with correct symbols, build them on the bench, and predict and measure what each arrangement does to the current. By the end you will be able to identify and draw resistors, state how series and parallel connection change total resistance, and build either arrangement from a diagram.
