Today the turbine is spun not by steam but by falling water and moving air. Hydroelectric stations use the weight of water dropping through a dam to turn turbines; pumped storage stations run the same water uphill at night and down again at the evening peak, acting as the grid's biggest battery; and wind turbines catch the wind on blades the length of a rugby field. South Africa has little big-river hydro but imports it from Cahora Bassa, runs three large pumped storage schemes in the Drakensberg and the Eastern Cape, and has built wind farms along the Cape coasts. We study how each works and weigh their advantages and disadvantages. By the end you will be able to explain hydro, pumped storage and wind, and compare them fairly with each other and with thermal stations.
