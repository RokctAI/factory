Today we go to the places that make most of South Africa's electricity. A thermal power station turns heat into electricity by boiling water into steam, blasting the steam through a turbine and spinning a generator, and the heat can come from burning coal or gas, from splitting uranium atoms, or from sunlight focused by mirrors. We follow the energy from fuel to wire, compare the four heat sources, and weigh each on cost, reliability, pollution, water, safety and jobs, using Medupi, Koeberg and the Northern Cape solar towers as our cases. By the end you will be able to describe how any thermal station works, name what differs between coal, gas, nuclear and solar heat, and set out the advantages and disadvantages of each in a balanced way.
