Today we follow electricity from a power station in Mpumalanga to a plug in a Durban kitchen, a journey of hundreds of kilometres that works only because of two ideas: alternating current, which swings back and forth fifty times a second, and the transformer, which can raise voltage to hundreds of thousands of volts for the journey and lower it to 230 volts for the house. We learn why the grid uses AC rather than the DC of a battery, how a step-up and a step-down transformer work, and how the national grid of lines, substations and transformers ties the whole country together. By the end you will be able to explain AC and DC, describe what a transformer does and why, and trace the path of power across the national grid.
