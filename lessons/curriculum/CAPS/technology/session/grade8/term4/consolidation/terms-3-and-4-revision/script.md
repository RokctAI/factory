# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers consolidation and revision of Terms 3 and 4 ahead of the end-of-year examination. The anchor is a revision sheet folded in half: on one side a circuit diagram, a truth table and a transformer; on the other a gear train, a headgear sketch and a mechanical advantage sum, with a column of past-paper question stems down the middle.

## Subtopic: Revising Term 4: Circuits, Energy and Generation

Begin where Term 4 began, with circuits. Every circuit needs an input device that supplies energy, a cell, a generator or a solar panel; an output device that uses it, a lamp, buzzer, motor, heater or resistor; and a control device, a switch, that decides when. Components are drawn with standard symbols, the cell's long thin positive line and short thick negative, the lamp's circle with a cross, the resistor's rectangle, the open switch, the buzzer's half-circle, the LED's triangle with arrows and its series resistor, and a correct diagram has straight lines, right-angle corners and a dot at every true junction. Series puts components on one path so they share the voltage and all die if one fails; parallel gives each its own branch at full voltage and the others survive a failure. A short circuit is a path of almost no resistance that bypasses the load, drawing a dangerous heating current; polarity matters for cells, LEDs and buzzers, not for lamps and resistors.

Energy in settlements: three needs, heating, lighting and cooking, plus phones; six options, wood, paraffin, candles, gas, solar and the grid, each with cost, safety, health, convenience and environmental trade-offs, and households stack several; the cheap options are the dangerous ones, so solutions must change the economics. Illegal connections arise from absence, cost and waiting; without a meter, earth, breaker or earth-leakage protection every fault runs to completion, causing shock, fire and burnt transformers; the ethics balance need against law, fairness, safety and responsibility, and the practical answer is to remove dangerous wires and deliver safe legal supply. A balanced report has a title as a question, introduction, sourced background, views from every side, discussion against criteria, a reasoned conclusion and sources, in neutral attributed language.

Cells: two electrodes and an electrolyte; chemistry fixes the voltage, size fixes capacity; primary cells die, secondary recharge; series adds voltage with the capacity of one cell, parallel adds capacity at one cell's voltage, and cells must match. Solar cells: light frees electrons across a silicon junction; half a volt a cell, so panels put cells in series; shade, dust, heat and angle cut output; a system adds a charge controller, a battery and an inverter; advantages are free clean durable fuel, disadvantages are night, winter, storage cost, up-front cost and materials. Generation: every thermal station boils water to steam to spin a turbine and generator, at about forty percent efficiency; coal is ours, cheap and dirtiest; gas is cleaner, flexible and imported; nuclear is clean in operation, costly, with waste and risk; solar thermal stores heat in salt; hydro uses head times flow, we lack rivers; pumped storage stores cheap night power for the evening peak; wind is cheap, variable and pairs with storage. The grid: AC at 50 hertz because transformers change its voltage; high voltage cuts losses; step-up at the station to 400 kilovolts, step-down to 132, 11 and 230; frequency at 50 hertz is the balance that load shedding protects.

The questions for this section are with you now: circuits and symbols, settlement energy and illegal connections, cells and solar, and generation and the grid.

## Subtopic: Revising Term 4: Ohm's Law, Logic Gates and the Panic Button

Ohm's law: voltage is the push in volts, current the flow in amps, resistance the squeeze in ohms. With the resistance unchanged, more cells in series means more voltage means more current and a brighter lamp; with the voltage unchanged, more resistance means less current and a dimmer lamp; that is the law in words, and the fair test that shows it changes one variable, measures the current, keeps the lamp and wires the same, and records honestly that a filament's resistance rises as it heats. An ammeter goes in series in the loop and never across a cell; a voltmeter goes across a component. Resistors: read the colour code as digit, digit, zeros, so brown black brown is 100 ohms and orange orange brown is 330; in series the resistances add and the current falls; in parallel the total drops below the smallest branch and the current rises, each branch at full voltage; draw series as rectangles in a line with no dots, parallel as rectangles stacked between rails with a dot at every junction.

Logic: signals are 0 or 1; a gate has inputs, an output and a rule. AND is switches in series, output 1 only when all inputs are 1, the D-shaped symbol, used for interlocks like the microwave door and start button and the two-handed guillotine. OR is switches in parallel, output 1 when any input is 1, the shield-shaped symbol, used for alarms like a burglar alarm on any door or window. A truth table has a column per input and one for the output, rows for every combination counted in binary, four rows for two inputs and eight for three; AND's output column is a single 1 at the bottom, OR's a single 0 at the top, and they differ only in the mixed rows; every row is tested on the bench, predicted against observed.

The panic button project runs through the whole design process. Investigate the need with who, where, when and what, and ask the user. Write a brief as problem, users and purpose, naming the need and not the circuit. List testable specifications, any one of three buttons, large and low, loud and bright, battery powered, findable in the dark, a reset, a year of standby, and honest constraints, the kit, the budget, low voltage, time, weatherproofing. Read the specification "any one button" as a three-input OR, so three push switches in parallel, with buzzer and lamp in parallel at the output, an LED with its 330 ohm resistor, a reset toggle and zero standby, drawn with standard symbols and six junction dots. Write the eight-row table with one 0 and seven 1s, build the circuit and test every row, then test loudness, reset and standby. Design the poster for its audience with one message, one action, a headline, picture, benefits and contact, honestly, in two languages, and evaluate it with the ten-second test.

The questions for this section are with you now: Ohm's law and resistors, AND and OR with truth tables, and the panic button from brief to poster.

## Subtopic: Revising Term 3 and Preparing for the End-of-Year Examination

Term 3 in brief, because the end-of-year paper covers both terms. Gears are toothed wheels that transfer rotation; meshing gears turn in opposite directions, and a small driver turning a large driven wheel gives a slower output with more turning force while the reverse gives speed; an idler between two gears restores the original direction without changing the ratio; bevel gears turn the axis through ninety degrees. Mechanical advantage is output force over input force, and for gears it is the ratio of teeth, driven over driver; for levers it is the ratio of distances from the pivot; a ratio greater than one multiplies force at the cost of distance or speed. Gear systems are drawn with templates or compasses, and systems diagrams show input, process and output. The mine headgear is a frame structure that holds the sheave wheel over a shaft so a winding engine can raise and lower the cage on a wire rope, braced with triangulation against the loads; the tender project ran from scenario and design brief through sketches, a working drawing, a budget of materials and labour, a scale model and a presentation to a tender board, with the impact of mining, indigenous iron-working and gender bias in the industry woven in.

Examination craft. The paper will ask you to draw, so practise the symbols and the gear diagram with a ruler until they are automatic; it will ask you to label and name, so know the terms, input, output, control, series, parallel, step-up, step-down, mechanical advantage, truth table; it will ask you to explain, so answer in full sentences that give a reason, "the lamp is dimmer because the two lamps in series share the voltage", not just "dimmer"; it will ask you to compare and to give advantages and disadvantages, so always give both sides and the same number of each; it will ask you to calculate a gear ratio or a mechanical advantage, so show the formula, substitute, and give the answer with the right form; and it will give you a scenario and ask for a design brief or a truth table, so follow the structure you have practised, problem, users, purpose, or binary rows with every combination.

Read every question twice and underline the command word, draw, label, explain, compare, calculate. Allocate time by marks. Answer every question, because a blank scores nothing and a partial answer often scores something. Check diagrams for dots, polarity and labels, and tables for the right number of rows. In the last five minutes, reread your longest answers for a reason in every sentence.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: a one-word answer to an explain question. Two: advantages given with no disadvantages, or the reverse. Three: a gear ratio with the teeth upside down, driver over driven. Four: a truth table with a missing row.

The questions for this section are with you now: gears and mechanical advantage, the headgear project, and examination craft.
