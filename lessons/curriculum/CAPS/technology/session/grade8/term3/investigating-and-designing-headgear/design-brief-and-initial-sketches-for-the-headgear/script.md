# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the design brief with specifications and constraints and the initial idea sketches of the mine shaft headgear. The anchor is a Grade 8 company in Rustenburg with the tender notice pinned above the table, the investigation record open at the list of borrowed features, the winder gear calculation on a card, and three blank A3 sheets waiting for three different towers.

## Subtopic: Writing the Headgear Design Brief

The design brief for the headgear is written from the client's point of view, in the problem form practised in Term 2: it names the need, the users, the setting and the purpose of the solution, and it leaves the form of the solution open. For this tender a brief might read: "A platinum mining company is sinking a vertical shaft to a reef about 800 metres down near Rustenburg and needs a headgear that will support a wire-rope winding system to raise and lower a cage of workers and a skip of ore safely and reliably, on a site where the winder house stands 40 metres from the shaft. Design a headgear structure with its winding mechanism, demonstrate it as a working scale model that lifts a load up a model shaft, and cost the full-size structure." Every phrase comes from the tender notice or the investigation, and nothing in it says A-frame, tower or how many gears.

The brief also records what the company already knows that shapes the design. From the investigation: the structural forms that work, the back-leg's job, the safety features the law requires. From the gear sessions: the winder's input, a small motor or crank, and the mechanical advantage and speed the lift needs. From the scenario: the depth, the loads and the distance to the winder house, which fixes the angle of the rope and so the angle of the back-leg. A short paragraph under the brief headed "what we know" carries these forward so that the sketches do not start from nothing.

The brief is signed by the company and dated, because it is the first document of the bid and the one the board reads to see whether the company understood what was asked. A board member will check it against the tender notice line by line, so the team does the same before moving on, ticking each requirement of the notice against a phrase in the brief: safety, people and ore, scale model, lift demonstration, budget, presentation.

The questions for this section are with you now: the problem form of the brief with its four elements, where each phrase of the brief comes from, and what the "what we know" paragraph carries forward.

## Subtopic: Specifications and Constraints From the Tender

Below the brief sit the specifications and constraints, measurable and traceable, in a table with a column for the force or principle involved. For the model the specifications might be: the model lifts a cage loaded to at least 300 grams through a vertical shaft of 1 metre in between 10 and 20 seconds; the tower stands at least 600 millimetres tall at a stated scale, 1 to 100 of a 60 metre headgear; the structure carries the lifted load and a 1 kilogram mass placed on the sheave platform without visible bending or sway greater than 10 millimetres at the top; the tower does not topple when the loaded cage swings 100 millimetres sideways; the winder has a mechanical advantage of at least 25 and the drum turns in the stated direction; the model has a working brake that holds the loaded cage stationary at mid-shaft; the cage runs on guides and has a safety stop that prevents it being drawn into the sheave; the sheave is a grooved wheel on an axle, not a fixed peg.

The constraints: the model is built from materials in the class store, card, dowel, craft sticks, string, a kit of gears and a 3 volt motor or a hand crank; total material cost for the model under a stated amount; the model fits a base of 300 by 300 millimetres and can be carried through a door; it is built in two double periods with safe working practices; the real structure's budget is realistic for South African prices and includes labour; the presentation lasts five minutes.

Each row names its reason and its principle. "Lifts 300 grams in 10 to 20 seconds; the loaded cage is the client's purpose and a lift that is too fast looks unsafe; mechanical advantage and drum speed." "Sway under 10 millimetres with 1 kilogram on the platform; the sheave and rope loads act at the top; triangulation and the back-leg against the rope's pull." "Does not topple when the cage swings; a real cage swings in the shaft; wide base and anchoring." The forces column makes the table a test plan for Week 8 and shows the board that the specifications came from the engineering, not from a wish list.

The questions for this section are with you now: four specifications for the model with their reasons, four constraints, and why each row names a force or principle.

## Subtopic: Initial Idea Sketches of the Headgear

With the brief and the table on the wall, the company sketches. The rule from Term 2 holds: free-hand, pencil, annotated, and at least two genuinely different ideas; for a headgear three is better, because the structural forms suggest three natural directions. Idea one, a steel A-frame: a braced tower over the shaft with a back-leg to the winder on the base, sheave on a beam at the top, drum and gear train at the foot of the back-leg. Idea two, a four-legged braced tower with the winder and its gearbox mounted inside the base of the tower, so the rope runs up inside the frame to the sheave and no back-leg is needed. Idea three, a concrete-style tower, modelled as a closed card box section, with the winder at the top directly over the shaft as a friction drive or a drum, which puts the motor's weight high and tests whether the closed section stands.

Each sketch shows the whole headgear in a rough three-dimensional view with the shaft indicated below it, the rope path drawn from drum to sheave to cage, and the gear train shown as circles with tooth counts in the position it will occupy. Annotations carry the material of each member, "dowel 6 mm legs, craft-stick diagonals", the forces where they act, "back-leg in compression against rope pull", "sheave beam in bending", "legs in compression, diagonals in tension or compression", and the safety features, "brake pawl on drum shaft", "bumper under sheave beam", "guide strings". Approximate overall dimensions and the scale are written on each. A second small sketch beside the main one shows the base plan, which is where toppling is decided, and a third shows the winder and gear train in more detail if the main view hides it.

The sketches are compared against the specification table, as in Term 2, with a row for each specification and a column for each idea, and the team notes where each idea is strong and weak: the A-frame is the proven form but its back-leg takes space on the base; the four-legged tower is compact but the rope inside the frame may foul the bracing; the closed tower is stiff but a motor at the top raises the centre of mass and makes toppling more likely. The team does not choose yet. In Week 6 the companies evaluate one another's sketches and each company selects its best idea for the bid with the benefit of that criticism, so this week's task is to make all three ideas clear enough to be judged by strangers.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: a brief that says "design an A-frame headgear", closing the comparison before it starts. Two: specifications without numbers, so Week 8 cannot test them. Three: sketches without the rope path, so no one can see how the mechanism and the structure meet. Four: three sketches that are one tower in three colours.

The questions for this section are with you now: the three natural ideas and their layouts, what each sketch must show and annotate, and why the choice waits for Week 6.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the tender notice on the wall and three blank sheets — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: What the Mine Asked For

Write the brief as the client's problem, like Term 2: the need, the users, the place, the purpose, and not the answer. "A platinum mine is sinking a shaft 800 metres down near Rustenburg and needs a headgear to carry a wire-rope winder that lifts a cage of workers and a skip of rock safely, with the winder house 40 metres from the shaft. Design the structure with its mechanism, show it as a working model that lifts a load up a model shaft, and cost the real one." Every phrase comes from the notice or your investigation. Nothing says A-frame or how many gears.

Under it, a paragraph headed "what we know": the tower shapes that work and the back-leg's job, from the investigation; a small motor in and the MA and speed you need, from the gear weeks; the depth, loads and 40 metres to the winder house, which fix the rope angle and so the back-leg angle. So the sketches do not start from nothing.

Sign and date it. The board reads it first and checks it against the notice line by line, so do that yourselves: safety, people and rock, model, lift, budget, presentation, each ticked against a phrase.

Hold the notice pinned above the table in mind and try the questions for this part: what the mine asked for.

## Subtopic: Must Lift, Must Stand, Must Fit

Now the rules, each with a number, a reason and the force behind it. Specifications: lifts a cage loaded to 300 grams up a 1 metre shaft in 10 to 20 seconds. Stands at least 600 millimetres tall at 1 to 100 of a 60 metre headgear. Carries the lift plus 1 kilogram on the sheave platform with less than 10 millimetres of sway at the top. Does not topple when the loaded cage swings 100 millimetres. Winder MA at least 25, drum turning the stated way. A working brake that holds the loaded cage mid-shaft. Guides and a stop so the cage cannot be pulled into the sheave. A real grooved sheave on an axle.

Constraints: class-store materials, card, dowel, craft sticks, string, the gear kit, a 3 volt motor or a crank. Model material cost under the stated amount. Fits a 300 by 300 base and goes through a door. Built in two double periods, safely. Real budget at South African prices with labour. Five-minute presentation.

Each row: rule, reason, force. "300 grams in 10 to 20 seconds; that is the client's job and too fast looks unsafe; MA and drum speed." "Sway under 10 millimetres; the loads act at the top; triangulation and back-leg." "No topple when the cage swings; real cages swing; wide anchored base." The forces column is your Week 8 test list and your proof to the board that the rules came from engineering.

Hold the three-column table in mind and try this part's questions: must lift, must stand, must fit.

## Subtopic: Three Towers on Paper

Sketch. Free-hand, pencil, words everywhere, and three truly different ideas. One: a steel A-frame, braced tower over the shaft, back-leg to the winder on the base, sheave on a top beam, drum and gears at the foot of the back-leg. Two: a four-legged braced tower with the winder inside its base, rope running up inside to the sheave, no back-leg. Three: a closed box tower like a concrete one, winder at the top right over the shaft, motor high.

Each sketch: the whole headgear in rough 3D with the shaft below, the rope drawn from drum to sheave to cage, and the gears as circles with tooth counts where they will sit. Write the material of each member, the forces where they act, "back-leg squashed against the rope's pull", "sheave beam bends", and the safety parts, brake, bumper, guides. Rough sizes and the scale. Beside it, a small plan of the base, where toppling is decided, and a close-up of the winder if the big view hides it.

Compare against the table: rows for rules, columns for ideas, strengths and weaknesses. A-frame proven, but the back-leg eats base space. Four-legged compact, but the rope inside may catch the bracing. Box tower stiff, but a motor on top makes it top-heavy. Do not choose yet. Next week the other companies judge your sketches, and you pick with their criticism in hand. This week, make all three clear enough for strangers.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The brief states the mine's problem, users, setting and purpose from the notice, never the shape. Specifications and constraints are numbered, reasoned and tied to a force. Three different towers sketched free-hand with rope path, gears, materials, forces and safety features, compared but not yet chosen.

Hold the three sheets side by side, and take the final questions of the lesson: three towers on paper.
