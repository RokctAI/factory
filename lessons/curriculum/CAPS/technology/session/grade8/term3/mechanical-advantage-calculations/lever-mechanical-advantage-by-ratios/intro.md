Today we put numbers on the idea that a machine makes work easier. Mechanical advantage is the ratio of the load a machine moves to the effort you put in, and for a lever it can also be found from the lengths of the two arms, without any talk of moments. We define both ratios, show why they give the same number, and work through real South African levers, a crowbar lifting a paving slab, a wheelbarrow, a bicycle brake lever, a pair of pliers, calculating mechanical advantage each way and checking that a value above one means force is multiplied and below one means distance or speed is multiplied. By the end you will be able to calculate the mechanical advantage of any lever from either forces or arm lengths and say what the number means.
