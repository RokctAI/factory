Today the gear ideas become calculations you can design with. We define the mechanical advantage of a gear pair from its tooth numbers and, equivalently, from its diameters, we connect it to velocity ratio and to the actual speeds of motor and drum, and we extend it to compound trains where stages multiply. Then we do the design calculation the headgear needs: given the load the model cage must lift and the force a small motor or crank can supply, work out the total mechanical advantage required and choose a gear train from the kit that delivers it, with the drum turning the right way at a sensible speed. By the end your team will have a first specification for its winder gear train.
