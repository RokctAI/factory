Today we draw a mechanism as a system. A systems diagram does not show gears or shafts; it shows what goes in, what happens to it and what comes out, in labelled boxes joined by arrows, so that a reader understands the purpose of the mechanism before looking at its parts. We learn the input-process-output convention, then draw two diagrams the curriculum asks for: a gear system with a mechanical advantage of 4 to 1, which turns a fast weak input into a slow strong output, and a gear train in which the driven gear turns faster than the driver. We then draw the systems diagram for the whole headgear winder, which becomes the flow chart the tender board will see. By the end you will be able to describe any mechanism in three boxes and the numbers that connect them.
