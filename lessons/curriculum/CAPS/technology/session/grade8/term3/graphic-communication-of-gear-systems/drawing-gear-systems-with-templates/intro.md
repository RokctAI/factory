Today we learn to draw gear systems so that another engineer can read them. A gear in a technical drawing is not a picture of every tooth; it is a pitch circle drawn with a compass or a circle template, with its tooth count written beside it, a centre mark, and an arrow showing which way it turns. We learn those conventions, then draw the four arrangements the curriculum asks for: two gears counter-rotating, three gears with an idler turning the ends the same way, a small driver turning a large gear slower, and a large driver turning a small gear faster. By the end you will be able to draw any simple gear train to scale, with ratios and directions marked, ready for the headgear working drawing.
