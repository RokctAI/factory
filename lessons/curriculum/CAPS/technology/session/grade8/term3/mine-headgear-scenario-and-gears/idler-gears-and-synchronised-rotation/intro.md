Today we study the gear that sits in the middle. An idler gear is placed between a driver and a driven gear so that the two turn in the same direction, and it has the curious property of changing direction without changing the speed ratio at all, whatever its size. We prove that with teeth, we see where synchronised rotation is needed, in a winder's two drums, in a conveyor's rollers, in a printing press, and in the model headgear you will build, and we ask why an idler is often made of a harder material than its neighbours. By the end you will be able to place an idler correctly in a gear train and explain its size, its effect and its material.
