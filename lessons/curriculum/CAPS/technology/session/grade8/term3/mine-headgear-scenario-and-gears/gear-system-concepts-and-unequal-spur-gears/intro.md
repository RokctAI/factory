Today we open the gear knowledge the winder needs. We revise the four ideas that describe any gear system, counter-rotation, the idler, velocity ratio and force multiplication, and then we study the most important case for the headgear: two spur gears of unequal size meshed together. We work out, from counting teeth, how much the driven gear slows down or speeds up, and we see that whatever a gear pair does to speed it does the opposite to force, so a mechanical advantage greater than one means slower and stronger and less than one means faster and weaker. By the end you will be able to say, for any pair of meshed gears, which turns faster, which turns harder, and by how much.
