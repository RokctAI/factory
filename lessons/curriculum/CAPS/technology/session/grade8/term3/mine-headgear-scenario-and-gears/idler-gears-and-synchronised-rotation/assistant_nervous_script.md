Hey — if a third gear feels like one gear too many, here is all it does.

Two gears turn opposite ways. A gear in the middle flips it back, so the ends turn the same way. That middle gear is the idler.

Its size does not matter to the speed. Last gear's teeth over first gear's teeth, that is the ratio; the idler just sets direction.

It gets pushed and pushes, so it wears fastest; make it the hardest gear.

Open the practice set and say which way the last gear turns in a train of three if the first turns clockwise. You've got this!
