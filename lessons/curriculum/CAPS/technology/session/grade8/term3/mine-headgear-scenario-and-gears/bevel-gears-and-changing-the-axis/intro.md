Today we meet the gear that turns a corner. Spur gears work on parallel shafts; when a mechanism needs to carry rotation from a horizontal shaft to a vertical one, or round any right angle, it uses bevel gears, whose teeth are cut on cones so that two of them mesh with their axes at 90 degrees. We see how they are made and meshed, how the same ratio rules apply, where they appear from a hand drill to a car's differential to the winder of a mine, and how a team might use them to put the model winder's motor where the frame has room for it. By the end you will be able to recognise a bevel pair, state what it does and calculate its ratio.
