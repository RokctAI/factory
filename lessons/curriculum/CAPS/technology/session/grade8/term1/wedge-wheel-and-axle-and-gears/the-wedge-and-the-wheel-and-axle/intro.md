Today we meet two of the oldest machines in the world. The wedge is an inclined plane that moves: an axe splitting firewood, a knife slicing bread, a door wedge holding a classroom door open. The wheel and axle is a big wheel fixed to a thin rod: a bicycle wheel, a shopping trolley, a tap handle. We find out how each one gives a mechanical advantage, why a thin blade cuts better than a thick one, and why turning a large wheel is easier than turning its axle. By the end you will recognise both machines in dozens of everyday objects.
