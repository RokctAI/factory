### subtopic_1 — Water for People
**Assistant:** What is a borehole and where does its water come from?

**Tutor:** A borehole is a deep, narrow hole drilled into the ground to reach water stored underground. When rain falls, some of it soaks down through the soil and into cracks and spaces in the rocks below, where it can be stored for a long time. This is called groundwater. In some places it is only a few metres down, and in others it is a hundred metres or more. A drilling machine makes the hole, a pipe is put in, and a pump, often powered by electricity, the Sun or a windmill, lifts the water to the surface into a tank. Many towns and farms in South Africa, especially in dry areas like the Karoo, depend on boreholes. Groundwater is often cleaner than river water, because the soil and rock filter it as it soaks down, but it should still be tested, because it can contain germs or too many dissolved salts. And if too much is pumped out, faster than rain can refill it, boreholes can run dry.

### subtopic_2 — Water for Plants, Animals and Farms
**Assistant:** How did Cape Town avoid Day Zero?

**Tutor:** It was a combination of rain arriving and, very importantly, millions of people changing how they used water. After three very dry winters, the dams that supply Cape Town fell to very low levels. The city warned that, if things did not change, there would be a Day Zero, when most taps would be turned off and people would have to queue for water at collection points. People were asked to use no more than fifty litres each per day, for everything. Families showered for two minutes, flushed toilets only when needed, reused washing water to flush toilets and water plants, and stopped watering lawns and filling pools. Farmers and businesses cut their use too. The city fixed leaks, lowered the water pressure in pipes, and drilled new boreholes. Together, these steps cut the city's water use by about half. Then the winter rains came and began to fill the dams again. It showed how much water can be saved when everybody helps.

### subtopic_3 — A Water-Scarce Country
**Assistant:** Why does most of our water go to farms?

**Tutor:** Growing food needs a lot of water, especially in a dry country. A single maize plant, an orange tree or a sugar cane field needs water for photosynthesis every day of the growing season. In many parts of South Africa, there is not enough rain at the right times for crops to grow well, so farmers irrigate them with water pumped from rivers, dams and boreholes. It takes many litres of water to grow the food for one meal. Farmers also need water for their animals. Because farming uses so much water, it is important that farmers use it carefully, for example with drip irrigation, which drips water slowly onto the roots of each plant, instead of spraying it into the air where much of it evaporates. When we waste food, we also waste all the water that was used to grow it. So eating the food on our plates and not throwing it away is another way to save water.
