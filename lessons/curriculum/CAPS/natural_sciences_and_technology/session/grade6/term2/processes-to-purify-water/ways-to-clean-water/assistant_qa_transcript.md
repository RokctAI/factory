### subtopic_1 — Removing the Dirt You Can See
**Assistant:** Can sunlight make water safe to drink?

**Tutor:** Yes, in some situations sunlight can help. There is a method in which clear water is put in clean, clear plastic bottles and left lying in strong, direct sunlight for at least six hours, or for two days if it is cloudy. The invisible ultraviolet light in sunlight, the same light that causes sunburn, damages germs, and the warmth helps too. Health organisations have tested this method, and it can kill many germs in clear water. But it has important limits. The water must already be clear, because mud blocks the light, so it must first be settled and filtered. It needs strong sunshine, so it does not work well on rainy days. And it does not remove chemicals or dirt. It is a useful method in emergencies where there is no fuel for boiling and no bleach, but where they are available, boiling or adding the right amount of bleach is more reliable. And in South Africa, the safest choice is always treated tap water, if you can get it.

### subtopic_2 — Filtering the Water
**Assistant:** Why does boiled water taste flat?

**Tutor:** Water from a tap or a stream has small amounts of air dissolved in it, including oxygen, which you cannot see. This dissolved air gives water some of its fresh taste. When water is boiled, the heat drives most of the dissolved air out of the water, because hot water can hold much less gas than cold water. That is why you see small bubbles forming on the bottom and sides of a pot long before the water actually boils. When the water cools, it has much less air in it, so it can taste flat or dull. The boiling has not made the water bad. It is still safe and healthy to drink. If the taste bothers you, you can pour the cooled water back and forth between two clean jugs a few times. This mixes air back into it and improves the taste. Keeping it in the fridge helps too, because cold water tastes fresher to most people.

### subtopic_3 — Killing the Germs
**Assistant:** Can a filter ever remove germs?

**Tutor:** Some special filters can, but our simple bottle filter cannot be trusted to. Germs like bacteria are tiny, much smaller than the gaps between sand grains, so most of them can pass straight through a homemade filter. Special water filters sold in shops use materials with extremely tiny holes, like ceramic or special plastic fibres, and some of these can remove most bacteria. Some also contain silver, which helps to kill germs. But even these may not stop the very smallest germs, called viruses, which are much smaller than bacteria, and they need to be cleaned and replaced regularly, or they can become dirty themselves. Big sand filters at water treatment works do trap some germs, partly because a layer of useful living things grows on top of the sand and feeds on them. But water works never rely on filters alone. After filtering, they always add chlorine to kill any germs that got through. So the safe rule is: filter for dirt, then boil or disinfect for germs.
