### subtopic_1 — Particles and Mixtures
**Assistant:** How do all the Term 2 topics connect to each other?

**Tutor:** They build on each other like steps on a ladder. First, we learnt that all matter is made of particles that move, with spaces between them. That idea explains everything that follows. A mixture is two or more substances whose particles are mixed but not joined, so we can separate them using differences in size or weight. A solution is a special mixture where the solute particles have been pulled apart by the solvent particles and spread evenly between them, which is why it is clear and cannot be filtered. The particle model also explains why hot water and stirring speed up dissolving. Then we used all of this to understand water. River water is a mixture, polluted water contains insoluble and soluble substances and germs, and cleaning water uses the separating methods we learnt: sieving, settling, decanting and filtering for the insoluble dirt, and boiling or chemicals for the germs. So understanding particles helps us understand how to keep our water safe.

### subtopic_2 — Solutions and Dissolving
**Assistant:** What is the difference between evaporation and boiling?

**Tutor:** Both change a liquid into a gas, but in different ways. Evaporation happens at any temperature, but only at the surface of the liquid. Some of the fastest-moving particles at the surface escape into the air. That is how a puddle dries on a cool day, how washing dries on the line, and how salt pans dry out in the sun. It is slow, and it speeds up when it is warm, windy and the air is dry. Boiling happens only when the liquid reaches its boiling point, which for water is about one hundred degrees Celsius at the coast, and a bit lower on the Highveld because the air pressure is lower. When water boils, particles turn into gas all through the liquid, not just at the top, so bubbles of vapour form inside it and rise to the surface. Boiling is much faster than evaporation. Both are useful: evaporation to recover salt, and boiling to kill germs in water.

### subtopic_3 — Clean Water
**Assistant:** Which separating method should I choose for a mixture?

**Tutor:** Look at the mixture and ask a few questions. First, are the parts big enough to see and pick up? If so, and there are not too many, hand sorting may work. Second, are they solids of different sizes, like sand and stones? Then use a sieve. Third, is there an insoluble solid mixed with a liquid, like mud in water? Then let it settle, decant the liquid, and filter it to catch the fine bits. Fourth, is a solid dissolved in a liquid, so that you cannot see it, like salt in water? Then filtering will not work, and you must evaporate the liquid to get the solid back. Fifth, if you need the water to be safe to drink, add a step to kill germs, by boiling or adding the right amount of bleach. Often you need several methods one after another, as at a water treatment works. Choosing the right method always means using a difference between the parts of the mixture.
