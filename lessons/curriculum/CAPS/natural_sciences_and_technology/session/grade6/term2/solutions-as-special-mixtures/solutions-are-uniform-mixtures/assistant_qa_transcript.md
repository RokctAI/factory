### subtopic_1 — A Special Kind of Mixture
**Assistant:** If you can't see the dissolved sugar, how can we get it back?

**Tutor:** You cannot get it back by filtering or by letting it settle, because the dissolved pieces are too small and they never sink. But you can get it back by evaporation. If you put the sugar water in a shallow dish in a warm, sunny place, or heat it gently, the water slowly evaporates into the air as water vapour. The sugar cannot evaporate, so it stays behind. When all the water has gone, sugar crystals are left in the dish. This works for salt too, and it is how salt is made from sea water at salt pans along the coast. We will look at this more closely in the next lesson. Notice that this way of separating keeps the solid but loses the water into the air. If you want to keep the water as well, you have to catch the vapour and cool it down so that it turns back into liquid water. Scientists call this distillation.

### subtopic_2 — Solution or Not?
**Assistant:** Is milk a solution? It looks the same all the way through.

**Tutor:** Milk is a clever trick question, because it looks uniform at first. But it is not a true solution. Milk is mostly water, and it does have some things dissolved in it, like a natural sugar and some minerals. But it also has tiny droplets of fat and tiny bits of protein floating in it that are not dissolved. These droplets and bits are too small to see with your eyes, but they are big enough to scatter light, which is why milk is white and cloudy and you cannot see through it. That is the clue: a true solution is always clear. If you leave fresh, unprocessed milk from a cow to stand, the cream, which is fat, slowly rises to the top. In shop milk, the fat droplets are broken into even tinier droplets, so the cream does not separate so easily. Scientists have a special name for mixtures like milk, but for now, the important point is that milk is not a solution.

### subtopic_3 — Solutions in Everyday Life
**Assistant:** Why is the clinic so careful about the amounts in the sugar and salt drink?

**Tutor:** Because the amounts really matter for the body. When a child has diarrhoea, the body loses a lot of water and also salts, which the body needs to work properly. The sugar in the drink is not there for taste. It helps the gut take in the salt and water more quickly. If too little sugar and salt are used, the drink does not replace what was lost. If too much salt is used, the drink can be dangerous, because too much salt in the body is also harmful, especially for babies. That is why health workers teach exact amounts: eight level teaspoons of sugar and half a level teaspoon of salt in one litre of clean water. Pharmacies also sell ready-made packets of powder that are dissolved in the right amount of water. And the drink is a first step only. A child who is very sleepy, will not drink, has a sunken soft spot on the head, or has blood in their stool must be taken to a clinic straight away.
