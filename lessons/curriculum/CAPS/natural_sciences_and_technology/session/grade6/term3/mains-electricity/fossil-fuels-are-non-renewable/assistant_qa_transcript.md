### subtopic_1 — How Coal Formed
**Assistant:** If fossil fuels come from living things, why aren't new ones forming now?

**Tutor:** In a way, they are, but far too slowly to help us. In some swamps and bogs today, dead plants are still piling up under water and slowly forming peat. If that peat is buried deeply enough for long enough, it could one day become coal. On some sea floors, the remains of tiny sea creatures are still being buried in mud. But the process takes millions of years, and it needs just the right conditions: little oxygen, lots of burial, and the right amounts of heat and pressure for a very long time. Meanwhile, people around the world are burning coal, oil and gas many thousands of times faster than they could ever form. Whatever forms in the next few thousand years would be a tiny drop compared with what we use in a single day. That is why, for people, fossil fuels are treated as non-renewable: they will not be replaced in any time that matters to us.

### subtopic_2 — Oil and Natural Gas
**Assistant:** Why does South Africa make so much electricity from coal?

**Tutor:** South Africa is one of the countries with the most coal in the world, and much of it lies close to the surface in Mpumalanga and Limpopo, where it is fairly cheap to mine. In the past century, when the country needed a lot of electricity for its mines, factories, cities and homes, building coal-fired power stations near the coal mines was the cheapest and easiest choice. The coal could be carried on conveyor belts straight from the mine to the power station. That is why so many big power stations stand on the Highveld, close to the coal fields. Today, coal still makes most of South Africa's electricity, but the power stations are getting old, they cause a lot of air pollution, and the world is trying to cut down on carbon dioxide. So South Africa is building more solar and wind power, and planning to slowly use less coal, while trying to protect the jobs of people who work in coal mining.

### subtopic_3 — Non-Renewable Fuels
**Assistant:** Are fossil fuels the same as fossils?

**Tutor:** They are related, which is why they share a name, but they are not quite the same thing. A fossil is the remains or trace of a living thing from long ago that has been preserved in rock, like a bone, a shell, a leaf print or a footprint. Fossil fuels are fuels made from the remains of ancient living things, so they are a bit like fossils that we can burn. In coal, you can sometimes see real fossils, like the leaf print Karabo found, because coal is made of plants that have been squashed and changed. In oil and natural gas, the remains have been changed so much that nothing of the original living things can be seen. A common mistake is to think that oil comes from dinosaurs. In fact, most oil formed from tiny sea creatures and plants, and most coal from plants. Dinosaur bones are found as fossils in other kinds of rock, like those in the Karoo and the Free State.
