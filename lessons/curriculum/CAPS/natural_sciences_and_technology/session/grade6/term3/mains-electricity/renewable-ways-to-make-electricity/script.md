# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers renewable ways of generating electricity. The anchor is a Grade 6 class in Jeffreys Bay, a surfing town on the coast of the Eastern Cape. On the hills just outside the town stands a wind farm: rows of enormous white wind turbines, each taller than a twenty-storey building, with three long blades that turn slowly in the strong sea wind. The class walks to a viewpoint with their teacher, Ms Jacobs. Standing below one of the turbines, they can hear the deep whoosh of each blade as it sweeps past. Ms Jacobs reminds them that coal, oil and gas are non-renewable and pollute the air. Then she asks: what if we could make electricity from things that will never run out? The big idea is that electricity can be generated from renewable sources, such as sunlight, wind and moving water, which are replaced naturally and do not run out, and that each has advantages and disadvantages. Renewable ways to generate electricity are examinable.

## Subtopic: Wind Power

A renewable energy source is one that is naturally replaced and will not run out. The Sun will keep shining, the wind will keep blowing, and rivers will keep flowing for as long as people need them.

A wind turbine uses the energy of moving air to make electricity. The wind pushes on the long, curved blades and makes them turn. The blades turn a shaft, which is connected, through gears, to a generator at the top of the tower. Just as in a coal power station, a generator changes movement energy into electrical energy. The difference is that there is no coal and no steam: the wind spins the generator directly.

Wind farms are built where strong winds blow often, such as on hills near the coast in the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape. A wind farm may have dozens of turbines, all connected to the national grid.

Advantages: wind is free and will never run out; turbines make no smoke and no carbon dioxide while they work; and farmers can still graze sheep and cattle between the turbines.

Disadvantages: the wind does not blow all the time, so a turbine sometimes stands still; turbines are expensive to build; some people dislike how they look and the noise they make; and birds and bats can be killed if they fly into the blades.

The questions for this section are with you now: renewable energy and how wind turbines work.

## Subtopic: Solar Power

The Sun is the biggest energy source of all, and South Africa is one of the sunniest countries in the world, especially the Northern Cape.

There are two main ways to make electricity from sunlight.

Solar panels. A solar panel is made of many solar cells, usually made from a material called silicon. When sunlight falls on a solar cell, it changes the light energy directly into electrical energy, with no moving parts. Solar panels can be put on the roofs of homes, schools and clinics, or thousands of them can be laid out in rows in a solar farm. In the Northern Cape, near towns like De Aar, Kathu and Upington, there are huge solar farms that cover land the size of many soccer fields.

Concentrating solar power. Some power stations use thousands of mirrors to reflect sunlight onto one spot, making it extremely hot. The heat is used to boil water into steam, which spins a turbine and a generator, just like in a coal power station, but without burning anything. Some of these can store the heat in hot melted salt and keep making electricity for hours after sunset.

Advantages: sunlight is free and will not run out; solar power makes no smoke or carbon dioxide while working; solar panels are quiet and last for many years; and they can be put right where the electricity is needed, even on a farm far from power lines.

Disadvantages: solar panels make no electricity at night and less on cloudy days, so batteries are needed to store energy; and large solar farms need a lot of land.

The questions for this section are with you now: solar panels and solar power stations.

## Subtopic: Water Power and Other Sources

Moving water can also make electricity. This is called hydroelectric power, or hydropower.

At a hydro power station, a dam wall holds back a river, making a deep lake. Water is let through pipes in the dam wall, and as it rushes down, it spins turbines, which turn generators. The Gariep Dam on the Orange River, the biggest dam in South Africa, has a hydro power station. Rivers are fed by rain, which comes from water evaporated by the Sun, so hydropower is renewable.

Advantages: no fuel is burned and no smoke is made, and the power can be switched on very quickly when needed. Disadvantages: South Africa has few big rivers and often has droughts, so it cannot make much hydropower; and building dams floods land and changes rivers.

Other renewable sources include burning biogas, made from rotting plant and animal waste, and, in some countries, the energy of ocean waves and tides, and heat from deep underground.

Most countries, including South Africa, now use a mix: some coal and other sources, with more and more wind and solar power each year. Many families and schools have also put solar panels on their roofs.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking renewable means free to build; building turbines and solar farms costs a lot, but the wind and sunlight are free. Two: thinking solar panels work at night; they need light, so batteries store energy for later. Three: thinking wind turbines are fans that make wind; the wind turns them to make electricity. Four: thinking renewable energy has no disadvantages; each source has some.

The questions for this section are with you now: hydropower and the mix of energy sources.

## Subtopic: Falling Water

Water rushing through a dam spins turbines too.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Renewable energy sources, like wind, sunlight and moving water, are replaced naturally and will not run out. In a wind turbine, the wind turns the blades, which spin a generator. Solar panels change sunlight directly into electricity, and some solar power stations use mirrors to make steam. In a hydro power station, water flowing through a dam spins turbines and generators. These sources make no smoke or carbon dioxide while working, but they cost a lot to build, and wind and sunlight are not always available.
