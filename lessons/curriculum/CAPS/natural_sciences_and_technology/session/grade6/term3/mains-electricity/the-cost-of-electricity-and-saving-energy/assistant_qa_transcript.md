### subtopic_1 — Why Electricity Costs Money
**Assistant:** What exactly is a kilowatt-hour?

**Tutor:** It is the unit used to measure how much electrical energy has been used, and it is what you buy with prepaid electricity. It comes from two ideas. The power of an appliance, in watts, tells us how fast it uses energy. A kilowatt is a thousand watts. The time it runs is measured in hours. So one kilowatt-hour is the energy used by an appliance with a power of one thousand watts running for one hour. A two-thousand-watt heater running for one hour uses two kilowatt-hours. A ten-watt LED bulb would have to run for one hundred hours to use one kilowatt-hour. That is why the heater makes your units disappear much faster than the lights. The prepaid meter counts down the units as you use them, and many meters show a red light that flashes faster when more electricity is being used, so you can see what happens when the kettle or heater is switched on.

### subtopic_2 — Which Appliances Use the Most
**Assistant:** Why does a geyser use so much electricity even when nobody is showering?

**Tutor:** A geyser is a large, insulated tank, often holding one hundred and fifty litres or more, with a heating element inside. It heats the water up to a set temperature, and a thermostat switches the element off when the water is hot enough. But the tank slowly loses heat to the cold air around it, especially if it is in a cold roof in winter, and the pipes lose heat too. As the water cools a few degrees, the thermostat switches the element on again to heat it back up. This happens again and again, day and night, even if nobody uses any hot water. On top of that, every time someone uses hot water, cold water flows in to replace it, and that must be heated too. A geyser blanket reduces the heat lost from the tank, setting the thermostat a little lower means less heat is lost, and a timer can switch the geyser off when hot water is not needed.

### subtopic_3 — Ways to Save Energy
**Assistant:** Is it true that leaving a charger plugged in uses electricity?

**Tutor:** Yes, a little. A charger contains a small transformer and other electronics that change mains electricity into the low voltage a phone needs. Even when no phone is connected, many chargers keep these parts slightly active, and they use a small amount of electricity and often feel slightly warm. Newer chargers use very little in this state, but older ones can use more. Televisions, decoders, microwaves with clocks and computers on standby also use some electricity all the time, so that they can respond to a remote control or keep a clock running. On its own, one charger uses only a tiny amount. But a home may have many such devices, and they are plugged in all day and all night, every day of the year. Added together, across millions of homes in South Africa, it becomes a lot of electricity. Switching off at the wall when you are not using them is an easy habit that helps a little.
