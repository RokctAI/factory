### subtopic_1 — Lights for Safety
**Assistant:** How does a light sensor know that it is getting dark?

**Tutor:** Most street lights use a small component called a light-dependent resistor, or a similar light sensor. It is made of a special material that changes how easily electricity can flow through it, depending on how much light falls on it. In bright light, electricity flows through it easily. In the dark, it lets very little electricity through. The street light's control circuit keeps checking how much electricity flows through the sensor. When the light fades in the evening and the flow drops below a certain level, the control circuit closes a switch and turns on the lamp. In the morning, when the light grows, the flow goes up and the lamp is switched off. The same kind of sensor is used in solar garden lights, and in some cell phones to make the screen brighter or dimmer. If you cover the sensor on a solar garden light with your hand in the daytime, it may switch on, because it thinks night has come.

### subtopic_2 — Alarms and Electric Gates
**Assistant:** Why do robots in South Africa have that name?

**Tutor:** The name goes back to the early days of traffic lights. Before electric traffic lights were common, traffic at busy crossings was controlled by police officers standing in the middle of the road, waving their arms to tell cars when to stop and go. When automatic traffic lights were introduced, they did the same job as the officer but without a person, all by themselves, so people called them robot policemen, or simply robots. The name stuck in South Africa and some neighbouring countries, and it is still used today, while most other countries call them traffic lights or traffic signals. It is a good name for a Technology lesson, because a robot is a machine that does a job automatically, and a traffic light is exactly that: a system with a timer and sometimes sensors that controls the lights without anyone touching a switch.

### subtopic_3 — Fans and Heaters
**Assistant:** Why does a heater use so much more electricity than a fan?

**Tutor:** Making heat directly from electricity takes a lot of energy. A heater's job is to warm up a whole room of air, and its heating element turns almost all the electrical energy that flows through it into heat. To heat a room quickly, a lot of electrical energy must flow through it every second. A fan's job is much easier. Its motor only has to turn some light blades and push air around, and that needs much less energy. A typical heater may use about twenty times as much electricity as a ceiling fan. Kettles, stoves, irons and geysers also make heat, and they are among the biggest users of electricity in a home. That is why saving electricity often means being careful with these. For example, wearing warm clothes and closing doors and curtains in winter means the heater is needed less, and a thermostat switches it off when the room is warm enough.
