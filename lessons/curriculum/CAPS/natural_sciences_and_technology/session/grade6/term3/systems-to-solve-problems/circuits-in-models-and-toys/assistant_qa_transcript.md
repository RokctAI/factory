### subtopic_1 — Circuits in Toys
**Assistant:** How does a toy robot that follows a black line on the floor work?

**Tutor:** A line-following robot uses sensors, a control circuit and motors working together as a system. Underneath the robot, there are usually two small light sensors, each with a tiny light that shines on the floor. A white floor reflects a lot of light back into the sensor, but a black line absorbs most of the light, so very little bounces back. The control circuit keeps checking both sensors. If the left sensor sees the black line, the robot has drifted to the right, so the control circuit slows down the left motor and speeds up the right one, turning the robot back towards the line. If the right sensor sees black, it does the opposite. This happens many times every second, so the robot wiggles along, following the line. The input is light reflected from the floor, the process is the control circuit deciding which motor to speed up, and the output is the movement of the wheels. Many school robotics clubs in South Africa build robots like this.

### subtopic_2 — Designing a Model
**Assistant:** Why does a motor need a rubber band or gears to drive a cart?

**Tutor:** A small electric motor spins its little spindle very fast, often thousands of times a minute, but it does not push very hard. If you fixed a wheel straight onto the spindle, the wheel would try to spin very fast, but it would not have enough turning force to move the cart, especially on a rough floor. A rubber band running from a small spindle to a bigger wheel or a bigger pulley on the axle changes this. The small spindle turns many times for every one turn of the bigger pulley, so the axle turns more slowly, but with much more turning force. This is the same idea as the gears on a bicycle: a low gear makes it easier to ride up a hill, but you go more slowly. Toy cars and electric gates use gears made of plastic or metal to do the same job. Choosing the right sizes of pulleys or gears is an important part of designing a model that moves.

### subtopic_3 — Testing and Improving
**Assistant:** Are LEDs better than ordinary bulbs for models?

**Tutor:** In many ways, yes. An LED, which stands for light-emitting diode, is a small component that gives off light when electricity flows through it. It uses much less energy than an old-style filament bulb to give the same amount of light, so the cells in your model last much longer. It hardly gets warm, so it is safer near paper and cardboard. It is small, comes in many colours, and lasts for a very long time without blowing. There is one thing to watch out for: an LED only works if it is connected the right way round. It has two legs, a longer one that must be connected towards the positive terminal of the cell, and a shorter one towards the negative terminal. If you connect it backwards, it simply will not light. Also, some LEDs need a small extra component, called a resistor, in the circuit, to stop too much electricity flowing through them and damaging them. Your teacher can help you choose the right one.
