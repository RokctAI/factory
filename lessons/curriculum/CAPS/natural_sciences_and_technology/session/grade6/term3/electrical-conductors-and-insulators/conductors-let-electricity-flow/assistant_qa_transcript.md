### subtopic_1 — Testing Materials
**Assistant:** Why do metals conduct electricity when other materials don't?

**Tutor:** It comes down to the tiny particles inside the material. All materials are made of particles, and those particles contain even tinier, electrically charged bits. In most materials, like plastic, wood and glass, these charged bits are held tightly in place and cannot move about. In metals, some of the charged bits are free to move. They are not tied to any one particle, and they drift around all through the metal, like a crowd of people in a big hall. When a metal wire is connected to a cell, the cell pushes these free charged bits, and they all start to drift along the wire in the same direction. That flow is the electricity. Because metals have so many free charged bits, electricity flows through them easily. In insulators, there are almost none free to move, so electricity cannot flow. Graphite is unusual because its particles are arranged in flat sheets, with some free charged bits that can move along the sheets.

### subtopic_2 — Most Metals Conduct
**Assistant:** Is the human body a conductor?

**Tutor:** Yes, and that is exactly why electricity is dangerous. Our bodies are mostly water, with many dissolved salts in it, and that salty water conducts electricity. Dry skin conducts quite poorly, which gives us a little protection, but wet or sweaty skin conducts much better. If a person touches a live wire or a faulty appliance, and also touches something connected to the ground, like a metal tap, a wet floor or the earth, electricity can flow through their body. Even a small amount flowing through the body can cause painful burns, stop the muscles working properly, and make the heart beat wrongly or stop. That is why we must never touch plugs or switches with wet hands, never use electrical appliances near a bath or a swimming pool, and never touch fallen power lines. The small cells we use in class push so gently that we cannot feel them, but mains electricity is about one hundred and fifty times stronger than a torch cell.

### subtopic_3 — Why Copper Is Used for Wires
**Assistant:** If gold is a good conductor that does not rust, why isn't it used for house wiring?

**Tutor:** Gold would actually work well as a wire. It is a good conductor, and it never rusts or tarnishes, which is why it lasts so long in jewellery. The problem is cost. Gold is one of the most expensive metals in the world. The wiring in one house uses many kilograms of copper. Making it from gold would cost more than the whole house. Gold is also softer than copper and not as good a conductor, so it would not even be better. Instead, gold is used where only a tiny amount is needed and where it is very important that the contact never corrodes. Inside a cell phone, a computer or a bank card chip, there are very thin layers of gold on the small contacts that touch each other. Because the layers are so thin, the amount of gold in one phone is very small, but when millions of old phones are thrown away, the gold in them adds up, which is one good reason to recycle old electronics properly.
