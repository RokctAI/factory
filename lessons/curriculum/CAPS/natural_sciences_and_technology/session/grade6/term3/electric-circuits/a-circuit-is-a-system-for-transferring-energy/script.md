# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the idea that an electric circuit is a system for transferring energy, and the components that a circuit is made of. The anchor is a Grade 6 Technology lesson in East London, in the Eastern Cape. Their teacher, Mr Fikeni, has brought a box of broken gadgets from home: a bicycle light, a small battery-powered fan, a toy that plays a tune, and a torch. The groups unscrew them carefully and lay out the parts. In every gadget they find the same kinds of things: one or more cells, some thin wires or metal strips, a switch, and something that does a job, like a bulb, a little motor or a buzzer. Then Mr Fikeni gives each group a kit with cells in holders, wires with crocodile clips, small bulbs, a buzzer and a motor with a paper propeller. The big idea is that an electric circuit is a system that transfers energy from an energy source, through conducting wires, to a device that changes it into a useful form. Circuits as systems, and the components of a circuit, are examinable.

## Subtopic: A Circuit Is a System

In Technology, a system is a set of parts that work together to do a job. A bicycle is a system: the pedals, chain, gears and wheels work together to move you along. Your body has systems, like the system of heart and blood vessels that carries blood. An electric circuit is a system too.

We can describe a system by its input, its process and its output.

The input is what goes into the system. In a circuit, the input is energy from an energy source, such as a cell or a battery.

The process is what happens inside the system. In a circuit, electrical energy is carried, or transferred, through conducting wires from the energy source to the device.

The output is the result, what comes out of the system. In a circuit, the output is the useful job the device does: light from a bulb, sound from a buzzer, movement from a motor, or heat from a heater.

So in the bicycle light: the input is energy stored in the cells, the process is the transfer of electrical energy along the metal strips, and the output is light. In the toy fan: the input is energy from the battery, the process is the transfer through wires, and the output is the turning blades that move air.

The job of every circuit is to transfer energy from where it is stored to where it is needed, and to change it into a useful form.

The questions for this section are with you now: systems, input, process and output.

## Subtopic: The Components of a Circuit

The parts of a circuit are called components. Every working circuit needs at least three.

An energy source. This is a cell or a battery. A cell stores energy in chemicals and releases it as electrical energy. It has two ends, called terminals: a positive end, marked with a plus sign, and a negative end, marked with a minus sign. A battery is two or more cells joined together, which gives a stronger push. Some circuits get their energy from a solar panel, or from mains electricity at a plug.

Conducting wires. These are made of metal, usually copper, covered in plastic. They connect the components and carry the electricity from the energy source to the device and back again.

A device, also called a load or an output device. This is the component that uses the electrical energy and changes it into another form. A bulb changes it into light, and some heat. A buzzer changes it into sound. A motor changes it into movement. A heating element changes it into heat.

Many circuits also have a switch, which lets us turn the circuit on and off. We will look at switches in the next lesson.

The groups now build three circuits, each with one cell, two wires and a different device. With the bulb, the circuit gives light. With the buzzer, it gives a loud, annoying buzz, and everyone laughs. With the motor, the paper propeller spins and blows the hair of the learner leaning over it.

The questions for this section are with you now: energy source, conducting wires and devices.

## Subtopic: Energy In, Energy Out

Mr Fikeni asks the class to trace the energy in each circuit.

In the bulb circuit, the energy starts as chemical energy stored in the cell. It is transferred as electrical energy along the wires. In the bulb, it is changed into light energy and heat energy. The light is useful, but the heat is mostly wasted.

In the buzzer circuit: stored chemical energy, then electrical energy, then sound energy.

In the motor circuit: stored chemical energy, then electrical energy, then movement energy, as the propeller turns.

He points out that energy is never used up or destroyed. It is changed from one form into another. As the cells keep working, the energy stored in them goes down, and in time they go flat. The energy has not vanished; it has gone out of the circuit as light, sound, movement and heat.

The class compares the gadgets from the box. The torch and the bicycle light: output light. The music toy: output sound. The fan: output movement. All four are systems with the same three parts: an energy source, conducting wires and a device.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a circuit makes energy; it only transfers energy from a source. Two: thinking the wires store the energy; the cell stores it, and the wires carry it. Three: thinking energy is used up in the bulb; it is changed into light and heat. Four: thinking a bulb is the only kind of device; buzzers, motors and heaters are devices too.

The questions for this section are with you now: how energy is transferred and changed in a circuit.
