### subtopic_1 — A Circuit Is a System
**Assistant:** Why does a bulb get hot if its job is to make light?

**Tutor:** An ordinary bulb with a thin wire inside, called a filament, makes light by getting extremely hot. When electricity flows through the very thin filament, it struggles to get through, and this makes the filament heat up until it glows white hot, more than two thousand degrees Celsius. Glowing things give off light, but they also give off a lot of heat. In these old-style bulbs, most of the electrical energy is actually changed into heat, and only a small part becomes light. That is why they feel so hot and why they are wasteful. Newer LED bulbs work in a completely different way. An LED makes light directly from electricity without having to get very hot, so far more of the energy becomes light. An LED bulb gives the same amount of light as an old bulb while using much less electricity, and it lasts many times longer. That is why most homes, streets and torches now use LEDs. The energy is never destroyed: it always ends up as light and heat.

### subtopic_2 — The Components of a Circuit
**Assistant:** Is a solar panel an energy source like a cell?

**Tutor:** Yes, a solar panel can be the energy source in a circuit, but it works differently. A cell stores energy in chemicals and releases it slowly. A solar panel does not store energy. It changes the energy in sunlight directly into electrical energy, while the light is shining on it. Small solar panels power calculators, garden lights and some radios. Bigger panels on roofs power homes and schools, and in South Africa many people use them to keep lights, fridges and Wi-Fi going during power cuts. Because a solar panel stops working at night or when it is very cloudy, it is often connected to a rechargeable battery. During the day, some of the electricity from the panel charges the battery, storing energy in chemicals, and at night the battery supplies the circuit. In a solar garden light, you can see this whole system: the panel on top, a rechargeable cell inside, a light sensor that switches it on at dark, and an LED as the device.

### subtopic_3 — Energy In, Energy Out
**Assistant:** Can a circuit have more than one device?

**Tutor:** Yes, and most real circuits do. You could build a circuit with a cell, a bulb and a buzzer, so that the bulb glows and the buzzer sounds at the same time. One way to do this is to join them one after another in a single loop, called a series circuit. Then the same electricity flows through each device in turn, and if one bulb breaks, the loop is broken and everything stops. The push from the cell is shared, so each bulb is a bit dimmer. Another way is to give each device its own separate path back to the cell, called a parallel circuit. Then each device gets the full push, and if one breaks, the others keep working. The lights and plugs in your home are connected in parallel, which is why you can switch off the kitchen light and still have light in the bedroom. You will learn more about these in later grades, but it is good to know that one energy source can supply many devices.
