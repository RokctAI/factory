# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the features of the eight planets. The anchor is a Grade 6 class in Bethlehem, a town in the eastern Free State, near the Maluti Mountains. Their teacher, Ms Mokoena, knows that her class loves swapping sports cards, so she gives them a project: make a set of planet cards. On the front of each card, the learners draw a planet. On the back, they write its features: its size, its position from the Sun, how long its orbit takes, whether it is rocky or made of gas, and how many moons it has. When the cards are finished, the class plays a game. Two players each turn over a card, and someone calls out a feature, such as size or number of moons, and the planet with the most wins. Very quickly, the learners discover that every planet is different, and that some planets win almost every time. The big idea is that each planet has its own features, including its size, its position, its orbit, what it is made of and its number of moons. The features of the planets are examinable.

## Subtopic: The Rocky Planets

The four inner planets are rocky. They have solid surfaces made of rock and metal, which you could stand on.

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no air, so it cannot hold on to heat. In the day, it is hot enough to melt lead, and at night it is far colder than any freezer. Its surface is covered with craters, like our Moon. It has no moons, and it goes around the Sun in only eighty-eight days, the shortest year of any planet.

Venus is almost the same size as Earth. It is covered in thick clouds that trap the Sun's heat, so it is the hottest planet of all, even hotter than Mercury, although it is further from the Sun. Venus is the brightest planet in our sky. People often call it the morning star or the evening star, because it shines just after sunset or before sunrise, but it is a planet, not a star. Venus has no moons, and it spins very slowly, the opposite way to most planets.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun. It is the only planet we know of with liquid water on its surface, air that we can breathe, and life. Earth has one moon.

Mars is about half the size of Earth. It is called the red planet, because its dusty surface contains iron that has rusted. Mars is cold, with thin air, and it has huge dust storms. It has the tallest volcano in the Solar System, and ice at its north and south poles. Mars has two small moons.

The questions for this section are with you now: the features of Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars.

## Subtopic: The Gas Giants

The four outer planets are gas giants. They are huge and made mostly of gas, with no solid surface to stand on. All four have rings and many moons.

Jupiter is the biggest planet. More than one thousand three hundred Earths could fit inside it. It has stripes of coloured clouds, and a giant storm called the Great Red Spot, which is wider than the whole Earth. Jupiter spins faster than any other planet, so its day is less than ten hours long. It has more than ninety moons.

Saturn is the second-biggest planet. It is famous for its bright, wide rings, made of billions of pieces of ice and rock, from tiny grains to pieces as big as a house. Saturn is so light for its size that it would float, if there were a bathtub big enough to hold it. It has more moons than any other planet, well over a hundred.

Uranus is a pale blue-green planet. It is tipped right over on its side, so it seems to roll around the Sun like a ball. It has more than twenty-five moons.

Neptune is the planet furthest from the Sun. It is a deep blue colour and has the strongest winds in the Solar System, much faster than any storm on Earth. It takes about one hundred and sixty-five Earth years to go around the Sun once. It has more than a dozen moons.

The questions for this section are with you now: the features of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

## Subtopic: Comparing the Planets

After the card game, Ms Mokoena asks the class to look for patterns.

Size: from biggest to smallest, the planets are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars and Mercury. The gas giants are all much bigger than the rocky planets.

Position and orbit: the further a planet is from the Sun, the longer its path, and the more slowly it moves, so the longer its year. Mercury's year is eighty-eight days, Earth's is three hundred and sixty-five days, and Neptune's is about one hundred and sixty-five Earth years.

Temperature: most planets get colder the further they are from the Sun, but Venus breaks the pattern, because its thick clouds trap heat.

Moons: Mercury and Venus have none, Earth has one, and Mars has two. The gas giants have dozens each. New small moons are still being discovered, so the exact numbers keep changing.

What they are made of: the inner planets are rocky, and the outer planets are gas giants.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Mercury is the hottest planet because it is closest to the Sun; Venus is hotter. Two: thinking Venus is a star because it is called the morning star; it is a planet that reflects sunlight. Three: thinking only Saturn has rings; all four gas giants have rings. Four: thinking every planet has one moon like Earth; Mercury and Venus have none, and the gas giants have many.

The questions for this section are with you now: comparing the size, orbit, temperature and moons of the planets.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the planet cards in Bethlehem — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Small and Rocky

Mercury is smallest. Venus is hottest. Earth has life. Mars is red.

Keep the four rocky cards in mind and try the questions for this part: small and rocky.

## Subtopic: Huge and Gassy

Jupiter is biggest. Saturn has bright rings. Uranus is on its side. Neptune is furthest.

Hold the four giant cards in mind and try this part's questions: huge and gassy.

## Subtopic: Every Planet Is Different

Further from the Sun means a longer year.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Each planet has its own features. Mercury is the smallest and closest to the Sun, with no moons. Venus is the hottest, with thick clouds and no moons. Earth has water, air, life and one moon. Mars is red and cold, with two small moons. Jupiter is the biggest, with the Great Red Spot. Saturn has bright rings and the most moons. Uranus is tipped on its side. Neptune is the furthest and windiest. The inner planets are rocky, and the outer planets are gas giants with rings and many moons. The further a planet is from the Sun, the longer its year.

Hold the card game in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: every planet is different.
