### subtopic_1 — The Rocky Planets
**Assistant:** Could people ever live on Mars?

**Tutor:** Scientists are thinking about it, but it would be very hard. Mars is much colder than Earth, and on most days it is colder than the inside of a freezer. Its air is very thin and made mostly of carbon dioxide, so people could not breathe it. They would need spacesuits outside and sealed buildings with air pumped in. Mars also has no thick layer of air to protect it from harmful rays from the Sun and space, and it has huge dust storms that can last for weeks. On the good side, Mars has ice at its poles and under the ground, which could be melted for water, and its day is only a little longer than ours. Getting there would take about seven months each way. So people may visit Mars one day, but living there would be like living in a tiny space station on a cold desert. It makes us realise how special the Earth is, with its air, water and comfortable temperatures.

### subtopic_2 — The Gas Giants
**Assistant:** If gas giants are made of gas, could a spacecraft fly straight through them?

**Tutor:** No, it could not. Although we call them gas giants, they are not like a cloud you could fly through and come out the other side. The gas is very thin high up, like our air, but it gets thicker and thicker the deeper you go, because of the enormous weight of all the gas above pressing down. Deep inside, the pressure is so great that the gas is squeezed into a hot liquid. Scientists think there may be a solid core right at the centre. A spacecraft that tried to fly into Jupiter would be crushed and melted long before it got anywhere near the middle. In fact, this is exactly what happened to a probe that was dropped into Jupiter on purpose many years ago. It sent back information about the clouds and winds for about an hour before it was destroyed. So there is no solid surface to land on, and no way through.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing the Planets
**Assistant:** Why do the gas giants have so many moons and Earth only one?

**Tutor:** The main reason is gravity. The gas giants are enormous, so their gravity is much stronger than Earth's, and they can hold on to many more objects in orbit around them. They also formed far from the Sun, where there was a lot of ice, gas and rock left over when the Solar System was young. Some of their moons formed from discs of material around the planets, a bit like a tiny solar system, and others were probably passing objects that were captured by the strong pull of the planet. Many of these moons are very small, only a few kilometres across, so they are hard to see, and new ones are still being found with powerful telescopes. Earth is much smaller, and its one Moon is unusual, because it is very big compared to the Earth. Scientists think our Moon formed when a large object crashed into the young Earth, and pieces thrown into space came together to make the Moon.
