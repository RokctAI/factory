### subtopic_1 — The Solar System and Our Moon
**Assistant:** How do all the Term 4 topics connect to each other?

**Tutor:** They take us on a journey outwards, from what we can see to how we explore it. We started with a map: the Sun at the centre, the eight planets, the asteroid belt and our Moon. Then we added movement. The map is not still. The Earth spins on its axis, giving day and night, and travels around the Sun, giving a year. The Moon spins and travels around the Earth, giving the month, and goes with the Earth around the Sun. Knowing these movements helps us predict where things will be in the sky. Next, we asked how we know all this. The answer is systems that people have designed. Telescopes collect light and radio waves to show us faint and distant objects. South Africa's SALT and MeerKAT are some of the best. Finally, we went there ourselves, or sent robots. The Moon buggy and the Mars rovers were designed to solve the special problems of other worlds. So we moved from looking, to understanding, to exploring.

### subtopic_2 — How the Earth and Moon Move
**Assistant:** Why do we use systems such as telescopes and rovers instead of just going there?

**Tutor:** Space is enormous, dangerous and very expensive to travel through. Even the nearest planet, Venus, is tens of millions of kilometres away, and the stars are so far that light takes years to reach us from them. No spacecraft could carry people there in a lifetime. Telescopes let us explore without travelling. They bring the light and radio waves from distant objects to us, and that light carries information about what the objects are made of, how hot they are and how they move. For the Moon and Mars, which are closer, we can send machines. Robots do not need air, food, water or a ride home, and if something goes wrong, no one is hurt. People are still very valuable explorers, because they can think quickly and notice surprises, which is why twelve astronauts went to the Moon. But for most of space, telescopes and robots are the only way. Each is a system: an input, a process and an output, designed by people to solve a problem.

### subtopic_3 — Exploring Space
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a star, a planet and a moon?

**Tutor:** A star is a huge ball of very hot, glowing gas that gives out its own light and heat. Our Sun is a star, and so are all the twinkling points of light in the night sky. A planet is a large, round object that travels around a star in an orbit and does not make its own light. We see planets because they reflect sunlight. Our Solar System has eight planets, and astronomers have found thousands of planets going around other stars. A moon is a natural object that travels around a planet. Like planets, moons do not make their own light; they also reflect sunlight. Our Moon goes around the Earth, Mars has two small moons, and the gas giants have dozens each. So the order is: moons go around planets, and planets go around stars. In the night sky, a good clue is that stars twinkle, while planets shine with a steadier light, and the Moon is easy to spot because it is so big and bright.
