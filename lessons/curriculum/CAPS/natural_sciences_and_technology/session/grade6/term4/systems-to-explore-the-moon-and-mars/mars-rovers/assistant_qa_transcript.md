### subtopic_1 — A Hard Place to Explore
**Assistant:** How do Mars rovers get their pictures back to Earth?

**Tutor:** They use radio waves, the same kind of waves that carry radio and television signals, but sent across an enormous distance. A rover has antennas that can send signals straight to Earth, but this is slow, a bit like a very weak internet connection. Usually, the rover sends its pictures and information up to a spacecraft that is orbiting Mars. These orbiters have bigger antennas and more power, so they can send the information much faster to Earth. On Earth, giant dish antennas in different countries catch the faint signals. They are spread around the world so that, as the Earth rotates, at least one of them can always point towards Mars. One of these big dishes is in Australia and another in Spain. The signals are so weak by the time they arrive that the dishes must be very large and sensitive. Then computers turn the information back into photographs and measurements for scientists to study.

### subtopic_2 — Robot Explorers
**Assistant:** Why is Perseverance collecting rock samples if it cannot bring them home?

**Tutor:** Perseverance is the first part of a plan that needs several missions. The rover has instruments to study rocks on Mars, but the best laboratories, with huge and delicate machines, are on Earth, and they could never fit on a rover. So Perseverance drills out small cores of rock, about the size of a piece of chalk, seals them in metal tubes, and stores them safely. Some tubes have already been left on the ground in a special spot. Space agencies are planning future missions to collect the tubes, launch them off Mars in a small rocket, and fly them back to Earth. This is very difficult and expensive, and the plans are still being worked out, so it may take many years. Scientists chose rocks from an old lake bed, because if tiny living things ever lived on Mars, signs of them might be locked inside rocks like these. Studying them on Earth could finally answer whether there was ever life on Mars.

### subtopic_3 — What the Rovers Found
**Assistant:** Will people ever go to Mars?

**Tutor:** Many space agencies and companies hope so, but it is a huge challenge. A trip to Mars would take about seven months each way, and because of the way Earth and Mars move around the Sun, astronauts might have to wait on Mars for more than a year before the planets lined up for the journey home. So a whole mission could take two or three years. Astronauts would need enough food, water and oxygen for the whole time, or ways to make them on Mars, for example from underground ice. They would need protection from the cold, the dust and harmful rays from space, because Mars does not have the thick air that protects us on Earth. They would also need a rocket that could take off from Mars to bring them home. Engineers are working on all of these problems, and the robot rovers are helping by studying the ground, the weather and the dangers. The first people to walk on Mars might be learners who are at school today.
