### subtopic_1 — People on the Moon
**Assistant:** How did astronauts breathe and stay safe on the Moon?

**Tutor:** Their spacesuits were like small, personal spacecraft. The Moon has no air, so the suit was sealed and filled with oxygen from a backpack. The backpack also removed the carbon dioxide that the astronauts breathed out, and pumped cool water through thin tubes in a special suit of underwear, to stop them from overheating in the bright sunshine. The suit had many layers, to protect against the extreme heat and cold, and against tiny specks of rock from space, which fly at great speed. The helmet had a gold-coated visor, a thin layer of real gold, to block the Sun's harsh rays. Gloves had to be strong but bendy enough to pick up rocks and use tools. Each backpack carried enough oxygen and cooling water for several hours outside. The astronauts went back into their lander to sleep, eat and refill their backpacks. The suits were heavy on Earth, but in the Moon's low gravity they felt much lighter.

### subtopic_2 — The Moon Rover
**Assistant:** Why has nobody walked on the Moon since 1972?

**Tutor:** The main reasons were cost and changing plans. The Apollo programme was enormously expensive, and it needed hundreds of thousands of people working on it. It was started partly because of a race between the United States and the Soviet Union to show which country was more advanced. Once the United States had landed on the Moon several times, the government decided to spend the money on other things, including space stations in orbit around the Earth and robot spacecraft that visit other planets. Robot missions are much cheaper and do not put people's lives at risk. Space agencies have also learnt a lot from studying the Moon rocks brought back by the astronauts. Now, several countries are planning to send people back to the Moon, this time to stay longer, build bases, and perhaps use ice near the south pole for water. Some of the astronauts who go may be young people who are at school today.

### subtopic_3 — Robot Rovers
**Assistant:** Could a Moon buggy drive on Mars?

**Tutor:** Not very well, because Mars has different problems from the Moon. Mars is much further away. Radio signals take minutes to travel between Earth and Mars, so people on Earth could not steer a rover like a remote-control car, and no astronauts have gone there to drive one. Mars rovers must be robots that can find their own safe path between the commands sent from Earth. Mars also has thin air and blowing dust storms, very cold nights, and rough, rocky ground. The Apollo batteries could not be recharged and only lasted a few days, but a Mars rover must keep going for years, so engineers use solar panels or a small nuclear power source. Mars rovers have six wheels, on special bendy legs, to climb over rocks without tipping over. So engineers took lessons from the Moon buggy, such as metal wheels and electric motors, and then redesigned the system to solve the problems of Mars.
