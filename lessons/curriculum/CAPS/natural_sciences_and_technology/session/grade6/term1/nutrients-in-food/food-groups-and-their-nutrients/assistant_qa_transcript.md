### subtopic_1 — Carbohydrates for Energy
**Assistant:** If fats give more energy than carbohydrates, why don't we eat mostly fat?

**Tutor:** It is true that one gram of fat gives about twice as much energy as one gram of starch or sugar. But the body handles them differently. Starch is broken down into glucose, which the body uses easily for everyday energy, especially in the brain and muscles. Fat is more like a savings account. The body stores extra fat under the skin and around the organs, and uses it when food is short. If we eat far more fat than we need, the extra is simply stored, and over time a person can become very overweight. Too much of some kinds of fat, especially the fat in fried foods and fatty meat, can also build up inside the blood vessels and make the heart work harder. So the best plan is to get most of our energy from starchy foods, and to eat a small amount of healthy fats, like the oils in peanuts, avocados, sunflower seeds and fish.

### subtopic_2 — Proteins for Growth and Repair
**Assistant:** Why do we need vitamins if we only need tiny amounts of them?

**Tutor:** Think of vitamins and minerals as the tools in a workshop. The carbohydrates, proteins and fats are the materials and the fuel, but without the right tools nothing gets built properly. The body needs vitamins to do thousands of small chemical jobs, like using energy from food, building bones, making blood and fighting germs. It needs only a tiny amount of each vitamin, sometimes less than a grain of salt a day, but it cannot make most of them for itself, so they must come from food. If one vitamin or mineral is missing from the diet for a long time, the body cannot do some of its jobs, and a person becomes ill. For example, sailors long ago who ate no fresh fruit or vegetables on long voyages got a disease called scurvy, because they had no vitamin C. That is why the school menu always includes vegetables or fruit.

### subtopic_3 — Fats, Oils, Vitamins and Minerals
**Assistant:** Is maas a protein food or a fat food?

**Tutor:** Maas, like milk, is a good example of a food that contains more than one nutrient. Maas is milk that has been soured and thickened by good bacteria. It contains protein to help us grow, calcium to build strong bones and teeth, some fat, especially if it is made from full-cream milk, and a natural sugar from the milk, which is a carbohydrate. It also has some vitamins. So it is not only one thing. When we put foods into groups, we usually put a food in the group of the nutrient it has the most of, or is most useful for. Maas and milk are often grouped with the protein foods, or as dairy foods, because they are such good sources of protein and calcium. Many foods are like this. In the next lesson, we will look more closely at how most natural foods contain a mix of nutrients.
