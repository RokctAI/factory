# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers processed foods and the things that are added to them. The anchor is a Grade 6 class from Kimberley in the Northern Cape, who visit the spaza shop across the road from their school. Mr Mokoena, who runs the shop, lets them walk along the shelves with a notebook. Near the door there are a few fresh foods: tomatoes, onions, potatoes, bananas and eggs. Everything else comes in a packet, a tin, a bottle or a box: chips, polony, tinned baked beans, instant noodles, biscuits, white bread, cooldrinks, sweets, jam and packets of soup powder. The learners pick up the packets and read the ingredients on the back. Many of the words are strange and long. The big idea is that processed foods have been changed from their natural form, and that salt, sugar, preservatives, flavourings and colourings are often added to them. What processed food is, what is added and why, and why we should eat these foods only in small amounts are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Processed Food Is

A natural food, sometimes called a whole food, is close to the way it grows or is collected: a fresh tomato, a mealie on the cob, an apple, an egg, a fish.

A processed food is a food that has been changed in some way from its natural form. Cooking, cutting, drying, freezing, grinding, tinning and adding other ingredients are all kinds of processing.

Some processing is very simple. Drying maize and grinding it into maize meal is processing. Milking a cow and heating the milk to kill germs, called pasteurising, is processing. Families have processed food for a long time. They dry meat into biltong, dry mopane worms in the sun, make jam from fruit, and pickle vegetables to make atchar.

Some processing is much bigger. Factories turn maize, potatoes, oil and salt into chips. They turn meat scraps, starch, salt and spices into polony. They turn flour, sugar and fat into biscuits.

Processing has good sides. It makes food last longer, so it can be stored and carried far. It can make food safer, like pasteurised milk. In South Africa, maize meal and bread flour have extra vitamins and minerals, like vitamin A and iron, added by law, which helps prevent some diseases. This is called fortifying food.

The questions for this section are with you now: natural and processed foods, and why food is processed.

## Subtopic: What Is Added and Why

When the learners read the ingredients, they keep finding the same kinds of added things.

Salt is added for taste, and because it helps food last longer. Chips, polony, instant noodles, soup powder and many tinned foods contain a lot of salt.

Doctors advise adults to eat less than one teaspoon of salt a day in total, and children even less. One packet of instant noodles with its flavour sachet can contain more than half of that amount.

Sugar is added for sweetness. Cooldrinks, sweets, biscuits, jam, breakfast cereals and even tomato sauce contain a lot of sugar. One can of ordinary cooldrink can have about eight teaspoons of sugar.

Preservatives are substances that stop food going bad, or going off, too quickly. They slow down the growth of the tiny germs, like bacteria and moulds, that make food rot. Salt, sugar and vinegar are old preservatives. Factories also use other chemicals as preservatives.

Flavourings are added to give food a certain taste, like the cheese or barbecue taste on chips, or the strawberry taste in a pink drink that has no strawberries in it at all.

Colourings are added to make food look more attractive, like the bright orange of some chips, or the bright red, green and blue of sweets and cooldrinks.

On a food label, many preservatives, flavourings and colourings are shown by their names or by codes that are numbers, sometimes with a letter E in front.

The questions for this section are with you now: salt, sugar, preservatives, flavourings and colourings.

## Subtopic: Choosing Wisely

Back in class, the teacher, Ms Pieterse, explains why it matters.

Many highly processed foods are full of energy from fat and sugar, but have little fibre, few vitamins and few minerals. Some of their natural goodness was removed during processing. Eating too much of them can fill you up without giving your body what it needs.

Eating a lot of salty food over many years can lead to high blood pressure, which can damage the heart. Eating a lot of sugary food and drinks can cause tooth decay and make a person overweight, and over time it can lead to diabetes. Some people are allergic to some added colourings or preservatives.

Ms Pieterse asks each learner to write down everything they ate yesterday, and to put a mark next to each food that had a lot of added salt or sugar. Many learners are surprised by how many marks they make.

So how do we choose wisely? Choose natural foods most of the time: fruit, vegetables, beans, eggs, milk, maize meal and fresh meat or fish. Drink water instead of cooldrinks. Treat chips, sweets and biscuits as a small treat, not as a meal. Read the label: the ingredients are listed from the biggest amount to the smallest, so if sugar or salt is near the top of the list, there is a lot of it.

Processed foods are not all bad. Maize meal, tinned pilchards, frozen vegetables and pasteurised milk are healthy and useful. The trouble is with foods that have lots of added salt, sugar and fat.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking all processed food is unhealthy; tinned fish, maize meal and pasteurised milk are healthy processed foods. Two: thinking a strawberry-flavoured drink contains strawberries; often it has only flavouring and colouring. Three: thinking preservatives make food healthier; they only make it last longer. Four: thinking the first ingredient on a label is the smallest amount; it is the biggest.

The questions for this section are with you now: the effects of too much processed food and how to read a label.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the spaza shop in Kimberley — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Changed Food

Processed food has been changed from how it grows.

Maize meal, biltong, jam and chips are processed foods.

Processing helps food last longer.

Keep the shelves of packets in mind and try the questions for this part: changed food.

## Subtopic: Added Extras

Salt, sugar, preservatives, flavourings and colourings are often added.

Preservatives stop food going bad quickly.

Hold the long list of ingredients in mind and try this part's questions: added extras.

## Subtopic: Read the Label

Eat natural foods most of the time.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Processed foods have been changed from their natural form by drying, grinding, cooking, tinning or adding ingredients. Processing makes food last longer and can make it safer. Many processed foods have added salt, sugar, preservatives, flavourings and colourings. Too much salt, sugar and fat can harm our health, so we eat these foods in small amounts and read the label, where the biggest ingredient comes first.

Hold the spaza shop shelves in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: read the label.
