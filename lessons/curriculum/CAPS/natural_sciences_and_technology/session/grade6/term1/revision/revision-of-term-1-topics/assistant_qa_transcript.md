### subtopic_1 — Photosynthesis and Starch
**Assistant:** How does the energy from the Sun end up in my muscles?

**Tutor:** Follow it step by step. Sunlight shines on a maize plant. The chlorophyll in its leaves traps the light energy, and the plant uses it to make glucose from water and carbon dioxide. The energy is now stored in the glucose. The plant changes some glucose into starch and stores it in the maize seeds. The maize is harvested, dried and ground into maize meal, and you eat it as pap. In your gut, the starch is broken down into glucose again, which passes into your blood. Your blood carries it to your muscles. There, your body uses oxygen, which you breathed in, to release the energy from the glucose, and you breathe out carbon dioxide. Your muscles use that energy to move. So when you kick a soccer ball, you are using energy that left the Sun, travelled through space, was caught by a leaf, and was stored in a seed. And the carbon dioxide you breathe out can be used by a plant to make food again.

### subtopic_2 — Nutrients and a Balanced Diet
**Assistant:** Why is compost good for a vegetable garden?

**Tutor:** Compost is made when decomposers break down plant waste, like vegetable peels, grass cuttings, dead leaves and old plants. Fungi and bacteria do most of the work, helped by earthworms, beetles and other small animals that break the material into smaller pieces. After a few months, the heap turns into dark, crumbly compost that smells like a forest floor. Compost is full of nutrients that the decomposers released from the dead plants, like nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, which vegetables need to grow. It also makes the soil soft and spongy, so that it holds more water and lets air reach the roots. Sandy soil holds water better with compost, and hard clay soil becomes easier for roots to push through. Using compost is a way of copying what happens in nature, where decomposers return nutrients to the soil. It also means less waste goes to the rubbish dump. A school garden with a compost heap is a small ecosystem that recycles its own food.

### subtopic_3 — Ecosystems and Food Webs
**Assistant:** How are photosynthesis and a balanced diet connected?

**Tutor:** They are connected because almost all our food, and all the nutrients in it, depend on plants making food. Photosynthesis makes glucose. Plants use that glucose not only for energy and to make starch, but also to build other substances, using minerals from the soil. They make oils, which we find in sunflower seeds, peanuts and avocados. They make proteins, which we find in beans, peas and soya. They make vitamins, like the vitamin C in oranges and the vitamin A in carrots. So many of the nutrients we need come straight from plants. Animal foods come from plants too, one step further along the food chain. The chicken got its protein from the maize and insects it ate. The milk came from a cow that ate grass. So every part of a balanced plate depends on photosynthesis. That is why looking after plants, soil and ecosystems is also about looking after our own health.
