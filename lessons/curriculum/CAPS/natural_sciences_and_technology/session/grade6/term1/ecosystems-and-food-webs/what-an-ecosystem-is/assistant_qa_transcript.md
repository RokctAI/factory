### subtopic_1 — What an Ecosystem Is
**Assistant:** Is a city an ecosystem?

**Tutor:** Yes, it can be thought of as one. A city like Bloemfontein has living things: people, trees in the streets and parks, grass, garden plants, birds like pigeons and hadedas, insects, rats, cats, dogs and countless bacteria. It also has non-living things: sunlight, air, water in pipes and rivers, soil, and also things that people have built, like roads and buildings. These living and non-living things interact. Birds nest in buildings and trees. Insects pollinate garden flowers. Water from the streets runs into rivers and wetlands. People bring food in from farms and send waste out to dumps and sewage works. A city ecosystem is very different from a natural one, because people change it so much and bring in a lot from outside. But scientists do study cities as ecosystems, to find ways to keep the air and water clean and to make space for plants and animals, for example by protecting rivers and planting indigenous trees.

### subtopic_2 — Water Ecosystems
**Assistant:** Why does the sea have so many different living things if it is so salty?

**Tutor:** The plants and animals that live in the sea are adapted to salty water. Their bodies are built to cope with it, in the same way that a cactus is built to cope with dry desert conditions. If you put a freshwater fish from the Orange River into the sea, it would not survive, and a sea fish would not survive in a river. The sea also has plenty of what living things need. Sunlight shines into the upper water, so tiny floating plants and seaweeds like kelp can make food by photosynthesis. Cold currents, like the one along the west coast of South Africa, bring up water from the deep that is rich in nutrients, so the tiny floating plants grow in huge numbers. They feed tiny animals, which feed small fish like sardines and anchovies, which feed bigger fish, seals, penguins, sharks and dolphins. That is why the waters around South Africa have so much life, and why fishing is so important here.

### subtopic_3 — Land Ecosystems
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a desert and a semi-desert?

**Tutor:** Both are dry places, but a desert is drier. Scientists often use the amount of rain that falls in a year to tell them apart. A desert gets very little rain, usually less than about two hundred and fifty millimetres in a whole year, and some years almost none. A semi-desert gets a bit more rain, but still not enough for grass to cover the ground or for many trees to grow. In South Africa, the Richtersveld and the area near the Namibian border are very dry, and parts of them are true desert. The Karoo is mostly semi-desert, with small shrubs and bare ground between them. The Kalahari is a semi-desert with red sand and some grass and trees, especially camel thorn trees. Plants in these places have clever ways to survive, like storing water in their stems and leaves, having long roots to reach deep water, or growing only after it rains and then quickly making seeds.
