# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers what an ecosystem is, with examples of different ecosystems in South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 6 classroom in Bloemfontein in the Free State. The teacher, Mrs Venter, has pinned six large photographs along the back wall, each taken on her travels. The first shows the Orange River, wide and brown, with willow trees along its banks. The second shows the sea at False Bay near Cape Town, with seals on a rock and kelp swaying under the water. The third shows the Nylsvley wetland in Limpopo, flooded and full of water birds. The fourth shows the open grassland just outside Bloemfontein, with springbok grazing. The fifth shows the tall, dark Knysna forest. The sixth shows the dry, rocky desert of the Richtersveld in the Northern Cape, with strange, fat-stemmed plants. Each group gets one photograph and must list every living and non-living thing they can find. The big idea is that an ecosystem is an area where living things and non-living things interact and depend on each other. What an ecosystem is, and examples of different ecosystems, are examinable.

## Subtopic: What an Ecosystem Is

An ecosystem is an area where living things and non-living things interact and depend on each other. The word eco comes from a Greek word meaning home, and a system is a set of parts that work together. So an ecosystem is a home where all the parts work together.

The living things in an ecosystem are the plants, animals and other small living things like fungi and bacteria. The non-living things are sunlight, air, water, soil, rocks and the temperature of the place.

Living things are always interacting with each other and with the non-living things around them. Plants need sunlight, water, air and soil. Animals eat plants or other animals, drink water and shelter under rocks or in trees. Dead plants and animals rot and become part of the soil.

An ecosystem can be very big or very small. A whole forest is an ecosystem. A small pond, a rotting log, or a rock pool on the beach is also an ecosystem, because inside it living and non-living things depend on each other.

An ecosystem is not the same as a habitat. A habitat is the place where one kind of plant or animal lives, like a frog's habitat at the edge of a pond. An ecosystem includes all the living things in an area, and the non-living things, and how they all work together.

The questions for this section are with you now: what an ecosystem is, and its living and non-living parts.

## Subtopic: Water Ecosystems

Three of the photographs show ecosystems where water is the most important non-living thing.

A river ecosystem. The Orange River is the longest river in South Africa. Its water flows all the time, carrying mud and nutrients. Fish such as yellowfish and catfish live in it. Fish eagles and kingfishers catch fish. Reeds and trees grow on the banks, and their roots hold the soil. Animals from far away come to the river to drink.

A sea ecosystem. The sea is salty, and in False Bay it is cold. Kelp, a giant seaweed, forms underwater forests where fish, crayfish and octopus hide. Tiny floating plants and animals, too small to see, drift in the water and are food for fish. Seals, dolphins, sharks and sea birds such as gannets hunt fish. Waves, tides and the salt in the water affect what can live there.

A wetland ecosystem. A wetland is land that is covered with shallow water, or soaked with water, for all or part of the year. Marshes, vleis and swamps are wetlands. Nylsvley floods in summer and attracts many kinds of water birds, like herons, storks and ducks. Reeds, sedges and water lilies grow in wetlands, and frogs and insects breed there. Wetlands are very important because they clean water and store it.

The questions for this section are with you now: river, sea and wetland ecosystems.

## Subtopic: Land Ecosystems

The other three photographs show land ecosystems. In each one, the amount of rain and the temperature decide which plants grow, and the plants decide which animals can live there.

A grassland ecosystem. Much of the Free State and Mpumalanga is grassland. There is good rain in summer, but cold, dry winters with frost, so few trees grow. Grasses grow quickly in summer and die back in winter. Grazing animals like springbok, blesbok and black wildebeest eat the grass. Meerkats, ground squirrels and many birds live there too. Much of our maize grows on land that was once grassland.

A forest ecosystem. A forest grows where there is plenty of rain through the year. The Knysna forest has very tall trees, like yellowwood and stinkwood, whose leaves form a roof called the canopy. It is shady and damp on the forest floor, where ferns, mosses and mushrooms grow. Forest birds like the Knysna turaco, and animals like bushbuck and blue duiker, live there.

A desert ecosystem. A desert gets very little rain, and is often very hot in the day and cold at night. In the Richtersveld, plants store water in fat stems and thick leaves, like the quiver tree and the halfmens. Animals like lizards, scorpions and some antelope, such as the gemsbok, can survive with very little water. Many animals hide from the heat in the day and come out at night.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking an ecosystem has only living things; it includes non-living things like water, soil and sunlight. Two: thinking an ecosystem must be big; a pond or a rotting log is an ecosystem too. Three: thinking an ecosystem and a habitat are the same; a habitat is where one kind of living thing lives. Four: thinking nothing lives in a desert; many plants and animals are adapted to live there.

The questions for this section are with you now: grassland, forest and desert ecosystems.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the ecosystem wall in Bloemfontein — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Living and Non-Living Together

An ecosystem is a place where living and non-living things depend on each other.

Plants and animals are living. Sun, air, water and soil are non-living.

Keep the six photographs in mind and try the questions for this part: living and non-living together.

## Subtopic: Wet Ecosystems

Rivers, the sea and wetlands are water ecosystems.

Fish, reeds and water birds live there.

Hold the Orange River in mind and try this part's questions: wet ecosystems.

## Subtopic: Dry Land Ecosystems

Grasslands, forests and deserts are land ecosystems.

Pocket summary of the lesson. An ecosystem is an area where living things, like plants and animals, and non-living things, like sunlight, air, water and soil, interact and depend on each other. Ecosystems can be big, like a forest, or small, like a pond. Water ecosystems include rivers, the sea and wetlands. Land ecosystems include grasslands, forests and deserts. Rain and temperature decide what lives in each one.

Hold the springbok on the grassland in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: land ecosystems.
