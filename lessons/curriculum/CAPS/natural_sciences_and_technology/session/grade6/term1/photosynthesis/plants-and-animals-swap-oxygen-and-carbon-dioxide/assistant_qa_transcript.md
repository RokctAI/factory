### subtopic_1 — Plants Give Off Oxygen
**Assistant:** If plants make oxygen, why do people say it is bad to keep lots of plants in your bedroom at night?

**Tutor:** This is an old worry, and it is mostly a myth. At night, plants cannot do photosynthesis, so they stop making oxygen. But they are still alive, so they keep using a small amount of oxygen and giving off a small amount of carbon dioxide, just like we do. The amount is tiny. One person sleeping in a room uses far more oxygen than a few pot plants. You would need a room completely packed with plants for it to make any difference at all, and even then, air from outside leaks in under the door and through the window. So a few plants in a bedroom are safe. What really matters in a bedroom is fresh air. That is why we should never bring a burning paraffin stove, a mbawula or a gas heater into a closed room at night. Burning uses up oxygen quickly and can make a poisonous gas called carbon monoxide, which can kill people while they sleep.

### subtopic_2 — Animals Breathe Out Carbon Dioxide
**Assistant:** Do fish breathe oxygen even though they live in water?

**Tutor:** Yes. Fish need oxygen just like we do, but they cannot breathe air. Instead, they use the oxygen that is dissolved in the water. You cannot see it, just as you cannot see the sugar in sweet tea, but it is there. A fish gulps water through its mouth and pushes it out over its gills, which are the red, feathery parts under the flaps on the sides of its head. The gills take oxygen out of the water and into the fish's blood, and pass carbon dioxide out into the water. Water gets oxygen from the air at its surface, especially when it splashes in waves and waterfalls, and from water plants. That is why a fish tank often has a small pump that makes bubbles. If a pond gets too warm or too full of rotting matter, the oxygen can run low, and the fish swim to the surface and gasp. Fish dying in a river can be a sign that the water is polluted.

### subtopic_3 — A Gas Swap That Keeps Us Alive
**Assistant:** Where did the oxygen in the air come from in the first place?

**Tutor:** Scientists have found that, a very long time ago, the air around the young Earth had almost no oxygen at all. Then, in the oceans, tiny living things appeared that could do photosynthesis. They used sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make food, and gave off oxygen. Over hundreds of millions of years, they slowly filled the oceans and then the air with oxygen. Only after that could animals like us, which need lots of oxygen, live on Earth. Today, land plants and the tiny green living things in the sea keep topping up the oxygen, while animals, people, fires and rotting things keep using it. The two are roughly balanced. Some of the oldest signs of tiny living things anywhere on Earth have been found in rocks near Barberton in Mpumalanga. So almost every breath you take was made by a plant, or by one of its tiny relatives in the sea.
