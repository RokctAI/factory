### subtopic_1 — Plants Make Their Own Food
**Assistant:** If a tree is made from air and water, why does a farmer still add fertiliser to the soil?

**Tutor:** Good question, because it sounds like the soil does not matter. The bulk of a tree is made from glucose, and glucose is built from carbon dioxide and water. But a plant also needs small amounts of other substances called minerals, which it takes in from the soil with the water. Nitrogen helps it grow green leaves. Phosphorus helps roots and flowers grow. Potassium keeps the plant healthy. Magnesium is even needed to build chlorophyll. Plants use these minerals together with glucose to build their bodies. So the soil does not give the plant its food, but it gives the plant important building materials. On a farm where the same trees grow year after year, the minerals in the soil get used up. The farmer adds fertiliser or compost to put them back. Think of it like baking a cake: the flour is the main ingredient, but without a pinch of salt and some baking powder, the cake does not turn out right.

### subtopic_2 — What Photosynthesis Needs
**Assistant:** Why are some leaves red or purple if chlorophyll is green?

**Tutor:** Leaves like that still have chlorophyll in them, so they can still make food. They also have other coloured substances, and in those leaves the red or purple colour is so strong that it hides the green. If you look carefully at many red leaves, you can see a hint of green underneath. Some trees, like the ones planted along streets in Pretoria and Johannesburg, change colour in autumn. As the days get shorter and colder, the tree stops making new chlorophyll and the green breaks down. Then the yellow and orange colours that were hidden all along can be seen, and some trees make red colours too. Soon after, the leaves fall. The tree rests through winter and uses the food it stored earlier. A leaf with no chlorophyll at all, like the white parts of some garden plants, cannot make food. Those white parts are fed by the green parts of the same plant.

### subtopic_3 — The Leaf Is the Kitchen
**Assistant:** Do plants do photosynthesis at night?

**Tutor:** No. Photosynthesis needs light for energy, so it stops when it gets dark. At night the leaves rest from making food. But the plant is still alive, so it still needs energy at night. It gets that energy by using some of the glucose it made during the day. This is a bit like you eating supper so that you have energy while you sleep. Plants also store extra food, so that they have enough to last through the night and through cloudy days. A plant can make food in bright electric light too, and some farmers grow vegetables like lettuce indoors under special lamps. But the Sun is by far the biggest source of light for plants on Earth. Without it, plants would not be able to make food, and animals and people would have nothing to eat.
