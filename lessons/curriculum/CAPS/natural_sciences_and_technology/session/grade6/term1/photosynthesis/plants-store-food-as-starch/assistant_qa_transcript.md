### subtopic_1 — Glucose Becomes Starch
**Assistant:** If starch is a food store, why does uncooked rice or a raw potato not taste sweet?

**Tutor:** Starch is made of many glucose pieces joined together in long chains. Our tongues taste sweetness only when sugar pieces are loose. A long chain of starch does not fit the taste buds on our tongue in the same way, so it does not taste sweet. But try this: chew a small piece of plain bread or a cracker slowly for about a minute without swallowing. It starts to taste a little sweet. That is because your saliva has a special substance in it that starts to break starch down into sugar. Your body does the same thing as the plant: it breaks starch into glucose so that it can use the energy. Cooking also helps. Raw starch is hard for us to digest, but when potatoes, rice and mealie meal are cooked in water, the starch grains swell and soften, and our bodies can digest them much more easily.

### subtopic_2 — Food Stores All Over the Plant
**Assistant:** How can we tell that a potato is a stem and not a root?

**Tutor:** It is a good question, because a potato grows under the ground like a root. But look closely at a potato and you will see small dents with tiny buds in them. These are called eyes. Buds grow only on stems, never on roots. If you leave a potato in a dark cupboard for a few weeks, the eyes start to sprout into pale shoots. Plant that potato, and each shoot can grow into a new potato plant with leaves. A root, like a sweet potato, does not have eyes in neat rows like that. Its shoots grow from the end that was joined to the stem. Another clue is that a potato can turn green if it is left in the light. The green colour is chlorophyll, which roots do not usually make. Green parts of a potato can contain a substance that is harmful to eat, so they should be cut off before cooking.

### subtopic_3 — Testing for Starch
**Assistant:** Why are so many staple foods around the world full of starch?

**Tutor:** A staple food is the food that people eat most often and that gives them most of their energy. In South Africa, maize meal is a staple, and so are bread, rice and potatoes. In other parts of Africa, people rely on cassava, sorghum, millet and plantain. All of these are starchy. There are good reasons for this. Starchy plant parts like seeds and tubers are packed with energy, because the plant stored food in them. Many of them, like dried maize, beans and rice, keep for a long time without going bad, so they can be stored for the whole year. They are also fairly cheap to grow in large amounts. Our bodies need a lot of energy every day, and starch gives that energy. But a healthy diet needs more than starch. We also need protein, fats, vitamins and minerals, so pap is best eaten with beans, vegetables, milk or meat.
