# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the gases that plants and animals swap with each other: oxygen and carbon dioxide. The anchor is a Grade 6 classroom in Gqeberha in the Eastern Cape. The class has saved up to buy a fish tank, and on Monday they fill it with water, sand, a few stones, some green pondweed and three small goldfish. On Wednesday morning the sun shines through the window onto the tank, and a learner called Aphiwe shouts that the plants are fizzing. Tiny silver bubbles are rising from the pondweed leaves. The teacher, Ms Jacobs, asks the class to work out what is in those bubbles, and why the fish need them. The big idea is that plants give off oxygen, which animals and people use for breathing, and that animals breathe out carbon dioxide, which plants use to make food. The exchange of these two gases is examinable.

## Subtopic: Plants Give Off Oxygen

Remember the word equation for photosynthesis: carbon dioxide plus water, with sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose plus oxygen.

The plant keeps the glucose as food. The oxygen is a waste product for the plant, so it lets most of it go. On land, the oxygen leaves through the stomata, the tiny holes in the leaves, and goes into the air. Under water, it comes out as small bubbles, like the ones Aphiwe saw rising from the pondweed.

Scientists can show that the bubbles are oxygen. They collect the gas in a test tube held upside down over the plant. Then they push a glowing splint, a thin stick that has just been blown out, into the gas. The splint bursts into flame again, because things burn much better in oxygen.

The class does an experiment of its own. They put one jar of pondweed on the sunny windowsill and another in a dark cupboard. After an hour, the sunny pondweed is covered in bubbles, but the pondweed in the dark makes almost none. More light means more photosynthesis, and more photosynthesis means more oxygen.

Plants also use a little oxygen themselves to stay alive, day and night. But in sunlight, they make far more oxygen than they use. The extra oxygen goes into the air, and the air we breathe is about one fifth oxygen.

The questions for this section are with you now: how plants give off oxygen and how we can show it.

## Subtopic: Animals Breathe Out Carbon Dioxide

Animals and people cannot make their own food, and they cannot make oxygen. But they need oxygen all the time.

When you breathe in, air goes down into your lungs. Oxygen from the air passes into your blood, and your blood carries it all over your body. Your body uses oxygen to release the energy stored in your food, so that you can move, think, grow and keep warm. This also makes carbon dioxide as a waste product. Your blood carries the carbon dioxide back to your lungs, and you breathe it out.

When you sit still, you breathe about twenty times a minute. After you run around the school field, you breathe faster and more deeply, and your heart beats faster. Your muscles are working hard, so they need more oxygen to release more energy from your food, and they make more carbon dioxide that must be breathed out.

Fish do the same thing under water. They take in oxygen that is dissolved in the water through their gills, and give out carbon dioxide into the water. The goldfish in the tank depend on the oxygen made by the pondweed.

Ms Jacobs shows the class how to test for carbon dioxide. She pours some clear limewater into two glasses. Using a straw, she gently blows her breath into the first glass for a minute. The limewater turns milky. Then she uses a pump to bubble ordinary room air into the second glass for the same time. It stays almost clear. Breathed-out air contains much more carbon dioxide than the air around us.

The questions for this section are with you now: breathing, oxygen and the limewater test.

## Subtopic: A Gas Swap That Keeps Us Alive

Put the two ideas together and you get a cycle.

Plants take in carbon dioxide and give off oxygen. Animals and people take in oxygen and give off carbon dioxide. So each group gives the other what it needs. The plants in the tank feed the fish with oxygen, and the fish feed the plants with carbon dioxide.

This happens all over the world. Forests, grasslands and gardens give off oxygen. So do tiny green living things floating in the sea, too small to see, which make a huge share of the oxygen on Earth. That is one reason the oceans around South Africa matter so much.

Ms Jacobs reminds the class that the balance in their tank can go wrong too. If they add too many fish and too few plants, the water may run short of oxygen. If the tank stands in the dark for days, the pondweed cannot make oxygen at all. A healthy tank, like a healthy planet, needs the right mix of plants and animals.

People change this balance. When we cut down forests, there are fewer plants to take in carbon dioxide. When we burn coal, petrol, wood and gas, we add extra carbon dioxide to the air. Too much carbon dioxide in the air traps heat and is making the Earth warmer. Planting trees and looking after forests helps to keep the balance.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking plants take in oxygen to make food; they take in carbon dioxide and give off oxygen. Two: thinking the bubbles from pondweed are carbon dioxide; they are mostly oxygen. Three: thinking we breathe out only carbon dioxide; breathed-out air is mostly the same gases we breathed in, with less oxygen and more carbon dioxide. Four: thinking animals do not need plants for air; the oxygen we breathe was made by plants.

The questions for this section are with you now: the cycle of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the fish tank in Gqeberha — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Bubbles from Leaves

When plants make food, they give off oxygen.

Under water, the oxygen comes out as bubbles.

More light means more bubbles.

Keep the fizzing pondweed in mind and try the questions for this part: oxygen from plants.

## Subtopic: Breathing In and Out

We breathe in oxygen.

We breathe out carbon dioxide.

Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky.

Hold the glass of milky limewater in mind and try this part's questions: breathing.

## Subtopic: Give and Take

Plants give oxygen to animals. Animals give carbon dioxide to plants.

Pocket summary of the lesson. When plants make food by photosynthesis, they give off oxygen into the air or water. Animals and people breathe in oxygen and use it to get energy from food, and they breathe out carbon dioxide. Plants take in carbon dioxide to make more food. So plants and animals depend on each other for these two gases. Cutting down forests and burning fuels upsets the balance.

Hold the goldfish and the pondweed in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: give and take.
