# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how green plants make their own food by a process called photosynthesis. The anchor is a Grade 6 class from Tzaneen in Limpopo, on a school trip to an avocado and mango farm in the green hills above the town. The trees stand in long rows, heavy with fruit, and the summer sun is hot. The farmer, Mrs Mathebula, picks a leaf and holds it up. She tells the learners that every avocado on the farm, every mango, and every new branch and root was built from food that the tree made itself, right here, in leaves like this one. The trees do not eat. They make. The big idea is that green plants use sunlight, carbon dioxide and water to make their own food, a sugar called glucose, and that this happens mainly in the leaves. Photosynthesis is examinable, including what a plant needs for it, where it happens and what it produces.

## Subtopic: Plants Make Their Own Food

Animals and people must find food and eat it. A cow eats grass, a lion eats a zebra, and we eat pap, vegetables and fruit. Green plants are different. They make their own food inside their own bodies. That is why plants are called producers: they produce food, and almost every other living thing depends on them for it.

The food a plant makes is a sugar called glucose. Glucose is the plant's energy food. The plant uses it to stay alive, to grow new leaves, stems and roots, and to make flowers, fruits and seeds. The plant can also change glucose into other things it needs, like starch for storing food and the tough material that builds its stems and wood.

The way a plant makes glucose is called photosynthesis. The word has two parts. Photo means light, and synthesis means putting together. So photosynthesis means putting together with light. The plant puts together simple things from the air and the soil, and it uses the energy of sunlight to do it.

Mrs Mathebula points out that an avocado tree can grow to more than ten metres tall. All of that wood, bark and leaf was built from glucose. A tree really is built mostly out of air and water, put together with the energy of the Sun.

The questions for this section are with you now: producers, glucose and what the word photosynthesis means.

## Subtopic: What Photosynthesis Needs

A plant needs three things for photosynthesis: sunlight, carbon dioxide and water.

Sunlight gives the energy. Without light, photosynthesis stops. This is why plants grow towards the light, and why a plant kept in a dark cupboard turns pale and weak, and in the end dies.

Carbon dioxide is a gas in the air. People and animals breathe it out, and it is made when fuels burn. It enters the leaf through tiny holes on the surface of the leaf, mostly on the underside. These holes are called stomata. They are so small that you need a microscope to see them, but one leaf can have thousands of them.

Water comes from the soil. The roots take in water, and it travels up through thin tubes in the stem and into the leaves. On a hot day, the farm workers turn on the drip pipes, because a tree without water cannot make food.

There is one more thing a plant needs: a green substance called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is what makes leaves green. Its job is to trap the energy of sunlight, a little like a solar panel traps sunlight on a roof. Without chlorophyll, the plant cannot use the light.

Scientists write photosynthesis as a word equation:

carbon dioxide plus water, with sunlight and chlorophyll, gives glucose plus oxygen.

The oxygen is given off by the leaf into the air. We will look at oxygen in another lesson.

The questions for this section are with you now: sunlight, carbon dioxide, water, chlorophyll and stomata.

## Subtopic: The Leaf Is the Kitchen

Photosynthesis happens mainly in the leaves. You can think of each leaf as a kitchen, where the ingredients come in and the food is made.

Leaves are well shaped for this job. Most leaves are flat and wide, so they catch a lot of sunlight. They are thin, so carbon dioxide does not have far to travel inside them. They are full of chlorophyll, which is why they are green. Leaves are spread out on the branches so that they do not shade each other too much. Look up into the avocado tree and you will see that the leaves make a kind of green roof, catching as much light as they can.

Some green stems can also do photosynthesis, like the stems of young plants and cactus stems. A cactus has spines instead of wide leaves, so that it loses less water in the desert, and its thick green stem does the food making.

Once the glucose is made in the leaf, it travels to every part of the plant: down to the roots, along the branches, and into the growing mangoes and avocados. Some of it is used straight away for energy. Some is stored for later.

Photosynthesis is not only important for plants. Every time you eat a mealie, an apple or a piece of chicken, you are eating energy that was first trapped by a green plant from sunlight. Even the chicken got its energy from the maize it ate.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking plants take their food from the soil; they take water and some minerals from the soil, but they make their food in their leaves. Two: thinking plants use oxygen to make food; they use carbon dioxide and give off oxygen. Three: thinking a plant can make food in the dark; it needs sunlight for energy. Four: thinking photosynthesis happens in the roots; it happens mainly in the green leaves.

The questions for this section are with you now: why leaves are good at photosynthesis and where the glucose goes.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the trees on the Tzaneen farm — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Food from Light

Green plants make their own food.

The food is a sugar called glucose.

Making food with light is called photosynthesis.

Keep the tall avocado tree in mind and try the questions for this part: food from light.

## Subtopic: Three Things In

A plant needs sunlight, carbon dioxide and water.

Water comes up from the roots. Carbon dioxide comes in through tiny holes in the leaves.

Green chlorophyll traps the sunlight.

Hold the leaf in the sun in mind and try this part's questions: what photosynthesis needs.

## Subtopic: Leaves Do the Work

Most food is made in the green leaves.

Leaves are flat, wide and thin to catch light.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Green plants are producers: they make their own food, a sugar called glucose. They do this by photosynthesis, mainly in their leaves. They need sunlight for energy, carbon dioxide from the air, water from the soil and green chlorophyll to trap the light. Glucose and oxygen are made. The glucose feeds the whole plant, and through the plant, almost every living thing on Earth.

Hold the green roof of leaves in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: leaves at work.
