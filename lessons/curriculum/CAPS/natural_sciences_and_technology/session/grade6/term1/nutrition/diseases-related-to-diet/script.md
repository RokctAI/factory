# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers diseases that can be related to diet. The anchor is a Grade 6 classroom in Upington in the Northern Cape, on the day Sister Botha, a nurse from the local clinic, comes to visit. She brings a scale, a tape measure, a blood pressure machine and a set of posters. She explains that at the clinic she weighs and measures babies and young children regularly, and checks the blood pressure and sugar levels of adults. Many of the problems she sees, she says, are linked to food: some people are ill because they do not get enough of the right food, and others because they eat too much of the wrong food. The big idea is that a diet that is missing important nutrients can cause deficiency diseases, and that a diet with too much sugar, salt or fat can lead to other diseases. Naming some diseases related to diet, their causes and how to prevent them is examinable.

## Subtopic: Too Little Food and Protein

Sister Botha starts with the babies and young children she sees at the clinic. She says that some children do not get enough food, or enough of the right food, for a long time. This is called malnutrition, which means bad nutrition.

The first poster shows a child with kwashiorkor. Kwashiorkor is caused by a lack of protein in the diet, even when the child gets some energy food. It often happens when a toddler stops breastfeeding and is fed mostly on thin maize porridge with no milk, eggs, beans or meat. The child has a swollen belly, swollen legs and feet, and a round, puffy face, because water builds up in the body. The child's hair may become thin and change colour, the skin may peel, and the child is unhappy and does not want to play. The swelling makes the child look fat, but the child is actually very ill.

The second poster shows a child with marasmus. Marasmus is caused by a lack of food in general, so the body does not get enough energy or protein. The child is extremely thin, with bones showing through the skin, a wrinkled face that looks old, and very little fat or muscle.

Both diseases are very dangerous for young children, and can slow down their growth and the development of their brains. Children who do not get enough good food over a long time may become stunted, which means they are much shorter than they should be for their age. Both diseases can be prevented by a balanced diet with enough energy food and protein, such as beans, peanuts, eggs, milk, fish or meat, and by breastfeeding babies.

The questions for this section are with you now: malnutrition, kwashiorkor, marasmus and stunting.

## Subtopic: Missing Vitamins and Minerals

The next posters show diseases caused by a lack of a vitamin or a mineral. These are also called deficiency diseases.

Scurvy is caused by a lack of vitamin C. A person with scurvy has bleeding, swollen gums, loose teeth, bruises that appear easily and wounds that heal slowly. Long ago, sailors on long sea voyages often got scurvy, until it was discovered that eating oranges, lemons or limes prevented it. Vitamin C is found in citrus fruit, guavas, tomatoes, cabbage and other fresh fruit and vegetables.

Rickets is caused by a lack of vitamin D or calcium. The bones of a child with rickets become soft and weak and may bend, so the child's legs become bowed. Our skin makes vitamin D in sunlight, and calcium comes from milk, maas, cheese and small fish eaten with their bones.

Anaemia is often caused by a lack of iron. Iron is needed to make blood that carries oxygen. A person with anaemia is pale, tired and weak, and gets out of breath easily. Iron is found in red meat, liver, beans, lentils and dark green leaves like spinach and morogo.

Night blindness is caused by a lack of vitamin A. A person struggles to see in dim light. Vitamin A also helps the body fight infections. It is found in orange and yellow vegetables like carrots, butternut, sweet potatoes and pumpkin, in dark green leaves, and in liver, eggs and milk.

The questions for this section are with you now: scurvy, rickets, anaemia and night blindness.

## Subtopic: Too Much Sugar, Salt and Fat

Sister Botha now turns to the problems caused by eating too much of some foods.

Obesity means being so overweight that it harms health. It happens when a person takes in much more energy from food than the body uses, over a long time, and the extra is stored as fat. Eating a lot of sugary and fatty food, drinking cooldrinks, and sitting still for many hours a day all add to it. Obesity can cause problems with the heart, joints and breathing.

Type 2 diabetes is a disease in which the body cannot control the amount of sugar in the blood. It is linked to being overweight and to eating a lot of sugar and refined starch, and to not exercising. It can damage the eyes, the kidneys and the nerves in the feet. It used to be seen mainly in adults, but more and more young people are getting it.

High blood pressure means the heart has to push the blood too hard around the body. Eating too much salt over many years is one of the causes. It can lead to heart attacks and strokes.

Tooth decay is caused when bacteria in the mouth feed on sugar and make acid, which eats holes in the teeth.

Sister Botha uses her tape measure and scale to show how nurses check a child's growth, and says that regular exercise is just as important as good food.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the child with kwashiorkor is well fed because the belly is big; the swelling is from water, and the child lacks protein. Two: thinking only poor people get diet diseases; anyone who eats too much sugar, salt and fat can become ill. Three: thinking vitamins come only from tablets; fresh fruit and vegetables are full of them. Four: thinking diabetes only affects old people; young people can get it too.

The questions for this section are with you now: obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure and tooth decay.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the clinic nurse in Upington — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Not Enough Food

Too little protein causes kwashiorkor: a swollen belly and legs.

Too little food causes marasmus: very thin.

Keep the clinic posters in mind and try the questions for this part: not enough food.

## Subtopic: Missing Vitamins

No vitamin C: scurvy. No vitamin D or calcium: rickets.

No iron: anaemia. No vitamin A: night blindness.

Hold the bowl of oranges in mind and try this part's questions: missing vitamins.

## Subtopic: Too Much of a Good Thing

Too much sugar, salt and fat can make us ill.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Some diseases are caused by a diet that lacks nutrients. Kwashiorkor is caused by too little protein, and marasmus by too little food. Scurvy comes from a lack of vitamin C, rickets from a lack of vitamin D or calcium, anaemia from a lack of iron, and night blindness from a lack of vitamin A. Too much sugar, salt and fat can lead to obesity, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and tooth decay. A balanced diet and exercise help prevent these diseases.

Hold Sister Botha's scale and tape measure in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: too much of a good thing.
