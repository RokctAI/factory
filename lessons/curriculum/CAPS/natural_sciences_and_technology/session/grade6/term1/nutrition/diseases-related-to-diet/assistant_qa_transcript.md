### subtopic_1 — Too Little Food and Protein
**Assistant:** How can a person be overweight and still be malnourished?

**Tutor:** It sounds strange, but it happens often. Malnutrition means bad nutrition, not only too little food. A person can eat a lot of energy, from chips, white bread, sweets, fried food and cooldrinks, and so they put on weight. But those foods have very few vitamins and minerals, and little protein and fibre. So the person's body gets too much energy but too little of the other nutrients it needs. They may be overweight and also have anaemia from a lack of iron, or get sick often because they lack vitamins. Doctors sometimes call this hidden hunger, because you cannot see it by looking at someone's size. In South Africa, some families have both problems in the same house: a young child who is stunted and an adult who is obese. The answer is the same for both: a varied diet with plenty of vegetables, fruit, beans and other protein foods, and fewer sugary and fatty processed foods.

### subtopic_2 — Missing Vitamins and Minerals
**Assistant:** Why do we need sunlight for strong bones?

**Tutor:** Our bones are made hard by calcium, a mineral we get from milk, maas, cheese, small fish with soft bones, and some green vegetables. But the body cannot take in calcium properly from the food in the gut unless it has enough vitamin D. Vitamin D is a little unusual, because our skin can make it when sunlight shines on it. In a sunny country like South Africa, most children make enough vitamin D just by playing outside for a while each day, with their arms and legs uncovered. Children who stay indoors almost all the time, or who are always completely covered, may not make enough. Some foods, like eggs, oily fish such as pilchards, and fortified foods, also contain vitamin D. Without enough vitamin D and calcium, a growing child's bones stay soft and can bend, which is the disease called rickets. Of course, too much sun burns the skin, so we also need hats and shade at midday.

### subtopic_3 — Too Much Sugar, Salt and Fat
**Assistant:** Can a person get better from kwashiorkor?

**Tutor:** Yes, especially if the child is treated early. A child with kwashiorkor is very ill and usually needs to be treated at a clinic or hospital, because their body is weak and they catch infections easily. Nurses and doctors start feeding the child slowly and carefully with special milk-based foods, because a very weak body cannot handle a big meal all at once. They treat any infections and give vitamins and minerals. Over a few weeks, as the child gets enough protein and energy, the swelling goes down, the skin heals, the hair grows back with its normal colour, and the child starts to smile and play again. After going home, the family is taught how to give the child a balanced diet with protein foods like beans, eggs, milk, peanut butter or fish. If a child has been malnourished for a very long time, their growth may stay slowed, which is why clinics weigh and measure young children regularly, to catch problems early.
