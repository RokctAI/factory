### subtopic_1 — The Moon Seems to Change Shape
**Assistant:** Why can we sometimes see the dark part of the Moon glowing faintly?

**Tutor:** Well spotted! When the Moon is a thin crescent, you can sometimes see the rest of the round Moon glowing very faintly, a soft grey. This is called earthshine. Here is how it works. Sunlight falls on the Earth, and the Earth's clouds, oceans and land bounce some of it back out into space. Some of that light lands on the dark part of the Moon. The Moon then bounces a little of that light back to us. So you are seeing sunlight that has bounced off the Earth, travelled to the Moon and bounced back to your eyes. Earthshine is easiest to see a few days before or after the new Moon, just after sunset or before sunrise, when the sky is dark and the crescent is thin. Next time you see a thin crescent Moon from your window, look carefully at the dark part. If the sky is clear, you may see the whole round Moon, a bright crescent with a ghostly grey ball beside it.

### subtopic_2 — Why We See Phases
**Assistant:** What is an eclipse of the Moon?

**Tutor:** An eclipse of the Moon, also called a lunar eclipse, happens when the Earth passes exactly between the Sun and the Moon, so the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. This can only happen at full Moon, when the Moon is on the opposite side of the Earth from the Sun. Most full Moons are a little above or a little below the Earth's shadow, so most months there is no eclipse. But a few times a year, or sometimes less, everything lines up. During the eclipse, the bright full Moon slowly darkens, and often it turns a deep orange or red colour. People sometimes call this a blood Moon. Unlike an eclipse of the Sun, an eclipse of the Moon is safe to look at with your eyes. It is important to remember that eclipses are not the same as phases. Phases happen every month as the Moon goes around the Earth. An eclipse is a rare event when the Earth's shadow actually falls on the Moon.

### subtopic_3 — The Phases and the Cycle
**Assistant:** Why does the full Moon rise when the Sun sets?

**Tutor:** At full Moon, the Moon is on the opposite side of the Earth from the Sun. That is why we can see its whole lit half. Because it is opposite the Sun, it does the opposite of what the Sun does. When the Sun sets in the west in the evening, the full Moon rises in the east. The full Moon then stays in the sky all night, and when the Sun rises in the east in the morning, the full Moon sets in the west. So the full Moon is a night-time Moon. Other phases rise and set at different times. A waxing crescent is close to the Sun in the sky, so it is seen low in the west just after sunset and sets soon after. A last quarter Moon rises around midnight and can still be seen in the morning sky after sunrise.
