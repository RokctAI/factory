### subtopic_1 — Closer Is Hotter, Further Is Colder
**Assistant:** Could people ever live on Mars?

**Tutor:** Scientists are working on this question right now. Mars is the planet most like the Earth, but it would still be very hard to live there. Mars is very cold, much colder than Antarctica on most days. Its air is very thin and does not have enough oxygen to breathe, so people would need special suits and sealed buildings. The water on Mars is frozen as ice in the ground and at its poles, so people would have to melt it. Mars also does not protect people from the Sun's harmful rays as well as the Earth's air does. And Mars is very far away. A trip there would take many months. Robot explorers called rovers have already landed on Mars, driving around, taking photographs and testing the rocks and soil. One day, people may visit Mars, but they would have to bring almost everything they need with them. The Earth is still the best home we have, and that is a good reason to look after it.

### subtopic_2 — The Earth Is Just Right
**Assistant:** Is there life anywhere else in space?

**Tutor:** Nobody knows yet, and it is one of the biggest questions in science. So far, living things have only been found on the Earth. But space is enormous. There are billions of stars in our Milky Way alone, and scientists have discovered thousands of planets travelling around other stars. Some of those planets are at just the right distance from their star for water to be liquid, like the Earth. Scientists look at these planets with powerful telescopes, searching for signs of water and air. In our own solar system, scientists are also curious about some of the moons of Jupiter and Saturn, which may have oceans of liquid water hidden under thick ice. If there is life in those places, it might be tiny, like germs, rather than animals or people. Telescopes like the ones at Sutherland, and big radio telescopes being built in the Karoo, help scientists explore space and search for answers.

### subtopic_3 — Air and Water Help
**Assistant:** Why is it hot in summer and cold in winter if the Earth stays the same distance from the Sun?

**Tutor:** This is a clever question, and the answer surprises many people. Summer and winter are not caused by the Earth moving closer to and further from the Sun. The Earth is tilted, a bit like a spinning top that leans to one side. As the Earth travels around the Sun during the year, sometimes the southern half of the Earth, where South Africa is, leans towards the Sun. Then the Sun is high in the sky at midday, its rays hit the ground more directly, and the days are long, so it is summer. Six months later, the southern half leans away from the Sun. The Sun is lower in the sky, its rays are more spread out, and the days are short, so it is winter. That is why, when it is summer in South Africa, it is winter in Europe, and the other way around. You will learn more about this in later grades.
