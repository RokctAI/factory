# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the Sun: what it is made of, how big it is and how far away it is. The anchor is a primary school in Polokwane, in Limpopo, where summer days are long and hot. Ms Dlamini's Grade 4 class has a question: if the Sun is a star, why does it look so different from the stars at night? To find out, the class builds a model on the school soccer field. They use a marble for the Earth and a huge yellow exercise ball for the Sun, and they measure out the distance between them in big steps. The big idea is that the Sun is a star, a giant ball of hot gas that gives out heat and light, that it is far bigger than the Earth and very far away, and that it is the closest star to us. Describing the Sun as a star and comparing its size and distance to the Earth is examinable.

## Subtopic: The Sun Is a Star Made of Hot Gas

The Sun is a star. Like all stars, it is a giant ball of hot, glowing gas. It is not solid like the Earth. There is no ground on the Sun to stand on.

The Sun is extremely hot. Its surface is thousands of degrees, many times hotter than the flame on a gas stove. Its centre is millions of degrees, hotter than anything on Earth.

The Sun is not a fire like a braai fire. A braai fire needs wood and air to burn. There is no air in space. Instead, deep inside the centre of the Sun, tiny pieces of gas are squeezed together so hard that they join up, and this gives out enormous amounts of energy. That energy travels out from the Sun as heat and light.

The Sun makes its own light. That is what makes it a star. The Earth and the Moon do not make their own light. We see the Moon because sunlight bounces off it.

The heat and light from the Sun travel through space to the Earth. That is why we feel warm when we stand in the sunshine and cooler when we stand in the shade. On a hot summer afternoon in Polokwane, a car parked in the sun becomes so hot inside that it is dangerous for anyone, especially children and pets, to be left in it. All that heat came from the Sun, far away in space.

The Sun has been shining for a very, very long time, more than four thousand million years, and it will keep shining for a very long time still.

The questions for this section are with you now: what the Sun is made of and what it gives out.

## Subtopic: The Sun Is Huge but Very Far Away

The Sun looks like a small, bright circle in the sky. It is about the same size in the sky as the Moon. But the Sun is really much, much bigger than the Earth.

The Sun is more than one hundred times wider than the Earth. If the Sun were a hollow ball, more than a million Earths could fit inside it.

So why does it look small? Because it is very, very far away. The Sun is about one hundred and fifty million kilometres from the Earth. If you could drive a car to the Sun at one hundred kilometres an hour without ever stopping, the trip would take more than one hundred and seventy years. Even light, the fastest thing there is, takes about eight minutes to travel from the Sun to the Earth.

This is where Ms Dlamini's model comes in. If the Earth is a marble about one centimetre across, then the Sun must be a ball more than a metre across. And to show the distance, the Sun ball must be more than one hundred metres away from the marble. The class puts the marble on one goal line and has to carry the big yellow ball right past the other goal line before it is far enough. From the marble, the giant ball looks quite small.

Things far away always look smaller than they really are. An aeroplane high in the sky looks tiny, but it is really bigger than a bus.

The questions for this section are with you now: the size of the Sun and how far away it is.

## Subtopic: The Closest Star to Earth

If the Sun is a star, why does it look so different from the stars at night? The answer is distance.

The Sun is the star closest to the Earth. All the other stars are much, much further away. Light from the Sun reaches us in about eight minutes, but light from the next closest star takes more than four years. That is why the Sun looks like a big, blazing ball, while the other stars look like tiny points of light.

South Africans can see one of the Sun's nearest neighbours. The brighter of the two Pointer stars next to the Southern Cross is one of the closest stars to us after the Sun. It still looks like just a bright dot, because even it is so far away.

The Sun is not the biggest star. Some stars are many times bigger and brighter than the Sun. Some are smaller. The Sun is a medium-sized star. It only looks like the most important star to us because it is so close, and because it gives the Earth the heat and light we need.

Stars also come in different colours. Hotter stars shine bluish white, and cooler stars shine orange or red. The Sun is often called a yellow star.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying the Sun is a ball of fire that burns wood or air; it is a ball of hot gas that makes energy deep inside. Two: saying the Sun is smaller than the Earth because it looks small; it is more than a hundred times wider. Three: saying the Sun is not a star; it is a star, just very close. Four: saying the Sun is the biggest star; it is a medium-sized star.

The questions for this section are with you now: why the Sun is the closest and brightest star to us.
