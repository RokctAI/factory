### subtopic_1 — The Sun Is a Star Made of Hot Gas
**Assistant:** Will the Sun ever stop shining?

**Tutor:** Yes, one day, but not for a very, very long time. The Sun has been shining for more than four thousand million years, and scientists think it has enough gas in its centre to keep shining for about another five thousand million years. That is so far in the future that it is hard to imagine. No person, and probably no animal or plant alive today, will be around to see it. Scientists have learnt this by studying many other stars. Because there are billions of stars, they can see stars at every stage of their lives, from young ones to very old ones, a bit like looking at a crowd and seeing babies, children, adults and grandparents. When the Sun is very old, it will swell up into a much bigger star, then slowly shrink and cool down. So you do not need to worry about the Sun running out. It will be shining every day of your life, and for a very long time after that.

### subtopic_2 — The Sun Is Huge but Very Far Away
**Assistant:** Why is the Sun so dangerous to look at if it is so far away?

**Tutor:** Even though the Sun is about one hundred and fifty million kilometres away, it is so big and so bright that its light is still very strong when it reaches us. The inside of your eye has a part at the back that works like the screen of a camera. When you look at something, your eye focuses its light onto that back part. If you look straight at the Sun, your eye focuses that very strong light into a tiny spot, a bit like using a magnifying glass to focus sunlight onto a leaf until it smokes. This can burn the back of your eye. The scary part is that it does not hurt while it is happening, so you may not notice until it is too late. The damage can last forever. That is why you must never look straight at the Sun, not with sunglasses, not through binoculars and not through a telescope.

### subtopic_3 — The Closest Star to Earth
**Assistant:** If the Sun is so hot, why is space so cold?

**Tutor:** This is a clever question. Heat from the Sun travels through space, but space is almost completely empty. There is no air and nothing much to warm up. Heat only makes something feel warm when it lands on something, like the ground, the sea, your skin or a rock. So space itself does not get warm. An object in space in the sunshine, like the Moon or a spacecraft, gets very hot on the side facing the Sun. But the side facing away from the Sun, in the shade, gets extremely cold, much colder than any freezer. On the Earth, our layer of air acts like a blanket. It holds some of the Sun's heat and spreads it around, so even at night it does not get freezing cold in most places. Places far from the Sun, like the outer planets, get very little of the Sun's heat at all, so they are extremely cold.
