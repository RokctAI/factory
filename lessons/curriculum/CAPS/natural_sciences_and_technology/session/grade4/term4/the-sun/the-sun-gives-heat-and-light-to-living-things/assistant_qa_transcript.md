### subtopic_1 — Plants Need Sunlight
**Assistant:** Can you get sunburnt on a cloudy day?

**Tutor:** Yes, you can, and many people are surprised by this. Sunburn is caused by invisible rays in sunlight. These rays are not the same as the heat you feel or the light you see. Thin clouds let most of these invisible rays through, so even if the day feels cool and grey, your skin can still burn. Water, sand and snow also bounce the rays back up at you, so you can get burnt sitting in the shade at the beach or swimming in a pool. In South Africa the Sun is strong for most of the year, so it is wise to use sunscreen, wear a hat and cover your shoulders whenever you will be outside for a long time, even in winter and even when it is cloudy. People with all skin colours need to look after their skin and eyes in the sun, because too much sun over many years can damage the skin and the eyes.

### subtopic_2 — Animals Need the Sun's Heat
**Assistant:** How does a solar panel turn sunlight into electricity?

**Tutor:** A solar panel is made of many small, flat squares, usually dark blue or black. Each square is made from a special material that comes from very pure sand. When sunlight shines on this material, the energy of the light pushes on tiny particles inside it and gets them moving. Wires collect the moving particles, and that flow is electricity. There are no moving parts, no noise and no smoke. The panel just sits in the sun and makes electricity. The more sunlight falls on it, the more electricity it makes, which is why solar panels face the Sun and work best on clear, sunny days, like most days in Upington. At night they make no electricity at all, so many homes store some of the electricity in batteries to use after dark. In South Africa, more and more homes, schools, farms and businesses use solar panels, because they help when there is load shedding.

### subtopic_3 — People Use the Sun, and Stay Safe
**Assistant:** Why do some plants grow well in the shade?

**Tutor:** All plants need some light to make food, but different plants need different amounts. Plants that grow in sunny places, like maize, grapes and most grasses, need lots of strong light. Plants that grow on a shady forest floor, like many ferns, are suited to living with less light. Many shade plants have big, wide, thin leaves to catch as much of the weak light as possible. Their leaves are often a very dark green, which helps them catch more light. If you move a shade plant into strong sunshine, its leaves may burn and turn brown. If you move a sun-loving plant into deep shade, it grows tall and thin, stretching towards the light, and makes very little food. Gardeners in South Africa learn which plants like sun and which like shade, and they plant them in the right places. So shade plants still need the Sun. They just need less of it.
