# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 4 topics of Planet Earth and Beyond: Planet Earth, the Sun, the Earth and the Sun, the Moon, and rocket systems. The anchor is a space exhibition in the school hall of a primary school in Kimberley in the Northern Cape. The Grade 4 class has spent a week preparing it for their families. Station 1 has a big globe. Station 2 has a glowing poster of the Sun. Station 3 has a model of the solar system hanging from the ceiling. Station 4 has a wall of Moon diaries and Moon stories. Station 5 has a balloon rocket on a string. Each learner takes a turn to guide visitors. The big idea is that the Earth is a planet of rock, water and air that travels around the Sun, the closest star, at just the right distance for life, that the Moon is a ball of rock that travels around the Earth, and that people use rockets to explore space. The whole of Term 4 is examinable.

## Subtopic: Planet Earth and the Sun

Station 1, the globe. The Earth is a sphere, a ball, made mostly of rock. Its surface has three main parts: land, water and air. Oceans and seas cover most of the surface, about seven out of every ten parts, which is why the Earth looks blue from space. There are five oceans, and South Africa has the Atlantic on its west coast and the Indian Ocean on its east coast. The land is made up of seven continents, like Africa and Asia, and many islands, like Madagascar and Robben Island. A thin layer of air surrounds the whole Earth.

The Earth has many habitats, such as deserts like the Kalahari, grasslands, forests, mountains, wetlands and oceans. They differ in how hot, cold, wet or dry they are, and each has plants and animals suited to it.

The Earth is a planet floating in space. It spins once a day, giving us day and night. From the Earth we see the Sun in the day, and the Moon and stars at night.

Station 2, the Sun. The Sun is a star, a giant ball of hot, glowing gas that gives out heat and light. It is more than one hundred times wider than the Earth, but it looks small because it is about one hundred and fifty million kilometres away. It is the closest star to the Earth, so it looks much bigger and brighter than the other stars. The Sun gives plants the light they need to make food, and gives animals and people heat. Never look straight at the Sun, and protect your skin with a hat and sunscreen.

The questions for this section are with you now: Planet Earth and the Sun.

## Subtopic: The Earth, the Sun and the Moon

Station 3, the solar system. The Earth moves around the Sun in a path called an orbit, and one orbit takes one year, about three hundred and sixty-five days. The Sun is at the centre of the solar system, and its pull, gravity, keeps the planets in their orbits. The eight planets in order from the Sun are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. My Very Easy Method Just Speeds Up Naming. Jupiter is the biggest, and the Earth is the third planet from the Sun.

The Earth is at just the right distance from the Sun. Venus, closer to the Sun, is far too hot. Mars, further away, is too cold. On the Earth, water stays liquid and plants get enough light. The Earth's air acts like a blanket and blocks harmful rays, so the Earth can support life.

Station 4, the Moon. The Moon is a ball of rock covered with craters. It has no air and no water, so nothing lives there. It does not make its own light but reflects sunlight. It is only about a quarter as wide as the Earth, and it is much closer to the Earth than the Sun is.

The Sun always lights up half of the Moon. As the Moon travels around the Earth, we see different amounts of the sunlit half. These shapes are the phases: new Moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full Moon, waning gibbous, last quarter and waning crescent. The pattern repeats every twenty-nine and a half days. People all over the world have used the Moon as a calendar and told stories about it, like the San and Khoikhoi story of the Moon and the hare.

The questions for this section are with you now: the solar system, the right conditions for life, and the Moon.

## Subtopic: Rocket Systems

Station 5, the rocket. People have used rockets to go into space. The first satellite was launched in 1957, and Yuri Gagarin became the first person in space in 1961. In 1969, Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon, and twelve astronauts have walked there. Today astronauts live on the International Space Station, and in 2002 Mark Shuttleworth became the first South African in space.

A rocket is a system that moves forward by pushing hot exhaust gases out of its back end. Its parts are the nose cone with the payload, the body with fuel tanks, the engine with its nozzle, and the fins. The input is fuel. The process is burning the fuel in the engine and pushing out hot gas. The output is movement. A rocket carries its own oxygen, so it can work in space.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying land covers most of the Earth; oceans and seas do. Two: saying the Sun is not a star because it looks so different; it is a star, just very close. Three: saying the Earth's shadow causes the phases of the Moon; we see different amounts of its sunlit half as it travels around the Earth. Four: saying a rocket pushes against the ground or the air; it pushes its own exhaust gas backwards.

The questions for this section are with you now: people in space and how a rocket works.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the space exhibition — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Earth and Sun

The Earth is a ball of rock, mostly covered by water, with a thin layer of air.

The Sun is a star. It is huge, hot and far away. It gives us heat and light.

Keep the globe in mind and try the questions for this part: the Earth and the Sun.

## Subtopic: Planets and the Moon

The Earth orbits the Sun once a year. There are eight planets.

The Moon is a ball of rock. Its phases repeat every twenty-nine and a half days.

Hold the hanging model in mind and try this part's questions: planets and the Moon.

## Subtopic: Blast Off

Rockets push gas out the back and go forward.

Pocket summary of the term. The Earth is a sphere of rock with land, water and air, and many habitats. It is a planet that orbits the Sun once a year. The Sun is a star of hot gas, the closest star to us. The Earth is one of eight planets and gets just the right heat and light for life. The Moon is a ball of rock with phases that repeat every twenty-nine and a half days. Rockets carry people and satellites into space by pushing exhaust gas out of their back ends.

Hold the whole exhibition in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: rockets.
