### subtopic_1 — Planet Earth and the Sun
**Assistant:** How can I remember the order of the planets?

**Tutor:** The easiest way is to use a sentence where each word starts with the same letter as a planet, in order from the Sun. The one we learnt is My Very Easy Method Just Speeds Up Naming: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. You can also make up your own sentence, which often makes it even easier to remember. For example: My Very Excited Mother Just Served Us Nachos. Or something South African, like: My Very Energetic Meerkat Jumped Straight Up Now. Another trick is to split the planets into two groups of four. The first four, Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars, are the small rocky planets close to the Sun. Then comes a belt of rocks called asteroids. The next four, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, are the giant planets far away. If you remember that the Earth is third and Jupiter, the biggest, is fifth, the rest falls into place. Practise by drawing them in order on a strip of paper.

### subtopic_2 — The Earth, the Sun and the Moon
**Assistant:** How can I tell the difference between the phases when I look at the Moon?

**Tutor:** Start by asking two questions. First, how much of the Moon is bright? If you cannot see it at all, it is a new Moon. If it is a thin, curved sliver, it is a crescent. If it looks like half a circle, it is a quarter. If it is more than half but not a full circle, it is gibbous. If it is a full circle, it is a full Moon. Second, is the bright part growing or shrinking? In South Africa, if the bright side is on the left, the Moon is waxing, or growing towards full. If the bright side is on the right, it is waning, or shrinking towards new. Another clue is time. A waxing Moon is easy to see in the evening, and a waning Moon is often still in the sky in the morning. Keeping a Moon diary for a month, like Ayanda did, is the best way to learn the phases.

### subtopic_3 — Rocket Systems
**Assistant:** What is the most important idea to remember from this term?

**Tutor:** If you remember only one big idea, remember this: the Earth is a special planet in a huge space. It is a ball of rock, mostly covered by water, with a thin blanket of air, and it travels around the Sun, our closest star, once a year. Because it is at just the right distance from the Sun, it gets the right amount of heat and light for liquid water and living things. Nearly everything else in the term connects to this idea. The habitats on Earth exist because of the Sun's heat and light, water and air. The other planets help us see how special the Earth is, because they are too hot or too cold. The Moon shows us what a world with no air or water is like. And rockets show how people have managed to leave the Earth to explore it all. When you understand that big picture, the facts are much easier to remember.
