### subtopic_1 — The Earth Is a Planet in Space
**Assistant:** Why do stars twinkle?

**Tutor:** The stars themselves do not really flicker on and off. The twinkling happens because of the Earth's layer of air. The air above us is always moving. There are warm parts and cool parts, and wind blowing in different directions. When the thin beam of light from a faraway star passes through all this moving air, it gets bent a tiny bit this way and that way, many times every second. That makes the star seem to jump about and change brightness, so it twinkles. Stars near the edge of the sky, close to the ground, twinkle the most, because their light passes through much more air to reach us. Planets usually twinkle less, because they are much closer and look like tiny discs instead of points, so the wobbles even out. Astronauts in space, above the air, see the stars shining steadily without any twinkle at all. This is one reason telescopes are built on high, dry places like the hill at Sutherland.

### subtopic_2 — What We See in the Day and Night Sky
**Assistant:** If the Earth is spinning, why don't we feel it moving?

**Tutor:** The Earth is spinning quite fast. At the middle of the Earth, the ground moves at more than a thousand kilometres an hour. But we do not feel it because the movement is smooth and steady, and everything moves with us: the ground, the buildings, the air and the oceans. Think of sitting in a car on a smooth, straight highway. If you close your eyes, it can feel as if you are not moving at all. You only feel movement when the car speeds up, slows down or turns suddenly. The Earth never speeds up or slows down suddenly. It just keeps turning at the same speed, once every day. So we cannot feel the spin, but we can see what it does. We see the Sun rise and set, and if you watch the stars for a few hours, you will see them slowly move across the sky too. That movement is really us, turning with the Earth.

### subtopic_3 — Stars Are Suns Far Away
**Assistant:** What is an astronomer and what do they do at Sutherland?

**Tutor:** An astronomer is a scientist who studies stars, planets, moons and everything else in space. Astronomers ask questions like how stars are born, how far away they are and what they are made of. Because space is so far away, astronomers cannot visit most of the things they study. Instead, they collect light coming from space using telescopes. At Sutherland there are many telescopes, and the biggest, called SALT, has a giant mirror made of ninety-one smaller mirrors fitted together. The bigger the mirror, the more light it can collect, so it can see things that are extremely faint and far away. Scientists from South Africa and other countries use the telescopes at Sutherland. Many work at night, when the sky is dark, and sleep during the day. Visitors can also go to Sutherland to look through smaller telescopes and learn about the night sky. Maybe one day you will be an astronomer too.
