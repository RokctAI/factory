### subtopic_1 — The Earth Is a Sphere of Rock
**Assistant:** If the Earth is a ball, why don't people at the bottom fall off?

**Tutor:** This is one of the best questions anyone can ask about the Earth. The answer is a pull called gravity. The Earth pulls everything towards its centre. When you drop a ball, it falls down because the Earth pulls it. Down always means towards the middle of the Earth, wherever you are standing. So people in South Africa, which is in the bottom half of the globe, are pulled towards the centre of the Earth, just like people in Europe at the top. Nobody feels upside down, because down is always towards the ground under their feet. Looking at a globe, South Africa seems to be underneath, but that is just how we usually turn the globe. There is no real top or bottom in space. The same pull of gravity also holds the oceans and the layer of air close to the Earth, so they do not float away into space.

### subtopic_2 — Oceans, Continents, Islands and Air
**Assistant:** If most of the Earth is water, why is there sometimes not enough water to drink?

**Tutor:** That is a very important question for South Africa. Most of the water on Earth is in the oceans, and ocean water is salty. People, farm animals and most plants on land cannot use salty water. If you drink sea water, it makes you more thirsty. Only a small part of the Earth's water is fresh water, and much of that is frozen as ice near the North and South Poles or hidden deep under the ground. The fresh water we can easily use is in rivers, lakes, dams and rain. South Africa is a dry country, and some years very little rain falls. That is why towns sometimes run short of water, even though the sea is close by. Salt can be removed from sea water in special factories, but it costs a lot of money and uses a lot of energy. So it is always wise to save water at home and at school.

### subtopic_3 — Land, Water and Air Together
**Assistant:** How do we know the Earth is round if it looks flat?

**Tutor:** People have known for more than two thousand years that the Earth is round, long before anyone flew into space. One clue is ships. When a ship sails away from Cape Agulhas, it does not just get smaller and smaller. The bottom of the ship disappears first, and the top of the mast disappears last, as if the ship is going over a hill. That hill is the curve of the Earth. Another clue comes during an eclipse of the Moon, when the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. The shadow is always curved, and only a round object makes a round shadow from every side. People have also sailed and flown all the way around the world and come back to where they started. Today the best proof is photographs taken from space, which show the Earth as a round blue ball with white clouds.
