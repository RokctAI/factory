### subtopic_1 — What Makes Habitats Different
**Assistant:** Do people have a habitat too?

**Tutor:** Yes, people are living things, so we need a habitat too. Like other animals, we need food, water, shelter and a safe place to raise children. What makes people different is that we can live in almost every habitat on Earth. We do this by changing things around us. We build houses to keep out the heat and the cold, wear warm clothes in winter, dig wells for water and grow our own food on farms. People live in the dry Kalahari and on the cold slopes of the mountains, in busy cities like Johannesburg and in small villages near the sea. Scientists even live in Antarctica for a few months at a time, in heated buildings with food brought in by ship. But all these homes still depend on healthy habitats around us, for clean water, clean air and the plants and animals that feed us. That is why looking after other habitats also looks after people.

### subtopic_2 — Habitats on Land
**Assistant:** Are there any living things in the deepest parts of the ocean?

**Tutor:** Yes, even though it is completely dark down there. The deepest parts of the ocean are more than ten kilometres below the surface. No sunlight reaches that far, the water is icy cold, and the weight of all the water above pushes down very hard. For a long time, people thought nothing could live there. Then scientists sent down special submarines and cameras, and they found strange fish, worms, shrimps and other animals. Some deep-sea fish make their own light, like a torch, to find food or attract other animals. Many deep-sea animals eat bits of dead plants and animals that sink slowly down from the sunny water far above. The deep sea is one of the least explored habitats on Earth, and scientists find new kinds of animals there almost every time they look.

### subtopic_3 — Habitats in Water and Around the World
**Assistant:** Why does South Africa have so many different habitats?

**Tutor:** South Africa has many habitats because the land and weather change so much from place to place. The country has two oceans, a cold one on the west coast and a warm one on the east coast, and they change the weather on the land near them. The east of the country gets much more rain than the west, so the east has grasslands, forests and wetlands, while the west has dry semi-desert. South Africa also has high mountains, like the Drakensberg, where it is cold and snowy in winter, and low, hot valleys. The Western Cape gets its rain in winter, while most of the rest of the country gets rain in summer, and this helps to explain why fynbos grows there. All these differences in height, rain and temperature create many different homes. That is why South Africa has more kinds of plants and animals than most countries of its size.
