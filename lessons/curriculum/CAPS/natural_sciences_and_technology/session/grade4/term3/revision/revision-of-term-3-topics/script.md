# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 3 topics of Energy and Change: energy and energy transfer, including energy from food, plants and the Sun, and food chains; energy around us, including stored energy and input and output energy; movement and energy in a system, with musical instruments; and energy and sound, including vibrations, volume, pitch and noise pollution. The anchor is an energy trail around a primary school in Pietermaritzburg. Stop 1 is the tuck shop. Stop 2 is the vegetable garden. Stop 3 is the school kitchen, with its gas stove, kettle and fridge. Stop 4 is the music room, with drums, marimbas and recorders. Stop 5 is the school gate next to the busy road. The big idea is that energy comes from the Sun, passes through plants and animals, is stored in food and fuels, is changed by machines and instruments, and travels as sound. The whole of Term 3 is examinable.

## Subtopic: Energy from Food and the Sun

Stop 1, the tuck shop. We use energy for everything we do, even sleeping, when we still breathe, keep our hearts beating and stay warm. We get our energy from food, which is fuel for our bodies. Starchy foods like pap, bread, rice and potatoes give lots of energy, and fruit and honey give quick energy. Water is vital, but it does not give energy. Animals also get their energy from food: cattle eat grass and cheetahs eat meat.

Stop 2, the vegetable garden. Plants do not eat. They make their own food in their green leaves, using sunlight, water and a gas from the air, and they give off oxygen. Plants store energy in their seeds, like maize and beans, in roots, like carrots, in underground stems, like potatoes, and in fruits. When we eat plants, or animals that ate plants, we get that stored energy. So the energy in all our food comes from the Sun.

Food chains show how energy passes along. Sun, arrow, grass, arrow, zebra, arrow, lion. The arrow means is eaten by, and it points from the food to the eater. Every food chain starts with a plant, which is a producer. Animals are consumers. Plant eaters are herbivores, meat eaters are carnivores, and animals that eat both, like people, are omnivores. If one link is lost, such as grass in a drought, the animals further along the chain go hungry.

The questions for this section are with you now: energy from food, plants and the Sun, and food chains.

## Subtopic: Energy Around Us and Machines

Stop 3, the school kitchen. Energy around us includes movement, heat, light and sound. The pot bubbling on the stove has heat. The light from the window is light energy. The fan turning has movement energy. The radio playing has sound energy.

Energy can also be stored. Food, wood, coal, oil products like petrol, diesel and paraffin, and natural gas are stores of energy. The things we burn for energy are called fuels. The gas in the kitchen's gas bottle releases heat and light when it burns. Once a fuel is burnt, its stored energy is used up. Fuels must always be used safely.

Energy is transferred from a source to where it is needed: from the Sun to Earth, from a power station along wires to the school, from a battery to a torch bulb, and from food to our muscles.

Machines and appliances take in input energy and give out useful output energy. The kettle: electricity in, heat out. The radio: electricity in, sound out. A torch: battery in, light out. A windpump: wind movement in, pumping movement out. Some output energy is always wasted, usually as heat and sound, which is why an old light bulb gets hot. Switching off appliances saves energy. A bicycle is a machine too: the input energy comes from the rider's legs, using energy from food, and the output is movement of the wheels.

The questions for this section are with you now: kinds of energy, stored energy, energy transfer, and input and output energy.

## Subtopic: Instruments and Sound

Stop 4, the music room. A musical instrument is a system, with parts that work together to make sound. Its input energy is movement from the player, by blowing, like a recorder or kudu horn, beating, like a drum or marimba, or plucking, like a guitar or mbira. Its main output energy is sound. Every sound is made by something vibrating, moving quickly backwards and forwards: a drum skin, a string, a wooden key or the air inside a tube. When the vibrating stops, the sound stops.

Vibrations travel outwards in all directions, through air, water, plastic, metal and wood, until they reach our eardrums. Sound cannot travel through empty space. Put your ear on a desk and a tap at the far end sounds louder, because sound travels well through wood. A string telephone carries a voice along a tight string.

Volume is how loud or soft a sound is: bigger vibrations, from more energy, make louder sounds. Pitch is how high or low a sound is: faster vibrations make higher sounds. Thin, short, tight and small things sound high. Thick, long, loose and big things sound low.

Stop 5, the gate by the road. Loud or unwanted sound is noise pollution. It makes it hard to concentrate and sleep, and it disturbs animals. Very loud sounds can damage the tiny hair cells in our ears, and the damage is permanent. We protect our hearing by turning the volume down, taking breaks, moving away and wearing earplugs or earmuffs.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: drawing food chain arrows from the eater to the food; they point from the food to the eater. Two: saying the input energy of a kettle is heat; it is electricity. Three: mixing up volume and pitch; volume is loud or soft, pitch is high or low. Four: thinking ringing ears do not matter; they are a warning of damage.

The questions for this section are with you now: instruments as systems, vibrations, volume, pitch and noise pollution.

## Subtopic: Sun, Plants, Food

We get energy from food. Plants make food using sunlight.

So all our food energy comes from the Sun.

Food chain: Sun, grass, zebra, lion. Arrows point to the eater.

## Subtopic: Stored, Moved, Changed

Movement, heat, light and sound are around us.

Food, wood, coal, petrol and gas store energy.

Machines change input energy into output energy. Some is wasted.

Hold the kettle in mind and try this part's questions: energy and machines.

## Subtopic: Vibrations and Volume

Instruments: movement in, sound out. Something vibrates.

Loud or soft is volume. High or low is pitch. Protect your ears.

Pocket summary of the term. Energy comes from the Sun, is captured by plants and passed along food chains. It is stored in food and fuels, transferred to where it is needed, and changed by machines from input to output energy. Instruments turn movement into sound through vibrations. Sounds have volume and pitch, and loud noise can damage hearing.
