### subtopic_1 — Energy from Food and the Sun
**Assistant:** How can I remember which way the arrows go in a food chain?

**Tutor:** Think of the arrow as showing where the energy travels, not who is doing the eating. The energy starts in the Sun, moves into the grass, moves from the grass into the zebra when the zebra eats it, and moves from the zebra into the lion when the lion eats the zebra. So the arrows point Sun to grass, grass to zebra, zebra to lion. Another trick is to read each arrow as the words is eaten by. Grass is eaten by zebra makes sense. Zebra is eaten by lion makes sense. If you drew the arrow the other way, it would read lion is eaten by zebra, which is clearly wrong. You can also check that your chain always starts with a plant, or with the Sun and then a plant, because plants are the only living things that make their own food. And the animal at the end of the chain is the one that nothing else in the chain eats.

### subtopic_2 — Energy Around Us and Machines
**Assistant:** What is the difference between energy that is stored and energy that is transferred?

**Tutor:** Stored energy is energy that is waiting to be used. It is locked inside a material and is not doing anything yet. A loaf of bread sitting on the table, a pile of firewood, a lump of coal, petrol in a car's tank, gas in a bottle and a battery in a drawer all have stored energy. Nothing happens until the store is used: the bread is eaten, the wood is burnt, the petrol is burnt in an engine, or the battery is put in a torch and switched on. Transferred energy is energy that is moving from one place to another, or from one thing to another. Sunlight travelling to Earth, electricity flowing along wires to your home, heat moving from a hot pot into your hand, and sound travelling through the air to your ear are all energy being transferred. Often both happen one after the other. Energy stored in coal is released at the power station and then transferred along wires to your kettle.

### subtopic_3 — Instruments and Sound
**Assistant:** How are a drum, a kettle and a lion alike?

**Tutor:** At first they seem to have nothing in common, but all three take in energy and change it into another kind. A lion takes in energy stored in the meat it eats, and its body changes that energy into movement when it runs and hunts, into heat to keep warm, and into sound when it roars. A kettle takes in electrical energy and changes it into heat to boil water, with a little wasted as sound when it bubbles and clicks. A drum takes in movement energy from the drummer's hands and changes it into sound, with a little wasted as heat. Each one can be shown as input energy, arrow, system, arrow, output energy. That is the big idea of Term 3: energy is never simply made from nothing. It comes from somewhere, often first from the Sun, and is passed along and changed from one form to another as it does useful work, with some always spreading out as heat.
