# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the energy around us, which includes movement, heat, light and sound, and the energy that is stored in food, wood, coal, oil products and natural gas. The anchor is a weekend camping trip in the Magaliesberg mountains near Pretoria. The family's car, filled with petrol, drives up a dusty road to a campsite next to a rushing river. The wind flaps the tent. In the evening, Dad lights a campfire with wood and cooks on a small gas stove. Lindiwe shines a torch, her brother plays the guitar, and everyone toasts marshmallows from the cooler box. The big idea is that energy is all around us in forms we can notice, like movement, heat, light and sound, and that energy can also be stored, waiting to be used, in food and fuels. Identifying kinds of energy and stores of energy is examinable.

## Subtopic: Movement, Heat, Light and Sound

Energy is all around us. We cannot hold it in our hands, but we can notice what it does. At the campsite there are four kinds of energy that the class learns to spot.

Movement energy. Anything that moves has movement energy. The river rushing over the rocks has movement energy. The wind flapping the tent has movement energy. The car driving up the road, a ball being kicked and Lindiwe running to fetch water all have movement energy. The faster something moves and the heavier it is, the more movement energy it has.

Heat energy. Heat makes things warm or hot. The campfire gives out heat energy that warms the family's hands and cooks the food. The Sun gives out heat that warms the rocks during the day. A hot cup of tea has heat energy. We feel heat with our skin.

Light energy. Light lets us see. The Sun gives out light during the day. At night, the campfire glows with light, and Lindiwe's torch shines a beam of light. Stars and the Moon light up the sky too, although the Moon only reflects light from the Sun. We see light with our eyes.

Sound energy. Sound is energy we hear. The guitar, the river splashing, a frog croaking and the crackle of the fire all make sound energy. We hear sound with our ears.

Some things give out more than one kind of energy at once. The campfire gives out heat, light and even sound when it crackles. A car engine gives out movement, heat and sound. A cell phone gives out light from its screen and sound from its speaker, and it gets warm when it is used for a long time.

The questions for this section are with you now: movement, heat, light and sound energy around us.

## Subtopic: Stored Energy in Food and Fuels

Some energy is not doing anything yet. It is stored, waiting to be used. Stored energy is hidden inside a material, and it is released when the material is eaten, burnt or used.

Food stores energy. The marshmallows, bread, boerewors and fruit in the cooler box are full of stored energy. When the family eats them, their bodies release that energy and use it to walk, swim, climb and keep warm.

Wood stores energy. The logs for the campfire grew in the sunshine, and the tree stored energy in its wood. When the wood burns, it releases that stored energy as heat and light.

Coal stores energy. Coal is a black rock that formed from plants that lived and died millions of years ago. Most of South Africa's electricity comes from power stations that burn coal, many of them in Mpumalanga.

Oil products store energy. Oil is pumped from deep underground and made into petrol, diesel and paraffin. The petrol in the car's tank stores the energy that made the car move up the mountain road. Paraffin is burnt in lamps and stoves in many homes.

Natural gas stores energy. Natural gas is found deep underground, often near oil. It is burnt for cooking and heating, and in some power stations. The small gas stove at the campsite burns a gas that is stored under pressure in a metal bottle.

Food, wood, coal, oil products and natural gas are called stores of energy. The materials we burn to release energy, such as wood, coal, petrol, paraffin and gas, are called fuels.

The questions for this section are with you now: the stores of energy in food, wood, coal, oil products and natural gas.

## Subtopic: Releasing Stored Energy Safely

Stored energy is released when we use the store. Food releases its energy inside our bodies after we eat it. Fuels release their energy when they burn. When Dad lights the wood, the stored energy comes out as heat and light. When the gas stove is lit, the stored energy in the gas comes out as heat for cooking. When petrol burns inside the car's engine, its stored energy is turned into movement, with heat and sound as well.

Once a fuel is burnt, its stored energy is used up. The wood turns into ash and smoke, and it cannot burn again. That is why we must fetch more firewood, fill the car with petrol again and buy new gas bottles.

Burning fuels can be dangerous. Fires can spread to grass and trees, especially in the dry winter months in the Magaliesberg. Gas can leak and explode. Paraffin stoves and candles can tip over and start fires in homes. Burning fuels inside a closed room can make poisonous gases. So we must always use fuels carefully, with an adult, and never leave a fire burning alone.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying the Moon makes its own light; it reflects light from the Sun. Two: saying wood has no energy until it is lit; the energy is stored in it and lighting only releases it. Three: thinking a burnt log can be burnt again; its stored energy has been used up. Four: forgetting that food is a store of energy; food is the fuel for our bodies.

The questions for this section are with you now: how stored energy is released and how to use fuels safely.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the camping trip — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Energy You Can Notice

Movement: the river, the wind, the car.

Heat: the warm campfire.

Light: the torch and the Sun. Sound: the guitar and the river.

Keep the campsite in mind and try the questions for this part: kinds of energy.

## Subtopic: Energy Waiting Inside

Some energy is stored, waiting to be used.

Food, wood, coal, oil products like petrol and paraffin, and natural gas all store energy.

Things we burn for energy are called fuels.

Hold the cooler box and the firewood in mind and try this part's questions: stored energy.

## Subtopic: Burning Fuels

Burn a fuel and its stored energy comes out as heat and light.

Once it is burnt, the energy is used up. Always be careful with fire.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Energy around us includes movement, heat, light and sound. Energy is stored in food, wood, coal, oil products like petrol and paraffin, and natural gas. Stored energy is released when food is eaten or when fuels are burnt, and fuels must always be used safely.

Hold the campfire in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: releasing stored energy.
