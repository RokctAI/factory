# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how sounds can be loud or soft, which is their volume, and high or low, which is their pitch. The anchor is a Grade 4 class at a primary school in Langa, Cape Town, getting ready for an end-of-term concert. Sipho practises on a big drum and a small drum. Aviwe tunes her guitar, turning the pegs to tighten and loosen the strings. The rest of the class lines up eight glass bottles, fills them with different amounts of water and blows across the tops. The music teacher asks them to explain why each sound is different. The big idea is that bigger vibrations make louder sounds, and faster vibrations make higher sounds. Describing loud and soft sounds and high and low sounds, and explaining what changes them, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Loud and Soft Sounds

Volume means how loud or soft a sound is. A whisper has a low volume. A shout has a high volume. A lion's roar is loud, and a cat's purr is soft.

Sipho taps the big drum gently with his fingertips. The skin vibrates only a little, and the sound is soft. Then he hits it hard with his whole hand. The skin moves much further up and down, and the sound is loud. If there is rice on the drum, it jumps only a little when the drum is tapped softly, but it flies into the air when the drum is hit hard.

So, the bigger the vibrations, the louder the sound. To make bigger vibrations, you need more input energy. Hitting harder, blowing harder or plucking harder gives the instrument more energy, which comes out as a louder sound.

Distance matters too. A sound is loudest close to where it is made. As the vibrations spread out, they get weaker, so the further away you are, the softer the sound.

We can make sounds louder in other ways. A hollow body on an instrument, like the box of a guitar or the calabash of a musical bow, makes the sound louder. Cupping your hands around your mouth when you call your friend across the playground makes your voice louder in one direction. A microphone and speakers can make a singer's voice loud enough for a whole stadium.

The loudness of a sound can be measured with a sound meter. Scientists measure volume in units called decibels. A whisper is very low on the scale, normal talking is in the middle, and a jet engine or a vuvuzela blown right next to your ear is very high. In the next lesson, we will see why very loud sounds can be dangerous.

The questions for this section are with you now: loud and soft sounds and what changes the volume.

## Subtopic: High and Low Sounds

Pitch means how high or low a sound is. A bird's tweet and a whistle have a high pitch. A big drum, a lion's roar and a truck's engine have a low pitch. Pitch is not the same as volume: a whisper can be high, and a shout can be low.

The pitch depends on how fast something vibrates. Fast vibrations make high sounds. Slow vibrations make low sounds.

Aviwe's guitar shows this. The thin strings make high notes, and the thick strings make low notes, because thin strings vibrate faster. When she turns a peg to tighten a string, its note goes higher. When she loosens it, its note goes lower. When she presses a string down on the neck of the guitar with a finger, she makes the vibrating part shorter, and the note goes higher.

The ruler on the desk shows the same rule. With a long piece sticking out over the edge, the twang is low. With a short piece sticking out, it vibrates faster, and the twang is higher.

Size matters for drums and other instruments too. Sipho's big drum makes a low, booming sound. The small drum makes a higher sound. In a marimba, the long keys make low notes and the short keys make high notes.

Animals show the same rule. Small animals, like mice, birds and crickets, usually make high sounds. Big animals, like lions, cattle and elephants, usually make low sounds. Elephants even make rumbles so low that people cannot hear them, while bats make squeaks so high that people cannot hear those either.

So: short, thin, tight and small things vibrate faster and make higher sounds. Long, thick, loose and big things vibrate more slowly and make lower sounds.

The questions for this section are with you now: high and low sounds and what changes the pitch.

## Subtopic: Making Music with Bottles

The class tests pitch with their eight bottles. They pour a little water into the first bottle and more into each one after that, so that the last bottle is nearly full. Then they blow gently across the top of each bottle.

When you blow across a bottle, the air inside it vibrates. The bottle with only a little water has a tall column of air inside, and it makes a low note. The nearly full bottle has only a short column of air at the top, which vibrates faster and makes a high note. So, when blowing, less air means a higher pitch. By blowing across the bottles in order, the class plays a scale, from low to high. Then they pick out bottles in a different order and play the first few notes of a song they know, which the whole class recognises.

They also find that blowing harder makes the same note louder, but it does not change the pitch. Volume and pitch are two different things.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: mixing up volume and pitch; volume is loud or soft, pitch is high or low. Two: thinking a big drum makes a high sound; big drums make low sounds. Three: thinking a tighter guitar string makes a lower note; tightening a string makes its note higher. Four: thinking hitting a drum harder makes the note higher; hitting harder makes it louder.

The questions for this section are with you now: using bottles to make high and low sounds, and telling volume from pitch.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Langa concert practice — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Loud and Soft

Volume means loud or soft.

Hit the drum hard: big vibrations, loud sound. Tap it gently: small vibrations, soft sound.

Sounds are softer when you are far away.

Keep Sipho's drum in mind and try the questions for this part: volume.

## Subtopic: High and Low

Pitch means high or low.

Fast vibrations make high sounds. Slow vibrations make low sounds.

Thin, short, tight strings sound high. Thick, long, loose strings sound low. Big drums sound low.

Hold Aviwe's guitar in mind and try this part's questions: pitch.

## Subtopic: Bottle Tunes

Blow across a bottle. Less air inside means a higher note.

Blowing harder makes it louder, not higher.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Volume is how loud or soft a sound is. Bigger vibrations, from more input energy, make louder sounds. Pitch is how high or low a sound is. Faster vibrations make higher sounds. Short, thin, tight and small things make high sounds; long, thick, loose and big things make low sounds.
