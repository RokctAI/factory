# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how energy is transferred from the Sun to plants, from plants to animals, and from animals to other animals in an energy chain, also called a food chain. The anchor is a game drive in the Kruger National Park for a Grade 4 class from Phalaborwa, a town right on the edge of the park. From the open truck they watch zebra grazing on the grass. Further on, a lioness lies in the shade beside the remains of a zebra. When they stop for a break, they spot a grasshopper in the grass, a lizard catching insects on a rock, and an eagle circling above. The ranger tells them that every animal they see is part of a chain that starts with the Sun. The big idea is that energy from the Sun is captured by plants and then passed along from one living thing to the next when one eats another. Drawing and explaining simple food chains is examinable.

## Subtopic: The Sun and the Plants

Every food chain begins with energy from the Sun. As we learnt in the last lesson, plants capture the energy in sunlight with their green leaves and use it to make their own food. They store that energy in their leaves, stems, roots, fruits and seeds.

In the Kruger National Park, the grass on the open plains, the leaves on the mopane trees and the fruits of the marula trees are all full of stored energy from the Sun. Plants are always the first living things in a food chain, because they are the only living things that can make their own food. Scientists call them producers, because they produce food.

Animals cannot make their own food. They must eat to get energy. Animals are called consumers, which means eaters. Some consumers eat plants. Others eat animals that have eaten plants.

When we write a food chain, we often start with the Sun, then the plant, then the animals. We join them with arrows. Sun, arrow, grass. The arrow shows that energy moves from the Sun to the grass.

Without the Sun, plants could not make food. Without plants, there would be no food for any animal. That is why the Sun and the plants are at the start of every food chain on land. In the sea, tiny floating plants near the surface catch sunlight and are the start of the food chains there.

The questions for this section are with you now: why every food chain starts with the Sun and a plant.

## Subtopic: Building a Food Chain

The class builds a food chain from what they saw. Sun, arrow, grass, arrow, zebra, arrow, lion.

What does each arrow mean? It means is eaten by, or gives energy to. The grass is eaten by the zebra, so energy passes from the grass to the zebra. The zebra is eaten by the lion, so energy passes from the zebra to the lion. The arrows always point from the food to the eater, in the direction the energy travels. They do not point from the eater to its food.

Here is a second chain from the game drive. Sun, arrow, grass, arrow, grasshopper, arrow, lizard, arrow, eagle. This chain has four living links. The grasshopper eats the grass, the lizard eats the grasshopper, and the eagle eats the lizard.

Animals can be sorted by what they eat. Animals that eat only plants, like zebra, grasshoppers and giraffes, are plant eaters, also called herbivores. Animals that eat only other animals, like lions and eagles, are meat eaters, also called carnivores. Animals that eat both plants and animals, like baboons, bushpigs and people, are called omnivores.

People are part of food chains too. Sun, arrow, maize, arrow, person. Or Sun, arrow, grass, arrow, cow, arrow, person.

The class makes a few more chains from the drive. Sun, arrow, marula fruit, arrow, elephant. Sun, arrow, mopane leaves, arrow, mopane worm, arrow, bird. Mopane worms are the caterpillars of a moth. Many people in Limpopo also collect and eat them, so there is another chain: Sun, arrow, mopane leaves, arrow, mopane worm, arrow, person. The same animal can be food for different eaters.

The questions for this section are with you now: building food chains and what the arrows mean.

## Subtopic: Every Link Matters

Each living thing in a food chain depends on the one before it. If one link disappears, the animals further along the chain go hungry.

The ranger explains what happens in a drought. When the rains fail, the grass dries up and dies. With little grass, the zebra and other grazers struggle to find food. Many become weak, some move away, and some die. Then the lions have fewer zebra to hunt, so they go hungry too. The lion never eats grass, but it still depends on grass, because grass feeds the animals the lion eats.

The same is true if one animal is removed. If people killed all the lizards, there might be too many grasshoppers, which would eat lots of grass, and the eagles would have less to eat. That is why national parks protect whole habitats, not just the big animals.

Energy is also used up along the way. The zebra uses most of the energy from the grass to walk, keep warm and stay alive. Only a small part is stored in its body and passed on to the lion. That is why a lion must eat a lot, and why there are always many more plants than plant eaters, and more plant eaters than meat eaters.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: drawing the arrows backwards, from the lion to the zebra; arrows point from the food to the eater. Two: starting a food chain with an animal; every chain starts with a plant, or with the Sun and then a plant. Three: thinking the lion does not need grass; it eats animals that need grass. Four: calling a person a carnivore; people eat plants and animals, so we are omnivores.

The questions for this section are with you now: how the links in a food chain depend on each other.

## Subtopic: It Starts with Sunshine

The Sun gives energy to plants.

Plants make food. Plants are always first in a food chain.

Animals cannot make food. They must eat.

Sun, grass, zebra, lion.

The arrow means is eaten by. It points from the food to the eater.

Plant eaters, meat eaters, and both.

Hold the zebra in mind and try this part's questions: building food chains.

## Subtopic: Pull Out a Link

No grass means hungry zebra. Hungry zebra means hungry lions.

Every link matters.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Energy from the Sun is captured by plants, which make food. Animals get energy by eating plants or other animals. A food chain shows this, with arrows pointing from the food to the eater. Every chain starts with a plant. If one link is lost, the living things further along the chain suffer.
