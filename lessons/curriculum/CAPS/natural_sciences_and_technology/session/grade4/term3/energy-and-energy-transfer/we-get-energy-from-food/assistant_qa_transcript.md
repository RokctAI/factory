### subtopic_1 — We Use Energy All the Time
**Assistant:** Why do I feel warm after running?

**Tutor:** When your muscles work hard, they use up a lot of the energy from your food. Only part of that energy goes into moving your body. A big part of it is changed into heat. That is why you feel hot, your face goes red and you start to sweat after the 100 metres. Your body has to get rid of the extra heat so that it does not overheat. Sweat comes out onto your skin, and as the sweat evaporates, it carries heat away, which cools you down. That is also why you need to drink water on a hot sports day, to replace the water you lose in sweat. The same thing happens in cold weather, but then the heat is useful. When you shiver, your muscles are making small, fast movements that turn energy from food into heat to keep you warm. So your body uses food energy both to move and to keep its temperature just right.

### subtopic_2 — Food Gives Us Energy
**Assistant:** Why do some foods give more energy than others?

**Tutor:** Different foods store different amounts of energy, packed into them in different ways. Starchy foods like pap, bread, rice and potatoes are full of starch, which the body breaks down into sugar and uses for energy. Fruit and honey contain sugar already, so they give quick energy. Fats and oils, like those in peanut butter, nuts, avocado and cooking oil, store even more energy in a small amount of food, but the body takes longer to use them. Vegetables like lettuce, cabbage and cucumber are mostly water, so they give only a little energy, but they are full of other things the body needs, like vitamins. Foods like eggs, fish, beans and meat help the body build muscles and repair itself. That is why a healthy plate has some of each kind of food. A food label on a packet shows how much energy is in the food, measured in units called kilojoules.

### subtopic_3 — Animals Need Food for Energy Too
**Assistant:** How can a crocodile go for weeks without eating?

**Tutor:** A crocodile uses its energy very carefully. It spends most of its time lying still in the sun or floating quietly in the water, so it uses very little energy for moving. People, dogs and cattle use a lot of food energy just to keep their bodies warm all the time, whatever the weather. A crocodile does not do that. Its body temperature goes up and down with its surroundings. It warms itself by lying in the sun in the morning and cools off in the water or the shade when it gets too hot. Because it does not burn food to keep warm, it needs much less food. When it does eat, it can swallow a large meal and live on that energy for a long time. A sunbird is the opposite: it is small, always active and keeps its body warm, so it must eat again and again every day.
