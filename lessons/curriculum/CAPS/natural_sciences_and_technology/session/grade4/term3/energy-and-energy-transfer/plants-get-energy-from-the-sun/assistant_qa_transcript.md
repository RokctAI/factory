### subtopic_1 — Plants Make Food Using Sunlight
**Assistant:** If plants make their own food, why do farmers add fertiliser to the soil?

**Tutor:** This is a great question, because it sounds as if the plant is eating the fertiliser. It is not. The plant makes its food, the sugar that gives it energy, in its leaves, using sunlight, water and a gas from the air. But to grow healthy leaves, strong stems and big cobs, a plant also needs small amounts of minerals, a bit like the vitamins people need. These minerals come from the soil and are taken up with water through the roots. When the same field is farmed year after year, the crops take more and more minerals out of the soil, and the soil gets poorer. Fertiliser puts those minerals back. Animal manure and compost do the same job. So fertiliser is not food for the plant in the sense of giving it energy. It is more like a vitamin supplement. The energy always comes from sunlight. That is why even a well-fertilised plant will die if you keep it in the dark.

### subtopic_2 — Plants Store Energy in Food
**Assistant:** Why are leaves green?

**Tutor:** Leaves are green because they are full of a special green substance that catches sunlight. Sunlight looks white, but it is really a mixture of all the colours of the rainbow. The green substance in leaves takes in mostly the red and blue parts of the light and uses that energy to make food. It does not use much of the green part of the light. That green light bounces off the leaf and into our eyes, so the leaf looks green to us. In autumn, some trees stop making the green substance before they drop their leaves. When the green fades, other colours that were hidden in the leaf, like yellow and orange, can be seen. That is why some trees, like the oak trees in many South African towns, turn yellow, orange and brown in autumn. Without the green substance, the leaf can no longer make food, and the tree lets it fall.

### subtopic_3 — All Our Energy Comes from the Sun
**Assistant:** Does a plant use all the food it makes?

**Tutor:** A plant uses some of its food straight away. Just like us, a plant needs energy to stay alive and to grow. It uses food energy to build new leaves, stems, roots and flowers, and to take in water and minerals. The food it does not need straight away is stored. Some plants store food to survive hard times. A sweet potato plant stores food in its swollen roots so it can survive a cold, dry winter, and then use that stored food to grow new shoots in spring. Many plants also store food in their seeds, so that each baby plant has a packed lunch to start growing. Fruit trees store sugar in their fruits, which attracts animals to eat the fruit and spread the seeds. Farmers grow crops because of these stores. When we harvest maize cobs, potatoes or apples, we are collecting the food the plant had saved for itself, along with the energy from the Sun stored inside it.
