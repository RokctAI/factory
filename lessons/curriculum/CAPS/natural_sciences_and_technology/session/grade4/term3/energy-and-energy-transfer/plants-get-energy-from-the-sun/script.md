# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how the energy in our food comes from the Sun, because plants use sunlight to make food. The anchor is a maize farm near Ventersdorp in the North West, one of the areas that grows South Africa's maize. Mrs Mokoena, the farmer, walks a Grade 4 class between rows of tall green maize plants in the summer sun. She shows them the leaves spread out to catch the light and the fat cobs full of yellow and white kernels. She explains that the cobs will be dried, milled into maize meal and cooked as pap in kitchens all over the country. The big idea is that plants capture energy from sunlight and store it in the food they make, so the energy in all our food can be traced back to the Sun. Explaining how plants use sunlight to make food and why the energy in our food comes from the Sun is examinable.

## Subtopic: Plants Make Food Using Sunlight

Animals and people must eat to get energy. Plants do not eat. Instead, plants make their own food, and they need sunlight to do it.

The leaves of a plant are its food factories. They are green because they contain a green substance that can catch the energy in sunlight. The leaf takes in air through tiny holes, and water comes up from the roots through the stem. Using the energy from sunlight, the leaf turns the water and a gas from the air into food, a kind of sugar. This process is called photosynthesis, a long word that means making with light.

While the plant makes its food, it gives off oxygen, the gas that people and animals need to breathe. So plants feed themselves and refresh the air for us at the same time.

Mrs Mokoena points out how the maize leaves are long and wide and spread out from the stem in different directions. This helps each leaf catch as much sunlight as possible. Maize grows best in full sun with good summer rain. If the summer is cloudy for many days or very dry, the plants make less food and the cobs are small. Mrs Mokoena plants her maize in late spring, after the first good rains, so that the plants grow through the long, sunny summer days. By winter the plants have dried out, and the cobs are ready to harvest.

Without sunlight, a plant cannot make food. Remember the bean plant in the dark cupboard in Term 1: it grew pale and weak and would have died, because it had no light to make food with.

The questions for this section are with you now: how plants use sunlight, water and air to make their food.

## Subtopic: Plants Store Energy in Food

A plant uses some of the food it makes straight away, for energy to live and grow. The rest it stores for later. The energy from the sunlight is now stored inside that food.

Plants store food in different parts. Maize stores food in its seeds, the kernels on the cob. Wheat, rice, beans and peas store food in their seeds too. Potatoes store food in swollen underground stems. Carrots, beetroot and sweet potatoes store food in their roots. Cabbage and spinach store food in their leaves. Sugar cane stores sugar in its tall stems, and fruit trees store sugar in their fruits, like mangoes and oranges.

Why does a plant store food? A seed needs a food store so that the baby plant can grow until its leaves can make food. A potato or a sweet potato stores food so the plant can survive the cold, dry winter and grow again in spring.

When we eat these plant parts, we take in the energy the plant stored. A plate of pap is maize kernels, full of stored energy. Bread is made from wheat seeds. Chips come from potatoes. Sugar comes from sugar cane grown in KwaZulu-Natal. Peanut butter comes from peanuts, which are seeds packed with stored energy. Even a cup of rooibos tea comes from the dried leaves and stems of the rooibos plant, which grows in the Cederberg mountains.

The questions for this section are with you now: where plants store food and how we use that stored energy.

## Subtopic: All Our Energy Comes from the Sun

Now follow the energy backwards. Thandeka's energy for sports day came from her plate of pap. The pap came from maize. The maize got its energy from sunlight. So Thandeka's energy came from the Sun.

What about meat, milk and eggs? A beef stew comes from cattle. Cattle get their energy by eating grass. The grass got its energy from sunlight. Milk comes from cows that eat grass, and eggs come from hens that eat seeds and grain. So the energy in meat, milk and eggs also came from the Sun, passed along through plants and animals.

Every food we eat can be traced back to a plant, and every plant got its energy from the Sun. The Sun is the source of the energy in all our food.

The Sun's energy is stored in other things that came from plants too. Wood from a tree burns in a fire and gives out heat and light. That energy was stored in the tree as it grew in the sunshine. Coal formed from plants that lived and grew in sunlight millions of years ago. In the next topic, we will look at energy stored in fuels like these. Without the Sun, plants could not make food, animals would have nothing to eat, and people would have no food at all.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying plants get their food from the soil; plants make their own food in their leaves using sunlight, water and air. Two: saying meat does not get its energy from the Sun; the animal ate plants that used sunlight. Three: thinking only the leaves store food; plants store food in seeds, roots, stems, leaves and fruits. Four: thinking plants can make food in the dark; they need light.

The questions for this section are with you now: why the energy in all our food comes from the Sun.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the maize farm — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Leaves Catch Sunlight

Plants make their own food.

Leaves catch sunlight. They use water and air to make food.

No light, no food.

Keep the green maize leaves in mind and try the questions for this part: making food.

## Subtopic: Food Stored for Later

Plants store extra food.

Maize and wheat store it in seeds. Potatoes and carrots store it under the ground. Fruit trees store it in fruits.

When we eat these, we get the stored energy.

Hold the maize cob in mind and try this part's questions: stored food.

## Subtopic: Back to the Sun

Pap comes from maize, and maize used sunlight.

Meat comes from cattle, cattle ate grass, and grass used sunlight.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Plants make their own food in their leaves using sunlight, water and air. They store energy in their seeds, roots, stems, leaves and fruits. When people and animals eat plants, or eat animals that ate plants, they get that energy. So the energy in all our food comes from the Sun.

Hold the Sun in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: energy from the Sun.
