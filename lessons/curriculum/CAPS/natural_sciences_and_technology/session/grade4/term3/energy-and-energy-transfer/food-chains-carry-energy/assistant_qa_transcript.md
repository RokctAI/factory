### subtopic_1 — The Sun and the Plants
**Assistant:** What about animals that eat dead things, like vultures? Where do they fit?

**Tutor:** Vultures, hyenas, jackals and many beetles and worms eat animals or plants that are already dead. They are a very important part of nature. When a lion has finished eating a zebra, there is still meat left on the bones. Vultures and hyenas move in and eat what is left. That is still energy passing along a food chain. The energy came from the Sun, into the grass, into the zebra, and then into the vulture. Then there are even smaller living things, like fungi and bacteria, that feed on dead plants and animals and break them down until they rot into the soil. They are nature's clean-up team. They also return minerals to the soil, which helps new grass grow. So the energy and materials from dead things are not wasted. Without these clean-up creatures, the veld would be covered in dead animals and fallen leaves, and the soil would slowly lose the goodness that plants need.

### subtopic_2 — Building a Food Chain
**Assistant:** Why are there more zebra than lions in the park?

**Tutor:** It is all about energy. When a zebra eats grass, it uses most of the energy from that grass just to stay alive. It walks to find water, keeps its body warm, breathes, and its heart beats day and night. Only a small part of the energy is stored in its body as it grows. When a lion eats the zebra, it only gets that small stored part. So a lion needs to eat a lot of zebra to get enough energy, and a lot of zebra need a huge amount of grass. If you counted all the living things in the park, you would find a giant amount of grass, a large number of plant eaters like zebra, impala and wildebeest, and far fewer meat eaters like lions and leopards. There simply is not enough energy left at the end of the chain to feed many large meat eaters. That is why lions are much rarer than zebra, and why big predators need large areas to hunt in.

### subtopic_3 — Every Link Matters
**Assistant:** Can an animal be part of more than one food chain?

**Tutor:** Yes, almost every animal is. A food chain shows just one path that energy can take, but in real life animals eat many different things, and many different animals may eat them. A lion eats zebra, but it also eats wildebeest, buffalo and impala, so it is at the end of several chains. A zebra is eaten by lions, but also by hyenas and crocodiles when it crosses a river. A baboon eats fruit, seeds, insects and sometimes small animals. When scientists join many food chains together, the result looks like a spider's web, with lines crossing everywhere. They call this a food web. It shows how all the living things in a habitat are connected. In Grade 4 we focus on single food chains, because they show clearly how energy moves from the Sun to plants to animals. In later grades, you will learn more about food webs and how changing one animal can affect many others.
