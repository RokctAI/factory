# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the properties of raw and manufactured materials: hard or soft, stiff or flexible, strong or weak, light or heavy, and waterproof or absorbent. A property is a quality that tells us how a material behaves. The anchor is a materials testing lab in a Grade 4 classroom in George, a town on the Garden Route where it rains a lot. On the tables are samples of wood, glass, a plastic ruler, paper, cotton cloth, a rubber band, a metal spoon, a piece of foam, a stone and a wool sock. The class tests each material and records the results in a table. Then they must choose materials for a raincoat, a towel, a school bag and a window. The big idea is that every material has properties, and we choose materials for a job because their properties suit that job. Describing and comparing the properties of materials and choosing materials for a purpose are examinable.

## Subtopic: Hard or Soft, Stiff or Flexible

Hard materials do not dent or scratch easily. Soft materials can be dented, squashed or scratched easily. The class tests hardness by pressing a fingernail into each material and, with the teacher, trying to scratch it with an iron nail. The stone, the metal spoon and the glass are hard. The foam, the wool sock and the cotton cloth are soft. Wood is fairly hard, but a nail can scratch it.

We choose hard materials when we need something that will not wear away or dent, like a floor tile, a hammer head or a kitchen counter. We choose soft materials for comfort, like a pillow, a mattress or a sofa cushion.

Stiff materials do not bend easily. Flexible materials bend easily without breaking. The class tests this by trying to bend a strip of each material. The plastic ruler bends a little and springs back, so it is fairly flexible. The rubber band and the cloth are very flexible. The wood, the glass and the stone are stiff. If you bend glass too far it does not bend, it breaks.

We choose stiff materials when something must keep its shape, like a table leg, a ladder or a door. We choose flexible materials when something must bend, like a hosepipe, a shoelace, a seat belt or the strap of a school bag.

Hard and stiff are not the same property. A metal paper clip is hard, but its thin wire is flexible. A thick foam block is soft, but it can be quite stiff.

The questions for this section are with you now: hard and soft materials, stiff and flexible materials, and what each is used for.

## Subtopic: Strong or Weak, Light or Heavy

Strong materials do not break, tear or snap easily when they carry a load or are pulled. Weak materials break or tear easily. The class tests strength by hanging a strip of each material from a hook and adding weights, such as small bags of sand, until it tears or snaps. The paper strip tears quickly, so paper is weak. The cotton cloth holds more. A strip of plastic packet stretches and then tears. A rope or a strip of leather holds a lot, so they are strong. Metal and wood are strong too.

We need strong materials for things that carry heavy loads, like bridges, chairs, ropes, shopping bags and the straps of a school bag.

Light materials have little mass for their size. Heavy materials have a lot of mass for their size. To compare fairly, the class weighs blocks of the same size on a kitchen scale. The block of foam is very light. The wood block is a bit heavier. The stone is heavy, and the metal block is the heaviest. Same size, very different mass.

We choose light materials when things must be carried or must move easily, like a cooler box, a bicycle frame or an aeroplane. We choose heavy materials when things must stay put, like the base of a lamp, an anchor or a doorstop.

Light is not the same as weak. Some materials, like aluminium and bamboo, are light but strong, which makes them very useful.

The questions for this section are with you now: strong and weak materials, light and heavy materials, and fair ways to test them.

## Subtopic: Waterproof or Absorbent

Waterproof materials do not let water through and do not soak it up. Absorbent materials soak up water. The class drips water onto each material with a dropper and watches. On plastic, glass, metal and rubber, the water stays as round drops sitting on top. These materials are waterproof. On paper towel, cotton cloth and the wool sock, the water disappears into the material and makes a wet patch. These materials are absorbent.

In George it rains a lot, so waterproof materials matter. Raincoats, umbrellas, gumboots and tent roofs are made from waterproof materials like plastic, rubber and specially treated fabric. Absorbent materials are useful for drying and cleaning, like a towel, a nappy, a dishcloth, a mop or a sponge.

Now the class chooses materials for jobs. A raincoat needs to be waterproof and flexible: plastic or treated fabric. A towel needs to be absorbent and soft: cotton. A school bag needs to be strong, light, flexible and fairly waterproof: tough nylon fabric. A window needs to be see-through, hard and waterproof: glass.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: making a raincoat from cotton because it is soft; cotton is absorbent, so the coat would soak through. Two: thinking hard and strong mean the same thing; glass is hard but can break easily when knocked. Three: comparing the mass of a big block and a small block; for a fair test, the blocks must be the same size. Four: thinking light materials must be weak; aluminium and bamboo are light but strong.

The questions for this section are with you now: waterproof and absorbent materials and choosing materials for a job.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the testing lab in George — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Scratch and Bend

Hard things do not dent or scratch easily, like stone and metal. Soft things squash, like foam and wool.

Stiff things do not bend, like wood. Flexible things bend, like rubber and cloth.

Keep the scratch test in mind and try the questions for this part: hard, soft, stiff and flexible.

## Subtopic: Pull and Lift

Strong things do not tear or snap easily, like rope and metal. Weak things tear easily, like paper.

Light things are easy to lift, like foam. Heavy things are hard to lift, like stone.

Hold the hanging strips in mind and try this part's questions: strong, weak, light and heavy.

## Subtopic: Wet or Dry

Waterproof things keep water out, like plastic and rubber.

Absorbent things soak up water, like a towel or paper towel.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Materials have properties: hard or soft, stiff or flexible, strong or weak, light or heavy, waterproof or absorbent. We test them fairly and choose a material for a job because its properties suit the job. A raincoat must be waterproof, and a towel must be absorbent.

Hold the raincoat and the towel in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: waterproof, absorbent and choosing materials.
