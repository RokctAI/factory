### subtopic_1 — Hard or Soft, Stiff or Flexible
**Assistant:** Why is glass hard but still breaks so easily?

**Tutor:** Hard and strong are different properties. Hard means a material does not scratch or dent easily. Glass is very hard: you cannot scratch it with a fingernail, and even a metal nail struggles to mark it. But glass is also brittle, which means it cannot bend or stretch even a tiny bit before it cracks. When you drop a glass on a tiled floor, the sudden knock pushes on one small spot. Glass cannot bend to spread out that push, so a crack starts and races through it, and it shatters. A rubber ball is not hard at all, but it bends and squashes when it hits the floor, so it does not break. Metal is both hard and able to bend a little, which is why a metal spoon survives being dropped. Car windscreens are made of special safety glass with a thin layer of plastic inside, so that when they crack, the pieces stay stuck together instead of flying around.

### subtopic_2 — Strong or Weak, Light or Heavy
**Assistant:** Can a material be waterproof and absorbent at the same time?

**Tutor:** Not one single material, but people often join materials together to get both properties. A disposable nappy is a good example. On the inside, it has a soft, absorbent layer that soaks up wetness and keeps a baby's skin dry. On the outside, it has a thin, waterproof plastic layer that stops the wetness from leaking onto clothes. A good raincoat often works the same way: a waterproof outer layer keeps the rain out, while a soft inner lining feels comfortable. Some materials can also be changed. Ordinary cotton cloth is absorbent, but if it is coated with wax or a special plastic, it becomes waterproof. That is how many tents and rain jackets are made. Paper is absorbent, but a waxed paper cup or a milk carton is coated so that it does not soak up the drink. Designers choose and combine materials so that each part of a product has the property it needs.

### subtopic_3 — Waterproof or Absorbent
**Assistant:** Why did the class weigh blocks that were all the same size?

**Tutor:** To make the test fair. A fair test changes only one thing, so we know what caused the result. In this test, the thing we want to compare is the material. If one block of wood is the size of a matchbox and another block of foam is the size of a shoebox, the foam might weigh more simply because there is so much more of it, even though foam is a light material. Then the test would tell us nothing useful about the materials. When all the blocks are exactly the same size and shape, the only difference between them is the material. Now, if the metal block is heavier than the wood block, we know it is because metal is a heavier material. The class used the same scale for every block too, and weighed them all in the same way. Keeping everything else the same is what makes the results trustworthy.
