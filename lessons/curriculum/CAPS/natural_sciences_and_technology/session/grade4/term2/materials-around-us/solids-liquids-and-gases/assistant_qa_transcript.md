### subtopic_1 — Solids
**Assistant:** Is toothpaste a solid or a liquid?

**Tutor:** Toothpaste is a tricky one, and scientists find it interesting too. When it sits on your toothbrush, it keeps its shape in a little worm, like a solid. But when you squeeze the tube, it flows out like a very thick liquid. Materials like toothpaste, tomato sauce and wet mud can act a bit like a solid and a bit like a liquid, depending on how hard you push them. For Grade 4, the best way to decide is to ask the main questions. Does it keep its shape on its own? Toothpaste does, mostly, as long as nobody pushes it. Does it flow when you pour it? Toothpaste does not really pour on its own. So most people would call it a soft, paste-like solid. Tomato sauce, on the other hand, does flow out of the bottle and spread over your chips, even if slowly, so we call it a thick liquid. Not every material fits neatly into one box, and that is part of what makes science fun.

### subtopic_2 — Liquids
**Assistant:** If gases spread out everywhere, how does a soccer ball stay pumped up?

**Tutor:** The air inside the soccer ball is trying to spread out, just like any gas. When Uncle Sipho pumps the ball, he squashes more and more air inside it. All that air pushes hard outwards on the inside of the ball, in every direction. That push is what makes the ball firm and round, so it bounces well. The air cannot escape, because the ball is made of a strong skin that air cannot get through, and the little valve where the pump goes in closes tightly. If you let the air out by opening the valve, you can hear it rush out, and it spreads into the yard just like the smell of the braai. If the ball gets a hole, the air escapes slowly and the ball goes flat. Car tyres and bicycle tyres work in exactly the same way. They are filled with squashed air that pushes outwards and holds the car or the bike up.

### subtopic_3 — Gases and Sorting Materials
**Assistant:** Why does a liquid always have a flat top when it is still?

**Tutor:** A liquid flows downwards whenever it can, because it is pulled towards the ground, the same pull that makes a ball fall. In a glass of water, every bit of the water is pulled down. If one part of the surface were higher than another, the water there would flow down into the lower part until both parts were the same height. So when the water stops moving, the top is flat and level. That is why the surface of a dam is flat on a calm day, and why builders sometimes use a tube of water to check that two points are at the same height. If you tilt the glass, the glass moves, but the water's surface stays level with the ground. A solid like a brick does not do this. You can stand a brick at an angle and it keeps that angle, because a solid does not flow.
