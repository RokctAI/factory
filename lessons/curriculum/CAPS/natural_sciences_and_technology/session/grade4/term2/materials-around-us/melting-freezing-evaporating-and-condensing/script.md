# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how heating and cooling cause changes of state: melting, evaporating, condensing and freezing, which is also called solidifying. The anchor is a hot December day in Upington in the Northern Cape, where summer days can be well above 35 degrees Celsius. Lerato and her brother buy ice lollies that start dripping almost at once. Their mother boils water in a pot with a lid to cook pap. Lerato pours a cold cool drink from the fridge into a glass, and soon the outside of the glass is covered in drops. In the evening, when the electricity goes off, they light a candle and watch the wax drip and harden. The big idea is that heating can change a solid into a liquid and a liquid into a gas, while cooling can change a gas back into a liquid and a liquid back into a solid, and the material stays the same material all the time. Naming and describing the four changes of state and saying whether each needs heating or cooling are examinable.

## Subtopic: Melting and Freezing

When a solid is heated, it can melt and turn into a liquid. The ice lolly is a solid made of frozen water and juice. In the hot Upington sun, it takes in heat and starts to melt. Drops of sweet liquid run down the stick and onto Lerato's fingers. Ice melts when it warms up above 0 degrees Celsius.

Other solids melt when they are heated too. Butter melts in a hot pan. Chocolate melts in your pocket on a hot day. Candle wax melts near the flame. Some solids need much more heat to melt. Metals and rock need very high temperatures, like those inside a furnace or deep inside the Earth.

When a liquid is cooled enough, it freezes and turns into a solid. Freezing is also called solidifying. Lerato's mother fills an ice tray with water and puts it in the freezer. A few hours later, the liquid water has frozen into solid ice cubes. Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius. The melted candle wax that drips down the side of the candle cools and becomes solid again within seconds. Melted chocolate put in the fridge becomes solid again.

Melting and freezing are opposites. Melting needs heating. Freezing needs cooling. The material does not become a new material. Ice, liquid water and the water in a lolly drip are all water. Melted wax is still wax.

The questions for this section are with you now: melting and freezing and whether each needs heating or cooling.

## Subtopic: Evaporating and Condensing

When a liquid is heated, it can evaporate and turn into a gas. Lerato's mother heats the water in the pot. As it gets hotter, more and more of the water turns into an invisible gas called water vapour, which rises into the air. When the water reaches about 100 degrees Celsius, it boils. Big bubbles of water vapour form inside the water and burst at the top. If she left the pot boiling for a long time without a lid, the water would slowly disappear into the air.

Evaporation does not only happen when water boils. Wet washing on the line dries in the sun because the water in the clothes evaporates. Puddles dry up after rain. On a hot, windy day in Upington, washing dries very quickly, because heat and wind speed up evaporation.

When a gas is cooled, it can condense and turn back into a liquid. When Lerato's mother lifts the lid off the pot, the inside of the lid is covered in drops of water. Water vapour from the pot touched the cooler lid, cooled down and condensed into liquid water. The cold glass of cool drink works the same way. The air around us has water vapour in it. When that vapour touches the cold glass, it cools and condenses into drops on the outside. The glass is not leaking. A bathroom mirror steams up after a hot shower for the same reason.

The white cloud you see above a boiling kettle is not water vapour, because water vapour is invisible. It is a cloud of tiny drops of water that formed when the vapour cooled in the air and condensed.

The questions for this section are with you now: evaporating and condensing and whether each needs heating or cooling.

## Subtopic: Heating and Cooling Change the State

Put all four changes together and you get a loop. Heating changes a solid into a liquid, which is melting, and a liquid into a gas, which is evaporating. Cooling changes a gas into a liquid, which is condensing, and a liquid into a solid, which is freezing or solidifying.

For water, the loop looks like this. Solid ice melts into liquid water. Liquid water evaporates into water vapour. Water vapour condenses into liquid water. Liquid water freezes into ice. These changes can go backwards and forwards many times, because no new material is made.

The candle shows the same idea with wax. Near the flame, solid wax melts into liquid wax. As the liquid runs down the side, it cools and solidifies again. Some wax even turns into gas, which burns in the flame.

People use changes of state every day. We freeze water to make ice for the cooler box. Farmers in the Karoo dry apricots and peaches in the sun, so the water in the fruit evaporates and the dried fruit keeps for months. Near the sea and at salt pans in the Northern Cape, salty water is left in shallow ponds in the sun. The water evaporates and the salt is left behind.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying the cold glass is leaking; water vapour from the air condensed on it. Two: calling the white cloud above a kettle water vapour; water vapour is invisible, and the white cloud is tiny drops. Three: saying melted ice is a new material; it is still water, only in a different state. Four: saying freezing needs heating; freezing needs cooling.

The questions for this section are with you now: the four changes of state and how heating and cooling cause them.

## Subtopic: Ice to Water and Back

Heat a solid and it melts into a liquid. The ice lolly melts in the sun.

Cool a liquid and it freezes into a solid. Water in the freezer becomes ice.

Freezing is also called solidifying.

## Subtopic: Water to Vapour and Back

Heat a liquid and it evaporates into a gas. Washing dries on the line.

Cool a gas and it condenses into a liquid. Drops form on the pot lid and on a cold glass.

Water vapour is invisible.

Hold the sweaty glass in mind and try this part's questions: evaporating and condensing.

## Subtopic: Hot and Cold

Heating: melting and evaporating.

Cooling: condensing and freezing.

It is still the same material.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Heating makes solids melt into liquids and liquids evaporate into gases. Cooling makes gases condense into liquids and liquids freeze, or solidify, into solids. Ice, water and water vapour are all water. These changes can go backwards and forwards.
