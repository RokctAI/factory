### subtopic_1 — Squares Wobble, Triangles Do Not
**Assistant:** If triangles are so strong, why are most windows and doors square or rectangular?

**Tutor:** Windows and doors are rectangles because they need to fit people, furniture and the way we build walls. A rectangle fits neatly into a brick wall, and a person walks through a tall rectangle easily. But a window frame or door frame is not left to wobble on its own. It is held firmly on all four sides by the solid wall around it, which stops it changing shape. The door itself is a stiff solid board, not a loose frame. If you build a gate or a wooden door from thin planks, you will often see a diagonal plank across it, making a Z shape. That diagonal turns the rectangle into triangles so the gate does not sag over time. Look at farm gates, garden gates and old wooden doors and you will see these diagonals. So rectangles are used where they are useful, and triangles are added wherever a frame needs to stay firm on its own.

### subtopic_2 — Triangles in Roofs, Bridges and Cranes
**Assistant:** How is a skeleton like a pylon, and how is it different?

**Tutor:** Both are frame structures. A pylon is made of steel struts joined at joints, and a skeleton is made of bones joined at joints. Both hold something up and give it shape: the pylon holds up power lines, and the skeleton holds up your body. Both use a frame instead of being solid all the way through, which keeps them lighter than a solid block would be. But there are big differences. A pylon must never move, so its joints are bolted tight and its struts are arranged in triangles so that it cannot change shape. A skeleton must be able to move, so many of its joints, like the knee, elbow and shoulder, can bend. Muscles attached to the bones pull on them to move the body and also hold the joints firmly when you stand still. Bones can also grow and repair themselves when they break, which steel cannot do. So a skeleton is a living, moving frame.

### subtopic_3 — Pylons and Skeletons
**Assistant:** Why do pylons have open spaces instead of being solid?

**Tutor:** A solid steel tower as tall as a pylon would be extremely heavy and expensive, and it would need huge foundations to stop it sinking into the ground. An open frame uses steel only along the lines where it is needed, in struts that form triangles, and leaves air everywhere else. That makes it much lighter and cheaper while still being very strong. The open spaces also help in storms. Wind pushing on a large solid surface acts like wind pushing on a sail, and could push a solid tower over. An open frame lets most of the wind blow straight through between the struts, so there is much less push. Because the pylon is also wider at the bottom, it is stable and hard to tip over. Engineers use the same ideas for cell phone towers, radio masts and the arms of cranes, which all need to be tall, light, strong and safe in high winds.
