### subtopic_1 — Plants Need Light
**Assistant:** Why does a plant in the dark grow taller than a plant in the sun?

**Tutor:** It seems strange, but it is the plant's way of trying to survive. When a young plant is in the dark, it has no light to make new food. The only food it has is what was stored in its seed. So it uses that stored food to stretch its stem up as fast as it can, hoping to reach light. It does not waste food on making big leaves or a thick, strong stem. That is why the plant in the cupboard is tall, thin and floppy, with tiny leaves. It is also pale yellow, because a plant only makes its green colour when it gets light. The plant on the windowsill has plenty of light, so it does not need to stretch. It puts its food into thick stems and wide green leaves instead. So the taller plant is not the healthier one. Once the dark plant's stored food runs out, it will die unless it finds light.

### subtopic_2 — Plants Need Water
**Assistant:** If plants need air, how do plants that live under water get air?

**Tutor:** Plants that live in ponds, rivers and the sea still need air, but they get it in a different way. Water is not just water. A little bit of air is mixed into it, which we cannot see. You may have noticed tiny bubbles in a glass of tap water that has stood for a while; those bubbles are air that was mixed into the water. Water plants take in this air through their thin leaves and stems. Many pond plants, like water lilies, also have leaves that float flat on top of the water, with their tiny holes on the upper side, facing the air. Some water plants have stems full of little air spaces that help them float and carry air down to their roots in the mud. Fish breathe the air in the water in a similar way, through their gills. So the rule stays the same: all plants need air, even those that live under water.

### subtopic_3 — Plants Need Air and a Fair Test
**Assistant:** Do plants need soil to grow?

**Tutor:** Soil helps most plants a lot, but it is not one of the three things in this lesson. Soil gives a plant a place to hold on with its roots, and it stores water and minerals that the roots take in. Minerals are like vitamins for plants and help them grow strong and green. But a plant can grow without soil if it gets water with minerals mixed into it. Some farmers grow lettuce and tomatoes with their roots in trays of water that has plant food added. Bean seeds can sprout on wet cotton wool without any soil at all, for a while, using the food stored inside the seed. What plants cannot do without is light, water and air. Take away any one of those and the plant will die, no matter how good the soil is. That is why our lesson focuses on light, water and air.
