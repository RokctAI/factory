# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how new plants can grow from cuttings and from seeds, and how seeds need water and warmth to germinate. The anchor is Mrs Booysen, the gardener at a primary school in Graaff-Reinet in the Karoo. Spekboom grows wild on the hills around the town, and Mrs Booysen fills the school garden with new spekboom plants grown from cuttings. She also saves bean and pumpkin seeds every autumn to plant the next spring. The Grade 4 class helps her and runs a test with bean seeds in warm and cold places. The big idea is that a new plant can grow from a piece of an old plant or from a seed, and that a seed only wakes up and starts to grow when it has water and warmth. Describing how to grow plants from cuttings and seeds and explaining what seeds need to germinate are examinable.

## Subtopic: New Plants from Cuttings

A cutting is a piece cut from a plant, usually a short piece of stem with a few leaves on it. Many plants can grow new roots from a cutting and become a whole new plant.

Mrs Booysen shows the class how. She chooses a healthy spekboom branch and cuts off a piece about as long as a pencil. She pulls the leaves off the bottom half. Then she pushes the bottom part into a pot of damp soil and puts the pot in a bright place, out of the hot afternoon sun. After a few weeks, she gently tugs the cutting. It does not come out, because new roots have grown from the stem and are holding it in the soil.

Some cuttings will grow roots in a jar of water, so you can watch them. A piece of sweet potato, a stem of mint or a geranium cutting in water will grow small white roots within one or two weeks.

The new plant grown from a cutting is exactly like its parent plant. If the parent had pink flowers, so will the new plant. Farmers and gardeners use cuttings to grow many new plants quickly and cheaply. Sugar cane in KwaZulu-Natal is planted from pieces of cane stem, and grape vines in the Western Cape are grown from cuttings.

Spekboom is a good plant for this, because it grows easily and survives with little water in the dry Karoo.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a cutting is and how a new plant grows from it.

## Subtopic: New Plants from Seeds

Most plants grow from seeds. A seed has three main parts. On the outside is a tough seed coat that protects it. Inside is a tiny baby plant, and around it is a store of food that the baby plant uses until it can make its own food.

When a seed starts to grow, we say it germinates. The class watches bean seeds germinate on wet paper towel in a clear jar. First the seed soaks up water and swells. The seed coat splits open. A small root pushes out and grows downwards, even if the seed is lying upside down. Then a shoot pushes upwards, towards the light. Two thick seed leaves open up, full of stored food, and soon the first true green leaves appear. Once the green leaves can make food from sunlight, the young plant no longer needs the food store.

Seeds are spread in many ways, so that new plants grow in new places. Some seeds blow in the wind, like the fluffy seeds of a dandelion. Some are eaten in fruits by birds and come out in their droppings. Some, like blackjacks, hook onto animal fur and your socks. Some seed pods dry out and burst open, flinging the seeds away from the parent plant. Spreading out means the young plants do not have to fight their parent for light, water and space.

Mrs Booysen saves seeds from her best pumpkins. She scoops them out, washes them, dries them in the shade and stores them in a paper bag in a cool, dry cupboard until spring.

The questions for this section are with you now: the parts of a seed and the steps of germination.

## Subtopic: Water and Warmth for Germination

What does a seed need to germinate? The class sets up a test with three jars, each with four bean seeds. Jar 1 has wet cotton wool and stays in a warm cupboard. Jar 2 has wet cotton wool and goes into the fridge, where it is cold. Jar 3 has dry cotton wool and stays in the warm cupboard. Everything else is the same.

After a week, only the seeds in Jar 1 have germinated. The seeds in the fridge had water but were too cold, so they did not grow. The seeds on dry cotton wool were warm but had no water, so they stayed dormant. Seeds need both water and warmth to germinate. They also need air, which is why seeds sunk deep in a jar full of water often rot instead of growing.

Interesting fact: seeds do not need light to germinate. The seeds in the dark warm cupboard germinated well. That makes sense, because seeds usually germinate under the soil, in the dark. The young plant only needs light once its leaves come up and must start making food.

This is why farmers in South Africa plant seeds like maize in spring, when the soil warms up and the rains come.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a cutting needs a seed to grow; a cutting grows new roots from its stem. Two: saying seeds need light to germinate; they need water and warmth. Three: planting seeds in winter and expecting them to germinate quickly; cold soil slows them down or stops them. Four: thinking the shoot comes out first; the root comes out first and grows down.

The questions for this section are with you now: what seeds need to germinate and the warm and cold test.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with Mrs Booysen's garden — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: A Piece Grows Roots

Cut a piece of stem. Push it into damp soil.

After a few weeks, roots grow from the stem. Now it is a new plant.

Spekboom, mint and sweet potato grow well from cuttings.

Keep the spekboom twig in mind and try the questions for this part: cuttings.

## Subtopic: A Seed Wakes Up

A seed has a coat, a baby plant and a food store.

It soaks up water and swells. The coat splits.

The root grows down first. Then the shoot grows up.

Hold the bean jar in mind and try this part's questions: seeds.

## Subtopic: Wet and Warm

Seeds need water and warmth to germinate.

Cold seeds did not grow. Dry seeds did not grow.

Seeds do not need light to germinate.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A new plant can grow from a cutting, which grows new roots from its stem, or from a seed. A seed has a seed coat, a baby plant and a food store. To germinate, a seed needs water and warmth. The root comes out first, then the shoot. Light is only needed once the leaves appear.

Hold the warm jar in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: water and warmth.
