# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers what plants need to grow: light, water and air. The anchor is a Grade 4 class in Pietermaritzburg. Their teacher gives them three pots, each with a young bean plant about the same size, planted in the same kind of soil. Pot A goes into a dark cupboard and is watered every day. Pot B stays on a sunny windowsill but gets no water. Pot C stays on the sunny windowsill and is watered every day. Later they test a fourth plant to see what happens when its leaves cannot take in air. The big idea is that a plant must have light, water and air to make its food and stay healthy, and that a fair test changes only one thing at a time. Explaining what plants need and why, and describing a simple investigation, are examinable.

## Subtopic: Plants Need Light

Plants make their own food in their leaves. To do this, they need energy, and that energy comes from light. Sunlight is the best light for plants.

After two weeks, the class opens the cupboard. The plant in Pot A has grown, but it looks strange. Its stem is long, thin and floppy, and its leaves are small and pale yellow instead of green. The plant stretched up, trying to find light, using up the food that was stored in its seed. Without light it could not make new food, and if it stays in the dark much longer it will die.

The plant in Pot C, on the windowsill, is short and sturdy with big, dark green leaves. It had light all day, so it could make plenty of food.

The class notices something else. The plant on the windowsill leans towards the window. Plants grow towards the light, because that is where they can make the most food. If you turn the pot around, after a few days the plant bends back towards the window again.

This is why farmers do not plant crops in the shade of big trees, and why grass under a parked car or a plank turns yellow after a few days. Gardeners choose sunny spots for vegetables like tomatoes and spinach. Some plants, like ferns and many indoor pot plants, are happy with less light, but even they will die in complete darkness.

Light does not have to come from the Sun. Some farmers grow herbs and lettuce indoors under special bright lamps. The plants use the lamp light to make food, just as they would use sunlight.

The questions for this section are with you now: why plants need light and what happens without it.

## Subtopic: Plants Need Water

All living things need water, and plants are no different. A plant takes in water through its roots. The water travels up through thin tubes in the stem to the leaves. The plant uses water to make its food, and water also keeps the plant firm and upright, a bit like air in a balloon.

The plant in Pot B was never watered. After a few days its leaves began to droop and hang down. This is called wilting. After two weeks its leaves have turned brown and crispy and the plant has died. The soil in the pot is dry and cracked.

If you water a wilting plant quickly enough, it will often stand up again within a few hours. But if it is left too long, it cannot recover.

Plants can also get too much water. If the soil is soaked all the time and never dries out, the roots cannot get the air they need, and they start to rot. So plants need the right amount of water, not as much as possible.

Some plants are very good at saving water. The aloe and other Karoo plants store water in their thick leaves, so they can survive long dry spells. But even they need some water to stay alive.

Water also carries minerals from the soil into the plant. Minerals help the plant grow strong and healthy. In South Africa, many places have dry seasons, so farmers often water their crops from rivers, dams or boreholes.

The questions for this section are with you now: why plants need water and what happens with too little or too much.

## Subtopic: Plants Need Air and a Fair Test

Plants also need air. The leaves take in air through tiny holes, mostly on their undersides. The plant uses a gas from the air to make its food, and it gives off a different gas, which animals and people breathe.

The class tests this with a fourth plant. They gently cover the undersides of its leaves with a thin layer of petroleum jelly, which blocks the tiny holes. After about a week, the covered leaves turn yellow and start to die, while uncovered leaves on a plant nearby stay green. The covered leaves could not take in air. People and animals depend on this too. The gas that plants give off through their leaves is the gas we need to breathe, so plants keep the air around us fresh.

The class has run a fair test. In a fair test, you change only one thing and keep everything else the same. All the pots had the same kind of plant, the same soil and the same size pot. Only one thing was different each time: light, water or air. That way, when a plant got sick, the class knew exactly what caused it. If Pot A had also been missing water, they would not know whether the dark or the dryness had made it pale.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying plants get their food from the soil; plants make their own food in their leaves using light. Two: thinking more water is always better; soaked roots rot. Three: thinking plants do not need air because they have no nose; leaves take in air through tiny holes. Four: changing two things at once in a test; then you cannot tell which change made the difference.

The questions for this section are with you now: why plants need air and how to plan a fair test.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the three bean pots — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Sunshine

A plant needs light to make its food.

In the dark cupboard, the plant grew long, thin and yellow.

On the windowsill, it grew green and strong, and it leaned towards the light.

Keep the dark cupboard in mind and try the questions for this part: light.

## Subtopic: A Drink of Water

Roots drink up water. Water keeps the plant firm.

No water: the plant wilts, then dies.

Too much water: the roots rot. Just right is best.

Hold the droopy plant in mind and try this part's questions: water.

## Subtopic: Fresh Air

Leaves take in air through tiny holes.

Block the holes and the leaves turn yellow.

A fair test changes only one thing at a time.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Plants need light, water and air to grow. Light gives them energy to make food in their leaves. Water keeps them firm and helps make food. Air goes in through tiny holes in the leaves. Without any one of these, a plant becomes weak and dies. A fair test changes just one thing.

Hold the three pots in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: air and fair tests.
