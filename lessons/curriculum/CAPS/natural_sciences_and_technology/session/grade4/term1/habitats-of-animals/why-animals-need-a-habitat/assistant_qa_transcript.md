### subtopic_1 — Food and Water
**Assistant:** Why do turtles go back to the same beach to lay their eggs?

**Tutor:** Scientists believe that female sea turtles return to lay their eggs on the same stretch of beach where they themselves hatched, many years before. A baby loggerhead that hatches on a beach in northern KwaZulu-Natal swims out into the ocean and may travel thousands of kilometres. When she is grown up, after many years, she somehow finds her way back to the same coast to nest. Scientists think turtles can sense the Earth's magnetism, a bit like having a compass inside them, and use it to find their way. The beach must also give the eggs what they need: soft sand to dig in, the right warmth, and a place above the high-tide mark so the eggs do not drown. That is why protecting these beaches is so important. If a nesting beach is spoilt by buildings, bright lights, noise or cars driving on the sand, the turtles may not be able to nest anywhere else.

### subtopic_2 — Shelter and Safety
**Assistant:** How do animals in the same habitat share the food without running out?

**Tutor:** Animals in the same habitat often eat different foods, or eat in different places or at different times. In the wetland, the hippo eats grass on the banks at night. The pelican catches fish in the open water. The flamingo filters tiny living things out of the mud in the shallows. The reed frog catches insects among the reeds. The fish eagle swoops down to grab fish near the surface. Because each animal has its own food, they do not all compete for the same thing. Even animals that eat the same kind of food can share. Giraffes eat leaves high up in the trees, while smaller antelope eat leaves lower down on the same bushes. Some animals feed during the day and others feed at night. This sharing allows many different kinds of animals to live together in one habitat. When something changes, like a drought, the food can become scarce, and then animals have to compete or move away.

### subtopic_3 — A Place for Babies
**Assistant:** Why does the reedbuck's colour help it escape danger?

**Tutor:** The reedbuck is a brown antelope, about the colour of dry grass and reeds. When it lies down or stands very still in the long grass, its colour blends in with everything around it. This is called camouflage. A predator like a leopard or a hyena looks for movement and for shapes that stand out. A brown reedbuck lying still in brown grass is very hard to see, even when it is quite close. Many animals use camouflage to hide in their habitat. Reed frogs are green or patterned like the reeds they sit on. Chameleons can change their colour to match the leaves. Some predators use camouflage too, to sneak up on their prey. A leopard's spots break up its outline in the shadows of the forest. Camouflage only works in the right habitat. A brown reedbuck standing on bright green lawn, or on white sand, would be easy to spot.
