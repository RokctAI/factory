# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers what a habitat is, and four kinds of habitat found in South Africa: grassland, forest, river and sea. The anchor is a Grade 4 class in Bloemfontein painting a large mural of South Africa on their classroom wall. They paint the open grassland of the Free State highveld, the green Knysna forest on the Garden Route, the Orange River flowing west to the Atlantic, and the sea along the coast. Then they add animals and plants to each part. The big idea is that a habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives, and that each habitat gives its living things what they need, so different habitats have different plants and animals. Defining a habitat and naming plants and animals found in grassland, forest, river and sea habitats are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Is a Habitat?

A habitat is the place where a plant or an animal lives. It is like its home address. A habitat gives a living thing the food, water, shelter and space it needs.

A habitat can be very big, like a whole grassland, or very small, like the space under a rock where a beetle and a few woodlice live, or a rotting log full of termites and fungi. A tree can be a habitat for birds in its branches, caterpillars on its leaves, ants on its bark and fungi on its roots.

Every habitat has non-living parts and living parts. The non-living parts include the soil, the rocks, the water, the air, the sunlight and the weather. The living parts are all the plants, animals and other living things that live there together.

Living things are suited to their habitats. Their bodies and the way they behave help them to survive there. A fish has gills to breathe under water and fins to swim, so its habitat is water. A camel thorn tree has very long roots that reach water deep underground, so it can live in the dry Kalahari. If you took a fish out of the water, or planted a water lily in the desert, it would die, because it is not suited to that habitat.

People can damage habitats by cutting down forests, ploughing grasslands, polluting rivers and leaving plastic on beaches. When a habitat is damaged, the plants and animals that live there lose their home.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a habitat is and what it provides.

## Subtopic: Grassland and Forest

A grassland is a wide, open habitat covered mostly with grasses, with very few trees. The highveld of the Free State and Gauteng is grassland. Summers are warm and rainy, and winters are cold and dry, often with frost. Because there are few trees, grassland animals can see a long way. Many of them are grazers, which eat grass, like zebra, blesbok and black wildebeest. They live in herds and can run fast across the open ground to escape danger. Birds like the secretary bird stalk through the grass hunting snakes and insects. Underground, mole rats and termites make tunnels. In winter the grass turns brown and dry, and in summer it grows green again after the rain. One of the most common grasses is red grass, which turns a rusty red colour when its seeds ripen. Grassland also has many wild flowers that bloom after the first summer rains, and bulbs that store food underground through the cold, dry winter.

A forest is a habitat with many tall trees growing close together. Their leaves join up overhead and make the forest floor shady, cool and damp. The Knysna forest on the Garden Route is the biggest forest in South Africa. It has huge yellowwood trees, ferns and mosses. The Knysna turaco, a green bird with a red flash under its wings, lives in the tree tops. Small antelope like the blue duiker hide among the shady bushes, and bushbuck move quietly between the trees. The forest floor is covered with fallen leaves, where beetles, millipedes and fungi break them down into rich soil.

The questions for this section are with you now: grassland and forest habitats and the living things found there.

## Subtopic: River and Sea

A river is a habitat of fresh water that flows from high ground down to the sea. The Orange River is the longest river in South Africa. Fish such as the yellowfish swim in the flowing water. Frogs lay their eggs at the edges, where the water is calm. Reeds grow along the banks, and weaver birds hang their nests from them. In warmer rivers in the north and east, like the Limpopo, hippos spend the day in the water to stay cool, and crocodiles wait in the shallows. Dragonflies dart over the surface, catching insects.

The sea is a habitat of salty water. It is the biggest habitat on Earth. South Africa has the Atlantic Ocean on the west coast and the Indian Ocean on the east coast. Sea animals include fish such as sardines, dolphins, whales, sharks, octopuses, crabs and seals. Cape fur seals live in big groups on rocky islands off the west coast. Every winter, huge shoals of sardines swim up the KwaZulu-Natal coast, followed by dolphins, sharks and sea birds; this is called the sardine run. Seaweed grows in the sea and gives many small animals food and shelter. Animals from a river cannot live in the salty sea, and most sea animals cannot live in fresh river water.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a habitat is only an animal's nest; a habitat is the whole place where it lives. Two: thinking a grassland has many trees; it is mostly grass with very few trees. Three: thinking river water and sea water are the same; a river is fresh water and the sea is salty. Four: thinking plants do not have habitats; plants live in habitats too.

The questions for this section are with you now: river and sea habitats and the living things found there.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the class mural — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: A Home Place

A habitat is the place where a plant or an animal lives.

It gives food, water, shelter and space.

Fish suit water. A camel thorn tree suits the dry Kalahari.

Keep the mural in mind and try the questions for this part: what a habitat is.

## Subtopic: Grass and Trees

Grassland: wide and open, lots of grass, few trees. Zebra, blesbok and the secretary bird live there.

Forest: many tall trees, shady and damp. The Knysna turaco and the blue duiker live there.

Hold the open veld and the shady forest in mind and try this part's questions: grassland and forest.

## Subtopic: Fresh Water and Salt Water

River: fresh, flowing water. Fish, frogs, reeds, hippos and crocodiles.

Sea: salty water. Dolphins, whales, sharks, seals, crabs and seaweed.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives and finds what it needs. Grasslands are open and grassy, forests have many tall trees, rivers have fresh flowing water, and the sea is salty. Each habitat has its own plants and animals, suited to living there.

Hold the four corners of the mural in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: river and sea.
