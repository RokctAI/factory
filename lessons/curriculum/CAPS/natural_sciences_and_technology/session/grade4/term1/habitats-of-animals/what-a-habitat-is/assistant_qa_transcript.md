### subtopic_1 — What Is a Habitat?
**Assistant:** Can one animal live in more than one habitat?

**Tutor:** Some animals can, and some cannot. Many animals are suited to just one habitat. A fish that lives in a river needs fresh water and would die in the salty sea, and a blue duiker needs the shade and cover of a forest. But some animals move between habitats. A hippo spends the day in the river to stay cool and to protect its skin from the sun, then comes out at night to graze on the grass near the river. So it uses both a river habitat and a grassland habitat. Many birds fly between habitats, feeding in one place and nesting in another. Some animals even travel very far. Southern right whales spend summer feeding in the cold seas near Antarctica, and in winter they swim to the warmer waters off our coast to have their babies. The important idea is that every animal must find what it needs, food, water, shelter and safety, in the habitats it uses.

### subtopic_2 — Grassland and Forest
**Assistant:** Why are there so few trees in a grassland?

**Tutor:** There are a few reasons. On the highveld, winters are very cold, with frost on many nights, and the winters are also dry. Many young trees are killed by the frost, while grasses survive because their growing parts are low down near the soil. Grasslands also often have fires in the dry season, started by lightning or by people. The fires burn quickly over the dry grass. Grass grows back fast from its roots after a fire, but young trees are burnt and killed. Grazing animals also eat tree seedlings along with the grass. All of these things together keep the grassland open. In places where there is more rain and no frost, more trees can grow, and the habitat slowly changes into bushveld or forest. So the weather, fire and animals all help to decide what kind of habitat a place becomes.

### subtopic_3 — River and Sea
**Assistant:** Why can sea fish not live in a river?

**Tutor:** Sea water and river water are very different. Sea water is salty, while river water is fresh, with almost no salt. The bodies of fish are suited to the kind of water they live in. A sea fish is used to salty water all around it. Its body is built to deal with all that salt and to keep the right amount of water inside it. If you put a sea fish into fresh water, its body would take in too much water and it would not be able to control it, so it would become sick and die. The same problem happens the other way around for a river fish in the sea. That is why river habitats and sea habitats have different fish. A few special fish, like some eels, can move from one to the other, but their bodies have to change slowly before they can do it.
