# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers why animals need a habitat. A habitat gives an animal food, water, shelter, a place to have its babies and a way to escape from danger. The anchor is iSimangaliso Wetland Park on the north coast of KwaZulu-Natal, one of South Africa's World Heritage Sites. It has Lake St Lucia, swamps full of reeds and papyrus, coastal forest, grassland and long sandy beaches. A Grade 4 class from Richards Bay visits on a school trip with a park ranger. They see hippos in the lake, crocodiles on the banks, flamingos and pelicans, reed frogs, and the beach where sea turtles come to lay their eggs. The big idea is that an animal can only survive in a habitat that gives it everything it needs. Explaining what animals need from their habitat, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Food and Water

Every animal needs food for energy and to grow. Its habitat must provide the right kind of food. Hippos graze on grass on the banks of Lake St Lucia at night. Pelicans and fish eagles catch fish from the lake. Flamingos wade in the shallow water and filter tiny animals and plants out of the mud with their special beaks. Reed frogs eat insects that fly among the reeds. Each animal finds its own kind of food in the habitat.

Different animals in the same habitat often eat different foods, so they do not all compete for the same thing. Some are plant eaters, some are meat eaters, and some, like the bushpig, eat both.

Every animal also needs water. Many animals in the wetland drink from the lake and streams every day. Animals in very dry habitats have special ways of getting water. The gemsbok in the Kalahari can go for long periods without drinking, because it gets water from the juicy plants and wild melons it eats. Frogs take in water through their skin, so they must live where it is damp.

When the rains fail and there is a drought, lakes and pans dry up. Then animals must move to find water, or they may die. In some years Lake St Lucia has become very shallow and salty in droughts, and the fish and birds have had to move.

The questions for this section are with you now: how a habitat gives animals food and water.

## Subtopic: Shelter and Safety

Animals need shelter, a place to rest, to sleep and to be protected from the weather. Hot sun, cold wind, rain and frost can harm animals. The hippo shelters from the hot sun by lying in the water during the day, because its skin can burn and dry out. Birds roost in the trees of the coastal forest at night. Frogs hide under damp leaves and in the reeds during the heat of the day. Monkeys sleep high in the trees.

Animals also need a way to escape from danger. Many animals are hunted by other animals, called predators. A habitat helps prey animals hide or get away. A reedbuck hides in the long grass and the reeds, where its brown colour blends in. A crab scuttles into its burrow in the sand when a bird comes near. A frog leaps into the water when a snake comes close. Small birds dive into thick bushes where bigger birds of prey cannot follow.

Predators need their habitat too. A crocodile lies hidden in the water, with only its eyes and nostrils above the surface, waiting for animals to come and drink. A leopard in the forest uses the shade and branches to stay hidden while it hunts.

Some animals stay safe by living in groups. Zebras and wildebeest on the grassland near the lake move in herds, because many eyes and ears notice a lion sooner than one animal alone. Monkeys call out loudly to warn the rest of the troop when they see a leopard or an eagle.

So the shape of the habitat, with its water, long grass, burrows and thick bushes, gives shelter and safety to the animals that live there.

The questions for this section are with you now: how a habitat gives shelter and helps animals escape danger.

## Subtopic: A Place for Babies

Animals need a safe place to have and raise their young. The habitat must give them that. Weaver birds build their nests at the ends of thin branches, often over water, where snakes find it hard to reach them. Crocodiles dig nests in the sandy banks and lay their eggs there, and the mother guards them. Frogs lay their eggs in calm water, where the tadpoles can grow.

The most amazing story at iSimangaliso is the sea turtles. Loggerhead and leatherback turtles swim great distances across the ocean. Every summer the females come ashore at night on the beaches of northern KwaZulu-Natal. Each one digs a hole in the sand above the high-tide mark and lays about a hundred eggs. The warm sand keeps the eggs warm. About two months later, the baby turtles hatch, dig their way out and race to the sea. The beach is protected so that people do not disturb the nests or drive on the sand.

When a habitat is damaged, animals lose what they need. If a wetland is drained, the frogs and birds lose their food, water and nesting places. If beaches are full of lights and noise, turtles stop coming to nest. Looking after habitats keeps animals alive.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying animals only need a habitat for food; they also need water, shelter, safety and a place for babies. Two: thinking all animals in one habitat eat the same food; they eat different foods. Three: thinking predators do not need a habitat; they need it to hunt and hide. Four: thinking a damaged habitat does not matter because animals can just move; often there is nowhere else for them to go.

The questions for this section are with you now: why animals need a habitat to have their young, and why habitats must be protected.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the iSimangaliso wetland — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Eat and Drink

A habitat gives animals food. Hippos eat grass. Pelicans eat fish. Frogs eat insects.

A habitat gives animals water. They drink from lakes and rivers.

The gemsbok gets water from juicy plants.

Keep the lake in mind and try the questions for this part: food and water.

## Subtopic: Hide and Rest

Animals need shelter from the sun, rain and cold. The hippo lies in the water.

Animals need to escape danger. The reedbuck hides in the reeds. The crab runs into its hole.

Hold the reeds in mind and try this part's questions: shelter and safety.

## Subtopic: Babies and Caring

Animals need a safe place for babies. Weavers nest over water. Turtles lay eggs in warm sand.

If we damage a habitat, animals lose their home.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A habitat gives animals five things: food, water, shelter, a safe place to have babies, and ways to escape danger. Different animals use the same habitat in different ways. When a habitat is damaged, the animals that live there are in trouble, so we must protect habitats.

Hold the turtle beach in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: babies and protecting habitats.
