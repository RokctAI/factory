# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the many different kinds of living things and the seven life processes that all living plants and animals carry out: feeding, growing, reproducing, breathing, excreting, sensing and moving. The anchor is a Grade 4 class on a hunt in their school garden in Polokwane. They find a marula tree, grass, a beetle, an earthworm, a weaver bird building a nest, a lizard on a wall and a few mushrooms after the rain. The big idea is that living things look very different from each other, but they all do the same seven things to stay alive. Naming the seven life processes and explaining how plants and animals carry them out are examinable.

## Subtopic: Many Kinds of Living Things

The world is full of living things. Some are huge, like a baobab tree in Limpopo or an elephant in the Kruger National Park. Some are tiny, like an ant or a flea. Some are so small that we need a microscope to see them.

Scientists sort living things into groups. The two biggest groups you will study this year are plants and animals. Plants include trees, grass, flowers, ferns and vegetables like spinach. Animals include mammals like dogs and springbok, birds like the weaver, reptiles like the lizard, insects like the beetle, fish, frogs and worms. People are animals too.

Mushrooms are living things as well, but they are not plants and not animals. They belong to their own group, called fungi. The mushrooms in the garden grew because the soil was damp after the rain.

Living things live almost everywhere: in soil, in water, in the air, in hot deserts like the Kalahari and in cold mountains like the Drakensberg. South Africa has an amazing number of different plants and animals. The Cape fynbos alone has thousands of kinds of plants that grow nowhere else on Earth.

Living things also come in many shapes and colours. A giraffe has a long neck to reach the top leaves of a thorn tree. A tortoise carries a hard shell on its back. A cactus has spines instead of flat leaves. A frog starts life as a tadpole in a pond and later hops onto land. These differences help each living thing survive in the place where it lives.

The class makes a list of everything they find. They have a tree, grass, a beetle, a worm, a bird, a lizard and mushrooms. Seven very different things, and every one of them is alive. Next to their list they make a second list of things that are not alive: the stones on the path, the tap, the fence and a plastic bottle.

The questions for this section are with you now: the many kinds of living things and the groups they belong to.

## Subtopic: The Seven Life Processes

How do we know that something is alive? We check whether it carries out the seven life processes. A life process is something a living thing must do to stay alive or to make more of its kind.

Feeding: living things need food for energy. Animals eat plants or other animals. Plants make their own food in their leaves, using sunlight.

Growing: living things get bigger and change as they get older. A baby grows into an adult, and a seed grows into a tree.

Reproducing: living things make new living things like themselves. Birds lay eggs, dogs have puppies and plants make seeds.

Breathing: living things take in air. Animals use lungs, gills or tiny holes in their skin. Plants take in air through tiny holes in their leaves.

Excreting: living things get rid of waste they do not need. Animals breathe out waste gas, sweat and pass urine and droppings. Plants give off waste gases and water through their leaves.

Sensing: living things notice what is happening around them. Animals see, hear, smell, taste and feel. Plants sense light and grow towards it.

Moving: living things move by themselves. Animals walk, swim, fly, crawl or slither. Plants move slowly, as their leaves turn to face the light and their roots grow down.

A good way to remember the seven is to count them on your fingers: feed, grow, reproduce, breathe, excrete, sense, move.

The questions for this section are with you now: the seven life processes and what each one means.

## Subtopic: Plants Do All Seven Too

Some learners think plants are not really alive because they stay in one place. Let us check the marula tree against all seven processes. It feeds by making food in its leaves. It grows from a seed into a tall tree. It reproduces by making fruits with seeds inside. It breathes through tiny holes under its leaves. It excretes waste gases and water through its leaves. It senses light, and its branches grow towards it. It moves when its leaves turn and its roots spread through the soil. So the tree does all seven, just more slowly and quietly than an animal.

Now check the weaver bird. It feeds on seeds and insects. It grows from a chick into an adult. It reproduces by laying eggs in the nest it weaves. It breathes with lungs. It excretes droppings. It senses with its eyes and ears, and it flies away when a cat comes near. It moves by hopping and flying. To be called living, a thing must carry out all seven processes, not just one or two.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a plant is not alive because it cannot walk; plants move slowly by growing and turning. Two: saying plants do not feed; they make their own food in their leaves. Three: saying only animals breathe; plants take in air through tiny holes in their leaves. Four: saying a car is alive because it moves; a car cannot grow, feed or reproduce, so it does not carry out all seven processes.

The questions for this section are with you now: how plants carry out the seven life processes.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the school garden — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: So Many Living Things

The garden is full of living things: a tree, grass, a beetle, a worm, a bird, a lizard and mushrooms.

Plants are one big group. Animals are another big group. People are animals too.

Mushrooms are not plants or animals. They are fungi.

Keep the garden in mind and try the questions for this part: kinds of living things.

## Subtopic: Seven Things Living Things Do

Count them on your fingers. Feed. Grow. Reproduce. Breathe. Excrete. Sense. Move.

Feed means take in food. Reproduce means make babies or seeds. Excrete means get rid of waste. Sense means notice things around you.

Every living thing does all seven.

Hold your seven fingers up and try this part's questions: the life processes.

## Subtopic: Plants Are Alive Too

A plant makes its own food in its leaves. It breathes through tiny holes in its leaves. It moves slowly towards the light.

So a plant does all seven, just quietly.

Pocket summary of the lesson. There are many kinds of living things, such as plants, animals and fungi. All living things carry out seven life processes: feeding, growing, reproducing, breathing, excreting, sensing and moving. Plants do all seven too, but slowly.

Hold the marula tree in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: plants and the seven processes.
