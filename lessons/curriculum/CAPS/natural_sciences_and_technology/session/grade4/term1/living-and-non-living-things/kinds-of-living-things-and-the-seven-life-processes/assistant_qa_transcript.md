### subtopic_1 — Many Kinds of Living Things
**Assistant:** If a plant cannot walk, how can we say that it moves?

**Tutor:** Moving does not only mean walking from place to place. It means that a living thing can change the position of its own parts. Animals move fast, so we notice it easily. Plants move very slowly, so we have to watch carefully over hours or days. Put a pot plant on a windowsill and look at it after a few days. Its leaves and stems will have turned to face the window, because they are moving towards the light. A sunflower head follows the Sun when it is young. Roots move too, as they grow down into the soil and spread out to find water. Some plants even move quickly. The leaves of a sensitive plant fold up when you touch them, and a Venus flytrap snaps shut on an insect. So plants do move, they just do it in their own slow way, without legs or wings.

### subtopic_2 — The Seven Life Processes
**Assistant:** Why are mushrooms not plants if they grow in the soil like plants?

**Tutor:** Mushrooms look a bit like plants because they grow in the ground and stay in one place, but they are different in an important way. Plants have green leaves, and they use sunlight to make their own food. Mushrooms are not green and they cannot make their own food. Instead, they feed on dead leaves, old wood and other rotting things in damp soil. That is why mushrooms pop up in the garden after rain, in shady, damp places. Because they feed in a different way, scientists put them in their own group, called fungi. Mould on old bread is a fungus too. Fungi are still living things, because they feed, grow, reproduce, breathe, excrete, sense and move in their own way. They reproduce by making tiny spores, a bit like very small seeds that blow in the air.

### subtopic_3 — Plants Do All Seven Too
**Assistant:** Do plants really get rid of waste? I have never seen a plant go to the toilet.

**Tutor:** Plants do excrete, but they do it quietly, so we do not notice. Excreting means getting rid of things a living thing does not need, and that could harm it if it stayed inside. Animals get rid of waste by breathing out, sweating and passing urine and droppings. Plants have no toilet and no bladder. They get rid of waste gases through the tiny holes in their leaves. They also give off extra water as an invisible gas through those same holes. If you tie a clear plastic bag over some leaves on a sunny day, you will see little drops of water collect inside the bag after a few hours. That water came out of the leaves. Some trees also store waste in their old leaves and then drop those leaves in autumn. So plants do excrete. It is just much less smelly than when animals do it.
