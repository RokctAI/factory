### subtopic_1 — Dried Beans Are Waiting to Grow
**Assistant:** If a seed is alive, does it breathe while it is sitting in the packet?

**Tutor:** Yes, but only a tiny bit. A dormant seed is like a person in a very deep sleep. Its life processes slow down so much that we cannot see them. It still uses a very small amount of air and a very small amount of its stored food, just enough to stay alive. This is why seeds do not last forever. Old seeds that have been kept for many years may slowly use up their energy, and then they will not grow at all. When the seed gets water and warmth, its life processes speed up again. It starts breathing faster, it uses its food store to grow, and it pushes out a root and a shoot. Farmers keep their seeds in a cool, dry place so that they stay dormant and healthy until planting time. If seeds get damp in the store, they may start to sprout too early or go mouldy.

### subtopic_2 — Yeast Wakes Up
**Assistant:** Why does yeast make bread rise?

**Tutor:** Yeast is made of millions of tiny living things. When they get warm water and food, they wake up and start to feed. The food they like best is sugar, and there is also food in the flour itself. As the yeast feeds, it gives off a gas. In a cup of water, that gas escapes as bubbles at the top. In bread dough, the gas cannot escape easily because the dough is stretchy. The gas gets trapped in thousands of little pockets, and the dough puffs up, a bit like many tiny balloons being blown up inside it. That is why a bowl of dough left in a warm place for an hour can grow to twice its size. When the bread is baked, the heat kills the yeast, but the little holes stay. If you look closely at a slice of bread, you can see those holes. They were made by yeast that was alive.

### subtopic_3 — A Fertilised Egg Is Alive
**Assistant:** How does the chick breathe inside an egg if the shell is hard?

**Tutor:** An eggshell looks solid, but it is covered with thousands of tiny holes called pores. They are far too small to see with your eyes, but air can pass through them. Inside the shell, the growing chick is surrounded by thin layers that carry air to it. At the round end of the egg there is a small pocket of air as well. The chick takes in air through these parts and gets rid of the waste gas the same way. This is why a hen turns her eggs and why the eggs must not be covered in paint or oil, which would block the pores. Near the end of the 21 days, the chick breaks into the air pocket and takes its first real breaths with its lungs. Then it uses a tiny hard point on its beak, called an egg tooth, to chip its way out of the shell.
