# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers things that appear not to be living but carry on living when they are given the right conditions. The three examples are dried beans, dried yeast and a fertilised egg. The anchor is Gogo's kitchen in Gqeberha. On her shelf are a packet of dry sugar beans, a packet of dried yeast for baking vetkoek, and a bowl of eggs collected from her hens. In the yard a rooster crows. The big idea is that something can look completely lifeless, yet still be alive and resting, waiting for water, warmth or food before it carries out its life processes again. Explaining why these things are living, and what conditions they need, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Dried Beans Are Waiting to Grow

A dry bean is hard, it does not move, it does not feed and it does not grow. It sits in a packet for months. So is it dead? No. A bean is a seed, and inside every seed is a tiny baby plant with a store of food packed around it. While the seed is dry, the baby plant is resting. Scientists call this resting state dormant.

Gogo's grandson puts four dry beans on wet cotton wool in a jam jar and keeps the jar in a warm place. After two or three days the beans swell up as they soak up water. Their skins split. A small white root pushes out and grows downwards. A few days later a shoot pushes up with two small leaves. The baby plant has started its life processes again: it is growing, using its stored food, breathing and sensing which way is up.

The seed needed water and warmth to wake up. Without water, the bean can stay dormant in the packet for a long time. This is very useful. It means seeds can wait through a dry winter, or travel a long way in the wind or in an animal's droppings, and still grow when conditions are right.

Nature shows this every year in Namaqualand, in the dry Northern Cape. For most of the year the ground looks bare and dusty. But the soil is full of dormant seeds. When the winter rains come, the seeds soak up water, and by August and September the whole veld is covered in orange, yellow and white daisies. The seeds were alive the whole time, waiting for water.

Not every seed will wake. If a seed is cooked, the baby plant inside is killed by the heat. A cooked bean on wet cotton wool will only go mouldy. That is why farmers plant raw seeds, never cooked ones.

The questions for this section are with you now: why a dry bean is living and what it needs to start growing.

## Subtopic: Yeast Wakes Up

Dried yeast looks like tiny brown grains of sand. But each grain holds millions of living things far too small to see. Yeast is a fungus, from the same group as mushrooms. When it is dried, it rests, just like a seed.

To make vetkoek, Gogo mixes the yeast with warm water and a spoon of sugar. After about ten minutes the mixture turns frothy and bubbly, and it smells like bread. The yeast has woken up. It is feeding on the sugar, and as it feeds it gives off a gas. That gas makes the bubbles. When the yeast is mixed into the dough, the gas gets trapped and the dough rises and becomes soft and puffy.

The yeast needed three things: water, warmth and food. If Gogo uses cold water, the yeast wakes very slowly. If she uses boiling water, the heat kills the yeast, and there are no bubbles at all. The dough stays flat and hard.

This is a simple test the class can do. Put dried yeast in three cups: one with cold water and sugar, one with warm water and sugar, and one with very hot water and sugar. The warm cup bubbles best. The yeast was alive all the time, waiting in the packet.

Yeast shows us that being alive is not about size or shape. A yeast grain does not look like a plant or an animal, but when it wakes up it feeds, gives off waste gas, grows and makes more yeast. That is how we know it is living.

The questions for this section are with you now: how dried yeast shows it is alive and the conditions it needs.

## Subtopic: A Fertilised Egg Is Alive

An egg from a hen also looks like it is not alive. It cannot move and it does not feed. But if a hen has mated with a rooster, her eggs are fertilised. Inside a fertilised egg is the start of a baby chick, with the yolk as its food store.

When the hen sits on her fertilised eggs, her body keeps them warm. The tiny chick inside starts to grow. It uses the yolk for food and takes in air through tiny holes in the shell. After about 21 days, the chick pecks its way out of the shell. Gogo has a rooster in her yard, so many of her eggs are fertilised and some will hatch if a hen sits on them.

Most eggs from a shop come from farms that have no roosters. Those eggs are not fertilised, so they have no chick starting inside and they will never hatch, no matter how warm they are kept.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a dry bean is dead because it does not move; it is dormant and will grow when given water and warmth. Two: saying yeast is a powder, not a living thing; it is made of tiny living fungi. Three: thinking any egg will hatch if kept warm; only fertilised eggs have a chick inside. Four: waking yeast with boiling water; too much heat kills it.

The questions for this section are with you now: how a fertilised egg carries on living and what it needs.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with Gogo's kitchen shelf — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Sleeping Seeds

A dry bean has a baby plant inside. It is resting, not dead.

Give it water and warmth. It swells, a root comes out, then a shoot.

A cooked bean will never grow. The heat killed it.

Keep the jam jar in mind and try the questions for this part: seeds that wait.

## Subtopic: Bubbly Yeast

Dried yeast is made of tiny living things. They rest in the packet.

Add warm water and sugar. The yeast feeds and makes bubbles. The dough rises.

Boiling water kills the yeast.

Hold the bubbly cup in mind and try this part's questions: yeast that wakes up.

## Subtopic: Eggs That Hatch

A fertilised egg has a tiny chick starting inside.

Keep it warm for about 21 days and the chick hatches.

Shop eggs usually have no chick inside, so they never hatch.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Some things look dead but are only resting. A dry bean grows when it gets water and warmth. Dried yeast feeds and makes bubbles in warm water with sugar. A fertilised egg kept warm hatches into a chick. Too much heat kills all of them.

Hold the warm hen in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: eggs that hatch.
