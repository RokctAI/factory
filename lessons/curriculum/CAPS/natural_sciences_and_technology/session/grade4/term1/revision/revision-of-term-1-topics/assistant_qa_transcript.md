### subtopic_1 — Living and Non-Living Things
**Assistant:** How can I remember the difference between once living and never living?

**Tutor:** Ask yourself one question: did this thing ever come from a plant, an animal or a fungus? If the answer is yes, it is once living. Wood comes from trees, so a wooden spoon is once living. Paper is made from wood, so it is once living too. Cotton comes from a plant, leather and wool from animals, and a shell from a sea animal. All of these are once living. If the answer is no, the thing was never alive. Stones, sand, water, air, metal, glass and plastic did not come from a living thing, so they are never living. A good trick is to trace the thing back to where it came from. A cotton T-shirt goes back to a cotton field. A metal spoon goes back to rock dug out of a mine. A plastic bottle goes back to a factory. Keep tracing until you reach the start, and you will know which group it belongs to.

### subtopic_2 — Plants, Animals and Growing
**Assistant:** Why does a seed not need light to germinate, but a young plant does?

**Tutor:** A seed carries its own packed lunch. Inside the seed is a store of food that the baby plant uses to start growing. When the seed germinates under the soil, in the dark, it uses this food to push out a root and a shoot. It does not need to make any new food yet, so it does not need light. That is lucky, because most seeds are buried in dark soil. But the food store is small, and it runs out after a few days or weeks. By then, the shoot must have reached the surface and opened its green leaves. From then on, the young plant must make its own food in its leaves, and to do that it needs light. That is why a bean plant in a dark cupboard grows for a while, using its stored food, and then turns pale and dies. Germination needs water and warmth; growing on needs light, water and air.

### subtopic_3 — Habitats and Shelters
**Assistant:** Why do so many animals need shelter if they already have a habitat?

**Tutor:** A habitat is the whole area where an animal lives, like a grassland, a forest or a river. A shelter is one special safe spot inside that habitat. The habitat gives the animal food and water, but the animal still needs a place to rest, hide from the weather and keep its babies safe. A meerkat's habitat is the dry Kalahari, but its shelter is the burrow it digs, where it sleeps and escapes from eagles and jackals. A weaver's habitat might be a river bank, but its shelter is its woven nest. Some animals make their own shelters, some grow them, like a tortoise shell, and some use what they find, like an owl in a hollow tree. Animals that live with people, like dogs, cattle and horses, often rely on us to build their shelters. So habitat and shelter work together: the habitat is the neighbourhood, and the shelter is the house.
