# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 1 topics of Life and Living: living and non-living things and the seven life processes; the structure of plants and animals; what plants need to grow; habitats of animals; and structures for animal shelters. The anchor is a Life and Living revision morning in the school hall of a primary school in East London. The Grade 4 class has set up five stations. Station 1 has a bean, a stone, a shell and a packet of yeast. Station 2 has a bean plant pulled out of its pot and pictures of animals. Station 3 has three bean pots from the plant test. Station 4 has a map of habitats. Station 5 has a bird nest, a snail shell and a model kennel. The big idea is that living things carry out seven life processes, have parts suited to their lives, and need the right conditions, habitats and shelters to survive. The whole of Term 1 is examinable.

## Subtopic: Living and Non-Living Things

At Station 1, the class sorts things into three groups. Living things carry out all seven life processes: feeding, growing, reproducing, breathing, excreting, sensing and moving. Count them on your fingers. Plants do all seven too: they make their food in their leaves, breathe through tiny holes in their leaves and move slowly towards the light.

There are many kinds of living things, including plants, animals and fungi such as mushrooms. People are animals.

Some things look dead but are only resting. A dry bean is a seed with a baby plant inside. Give it water and warmth and it grows. Dried yeast is made of tiny living fungi. In warm water with sugar, it feeds and makes bubbles. A fertilised egg kept warm by a hen hatches into a chick after about 21 days.

Non-living things, like stones, water, air, plastic and metal, cannot carry out all seven life processes and were never alive. Some non-living things, like fire and clouds, seem to move or grow, but that is not enough. Things that were once living, like an empty shell, a feather, a wooden desk or a cotton shirt, came from living things but are now dead.

To sort anything, ask two questions. Does it carry out all seven life processes now? If not, did it come from something that was alive?

The questions for this section are with you now: life processes, things that seem not to be alive, and sorting living, once living and never living.

## Subtopic: Plants, Animals and Growing

At Station 2, the class labels the bean plant. Roots hold the plant in the soil and take in water and minerals. A carrot has a tap root and grass has fibrous roots. The stem holds the plant up and carries water and food; a tree trunk is a stem. Leaves make food using sunlight. Flowers attract insects and birds and help make seeds. Fruits hold the seeds; a tomato is a fruit. Seeds grow into new plants. Plants differ in the size, shape and colour of their parts.

Animals have a head, a body, a tail and limbs, such as legs, wings, fins and flippers. Sense organs, like eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin, help them find food and notice danger. Animals differ in size, shape, body covering and sense organs. Lions have fur, ostriches have feathers, crocodiles have scales, tortoises have shells and frogs have smooth, wet skin.

At Station 3, the bean pots remind everyone what plants need. The plant in the dark cupboard was tall, thin and pale. The plant with no water wilted and died. Leaves covered with petroleum jelly turned yellow. Plants need light, water and air. A fair test changes only one thing.

New plants can grow from cuttings, which grow new roots from a piece of stem, and from seeds. Seeds need water and warmth to germinate. The root comes out first, then the shoot. Seeds do not need light to germinate.

The questions for this section are with you now: the parts of plants and animals, what plants need, and growing new plants.

## Subtopic: Habitats and Shelters

At Station 4, the map shows four habitats. A habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives. A grassland is wide and open, with lots of grass and few trees, and is home to zebra and blesbok. A forest has many tall trees and is shady and damp, like the Knysna forest. A river has fresh, flowing water, with fish, frogs and hippos. The sea is salty, with dolphins, seals, sharks and whales. Animals need their habitat for five things: food, water, shelter, a safe place to have babies, and a way to escape danger.

At Station 5, the class looks at shelters. Natural shelters are made or grown by animals: weaver nests, swallows' mud nests, snail and tortoise shells, hollow trees, wasp nests made of chewed wood, and burrows. Human-made shelters are built by people: kennels, cages, kraals and stables. A good shelter protects the animal from the weather and danger, and is the right size. It is made from materials that suit the job: wood is strong, wire mesh lets in air and light, plastic keeps out rain and thatch keeps a shelter cool.

Shelters can be shell structures, which are hollow with a strong outer skin, like an egg or a plastic kennel, or frame structures, which are parts joined like a skeleton, like a bird cage or a kraal. Triangles make frames strong.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a plant is not alive because it cannot walk; plants carry out all seven processes, slowly. Two: saying a tomato is a vegetable in a science test; it holds seeds, so it is a fruit. Three: saying seeds need light to germinate; they need water and warmth. Four: calling a bird cage a shell structure; it is a frame.

The questions for this section are with you now: habitats, animal needs, and shell and frame shelters.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the five stations — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Alive or Not

Living things feed, grow, reproduce, breathe, excrete, sense and move.

Dry beans, dried yeast and fertilised eggs are alive but resting.

Shells and wood were once alive. Stones and plastic were never alive.

Keep Station 1 in mind and try the questions for this part: living and non-living.

## Subtopic: Parts and Needs

Plants: roots, stem, leaves, flowers, fruits, seeds.

Animals: head, body, tail, limbs and sense organs.

Plants need light, water and air. Seeds need water and warmth.

Hold the bean pots in mind and try this part's questions: parts and needs.

## Subtopic: Homes and Shelters

A habitat is a home place: grassland, forest, river or sea.

Animals need food, water, shelter, a place for babies and a way to escape.

Shells are hollow with a strong skin. Frames are parts joined together.

Pocket summary of the term. Living things carry out seven life processes. Some things only seem dead. Plants and animals have parts with jobs, and they differ in size, shape and colour or covering. Plants need light, water and air, and seeds need water and warmth. Habitats give animals what they need, and shelters keep them safe as shells or frames.

Hold the whole term in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: habitats and shelters.
