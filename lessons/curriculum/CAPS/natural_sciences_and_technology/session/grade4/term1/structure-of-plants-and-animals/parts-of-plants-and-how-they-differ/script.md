# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the basic structure of plants, with their roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds, and the visible differences between plants in the size, shape and colour of their parts. The anchor is the vegetable garden at a primary school in Mthatha. The Grade 4 class looks after rows of carrots, bean plants climbing up sticks, tomato plants and a big pumpkin plant spreading over the ground. Around the edge of the garden grow tall gum trees, an aloe and a bed of orange marigolds. The big idea is that almost every plant is built from the same six parts, and each part has a job, but the parts can look very different from one plant to the next. Naming and labelling the parts of a plant, giving the job of each part and describing differences between plants are examinable.

## Subtopic: Roots and Stems

Roots grow under the ground. They do two jobs. They hold the plant firmly in the soil so that the wind cannot blow it over, and they take in water and minerals from the soil. Minerals are tiny bits of goodness from the soil that the plant needs to be healthy.

There are two main kinds of roots. Some plants, like the carrot and the bean, have one big main root that grows straight down, with small roots growing out of its sides. This is called a tap root. The orange part of the carrot that we eat is its tap root, swollen with stored food. Other plants, like grass and maize, have many thin roots that spread out in all directions like a messy bunch of strings. These are called fibrous roots, and they are very good at holding soil together.

The stem grows up from the roots. It holds the plant up so that its leaves can reach the sunlight. Inside the stem are thin tubes, like tiny pipes. Some tubes carry water from the roots up to the leaves, and others carry food made in the leaves to the rest of the plant.

Stems can be soft and green, like the stem of a bean plant, or hard and woody, like the trunk of a gum tree. A tree trunk is just a very thick, strong stem. The bean plant has a weak stem that cannot hold it up on its own, so it climbs up a stick. The pumpkin stem creeps along the ground instead. A potato is a surprise: the part we eat grows under the ground, but it is a swollen stem that stores food, not a root. Look closely at a potato and you will see little eyes, which are buds that can grow into new shoots.

The questions for this section are with you now: the jobs of roots and stems and the different kinds of each.

## Subtopic: Leaves, Flowers, Fruits and Seeds

Leaves are the food factories of the plant. They are usually green and flat, so that they can catch as much sunlight as possible. The plant uses sunlight, air and water to make its food in the leaves. Leaves also have tiny holes underneath, through which the plant takes in air and gives off water.

Flowers are the parts that help a plant reproduce. Many flowers have bright petals and a sweet smell. These attract insects such as bees and butterflies, and birds such as sunbirds. When the visitors drink the sweet nectar, they carry a yellow powder called pollen from flower to flower. This helps the flower to make seeds.

After a flower has been visited, its petals fall off and part of it swells into a fruit. A fruit holds and protects the seeds. The tomato, the pumpkin and the bean pod are all fruits, because they have seeds inside. We often call tomatoes and pumpkins vegetables in the kitchen, but to a scientist they are fruits.

Seeds are the start of new plants. Each seed holds a tiny baby plant and a store of food. Seeds come in many sizes, from the tiny seeds of a tomato to the big, hard seeds of an avocado or a marula. Some plants make only a few seeds, while one sunflower head can hold hundreds. When a seed lands in damp, warm soil, it can grow into a new plant, and the life of the plant starts all over again.

The questions for this section are with you now: the jobs of leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.

## Subtopic: Plants Look Different

All plants have the same main parts, but the parts can look very different. Plants differ in the size, shape and colour of their parts.

Size: a baobab tree in Limpopo can have a trunk so wide that many people holding hands cannot reach around it. Some plants that float on ponds are smaller than your fingernail. The leaves of a banana plant are longer than a person, while the leaves of a thyme plant are tiny.

Shape: leaves can be wide and flat like a pumpkin leaf, thin and needle-like like a pine tree, or thick and spiky like the aloe, which stores water in its fat leaves. Roots can be one thick tap root or many thin fibrous roots.

Colour: most leaves are green, but flowers come in almost every colour. Marigolds are orange, jacaranda trees in Pretoria turn purple in spring, and the king protea, South Africa's national flower, is pink. Fruits change colour too: a tomato starts green and turns red when it is ripe.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a tree has no stem; its trunk is its stem. Two: saying a tomato is not a fruit; it has seeds inside, so it is a fruit. Three: thinking roots are only for holding the plant up; they also take in water and minerals. Four: thinking all plants have green flowers or no flowers; many plants have brightly coloured flowers.

The questions for this section are with you now: differences between plants in size, shape and colour.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the school vegetable garden — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Down Below and Holding Up

Roots hold the plant in the soil and drink up water.

A carrot is a big tap root. Grass has many thin roots.

The stem holds the plant up and carries water to the leaves. A tree trunk is a stem.

Keep the carrot in mind and try the questions for this part: roots and stems.

## Subtopic: Food Factories and New Plants

Leaves make food from sunlight.

Flowers bring bees and help make seeds.

Fruits hold the seeds. Tomatoes and pumpkins are fruits.

Seeds grow into new plants.

Hold the tomato in mind and try this part's questions: leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.

## Subtopic: Big, Small, Every Colour

Some plants are huge, like the baobab. Some are tiny.

Leaves can be flat, thin like needles, or fat and spiky like an aloe.

Flowers can be orange, purple, pink or almost any colour.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Plants have six main parts. Roots hold the plant and take in water. Stems hold it up and carry water and food. Leaves make food. Flowers help make seeds. Fruits protect the seeds. Seeds grow into new plants. Plants differ in the size, shape and colour of these parts.

Hold the whole garden in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: how plants differ.
