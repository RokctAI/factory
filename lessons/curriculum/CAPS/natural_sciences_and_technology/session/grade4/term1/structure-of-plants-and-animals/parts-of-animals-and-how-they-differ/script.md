# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the basic structure of animals, with a head, tail, body, limbs and sense organs, and the visible differences between animals in size, shape, body covering and sense organs. The anchor is a Grade 4 class from Soweto on a trip to the Johannesburg Zoo. They walk past a lion resting in the shade, an elephant spraying dust over its back, zebras, an ostrich, a crocodile lying by the water, a tortoise, a frog in the reptile house and a beetle on the path. The big idea is that most animals are built on the same basic plan, but the parts look very different because each animal is suited to the way it lives. Labelling the parts of an animal and describing differences in size, shape, body covering and sense organs are examinable.

## Subtopic: Head, Body, Tail and Limbs

Look at the lion. At the front is its head. The head holds the brain, which controls the whole body, and the mouth, which the lion uses to eat. Most of the sense organs are on the head too.

Behind the head is the body. The body holds the heart, the lungs and the stomach, which keep the animal alive. The body is the biggest part of most animals.

At the back is the tail. Tails have different jobs. The lion and the zebra swish their tails to chase away flies. A monkey uses its tail to help it balance in the trees. A fish swishes its tail from side to side to push itself through the water. A crocodile's strong tail helps it swim too.

Limbs are the parts an animal uses to move. Legs, arms, wings, flippers and fins are all limbs. The lion and the zebra walk on four legs. The ostrich walks and runs on two legs and has two wings, but it cannot fly. People have two legs and two arms. A beetle, like all insects, has six legs, and a spider has eight.

Not every animal has every part. A snake has a head, a long body and a tail, but no limbs at all. It moves by bending its body. An earthworm has no legs either. A frog has no tail when it is grown up, though it had one when it was a tadpole.

Limbs also come in different shapes for different jobs. A duck has webbed feet that work like paddles in the water. An eagle has sharp, curved claws for catching prey. A mole rat has strong front feet for digging tunnels. A dolphin's front limbs are flippers for steering through the sea. Each limb shape suits the way the animal moves and lives.

The questions for this section are with you now: the head, body, tail and limbs of animals and their jobs.

## Subtopic: Sense Organs

Sense organs help an animal find out what is happening around it. People have five sense organs. Eyes see, ears hear, the nose smells, the tongue tastes and the skin feels touch, heat and cold.

Animals use their senses to find food, to find water, to find each other and to notice danger. The zebra has eyes on the sides of its head, so it can see a lion creeping up from almost any side. The lion has eyes at the front of its head, which help it judge how far away its prey is before it pounces.

The elephant's trunk is its nose and its top lip joined together. It smells very well with its trunk and uses it to pick up food and to drink. Its big ears help it hear far away sounds, and flapping them helps it cool down.

Some animals have sense organs that people do not have. A beetle has two feelers, called antennae, on its head. It uses them to touch and smell. A snake flicks out its forked tongue to pick up smells from the air. A fish can feel tiny movements in the water along the sides of its body. A cat has long whiskers that help it feel its way in the dark and tell whether a gap is wide enough to squeeze through.

The questions for this section are with you now: sense organs and how animals use them.

## Subtopic: Animals Look Different

Animals differ in size. An African elephant is the biggest land animal on Earth and can weigh as much as four small cars. A beetle can sit on your fingertip. Some animals are so small we need a microscope to see them.

Animals differ in shape. A giraffe is tall with a long neck, a snake is long and thin, a tortoise is round and flat underneath, and a fish is smooth and pointed at the front so that it slides through water.

Animals differ in their body covering, the outside layer of the body. The lion has fur, and the zebra has short hair with black and white stripes. The ostrich has feathers. The crocodile and snakes have dry, tough scales. Fish have smooth, wet scales. The tortoise has a hard shell. The frog has smooth, wet skin with no hair or scales. The beetle has a hard outer covering, like a suit of armour. Body coverings keep animals warm or cool, protect them and help them hide.

Animals differ in their sense organs too. An owl has huge eyes to see at night. A bat has big ears and hunts by listening for echoes in the dark. A mole rat lives underground and has tiny eyes.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a snake is not an animal because it has no legs; limbs are not needed to be an animal. Two: calling an ostrich a mammal because it is big; it has feathers and is a bird. Three: thinking all animals have five sense organs like people; a beetle senses with antennae. Four: saying a frog has scales; a frog has smooth, wet skin.

The questions for this section are with you now: differences in size, shape, body covering and sense organs.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the zoo trip — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Head to Tail

Most animals have a head, a body and a tail.

Legs, arms, wings, fins and flippers are limbs. Limbs help animals move.

A snake has no limbs. It slides on its body.

Keep the lion in mind and try the questions for this part: animal parts.

## Subtopic: Eyes, Ears, Nose, Tongue, Skin

Sense organs tell an animal what is around it.

Eyes see. Ears hear. The nose smells. The tongue tastes. The skin feels.

A beetle uses its feelers. An elephant smells with its trunk.

Hold the elephant in mind and try this part's questions: senses.

## Subtopic: Fur, Feathers, Scales

Animals come in all sizes and shapes.

Lions have fur. Ostriches have feathers. Crocodiles have scales. Tortoises have shells. Frogs have wet skin.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Most animals have a head, a body, a tail and limbs. Sense organs, like eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin, help them find food and notice danger. Animals differ in size, shape, body covering and sense organs, and each one suits the way the animal lives.

Hold the zoo in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: how animals differ.
