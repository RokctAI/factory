### subtopic_1 — Roots and Stems
**Assistant:** Why is a tomato called a fruit when we cook it like a vegetable?

**Tutor:** Scientists and cooks use the word fruit in different ways. To a cook, a fruit is something sweet that we eat as a snack or a pudding, like an apple or a mango, while a vegetable is something we cook with a meal. To a scientist, a fruit is the part of a plant that grows from a flower and holds the seeds. If you cut a tomato open, you can see lots of little seeds inside, so it is a fruit. The same is true for pumpkins, green peppers, cucumbers, butternut, chillies and the pods of beans and peas. They all grow from flowers and they all hold seeds. A carrot, on the other hand, is a root, and a potato is a swollen underground stem. Neither of them grew from a flower, and neither has seeds inside, so they are not fruits. In the science lesson, always use the scientist's meaning: if it grew from a flower and holds seeds, it is a fruit.

### subtopic_2 — Leaves, Flowers, Fruits and Seeds
**Assistant:** Why are leaves usually green and flat?

**Tutor:** Leaves are green because they are full of a green substance that catches sunlight. The plant needs that light to make its food. The greener the leaf, the more of this substance it has. Leaves are usually flat and thin so that they have a big surface facing the Sun, like a solar panel on a roof. A big, flat surface catches more light than a round lump would. That is why trees spread their branches out and hold their leaves in layers, so that as many leaves as possible get some sunshine. Not all leaves follow this rule. Plants that live in very dry places, like the aloe and many Karoo plants, often have thick, fat leaves that store water, or very small leaves that lose less water in the heat. Each shape suits the place where the plant lives.

### subtopic_3 — Plants Look Different
**Assistant:** How do bees help a flower make seeds?

**Tutor:** Inside a flower is a yellow, dusty powder called pollen. For most flowers to make seeds, pollen must be carried from one flower to another flower of the same kind. Flowers cannot walk, so they need helpers. The bright petals and sweet smell invite bees, butterflies, beetles and birds like sunbirds to visit. Deep inside the flower is a sweet drink called nectar, which the visitors come to drink. While a bee is reaching for the nectar, pollen sticks to its hairy body. When the bee flies to the next flower, some of that pollen rubs off onto it. This is called pollination. Once a flower has been pollinated, its petals dry up and drop off, and the part at the base of the flower starts to swell. It grows into a fruit with seeds inside. So every time you eat a pumpkin or a tomato, you can thank a busy bee.
