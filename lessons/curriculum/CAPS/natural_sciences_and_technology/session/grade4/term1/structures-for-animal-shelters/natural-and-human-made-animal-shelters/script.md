# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers natural animal shelters, such as nests, shells, hollow trees and wasp nests, and human-made animal shelters, such as kennels, cages, kraals and stables. The anchor is a farm near Upington in the Northern Cape, where a Grade 4 class from the local school spends a day. In a camel thorn tree hangs a huge sociable weaver nest like a haystack. A tortoise plods past, owls peer out of a hollow tree, and paper wasps have built a nest under the roof of the shed. The farmer's dog sleeps in a kennel, his cattle come into a kraal at night, his horses live in a stable, and his canaries sing in a cage on the stoep. The big idea is that animals need shelter, and that some animals build or grow their own shelters, while people build shelters for the animals they keep. Naming and describing natural and human-made animal shelters and explaining why animals need them are examinable.

## Subtopic: Shelters Animals Make

Many animals make their own shelters. These are natural shelters.

Birds build nests to lay their eggs and raise their chicks. Each kind of bird builds its own kind of nest. The sociable weaver of the Kalahari builds the biggest nest of any bird. Many birds work together, pushing dry grass and sticks into a huge thatched roof with many rooms underneath. One nest can be home to over a hundred birds. Inside, it stays cool in the burning summer days and warm on freezing winter nights. Other weavers knot single nests out of green grass strips. Swallows build cup-shaped nests out of little balls of mud stuck under the edge of a roof.

Some animals carry their shelter with them. A snail grows a shell on its back and pulls its soft body inside when it is in danger or when the weather is dry. A tortoise has a hard shell and pulls in its head and legs.

Hollow trees make shelters too. When a branch breaks off an old tree, the wood inside may rot away and leave a hole. Owls, hornbills, squirrels and bats use these holes to sleep and nest in. Woodpeckers chip out their own holes in tree trunks.

Paper wasps make their nests out of paper. They chew tiny bits of dry wood and mix it with spit to make a paste. Then they shape it into rows of six-sided cells, each one holding an egg. When the paste dries, it is light and strong, like grey paper. Honey bees make their cells from wax instead.

Other animals dig shelters. Meerkats and aardvarks dig burrows underground, and termites build tall mounds of soil.

The questions for this section are with you now: natural shelters that animals make.

## Subtopic: Shelters People Make for Animals

People keep animals as pets and on farms. These animals need shelter too, so people build human-made shelters for them.

A kennel is a small house for a dog. It has a roof to keep off the rain and sun, walls to keep out the wind, and an opening for the dog to go in and out. A good kennel is raised a little off the ground so that the floor stays dry.

A cage is a shelter made of wire or bars, used for birds, rabbits or hamsters. The bars let in air and light and let us see the animal, while stopping it from escaping and keeping out cats.

A kraal is a fenced enclosure for cattle, goats or sheep. Traditional kraals in South Africa are made of thorn branches, wooden poles or packed stones built into a circle. The cattle are brought into the kraal at night to keep them safe from jackals, leopards and thieves. In many homesteads, the kraal is an important place at the centre of family life.

A stable is a building for horses. It has a roof, strong walls and a door, with straw on the floor for the horses to rest on. A chicken coop or hen house gives chickens a dry place to sleep and lay eggs, safe from snakes and wild cats. People also build beehives from wooden boxes so they can collect honey. Inside the box, the bees still build their own wax cells, so a beehive is a human-made shelter with a natural shelter inside it. Zoos build shelters too, such as night houses for elephants and warm, damp houses for frogs and snakes.

The questions for this section are with you now: human-made shelters for pets and farm animals.

## Subtopic: What a Good Shelter Does

Natural or human-made, a good animal shelter does the same jobs. It protects the animal from the weather: hot sun, rain, wind, cold and frost. It keeps the animal safe from predators and, for farm animals, from thieves. It gives a safe place to sleep and rest. And it gives a safe place to lay eggs or have babies and keep them safe while they are small.

A shelter must suit the animal that uses it. A kennel for a big dog must be bigger than a kennel for a small dog, so it can stand up and turn around. A bird cage must have spaces between the bars that are smaller than the bird. A kraal fence must be tall and strong enough that a calf cannot squeeze out and a jackal cannot jump in.

Shelters are made from materials that suit the job and the place. Birds use grass, twigs, mud and feathers. Wasps use chewed wood. People use wood, bricks, metal, wire, thatch, stones and thorn branches. The sociable weaver's thick grass nest keeps out heat and cold, which suits the extreme Kalahari weather.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a tortoise's shell is a shelter someone built for it; it grows its own shell. Two: thinking a kraal is a building with a roof; it is an open enclosure with a fence or wall. Three: thinking wasps make their nests from leaves; paper wasps make theirs from chewed wood. Four: thinking any size of shelter will do; a shelter must fit the animal.

The questions for this section are with you now: the jobs of a good shelter and how a shelter must suit its animal.
