# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how animal shelters can be shell structures or frame structures, and how they come in different shapes, sizes and materials. The anchor is a Grade 4 class in Kimberley that has two pet rabbits in the school garden. The rabbits need a new shelter, so the class sets out to design and build one. First they collect examples: an egg, a snail shell, a tortoise shell, a plastic dog kennel, a wire bird cage, a photo of a kraal and a hen run made of poles and wire mesh. The big idea is that a shelter can get its strength from a strong outer skin, which is a shell structure, or from a skeleton of parts joined together, which is a frame structure, and that a designer chooses the shape, size and materials to suit the animal. Identifying shell and frame structures and describing their shapes, sizes and materials are examinable.

## Subtopic: Shell Structures

A shell structure is hollow inside, with a thin, strong outer layer that holds its shape and protects what is inside. There is no skeleton inside it. The outer layer does all the work.

Nature is full of shell structures. An eggshell is very thin, but its curved shape makes it surprisingly strong. Hold an egg in your palm, wrap your fingers around it and squeeze evenly. It is very hard to break, because the curve spreads the push over the whole shell. Do this test over a bowl, just in case. A snail shell is a spiral shell, and a tortoise shell is a dome. A tortoise shell is so strong that the tortoise can hide inside it when a jackal tries to bite it.

People make shell structures too. A plastic dog kennel is a shell: it is moulded in one piece, or in two halves clipped together, with no poles inside. A plastic pet carrier for taking a cat to the vet is a shell. A dome tent with its fabric pulled tight, a metal water tank and a clay pot are shell structures too.

Shell structures are often curved, because curves are strong. They are light for their size and keep out wind and rain well, because the whole outside is covered. Their weak point is a hole or a crack. Once an eggshell is cracked, it breaks easily.

## Subtopic: Frame Structures

A frame structure is made of parts, such as sticks, poles, rods or wire, joined together to make a skeleton. The frame holds the shape, and the spaces between the parts are left open or covered with another material.

A bird cage is a frame structure. Thin metal bars are joined to make the sides and the top. The open spaces let in air and light. A kraal fence is a frame of poles or branches. The hen run in the photo is a frame of wooden poles with wire mesh stretched over it. A traditional hut starts as a frame of bent poles tied together, which is later covered with grass or mud.

Nature has frames too. A weaver bird starts its nest by tying a ring of grass strips between two twigs, then weaves the rest onto that frame. The skeleton inside your body is a frame of bones that holds you up.

The class makes a small frame from drinking straws joined with pipe cleaners. A square frame wobbles and squashes sideways when they push its corner. When they add a straw across from one corner to the opposite corner, making two triangles, the frame becomes stiff and strong. Triangles make frames strong.

Frames are good for big shelters, because they use less material than a solid wall, and they let in air and light. They can be covered with any material that suits the job, such as wire, cloth, plastic sheeting or thatch.

## Subtopic: Shapes, Sizes and Materials

Animal shelters come in many shapes. Domes, like the tortoise shell and some kennels, are strong and shed rain easily. Boxes, like most hutches and stables, are easy to build and fit neatly in a corner. Cylinders, like some beehives, and cones, like termite mounds, are also common. Many shelters have a sloping roof so that rainwater runs off.

Shelters come in many sizes. A wasp's cell is smaller than your fingernail. A sociable weaver nest can be wider than a car. A shelter must be big enough for the animal to move, turn around and lie down, but not so big that it is cold and hard to keep clean.

Shelters are made from many materials. Natural materials include grass, twigs, mud, leaves, wax and stone. Human-made materials include plastic, metal, glass, bricks and wire. The best material depends on the job. Wood is strong and easy to cut. Metal wire is strong and lets air through. Plastic is light and waterproof. Thatch keeps a shelter cool.

The class writes a design brief: design a rabbit shelter that keeps the rabbits dry, safe from dogs and cats, and cool in the hot Kimberley summer. They choose a frame of wooden sticks for strength, covered with wire mesh on the sides for air and a sloping plastic roof to keep out rain.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying a bird cage is a shell structure; it is a frame of bars with open spaces. Two: thinking a shell structure has poles inside; the outer skin holds the shape. Three: thinking a square frame is strongest; adding a triangle makes it much stronger. Four: choosing a material without thinking about the job; a cardboard kennel will soften in the rain.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the rabbit shelter — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Hollow Covers

A shell structure is hollow with a strong outside.

An egg, a snail shell, a tortoise shell and a plastic kennel are shells.

Curved shapes make shells strong.

Keep the egg in mind and try the questions for this part: shell structures.

## Subtopic: Skeletons of Sticks

A frame structure is parts joined together, like a skeleton.

A bird cage, a kraal fence and a hen run are frames.

Add a triangle and a wobbly frame becomes strong.

Hold the straw frame in mind and try this part's questions: frame structures.

## Subtopic: Choose the Best

Shelters can be domes, boxes, cylinders or cones.

They must be the right size for the animal.

Choose materials for the job: wood is strong, wire lets in air, plastic keeps out rain.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A shell structure has a strong outer skin and is hollow inside, like an egg or a plastic kennel. A frame structure is a skeleton of parts joined together, like a bird cage or a kraal. Triangles make frames strong.
