# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers why animals need food for their life processes, how all animals depend on plants as their primary food source, and how animals can be grouped as herbivores, carnivores and omnivores. The anchor is a school trip by a Grade 5 class from Richards Bay to the Hluhluwe-iMfolozi Park, one of the oldest game reserves in Africa and the place where the white rhino was saved from dying out. From the bus they see white rhinos grazing, giraffes browsing on thorn trees, a pride of lions lying in the shade, wild dogs trotting along the road, and a troop of baboons turning over stones and picking fruit. Each learner keeps a feeding diary, writing down what every animal eats. The big idea is that every animal needs food, that animals cannot make their own food, and that plants are the starting point of food for all of them. Grouping animals as herbivores, carnivores and omnivores, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Why Animals Need Food

All animals need food. Food gives them energy, and it gives them the materials they need to grow and repair their bodies.

Animals need energy for all their life processes. Remember the seven life processes: moving, feeding, growing, breathing, sensing, excreting and reproducing. Running, walking and flying use energy. Keeping warm uses energy. Even sleeping uses energy, because the heart keeps beating and the lungs keep breathing all night.

Animals need food to grow. A newborn white rhino weighs about as much as a grown-up person, but it grows into an adult that weighs as much as two cars. All that new body is built from the food it eats.

Animals need food to repair their bodies. When a lion is scratched by a buffalo's horn, its body uses materials from food to heal the wound.

Unlike green plants, animals cannot make their own food. They must find and eat it. That is why animals spend so much of their time looking for food. A white rhino grazes for about half of the day and night. A giraffe browses for many hours a day.

The class notices that the animals that move a lot, or are very big, eat a great deal. A young, growing animal also needs plenty of food.

The ranger adds a surprise. A crocodile in the river may eat a big meal and then go for weeks without eating again. Reptiles like crocodiles, snakes and lizards do not use food energy to keep their bodies warm. They warm up by lying in the sun instead. Mammals and birds, like lions, rhinos and people, use a lot of their food energy just to keep their bodies warm all the time, so they need to eat much more often.

The questions for this section are with you now: why animals need food for energy, growth and repair.

## Subtopic: Plants Feed Them All

Green plants are the primary food source for animals. Primary means first, or most important. Plants make food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide, and store it in their leaves, stems, roots, fruits and seeds. Animals get that stored food when they eat plants.

Even animals that never eat plants depend on them. A lion eats zebra and buffalo, but zebra and buffalo eat grass. Without grass, there would be no zebra, and without zebra, the lion would starve. So all animal food starts with plants.

Different animals eat different parts of plants. White rhinos, zebra and wildebeest graze on grass. Giraffes and black rhinos browse on the leaves and twigs of trees and bushes. Elephants eat grass, leaves, bark, roots and fruit. Sunbirds drink nectar from flowers. Many birds eat seeds. Monkeys and fruit bats eat fruit. Porcupines dig up roots and bulbs.

Animals have body parts suited to what they eat. The white rhino has a wide, square mouth like a lawnmower, perfect for cropping short grass. The black rhino has a pointed, hooked upper lip that grips the leaves and twigs of bushes. A giraffe's long neck and long tongue reach leaves that other animals cannot reach.

The questions for this section are with you now: why plants are the primary food source, and how different animals eat different plant parts.

## Subtopic: Herbivores, Carnivores and Omnivores

Scientists sort animals into three groups by what they eat.

Herbivores eat only plants. Examples are rhinos, zebra, giraffes, impala, elephants, hippos, cows, sheep, rabbits and grasshoppers. Herbivores usually have flat, broad back teeth for grinding plant material. Many have eyes on the sides of their heads, so they can see danger coming from almost all around them.

Carnivores eat only other animals, or almost only meat. Examples are lions, leopards, cheetahs, wild dogs, crocodiles, eagles, owls, snakes and spiders. Carnivores that hunt usually have sharp, pointed teeth or a sharp beak for tearing meat, strong claws or talons for catching, and eyes facing forward to judge distances when they pounce. Some carnivores, like vultures and hyenas, often eat animals that are already dead. They are called scavengers.

Omnivores eat both plants and animals. Examples are baboons, bushpigs, warthogs, honey badgers, chickens, many birds and people. The baboons on the trip ate fruit and seeds, but also dug up insects and scorpions from under stones. Omnivores often have a mix of teeth: sharp teeth at the front and flatter teeth at the back for grinding.

People are omnivores. We eat maize meal, vegetables and fruit, and many people also eat meat, fish, eggs and milk.

At the end of the day, the class sorts the feeding diary into three columns on the bus. Rhino, giraffe, impala and zebra go under herbivores. Lion, wild dog and the crocodile in the river go under carnivores. Baboon and warthog go under omnivores, because warthogs mostly eat grass and roots but will also eat insects, eggs and even dead animals.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a big animal must be a carnivore; elephants, rhinos and hippos are huge herbivores. Two: thinking carnivores do not depend on plants; the animals they eat depend on plants. Three: thinking people are carnivores; people are omnivores. Four: thinking all animals can make their own food in sunlight; only green plants can.

The questions for this section are with you now: herbivores, carnivores and omnivores, with examples and teeth.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Hluhluwe-iMfolozi trip — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Fuel for Life

Animals need food for energy, to grow and to heal.

Animals cannot make their own food. They must find it.

Keep the grazing rhino in mind and try the questions for this part: why animals need food.

## Subtopic: It Starts with Plants

Plants are the first food for all animals.

Even a lion needs plants, because zebra eat grass.

Animals eat grass, leaves, fruit, seeds, roots and nectar.

Hold the browsing giraffe in mind and try this part's questions: plants as food.

## Subtopic: Plant, Meat or Both

Herbivores eat plants. Carnivores eat meat. Omnivores eat both.

Rhino: herbivore. Lion: carnivore. Baboon: omnivore.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Animals need food for energy, growth and repair, and they cannot make their own food. Plants are the primary food source for all animals. Herbivores eat only plants, carnivores eat other animals, and omnivores eat both plants and animals. People are omnivores.

Hold the rhino, the lion and the baboon in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: herbivores, carnivores and omnivores.
