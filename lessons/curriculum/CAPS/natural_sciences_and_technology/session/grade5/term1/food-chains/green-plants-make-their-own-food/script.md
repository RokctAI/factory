# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how green plants make their own food using water, carbon dioxide and energy from sunlight, and how they release oxygen into the air while they do it. The anchor is the school vegetable garden at a primary school in Thohoyandou in Limpopo. The Grade 5 class waters rows of spinach, beans, tomatoes and maize every morning before school. One learner asks a question that stops the whole class. We eat food to grow, but nobody feeds these plants, so how do they get so big? To find out, the class plans an investigation with two bean plants, one in the sun and one in a dark cupboard. The big idea is that green plants are the only living things in a habitat that can make their own food, and that they use sunlight to do it. Explaining what plants need to make food, and what they give off, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Plants Are Food Factories

All living things need food. Food gives them energy to live and materials to grow. Animals get food by eating. Plants cannot eat, but they do something amazing: they make their own food. That is why we call green plants food factories.

A factory takes in raw materials and turns them into a product. A plant's raw materials are water and a gas called carbon dioxide. It uses energy from sunlight to turn them into food, a kind of sugar. The plant uses some of this sugar for energy straight away, and it turns the rest into starch, which it stores in its leaves, stems, roots, fruits and seeds.

This process is called photosynthesis. Photo means light, and synthesis means putting together. So photosynthesis means putting food together using light.

The plant makes its food mainly in its green leaves. Leaves are green because they contain a green substance called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll traps the energy from sunlight, the way a solar panel on a roof traps sunlight to make electricity.

Leaves are well suited to this job. Most are thin and flat, so they have a large surface to catch sunlight, and the gas they need can easily reach the inside. Plants arrange their leaves so that they do not shade each other too much.

Not every plant part is green. Roots, which grow in the dark soil, are usually white or brown and cannot make food. They get their food from the leaves, which send sugar down through the stem.

When we eat the leaves of spinach, the seeds of beans and maize, or the potato, which is a swollen underground stem, we are eating the food that the plant made and stored.

The questions for this section are with you now: why plants are called food factories, chlorophyll and the word photosynthesis.

## Subtopic: Water, Carbon Dioxide and Sunlight

A plant needs three things to make its food: water, carbon dioxide and sunlight.

Water comes from the soil. The plant's roots take up water, and it travels up through thin tubes in the stem to the leaves. That is why the class waters the garden every morning, especially in the hot, dry months before the summer rains.

Carbon dioxide comes from the air. It is a gas that we cannot see. People and animals breathe it out, and it is also given off when things burn. It enters the leaf through tiny holes, mostly on the underside of the leaf. Each hole is so small that you would need a microscope to see it.

Sunlight gives the energy. Without light, the food factory stops.

The class tests this. They take two young bean plants of the same size in the same kind of pot, with the same soil. They give them the same amount of water. They put one on a sunny windowsill and the other in a dark cupboard. Changing only one thing, the light, makes this a fair test. After two weeks, the plant in the sun is green and strong. The plant in the cupboard is tall, thin, weak and pale yellow, because without light it could not make enough food, and it stopped making its green chlorophyll.

The plant also needs minerals from the soil, such as those in compost and fertiliser, to stay healthy. But minerals are not food. They are a bit like the vitamins in our food. The plant's food is the sugar and starch it makes in its leaves.

The questions for this section are with you now: where a plant gets water, carbon dioxide and light, and the bean plant investigation.

## Subtopic: Plants Give Off Oxygen

When a plant makes food, it also makes oxygen. Oxygen is the gas that people and animals need to breathe. The plant uses some of it, and lets the rest out into the air through the tiny holes in its leaves.

You can see this happening in water plants. If you put a pondweed plant in a glass jar of water in bright sunlight, small bubbles rise from its leaves. Those bubbles are mostly oxygen. In the shade, far fewer bubbles appear.

Almost all of the oxygen in the air was made by green plants, including the tiny green living things that float in the sea. Every breath you take depends on them.

So plants and animals help each other. Animals breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide. Plants take in carbon dioxide and give off oxygen when they make food in the light.

We can sum up photosynthesis like this: water plus carbon dioxide, using sunlight, makes food and oxygen.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking plants get their food from the soil; soil gives water and minerals, but the plant makes its own food in its leaves. Two: thinking plants take in oxygen to make food; they take in carbon dioxide and give off oxygen. Three: thinking a plant in the dark grows healthy because it grows tall; it grows tall, thin and pale because it cannot make enough food. Four: thinking only leaves are food; many plant parts store food, such as roots, stems, fruits and seeds.

The questions for this section are with you now: oxygen from plants, and the summary of photosynthesis.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the school vegetable garden — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Making Their Own Food

Animals eat food. Green plants make their own food.

They make it in their green leaves. Chlorophyll makes leaves green and catches sunlight.

Keep the spinach leaves in mind and try the questions for this part: plants make food.

## Subtopic: Three Things Needed

Water from the soil, through the roots.

Carbon dioxide from the air, through tiny holes in the leaves.

Sunlight for energy.

Hold the two bean plants in mind and try this part's questions: what plants need.

## Subtopic: A Gift of Oxygen

Plants give off oxygen when they make food.

We breathe in that oxygen.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Green plants make their own food in their leaves. They use water from the soil, carbon dioxide from the air and energy from sunlight. Chlorophyll traps the sunlight. They make sugar, store it as starch, and give off oxygen, which people and animals breathe. This is called photosynthesis.

Hold the bubbles rising from the pondweed in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: oxygen from plants.
