### subtopic_1 — Why Animals Need Food
**Assistant:** How can such a big animal as an elephant grow so large by eating only plants?

**Tutor:** An elephant eats an enormous amount of plants. An adult can eat one hundred and fifty kilograms or more of grass, leaves, bark, roots and fruit in a day, about as much as two grown men weigh. It spends most of the day and part of the night feeding. Plant food has less energy in each mouthful than meat, and much of it, like tough grass and bark, is hard to digest. So herbivores have long, large stomachs and intestines, and many of them have tiny helpers, called microbes, living in their stomachs to break down the tough plant material. Elephants also have huge, flat grinding teeth that crush plants into small pieces. Being big also helps, because a large animal loses heat more slowly and can keep its food in its gut for longer. So the secret is not that plants are especially rich food. It is that elephants eat a great deal of them, all day long, and have bodies built to get the most out of them.

### subtopic_2 — Plants Feed Them All
**Assistant:** Is a vulture a carnivore if it does not hunt?

**Tutor:** Yes. A vulture eats meat, so it is a carnivore, but a special kind called a scavenger. A scavenger mostly eats animals that have already died, either from old age, sickness or after a lion or leopard has made a kill and eaten its fill. Vultures soar high in the sky for hours, using their very sharp eyesight to spot a carcass, and they also watch other vultures dropping down to feed. They have strong, hooked beaks to tear meat and tough skin, and many have bare heads and necks, which are easier to keep clean when feeding inside a carcass. Scavengers do a very important job. They clean up dead animals quickly, which helps stop diseases from spreading. Sadly, many vultures in South Africa are in danger because of poisoning and power lines, so people protect them at special feeding sites called vulture restaurants. Hyenas and jackals also scavenge, but they hunt as well.

### subtopic_3 — Herbivores, Carnivores and Omnivores
**Assistant:** If I don't eat meat, am I still an omnivore?

**Tutor:** This is a good question, because there are two ideas mixed together. Scientists call people omnivores because our bodies are able to eat and digest both plant food and animal food. Our teeth show it: we have sharp front teeth for biting and cutting, pointed teeth beside them, and flat back teeth for grinding. Our stomachs and intestines can digest both kinds of food. But each person can choose what to eat. Vegetarians choose not to eat meat, often for health, religious or animal-welfare reasons, and vegans also choose not to eat eggs, milk or any other animal products. Their bodies are still omnivore bodies, but their diet is made of plants. With careful planning, people can stay healthy eating only plants, by including beans, lentils, nuts, seeds and grains for building their bodies. So you are still an omnivore by your body, even if you choose not to eat meat.
