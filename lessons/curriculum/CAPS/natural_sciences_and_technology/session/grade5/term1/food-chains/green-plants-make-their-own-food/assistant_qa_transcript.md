### subtopic_1 — Plants Are Food Factories
**Assistant:** If plants make their own food, why do farmers still add fertiliser to the soil?

**Tutor:** Fertiliser is not food for the plant, even though people sometimes call it plant food. The plant makes its food, sugar and starch, in its leaves, from water, carbon dioxide and sunlight. But to stay healthy and build new leaves, stems and roots, it also needs small amounts of minerals from the soil, a bit like the way we need vitamins and minerals as well as food. Some of these minerals help make chlorophyll, and others help roots, flowers and fruits to grow. In wild places, minerals go back into the soil when leaves and dead animals rot. But on a farm or in a vegetable garden, we pick the crops and take them away, so the minerals they took from the soil are taken away too. Over time, the soil runs short of them. Farmers add fertiliser or compost to put the minerals back, so the plants can keep growing well. Without minerals, plants turn yellow and grow poorly, even in bright sunlight.

### subtopic_2 — Water, Carbon Dioxide and Sunlight
**Assistant:** Do plants breathe like we do?

**Tutor:** Yes, plants do breathe, in the sense that they use oxygen to release energy from their food, just like we do. This happens day and night, in every living part of the plant, including the roots. That is one reason roots need air spaces in the soil and can die if the soil stays waterlogged. Plants also make food by photosynthesis, but only when there is light. During the day, a healthy green plant makes much more oxygen through photosynthesis than it uses for breathing, so overall it gives oxygen out into the air. At night, there is no photosynthesis, so the plant only breathes, taking in a little oxygen and giving off a little carbon dioxide, just like an animal. But this is such a small amount that it is not dangerous to have plants in your bedroom. Over a whole day, green plants give off far more oxygen than they use.

### subtopic_3 — Plants Give Off Oxygen
**Assistant:** Why do leaves turn yellow or brown in autumn?

**Tutor:** Many trees, especially ones that come from cold countries, drop their leaves before winter. In winter, the days are short and cold, and in some places the water in the soil freezes, so the leaves cannot make much food. Keeping the leaves would waste water and energy. So the tree stops making green chlorophyll and takes some of the useful materials back into its branches. As the green chlorophyll breaks down, other colours that were hidden in the leaf all along, yellow and orange, start to show. In some trees, red colours are made too. Then the leaf dries out and falls. In spring, new green leaves grow, and photosynthesis starts again. Many of our indigenous trees, like the real yellowwood, are evergreen and keep their leaves all year. Others, like the marula and the baobab, drop their leaves in the dry season instead of the cold season, because they also need to save water.
