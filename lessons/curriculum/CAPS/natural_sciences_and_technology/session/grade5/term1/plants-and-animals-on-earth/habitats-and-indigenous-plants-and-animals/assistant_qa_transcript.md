### subtopic_1 — A Planet Full of Life
**Assistant:** Why does the Earth have so many different kinds of living things?

**Tutor:** It is because the Earth has so many different places to live in, and they are very different from each other. Some places are hot and dry, some are cold and icy, some are wet all the time, and some are dark, deep oceans. A plant or animal that is well suited to one place is often badly suited to another. Over a very long time, living things in each place have become suited to the conditions there. A meerkat is suited to digging in dry ground, and a dolphin is suited to swimming in the sea. Because there are so many different habitats, there are so many different ways of living, and so many different kinds of plants and animals. South Africa is special because it has many habitats packed into one country, from the fynbos and the Karoo to the bushveld, grassland, forests, wetlands and two oceans. That is why we have so much biodiversity.

### subtopic_2 — Indigenous Plants and Animals
**Assistant:** Is a jacaranda tree bad for the environment if it is not indigenous?

**Tutor:** Not every alien plant is a big problem, but some are. A jacaranda tree comes from South America. People planted thousands of them in the streets of Pretoria and Johannesburg because their purple flowers are so beautiful. Jacarandas can spread along rivers and streams, so the law no longer allows new ones to be planted, but the old street trees may stay. Plants like black wattle, pine and prickly pear are a bigger problem. They spread very fast, crowd out our indigenous plants, and the trees use lots of water that would otherwise flow into our rivers and dams. Indigenous animals often cannot use them for food or shelter in the same way. So scientists sort alien plants into groups. The worst invaders are removed, and others are controlled so that they do not spread. A good choice for your garden is an indigenous plant, because it suits our weather and feeds our birds and insects.

### subtopic_3 — South Africa's Habitats
**Assistant:** How can plants live in the Karoo if it hardly ever rains there?

**Tutor:** Karoo plants have clever ways of saving water. Many of them are succulents, which means they have thick, juicy leaves or stems that store water, like a water bottle. When a little rain falls, they soak it up quickly and keep it for the dry months. Their leaves are often small, thick or covered in wax or hairs, so less water escapes into the dry air. Some have roots spread wide just under the surface to catch every drop of rain, and others have long roots that go deep. The quiver tree stores water in its thick trunk and branches, and its branches are covered in a white powder that reflects some of the hot sunlight. Some plants rest during the hottest, driest time and only grow and flower after rain. That is why, after good rains, parts of the Karoo and Namaqualand suddenly burst into carpets of colourful flowers. The plants were alive all along, just waiting for water.
