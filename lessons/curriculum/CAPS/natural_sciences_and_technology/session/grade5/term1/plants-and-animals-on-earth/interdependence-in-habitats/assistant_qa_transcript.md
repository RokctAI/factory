### subtopic_1 — Animals Depend on Plants
**Assistant:** Do all animals depend on plants, even ones that live deep in the sea?

**Tutor:** Almost all of them do, even if they never touch a plant. In the sea, the plants are mostly tiny green living things that float near the surface, where there is sunlight. They are so small that you need a microscope to see one, but there are billions of them. They make food using sunlight, just like the marula tree, and they give off a lot of the oxygen in the air. Tiny sea animals eat them, small fish eat those animals, bigger fish eat the small fish, and so on, all the way to sharks and seals. Dead plants and animals slowly sink down into the deep sea and feed the animals living there in the dark. So a fish swimming deep down still depends on plants growing in the sunlight far above it. There are a few very unusual animals that live near hot springs on the deep sea floor and get their food in another way, but nearly every animal on Earth depends on plants.

### subtopic_2 — Plants Depend on Animals
**Assistant:** Why do plants make sweet nectar for bees and birds?

**Tutor:** Nectar is a reward, a bit like paying someone for a job. A plant cannot walk over to another plant to share its pollen, so it needs a helper to carry it. The plant makes sweet nectar deep inside its flower. A bee or a sunbird pushes its head or tongue into the flower to reach the nectar, and pollen brushes onto its body. When it flies to another flower of the same kind to get more nectar, some of the pollen rubs off there. That is pollination, and it lets the flower make seeds. The animal gets food, and the plant gets its pollen moved. Both of them win. The bright colours and sweet smells of flowers are like signs that say, nectar here. Proteas often have large, sturdy flower heads where sugarbirds can perch while they feed. Without their pollinators, many plants could not make seeds, and we would have far fewer fruits and vegetables.

### subtopic_3 — Everything Needs the Habitat
**Assistant:** If plants and animals depend on each other, what is the most important thing in a habitat?

**Tutor:** That is a great question, and the honest answer is that everything matters, because it is all linked. But if we had to start somewhere, we would start with the non-living things: sunlight, water, soil and air. Without sunlight, plants cannot make food. Without water, nothing can stay alive. Without soil, most land plants have nowhere to grow. Next come the plants, because they are the only living things that can make their own food from sunlight, so every food chain starts with them. Then come the animals that depend on the plants and help them in return. Think of a habitat like a soccer team. The goalkeeper, the defenders and the strikers each have a different job, and if one player leaves the field, the whole team struggles. That is why we protect whole habitats, not just one favourite animal. When we save the habitat, we save all the living things that depend on it.
