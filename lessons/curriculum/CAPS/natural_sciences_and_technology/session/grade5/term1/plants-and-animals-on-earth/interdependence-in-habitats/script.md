# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers interdependence: how plants and animals in a habitat depend on each other, and how they all depend on the non-living resources of the habitat, such as water, soil, air and sunlight. The anchor is a single large marula tree on a farm near Mbombela, close to the Kruger National Park. A Grade 5 class sits in its shade with notebooks and a pair of binoculars. For one morning they record every living thing that uses the tree, and every way the tree uses something around it. By lunchtime their page is full of arrows. The big idea is that no plant or animal lives alone: each one needs other living things and the resources of its habitat to survive. Explaining how plants and animals depend on each other and on their habitat is examinable.

## Subtopic: Animals Depend on Plants

The first thing the class notices is how many animals use the marula tree.

Animals depend on plants for food. Elephants break off branches and eat the bark and leaves. In late summer, the tree drops hundreds of yellow fruits, and impala, warthogs, monkeys and baboons gather underneath to eat them. Caterpillars chew the leaves, and bees and other insects drink nectar from the flowers. Even animals that never eat plants depend on them, because the animals they eat feed on plants.

Animals depend on plants for shelter. A pair of weaver birds has woven nests at the ends of the thin branches, where snakes find it hard to reach. A hornbill nests in a hole in the trunk. Lizards hide in cracks in the bark, and a leopard could rest on a thick branch in the shade during the hottest part of the day.

Animals also depend on plants for the oxygen they breathe. When green plants make their food in sunlight, they give off oxygen into the air. Animals, including people, breathe in this oxygen.

Even dead plants are useful to animals. A fallen, rotting branch from the marula tree becomes a home for termites, beetle larvae and fungi, and a scorpion may hide underneath it.

People depend on plants as well. We eat plant foods, such as maize, wheat, beans and fruit, and we use plants for wood, medicines, cotton cloth and paper. In many parts of Limpopo and Mpumalanga, families collect marula fruit to make jam and drinks.

The questions for this section are with you now: how animals and people depend on plants for food, shelter and oxygen.

## Subtopic: Plants Depend on Animals

The class then turns the question around. How does the marula tree depend on animals?

Many plants need animals to help them make seeds. This is called pollination. When a bee visits a marula flower to drink nectar, a yellow dust called pollen sticks to its hairy body. When it flies to the next flower, some of that pollen rubs off. Only then can the flower grow into a fruit with seeds inside. In the fynbos, sugarbirds and sunbirds pollinate proteas in the same way, and in the Karoo many flowers are pollinated by flies, beetles and bees.

Plants also need animals to spread their seeds. An elephant swallows marula fruits whole and walks many kilometres before it drops the hard seeds in its dung, far away from the parent tree. That gives the young plant space and light to grow. Birds eat berries and drop the seeds in new places. In the fynbos, ants carry some seeds underground into their nests, where the seeds are safe from fire and mice.

Plants also depend on animals for food in the soil. Dung beetles roll balls of dung and bury them. Earthworms, termites and beetles break down dead leaves and dung into tiny pieces. This adds nutrients to the soil that plant roots can take up. Animals also breathe out carbon dioxide, a gas that plants need to make food.

Some animals even protect plants. Birds and lizards eat many of the caterpillars and beetles that would otherwise chew the leaves of the tree. Without them, insects could strip a tree bare.

The questions for this section are with you now: pollination, seed spreading, and how animals keep the soil rich for plants.

## Subtopic: Everything Needs the Habitat

Plants and animals do not only depend on each other. They also depend on the non-living resources of their habitat.

All plants and animals need water. The marula tree takes up water from the soil through its roots. The elephants and impala drink from a nearby river or waterhole.

Plants need soil. Soil holds their roots firmly in place and gives them water and nutrients.

Plants and animals need air. Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air to make food, and plants and animals both take in oxygen to stay alive.

Plants need sunlight to make their food. Animals depend on that food, so sunlight is the first source of energy for almost every habitat.

When one part of a habitat changes, many living things are affected. If a drought dries up the river, the grass dies, the antelope go hungry or move away, and the lions that hunt them go hungry too. If people cut down all the marula trees, the weavers lose their nesting place, the baboons lose their fruit and the bees lose their nectar. When we look after a habitat, we look after all the living things that depend on each other within it. This web of links is called interdependence.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking only animals depend on plants; plants depend on animals too, for pollination, seed spreading and rich soil. Two: thinking an animal that eats only meat does not need plants; the animals it eats need plants. Three: thinking soil is just dirt; it holds water, air and nutrients that plants need. Four: thinking one change only affects one animal; a change in a habitat affects many living things.

The questions for this section are with you now: how living things depend on water, soil, air and sunlight, and what happens when a habitat changes.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the marula tree — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Tree Helps the Animals

The tree gives food: leaves, fruit and nectar.

The tree gives shelter: nests in the branches, holes in the trunk.

Green plants give off oxygen that animals breathe.

Keep the busy marula tree in mind and try the questions for this part: how animals depend on plants.

## Subtopic: The Animals Help the Tree

Bees and birds carry pollen from flower to flower. This helps make seeds.

Elephants and birds spread seeds far away in their dung.

Dung beetles and earthworms make the soil rich.

Hold the bee and the elephant in mind and try this part's questions: how plants depend on animals.

## Subtopic: All Need the Habitat

Everything needs water and air.

Plants need soil and sunlight.

Change one part, and many living things are affected.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Plants and animals in a habitat depend on each other. Animals need plants for food, shelter and oxygen. Plants need animals for pollination, spreading seeds and rich soil. All living things depend on the water, soil, air and sunlight of their habitat. This is called interdependence.

Hold the whole marula tree in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: depending on the habitat.
