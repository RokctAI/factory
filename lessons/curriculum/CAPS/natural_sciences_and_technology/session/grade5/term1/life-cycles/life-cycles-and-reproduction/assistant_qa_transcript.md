### subtopic_1 — What Is a Life Cycle?
**Assistant:** Why do people eat mopane worms, and is it bad for the moths?

**Tutor:** Mopane worms are a traditional food in Limpopo, Zimbabwe, Botswana and other parts of southern Africa. They are very nutritious, full of protein, which our bodies use to grow and repair, and many people enjoy them fried, cooked in a stew or dried as a snack. Collecting and selling them also earns money for many families. Eating them is not bad in itself. The problem comes when too many are collected. If every caterpillar is taken from an area, none can become a pupa, so no moths come out to lay eggs, and the next year there may be no worms at all. Droughts and the cutting down of mopane trees also make the problem worse. That is why many communities have rules about when worms may be collected and how many must be left behind. Leaving some caterpillars on the trees keeps the life cycle turning, so there will be mopane worms for the next generation of people too.

### subtopic_2 — The Life Cycle of a Plant
**Assistant:** How can a caterpillar turn into a moth inside a pupa?

**Tutor:** It is one of the most amazing changes in nature. Before the caterpillar becomes a pupa, it has already been storing up energy by eating huge amounts of leaves. When it is fully grown, it burrows into the soil and sheds its skin one last time, and underneath is the hard, brown case of the pupa. From the outside, the pupa looks still and lifeless. But inside, much of the caterpillar's body is broken down, and its cells are rebuilt into the body of a moth. Small groups of cells that were waiting inside the caterpillar grow into wings, long legs, feelers and a new head. The chewing mouth of the caterpillar is replaced by the mouthparts of a moth. Mopane emperor moths can stay as pupae in the soil through the whole dry winter. When the summer rains soften the ground, the adult moth breaks out and pushes its way to the surface, pumps up its wings and flies off.

### subtopic_3 — Animal Life Cycles
**Assistant:** Do plants that live for many years still have a life cycle?

**Tutor:** Yes, every plant has a life cycle, but the cycle can be short or very long. Maize, beans and many garden flowers complete their whole life cycle in one growing season. They grow from seed, flower, make seeds and die, all in a few months. Their seeds wait for the next season to start the cycle again. Other plants, like trees and many shrubs, live for many years. A mopane tree or a marula tree grows for several years before it flowers for the first time. After that, it flowers and makes seeds again and again, year after year, for a long time. So the tree goes around the reproducing part of its cycle many times before it dies. Its seeds grow into new trees, the next generation. Some plants, like proteas, need fire to complete their cycle: many protea seeds are only released and germinate well after a fire has passed through the fynbos.
