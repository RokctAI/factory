# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers two parts of every life cycle: that death can happen at any stage, and that many animals care for their young to help them survive. The anchor is a summer night walk on a beach in iSimangaliso Wetland Park, in the far north of KwaZulu-Natal, near Kosi Bay. A Grade 5 class from Manguzi joins the rangers with red torches, so the turtles are not disturbed. They watch a huge leatherback turtle drag herself up the sand, dig a deep hole with her back flippers, lay about a hundred eggs and cover them before slipping back into the sea. The ranger tells them that the mother will never see her babies. When the eggs hatch about two months later, the hatchlings must find their own way to the sea. The big idea is that living things can die at any stage of their life cycle, and that some animals produce many young and give no care, while others produce few young and look after them carefully. Explaining why death can occur at any stage, and how animals care for their young, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Death Can Happen at Any Stage

All living things die in the end. Death is part of every life cycle. But not every living thing reaches old age. Death can happen at any stage.

Think of the turtle eggs. Some eggs never hatch, because they are dug up by honey badgers or jackals, or washed away by very high waves. The hatchlings that do come out must scramble across the sand to the sea, often at night, while ghost crabs and birds try to catch them. In the sea, fish and sea birds eat many of them. Only a tiny number, maybe one or two out of every thousand hatchlings, survive to become adults.

Plants die at different stages too. Many seeds are eaten by birds, mice and insects before they can germinate. Seedlings can dry out, be trampled or be eaten by animals. A full-grown tree can be killed by drought, fire, disease, lightning or being cut down.

Living things die for many reasons. They may be eaten by a predator, which is an animal that hunts and eats other animals. They may get sick, not find enough food or water, or be killed by very cold, very hot or very dry weather. People also cause many deaths, by destroying habitats, hunting, polluting and leaving plastic in the sea. Turtles can mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish, their food, and die from eating them.

Even when a living thing reaches old age, its body slowly wears out until it dies.

The questions for this section are with you now: why death can occur at any stage of a life cycle, and the dangers that young plants and animals face.

## Subtopic: Many Young, No Care

Some animals produce huge numbers of young and give them no care at all.

A sea turtle lays about a hundred eggs at a time, and several nests in one season, but leaves them buried in the sand. Many fish release thousands or even millions of tiny eggs into the water and swim away. A frog may lay hundreds or thousands of eggs in a pond. Many insects lay hundreds of eggs and never see their young.

The young must look after themselves from the start. Many of them die, because they are small, they cannot defend themselves and nobody protects them. But because so many young are produced, a few survive to become adults and reproduce. That is enough to keep the life cycle going.

The class works it out with a simple sum. If a frog lays one thousand eggs and only two survive to become adult frogs, the number of frogs in the pond stays about the same. The many young are like buying lots of tickets in a draw: most lose, but a few win.

Plants work in a similar way. A single marula tree can drop thousands of fruits, and a fynbos bush thousands of seeds. Most never grow into adult plants, but a few do.

The questions for this section are with you now: animals that produce many young and give no care, and why so many young are needed.

## Subtopic: Few Young, Lots of Care

Other animals produce only a few young at a time, but care for them, so that more of them survive.

Birds sit on their eggs to keep them warm, and many feed their chicks until they can fly. The African penguins at Boulders Beach in Simon's Town lay two eggs, and both parents take turns to keep them warm and to bring food from the sea.

Most mammals carry their babies inside their bodies until they are born, and then feed them on milk. An elephant mother carries her calf for almost two years, the longest of any land animal. The whole herd of females helps protect the calf, and it stays close to its mother for many years.

Some reptiles care for their young too. A Nile crocodile mother guards her nest on a river bank, and when the babies call from inside their eggs, she digs them out and gently carries them in her mouth to the water. She guards them for weeks afterwards.

People give the most care of all. Human babies are helpless for a long time, and children are cared for by their families for many years, learning to walk, talk, read and look after themselves.

Caring for young includes feeding them, keeping them warm, protecting them from predators and bad weather, keeping them clean, and teaching them how to find food and stay safe.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking all young animals are cared for by their parents; turtles, most fish and many frogs give no care. Two: thinking death happens only in old age; it can happen at any stage. Three: thinking the turtle laid too many eggs; most will not survive, so many are needed. Four: thinking only mammals care for their young; birds and some reptiles, like crocodiles, care for them too.

The questions for this section are with you now: animals that produce few young and care for them, and the ways they give care.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the turtle beach — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: At Any Time

All living things die in the end.

But death can happen at any stage: egg, young or adult.

Predators, sickness, hunger, weather and people can cause death.

Keep the hatchlings racing to the sea in mind and try the questions for this part: death at any stage.

## Subtopic: Lots of Eggs

Turtles, fish and frogs lay many eggs and leave them.

Most die, but a few survive.

Hold the turtle nest in mind and try this part's questions: many young, no care.

## Subtopic: Looking After Babies

Birds, mammals and crocodiles care for their young.

They feed, warm, protect and teach them.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Death can happen at any stage of a life cycle, from seed or egg to old age. Some animals, like turtles, fish and frogs, produce many young and give no care, so most die but a few survive. Others, like birds, mammals and crocodiles, produce fewer young and care for them, so more survive. People care for their young for the longest time.

Hold the crocodile mother carrying her babies in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: caring for young.
