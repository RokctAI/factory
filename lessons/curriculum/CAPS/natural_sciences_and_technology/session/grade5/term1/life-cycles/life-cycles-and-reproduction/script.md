# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers life cycles: the stages a plant or animal goes through from the start of its life until it is an adult that can reproduce, and how reproduction links one generation to the next. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Giyani in Limpopo, where mopane trees grow everywhere. In summer, many families collect mopane worms, the fat, spotted caterpillars that feed on mopane leaves, and cook or dry them. The class decides to find out where the worms come from and where they go. They also keep a jar of frogspawn from the school pond, and they plant maize seeds in the school garden. The big idea is that every living thing has a life cycle: it is born or starts as a seed, grows and develops, reproduces to make young like itself, and in time dies, while its young carry the cycle on. Describing the stages of plant and animal life cycles is examinable.

## Subtopic: What Is a Life Cycle?

In the last lesson, we saw that living things go through stages as they grow and develop. A life cycle joins these stages into a circle.

It is called a cycle because it goes round and round, like the wheels of a bicycle. The adult reproduces, which means it makes new young of its own kind. Those young grow and develop into adults, and then they reproduce too. So the last stage leads back to the first.

Each turn of the cycle is a new generation. You belong to one generation, your parents to the generation before, and your grandparents to the generation before that.

Reproduction is one of the seven life processes. It is the only process that a living thing does not need in order to stay alive itself, but without it, its kind would die out.

Plants and animals reproduce in different ways. Most plants make seeds. Most animals either lay eggs, like birds, frogs, fish, insects and most reptiles, or give birth to live young, like most mammals.

A life cycle is usually drawn as a circle of pictures joined by arrows, with the arrows going round in one direction. The last stage, the adult, has an arrow back to the first stage, the seed or the egg. This shows that the cycle continues.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a life cycle is, what reproduction means, and what a generation is.

## Subtopic: The Life Cycle of a Plant

The maize in the school garden shows a plant's life cycle.

It starts as a seed. A maize seed is a kernel, like the ones on a mealie cob. It holds a tiny baby plant and a store of food.

When it is planted in warm, damp soil, the seed germinates. A root grows down and a shoot grows up.

The shoot becomes a seedling with a few green leaves, which makes its own food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide.

The seedling grows into an adult plant, taller than most learners in the class.

The adult plant makes flowers. Maize has a tassel at the top, which makes pollen, and silky threads on the young cobs lower down. The wind blows pollen from the tassels onto the silk. This is pollination. Many other plants are pollinated by insects or birds instead.

After pollination, the cob grows new seeds, the kernels. When they are ripe and dry, they can be planted, and the cycle starts again. Seed, germination, seedling, adult plant, flowers, pollination, new seeds.

Many plants spread their seeds before they die. Some, like maize, die after one season. Others, like the mopane tree, live for many years and make seeds again and again.

The questions for this section are with you now: the stages of a plant's life cycle, from seed to new seeds.

## Subtopic: Animal Life Cycles

Now for the mopane worm. The class discovers that it is not really a worm, but the caterpillar of a large moth called the mopane emperor moth.

Its life cycle has four stages. First, the adult female moth lays a cluster of eggs on a mopane tree. The eggs hatch into tiny caterpillars, which eat mopane leaves and grow fast. They shed their skin several times as they grow. That is the stage that people collect. When a caterpillar is fully grown, it climbs down and burrows into the soil, where it becomes a pupa. Inside the pupa, its body changes completely. Months later, after the rains come, an adult moth with large wings climbs out of the soil. The adult moths mate, the females lay eggs, and the cycle begins again. Egg, caterpillar, pupa, adult. This is metamorphosis.

That is why it matters not to collect every single worm. Some must be left to become moths, or there will be no eggs for next season.

The frogspawn in the jar shows another life cycle. Eggs hatch into tadpoles, which breathe with gills and swim with tails. Back legs appear, then front legs, the tail shrinks and the lungs develop. The young froglet climbs out of the water, grows into an adult frog and later lays eggs in water.

A chicken's life cycle has three stages: egg, chick and adult hen or rooster. A mammal, like a goat, gives birth to live young, called kids, which drink milk, grow up and have kids of their own.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a mopane worm is a worm; it is the caterpillar of a moth. Two: thinking the pupa is dead because it does not move; huge changes are happening inside. Three: drawing a life cycle as a straight line; the adult links back to the first stage, so it is a circle. Four: thinking all animals lay eggs; most mammals give birth to live young.

The questions for this section are with you now: the life cycles of the mopane moth, the frog, the chicken and mammals.

## Subtopic: Round and Round

A life cycle goes round in a circle.

Adults reproduce. The young grow up and reproduce too.

Each turn is a new generation.

Mopane moth: egg, caterpillar, pupa, moth.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A life cycle shows the stages of a living thing's life, from seed or egg to adult, and how the adult reproduces to start a new generation.
