### subtopic_1 — Growing Bigger
**Assistant:** Why do I stop getting taller but trees keep growing?

**Tutor:** People and trees grow in different ways. In children, the long bones in the arms and legs have special growing areas near their ends, made of soft cartilage. New bone is added there, so the bones get longer and you get taller. When you finish growing, usually in your late teens, these growing areas turn into hard bone, and the bones can no longer get longer. That is why adults stop getting taller. Your body still makes new cells to replace old ones and to heal cuts, but you do not keep growing bigger. Trees are different. They have growing tips at the ends of every branch and root, which keep making new cells all their lives. They also have a thin growing layer just under the bark, which adds a new ring of wood each year, making the trunk thicker. That is why old trees can be enormous, and why counting the rings in a tree stump tells you roughly how old the tree was.

### subtopic_2 — Changing as We Develop
**Assistant:** What exactly happens when a seed germinates?

**Tutor:** A dry seed is resting. Inside it is a tiny baby plant and a store of food, wrapped in a tough seed coat. When the seed is planted in damp soil and it is warm enough, it soaks up water and swells. You can see this if you soak a dry bean overnight: it becomes fat and soft, and its skin wrinkles. The water wakes up the baby plant, and it starts to use its stored food for energy. First, the root breaks out through the seed coat and grows downwards, pulled by gravity, to anchor the seed and take up water. Then the shoot grows upwards, towards the surface, often bent over like a hook to protect its tip as it pushes through the soil. When it reaches the light, it straightens up, the first leaves open and turn green, and the young plant starts to make its own food. Seeds need water, warmth and air to germinate, but not light, because they start under the soil.

### subtopic_3 — Stages of Life
**Assistant:** Why do some animals change shape completely, like frogs and butterflies?

**Tutor:** Scientists think it helps the young and the adults to avoid competing with each other for food and space. A caterpillar is basically an eating machine. It crawls over leaves and chews, chews, chews, growing quickly and storing energy. A butterfly has a completely different life. It flies from flower to flower, drinking nectar through a long tube, finding a mate and laying eggs. Because the caterpillar and the butterfly eat different foods, they do not have to share. The same is true for frogs. A tadpole lives in water, breathes with gills and mostly eats algae and tiny bits of plants. An adult frog lives partly on land, breathes air and eats insects. Metamorphosis lets one animal use two different habitats and two different foods at different stages of its life. Many insects, such as beetles, flies, bees and moths, change completely like this, and so do most frogs and toads.
