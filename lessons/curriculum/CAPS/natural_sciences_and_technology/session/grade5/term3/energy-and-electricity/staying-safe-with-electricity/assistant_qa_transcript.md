### subtopic_1 — Why Mains Electricity Is Dangerous
**Assistant:** Why can birds sit on power lines without getting a shock?

**Tutor:** Electricity only flows through something if it has a path to go from a place with a strong push to a place with a weaker push, such as from a live wire down to the ground, or to another wire. When a bird sits on one power line with both feet on the same wire, its whole body is at the same push as the wire. There is no path for the electricity to flow through the bird to anywhere else, so it stays in the wire, which is a much easier path. That is why the bird is safe. But if a large bird, like an eagle, spreads its wings and touches two wires at once, or touches a wire and the metal pole at the same time, electricity flows through its body, and it is killed. Sadly, this happens to many large birds in South Africa, so electricity companies put special covers and perches on poles to protect them. A person on the ground who touches a line, or something touching it, makes a path to the ground, which is why it is so dangerous for people.

### subtopic_2 — Safe Habits Indoors
**Assistant:** Why is it dangerous to poke a fork into a toaster even when the switch is off?

**Tutor:** When you push the lever down on a toaster, it closes a circuit, and electricity flows through thin, bare metal wires inside, called elements, which get red hot and toast the bread. When the toaster pops up, the circuit is opened, and the elements cool down. But as long as the toaster is plugged in and the wall switch is on, some parts inside can still be connected to the mains. The elements are bare metal with no plastic covering, so if a metal fork touches the wrong part, electricity can flow through the fork, through your hand, and through your body to the ground. Metal is an excellent conductor, so this is a perfect path for a shock. The safe way is to switch off at the wall, pull out the plug, wait for the toaster to cool, and then gently lift the bread out with wooden tongs. The same rule applies to any appliance: always unplug it before you put your hand or anything else inside it.

### subtopic_3 — Safe Habits Outdoors
**Assistant:** What should we do if there is a thunderstorm?

**Tutor:** Lightning is a giant spark of natural electricity jumping between a cloud and the ground. It is far more powerful than mains electricity. South Africa, especially the highveld and KwaZulu-Natal, has a lot of thunderstorms in summer, and many people are struck by lightning each year. If you hear thunder, lightning is close enough to strike you, so go indoors straight away, into a solid building or a closed car. Do not shelter under a tree, because lightning often strikes tall objects, and the electricity can jump to you. Stay away from water, so get out of swimming pools, rivers and dams. Stay off open fields and hilltops, and away from metal fences and poles. Indoors, stay away from windows, do not bath or shower during the storm, and unplug appliances like the television. If you are caught in the open with nowhere to go, crouch down low with your feet together, but do not lie flat. Wait about half an hour after the last thunder before going outside again.
