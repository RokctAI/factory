### subtopic_1 — What Is a Fuel?
**Assistant:** Why does South Africa use so much coal?

**Tutor:** South Africa has some of the largest coal deposits in the world, mostly in Mpumalanga, Limpopo and parts of KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State. Coal is close to the surface in many places, so it has been fairly cheap to mine. For about a hundred years, the country built its power stations near the coal mines, especially around eMalahleni, so that coal could be carried straight from the mine to the power station on conveyor belts. Today, most of our electricity still comes from burning coal. Coal is also used in factories, for making steel, and at Secunda to make fuels and chemicals. But burning coal has big problems. It pollutes the air, which can make people sick, and it releases a lot of carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change. Mining can also damage land and water. That is why South Africa is building more solar and wind power, which use energy from the Sun and wind directly and do not run out.

### subtopic_2 — Food Is a Fuel
**Assistant:** If food is a fuel, why don't we burst into flames when we eat?

**Tutor:** Our bodies release the energy from food in a very slow, careful way, not all at once like a fire. When a peanut burns in a flame, its stored energy is released very quickly, as heat and light, because it reacts fast with oxygen in the air. Inside your body, food is first broken down in your stomach and intestines into tiny pieces, like sugar, which travel in your blood to every cell. In each cell, the sugar reacts with oxygen from the air you breathe, but in many small steps, each one controlled by special helpers in the cell. Each step releases a small amount of energy, which the cell can use or store. Some of it is released as gentle heat, which keeps your body at about thirty-seven degrees Celsius. So it is the same kind of change as burning, using oxygen and releasing energy, but slowed down and controlled so that it is safe and useful. That is why breathing faster during exercise helps: your muscles need more oxygen to release more energy.

### subtopic_3 — Where the Energy Came From
**Assistant:** Is electricity a fuel?

**Tutor:** Not exactly. A fuel is a material that has energy stored in it, like coal, wood or petrol. You can hold it, store it and carry it. Electricity is not a material. It is a way of moving energy from one place to another, along wires. Think of it like a delivery service. At a power station, coal, a fuel, is burnt, and the energy released is turned into electricity. The electricity carries that energy along wires to your home, where it is changed into light in a bulb, heat in a kettle or movement in a fan. Electricity can be made in other ways too, from the energy in sunlight with solar panels, from wind with wind turbines, or from flowing water in dams. A battery is a little different again: it stores energy in chemicals, and releases it as electricity when it is connected in a circuit. So electricity is very useful, but it is better to call it a carrier of energy than a fuel. We will learn more about this in the next topic.
