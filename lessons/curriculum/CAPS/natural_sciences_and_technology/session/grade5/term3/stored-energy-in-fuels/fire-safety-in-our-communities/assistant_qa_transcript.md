### subtopic_1 — How Fires Start
**Assistant:** Why do firefighters say to crawl under smoke?

**Tutor:** In a house fire, smoke is often more dangerous than the flames. Smoke is hot, and it is full of tiny bits of soot and poisonous gases, such as carbon monoxide. Breathing in just a few breaths of thick smoke can make a person confused, dizzy or unconscious, so they cannot find their way out. Hot smoke and gases rise, just like hot air, so they collect near the ceiling first and slowly fill the room downwards. That means the coolest, cleanest air, and the best chance of seeing, is close to the floor. By getting down on your hands and knees and crawling, you keep your nose and mouth in that cleaner air for longer, and you can often see the way out more easily. If you can, cover your nose and mouth with a cloth. Practise your escape route with your family, so that you know it even in the dark: which doors and windows to use, and where to meet outside.

### subtopic_2 — Preventing Fires
**Assistant:** Why is paraffin in a cooldrink bottle so dangerous?

**Tutor:** Paraffin is clear and runny, like water or some cooldrinks. When it is stored in an old cooldrink bottle, a thirsty toddler can easily think it is something to drink. Small children cannot read labels, and they put things in their mouths to explore them. When a child swallows paraffin, it can get into the lungs and cause a serious lung infection, which can be deadly. Every year, thousands of children in South Africa are poisoned by paraffin, and many more are burnt in paraffin fires. The safe way to store paraffin is in a strong container that is clearly marked, with a child-proof lid that small children cannot open, kept high up and out of reach. If a child swallows paraffin, do not make them vomit, because that can bring the paraffin back up into the lungs. Get them to a clinic or hospital straight away, and you can call the poison information helpline for advice.

### subtopic_3 — If There Is a Fire
**Assistant:** Why should you not put butter or toothpaste on a burn?

**Tutor:** When skin is burnt, the heat keeps damaging the deeper layers of skin even after the flame or hot water is gone. The most important thing is to cool the burn down fast. Cool, gently running water does this very well, and keeping it under the water for about twenty minutes stops the burn from getting deeper and reduces pain and scarring. Butter, oil and toothpaste do the opposite. They form a layer that holds the heat in, so the burn keeps cooking the skin underneath. They can also contain germs that cause infection, and they have to be scraped off later by a nurse, which hurts. Ice is also not a good idea, because it can damage the skin with cold. After cooling, cover the burn loosely with clean plastic cling wrap or a clean cloth, and get medical help for any burn bigger than a coin, or on the face, hands or private parts. Tell an adult straight away about any burn.
