### subtopic_1 — Let It Go
**Assistant:** Is bungee jumping safe if the cord stretches so much?

**Tutor:** Bungee jumping is carefully controlled, and that is what makes it safe for adults. The cord is made of many strands of strong rubber inside a tough woven cover. The staff weigh every jumper before they jump, and they choose the right cord and length for that person's weight. A heavier person stretches the cord more, so they need a stiffer or shorter cord, so that they do not fall too far. The cord is tested regularly and replaced after a set number of jumps, before the rubber can wear out. The jumper wears a harness and ankle straps, and there are safety checks before every jump. The cord is designed to store the jumper's movement energy gradually, slowing them down smoothly, rather than stopping them suddenly, which could hurt. Even so, there are age and health rules, and children are not allowed to bungee jump. It is a good example of engineers using what they know about stored energy to keep people safe.

### subtopic_2 — Toys and Tools That Use Springs and Elastic
**Assistant:** Why does a cotton-reel tractor go further with tyres made of rubber bands?

**Tutor:** When the twisted elastic band untwists, it tries to turn the cotton reel. For the reel to roll forwards, its edges must grip the floor. If the reel is smooth plastic or wood, and the floor is smooth, the reel can slip and spin on the spot, like a car wheel spinning in mud. Then much of the stored energy is wasted as heat from rubbing, and as noise, instead of moving the tractor forward. Rubber bands stretched around the edges of the reel act like tyres. Rubber grips the floor much better, because there is more friction between rubber and the floor. Friction is the force that stops things from sliding. With better grip, more of the stored energy is turned into useful forward movement, so the tractor travels further and in a straighter line. That is why real tractors have big rubber tyres with deep patterns, called treads, to grip muddy fields.

### subtopic_3 — The Cotton-Reel Tractor
**Assistant:** Could we use springs to power a car instead of petrol?

**Tutor:** Springs can power small things like toy cars, clocks and music boxes very well. But a real car needs a huge amount of energy to carry heavy people and luggage for long distances. A spring or elastic band can only store a fairly small amount of energy for its size. A spring big enough to drive a real car for even a few kilometres would be enormously heavy, and it would take a very long time and a lot of effort to wind up. Petrol stores far more energy in a small tank, and batteries in electric cars store much more energy than springs too, and can be recharged by plugging in. However, engineers do use stored energy in some clever ways in vehicles. Some buses and racing cars store energy when they brake, in spinning wheels called flywheels or in batteries, and use it to speed up again. And every car has springs in its suspension, which store and release energy every time the wheels go over a bump.
