# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers what happens when the energy stored in stretched or twisted elastic, or in a compressed spring, is released: it is changed into movement energy. The anchor is a school outing by a Grade 5 class from Plettenberg Bay to the Bloukrans Bridge on the Garden Route, one of the highest bridge bungee jumps in the world. From the viewing deck, they watch a jumper dive off the bridge with a thick, stretchy cord tied to her ankles. She falls, the cord stretches longer and longer, she slows down, stops, and then shoots back up into the air, bouncing a few times before she hangs still. Back at school, the learners make cotton-reel tractors that run on twisted elastic bands, and they race them across the classroom floor. The big idea is that stored energy in elastic and springs can be released to make things move. Explaining how releasing stretched elastic or compressed springs gives movement energy is examinable.

## Subtopic: Let It Go

When you hold a stretched elastic band, it has stored energy, but nothing moves. The moment you let go, the band snaps back to its normal shape, and it moves. Its stored energy has been released and changed into movement energy.

Movement energy is the energy of anything that is moving: a rolling ball, a running learner, a flying elastic band, a spinning wheel. The faster something moves, the more movement energy it has.

The same happens with springs. A compressed spring in a jack-in-the-box is held down by the closed lid. When you open the lid, the spring is released, and it pushes the clown up and out. A clicky pen spring pushes the pen point back inside. A stapler spring lifts the top back up after you press it.

The bungee cord shows energy going back and forth. As the jumper falls, she has more and more movement energy. When the cord starts to stretch, it slows her down, and her movement energy is changed into stored energy in the stretched cord. At the very bottom, she stops for an instant, and the cord is stretched the most. Then the cord pulls back, its stored energy is released, and it is changed back into movement energy, shooting her upwards.

Each bounce is a bit lower than the last, because some energy is changed into heat in the cord and into sound, until she hangs still.

The questions for this section are with you now: releasing stored energy, movement energy, and how a bungee cord stores and releases energy.

## Subtopic: Toys and Tools That Use Springs and Elastic

People have used stored energy in elastic materials for thousands of years.

A bow and arrow is one of the oldest examples. When the archer pulls the string back, the bow bends and stores energy. When the string is released, the bow straightens, and the energy is changed into movement energy in the arrow, which flies fast.

A catapult, which many South African children call a ketty, has a stretchy rubber strip. Pulling it back stores energy, and letting go launches a small stone or ball. A catapult must only ever be aimed at a target like a tin can, never at people, animals or windows.

Many toys use elastic or springs. A paper aeroplane can be launched with an elastic band hooked on a stick. A wind-up toy car has a spring inside that unwinds and turns the wheels. Some toy aeroplanes have a long, twisted elastic band that turns the propeller as it untwists. A pinball machine uses a spring plunger to shoot the ball into play. A trampoline's springs stretch when you land, and then push you back up into the air.

Sport uses stored energy too. A pole vaulter bends a long, flexible pole, which stores energy and then straightens, flinging the athlete up over the bar. The strings of a tennis racket stretch when the ball hits them and then push the ball away.

The questions for this section are with you now: bows, catapults, toys and sports that release stored energy as movement.

## Subtopic: The Cotton-Reel Tractor

In Technology, the class builds a cotton-reel tractor. Each learner pushes an elastic band through the hole in an empty cotton reel. At one end, a short piece of matchstick holds the band in place. At the other end, the band goes through a washer and is looped around a pencil. They wind the pencil round and round, which twists the elastic band tightly inside the reel. When they put the reel on the floor and let go, the band slowly untwists, turning the reel, and the tractor crawls across the floor.

The energy chain is: energy from food in the learner's muscles, to stored energy in the twisted elastic band, to movement energy as the tractor rolls. Some energy is also changed into sound and heat as the reel rubs on the washer and the floor.

Then they investigate. Does the number of twists change how far the tractor travels? For a fair test, they use the same tractor, the same elastic band and the same floor, and change only the number of twists: ten, twenty and thirty. The more twists, the further the tractor travels, because more stored energy is changed into movement energy. Some groups put rubber bands around the edges of the reel to make tyres, which grip the floor better.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the bungee jumper bounces because the bridge pushes her; the stretched cord releases its stored energy. Two: thinking a wind-up toy has a battery; it has a spring. Three: thinking all the stored energy turns into movement; some becomes heat and sound. Four: thinking a catapult is a toy to aim at people or animals; it can cause serious injuries.

The questions for this section are with you now: how the cotton-reel tractor works, its energy chain, and the investigation.

## Subtopic: Wind It and Watch It Go

Twist the band in the cotton reel. Let go. The tractor moves.

More twists, further travel.

Pocket summary of the lesson. When stretched or twisted elastic, or a compressed spring, is released, its stored energy is changed into movement energy. Bungee cords, bows, catapults, wind-up toys and trampolines all use this idea. In a cotton-reel tractor, energy from our muscles is stored in a twisted elastic band and released as movement. More twists store more energy, so the tractor goes further. Some energy is always changed into heat and sound.

Hold the racing cotton-reel tractors in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: the cotton-reel tractor.
