# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how people combine raw materials to make new materials. There are five main ways: mixing and setting, as with plaster of Paris and concrete; mixing, as with glue paste; mixing and cooking, as with dough; mixing and cooling, as with jelly; and mixing, drying and firing, as with clay bricks. The anchor is a busy Saturday at the Mahlangu family's home in Khayelitsha, near Cape Town. In the yard, Uncle Sipho is building an extra room. He mixes concrete for the floor and lays clay bricks for the walls. In the kitchen, Gogo is baking bread, and the twins are making red jelly for their little brother's birthday party. At the table, their cousin Ayanda, whose broken arm is in a white plaster cast, is helping the youngest child make glue paste for a school collage. The big idea is that when we combine materials and process them, we can make new materials that are very useful. Describing these five ways of combining materials, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Mixing and Setting

Some materials are mixed with water, and the mixture then sets hard. To set means to change from a soft, runny or sticky mixture into a hard solid, without being heated or cooled.

Plaster of Paris is a fine, white powder. When it is mixed with water, it makes a smooth, runny paste. Within about fifteen to thirty minutes, it sets into a hard, white solid. While it sets, it gets slightly warm. Plaster of Paris is used to make casts that hold broken bones still while they heal, like Ayanda's cast. Doctors wrap bandages soaked in wet plaster around the arm, and the plaster sets into a hard shell. It is also used to make moulds, models, ornaments and smooth finishes on ceilings.

Concrete is made by mixing cement powder, sand, small stones called gravel or stone, and water. Uncle Sipho mixes them on a flat board with a spade, then pours the wet, sloppy concrete into a wooden frame for the floor and smooths it flat. After a few hours, the concrete has set. But it keeps getting harder and stronger for weeks. He sprays it with water for the first few days, so that it does not dry out too quickly and crack. Concrete is used for floors, foundations, pillars, bridges, pavements and dams.

Cement itself is made in a factory by heating crushed limestone and clay together at a very high temperature, and grinding the result into powder.

The questions for this section are with you now: plaster of Paris, concrete and what setting means.

## Subtopic: Mixing, Cooking and Cooling

Some new materials are made just by mixing. The youngest child mixes flour with a little cold water and stirs until it becomes a smooth, sticky glue paste. It holds pieces of paper together on the collage. When it dries, it becomes stiff and holds the paper in place. Nothing is heated or cooled. The flour and water have simply been mixed into a new, sticky material.

Some materials are mixed and then cooked. Gogo mixes flour, water, a little salt, sugar and yeast to make a dough. She kneads it, pushing and folding it for ten minutes, until it becomes smooth and stretchy. She leaves it in a warm place, and the yeast makes tiny gas bubbles that make the dough rise. Then she bakes it in a hot oven. The soft, sticky dough becomes a loaf of bread with a hard, brown crust and a soft, spongy inside. Vetkoek is made from dough that is fried in hot oil instead of being baked.

Some materials are mixed and then cooled. The twins pour jelly powder into a jug and add hot water, stirring until the powder dissolves. Then they add some cold water, pour the liquid into a bowl and put it in the fridge. As it cools, the liquid sets into a wobbly, see-through solid. If it is left out on a hot day, it starts to melt again.

The questions for this section are with you now: glue paste, dough and bread, and jelly.

## Subtopic: Mixing, Drying and Firing

Uncle Sipho's walls are made of clay bricks. The class finds out how they are made.

At a brickworks, clay is dug out of the ground and mixed with water to make it soft and easy to shape. It is pressed into brick-shaped moulds. The soft bricks are then left to dry, often in the sun or in large drying sheds, for days or weeks. Dried bricks are hard, but if they get wet, they turn soft again. So the last step is firing: the dried bricks are stacked in a kiln, a special oven, and heated to a very high temperature, around a thousand degrees Celsius. Firing changes the clay completely. The bricks become very hard, strong and red or brown, and they no longer turn soft in the rain.

In many rural parts of South Africa, people make sun-dried mud bricks for their homes. These are cheaper, but they must be protected from rain with a plastered and painted surface, or they slowly wash away.

Pots, mugs, plates and tiles are made from clay in the same way: shaped, dried and fired. Fired clay is called ceramic.

So we have five methods: mix and set, mix only, mix and cook, mix and cool, and mix, dry and fire. In every case, the starting materials are changed into a new material.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking concrete dries hard like washing; it sets because of a change in the cement, and it must be kept damp so it does not crack. Two: thinking a sun-dried brick is the same as a fired brick; a sun-dried brick goes soft in water, but a fired brick does not. Three: thinking jelly sets because it dries; it sets because it cools. Four: thinking cement and concrete are the same thing; cement is a powder, and concrete is cement mixed with sand, stone and water.

The questions for this section are with you now: making clay bricks, sun-dried bricks, and the five ways of combining materials.

Now the same lesson again with the busy Saturday in Khayelitsha — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Mix and Set

Plaster of Paris and water set hard. It makes casts for broken arms.

Cement, sand, stone and water make concrete. It sets hard for floors and bridges.

Keep Ayanda's cast and Uncle Sipho's floor in mind and try the questions for this part: mixing and setting.

## Subtopic: Mix, Cook and Cool

Flour and water make glue paste.

Dough is baked into bread.

Jelly is mixed with hot water, then cooled until it sets.

Hold Gogo's bread and the twins' jelly in mind and try this part's questions: mixing, cooking and cooling.

## Subtopic: Mix, Dry and Fire

Clay and water are shaped into bricks, dried, then fired in a kiln.

Fired bricks are hard and do not go soft in the rain.

Jelly is mixed and cooled. Clay bricks are mixed, dried and fired. Each method changes the starting materials into something new and useful.
