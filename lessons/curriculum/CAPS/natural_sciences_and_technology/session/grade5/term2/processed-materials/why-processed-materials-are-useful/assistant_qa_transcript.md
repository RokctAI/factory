### subtopic_1 — Strong and Durable
**Assistant:** Why do shack fires spread so quickly?

**Tutor:** Several dangers come together in many informal settlements. Shacks are often built from materials that burn easily, like wood, cardboard, plastic sheeting and old fabric, and these are fuel for a fire. They are built very close together, sometimes touching, so flames and heat can jump from one home to the next. Many families cook, heat their homes and light them with paraffin stoves, candles or open fires, especially in winter, and these can be knocked over. Strong winds, like Cape Town's winter winds, push the flames along and bring more oxygen to the fire. Narrow paths between homes make it hard for fire engines to get close. This is why safer building materials are so important. Walls and roofs that do not burn, enough space between homes and safe ways of cooking and lighting can stop a small fire from becoming a disaster. Organisations, scientists and communities are working together on cheap, fire-resistant building boards and smoke alarms for informal homes.

### subtopic_2 — Waterproof and Fire Resistant
**Assistant:** Is a material that is strong always durable?

**Tutor:** Not always. The two properties are different, even though some materials have both. Strong means a material can carry a heavy load, or be pulled or pushed, without breaking. Durable means it keeps its properties for a long time without wearing out, rotting, rusting or breaking down. Untreated wood can be very strong. A wooden beam can hold up a heavy roof. But if it stays damp, it can rot, or termites can eat it, so it is not very durable unless it is treated or painted. Steel is very strong, but if it is not protected, it rusts and becomes weak, especially near the sea. Fired bricks and ceramic tiles are both strong and very durable, which is why ancient pots and brick buildings have lasted for thousands of years. Some plastics are very durable, lasting for hundreds of years, but they are not very strong. Good builders choose materials that are both strong enough and durable enough for the job.

### subtopic_3 — Colourful and Textured
**Assistant:** Can a material be waterproof but still let air through?

**Tutor:** Yes, and this is a clever bit of material design. Some modern raincoats and tents use special fabrics with millions of tiny holes in them. Each hole is far too small for a drop of water to squeeze through, so rain cannot get in. But the holes are big enough for water vapour, which is water as a gas, to escape. So sweat from your body can get out as vapour, but rain cannot get in. This makes the raincoat waterproof but breathable, so you do not feel clammy and wet inside. Builders use a similar idea in walls and roofs. Under the roof sheets, they may lay a special breathable sheet that stops rain and dust from getting in but lets damp air escape, so the roof timber does not rot. Ordinary plastic sheets and glass are fully waterproof and do not let air or vapour through at all. So when you choose a material, it helps to ask not just, is it waterproof, but also, does it need to breathe?
