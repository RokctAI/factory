# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers why processed materials are so useful. Different processed materials can be strong, durable, waterproof, fire resistant, colourful or textured, and we choose materials for a job because they have the properties the job needs. The anchor is a design competition at a primary school in Langa, one of Cape Town's oldest townships. In winter, a fire destroyed several shacks in a nearby informal settlement, and many families lost everything. The Grade 5 class is asked to design a safe, comfortable two-room home for one of the families. For every part of the house, they must choose a processed material and explain why. Their teacher gives them a list of six properties to think about: strong, durable, waterproof, fire resistant, colourful and textured. The big idea is that each processed material has special properties, and good design means choosing materials whose properties suit the job. Explaining why processed materials are useful, using these properties, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Strong and Durable

The class starts with the walls, floor and roof.

A strong material can carry a heavy load, or be pushed or pulled, without breaking or bending. Concrete is very strong when it is pressed, so the class chooses it for the foundations and the floor slab. Steel reinforcing bars inside the concrete make it strong in every direction. Fired clay bricks and concrete blocks are strong enough to build walls that hold up a roof. The roof frame is made of treated timber or light steel.

A durable material lasts a long time without wearing out, rotting or breaking down. Fired bricks, concrete, ceramic tiles and glass are very durable. They can last for a hundred years or more. A ceramic floor tile can be walked on by thousands of feet without wearing through. A good paint on the outside walls lasts for years before it needs repainting.

Materials that are not durable must be replaced often, which costs money. The shacks were built from wooden planks, cardboard and old metal sheets. The wood rots, the cardboard falls apart in the rain, and the metal sheets rust.

The class decides: concrete foundations and floor, fired brick walls, and a galvanised steel roof on a strong frame. Their house will be strong and durable. It will cost more to build at first, but it will last for many years and will not need to be rebuilt after every storm.

The questions for this section are with you now: what strong and durable mean, and which processed materials have these properties.

## Subtopic: Waterproof and Fire Resistant

Cape Town has wet, windy winters, so the house must keep out the rain.

A waterproof material does not let water through or soak it up. Glass, plastics, glazed ceramics, metal sheets and good paint are waterproof. The class chooses glass for the windows, which keeps the rain out and lets light in. They choose glazed ceramic tiles for the kitchen and bathroom floors and walls, which can be washed and do not soak up water. A plastic sheet called a damp-proof course is laid in the brick walls just above the ground, to stop water rising up from the soil into the walls. Plastic pipes carry water without leaking or rusting. Paint on the outside walls helps keep rain out of the bricks.

The most important property for this family is fire resistance. A fire-resistant material does not catch fire easily and does not burn. Wood, cardboard, paper, many plastics and some fabrics burn easily. Fired bricks, concrete, ceramic tiles, glass and metal are fire resistant. A house made mostly of these materials is much less likely to burn down, and a fire in one room is less likely to spread to the house next door.

The class also adds a smoke alarm to their design, because even in a brick house, furniture, curtains and bedding can burn. Some fabrics are made fire resistant too. Firefighters wear suits made of special fabrics that do not burn, so they can work close to flames.

The questions for this section are with you now: waterproof and fire-resistant materials, and why they matter for a home.

## Subtopic: Colourful and Textured

The house must also be a pleasant, safe place to live.

A colourful material makes things bright and attractive, and colour can also send messages. Paints come in every colour. Fabrics can be dyed or printed with patterns, like the bright shweshwe cloth that many South Africans love. Plastics can be made in any colour. Ceramic tiles can be glazed in many colours. The class paints the outside walls a cheerful yellow and chooses bright curtains for the windows. They also suggest a red-painted fire extinguisher on the kitchen wall, because red is easy to spot in an emergency.

A textured material has a surface you can feel: rough, bumpy, ridged, smooth or soft. Texture can be useful. Floor tiles for bathrooms often have a slightly rough texture, so wet feet do not slip. Concrete paths outside are brushed while they are wet to leave little ridges for grip. Rubber shoe soles have patterns for grip. Fabrics have textures too: a towel is rough and fluffy so it soaks up water, while a cotton sheet is smooth and soft for sleeping.

When the class presents their design, the judges ask them to explain one choice. A learner says: we chose fired bricks for the walls because they are strong, durable and fire resistant, and we painted them because paint is waterproof and colourful.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking strong and durable mean the same; strong means it carries a load, and durable means it lasts a long time. Two: thinking all plastics are fire resistant; many plastics melt and burn. Three: thinking texture is only for looks; rough tiles stop slipping. Four: choosing a material for only one property; good design looks at all the properties the job needs.

The questions for this section are with you now: colourful and textured materials, and choosing materials for all the properties a job needs.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Langa design competition — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Strong and Long-Lasting

Strong materials carry heavy loads, like concrete and bricks.

Durable materials last a long time, like tiles and glass.

Keep the brick walls in mind and try the questions for this part: strong and durable.

## Subtopic: Keep Out Rain and Fire

Waterproof materials keep water out, like glass, plastic and glazed tiles.

Fire-resistant materials do not burn, like bricks, concrete and tiles.

Hold the safe new home in mind and try this part's questions: waterproof and fire resistant.

## Subtopic: Bright and Grippy

Paint and fabrics make things colourful.

Rough tiles and shoe soles give grip.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Processed materials are useful because of their special properties. They can be strong and durable, like concrete and bricks; waterproof, like glass, plastic and glazed tiles; fire resistant, like bricks, concrete and ceramics; colourful, like paints and dyed fabrics; and textured, like non-slip tiles. We choose materials whose properties suit the job.

Hold the yellow walls and the non-slip tiles in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: colourful and textured.
