# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers non-metals and how they differ from metals. Solid non-metals, such as carbon and sulfur, are dull and brittle. Unlike metals, they cannot be hammered into shape or drawn into wire, and most do not carry heat or electricity well. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Kroonstad in the Free State, getting ready for a school braai to raise money for a class outing. On a table, the teacher lays out a bag of charcoal, a box of matches, some pencils, a small lump of yellow sulfur from the science cupboard, and the metal braai grid and tongs. She says: today we compare the things that will make the fire with the things that will hold the meat. The big idea is that materials can be sorted into metals and non-metals by testing their properties, and that solid non-metals are dull and brittle. Describing the properties of non-metals and sorting materials into metals and non-metals are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Are Non-Metals?

Everything around us is made of basic substances called elements. Scientists know of more than a hundred elements. Most of them are metals, like iron, copper, gold and aluminium. The rest are non-metals.

Some non-metals are gases at room temperature. Oxygen and nitrogen, which make up most of the air, are non-metals. So is the gas inside some party balloons that makes them float, called helium.

Some non-metals are solids. The two you are most likely to meet are carbon and sulfur.

Carbon is black or dark grey. Charcoal is mostly carbon. It is made by heating wood with very little air, so that the wood does not burn away completely. Coal is also mostly carbon. The grey core of a pencil, which people call lead, is not lead at all. It is a soft form of carbon called graphite, mixed with clay.

Sulfur is a pale yellow solid. It is found near volcanoes and hot springs, and it is used in making matches, fertilisers and medicines, and to harden rubber for car tyres. When sulfur burns, it gives off a choking gas with a sharp smell that makes you cough, so we only look at it, and do not heat it in class.

In everyday life, we also call materials that are not metals, such as wood, plastic, rubber, glass, paper and stone, non-metal materials. They are not single elements, but like the solid non-metals, they are not metals and do not have the properties of metals.

The questions for this section are with you now: elements, metals and non-metals, and solid non-metals such as carbon and sulfur.

## Subtopic: Dull and Brittle

The class now tests the solid non-metals and compares them with the metal tongs.

First, the shine test. The metal tongs are shiny. The charcoal and the sulfur are dull. They do not reflect light like a mirror, even when you rub them. Graphite in a pencil has a slight grey sheen, but not the bright shine of a polished metal. So solid non-metals are dull.

Second, the hammer test. The teacher puts a piece of charcoal inside a plastic bag on a hard surface and taps it once with a hammer. It does not flatten like the lead sinker did. It cracks and crumbles into black pieces and dust. A small piece of sulfur does the same. A material that breaks, cracks or crumbles easily when it is hit or bent is called brittle. So solid non-metals are brittle. They are not malleable.

Third, the bend and stretch test. A pencil lead snaps if you bend it, while a copper wire bends. You cannot pull charcoal or sulfur into a wire. So they are not ductile.

Fourth, the heat test. The metal tongs get hot right up to the handle when they are left near the fire. The braai master uses a wooden handle on the tongs because wood does not carry heat well. Most non-metals and non-metal materials are poor carriers of heat.

Most non-metals do not carry electricity either. Graphite is an unusual exception, because it does carry electricity.

The class records the results in a table with four columns: shiny or dull, malleable or brittle, bends or snaps, and carries heat or not. The metal tongs score shiny, malleable, bends and carries heat. The charcoal, the sulfur and the pencil lead score dull, brittle, snaps and does not carry heat well. The pattern is clear.

The questions for this section are with you now: the shine test, the hammer test and what dull and brittle mean.

## Subtopic: Sorting Metals and Non-Metals

To sort a material, ask questions about its properties. Is it shiny when it is clean? Can it be hammered flat without breaking? Can it be bent or drawn into wire? Does it carry heat well?

If the answers are mostly yes, it is probably a metal. If the answers are mostly no, it is probably a non-metal.

Here is the class's table. The braai grid: shiny, bends without breaking, carries heat, so it is a metal, steel. The charcoal: dull, crumbles, does not bend, so it is a non-metal, carbon. The matches: the wooden stick is a non-metal material, and the head contains sulfur and other chemicals. The pencil lead: dull grey, snaps when bent, so it is a non-metal, graphite.

We often use metals and non-metals together, so that each does the job it suits. A pot is made of metal, which carries heat to the food, but its handle is often plastic or wood, which stays cool to hold. An electrical cable has copper wire inside to carry electricity, and plastic on the outside to keep people safe.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking pencil lead is the metal lead; it is graphite, a form of carbon. Two: thinking all non-metals are solids; oxygen and nitrogen are non-metals that are gases. Three: thinking brittle means soft; brittle means it breaks or crumbles easily when hit or bent. Four: thinking anything shiny is a metal; glass can be shiny but it is not a metal, so we must test several properties.

The questions for this section are with you now: how to sort materials into metals and non-metals, and using them together.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the school braai — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Not Metal

Most elements are metals. The rest are non-metals.

Carbon and sulfur are solid non-metals. Oxygen is a non-metal gas.

Charcoal and pencil lead are carbon. Sulfur is yellow.

Keep the bag of charcoal in mind and try the questions for this part: non-metals.

## Subtopic: Dull and Crumbly

Non-metals are dull, not shiny.

They are brittle: they crack and crumble when hit.

They do not carry heat well.

Hold the crumbling charcoal in mind and try this part's questions: dull and brittle.

## Subtopic: Metal or Not?

Shiny, bendy and carries heat: metal.

Dull, crumbly and stays cool: non-metal.
