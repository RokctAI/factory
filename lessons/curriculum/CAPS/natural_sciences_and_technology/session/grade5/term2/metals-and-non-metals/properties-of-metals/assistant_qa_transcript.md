### subtopic_1 — Shiny, Hard and Strong
**Assistant:** Why is gold so valuable if it is soft and not as strong as steel?

**Tutor:** Gold is valuable for different reasons from steel. It is rare, which means there is not much of it, and it is hard and expensive to mine. In South Africa, miners have to go kilometres underground and crush tonnes of rock to get just a few grams of gold. Gold also never rusts or tarnishes. A gold coin or ring dug up after thousands of years still shines like new. That makes it beautiful and long-lasting, so people have used it for jewellery, coins and treasure for thousands of years. Because it is malleable, it is easy to shape into fine jewellery. Gold is also a very good conductor of electricity that does not go dull, so tiny amounts are used in cell phones and computers. Because gold is so soft, jewellers usually mix it with other metals, such as copper or silver, to make it harder. A mix of metals is called an alloy. So gold is valuable because it is rare, beautiful and long-lasting, not because it is strong.

### subtopic_2 — Bend, Hammer and Stretch
**Assistant:** How can a thick metal rod become a thin wire without breaking?

**Tutor:** This is what ductile means, and it is one of the special things about metals. In a wire factory, a metal rod is pulled, not pushed, through a hard block with a hole in it called a die. The hole is a bit smaller than the rod. As the rod is pulled through, the metal stretches out and gets thinner and longer. Then it is pulled through a slightly smaller hole, and a smaller one again, many times, until it is as thin as needed. The metal does not break because the tiny particles it is made of can slide past each other while still holding on, so the metal stretches instead of snapping. If you tried this with a stick of chalk or a piece of glass, it would just crack, because their particles cannot slide like that. Copper is drawn into wire for electrical cables, and steel for fences, springs and the strings of guitars. Some wires are thinner than a human hair.

### subtopic_3 — Metals from the Earth
**Assistant:** Is it dangerous to work deep underground in a mine?

**Tutor:** Yes, mining is one of the most dangerous jobs, especially in very deep mines like South Africa's gold mines. Deep underground, the rock is very hot, so mines must pump cold air down to keep the miners cool. The weight of all the rock above presses down, and sometimes rocks fall or burst from the walls. There can be dangerous gases and dust. Breathing in rock dust for many years can cause lung disease. Mining companies and the government have strict safety laws to protect workers. Miners wear hard hats with lamps, boots with steel toecaps, ear protection and sometimes masks. Tunnels are held up with strong supports, and there are rescue teams ready. The mines test the air, spray water to keep the dust down and train every worker in safety. Over the years, safety has improved a lot, but accidents still happen. That is one more reason why recycling metals is so important: every recycled can or pot means less rock has to be dug up.
