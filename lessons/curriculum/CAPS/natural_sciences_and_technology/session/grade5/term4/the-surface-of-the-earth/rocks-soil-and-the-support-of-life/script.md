# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the surface of the Earth: the Earth's outer layer, called the crust, is made of rock, which in many places is covered by soil, and soil, air, water and sunlight together support life. The anchor is a hike by a Grade 5 class from Rustenburg in the Magaliesberg mountains in North West. The path climbs over bare grey rock, very hard and very old. Lower down, it runs through grassland and trees, where the ground is covered in soft brown soil. A guide shows the learners how to dig a small hole with a trowel, and they find roots, a beetle, ants and a pink earthworm. Rustenburg itself is famous for its platinum mines, which dig deep into the rock beneath the town. The big idea is that the Earth's crust is made of rocks and soil, and that soil, air, water and sunlight make life on Earth possible. Describing the crust and the things that support life is examinable.

## Subtopic: The Earth's Crust

The guide asks the class to imagine cutting the Earth in half, like an apple. They would see layers.

The outer layer is the crust. It is made of solid rock, and it is the layer we live on. The crust is very thin compared with the whole Earth. Under the oceans, it is only about five to ten kilometres thick. Under the land, it is thicker, about 30 to 50 kilometres, and thickest under high mountains. If the Earth were an apple, the crust would be thinner than the apple's skin.

Beneath the crust is the mantle, a very thick layer of extremely hot rock. Some of it is soft enough to flow, very slowly. At the centre of the Earth is the core, the hottest part of all, made mostly of the metals iron and nickel.

Nobody has ever travelled to the mantle or the core. The deepest mines in the world, some of them gold mines near Johannesburg, go down about four kilometres, and even they are still in the crust. Scientists learn about the deep layers by studying how earthquake vibrations travel through the Earth.

More than two thirds of the crust is covered by oceans. The rest is land, where we live, and that is where the rocks and soil of this lesson are found.

The questions for this section are with you now: the layers of the Earth and the crust.

## Subtopic: Rocks and Soil on the Surface

On the hike, the learners notice that the crust does not look the same everywhere. High up on the mountain, there is bare rock. The Magaliesberg rock is a hard rock called quartzite, and it is very old: it formed more than two billion years ago. Rock is hard and solid, and it is made of tiny pieces called grains or crystals, packed tightly together.

In most other places, the rock is hidden under a layer of soil. Soil is the loose, crumbly material on top of the rock, where plants grow. It is made mostly of tiny grains of broken rock, mixed with water, air, and the rotting remains of dead plants and animals. In the next lessons, we will see how rock slowly breaks down to form soil.

In many places, you can see rock and soil together. On the side of a road cut through a hill, or on the bank of a river, there is often a layer of dark soil on top, a paler layer below, and solid rock at the bottom.

Soil and rock feel very different. Rock is hard, and you need a hammer to break off a piece. Soil is soft enough to dig with a trowel, and it crumbles in your hand.

Rocks are very useful to people. The platinum mined at Rustenburg is found inside rock. South Africa mines gold, coal, iron and many other minerals from the rocks of the crust. Rocks are also cut for building, and crushed into gravel for roads and concrete.

The questions for this section are with you now: rocks and soil, and how we use rocks.

## Subtopic: What Life Needs: Soil, Air, Water and Sunlight

Everywhere on the hike, there is life: grass, trees, flowers, insects, lizards, birds, and a troop of baboons on the cliffs. The guide explains that four things on the Earth's surface work together to support all this life: soil, air, water and sunlight.

Soil holds plants in place with their roots, and it gives them water and minerals, which are nutrients they need to grow. Soil is also a home for many living things, such as earthworms, ants, termites, beetles and moles, and for tiny living things too small to see.

Air contains oxygen, which plants and animals need to breathe, and carbon dioxide, which plants need to make food.

Water is needed by every living thing. Plants take it up through their roots, and animals drink it or get it from their food.

Sunlight gives the energy that green plants use to make their own food, and it keeps the Earth warm. Animals depend on plants for food, so in the end, all of them depend on sunlight too.

The four work as a team, and if one is missing, life struggles. On the bare rock at the top of the mountain, there is plenty of air, water when it rains, and sunlight, but almost no soil, so only tough little plants called lichens and mosses grow in the cracks. In a dark cave, there is soil, air and water, but no sunlight, so green plants cannot grow at all. In a dry riverbed in winter, there is soil, air and sunlight, but little water, so the grass turns brown and waits for the rain.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the crust is the thickest layer; it is the thinnest. Two: thinking the Earth is soil all the way down; soil is a thin layer on top of rock. Three: thinking rock and soil are the same thing; rock is hard and solid, soil is loose and crumbly. Four: thinking plants get their food from the soil; they make food using sunlight, and take only water and minerals from the soil.

The questions for this section are with you now: how soil, air, water and sunlight support life.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Magaliesberg hike — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: A Thin Rocky Skin

The crust is the Earth's thin, rocky outer layer.

Under it are the hot mantle and the core.

Keep the apple skin in mind and try the questions for this part: the crust.

## Subtopic: Rock Below, Soil on Top

Rock is hard and solid.

Soil is loose and crumbly, and it lies on top of the rock.

We mine rock for metals and use it to build.

Hold the bare mountain rock in mind and try this part's questions: rocks and soil.

## Subtopic: Four Things Life Needs

Soil, air, water and sunlight.

Together they keep plants and animals alive.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The Earth has a thin outer layer called the crust, made of rock, with the mantle and the core beneath it. On land, the rock of the crust is often covered by a layer of loose soil. Rocks are useful for metals, building and roads. Soil, air, water and sunlight support life: soil holds plants and gives them water and minerals, air gives oxygen and carbon dioxide, all living things need water, and sunlight gives plants energy to make food.

Hold the beetle and the earthworm in the soil in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: what supports life.
