### subtopic_1 — Sandstone and Shale
**Assistant:** Why does limestone fizz in vinegar but sandstone does not?

**Tutor:** It is because of what each rock is made of. Limestone is made mostly of a mineral called calcium carbonate, the same mineral that sea shells, eggshells and the white crust inside a kettle are made of. Vinegar is a weak acid. When an acid touches calcium carbonate, they react, which means they change into new substances, and one of those new substances is a gas, carbon dioxide. The gas makes the bubbles you see, so the limestone fizzes. You can try the same thing with an eggshell in vinegar: it fizzes too, and if you leave it long enough, the shell dissolves. Sandstone is made mostly of grains of a hard mineral called quartz, which does not react with vinegar, so nothing happens. That is why geologists sometimes carry a small bottle of weak acid when they go out looking at rocks: a quick drop tells them at once whether a rock is limestone. Always wash your hands and never put acids near your eyes.

### subtopic_2 — Limestone
**Assistant:** Why are so many buildings in the eastern Free State made of sandstone?

**Tutor:** Because builders long ago used what they could find nearby. Before trucks and good roads, moving heavy stone over long distances was very difficult and expensive, so people built with local materials. The eastern Free State, around towns like Ficksburg, Clarens and Bethlehem, sits on thick layers of sandstone, the same rock as the Golden Gate cliffs. It was easy to quarry, because it splits along its layers, and it is soft enough to cut and shape with simple tools, but strong enough to last for over a hundred years. It also has beautiful colours, from cream to golden to rusty red. So farmers, churches and town councils built farmhouses, churches, town halls and walls from it. Many of these buildings still stand today and are protected as part of the region's history. In the same way, people in other areas used the stone that was nearby, which is why old buildings in different parts of South Africa look different.

### subtopic_3 — Using Sedimentary Rocks
**Assistant:** Is cement the same as concrete?

**Tutor:** No, but they are closely related, and many people mix up the words. Cement is the fine grey powder that comes in bags. It is made by heating crushed limestone and clay in a huge kiln, then grinding the result into powder. On its own, cement is not used to build walls. Concrete is what you get when you mix cement with sand, small stones and water. The cement and water react and set hard, gluing the sand and stones together into a strong, rock-like material. So cement is like the glue, and concrete is the whole hard material. It is a bit like making a cake: cement is one ingredient, like flour, but concrete is the finished cake. Mortar, the paste between bricks in a wall, is made from cement, sand and water, without the stones. All of these depend on limestone, which is why cement factories are built close to limestone quarries, like the one at Lichtenburg.
