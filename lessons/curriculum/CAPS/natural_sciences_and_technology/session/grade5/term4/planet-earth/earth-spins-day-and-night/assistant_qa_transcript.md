### subtopic_1 — Spinning on an Axis
**Assistant:** If the Earth is spinning so fast, why don't we fall off or get dizzy?

**Tutor:** Two reasons. First, the Earth's gravity holds us firmly onto the ground. Gravity is the same pull that makes a ball fall back down when you throw it up. It is strong enough to hold you, the oceans and the air onto the spinning Earth, so nothing flies off. Second, we only feel dizzy when we spin quickly in a small circle, like on a merry-go-round, where we turn round completely every few seconds. The Earth takes about 24 hours to turn round just once. That is so slow and smooth that our bodies cannot feel it at all. The ground moves very far in that time, because the Earth is so big, but the turning itself is gentle. It is a bit like being on a huge, smooth ship on a calm sea: you can walk about, eat and sleep and never notice that you are moving.

### subtopic_2 — Day and Night
**Assistant:** Is a day always 12 hours of light and 12 hours of dark?

**Tutor:** No. A whole day, one spin of the Earth, takes about 24 hours, but the number of hours of daylight changes during the year. In South Africa, in summer, around December, the days are long. In Durban, the Sun rises before five in the morning and sets at about seven in the evening, so there are about 14 hours of daylight. In winter, around June, the Sun rises at nearly seven and sets before half past five, so there are only about 10 or 11 hours of daylight. This happens because the Earth is tilted, as we learnt in the lesson about the year. When our half of the Earth leans towards the Sun, we spend more of each spin in the light. When it leans away, we spend more of each spin in the dark. Only twice a year, in March and September, are day and night roughly equal everywhere.

### subtopic_3 — Sunrise, Sunset and Shadows
**Assistant:** Why do people say the Sun rises if it is not really moving?

**Tutor:** Because that is what it looks like from where we stand, and people used these words long before anyone knew that the Earth spins. For thousands of years, people watched the Sun come up over the horizon in the morning and go down in the evening, and it truly seemed as if the Sun was travelling across the sky around a still Earth. So the words sunrise and sunset became part of every language. Even scientists who know exactly what is happening still say the Sun rises and sets, because it is a quick, easy way to describe what we see. It is a bit like saying that the trees are rushing past when you look out of the taxi window. You know the trees are standing still and that you are the one moving, but that is how it looks. So it is fine to say sunrise and sunset, as long as you understand the real reason: our part of the Earth is turning towards the Sun in the morning and away from it in the evening.
