Hey, it's Thandi — measurement, maps AND data in one revision session feels like three subjects, but they share one skeleton: read carefully, match the units, do one calculation, answer in a sentence.

Measurement: three words tell you everything. Around is perimeter — add the sides. Cover is area — multiply length by width. Fill is volume — multiply all three. Then the money layer: R85 per metre times 29 metres of fence is R2 465. The units are half the mark — metres for edges, square metres for surfaces, cubic metres for space, and one cubic metre is 1 000 litres.

Units before arithmetic, always. A path 750 centimetres by 1,2 metres? Convert first — 7,5 metres — then multiply: 9 square metres. Mixed units make confident nonsense.

Scale: multiply to grow paper into reality, divide to shrink reality onto paper. 45 millimetres at 1 to 100 is 4,5 metres. 6 centimetres at 1 to 50 000 is 3 kilometres — big ugly number in the middle, clean conversion at the end. Floor plan for widths, elevation for heights.

Data: mean is total over count, median is the middle of the ordered list, mode is the popular one, range is spread. Outlier in the data? Trust the median and say why. And probability from data is just the fraction: 8 red taxis out of 40 is 0,2 — twenty percent.

Read, convert, calculate, sentence. Four beats, every question. Go take on the practice set now. You've got this!
