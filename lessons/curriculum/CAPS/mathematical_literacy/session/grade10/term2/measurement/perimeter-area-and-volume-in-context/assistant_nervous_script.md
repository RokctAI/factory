Hey, it's Thandi — if measurement feels like a pile of formulas you are supposed to have memorised, take a breath, because here is the secret: the paper GIVES you the formulas. They are printed on the sheet. Nobody is testing whether you remembered that a cylinder is pi r squared h.

What is being tested is three small decisions, and they are the same three every time. Decision one: which question is this — how far around, how much to cover, or how much to fill? Around is perimeter, cover is area, fill is volume. Decision two: are all my units the same? Decision three: which way do I round at the end?

That last one is the sneaky mark. Materials go UP — 9,47 litres of paint means two tins, because half a tin short is a wall that does not match. Days and whole items go DOWN — 5,47 days of water means 5, because the sixth day runs dry. Ask yourself "must I have enough, or must it be complete?" and you will get it right every time.

Two little habits that quietly protect you. Halve the diameter before you use a circle formula — everybody forgets, and forgetting makes the answer four times too big. And write the unit on every line, not just the answer, because a right number in the wrong unit is a wrong answer.

You have already done this maths. You have worked out whether one two-litre cooldrink is enough for the table. You have looked at a room and guessed how much paint. We are only teaching your hands to show what your head already does. You've got this!
