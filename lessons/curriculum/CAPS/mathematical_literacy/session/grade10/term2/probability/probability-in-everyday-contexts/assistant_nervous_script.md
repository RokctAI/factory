Hey, it's Thandi — probability has a reputation for being the weird topic, the one where the answer feels like an opinion. It is not. It is counting, and you have been counting since you were four.

Everything in this topic sits on one short line from 0 to 1. Zero is impossible. One is certain. A half is a coin. Below a half is unlikely, above is likely. If your answer ever comes out negative or bigger than 1, you have not found something exotic — you have made a mistake, and the scale just caught it for you for free.

The actual maths is a fraction: how many ways can the thing I want happen, over how many ways can anything happen? Twelve red sweets out of twenty-five is 12 over 25, which is 0,48, which is 48 percent. Fraction, decimal, percentage — same fact wearing three outfits, and the question tells you which one to put on.

Here is where the marks quietly live, so hear it twice: the bottom of the fraction. "Out of all 200 patients" is a different denominator from "out of the 120 under-30s". Before you write anything, say out loud "out of whom?" That single question is worth more marks in this topic than anything else I can teach you.

And two little rules that carry a lot of weight. Say "not" and reach for the complement — 1 minus what you have. Draw the tree before you calculate anything with two stages, and remember heads-tails and tails-heads are two different paths, not one.

You already do this. You know a taxi is more likely to be full at five in the afternoon than at eleven in the morning. We are just putting a number on what you already sense. You've got this!
