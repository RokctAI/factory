Hey, it's Thandi — VAT, discounts and interest all in one session sounds like three different monsters, and I promise you it is really only one animal wearing three coats. Every single question in this lesson is a percentage question, and percentages only ever ask you one thing: fifteen out of every hundred of WHAT?

That "of what" is the whole game. VAT on a shelf price is already inside the number, so you go backwards by dividing by 1,15 — never by subtracting 15 percent. A second discount comes off the already-reduced price, not off the original. Simple interest always comes off the amount you started with, year after year. Three situations, one question asked three times.

The thing that actually costs people marks here is not the maths, it is the months. Simple interest wants TIME IN YEARS. Six months is 0,5. Eight months is 8 divided by 12. Eighteen months is 1,5. Write the fraction down before you touch the calculator and that mark is safe forever.

Build yourself four habits and this topic pays you every time: write the substitution line before you press anything, keep the rand sign and the cents on every money answer, round only at the very end, and finish with a sentence — "the family should buy during the sale because…". Markers hand out marks for that sentence.

And you already do this maths in real life. You know a R100 shirt at 15 percent off is not R15. You know mielie meal costs the same at the till as it does on the shelf. We are just teaching your street sense to show its working. You've got this!
