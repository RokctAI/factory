Today we are learning to run a formula like a machine — values in, answer out — using a car-hire tariff of R450 plus R12,50 per kilometre. Substitution quietly carries marks on both Mathematical Literacy papers, because the formula is always printed for you; the marks are for feeding it in the right order and unwinding it patiently when the question runs backwards. We will price a 340 kilometre trip forwards, then flip the machine to find how far a R1 200 budget really goes, meeting the two most expensive errors in the topic along the way: adding before multiplying, and dividing a budget before removing the fixed charge. Master the order and the verification habit, and formula questions become marks you collect every single time.
