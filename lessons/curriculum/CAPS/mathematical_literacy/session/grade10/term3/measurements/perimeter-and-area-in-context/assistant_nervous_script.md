Hey, it's Thandi — if circles and formulas make your palms sweat, here is the secret: the formulas are PRINTED on the exam paper. Nobody expects you to remember them. You only make three small decisions, and you can practise all three tonight.

Decision one: edge or inside? Fence, edging, border — that is the walk around, perimeter, metres. Paving, seed, paint — that is covering the inside, area, square metres. Ask it before every question and half the battle is over.

Decision two: feed the formula honestly. If they give a diameter, halve it to a radius first. Square the radius BEFORE multiplying by pi. Triangle height means the straight-up height, not the slanted side.

Decision three: the pizza rule. Half a pizza has half the crust plus the straight cut — so a semicircle's border is the curve PLUS the diameter. Area never gains edges; only the perimeter does. Trace the shape with your finger and count every side you touch.

Then the shop: divide by the pack size and round UP, always. 3,85 rolls is 4 rolls. Even 3,1 rolls is 4 rolls — because 3 leaves a hole in the fence.

Go walk the practice garden: edge or inside, feed it honestly, crust plus cuts, round up, price it. You've got this!
