Hey, it's Thandi — four different graphs, each with its own rules, and a question paper that hands you one and says "interpret". If that feels like four topics pretending to be one, I understand. It is not. It is one topic with four outfits.

Start with the choosing rule, because it settles almost everything. Names or labels along the bottom? Bar graph, bars with gaps. Numbers grouped into bands along the bottom? Histogram, bars touching. Parts of one single whole? Pie chart. Months or days across the bottom? Line graph. That is the whole decision, and you can make it by glancing at the horizontal axis.

Pie charts have exactly two calculations and you can carry them both. Percentage to angle: multiply by 3,6, because 100 percent must fill 360 degrees. Angle to amount: put the angle over 360, then take that fraction of the total. And the trap that catches everyone — a pie chart on its own gives you shares, never rands. Hunt for the total on the page before you convert anything.

For line graphs, get in the habit of describing before calculating. Say out loud what the line does — flat, rising, steepest here, highest there — and only then work out the numbers behind it. Markers give marks for a described trend, and describing it first stops you reading the wrong month off the wrong axis.

Now the one that wins the most marks for the least work: criticising a graph. Three moves, in order. Name the feature — the axis starts at 400, not 0. Say what it does — it makes a small increase look enormous. Then give the honest number — the rise from 420 to 480 units is about 14,3 percent. Feature, effect, figure. Practise saying those three aloud on any graph you pass in a newspaper.

One glance at the bottom axis, two pie formulas, describe before you calculate, and three moves for a criticism. That is the entire topic. You've got this!
