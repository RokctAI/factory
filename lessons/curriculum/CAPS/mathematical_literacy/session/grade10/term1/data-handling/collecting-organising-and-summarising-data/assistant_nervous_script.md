Hey, it's Thandi — data handling looks like a swamp of words. Population, sample, primary, secondary, discrete, continuous, categorical, tally, frequency, mean, median, mode, range. Thirteen terms and a project due. Breathe, because the actual work is small and you have done it before, at home, with washing.

Here is the whole topic in four moves. One: ask a question a number could answer. Two: collect the answers without only asking the people who will say what you expect. Three: put the values in order and sort them into piles that do not overlap. Four: find the numbers that speak for the pile. That is it. Everything else is vocabulary sitting on top of those four moves.

The four summary numbers each have one job, and you can hold them in one hand. Mean is sharing out — total divided by how many. Median is the one standing in the middle once everybody is lined up in order, and you must line them up first, every single time. Mode is the one that shows up most often, and it is the only one that works for words instead of numbers. Range is biggest minus smallest — the stretch, not the centre.

The habit that saves the most marks is this: show the division. Write 825 divided by 15, then write 55 minutes. If your calculator slips, the marker can still see the method and give you the method mark. And keep the unit attached — minutes, rands, learners. A bare number is an unfinished answer.

One last thing that turns a good answer into a full one. When the mean and the median disagree, do not panic and do not pick one at random. Say WHY they disagree — a few very big values pulled the mean up — and say which one answers the question that was asked. That single sentence is often worth as much as the whole calculation.

Four moves, four numbers, one unit, one sentence of interpretation. You've got this!
