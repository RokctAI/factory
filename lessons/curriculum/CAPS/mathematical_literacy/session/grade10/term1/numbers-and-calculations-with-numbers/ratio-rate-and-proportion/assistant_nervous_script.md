Hey, it's Thandi — ratio, rate, direct proportion, indirect proportion. Four names that sound like they need four different brains. They do not. There are really only three moves in this whole topic, and you already do all three at the shops.

Move one is sharing. Any ratio question, no matter how dressed up: add the parts, divide the money by the total parts, multiply each share back out. Add the parts, find one part, hand them out. Then add your answers up and check you land on the original amount — that check catches almost every mistake before the marker does.

Move two is "what does ONE cost, or what does ONE do?" That is a rate, and you get there by dividing. Rands per kilogram, kilometres per litre, rands per kilolitre. The only place this bites is time: if the answer must be per HOUR, your minutes have to become hours first. Forty-five minutes is 0,75 of an hour, not 45.

Move three is deciding whether more means more, or more means less. More bread costs more money — go down to one, then up. More painters means fewer days — multiply the pair to find the size of the job, then divide. Ask yourself one question in words before you touch the calculator: when this goes up, does that go up or down? Answer it out loud and the method chooses itself.

Here is the habit that protects your marks in every single one of these: write the substitution down, keep the units on every line, and round only at the very end. A marker cannot give you method marks for a method they cannot see, and "17,20" without "per kg" is only half an answer.

Three moves, one habit, and a sentence at the end that actually answers what was asked. That is the whole topic in your pocket. You've got this!
