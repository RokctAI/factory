Welcome, student. Today we sharpen the single most-used tool in Mathematical Literacy: percentage calculations. Percentages run through VAT, discounts, interest, inflation and tariffs — they appear in Question 2 of every Paper 1 from now until matric. Our case study looks simple and hides a famous surprise: a jacket priced at R850 is discounted by 15 percent, then 15 percent VAT is added to the discounted price. What is the final price — and what single percentage change from the original does the whole journey amount to?

## Subtopic: What a Percentage Really Is — and the Decimal Trick

A percentage is a fraction with a fixed denominator of 100. Fifteen percent means fifteen out of every hundred — 15 over 100 — which as a decimal is 0,15. Every percentage calculation becomes easy the moment you translate the percentage into its decimal form: divide by 100, then multiply by whatever amount you are working with.

So 15 percent OF R850 is 0,15 times 850. Compute it: 0,1 times 850 is 85; 0,05 times 850 is 42,50; together, R127,50. That is the size of our discount — the amount the shop takes off.

Now the upgrade that separates strong candidates from calculator-fumblers: the multiplier method. Instead of finding the percentage amount and then adding or subtracting it, capture the whole change in ONE multiplication.

A 15 percent decrease leaves you with 85 percent of the original — 100 minus 15. So multiply by 0,85 in one step. A 15 percent increase gives you 115 percent of the original — multiply by 1,15. Decrease: one minus the decimal. Increase: one plus the decimal.

Check it against the long way. Long way: 850 minus 127,50 gives 722,50. Multiplier way: 850 times 0,85 — 850 times 0,8 is 680, plus 850 times 0,05 which is 42,50 — gives 722,50. Same answer, half the steps, and — as you are about to see — the multiplier method is the only comfortable way to handle chains of changes.

## Subtopic: The Discount Step, Then the VAT Step

Back to the jacket, working the chain properly. Rule number one for chained percentages: each step works on the RESULT of the previous step, never on the original. The question says VAT is added to the discounted price — read those words carefully, they define the chain.

Step one, the discount. The marked price is R850, and the discount is 15 percent. Using the multiplier: R850 times 0,85 equals R722,50. That is the till price before tax — the shop's reduced price.

Step two, the VAT. VAT — value-added tax — in South Africa is 15 percent, and it is ADDED to the price. Increase multiplier: 1,15. Apply it to the discounted price, not the original: R722,50 times 1,15.

Compute carefully: 722,50 times 1 is 722,50. 722,50 times 0,15: one tenth is 72,25; half of that is 36,125; together 108,375. Add: 722,50 plus 108,375 gives 830,875. Money rounds to two decimals: R830,88.

The final price of the jacket is R830,88.

Pause on the classic error before we celebrate. Many learners compute 15 percent of the ORIGINAL R850 for both steps: minus R127,50, plus R127,50 — and declare the final price R850, "because they cancel". They do not cancel. The discount acted on R850, but the VAT acted on the smaller R722,50 — so the VAT added back less than the discount removed. Percentages of different amounts are different amounts of money, even when the percentage rate looks identical. This single insight will protect you in every interest, inflation and mark-up question for the next three years.

## Subtopic: The Single Percentage Change — One Multiplier for the Whole Journey

Now the second half of the exam question: what single percentage change takes you from the original R850 to the final R830,88?

Elegant method first: multiply the multipliers. The whole journey was: times 0,85, then times 1,15. So the combined effect is 0,85 times 1,15. Multiply them: 0,85 times 1 is 0,85; 0,85 times 0,15 is 0,1275; total 0,9775.

Read the combined multiplier the same way you built the individual ones. A multiplier of 0,9775 means the final price is 97,75 percent of the original. Since 100 minus 97,75 is 2,25 — the whole journey is a 2,25 percent DECREASE.

Confirm with the direct method, because two roads to the same answer is verification. Percentage change equals the change divided by the ORIGINAL, times 100. The change: 850 minus 830,875 is 19,125. Divide by the original: 19,125 over 850 equals 0,0225. Times 100: 2,25 percent decrease. Both methods agree.

Notice the headline result: minus 15 percent followed by plus 15 percent is NOT zero — it is minus 2,25 percent. Down-then-up by the same rate always lands you slightly below where you started, because the increase works on a smaller base. Shops know this; now you do too.

And the direct percentage-change formula deserves its own spotlight, because it answers every "by what percentage did it change?" question: new minus original, divided by original, times 100. The original is always the denominator — the reference point. Divide by the new value by mistake and the answer shifts; the reference for change is always where you started.

## Subtopic: Reverse Percentages — and the Full Toolkit

One more weapon completes the kit: working backwards. Suppose a different question: a price tag reads R830,88 INCLUDING 15 percent VAT — what was the price before VAT?

The trap answer subtracts 15 percent: 830,88 times 0,85 gives about R706,25. Wrong. Why? Because the 15 percent VAT was calculated on the BEFORE-VAT price, not on the final price — subtracting 15 percent of the wrong base repeats the error we exposed earlier, just in reverse.

The correct move: the final price IS the before-VAT price times 1,15. So run the multiplication backwards — divide. R830,88 divided by 1,15 equals R722,50 (allowing the cent of rounding). There is our discounted price again, recovered perfectly. To undo a multiplier, divide by it: that is the whole theory of reverse percentages.

Assemble the complete toolkit, your five moves for any percentage question. Move one: percentage OF an amount — decimal times amount. Move two: increase or decrease — one single multiplier, 1 plus or 1 minus the decimal. Move three: chained changes — multiply the multipliers, each step acting on the previous result. Move four: percentage change — difference over ORIGINAL, times 100. Move five: reverse percentage — divide by the multiplier to recover the earlier price.

Verify the whole jacket problem end to end, as you should in the exam: 850 times 0,85 is 722,50; times 1,15 is 830,875, rounding to R830,88; combined multiplier 0,9775 confirms a 2,25 percent overall decrease; and 830,875 divided by 1,15 lands back on 722,50. Every arrow runs forwards and backwards without contradiction. That closed loop is what mastery feels like — and in Finance questions from tariffs to inflation to interest, you will run this loop again and again.
