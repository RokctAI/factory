**Student:** I don't trust the multiplier thing. Why does multiplying by 0,85 magically take off 15%?

**Thandi:** Let's make the magic visible! The price is 100% of itself. Take away 15% and what's left? 85%. And 85% as a decimal is 0,85. So "take off 15%" and "keep 85%" are the same sentence — multiplying by 0,85 just says "keep 85% of it" directly. Test it yourself on an easy number: 15% of R100 is R15, so R100 minus R15 is R85. And R100 × 0,85? Also R85. The multiplier isn't a trick — it's the leftover percentage wearing decimal clothes.

**Student:** In the jacket problem, why can't I just work out 15% of R850 once and use it for both the discount and the VAT?

**Thandi:** Because the two steps happen to different prices! Read the question's words: VAT is added "to the discounted price". So the discount bites R850 — taking off R127,50 — but the VAT bites what's LEFT, R722,50, adding back only R108,38. Same rate, different bases, different rand amounts. That's why the final price is R830,88 and not R850. Golden rule for chains: each step eats what the previous step left on the plate, never the original meal.

**Student:** For percentage change, how do I remember whether to divide by the old value or the new one?

**Thandi:** Ask: "compared to WHAT?" A percentage change always measures against your starting point — where you began is the reference. So: change ÷ ORIGINAL × 100. The jacket dropped R19,13 from a start of R850: 19,13 ÷ 850 × 100 = 2,25%. Here's a memory hook: when you say "prices rose 10% this year", you mean compared to *last* year — the older value. The past is the baseline; the change is judged against it. Original on the bottom, always.

**Student:** The reverse VAT question got me — I subtracted 15% and it was wrong. But adding 15% is how VAT got there, so why doesn't subtracting undo it?

**Thandi:** Because adding 15% wasn't a subtraction — it was a *multiplication* by 1,15! And multiplications are undone by division, not by subtraction. Look at the sizes: the VAT charged was 15% of R722,50 (the smaller pre-VAT price) = R108,38. But "subtract 15%" takes 15% of R830,88 (the bigger final price) = R124,63 — too much! You'd overshoot backwards. So to reverse any percentage step: divide by its multiplier. ÷1,15 lands you perfectly on R722,50. Multiplication in, division out — like walking back through the same door you entered.
