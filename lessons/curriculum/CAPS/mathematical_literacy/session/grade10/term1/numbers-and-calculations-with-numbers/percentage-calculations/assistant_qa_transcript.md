**Thandi:** Questions from the class before we close! The first one comes up every single time: why does multiplying by 0,85 take fifteen percent off? It feels like a trick somebody invented, and people don't trust tricks in an exam.

**Tutor:** It is not a trick, and the proof takes one sentence. Any price is one hundred percent of itself — R850 is one hundred percent of R850. Take fifteen percent away, and what remains is one hundred minus fifteen, which is eighty-five percent. So "take off fifteen percent" and "keep eighty-five percent" are two ways of saying exactly the same thing, and eighty-five percent written as a decimal is 0,85. Multiplying by 0,85 simply says: keep eighty-five percent of it. Test it on a friendly number. Fifteen percent of R100 is R15, so R100 less R15 is R85. And R100 times 0,85 is also R85. Identical. The increase multiplier comes from the same reasoning: adding fifteen percent means you keep your whole hundred percent and gain fifteen more, so you finish on one hundred and fifteen percent, which is 1,15. Decrease, one minus the decimal; increase, one plus the decimal. Once you see the multiplier as the leftover percentage wearing decimal clothes, there is nothing left to memorise.

***

**Thandi:** Next one, and it caused an argument. Someone says the jacket goes down fifteen percent and then up fifteen percent, so surely it must come back to R850. Why doesn't it?

**Tutor:** Because the two fifteens are taken from different amounts of money. Read the wording of the question: the VAT is added to the discounted price. So the discount acts on R850 and removes fifteen percent of R850, which is R127,50, leaving R722,50. Then the VAT acts on R722,50 and adds fifteen percent of R722,50, which is R108,38. Same rate, smaller base, smaller rand amount — R19,13 smaller. You come down a big step and go back up a smaller one, so you land below where you began: R722,50 plus R108,38 is R830,88. The percentage rate looking identical is the whole illusion; percentages are not amounts of money until you say "percent of what". And the overall trip has its own number: multiply the two multipliers, 0,85 times 1,15, and you get 0,9775 — the final price is 97,75 percent of the original, a 2,25 percent decrease. That single figure is what the second half of the exam question is asking for.

***

**Thandi:** Here's one from a learner who lost a mark for the arithmetic being right and the answer still being wrong. For percentage change, how do you know whether to divide by the old price or the new one?

**Tutor:** Ask yourself the question the phrase is secretly asking: changed compared with what? A percentage change is always measured against the starting point, so the original value goes in the denominator, every time. Change divided by original, times one hundred. On the jacket: the price fell by R19,13 — that is R850 minus R830,88 — and the original was R850. So 19,125 divided by 850 gives 0,0225, and times one hundred is a 2,25 percent decrease. Divide by the wrong figure and see what happens: 19,125 divided by 830,88 gives 0,0230, so you would report 2,30 percent. Close enough to look right, wrong enough to lose the mark. The everyday version fixes it permanently: when the news says petrol is six percent more expensive, six percent of what? Of last month's price — the one it moved away from. The past is the baseline. Original underneath, always.

**Thandi:** Last one, and it's the one that costs marks in every single test: taking VAT off. If adding VAT was plus fifteen percent, why can't you just take fifteen percent off to get back?

**Tutor:** Because adding the VAT was never an addition of fifteen percent of the final price — it was a multiplication by 1,15 applied to the smaller, pre-VAT price. Watch the actual rands. The pre-VAT price was R722,50, and the VAT charged on it was fifteen percent of R722,50, which is R108,38. Now try taking fifteen percent off the inclusive price: fifteen percent of R830,88 is R124,63. That is R16,25 more VAT than the shop ever charged, so you strip out too much and land on R706,25 instead of R722,50. The mistake is the same one from the jacket, running in reverse: you took a percentage of the wrong amount. The cure is to undo the operation that was actually performed. A multiplication is undone by a division, so divide the inclusive price by 1,15: R830,88 divided by 1,15 gives R722,50, exactly. Multiply in, divide out — and that rule holds for interest, mark-ups and inflation too.
