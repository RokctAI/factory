# Part 1 — Expert

This session is a full practice run of a National Senior Certificate Paper 1 — Finance and Data Handling, 150 marks — walked question by question at examination pace, the way a marker reads it. Every question carries its mark allocation, and the mark allocation is the instruction: two marks means a calculation and an answer; four marks means show the chain; six marks means the chain has stages and each stage pays. The paper's own architecture never changes, so learn it once and walk in knowing the map.

## Subtopic: How Paper 1 Is Built and How Marks Are Won

Paper 1 carries 150 marks in four or five questions. Question 1 is worth about 30 marks and is Level 1 ONLY — short, direct, knowing-type questions from Finance and Data Handling on a short context. Question 2 is Finance; Question 3 is Data Handling; Questions 4 and 5 integrate the two, and only there may unfamiliar contexts appear. Probability's roughly 7 marks hide inside any of them. Across the whole paper the taxonomy ladder is fixed: about 30 percent knowing, 30 percent routine procedures, 20 percent multi-step, 20 percent reasoning and reflecting.

Reading the allocation is a skill in itself. A two-mark question pays one mark for method and one for the answer. A three-mark question hides a middle step — usually a conversion or a subtotal. Anything from five marks up is a staged journey, and part-marks flow for every correct stage EVEN IF an earlier slip changed your numbers — the memo's consistent-accuracy principle. The strategy that follows: never leave a staged question blank, always show substitution, and carry wrong numbers forward boldly rather than stopping.

## Subtopic: Question 1 Walk-through: Level 1 Warm-ups

Question 1 is the confidence deposit — 30 marks of single-step credit. Four specimens at the pace the real paper expects.

First, for two marks: a school jersey costs R240,00 excluding VAT; calculate the VAT at 15 percent. One line — 240 times 0,15 is R36,00 — and if the question asks the total, R276,00. Second, for two marks: a payslip shows gross salary R15 800,00 and total deductions R3 240,00; calculate the net pay. Subtract: R12 560,00. The knowing mark is knowing WHICH way the arithmetic points — net is what remains.

Third, from the data side, for two marks: the mode of the marks 12, 15, 15, 18, 21 — the value appearing most, 15. And a probability warm-up for two: the probability of rolling a six on one die, written as a fraction — 1 over 6. No simplification drama, no decimals demanded; Level 1 pays for the definition applied cleanly.

The discipline in Question 1 is speed WITHOUT skipped writing: even two-mark answers show the one line of working, because an arithmetic slip with no working forfeits both marks, while the same slip beside visible method usually keeps one.

## Subtopic: Question 2.1: The Municipal Water Bill

Question 2 opens Finance with a tariff table, and this specimen is worth six marks. The municipal water tariff: the first 6 kilolitres each month are free; from 7 to 15 kilolitres, R18,00 per kilolitre; from 16 to 30, R26,00 per kilolitre. The Dlamini household used 22 kilolitres. Calculate the total bill including VAT at 15 percent.

Walk the blocks — each stage is a mark. Block one: 6 kilolitres at no charge, R0,00, and writing that line is itself worth a mark because it proves the block logic. Block two: from 7 to 15 is 9 kilolitres at R18,00 — R162,00. Block three: 22 minus 15 leaves 7 kilolitres at R26,00 — R182,00. Subtotal: 162 plus 182 is R344,00. VAT step: 344 times 1,15 is R395,60. Six marks: the free block, each charged block, the subtotal, the VAT, the final answer with its rand sign and two decimals.

The two mark-killers: charging all 22 kilolitres at R26,00 because 22 falls in the third block's range, and forgetting that the free block still consumes 6 of the 22. Consumption is SPLIT across blocks — the tariff table is a staircase, not a switch.

## Subtopic: Question 2.2: Compound Interest on a Fixed Deposit

The second Finance movement, for four marks: Mrs Mokoena invests R12 000,00 in a fixed deposit at 8,5 percent per year, compounded annually, for three years. Calculate the value at the end of the term.

The safest full-marks route is year by year, because it shows the compounding instead of asserting it. End of year one: 12 000 times 1,085 is R13 020,00. End of year two: 13 020 times 1,085 is R14 126,70. End of year three: 14 126,70 times 1,085 is R15 327,47 — rounded to the cent, stated with its rand sign. Four marks: the multiplier 1,085 built correctly from 8,5 percent, each year's interest applied to the NEW balance, and the final rounding.

The follow-up the paper loves, for two more marks: how much interest did she earn? Final value minus original deposit — 15 327,47 minus 12 000 is R3 327,47. And the Level 4 twin, for three: her friend earned simple interest at the same rate — explain who ends with more and why. Simple interest pays 12 000 times 0,085, which is R1 020,00, every year — R3 060,00 over three years — so compound wins by R267,47, because from year two onward its interest is calculated on a balance that already contains interest. The explanation mark needs that final clause, not just the numbers.

## Subtopic: Question 3: Summarising the Test Marks

Question 3 turns to Data Handling. The context: twelve learners' test percentages, already arranged — 38, 42, 47, 51, 54, 56, 60, 63, 67, 71, 76, 83.

For two marks, the range: 83 minus 38 is 45. For three marks, the mean: the sum is 708, divided by 12 gives exactly 59,0 — one mark for the sum, one for dividing by the correct count, one for the answer. For three marks, the median: twelve is even, so the median averages the sixth and seventh values — 56 and 60 — giving 58,0. The even-count rule IS the question; picking a single middle value forfeits it.

For four marks, the quartiles and interquartile range: the lower half's middle averages the third and fourth values, 47 and 51, so Q1 is 49,0; the upper half mirrors with 67 and 71, so Q3 is 69,0; the interquartile range is 69 minus 49, which is 20 — the spread of the middle half of the class, untouched by the 38 and the 83 at the ends.

Then the reasoning rand, for three marks: the teacher reports the class did well because the mean is 59. Comment, with calculations. The honest answer notes mean 59 and median 58 sit close together, so no outlier is distorting the picture — but a quarter of the class scored below 49, which the single number hides. Marks flow for citing computed values in the argument, not for opinion.

## Subtopic: Question 4: Integrated Finance, Data and Probability

The paper's back end mixes the families and climbs the taxonomy. The integrated specimen: the same twelve learners sell snacks to fund their matric dance, and the question stitches data to money to chance.

Part one, for two marks: a learner is chosen at random to audit the cash tin; what is the probability the chosen learner scored below 50 percent on the test? Count from the data — 38, 42 and 47 sit below 50, so 3 out of 12: 3 over 12, which is 1 over 4, or 0,25, or 25 percent — any dialect earns it unless one is demanded.

Part two, for six marks, the budget: the class buys 200 packets of chips at a wholesale price of R6,50 and sells them at R10,00. Costs: 200 times 6,50 is R1 300,00. Income if all sell: 200 times 10 is R2 000,00. Profit: R700,00. Then the Level 3 twist: only 85 percent of packets sell and the rest are given away. Income becomes 170 packets times 10, which is R1 700,00, and profit falls to R400,00. Each recalculated line pays.

Part three, for four marks, the Level 4 close: the class needs R650,00 profit to cover the venue deposit — advise them, with calculations, whether the 85 percent scenario meets the target, and what selling price would. R400,00 falls R250,00 short; keeping 170 sales, the price must satisfy 170 times price minus 1 300 equals 650, so the price is 1 950 over 170 — 11,4706, rounded UP to R11,48, because rounding down to R11,47 would bank only R1 949,90 and miss the target by ten cents. Advice questions pay for the verdict PLUS the supporting number, never the verdict alone.

## Subtopic: Memo Habits That Harvest Marks

The closing discipline — the habits that decide borderline scripts, all free of new content.

Habit one: SHOW SUBSTITUTION. The memo awards the method mark the moment correct values sit inside a correct structure — 344 times 1,15 written down banks a mark even if the multiplication then slips. Habit two: CONSISTENT ACCURACY is real — a wrong subtotal carried correctly forward keeps paying, so never abandon a staged question at a suspected slip; finish the chain. Habit three: UNITS AND FORM — rand answers take two decimals and the rand sign; the decimal comma is the convention of this subject; percentages declare their percent sign; a probability may live as fraction, decimal or percentage unless the question chooses. Habit four: ROUNDING serves the context — money to the cent, people and packets UP to whole units when buying and to the honest whole when counting, and premature rounding mid-chain is the quiet killer: keep full figures until the final line. Habit five: ANSWER THE VERB — calculate means numbers, show that means arrive at the given value visibly, comment and advise mean a sentence CONTAINING a number. A Paper 1 script that obeys the five habits typically finds ten marks the same knowledge would otherwise leak — the cheapest ten marks in the examination.
