Hey, it's Mandy — Paper 2 is the practical paper, and if Measurement and Maps feel like a pile of formulas, breathe: it's really five habits wearing different clothes.

Habit one: walk the unit ladder, never jump it. Map says 7 centimetres at 1 to 50 000? Multiply first — 350 000 centimetres — then divide by 100, then by 1 000: 3,5 kilometres. Two written divisions beat one guessed one every time.

Habit two: the clock runs on sixties. 4,2 hours is NOT 4 hours 20 — the decimal times 60 gives 12 minutes. Say times sixty out loud every time hours come out with a comma in them.

Habit three: radius before anything. The tank formula wants radius squared, and the paper loves handing you the diameter — halve it first, 1,4 across means 0,7 in the formula, and that halving is the memo's first mark. Then cubic metres times 1 000 lands you in litres, where real questions live.

Habit four: plans speak in centimetre-is-a-metre. 62 millimetres at 1 to 100 is 6,2 metres — and rooms are people-sized, two to eight metres, so a 62-metre bedroom means a dropped zero, caught by you before the marker.

Habit five: probability is three dialects of one number — 1 over 6, about 0,17, about 17 percent — and the complement always tops up to one whole: 30 percent rain means 70 percent dry.

Ladder, sixties, radius, room-sized, dialects. Five habits, most of Paper 2's 150 marks. You've got this!
