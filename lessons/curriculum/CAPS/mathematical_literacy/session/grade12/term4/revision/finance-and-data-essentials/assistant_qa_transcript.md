**Mandy:** Questions from the class before we close! First: the backwards VAT keeps eating marks. Why does dividing by 1,15 work, and why does taking 15 percent off the total fail?

**Tutor:** Build the total from the ground up once and the mystery dissolves. Every inclusive price is the exclusive price times 1,15 — one whole of the original plus fifteen hundredths of the original, glued into a single multiplier. So R287,50 was BORN as 250 times 1,15. To un-multiply, you divide by the same 1,15, and you land exactly back on R250,00. Now watch the wrong route: 15 percent OF R287,50 is R43,13, and subtracting gives R244,37 — too low, and here is the reason in one sentence: the VAT the shop charged was 15 percent of the SMALLER exclusive price, R37,50, not 15 percent of the bigger inclusive one, so the wrong route removes too much. The percentages are not symmetric — adding 15 percent and then removing 15 percent of the new total takes you below where you started, always. The exam habit that follows: before touching any VAT number, label it aloud as inclusive or exclusive. Exclusive travels forward by multiplying; inclusive travels backward by dividing; and the words on the slip — total, incl, excl — are the question's way of telling you which door you are standing at. One labelled line of working, one multiplier used in the correct direction, two marks banked.

**Mandy:** Next: the tax question has a base amount, a percentage of an excess, and then a rebate. Learners lose track of the order. Is there a fixed route through it?

**Tutor:** There is, and it never varies: bracket, excess, base plus percentage, rebate, then months. Step one, find the bracket — run the ANNUAL taxable income down the table until it fits between a bracket's limits; R310 000 lands in the row that reads R42 678 plus 26 percent of the amount above R237 100. Step two, the excess: 310 000 minus 237 100 is R72 900 — this subtraction is what the bracket's percentage applies to, never the whole income, and that is the single most common error in the question. The table has already taxed the first R237 100 inside its base figure of R42 678; only the overflow gets the 26 percent. Step three: 26 percent of 72 900 is R18 954,00, added to the base for R61 632,00. Step four, the rebate — subtract R17 235, because every taxpayer under sixty-five receives that discount, and forgetting it hands back a full mark on a silver tray. Annual tax R44 397,00. Step five, only if asked: divide by twelve for the monthly R3 699,75. Five steps, in that order, every year the paper is set. Write each line separately — the memo cannot give you the excess mark if the excess never appears on your page.

**Mandy:** From the data side: when the paper says comment on the averages, learners freeze. What does a full-marks comment actually contain?

**Tutor:** A full-marks comment is a calculation wearing a sentence, and it has three required parts: a number, a comparison, and a consequence. Take the fridge-door marks — mean 56, median 62, one flu-week disaster of 24 in the set. A freeze answer says the marks are okay, and earns nothing, because it contains no mathematics. The full answer runs: the median of 62 sits well above the mean of 56 — that is the number and the comparison — and the gap exists because the single outlier of 24 drags the mean down while leaving the median's position untouched, so the median better represents typical performance — that is the consequence, with the mechanism named. Notice what made it work: every claim leans on a value you computed, and the direction of the pull is stated, not implied. The same recipe covers box plots — same median, but Class A's interquartile range of 20 is half of Class B's, so A is more consistent — and misleading graphs, where the comment names the flaw, the false impression it creates, and what an honest version would show. If your comment could be written without doing any arithmetic, it is not yet an answer; go back and put a computed number inside the sentence. That single habit converts almost every hesitant Level 4 attempt into marks.
