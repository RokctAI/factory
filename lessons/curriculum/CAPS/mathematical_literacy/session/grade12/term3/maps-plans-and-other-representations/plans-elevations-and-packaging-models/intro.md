Today we read a building off a page — and then pack its boxes. The Grade 12 paper hands you an architect's floor plan and expects you to speak its symbol language, turn plan millimetres into real metres at 1 : 100, and match the flat elevations to the walls they describe from every side. From there we step into three dimensions: exploded assembly diagrams, cartons packed axis by axis, and the material estimates that tell a builder how many tiles and litres of paint to buy — always rounded up to what shops actually sell. It is the map skill wearing a hard hat: the same scale arithmetic, new territory, and a decision at the end of every calculation.
