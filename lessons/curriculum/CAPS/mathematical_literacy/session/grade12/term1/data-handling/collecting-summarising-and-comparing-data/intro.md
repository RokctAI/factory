Today we are collecting, summarising and comparing data — the discipline that settles South Africa's daily arguments about numbers honestly. Our investigation asks how long learners travel to school each morning, with two schools answering: one deep rural in the Eastern Cape, one urban in Gqeberha. We move from the question and the fair sample, through frequency tables, mean, median, quartiles and percentiles, to the box-and-whisker plots that put both schools on one scale — where a single average would hide the real story.
