Hey, it's Mandy — the body measurement section scares people because it feels medical, but underneath the white coat it is the same maths you have used all year: one division, one chart, one rate. Let me shrink it for you.

BMI first. The formula is given on the paper: mass divided by height squared. Your only real job is the units — height must be in METRES. See 168 on the clinic wall? Slide the comma: 1,68, before it touches the formula. Then square, then divide: 82 over 2,8224 gives 29,1. Now the two easy marks people leave behind: name the category from the table — 29,1 is overweight — and if asked, say the limitation: BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so it screens, it does not diagnose. And keep the alarm bell installed: human BMIs live between about 15 and 45. An answer like 0,003 is not a result, it is the calculator reporting a unit crime.

Growth charts are a queue of one hundred babies. The percentile is your baby's position in the queue — 50th is dead centre, 15th is small and can be perfectly healthy. Age across, mass up, dot where they meet. The examinable insight: a healthy baby TRACKS her own curve; the worry is crossing lanes downward across visits. Journey, not address.

Dosage is two steps and a safety rule. Rate times weight: 15 mg per kilogram times 18 kg is 270 mg. Label converts to syrup: 120 mg per 5 ml means 24 mg per ml, so 270 over 24 gives 11,25 ml. Doses per bottle round DOWN — 8,88 means 8, because a short dose is not a dose. Never double after a missed one.

Comma across for metres, queue position for percentiles, rate then label for doses, and round toward safety. You've got this!
