Hey, it's Mandy — maps in Grade 12 look like Grade 11 maps that went to gym: bigger scales, gradients, choosing your own scale. But every question still runs on the same three-step engine you already own: measure, multiply, tidy.

First habit, before the ruler moves: price one centimetre. The scale says 1 : 2 000 000 — breathe, convert once, every centimetre is 20 km. Now the whole map is a times table. Paper to ground, multiply: 28,5 cm becomes 570 km. Ground to paper, divide: 300 km becomes 15 cm. If your answer says a page distance is thousands of centimetres, you swapped the operations — the sense test catches it free of charge.

Making your own scale is one honest fraction: drawing over real, SAME units, tidied to 1 : something. Bakkie drawing 9 cm, real bakkie 450 cm — 1 : 50, done. Choosing a scale? Pick the round number that makes the model as big as the space allows, then SHOW the check: 30 m hall at 1 : 50 is 60 cm — fits the 80 cm board. The checking line is where the mark lives.

Directions: compass words belong to the world — Durban is south-east of Johannesburg, and swapping towns reverses the word. Left and right belong to your body and turn around when you do. Orient the map first, answer second.

And gradient, the new star: climb over distance, written 1 : n. One hundred metres up over two thousand along is 1 : 20. The trap has one cure — say the ratio as a sentence: a metre up every five steps beats a metre up every twenty. SMALLER second number, STEEPER path. Picture the 1 : 5 ladder standing almost upright.

Price the centimetre, one honest fraction, orient then answer, say the gradient aloud. Four habits, full marks. You've got this!
