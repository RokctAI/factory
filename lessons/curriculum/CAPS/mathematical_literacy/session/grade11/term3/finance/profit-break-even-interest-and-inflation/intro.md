Today we run a small business through its four money questions: what each item costs to make, what it sells for, how many must be sold before the venture stops losing, and what happens to the money afterwards in the bank and against rising prices. Our venture is a vetkoek stall at a school fundraiser — cost price, selling price and percentage profit on the right base, the break-even point rounded up with a reason, compound interest built year by year in a table, and inflation growing the ingredient basket while the deposit grows the savings. These four calculations anchor the finance questions of Paper 1, and each ends in a sentence a committee could act on.
