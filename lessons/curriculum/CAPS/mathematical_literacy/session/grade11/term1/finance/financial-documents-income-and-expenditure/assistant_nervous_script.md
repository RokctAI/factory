Hey, it's Bianca — finance questions look like a pile of scary paperwork, but every document in this topic is built from the same three moves, and none of them is new maths. It is multiply, add, and fifteen percent. That is the whole engine.

The municipal bill: what you used, times the price per unit, plus the fixed basic charge, plus VAT at fifteen percent on the lot. Work down the page in that order every time and the bill assembles itself. If the paper hands you the total WITH VAT and wants the amount before, divide by 1,15 — never take fifteen percent off, that lands in the wrong place every time.

Income and expenses: just sort the drawers. Fixed means the amount is promised — salary, rent, stokvel. Variable means the amount moves — wages, groceries, taxi fare. Occasional means no schedule — uniforms, gifts, funerals. Label with a reason, total honestly, and say surplus or deficit in a sentence. That sentence is a mark.

And simple interest, the one everyone fears: principal, times the rate as a decimal, times the years. R8 000 at twelve percent for three years is 8 000 times 0,12 times 3 — R2 880 — and the loan truly costs R10 880. Three numbers multiplied, then added back on. That is all simple interest has ever been.

Your mark-protecting habit: convert before you multiply. Percent to decimal, months to years. Twelve percent is 0,12; eighteen months is 1,5. Nearly every lost mark in this topic is a conversion skipped, not maths misunderstood.

Multiply, add, fifteen percent, and finish with a sentence in rands. You've got this!
