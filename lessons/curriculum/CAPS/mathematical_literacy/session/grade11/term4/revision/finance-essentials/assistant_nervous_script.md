Hey, it's Bianca — a whole year of finance looks like a mountain, but it is really five small machines, and you already know how to drive each one.

The payslip machine: gross minus named deductions equals net. UIF is 1 percent — slide the decimal, R98 on R9 800. Pension 7,5 percent — 0,075 times gross. Show the subtraction line; the memo pays for the path, not just the answer.

The VAT machine has two gears. Forward: times 1,15. Reverse — when the price INCLUDES VAT: divide by 1,15, never subtract 15 percent. And remember the zero-rated basics: brown bread, maize meal, rice — sort them out before applying VAT to a basket.

The tariff machine climbs steps: first block at its rate, the rest at theirs. 100 units at R1,80 is R180; the next 140 at R2,60 is R364; total R544. Never one rate times everything.

The exchange machine: the rate is just a price per dollar. Multiply to buy dollars into rand, divide to turn rand into dollars, and check that the rand number is the big one.

The slow-money machine: break-even is fixed cost over contribution — R1 500 over R12 is 125 sales. Compound interest grows year by year in a table — R5 000 at 10 percent runs 5 500, 6 050, 6 655. Inflation is the same table working against your wallet.

Five machines, one habit: end with the sentence that answers the question. Now go drive them through the practice set. You've got this!
