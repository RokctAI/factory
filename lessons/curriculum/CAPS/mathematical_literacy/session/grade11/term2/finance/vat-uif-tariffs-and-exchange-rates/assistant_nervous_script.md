Hey, it's Bianca — this topic looks like four different monsters, but VAT, UIF, exchange rates and tariffs are all the same small animal: a percentage or a rate, applied in the right direction. Direction is the whole game.

VAT: going up, times 1,15. Coming back down, divide by 1,15 — never subtract fifteen percent, because the inclusive price is 115% of the original, and fifteen percent of the wrong number is the classic lost mark. Say it before every VAT question: is the fifteen already inside this price?

UIF: one percent, the friendliest percentage alive. Slide the comma two places — R8 400 gross becomes R84. The worker pays it, the employer matches it, the fund gets both. Read whether the question wants the worker's side, the employer's side, or the total, because all three numbers will be waiting in the options.

Exchange rates: say the rate as a price. One dollar costs R18,50. Buying dollars with rands? Divide. Turning dollars into rands? Multiply. Then run the size check: rands into dollars must shrink, dollars into rands must grow. If your answer breaks that rule, you picked the wrong direction — fix it before the marker sees.

Tariffs: two deals, two formulae, test both at the given usage, pick the smaller, and say the saving in a sentence. The deal with the monthly fee wins for heavy users, pay-as-you-go wins for light users, and they swap at the crossing point.

One rate, one direction, one honest sentence at the end. You've got this!
