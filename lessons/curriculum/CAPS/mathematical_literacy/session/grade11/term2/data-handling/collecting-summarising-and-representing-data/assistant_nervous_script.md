Hey, it's Bianca — data handling looks like a mountain of words, but the whole topic is one story told five times: ask, collect, sort, squeeze, draw. Walk the story in order and every question finds its place.

Ask: the question must name a group, a quantity and a unit — rand per learner per week, not "too much airtime". Collect: population is everyone, sample is the stand-ins, and the only good sample is one chance picked — every twentieth name on the register, not your friends at break. When they say "identify the bias", answer in three parts: who got over-counted, why the method did it, and which way the answer tilts.

Sort: tally into bins that say "to under" — R20 to under R40, next bin starts AT R40 — so nobody lives in two houses. Then add the frequencies and make sure they hit the total. That little addition is a free mark, every time.

Squeeze: three middles and a spread. Mean is share-out — total divided by count. Median is the queue — sort first, tap the middle. Mode is the favourite. Range is biggest minus smallest. And when mean and median argue, point at the outlier: the mean shook hands with the R90, the median never met it.

Draw: bars compare, lines trend, scattered dots reveal partnerships. Before believing any graph, check where the axis starts — an axis starting at R35 turns a R4 gap into a skyscraper. Quote the real numbers and the picture loses its power.

Ask, collect, sort, squeeze, draw — and a reason in words at every step. You've got this!
