Hey, it's Bianca — probability feels like gambling with marks, but it is the most predictable topic in the paper. Every question is one of four moves, and you can name the move before you touch the numbers.

Move one: counting chances. Ways to win, over ways in total — three even faces out of six, one WIN slice out of eight. Keep the answer between 0 and 1, and remember the ruler: 0 never, 1 definitely, 0,5 coin-toss country. If your answer is 1,2, it is not a strong chance, it is a broken fraction — fix the denominator.

Move two: watching. No symmetry, no formula — taxis, rain, load shedding get counted instead: full on 12 of 40 mornings is 0,3, and it is an ESTIMATE, one that gets truer the longer you watch. Coins get theory; taxis get tallies. Say which one you used.

Move three: the tree, for one-thing-then-another. Branch, branch again, multiply along the path. The tree's whole job is catching the path you forget in your head — tails-heads, the missing twin of heads-tails. Draw it every time; the drawing itself earns marks. And when you hear "at least one", flip it: 1 minus the probability of NONE. One line instead of three branches.

Move four: the grid, for crowds with two labels. Fill the cells, add the totals, check they agree. Then the words choose your denominator: "a random visitor" means the grand total; "given that" or "of the learners" shrinks the world to one row. Underline the phrase, ask who is still in the room, divide.

Count, watch, branch, or grid — name the move, draw the picture, keep it between 0 and 1. You've got this!
