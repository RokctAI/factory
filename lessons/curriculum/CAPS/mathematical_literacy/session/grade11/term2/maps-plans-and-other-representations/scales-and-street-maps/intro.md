Today we are reading maps the way the examination requires: the number scale that shrinks the world by an exact ratio, the bar scale that survives a photocopier, the grid and compass that find and describe a place, and the chain that turns centimetres on paper into kilometres, minutes and petrol money. We will work with two maps of one journey — a street map for the taxi rank and clinic, and a road map for the trip to Tzaneen — and finish by deciding, in rands, whether the car or the taxi wins. Every step of measure, multiply, convert and compare carries its own mark.
