## Question (8 marks)

Muriel Davids (42 years old) earns a gross salary of R35 000 per month; she does not contribute to a pension fund, so her gross salary is her taxable income. She pays medical aid for herself only and receives no bonus. Her monthly PAYE deduction, read from the deduction table in ANNEXURE A, is R6 298. The 2024/2025 annual tax rates in ANNEXURE A give her bracket as R77 362 + 31% of taxable income above R370 500, a primary rebate of R17 235 and a monthly medical tax credit of R364 for the taxpayer. (c) Muriel stated that she overpaid tax in the 2024/2025 tax year. Verify, showing ALL calculations, whether her statement is VALID.

## Method

income tax with rebate and medical credit verification

## Memo working

First annualise the income: Annual taxable income = R35 000 × 12 = R420 000 (1A). Apply the tax bracket rule: Annual tax payable before rebates = R77 362 + 31% of (R420 000 - R370 500) (1MCA correct tax bracket) = R77 362 + 31% of R49 500 = R77 362 + R15 345 = R92 707,00 (1CA). Now subtract the primary rebate and a year of medical tax credits: Annual tax payable after rebates = R92 707,00 - R17 235 (1RT correct tax rebate) - (R364 × 12) = R92 707,00 - R17 235 - R4 368 (1MA subtracting MTC) = R71 104,00 (1CA). Convert to a monthly amount: Monthly tax payable = R71 104,00 ÷ 12 = R5 925,33 — but R6 298 was deducted each month. (Equivalently: annual tax actually deducted = R6 298 × 12 = R75 576, against R71 104,00 actually payable; 1CA tax amount, CA from Question 2.1.4(a).) She paid more than she owed, so her statement is VALID (1O conclusion). (The bracket values, rebate and medical tax credit are as printed in the memo; the annexure itself sits in the Special Answer Book. Arithmetic verified: R75 576 deducted vs R71 104 payable.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Annual taxable income = R420 000; annual tax payable after rebates = R71 104,00; monthly tax payable = R5 925,33 (or annual PAYE deducted = R6 298 × 12 = R75 576). Her statement is VALID.
