## Question (3 marks)

John and five other family members (a family of six) are camping at Baviaanskloof Uitspan. ANNEXURE A lists the 13 huts on the site with the number of campers each can hold (1: 6; 2A: 15, 2B: 4; 3A: 15, 3B: 4, 3C: 4; 4: 5; 5A: 15, 5B: 8; 6: 10; 7: 4-6; 8: 4-6; 9: 20). Calculate, as a percentage, the probability of John and his family randomly selecting a hut that can exactly accommodate the whole family.

## Method

probability as percentage

## Memo working

Probability = favourable outcomes over total outcomes. The family is 6 people (John plus five others). Count the huts that can accommodate exactly 6 campers: hut 1 takes 6, and huts 7 and 8 each take up to 6 (their range is 4 to 6) — that is 3 favourable huts out of 13 huts in total. So P = 3/13, and as a percentage: 3/13 x 100% = 23,076923...% which is about 23,08%. (Memo: 1A correct numerator; 1A correct denominator; 1CA simplification; no penalty for rounding.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

23,076923% (≈ 23,08%)
