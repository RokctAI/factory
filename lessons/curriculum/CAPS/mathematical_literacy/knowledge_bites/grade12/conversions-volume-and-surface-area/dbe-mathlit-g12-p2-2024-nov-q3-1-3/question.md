## Question (5 marks)

Mrs Kgomo stores classroom pillows in zip-up storage bags decorated with piping — thin cord covered in fabric used to decorate the bag and reinforce the seams (each bag is 46 cm long, 30 cm wide and 48 cm high). The piping around the bottom edge of some of the bags for the pillows needs to be replaced. The store sells the piping in half-metre lengths. Calculate the total length of piping she will have to purchase to replace the piping around the bottom edges of FOUR bags. You may use the following formula: Perimeter = 2 (length + width)

## Method

perimeter scaling and rounding

## Memo working

The piping runs around the bottom edge of the bag, so work out the perimeter of the base: Perimeter = 2(46 cm + 30 cm) = 2(76 cm) = 152 cm. Four bags need 152 x 4 = 608 cm, which is 6,08 m. Because the store only sells half-metre lengths, she cannot buy exactly 6,08 m — she must round up to the next half metre and buy 6,5 m. (Memo alternatives: convert to metres first, 2(0,46 + 0,3) = 1,52 m, x 4 = 6,08 m, buy 6,5 m; or count half-metre pieces, 608 cm ÷ 50 cm = 12,16, rounded up to 13 half-metre lengths.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

6,5 m (i.e. 13 half-metre lengths; 4 bags need 6,08 m)
