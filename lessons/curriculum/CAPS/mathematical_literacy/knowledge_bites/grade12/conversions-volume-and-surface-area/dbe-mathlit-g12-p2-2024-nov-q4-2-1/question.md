## Question (5 marks)

A worker at the oil refinery intends converting her double-door garage into a bigger living room by building double-bricked walls to replace both of the doors (the brick-laying diagram shows the bottom row of one wall face with 9 full bricks and a half brick, the side view shows the wall is 20 rows high, and the double-brick wall is two brick thicknesses). NOTE: 525 bricks per pallet at a price of R6,45 per brick. Bricks are sold per pallet. Half bricks are thrown away (not re-used). Determine the total number of pallets of bricks needed to replace both garage doors.

## Method

brick count and pallet rounding

## Memo working

Because the wall is double-bricked, each row of the double wall takes 19 full bricks (the half bricks are thrown away). The wall is 20 rows high, so one garage door needs 19 x 20 = 380 bricks. Both doors need 380 x 2 = 760 bricks. A pallet holds 525 bricks, and bricks are only sold per pallet, so she needs 2 pallets. (Memo alternative: count a single line of the wall as 20 rows x 10 bricks = 200 bricks, double line = 400, both doors = 800 bricks — still 2 pallets. The two memo routes count 760 or 800 bricks; both round up to 2 pallets.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

2 pallets (760-800 bricks needed)
