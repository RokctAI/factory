## Question (9 marks)

Mrs Kgomo paints the front surface area of her circular wooden birthday tags every year (each tag is a circle 8 cm high and 8 cm wide with a small circular hole at the top for hanging; the area of the circular hole is 0,3142 cm^2; the spread rate of the paint is 6 m^2/l). The hardware store sells paint in 50 ml, 1 litre, 5 litre and 20 litre containers. Mrs Kgomo claims that a 50 ml can of paint will be sufficient to paint ONE side of 36 circular wooden tags. Verify her statement. Show ALL calculations. Use the following formula: Area of a circle = 3,142 x radius^2

## Method

area paint spread rate verification

## Memo working

The radius of a tag is half of 8 cm, so 4 cm. Area of a circle = 3,142 x (4 cm)^2 = 3,142 x 16 cm^2 = 50,272 cm^2. The hole is not painted, so the painted area per tag is 50,272 - 0,3142 = 49,9578 cm^2. For 36 tags the total area is 36 x 49,9578 cm^2 = 1 798,4808 cm^2. Convert to square metres by dividing by 100^2 (that is, by 10 000): 1 798,4808 ÷ 10 000 = 0,17984808 m^2. The spread rate says 1 litre (1 000 ml) covers 6 m^2, so 50 ml covers 0,3 m^2. Since 0,3 m^2 is more than the 0,17984808 m^2 needed, 50 ml is more than sufficient. (Memo alternative: paint needed = 0,17984808 ÷ 6 = 0,02997466 l, which is about 30 ml — less than 50 ml.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Area per tag = 3,142 x 4^2 = 50,272 cm^2; minus hole = 49,9578 cm^2; 36 tags = 1 798,4808 cm^2 = 0,17984808 m^2; paint needed = 0,17984808/6 l ≈ 30 ml, which is less than 50 ml. Her statement is VALID (50 ml is more than sufficient).
