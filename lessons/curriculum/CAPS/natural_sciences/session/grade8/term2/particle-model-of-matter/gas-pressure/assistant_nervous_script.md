Hey — if gas pressure felt invisible and confusing, here is the calm version: it is just particles crashing into walls.

Pressure means force on each bit of area. Force divided by area, measured in pascals.

Gas particles fly about very fast in all directions. Every time one hits the wall of its container, it gives a tiny push. Billions of billions of those pushes every second add up to gas pressure. Because they come from every direction, the pressure pushes equally every way.

The air around us presses on everything, about 101 kilopascals at the coast and less up in Johannesburg. That is what crushes a steam-filled can when it is cooled, and what pushes a drink up a straw.

Three ways to raise gas pressure. More particles, like pumping a tyre. Hotter particles, which move faster, like an aerosol can in the sun. Less space, like pushing a blocked syringe.

For any explain question, talk about how often and how hard the particles hit the walls.
