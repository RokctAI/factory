Today we explain why a kilogram of bricks fits in a shoebox while a kilogram of feathers fills a wardrobe. The difference is density, the mass packed into each unit of volume, calculated as mass divided by volume and measured in grams per cubic centimetre or kilograms per cubic metre. We learn to find the volume of a regular block by measuring and of an irregular stone by water displacement, and work through calculations of density, mass and volume. We use the particle model to explain why gases are far less dense than solids and liquids, why ice floats and why heating lowers density. Finally we use one rule, compare the average density of an object with that of the fluid, to explain floating and sinking, from a steel ship at Saldanha Bay to a helium balloon.
