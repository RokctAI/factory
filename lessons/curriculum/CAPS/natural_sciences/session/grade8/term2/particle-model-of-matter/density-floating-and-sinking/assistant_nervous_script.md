Hey — if density sounded like hard maths, here is the calm version: it is just how tightly mass is packed into space.

The formula is density equals mass divided by volume. Mass in grams, volume in cubic centimetres, answer in grams per cubic centimetre. Water is one, so it makes a handy comparison.

To find volume, use a ruler for a box: length times breadth times height. For a stone, drop it into a measuring cylinder and see how far the water rises.

Size does not matter. Half a brick is exactly as dense as a whole brick.

Why some things are denser: heavier particles, or particles packed closer together. Gases are spread out, so they are hardly dense at all. Heat things up and they spread out, so they get less dense.

The floating rule: less dense than the liquid, it floats; more dense, it sinks. A steel ship floats because it is full of air, which lowers its average density.

For sums, write the formula, substitute with units, and give the unit in the answer.
