Today we explain why almost everything grows when it is heated and shrinks when it is cooled. A brass ball slips through a ring when cold, sticks on top when hot, and drops through again as it cools, with nothing added or taken away. We use the particle model to show that heating makes particles move faster and push further apart, while the particles themselves stay the same size and the mass stays the same, so the density falls. We compare how much solids, liquids and gases expand and why, and meet the strange behaviour of water between zero and four degrees. We look at the problems expansion causes for railway lines, bridges, power lines and glassware, and at the ways it is put to work in thermometers, bimetallic strips and shrink fits.
