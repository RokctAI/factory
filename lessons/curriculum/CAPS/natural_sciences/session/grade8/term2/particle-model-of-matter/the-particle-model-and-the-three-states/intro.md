Today we meet the most useful idea in physical science, the particle model of matter. We start with a puzzle: 50 millilitres of water mixed with 50 millilitres of ethanol makes only about 96 millilitres, and nothing has spilled. The explanation is that all matter is made of tiny particles with empty space between them, always moving and attracting one another. We look at the evidence, from sugar vanishing into a full glass to the endless jiggling of Brownian motion. Then we use the model to describe the three states. In a solid the particles are packed in a regular pattern and vibrate in place, held by strong forces. In a liquid they stay close but slide past one another. In a gas they are far apart, moving fast and randomly with very weak forces. We explain everyday properties with arrangement, movement and forces, and learn to draw all three states correctly.
