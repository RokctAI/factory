Today we explain what happens when matter changes from one state to another. Heat a beaker of crushed ice steadily and watch the thermometer: it climbs to zero, then waits there for minutes while the ice melts, climbs again to one hundred, and waits again while the water boils. We use the particle model to explain melting and freezing, evaporation and boiling, condensation, sublimation and deposition, and why energy is absorbed in some changes and released in others. We see why evaporation cools you, why water boils at about ninety-four degrees in Johannesburg, and why steam burns are so severe. We draw and read the heating curve of water, explain its flat sections, and finish by showing that every change of state is a physical change in which mass is conserved.
