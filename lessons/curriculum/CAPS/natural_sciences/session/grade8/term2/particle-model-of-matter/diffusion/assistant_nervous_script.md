Hey — if diffusion sounded complicated, here is the calm version: crowded to empty, by random wandering.

Particles are always moving in random directions. Where there are lots of them crowded together, more of them happen to wander out than wander in. So they spread from high concentration to low concentration. Nobody pushes them; there is no wind or stirring. That spreading is diffusion.

When they are evenly spread, they keep moving, but as many go one way as the other, so nothing changes any more.

Speed. Gases diffuse fastest, liquids much slower, solids hardly at all. Hotter means faster, because particles move faster. Lighter particles are faster too, which is why ammonia wins the race in the tube experiment.

Why it matters. In your lungs, oxygen diffuses from the air into your blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses out. In leaves, carbon dioxide diffuses in through tiny holes.

For any explain question, say three things: particles move randomly, more go from high to low, until evenly spread.
