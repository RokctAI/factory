Hey — if the particle model felt abstract, here is the calm version: four rules and a school day.

The four rules. Everything is made of tiny particles. There is empty space between them, not air, just nothing. They are always moving. And they pull on each other, more strongly when they are close.

Now the school day. Assembly is a solid: everyone in neat rows, close together, only fidgeting on the spot. Strong forces, fixed shape, fixed size, cannot be squashed.

Break in a crowded corridor is a liquid: still close, but no rows, sliding past each other. Weaker forces. It keeps its amount but flows and takes the shape of its container.

Home time on the field is a gas: everyone running in every direction, far apart. Very weak forces. It fills any space and can be squashed.

For any explain question, say three things in order: how the particles are arranged, how they move, how strong the forces are. Then the property.

And when you draw them, keep the circles the same size every time. Only the spacing changes.
