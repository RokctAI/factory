Today we explain the invisible push of a gas. Boil a little water in an empty cooldrink can, flip it into cold water, and it is crushed flat in an instant by nothing more than the air of the room. We start with pressure itself, force divided by area, measured in pascals, and see why knives cut and camels do not sink. We use the particle model to explain gas pressure as the result of countless particles colliding with the walls of their container, equally in all directions. We meet atmospheric pressure, about 101 kilopascals at sea level and less in Johannesburg, and use it to explain the crushed can and the drinking straw. Finally we show that gas pressure rises with more particles, a higher temperature or a smaller volume, and why aerosol cans must never be heated.
