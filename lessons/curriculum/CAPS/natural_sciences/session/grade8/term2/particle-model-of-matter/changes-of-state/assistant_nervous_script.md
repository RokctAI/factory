Hey — if changes of state felt like a lot of names, here is the calm version: energy changes how tightly the particles are held, never the particles themselves.

Add energy and particles break loose. Solid to liquid is melting. Liquid to gas is evaporation, from the surface at any temperature, or boiling, all through the liquid at the boiling point. Solid straight to gas is sublimation, like dry ice.

Take energy away and particles are pulled back together. Liquid to solid is freezing. Gas to liquid is condensation, like drops on a cold glass. Gas straight to solid is deposition, like frost.

For water, melting and freezing happen at zero degrees, and boiling at one hundred degrees at sea level, a bit lower up in Johannesburg.

The big trick: while the state is changing, the temperature stays flat. The energy is busy pulling particles apart, not speeding them up. That gives the flat bits on the heating curve.

And it is always a physical change. Ice, water and steam are all water, and the mass stays the same.
