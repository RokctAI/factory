Hey — if expansion felt like a list of random facts, here is the calm version: heat spreads the particles out, cold pulls them in.

Heat something and its particles get more energy. They move or jiggle harder and push further apart. The object gets bigger. That is expansion. Cool it and they settle closer together. That is contraction.

The particles never grow. Only the gaps change. No particles are added, so the mass stays the same, and the density goes down when heated.

Gases expand most, liquids less, solids least, because the forces holding the particles are weakest in gases.

Problems: rails need gaps, bridges need expansion joints, power lines need to sag, and bottles are not filled to the top.

Uses: a thermometer's liquid expands up a thin tube. A bimetallic strip bends because one metal expands more, which switches a kettle or iron off. A hot jar lid loosens.

Odd one out: water shrinks as it warms from zero to four degrees.
