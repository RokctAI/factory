Today we go looking for the smallest piece of a substance. Take a cube of gold and keep cutting it in half: after only about twenty-five cuts you reach a single gold atom, and the next piece would not be gold at all. We follow the idea of the atom from Democritus, who guessed at uncuttable particles about 2 400 years ago, to John Dalton, who in 1803 turned the guess into science with measurements, and we see which parts of his theory still stand. We try to picture the scale: an atom is about a tenth of a nanometre across, so a full stop is about five million atoms wide, and only special microscopes can image them. Then the key link: an element is a substance made of only one kind of atom. Finally the alphabet of matter, about ninety natural kinds of atom combining like letters into millions of substances, rearranged but never destroyed.
