Hey — if making and breaking compounds felt like a lot of reactions to remember, here is the calm version: build, recipe, break.

Build. Elements react to make a compound. Magnesium plus oxygen gives magnesium oxide. Reactants on the left, an arrow, products on the right. Signs it really happened: a new substance, a colour change, gas, heat or light, and you cannot easily undo it.

Recipe. A compound always uses the same ratio. Magnesium oxide is 3 grams of magnesium for every 2 grams of oxygen. Too much of one ingredient? The extra is left over. And the total mass stays the same, because atoms are never made or destroyed. That is why the burnt ribbon gets heavier: it picked up oxygen.

Break. Two ways. Heat: green copper carbonate goes black and gives off carbon dioxide, which turns limewater milky. Electricity: water splits into hydrogen at the negative side and oxygen at the positive side, twice as much hydrogen.

The tests: limewater milky for carbon dioxide, a pop for hydrogen, a glowing splint relighting for oxygen.
