Today we separate mixtures, and the whole lesson rests on one rule: the parts of a mixture are not chemically joined, so find a property in which they differ and use it. We start with the language of mixtures, solutions, solutes and solvents, and plan multi-step separations in the right order. Then the toolbox: sieves and filters for size, magnets for iron, settling and decanting for density, dissolving and filtering for solubility, evaporation and crystallisation to recover a dissolved solid, distillation to recover a pure liquid, fractional distillation for liquids with different boiling points, the separating funnel for oil and water, and paper chromatography for the dyes in an ink. Finally we follow muddy river water through screens, settling tanks, sand filters and chlorine to a clean tap, and see separation at work in gold panning, diamond grease tables, recycling plants and the blood centrifuge.
