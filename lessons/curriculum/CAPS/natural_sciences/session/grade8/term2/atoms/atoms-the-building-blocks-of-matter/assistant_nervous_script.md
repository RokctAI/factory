Hey — if atoms felt too small and strange to get hold of, here is the calm version: a cube, a scale and an alphabet.

The cube. Cut gold in half, again and again. After about twenty-five cuts you reach one gold atom, the smallest piece that is still gold. Democritus guessed this long ago and called it atomos, uncuttable. Dalton proved it in 1803 with measurements.

The scale. An atom is about a tenth of a nanometre wide. A full stop is about five million atoms across. That is why no ordinary microscope can see one; only special modern microscopes can map them.

The alphabet. An element is made of only one kind of atom, like a word made of one repeated letter. About ninety natural kinds of atom join together in different ways to make everything, the way twenty-six letters make every word. Your body is mostly oxygen, carbon, hydrogen and nitrogen.

One rule to carry everywhere: in chemical reactions, atoms are rearranged. Never created, never destroyed, never turned into a different element.
