Today we make compounds and then take them apart. A strip of magnesium burns with a dazzling white light and leaves a white powder that weighs more than the metal did, because the magnesium has combined with oxygen from the air: magnesium plus oxygen gives magnesium oxide. We learn to write word equations, to recognise the signs of a chemical reaction, and to use the fixed ratio in which elements always combine, so that 3 grams of magnesium always take 2 grams of oxygen and any extra reactant is simply left over. Then we reverse the process. Strong heat decomposes green copper carbonate into black copper oxide and carbon dioxide, which turns limewater milky, and turns limestone into quicklime. An electric current decomposes water into hydrogen and oxygen in a two-to-one ratio, the method industry uses to extract aluminium. We finish with the pop test, the glowing splint and the error museum.
