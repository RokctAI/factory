Hey — if the inside of the atom felt like a lot of particles and dates, here is the calm version: three particles, three facts each, and one balance.

Protons: positive, heavy, in the nucleus. Neutrons: no charge, just as heavy, also in the nucleus. Electrons: negative, almost weightless, outside the nucleus, moving around it.

The nucleus is tiny but holds nearly all the mass. Picture a grain of sand on the centre spot of a stadium; the electrons are out in the stands, and everything between is empty. That is what the gold foil experiment showed when almost every particle went straight through.

The balance: an atom has as many electrons as protons, so it is neutral. That gives you the counting trick. Atomic number gives protons, and electrons too. Mass number gives protons plus neutrons. So neutrons are mass number minus atomic number. Sodium, 11 and 23: 11 protons, 11 electrons, 12 neutrons.

To draw it, nucleus in the middle, then rings holding two, eight and eight electrons.
