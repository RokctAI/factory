Today we open the atom. In 1909 scientists fired tiny positive particles at gold foil; almost all passed straight through, but about one in eight thousand bounced back. From that surprise came the nuclear model: an atom is mostly empty space, with a tiny, dense, positive nucleus at its centre. We follow the discoveries of the electron in 1897, the nucleus in 1911 and the neutron in 1932, and describe each sub-atomic particle by its charge, its mass and where it is found: positive protons and neutral neutrons in the nucleus, and very light, negative electrons moving around it. We picture the scale, a grain of sand in a stadium. We see why every atom is neutral, with as many electrons as protons, and use the atomic number and mass number to count all three particles. Finally we draw simple models of atoms with their electrons in shells of two, eight and eight.
