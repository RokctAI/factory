Today we sort all matter into its basic kinds. A salt shaker is our starting point: table salt is made from sodium, a metal that bursts into flame on water, and chlorine, a poisonous gas, yet it is safe to eat. We begin by separating pure substances, with only one kind of particle and sharp melting points, from mixtures such as air and sea water. Pure substances come in two kinds. Elements contain one kind of atom, sometimes as single atoms like helium, sometimes as molecules like the seven diatomic elements, oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen and the halogens, and sometimes in large lattices like metals. Compounds contain two or more elements chemically joined in a fixed ratio, such as water, carbon dioxide and methane, and have completely new properties. We finish by reading chemical formulas, counting atoms with subscripts and coefficients, and classifying particle diagrams in two quick questions.
