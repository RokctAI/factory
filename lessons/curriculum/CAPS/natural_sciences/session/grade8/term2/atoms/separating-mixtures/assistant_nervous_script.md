Hey — if separating mixtures felt like a long list of methods, here is the calm version: one rule and five differences.

The rule. The parts of a mixture are not stuck together chemically, so you never need a reaction. Just find what is different about them and use it.

Different size? Sieve it or filter it. In filtering, what stays on the paper is the residue, and what drips through is the filtrate. Dissolved salt slips through, because it is too small to catch.

Magnetic? Use a magnet.

Different heaviness? Let it settle and pour off the top, or swirl it like a gold panner.

Does one part dissolve? Add water, then filter.

Different boiling point? Boil off the water to keep the salt, which is evaporation. Or catch the steam in a cold tube to keep the water, which is distillation.

And the order matters. Sand, salt and iron: magnet first, then water, then filter, then evaporate.
