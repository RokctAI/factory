Hey — if elements, compounds and mixtures all blur together, here is the calm version: two questions sort everything.

Question one: how many kinds of particle are there? One kind means a pure substance. More than one kind, not joined, means a mixture, like air or a fruit salad. And in science, pure just means one substance, not clean or healthy.

Question two, for pure substances: how many kinds of atom are in each particle? One kind means an element. Oxygen gas is pairs of oxygen atoms, O2, and it is still an element, because only oxygen is there. Two or more kinds joined together means a compound, like water, H2O.

A compound has a fixed recipe and a new personality. Sodium and chlorine are dangerous; salt is safe. Change the recipe and you change the compound: CO is poison, CO2 is what you breathe out.

Formulas are counting. The small number after a symbol counts that atom. The big number in front counts molecules. 2H2O is two water molecules, six atoms.
