Today we find chemical reactions in traditional knowledge from southern Africa. A clay pot of umqombothi, made from sprouted sorghum and maize porridge, bubbles and turns sour and mildly alcoholic over a few days, with nothing added: living yeasts are carrying out a chemical reaction. We explain alcoholic fermentation, glucose producing ethanol and carbon dioxide, and why the sorghum is malted first. We see how lactic acid bacteria turn milk into amasi and porridge into mageu, preserving food without refrigeration. We follow early iron smelters as charcoal and goatskin bellows remove oxygen from iron ore in clay furnaces. Finally we explain soap from fat and wood ash, leather from hide and bark, and fired clay pots, as reactions that rearrange atoms into useful new substances.
