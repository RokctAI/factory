Hey — if chemical reactions felt like magic, here is the calm version: same atoms, new partners.

The substances you start with are reactants. The new substances made are products. Write them as a word equation: reactants, arrow, products. The arrow means produces.

Inside a reaction, the bonds holding the atoms together break, and the atoms join up in new ways. Burning methane: one carbon and four hydrogens meet two pairs of oxygens, and become one carbon dioxide and two waters. Count them: the same atoms on both sides.

Breaking bonds needs energy, which is why a stove needs a spark. Making bonds releases energy. If more comes out than goes in, the reaction gets hot: exothermic. If more goes in, it gets cold: endothermic.

No atoms are made or destroyed, so mass stays the same. A sealed bottle of vinegar and baking soda weighs the same after fizzing.

Signs of a reaction: gas, colour change, heat or cold, light, a new solid, a new smell.
