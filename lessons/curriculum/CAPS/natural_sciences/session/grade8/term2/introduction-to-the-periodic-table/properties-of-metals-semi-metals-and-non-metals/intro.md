Today we find out what it really means to be a metal, a non-metal or a semi-metal. We set up a simple test bench, a cell, a bulb and a gap in the circuit, and try one element after another. Metals are lustrous, conduct heat and electricity, can be hammered into sheets and drawn into wires, and mostly have high melting points, with surprises such as liquid mercury and sodium that floats. Non-metals are dull, insulating and brittle, and many are gases, but carbon breaks the rule: graphite in a pencil lights the bulb, while diamond, the same element, is the hardest natural substance. Semi-metals such as silicon look metallic, shatter like non-metals and conduct only a little, which is exactly why they run computer chips and solar panels. We finish by designing a fair investigation and linking every use to the property that explains it.
