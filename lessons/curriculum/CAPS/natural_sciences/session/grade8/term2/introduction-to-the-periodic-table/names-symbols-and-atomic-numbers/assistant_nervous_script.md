Hey — if the Periodic Table felt like a pile of letters to memorise, here is the calm version: every box tells you four things.

One, the name. Names have stories: chlorine means pale green, helium comes from the Sun, germanium from Germany. You do not need every story, just the idea.

Two, the symbol. One capital letter, or a capital and a small letter. Never two capitals; CO is a gas made of two elements, Co is the metal cobalt. A few symbols come from Latin: Na for sodium, K for potassium, Fe for iron, Cu for copper, Au for gold, Pb for lead. Learn those few like a song.

Three, the atomic number. The small whole number in the box. It counts the protons in every atom, and it is the element's identity card. Change it and you change the element. The bigger number in the box is the mass; do not mix them up.

Four, the address. 1 and 2 are in the first row. 3 to 10 fill the second, 11 to 18 the third. Each row starts in Groups 1 and 2 and jumps to 13 to 18. So 16 is sulfur, Group 16, Period 3.

Open the practice set and read a few boxes. You've got this!
