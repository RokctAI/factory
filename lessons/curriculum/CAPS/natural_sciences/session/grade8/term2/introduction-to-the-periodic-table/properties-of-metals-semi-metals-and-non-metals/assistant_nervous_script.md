Hey — if the properties of metals and non-metals felt like two long lists, here is the calm version: three personalities and three quick tests.

The tests. Does it shine? Does it bend or shatter? Does it light a bulb in a circuit?

Metals say yes, bend, yes. Shiny, malleable, which means flattened into sheets, ductile, which means stretched into wires, and great conductors of heat and electricity. Surprises: mercury is liquid, sodium is soft and floats.

Non-metals say no, shatter, no. Dull, brittle, insulators, and lots are gases. The big surprise is carbon: pencil graphite does light the bulb. Diamond, also carbon, does not.

Semi-metals say a bit of each. Silicon shines but shatters, and only half-conducts. That makes it a semiconductor, perfect for chips and solar panels.

Then, for any use question, join the property and the use with the word because. Copper for wires because it conducts and stretches. Aluminium for planes because it is light.

Open the practice set and run the three tests on a few elements. You've got this!
