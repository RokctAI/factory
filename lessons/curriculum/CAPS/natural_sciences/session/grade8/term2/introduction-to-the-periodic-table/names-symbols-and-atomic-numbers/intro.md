Today we read the Periodic Table one box at a time. Every element has four pieces of identity: a name, a symbol, an atomic number and a position. Names tell stories, from chlorine, meaning pale green, to helium, detected in the light of the Sun before anyone found it on Earth. Symbols follow a strict rule, one capital letter or a capital and a small letter, and a few come from Latin, which is why sodium is Na, potassium K, iron Fe and lead Pb. Then the most important number in chemistry: the atomic number, the count of protons in every atom of an element, which decides which element it is and fixes its place in the table. We learn to tell it apart from the relative atomic mass in the same box, learn the first twenty elements, and use the atomic number to find any element's group and period.
