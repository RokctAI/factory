Hey — if this lesson on the Earth and life felt like a lot, here is the calm version: the Earth has just the right mix of conditions, all at once.

The Earth is the third planet from the Sun, about 150 million kilometres away. That puts it in the habitable zone, not too hot and not too cold, so water can stay liquid. Every living thing needs liquid water.

The Earth is big enough for its gravity to hold on to an atmosphere. The Moon is too small, so it has no air and no water, even though it is the same distance from the Sun.

The atmosphere gives us oxygen to breathe and carbon dioxide for plants. It also works like a blanket, trapping some heat so the Earth stays at about fifteen degrees on average. Venus has far too thick a blanket and cooks; Mars has almost none and freezes.

The ozone layer high up is like sunscreen, blocking most harmful ultraviolet rays.

The Earth's liquid iron core makes a magnetic field, an invisible shield against the solar wind.

And the Sun is a steady star, giving reliable energy.
