Today we explain what keeps the planets in their orbits. A tennis ball swung on a string travels in a circle, but the moment the string is released it flies off in a straight line. For the planets, the string is gravity. We learn that every mass attracts every other mass, that gravity grows with mass and weakens quickly with distance, and why astronauts weigh less on the Moon. We use Newton's cannon on a mountain to see that an orbit is an endless fall that keeps missing the ground. We compare the speeds and years of the planets, from Mercury's 88 days to Neptune's 165 years. Finally we discover why the whole Solar System is a flat disc, formed from a spinning, collapsing cloud of gas and dust.
