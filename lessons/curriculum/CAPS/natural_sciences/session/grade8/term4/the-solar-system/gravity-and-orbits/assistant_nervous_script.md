Hey — if gravity and orbits felt confusing, here is the calm version: a planet is always falling towards the Sun, and always missing.

Gravity is a pull between any two things with mass. Bigger masses pull harder. Further apart, the pull gets weaker, fast: double the distance, a quarter of the pull.

The Sun is huge, so its gravity holds all the planets. Gravity pulls each planet in towards the Sun. At the same time, the planet is moving sideways very fast. Together, these make it curve round and round. That is an orbit.

Think of a ball on a string: let go, and it flies off straight. Gravity is the string.

Planets closer to the Sun feel a stronger pull and move faster, so their year is shorter. Mercury takes 88 days; Neptune takes 165 years.

Astronauts float in the space station because they are falling around the Earth together with the station, not because there is no gravity.

The Solar System is flat like a disc because it formed from a spinning cloud that flattened, like pizza dough.
