Hey — if the Sun felt like a huge topic, here is the calm version: it is a star, and it shines by squeezing hydrogen into helium.

The Sun is a star, just much closer than the others. It is a giant ball of very hot gas, mostly hydrogen and helium, about 109 times wider than the Earth. It holds almost all the mass in the Solar System, and its gravity keeps the planets in orbit.

It is not burning like a fire. In its core, at about 15 million degrees, hydrogen nuclei are squeezed together so hard that they fuse into helium. That is nuclear fusion. A tiny bit of mass is turned into a huge amount of energy.

The energy slowly works its way out, then travels through space as light and heat. It reaches Earth in about eight minutes and twenty seconds.

That energy feeds plants, warms the Earth and drives the weather, so almost all life depends on it. But its ultraviolet rays can harm skin and eyes.

Sunspots are cooler, darker patches on its surface.
