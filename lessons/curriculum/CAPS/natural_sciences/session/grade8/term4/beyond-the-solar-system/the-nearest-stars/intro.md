Today we meet the two nearest stars to the Earth. The first is a surprise: it is the Sun, a star like all the others, only much closer, about eight light minutes away. The second is found beside the Southern Cross, where the brighter of the two Pointers looks like one golden star but is really a family of three. One of them, the small red dwarf Proxima Centauri, discovered from Johannesburg in 1915, is the nearest star to us after the Sun, about 4.24 light years away. We use a soccer-ball model to feel how empty and enormous the space between stars is, and we find out why the light we see from stars left them long ago. Finally we compare stars by colour, temperature, size and brightness.
