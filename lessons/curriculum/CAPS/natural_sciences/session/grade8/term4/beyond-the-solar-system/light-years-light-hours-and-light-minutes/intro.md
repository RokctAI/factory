Today we learn how astronomers measure the enormous distances in space. When Mars is on the far side of the Sun, a radio command to a rover takes more than twenty minutes to arrive, even though it travels at the speed of light, about 300 000 kilometres every second. We use the rule that distance equals speed multiplied by time to build new units of distance: the light second, the light minute, the light hour and the light year. We find that the Moon is about 1.3 light seconds away, the Sun about eight light minutes, Neptune about four light hours, and the nearest star about 4.24 light years. We practise converting between light distances and kilometres, and we discover why looking deep into space means looking back in time.
