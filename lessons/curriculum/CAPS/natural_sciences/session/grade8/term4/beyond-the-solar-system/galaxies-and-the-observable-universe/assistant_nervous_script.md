Hey — if the size of the Universe felt overwhelming, here is the calm version: our galaxy is one of billions, and there is a limit to how far we can see.

There are hundreds of billions of galaxies, maybe even two trillion. A space telescope found thousands of them in a patch of sky that looked empty.

Galaxies come in three main shapes: spiral, like our Milky Way; elliptical, smooth and egg-shaped; and irregular, with no neat shape.

Galaxies live in groups and clusters held together by gravity. Ours is the Local Group, with Andromeda as our big neighbour.

The Universe is expanding: the galaxy groups are moving apart, like raisins in rising bread. It started expanding about 13.8 billion years ago.

The observable Universe is the part we can see. Because space kept stretching while the light travelled, it is about 93 billion light years across.
