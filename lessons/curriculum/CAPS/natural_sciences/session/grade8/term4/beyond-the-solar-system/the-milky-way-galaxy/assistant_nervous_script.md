Hey — if the Milky Way lesson felt huge, here is the calm version: our Sun is one star in a giant spinning pinwheel of stars.

A galaxy is a huge group of stars, gas and dust, held together by gravity.

Our galaxy is the Milky Way. It has hundreds of billions of stars, and the Sun is just one ordinary star among them.

The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy: a bright middle with curved arms, flat like a dinner plate, about one hundred thousand light years across.

We live inside the plate, about halfway out from the middle. Looking along the plate, we see so many stars piled up that they make a glowing band across the night sky. That band is what we call the Milky Way.

The dark patches in the band are dust clouds, not empty holes.

Gravity holds it all together, and the whole galaxy turns. The Sun takes about two hundred and thirty million years to go round once.
