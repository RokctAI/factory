Today we look far beyond the Milky Way. In 1995 the Hubble Space Telescope stared for ten days at a tiny patch of sky that looked empty, and found about three thousand galaxies in it. We learn that our galaxy is one of hundreds of billions, and how Edwin Hubble proved in 1923 that the Andromeda Galaxy lies far outside our own. We meet the three main kinds of galaxies, spiral, elliptical and irregular, and find that galaxies gather into groups and clusters, with ours belonging to the Local Group. We discover that the Universe is expanding, like raisins moving apart in rising bread, and that it began expanding about 13.8 billion years ago. Finally we find out how big the observable Universe is, and why it is far more than 13.8 billion light years across.
