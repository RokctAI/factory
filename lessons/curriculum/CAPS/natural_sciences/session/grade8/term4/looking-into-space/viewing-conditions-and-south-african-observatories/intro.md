Today we find out what makes a good place and time to look at the sky. From a city suburb on a hazy, moonlit night you may see only a few dozen stars; from a dark Karoo farm on a clear winter night you can see thousands, with the Milky Way glowing overhead. The sky is the same, but the viewing conditions are not. We learn why astronomers need darkness, clear and dry air, steady air, height and a wide horizon, and how light pollution, moonlight, cloud and turbulence spoil the view. We visit South Africa's observatories, from the Royal Observatory founded in Cape Town in 1820 to Sutherland, the Karoo radio-quiet zone and Hartebeesthoek, and find out why each site was chosen. Finally we learn how to go stargazing ourselves.
