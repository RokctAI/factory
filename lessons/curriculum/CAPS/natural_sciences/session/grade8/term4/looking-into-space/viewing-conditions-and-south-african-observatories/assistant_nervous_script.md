Hey — if this lesson felt like a lot of places and conditions, here is the calm version: to see the stars well, you need dark, clear, steady skies.

Good viewing needs darkness, clear and dry air, steady air, a high place and a wide view of the horizon.

Light pollution, from lights shining upward, makes the sky glow and hides faint stars.

A full Moon also brightens the sky, so new Moon nights are best for faint stars.

Wobbling air makes stars twinkle and blurs telescope pictures.

South Africa's big optical telescopes, including SALT, are at Sutherland: high, dry, dark and clear. Radio telescopes like MeerKAT and the SKA are in the quiet Karoo, away from cellphone signals.

To stargaze: pick a clear night, find a dark spot, wait twenty to thirty minutes for your eyes to adjust, and use a red torch.
