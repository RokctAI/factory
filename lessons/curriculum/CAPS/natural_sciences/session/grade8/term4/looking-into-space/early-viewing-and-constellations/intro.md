Today we look at how people watched the sky long before telescopes. When the small star cluster isiLimela, the Pleiades, rises before dawn in June, farmers in the Eastern Cape know it is time to prepare the fields. We learn why the stars move across the sky each night and why different stars appear in different seasons. We find out what a constellation is, why astronomers use eighty-eight official ones, and why the stars in a constellation are not really close together. We hear southern African star stories: the Khoikhoi zebras of Orion's Belt, the giraffes of the Southern Cross, and the San story of the Milky Way. Finally we learn to find south using the Southern Cross and the Pointers.
