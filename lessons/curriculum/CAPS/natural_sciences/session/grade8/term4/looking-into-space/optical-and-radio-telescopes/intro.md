Today we find out how telescopes let us see far beyond what our eyes can. Near Sutherland in the Northern Cape stands SALT, the Southern African Large Telescope, with a mirror about eleven metres wide made of ninety-one pieces. In the Karoo, the sixty-four dishes of MeerKAT collect faint radio waves, and above the atmosphere the Hubble Space Telescope circles the Earth. We learn that a telescope's main job is to collect light, and how refracting and reflecting telescopes focus it. We see why Hubble's place above the atmosphere gives it such sharp images. We discover how radio telescopes and arrays reveal a hidden Universe of cold gas, pulsars and black hole jets, and how South Africa is helping build the Square Kilometre Array, the largest radio telescope in the world.
