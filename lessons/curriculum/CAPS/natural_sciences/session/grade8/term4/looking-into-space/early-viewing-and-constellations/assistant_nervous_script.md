Hey — if constellations felt like a lot to remember, here is the calm version: people have always used the sky as a calendar, a clock and a compass.

Stars rise in the east and set in the west each night because the Earth spins.

Different stars show up in different seasons because the Earth travels around the Sun. Orion is a summer star in South Africa; Scorpius is a winter one.

A constellation is a pattern of stars, like a join-the-dots picture. There are eighty-eight official ones covering the whole sky.

The stars in a constellation only look close; really, some are much further away than others.

Every culture has star stories. The Pleiades, isiLimela, mark planting time. The Khoikhoi saw three zebras in Orion's Belt.

To find south: use the long line of the Southern Cross and a line from the middle of the Pointers. Where they meet, drop down to the horizon.
