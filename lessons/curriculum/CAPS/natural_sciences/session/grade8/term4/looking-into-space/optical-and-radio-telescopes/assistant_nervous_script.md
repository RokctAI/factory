Hey — if telescopes felt technical, here is the calm version: telescopes are buckets that catch light or radio waves from space.

The main job of a telescope is to collect light. A bigger mirror or lens catches more light, so you can see fainter things.

Refracting telescopes use lenses. Reflecting telescopes use curved mirrors. All big modern telescopes use mirrors.

SALT, near Sutherland, has a mirror about eleven metres wide, made of ninety-one pieces. It is the biggest single optical telescope in the Southern Hemisphere.

Hubble is in space, above the air that blurs starlight, so its pictures are very sharp.

Radio telescopes use big dishes to catch radio waves, a kind of invisible light. MeerKAT in the Karoo has sixty-four dishes and is part of the SKA, which will be the biggest radio telescope in the world.
