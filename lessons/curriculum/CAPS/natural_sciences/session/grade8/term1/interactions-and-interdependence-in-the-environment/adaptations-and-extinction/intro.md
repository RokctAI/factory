Today we look at how living things are built for the places they live, and what happens when the place changes faster than they can. An adaptation is an inherited feature that improves an organism's chance of surviving and reproducing in its habitat, and we sort adaptations into three kinds: structural parts like the bat-eared fox's radiator ears, functional processes like the gemsbok letting its body heat past 45 degrees to save water, and behavioural habits like the meerkat on sentry duty. The gemsbok of the Kalahari shows all three at once. Then the other end of the story: extinction, the permanent loss of a species when no individuals remain, from the asteroid that ended the dinosaurs to the rifles and sheep farms that ended the quagga and the bluebuck in South Africa. We finish with the species on the edge today, the riverine rabbit, the African penguin and the black rhino, and the human causes behind every one of them.
