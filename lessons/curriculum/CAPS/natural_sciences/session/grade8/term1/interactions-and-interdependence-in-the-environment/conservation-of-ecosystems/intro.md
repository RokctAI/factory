Today we finish the ecology unit by asking what can be done about the damage. Conservation means protecting and managing ecosystems so that they keep working, and we start with why it matters: the free services nature gives us, from clean water to pollinated fruit, and South Africa's extraordinary biodiversity, with nearly a tenth of the world's plant species on about two percent of its land. Then the two scales of action. Environmentalists and organisations protect places such as the Kruger and Marine Protected Areas, write and enforce rules, rescue oiled penguins and poached rhino, clear alien trees and count what is left. Individuals save water and electricity, reduce, reuse and recycle, buy seafood wisely and plant indigenous. We finish with sustainable use, living on nature's interest rather than its capital, the Kosi Bay fish traps as a model, and a five-step method for judging any conservation plan.
