Today we sort the living things of an ecosystem by one question: how do you get your food? Green plants and algae make it themselves from sunlight, so they are the producers, the only organisms that bring energy into the living world. Everything else eats. Herbivores like impala and locusts eat plants; carnivores like lions, eagles and spiders eat animals; omnivores like baboons and humans eat both; and the predators, scavengers and insectivores among the meat-eaters each have their own way of getting it. When anything dies, the decomposers, bacteria and fungi, break it down and return its minerals to the soil for the next generation of plants, with earthworms and dung beetles helping to shred the pieces. We read teeth as evidence of diet, learn why a mushroom is not a plant and a vulture is not a decomposer, and fix the arrow convention that food chains will use next time: from the food to the feeder, the way the energy flows.
