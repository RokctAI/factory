Today we learn the language of ecology by standing next to one impala in the Kruger and zooming out. The impala is an organism; its herd and all the other impala around it are a population; every living thing in that stretch of bush is a community; add the sunlight, rain, soil and air and you have an ecosystem; the whole savanna region is a biome; and every ecosystem on Earth together is the biosphere. We sort what we see into biotic components, the living and once-living, and abiotic components, the never-living conditions, and watch the two shape each other. We fix the meaning of habitat as the kind of place an organism calls home, and finish with how living things cope when that home changes with the night or the seasons: swallows that migrate, bullfrogs that seal themselves in mud, and marula trees that drop their leaves. Six nested levels, two columns, and one three-step rule for marks.
