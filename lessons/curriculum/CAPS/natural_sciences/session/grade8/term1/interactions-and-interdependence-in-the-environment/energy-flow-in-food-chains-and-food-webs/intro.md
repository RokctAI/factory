Today we follow energy through the bush. It arrives as sunlight, is captured by grass, passes to the impala that eats the grass and to the lion that eats the impala, and at every step most of it leaks away. We write that path as a food chain with arrows pointing the way the energy goes, name each position as a trophic level, and then join all the chains of a community into a food web and learn to read it: tracing a single chain, placing an organism at its level, and predicting what happens to everyone else when one species is removed. Then the arithmetic: of 10 000 kilojoules stored in grass, about 1000 reach the impala and 100 reach the lion, because uneaten parts, dung and above all respiration as heat take the rest. That ten percent rule is why food chains are short, why there are so few lions and so much grass, why the pyramid is the natural shape, and why the world is fed on plants.
