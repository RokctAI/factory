Today we ask what keeps an ecosystem steady and what knocks it over. Balance in nature is not stillness: the impala and lion populations of a reserve rise and fall against each other year after year, each correcting the other, held within limits by food, water, space, predators and disease. We name those limiting factors and the carrying capacity they set, and learn to read a predator-prey graph. Then the disruptions. Natural ones, drought, flood, fire and disease, usually pass and the bush recovers, and fire in fynbos turns out to be a necessity in the right dose. Human ones, habitat destruction, pollution, overharvesting, overgrazing, climate change and above all alien invasive species such as water hyacinth and black wattle, are larger, faster and often permanent. We finish with a five-step method for any case study: name the disruption, trace who is affected first and next, state what it costs people, and propose a response you can justify.
