Hey — if conservation felt like a long list of organisations and rules, here is the calm version: one picture, two teams, one test.

The picture. Nature is a savings account. The fish, the grass and the trees are the money in the bank; what they regrow every year is the interest. Take only the interest and it lasts for ever. That is sustainable use. Conservation is just looking after the account so it keeps paying out clean water, pollination, fish and jobs.

Team one, the guardians. They guard places like the Kruger and no-fishing zones at sea, make rules like fishing limits and the ban on rhino horn trade, rescue oily penguins and chase poachers, repair land by cutting alien trees, and count species to see what is working.

Team two, you. Save water, save electricity, reduce then reuse then recycle, check the SASSI fish list, plant indigenous, pull out aliens, stay on the boardwalk.

The test. For any action, say what it does and which problem it fixes. For any plan, ask: does it fix the cause, who pays, how will we know it worked?

Open the practice set and try the savings account on a real example. You've got this!
