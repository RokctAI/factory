Hey — if adaptations and extinction felt like a long list of animals and dates, here is the calm version: a tool built for a job, three kinds, and what happens when the job disappears.

The tool. An adaptation is a feature you are born with that fits you to your home: helps you eat, stay safe, handle the weather, find water, raise young. You cannot learn one. It only works in its own place; a polar bear's coat would kill it in the Kalahari.

The three kinds. A part you can see, like big ears or a fat stem: structural. A process inside the body, like the gemsbok letting itself heat up or a camel making concentrated urine: functional. A habit, like coming out at night or standing guard: behavioural. Say which, then say the job it does. Feature, problem, how it helps. Three bits.

The ending. When the home changes faster than the creature can, the species dies out. Extinct means gone everywhere, for ever. The quagga and the bluebuck are South Africa's examples; the riverine rabbit, the penguin and the rhino are the ones still hanging on.
