Hey — if balance, limiting factors, droughts, pollution and alien plants felt like too many things at once, here is the calm version: a seesaw, two kinds of knock, and five steps.

The seesaw. Impala up, lions up a year later, impala down, lions down, impala up. A wobble that fixes itself. That is balance. Food, water, space, predators and disease are the limits that stop it tipping, and carrying capacity is the biggest crowd the land can feed for a long time.

The knocks. Natural ones, drought, flood, fire, disease, usually heal in months or years, and fire in fynbos is actually needed in the right dose. Human ones, habitat destruction, pollution, overharvesting, overgrazing, alien species, climate change, are bigger and often permanent. Alien means brought here; invasive means spreading and harming.

The five steps for any case study: name the disruption and say natural or human, who is hit first, who is hit next along the arrows, what it costs people, and what to do and why it would work.

Open the practice set, rock the seesaw, walk the five steps. You've got this!
