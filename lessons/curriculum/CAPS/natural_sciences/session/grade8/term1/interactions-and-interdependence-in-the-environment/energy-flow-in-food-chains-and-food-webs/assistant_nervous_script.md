Hey — if food chains, trophic levels, webs and ten percent all tangled together, here is the calm version: it is a relay race with a leaky bucket.

The relay. Energy comes from the Sun to the grass, from the grass to the impala, from the impala to the lion. Grass, arrow, impala, arrow, lion. The arrow points to the eater, because it shows where the energy goes. A chain always starts with a plant. The positions are the trophic levels: producer first, then primary consumer, secondary, tertiary. The herbivore is at level two.

The web. Draw every chain in one place together and you get a food web. Pull one strand, remove one animal, and you trace the arrows to see who gets more and who gets less.

The bucket. Only about ten percent of the energy reaches the next level. 10 000 in the grass, 1000 in the impala, 100 in the lions. The rest leaks as uneaten bits, dung, and mostly heat from respiration. That is why chains are short and lions are rare.

Open the practice set, point the arrows, count the tenths. You've got this!
