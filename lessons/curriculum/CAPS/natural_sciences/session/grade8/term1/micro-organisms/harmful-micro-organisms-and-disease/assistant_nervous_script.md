Hey — if four diseases with all their symptoms and treatments felt like too much, here is the calm version: every germ needs a door.

Door one, the air. TB, a bacterium, travels in coughs. A cough for more than two weeks means get tested. Tablets cure it, but only if you take them for the full six months.

Door two, dirty water and food. Cholera, a bacterium, causes watery diarrhoea that dries you out fast. Clean water with sugar and salt saves lives.

Door three, blood and body fluids. HIV is a virus that attacks the immune system. AIDS is what happens years later if it is not treated. No hugs, cups or mosquitoes spread it. Daily tablets keep it under control.

Door four, a bite. Malaria is a tiny protist carried by a mosquito at night. Fever, chills, sweating. Nets and spray stop the bite.

Then the trick for every prevention question: name the door, then name the lock. Cough into your elbow for the air. Wash hands and boil water for the water. Never share needles for the blood. Sleep under a net for the bite. And vaccines keep the guards inside your body ready.
