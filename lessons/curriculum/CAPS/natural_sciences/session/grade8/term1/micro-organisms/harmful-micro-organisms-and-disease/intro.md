Today we meet the small minority of micro-organisms that make us ill. A pathogen is a micro-organism that causes disease, and every infectious disease needs a route from one person to the next. The four diseases in this lesson each use a different one. TB, caused by a bacterium, travels through the air in coughs and is curable with six months of tablets that must be finished. Cholera, another bacterium, travels in water and food contaminated with faeces, and the life-saving treatment is clean water with sugar and salt. HIV, a virus, travels in blood and body fluids and attacks the immune system, with AIDS as the late stage of untreated infection; antiretroviral therapy keeps it under control. Malaria, caused by the protist Plasmodium, is carried by a mosquito. We finish by matching every prevention method to the route it blocks, from handwashing and ventilation to nets, condoms and vaccines.
