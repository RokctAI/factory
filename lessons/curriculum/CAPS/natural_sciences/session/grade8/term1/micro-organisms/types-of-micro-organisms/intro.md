Today we open a zoo you cannot see. A single drop of pond water under a microscope is crowded with living things, and we learn to measure them in micrometres and nanometres and to sort them into four groups by their structure. Viruses are not cells at all, just genetic material in a protein coat, and can copy themselves only inside a living host. Bacteria are single cells with no nucleus, shaped like balls, rods or spirals, and some split in two every twenty minutes, so one becomes more than a million in under seven hours. Protists are single cells with a nucleus, such as amoeba, paramecium, euglena and the Plasmodium that causes malaria. Fungi, the yeasts and moulds, feed by digesting food from the outside and spread by budding or spores. We finish with three sorting questions and why antibiotics work on bacteria but never on viruses.
