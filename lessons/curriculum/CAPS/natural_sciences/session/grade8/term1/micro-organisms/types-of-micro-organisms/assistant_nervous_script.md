Hey — if micro-organisms felt like a pile of strange names, here is the calm version: four families, four kinds of house.

Viruses have no house. They are not even cells, just instructions in a protein coat, and they can only copy themselves by breaking into a living cell. HIV, flu and measles are viruses.

Bacteria have a one-room house: a single cell with a wall but no nucleus. Balls, rods or spirals. They split in two, and in warm food some split every twenty minutes, so one becomes 512 in three hours.

Protists have a room with an office: a single cell with a nucleus. The amoeba oozes, the paramecium rows with tiny hairs, the euglena swims and can use sunlight. Plasmodium causes malaria.

Fungi have houses made of threads, like bread mould, or single budding cells, like yeast. They digest food from the outside.

To sort any microbe, ask three questions in order. Is it a cell? Does it have a nucleus? Is it threads or budding yeast, or a single moving cell?

One last thing worth keeping: antibiotics break bacteria, not viruses.
