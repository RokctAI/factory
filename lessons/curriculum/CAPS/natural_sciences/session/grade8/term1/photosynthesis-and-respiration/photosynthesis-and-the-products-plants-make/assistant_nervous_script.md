Hey — if the word equation and the ethanol and the iodine all blurred into one, here is the calm version: it is one recipe and one test.

The recipe. A leaf takes in carbon dioxide from the air and water from the roots, uses sunlight caught by chlorophyll, and makes glucose plus oxygen. Carbon dioxide and water are the ingredients, so they go on the left. Sunlight and chlorophyll are not used up, so they sit at the arrow as conditions. Four substances, two conditions, one arrow.

The test. Starch is the evidence that the leaf has been working, and iodine turns starch blue-black. Boil the leaf in water to soften it, boil it in ethanol to wash out the green, warm it in water again, lay it on a tile, add iodine. Ethanol burns easily, so it sits in hot water with the flame off. Leaf from the light: blue-black. Covered leaf: brown.

Then the sugar is rebuilt: starch to store, cellulose to build, sucrose to transport, oil for seeds, and with nitrates from the soil, protein.

Open the practice set, write the recipe, walk the test. You've got this!
