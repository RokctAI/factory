Today we take apart the glucose that last lesson built. Respiration is the process in which every living cell, in a plant, an animal, a fungus or a bacterium, combines glucose with oxygen and releases the energy stored inside it, giving off carbon dioxide and water. We write it as a word equation and set it beside the photosynthesis equation to see that the two are mirror images: one stores the energy of sunlight in sugar, the other spends it. We separate breathing, the physical movement of air that delivers oxygen, from respiration, the chemical release of energy inside the cell, because confusing them is the commonest error in the topic. Then we gather the evidence: limewater turning milky, germinating seeds warming a flask, and the plant that respires all night while it photosynthesises only by day. By the end you will know why you are warm, why you breathe harder on the stairs, and what the kilojoules on a food label actually count.
