Today we meet the one process that feeds the whole planet. Green plants take carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil, use the energy of sunlight captured by the green pigment chlorophyll, and build glucose, releasing oxygen as they do it. We will write that as a word equation and be exact about what is a raw material and what is only a condition, see why the leaf and its chloroplasts are built for the job, and run the classic investigation that proves it: boiling a leaf, washing out the green with ethanol in a water bath, and watching iodine turn starch blue-black. Then we follow the glucose onwards, into starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls, sucrose for transport, oils for seeds and, with nitrates from the soil, protein. By the end you will know where pap, sugar, cotton and the oxygen in your next breath all come from.
