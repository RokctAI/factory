Today we study series circuits, where every component is joined one after another in a single loop. An old string of festive lights stays completely dark because one tiny bulb has a broken filament, and that single gap stops the current through all fifty. We define a series circuit and recognise one in a circuit diagram, and show with ammeters that the current is the same at every point. We investigate what happens when more bulbs or resistors are added in series: the total resistance increases, the current decreases, and the bulbs grow dimmer. We compare this with adding cells in series, which increases the voltage and the current. Finally we weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of series circuits, and see why switches, fuses and ammeters are always connected in series.
