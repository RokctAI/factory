Today we study parallel circuits, where the current splits into two or more separate paths. In a kitchen in the evening, the light, the fridge, the kettle and a charging phone all work at full strength, and switching one off leaves the others running, because household circuits are connected in parallel. We define parallel circuits, recognise their ladder shape and junctions in circuit diagrams, and see why each branch receives the full voltage and works independently. We investigate adding resistors in parallel and find that it decreases the total resistance and increases the total current, while bulbs stay at full brightness. We show that branch currents add up to the main current. Finally we examine house wiring, circuit breakers and the danger of overloading a multi-plug, and compare series and parallel circuits.
