Hey — if the effects of current felt like three separate topics, here is the calm version: current always does three kinds of things.

One, heating. Electrons bump into atoms and the wire warms up. Kettles, toasters, geysers and old bulbs use this. A fuse is a thin wire that melts if the current gets too big, breaking the circuit to keep you safe. A short circuit is a shortcut with almost no resistance, giving a huge, dangerous current.

Two, magnetism. A current makes a magnetic field around a wire. Coil the wire around soft iron and you have an electromagnet. Make it stronger with more turns, more current, and an iron core. You can switch it on and off, which is why scrapyard cranes and doorbells use it.

Three, chemical change. Pass a current through salty or acidified water and new substances form at the rods. That is electrolysis. Water gives hydrogen at the negative rod and oxygen at the positive rod. Electroplating uses this to coat things in metal.
