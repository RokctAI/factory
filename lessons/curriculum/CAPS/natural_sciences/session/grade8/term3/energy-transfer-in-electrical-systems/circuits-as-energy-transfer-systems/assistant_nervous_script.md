Hey — if circuits felt complicated, here is the calm version: a circuit is just a loop that carries energy.

Every circuit has three parts. An energy source, like a cell, that gives the charges energy. A path of conductors, like copper wire. And a load, like a bulb, that turns the energy into something useful.

The loop must be closed, with no gaps. A gap anywhere stops the flow everywhere. A switch is just a controlled gap: on closes the loop, off opens it.

The charges are not used up. They go round and round like a bicycle chain. What runs out in a cell is its stored chemical energy.

Each component has an input and an output. Input is always electrical energy. A bulb outputs light and heat, a kettle heat, a fan movement, a speaker sound. The part you want is useful; the rest is wasted, usually as heat. Energy is never destroyed, only changed.

For any question, name the source, the path and the load, then give the energy in and out.
