Today we study the three main effects of an electric current. A scrapyard crane lifts a tangle of steel with a disc that is only a magnet while current flows through it, and drops the steel the moment the current stops. We explain the heating effect, caused by electrons colliding with atoms, and its uses in kettles, geysers, toasters and filament bulbs. We see how a fuse uses the heating effect to protect a circuit, why a short circuit is so dangerous, and how circuit breakers work. We repeat Ørsted's discovery that a current produces a magnetic field, build an electromagnet from a nail and a coil, and find what makes it stronger. Finally we pass a current through liquids to produce new substances by electrolysis, and see how electroplating and metal refining use this chemical effect.
