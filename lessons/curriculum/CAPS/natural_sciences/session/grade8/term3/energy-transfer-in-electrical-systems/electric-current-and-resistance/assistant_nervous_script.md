Hey — if current and resistance sounded tricky, here is the calm version: current is the flow, resistance is what slows it.

Current is how much charge flows past a point each second. It is measured in amperes, or amps, with an ammeter. The ammeter goes into the loop, in series, so every charge passes through it.

In a single loop, the current is the same everywhere. The bulb does not use up current; it uses energy.

Resistance makes it harder for charge to flow. Four things change it. Longer wire: more resistance. Thicker wire: less resistance. Material: copper low, nichrome high, plastic huge. Hotter metal: more resistance.

More resistance means less current. That is why the pencil-lead dimmer works: more lead, more resistance, dimmer bulb.

Resistance turns electrical energy into heat. That is useful in toasters, kettles and heaters.

Too little resistance is dangerous: a short circuit lets a huge current flow and can start a fire.

For any explain question: change in resistance, then change in current, then what the bulb does.
