Today we explain electric current and resistance. A bulb connected through a strip of pencil graphite glows brightly when the clips are close together and dims as one clip slides away, though the cells and bulb never change. We define current as the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes, and see why it is the same at every point in a single loop. We learn how to connect an ammeter in series and read it safely. We investigate the four factors that affect resistance: material, length, thickness and temperature, with a fair test using nichrome wire. Finally we see how more resistance means less current, how resistors control circuits, why heaters and toasters rely on resistance for their useful heat, and why a short circuit is so dangerous.
