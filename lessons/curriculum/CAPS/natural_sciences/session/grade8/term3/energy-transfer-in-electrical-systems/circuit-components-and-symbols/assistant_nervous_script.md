Hey — if all the parts and symbols felt like a lot to remember, here is the calm version: each part has one job, and each has a simple picture.

Cells push the charges. One torch cell gives about 1.5 volts, big or small. Two or more cells joined together make a battery, and lining them up plus to minus adds their voltages.

Wires are copper, which conducts well, covered in plastic, which insulates. Thick wires carry more current safely.

A switch opens or closes the loop. A resistor makes it harder for current to flow and turns some energy into heat. A variable resistor can be adjusted, like a dimmer.

Symbols: a cell is a long thin line and a short thick line, the long one positive. A bulb is a circle with a cross. A resistor is a rectangle. A switch is a little gate.

Draw diagrams with a ruler, straight lines, square corners, and no gaps unless a switch is open. Current goes from positive, round the loop, to negative.

Open the practice set and try drawing a few circuits. You've got this!
