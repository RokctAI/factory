Today we look at an electric circuit as a system for transferring energy. When load shedding switches the house lights off, a torch from the kitchen drawer lights at the slide of a switch, using nothing more than two cells, a switch, a bulb and a few strips of metal. We identify the three essential parts of every circuit: an energy source, a conducting path and a load. We see why a circuit must be closed for charges to flow, how a switch opens and closes it, and how to track down a fault. We describe the input and output energies of bulbs, heaters, motors and speakers, and use energy flow diagrams to show useful and wasted energy. Finally we follow electricity from a coal-fired power station in Mpumalanga to the plug in your wall.
