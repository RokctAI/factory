Hey — if the eye felt like a long list of parts, here is the calm version: the eye collects light, and the brain makes the picture.

Light goes in through the cornea, a clear window that does most of the bending. It passes through the pupil, a hole whose size is controlled by the coloured iris: small in bright light, wide in the dark. Then the lens fine-tunes the focus, getting fatter for close things and thinner for far things.

The light lands on the retina at the back of the eye. The picture there is upside down; the brain sorts that out.

The retina has two kinds of receptor cells. Rods work in dim light but only see grey. Cones need brighter light and see colour and detail.

When light hits them, they make nerve impulses. These travel along the optic nerve to the brain, which builds the picture.

Look after your eyes: never look at the Sun, wear sunglasses and goggles, and eat foods with vitamin A.
