Today we discover that white light is a mixture of colours. After a summer storm over Pretoria, with the Sun low behind you, a bright arch of colour appears across the dark clouds: red on the outside, violet on the inside. We follow Newton's prism experiments, which showed that a prism separates colours already present in sunlight rather than creating them, and learn the order of the visible spectrum. We explain refraction, the bending of light as it passes from one material into another, and dispersion, the spreading of colours because each is bent by a different amount. We use raindrops as tiny prisms to explain rainbows, why the Sun must be behind you, and why a secondary bow has its colours reversed. Finally we look beyond red and violet to infrared and ultraviolet.
