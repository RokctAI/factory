Hey — if shadows and eclipses felt confusing, here is the calm version: light goes in straight lines, and opaque things block it.

Transparent things let light straight through, like clear glass. Translucent things let some light through but scatter it, like frosted glass. Opaque things block light completely, like a wall.

A shadow is the dark patch behind an opaque object, where light cannot reach because it cannot bend round the object. It has the shape of the object's outline.

Move an object closer to the light and its shadow gets bigger. Move it closer to the wall and the shadow gets smaller and sharper.

A big light source gives a shadow with a dark middle, the umbra, and a fuzzy edge, the penumbra.

Outside, shadows are long when the Sun is low and short at midday. In South Africa, midday shadows point south.

Solar eclipse: the Moon blocks the Sun, and its shadow falls on Earth. Lunar eclipse: the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, which turns red.
