Today we investigate the reflection of light. On a still morning a Drakensberg dam mirrors the mountains perfectly; a breeze ruffles the water and the image breaks into glitter. We define the incident ray, reflected ray, normal and the angles between them, and test the law of reflection with a ray box, a mirror and a protractor. We explain why a plane mirror forms a virtual, upright, same-size image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, and why left and right are swapped. We compare regular reflection from smooth surfaces, which forms images, with diffuse reflection from rough surfaces, which lets us see objects from any direction. Finally we see reflection at work in periscopes, road reflectors, solar power towers and great telescopes.
