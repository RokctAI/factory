Today we investigate how materials let light through and how shadows form. At assembly on a winter morning, every learner's shadow stretches far across the quad; at lunchtime the shadows shrink; by home time they stretch the other way. We classify materials as transparent, translucent or opaque, and test them with a torch and a screen. We explain shadows as regions where an opaque object blocks light that travels in straight lines, and see how moving the object changes the size of its shadow. We learn why outdoor shadows change length and direction through the day, and why midday shadows in South Africa point south. We distinguish the dark umbra from the lighter penumbra, and finally use the shadows of the Moon and the Earth to explain solar and lunar eclipses.
