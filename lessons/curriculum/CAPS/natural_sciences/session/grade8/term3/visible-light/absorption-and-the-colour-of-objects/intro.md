Today we explain why objects have colour. At a school disco lit only by a red spotlight, a white shirt looks red, a green shirt looks black and a blue poster vanishes, although in daylight they look completely different. We see that light falling on an object can be reflected, absorbed or transmitted, and that an opaque object's colour is the colour of the light it reflects. We explain why apples are red, leaves are green, paper is white and soot is black. We use coloured filters, meet the primary colours of light, red, green and blue, and predict how objects look under coloured lights. Finally we show that dark surfaces absorb more light energy and heat up faster, and see why solar water heaters are black and houses in hot places are white.
