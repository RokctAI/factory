Today we begin the study of light. On a clear night in the Karoo near Sutherland, the stars blaze with their own light while the Moon, the brightest object in the sky, shines only with borrowed sunlight. We sort objects into luminous sources, natural and artificial, and non-luminous objects that we see by reflected light. We show that light travels in straight lines, using three cards in a row, sunbeams and sharp shadows, and draw it as rays and beams. We build a pinhole camera and explain its upside-down image. We meet the speed of light, about 300 000 kilometres per second, and calculate how long light takes to reach us from the Moon and the Sun. Finally we see light as energy that crosses empty space.
