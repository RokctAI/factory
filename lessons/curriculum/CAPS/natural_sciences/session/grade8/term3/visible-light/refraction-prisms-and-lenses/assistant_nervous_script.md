Hey — if refraction felt tricky, here is the calm version: light bends when it changes speed.

Light is fastest in air and slows down in water and glass. If it crosses from one into the other at a slant, it changes direction. That is refraction.

The rule: slowing down, from air into water or glass, it bends towards the normal. Speeding up, from water or glass into air, it bends away from the normal. Straight in along the normal, no bending.

That is why a pencil looks broken in water, why a pool looks shallower than it is, and why hot roads seem to have puddles, which are mirages.

Lenses are curved pieces of glass that bend light on purpose. A convex lens, fat in the middle, brings light together to a focus and can magnify. A concave lens, thin in the middle, spreads light out.

Lenses are in your eye, spectacles, cameras, projectors, microscopes and telescopes.

For any ray diagram, draw the normal dashed, and bend towards it when the light slows down.
