Hey — if light felt like a big new topic, here is the calm version: where it comes from, how it moves, and how fast.

Luminous things make their own light: the Sun, stars, fire, bulbs, screens, even fireflies. Non-luminous things do not; we see them because light bounces off them into our eyes. The Moon is non-luminous. It reflects sunlight.

Light travels in straight lines. That is why shadows have sharp edges and why you cannot see round corners. We draw light as rays: straight lines with arrows.

A pinhole camera gives an upside-down picture, because light from the top of an object travels straight down through the hole, and light from the bottom travels straight up.

Light is incredibly fast: about 300 000 kilometres per second. Sound is about a million times slower, so you see lightning before you hear thunder.

To find travel time, divide distance by speed. Moonlight takes about 1.3 seconds. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds.

Light carries energy and can cross empty space.
