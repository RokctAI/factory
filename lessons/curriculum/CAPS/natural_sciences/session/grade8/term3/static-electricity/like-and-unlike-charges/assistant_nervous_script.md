Hey — if the forces between charges felt confusing, here is the calm version: it is one rule plus one trick.

The rule: like charges repel, unlike charges attract. Two negative balloons push apart. A negative balloon and a positive jersey pull together. They do it without touching.

The force is stronger with more charge, and stronger when the objects are closer. It fades quickly as they move apart.

The trick: a charged object can attract a neutral one. A negative comb pushes the electrons in a scrap of paper slightly away, so the near side is a bit positive. Closer means stronger, so the pull wins. That is why a balloon sticks to a wall.

So be careful: attraction does not prove something is charged. Only repulsion does.

An electroscope shows charge. When the rod and leaf get the same charge, they repel, and the leaf lifts.

Uses: spray painting cars, photocopiers, and cleaning smoke at power stations.
