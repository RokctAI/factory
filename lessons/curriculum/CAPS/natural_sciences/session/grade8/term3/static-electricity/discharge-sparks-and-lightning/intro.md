Today we explain what happens when static charge escapes. The sting from a car door after sliding across the seat and a bolt of lightning over the Highveld on a summer afternoon are the same process on very different scales. We define discharge, the loss of charge from a charged object, and compare slow leaking with sudden sparks. We see how a large enough charge turns a narrow channel of air into a conductor, why everyday static shocks are harmless, and why sparks near fuel or dust are dangerous. We follow the separation of charge inside a thundercloud, the stepped leader and the return stroke, and explain thunder and how to estimate a storm's distance. Finally we learn the rules that keep people safe in South Africa's lightning season and how a lightning conductor protects a building.
