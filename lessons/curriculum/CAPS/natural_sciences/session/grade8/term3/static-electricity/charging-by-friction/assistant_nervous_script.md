Hey — if static electricity felt mysterious, here is the calm version: it is just electrons moving from one thing to another.

Atoms have positive protons in the middle and negative electrons on the outside. Normally they balance, so things are neutral.

When you rub two different materials together, some electrons jump from one to the other. The one that gains electrons becomes negative. The one that loses electrons becomes positive, by exactly the same amount. Nothing is made or destroyed; it is just moved.

Only electrons move. Protons are locked in the nucleus. So positive always means lost electrons.

A ranking list called the triboelectric series tells you who wins the electrons. Plastic beats wool, so plastic goes negative. Silk beats glass, so glass goes positive.

Insulators, like plastic and rubber, keep the charge where you rubbed. Conductors, like metal, let it flow away, unless they are standing on an insulator.

For any question, name the two materials, say which one gains electrons and which loses them, and give the charge on each. Then remember that the two charges are always equal and opposite.
