Today we explain static electricity. Rub a balloon on dry hair on a winter morning and the hair rises to follow it, and the balloon then clings to a wall with no glue at all. We begin with the atom: positive protons locked in the nucleus, negative electrons around it, normally in equal numbers so that objects are neutral. We see how rubbing two different materials transfers electrons from one to the other, leaving one negatively charged and the other equally positive, and why charge is never created or destroyed. We use the triboelectric series to predict which material gains electrons, and explain why static works poorly on humid days. Finally we compare insulators, which keep charge where it is rubbed, with conductors, which let it flow away, and meet the idea of earthing.
