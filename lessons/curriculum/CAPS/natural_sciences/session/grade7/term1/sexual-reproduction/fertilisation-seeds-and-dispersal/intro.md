Today we follow a pollen grain after it lands on a stigma. It grows a pollen tube down through the style to an ovule, where a male gamete fuses with the egg cell: fertilisation. We watch the petals fall away as each ovule becomes a seed, with its embryo and tough coat, and the ovary swells into a fruit, juicy like a marula or dry like a bean pod, which is why a tomato is a fruit. Then we send the seeds travelling: through elephants and baboons, on blackjack hooks and ants, on wings and parachutes, floating like coconuts, or flung from popping pods.
