Today we take a flower apart, ring by ring, from a hibiscus on the desk to the king protea, our national flower, which turns out to be hundreds of small flowers in one head. We meet the sepals that protect the bud and the petals that advertise to pollinators, sometimes with markings only bees can see. Then the male stamens, each a filament holding up an anther that makes pollen, and the female carpel in the centre: a sticky stigma, a style and an ovary full of ovules. We finish by seeing what each part becomes: ovules become seeds and the ovary becomes the fruit.
