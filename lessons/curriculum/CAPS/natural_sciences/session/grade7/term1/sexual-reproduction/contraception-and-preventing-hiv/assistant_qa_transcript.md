### subtopic_1 — Contraception
**Assistant:** Why do people say condoms are only effective if used correctly every single time?

**Tutor:** Because almost all condom failures are caused by how they are used rather than by the condom itself. When a condom is used perfectly, every time, it is very effective at preventing pregnancy, with only about two pregnancies per hundred couples in a year. In real life, where people sometimes forget, start using it too late, or use it incorrectly, the figure rises to around thirteen per hundred couples per year. The difference comes from avoidable mistakes. A condom put on partway through contact may be too late, because a little fluid released before ejaculation can contain sperm and can carry infections. A condom opened with teeth or scissors, kept in a hot place such as a car's cubbyhole or a wallet for months, or used after its expiry date may have tiny tears or weakened rubber. Oil-based products such as petroleum jelly, body lotion or cooking oil damage latex within minutes, so only water-based or silicone-based lubricants should be used. Air left in the tip can cause breakage, so the tip should be pinched as the condom is rolled on. After use, it must be held at the base during withdrawal so that it does not slip off, then wrapped and thrown away, never flushed or reused. Using two condoms at once increases friction and breakage. So the message is simple: a condom is a reliable tool, but only when it is used correctly, consistently and from the very beginning.

### subtopic_2 — HIV, AIDS and Other STIs
**Assistant:** Why did South Africa have such a severe HIV epidemic, and what changed it?

**Tutor:** South Africa's epidemic grew very quickly in the 1990s and early 2000s for a combination of reasons. The virus spread along routes of travel and migrant labour, as many workers lived far from their families for long periods, and inequality, poverty and the disruption of family life made people more vulnerable. Gender inequality meant that many young women could not insist on condom use, and relationships between young women and older men increased their risk. High rates of other untreated STIs made transmission easier. In the early 2000s, there was also a damaging period of government denial, when official statements questioned whether HIV caused AIDS and delayed the national provision of antiretroviral medicines; researchers later estimated that more than three hundred thousand lives were lost because of the delay. Civil society played a key role in changing this. The Treatment Action Campaign, founded in 1998, campaigned and went to court, and in 2002 the Constitutional Court ordered the government to provide medicine to prevent mother-to-child transmission. From 2004 a national treatment programme began, and it grew to become the largest in the world. Combined with wider testing, free condoms, medical male circumcision, which reduces men's risk, and prevention medicine called PrEP for people at high risk, new infections and AIDS deaths have fallen substantially, and life expectancy has risen. HIV remains a major challenge, especially for young women, so prevention, testing and treatment remain essential.

### subtopic_3 — Consequences of Early Sexual Activity
**Assistant:** How can a young person say no to pressure without losing friends or a relationship?

**Tutor:** It helps to understand that pressure works best when a person has not thought about their answer in advance, so the first step is deciding on personal limits before a difficult moment arrives. Once you know what you want, saying it becomes easier. Clear, simple words work best, for example: no, I'm not ready; I've decided to wait; I don't want to. You do not owe anyone a long explanation, and repeating the same sentence calmly is often more effective than arguing. Body language matters too: standing straight, looking the person in the eye and using a firm voice shows that you mean it. It can help to suggest an alternative, like going somewhere public with friends, or to leave the situation. Avoiding risky settings, such as being alone with someone who is pressuring you or being around alcohol and drugs, which weaken judgement, prevents many problems. A real friend or a partner who cares about you will accept your decision; someone who keeps pushing, threatens to leave, or spreads rumours is showing that they do not respect you, and that is useful information about the relationship. Having friends with similar values makes it easier to stand firm. And no one should face pressure alone. Talking to a parent, a trusted adult, a teacher, a counsellor or a clinic nurse is a sign of strength. If anyone uses force, threats or money, that is abuse, and it should be reported.
