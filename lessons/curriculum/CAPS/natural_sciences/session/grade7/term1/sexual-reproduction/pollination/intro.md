Today we follow pollen on its journey from anther to stigma, starting in an apple orchard in the Elgin valley where farmers rent beehives every spring. We compare self-pollination, reliable but with little variety, and cross-pollination, which mixes two parents. Then we meet the couriers: wind, which needs dull flowers with dangling anthers and feathery stigmas, water, used by a few submerged plants, and animals, attracted by colour, scent and nectar, from bees and moths to sunbirds and the strelitzia's perch. We finish with why about a third of our food depends on pollinators and how to protect them.
