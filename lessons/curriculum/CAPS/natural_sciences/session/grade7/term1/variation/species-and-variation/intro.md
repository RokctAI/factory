Today we look at a herd of Nguni cattle, all one species yet every animal patterned differently. We define a species as a group that can breed to produce fertile offspring, which is why a mule tells us horses and donkeys are separate species, and why all humans are one species. Then we measure variation in a class: height changes smoothly and makes a bell-shaped graph, while blood groups fall into four separate groups. We finish with the two causes of variation, inherited genes and the environment, and why variation helps species survive.
