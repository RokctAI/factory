# Part 1 — Expert

This session defines a species and explores variation, the differences between individuals of the same species. The anchor is a herd of Nguni cattle on a farm in KwaZulu-Natal: every animal belongs to the same species and breed, yet each has its own pattern of colours, and Nguni herders have traditionally given many of these patterns poetic names. The governing idea is that a species is a group of organisms that can breed together to produce fertile offspring, and that within every species individuals vary, in features that can be measured on a continuous scale, like height or mass, and in features that fall into distinct groups, like blood group. The definition of species, the meaning of variation, the difference between continuous and discontinuous variation, and the causes of variation are examinable, and they lead into inheritance in Grade 12 Life Sciences.

## Subtopic: What a Species Is

A species is a group of living things of the same type that can reproduce with each other to produce offspring that are also able to reproduce, called fertile offspring. All Nguni cattle, Afrikaner cattle, Jersey cows and Brahman cattle belong to one species, because they can interbreed and their calves can grow up to have calves of their own. All humans alive today belong to one species, Homo sapiens.

The definition explains some surprising cases. A horse and a donkey can mate, but their offspring, a mule, is almost always infertile, so horses and donkeys are separate species. Lions and tigers have produced offspring in zoos, but they do not interbreed in the wild and their offspring are usually less fertile, so they remain separate species. On the other hand, dogs as different as a Great Dane and a Chihuahua belong to one species, because they can interbreed and produce fertile puppies, even if size makes it difficult in practice.

Members of a species share the same basic features and the same number and arrangement of inherited instructions in their cells. That is why their sex cells are compatible: a sperm cell and an egg cell of the same species can combine to form a normal offspring, while those of different species usually cannot.

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in the same area at the same time, such as the herd of Nguni cattle on one farm or all the springbok in the Karoo National Park. Breeds of domestic animals and varieties of crop plants are groups within a species that people have bred for particular features.

The questions for this section are with you now: the definition of a species, why a mule shows that horses and donkeys are different species, and the meaning of population.

## Subtopic: Variation Within a Species

Variation is the differences between individuals of the same species. No two individuals are exactly alike, except some identical twins and organisms produced asexually, and even they differ slightly.

Variation is easy to see in the Nguni herd: coat colours and patterns of white, black, brown, red and speckled; horn shapes; body size. Nguni herders in Zulu tradition have hundreds of names for coat patterns, comparing them to birds, eggs or landscapes, which shows how closely people have observed variation. In a Grade 7 class, learners differ in height, mass, hand span, eye colour, hair type, blood group and many other features.

Variation is divided into two types. Continuous variation is variation in which a feature can take any value within a range, with gradual differences between individuals. Height, mass, hand span, foot length and the number of leaves on a plant show continuous variation. If the heights of all the learners in a school are measured and the results plotted as a bar graph, the graph has a bell shape: most people are near the average, and fewer are very short or very tall. Continuous variation is usually caused by many inherited factors acting together, plus the effects of the environment.

Discontinuous variation is variation in which a feature falls into a few distinct groups, with no in-between values. Human blood groups, A, B, AB and O, are an example: a person belongs to exactly one group. The ability to roll the tongue, for most purposes, and whether earlobes are attached or free are often given as examples, although careful studies show these are less simple than once thought. Discontinuous variation is usually caused by one or a few inherited factors, and the environment has little effect. Results are shown on a bar graph with separate bars for each group.

Measuring variation is a key scientific skill: choose the feature, measure or classify each individual carefully, record results in a table, group continuous data into ranges such as 140 to 144 cm, and draw the right graph.

The questions for this section are with you now: the definition of variation, the difference between continuous and discontinuous variation with examples, and the graph shape for each.

## Subtopic: Causes of Variation

Variation has two main causes: inheritance and the environment.

Inherited variation is caused by differences in the genetic instructions, called genes, that offspring receive from their parents. In sexual reproduction, each parent passes on half of its genetic instructions in its sex cell, and each sex cell carries a different random selection. When a sperm and an egg fuse, a new combination is formed. This is why brothers and sisters with the same parents look different from each other, and why a cow and a bull can produce a calf with a pattern unlike either. Inherited features include blood group, natural eye colour, the shape of the earlobes and the potential for height.

Environmental variation is caused by the conditions an organism experiences during its life, such as food, climate, sunlight, exercise, disease and, in humans, culture and education. A mealie plant grown in fertile, well-watered soil grows taller and produces bigger cobs than a genetically identical plant in poor, dry soil. A person who eats well as a child usually grows taller than they would have with poor nutrition. Scars, a sun tan, languages spoken and muscles built in the gym are environmental. These features are acquired during life and are not passed on through the sex cells.

Many features are affected by both. A person inherits a range of possible heights, and nutrition and health decide where in that range they end up. Studies of identical twins, who have the same genes, help scientists separate the two causes: differences between identical twins must come from the environment.

Variation matters. It allows people to breed better crops and animals by choosing parents with useful features, as Nguni breeders have selected cattle that resist ticks and heat. And variation allows species to survive change: if a disease strikes, some individuals may vary in a way that helps them survive, and they pass this on. Without variation, a whole species could be wiped out by a single change.

The error museum, five exhibits. One: saying variation is the difference between species; it is within a species. Two: classifying height as discontinuous; it is continuous. Three: saying all variation is inherited; much is environmental. Four: thinking acquired features like gym muscles are passed on to children; they are not. Five: thinking different breeds are different species; breeds can interbreed and are one species.

The questions for this section are with you now: inherited and environmental variation with examples, features affected by both, and why variation helps a species survive.

## Subtopic: One Kind, Many Looks

Look at a herd of Nguni cattle. Some are white with black spots, some brown, some speckled like an egg. They all look different, but they are all the same kind of animal.

A species is a group of living things that can have babies together, and those babies can grow up and have babies too. All cattle are one species, even very different breeds.

A horse and a donkey can have a baby, called a mule. But a mule cannot have babies of its own. So horses and donkeys are two different species.

And people? Every human on Earth is one species. We can look very different, but we are all one kind.

Keep the herd in mind and try the questions for this part.

## Subtopic: Smooth or in Groups

Even within one species, everyone is a bit different. That is called variation.

Line up everyone in your class from shortest to tallest. There are no big jumps; the heights change smoothly, a little at a time. Anything that changes smoothly like that, with in-between values, is continuous variation: height, mass, hand size.

Now ask everyone their blood group. There are only four answers: A, B, AB or O. Nobody is half A and half B. When a feature falls into separate groups like that, it is discontinuous variation.

If you graph the heights, you get a hill shape, with most people in the middle and only a few very short or very tall. If you graph blood groups, you get separate bars.

Hold the line-up in mind and try this part's questions.

## Subtopic: Genes and Life

Why are we all different? Two reasons.

First, genes. Genes are instructions passed from parents to children. You get half from your mother and half from your father, and each brother or sister gets a different mix. That is why siblings do not look the same.

Second, life. What you eat, how much sun you get, what sports you play, and what happens to you. A plant in good soil grows taller than the same kind of plant in poor soil. A scar or a sun tan comes from life, not from genes, and you cannot pass it on to your children.

Many features come from both. Your genes set a range for how tall you could be, and good food helps you reach the top of that range.

Variation is useful. Farmers choose the best animals to breed. And if a new disease comes along, some individuals may be different enough to survive it.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A species is a group that can breed together and have fertile offspring. Variation is the differences between individuals of one species. Continuous variation, like height, changes smoothly; discontinuous variation, like blood group, falls into separate groups. Variation comes from genes passed on by parents and from the environment, and it helps species survive.
