Today we start sorting the astonishing variety of life, called biodiversity, of which South Africa has a huge share. We see why scientists classify living things by reliable shared features, not by size or habitat, which is why a whale is a mammal and not a fish. Then we fill five kingdoms on a walk through a school garden: Bacteria without a nucleus, Protista with one, Fungi that absorb their food, Plants that make food, and Animals that eat it. We finish with the tricky cases: mushrooms, the pond-dwelling euglena, and viruses that fit no kingdom at all.
