Today we explore the animals without backbones, which make up about ninety-five per cent of all animal species, in a rock pool on the Wild Coast. We meet the arthropods, with jointed legs and an exoskeleton they must moult to grow, and learn to count our way to the right class: insects with three body parts and six legs, arachnids with two body parts and eight legs, and crustaceans with ten or more legs and gills. Then we meet the soft-bodied molluscs, snails and limpets with one shell, mussels with two, and the clever, shell-less octopus.
