Today we split the flowering plants into two teams in a South African vegetable garden. A soaked bean opens into two seed leaves and a mealie grain keeps one, so the bean is a dicot and the mealie a monocot. Then we read the leaves, parallel veins or a branching net, pull up the roots, a beard of fibrous roots or a single tap root, look inside the stems, and count the flower parts in threes or in fours and fives. We sort mealies, aloes and strelitzias from beans, proteas and the marula, and learn why one feature alone can fool you.
