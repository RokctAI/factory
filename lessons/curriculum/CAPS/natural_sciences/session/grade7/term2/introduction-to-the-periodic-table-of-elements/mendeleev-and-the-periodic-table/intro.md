Today we meet the most famous chart in science, the Periodic Table. We define an element as a substance made of one kind of atom and separate elements from compounds such as water, and see how many of South Africa's riches, from gold to platinum, are elements. Then we follow Dmitri Mendeleev in 1869 as he lays out sixty-three elements on cards, leaves gaps for elements nobody had found and predicts them so well that gallium, scandium and germanium arrive to fill the spaces. We finish by reading the modern table: periods, groups, metals on the left, non-metals on the right and semi-metals on the staircase.
