### subtopic_1 — Properties of Metals
**Assistant:** Why do metals conduct electricity so well when most other solids do not?

**Tutor:** Because of the way the atoms in a metal hold on to their electrons. In a metal, the atoms are packed closely together in a regular arrangement, and each atom lets go of one or more of its outer electrons. These electrons are not attached to any particular atom; instead they move freely through the whole piece of metal, forming what scientists describe as a sea of electrons around the positively charged atoms. When a battery or a power source is connected across a metal wire, it pushes these free electrons so that they drift along the wire, and this flow of charge is an electric current. Most non-metals, such as sulphur, and compounds such as plastic, glass and rubber, hold their electrons tightly in fixed positions or in bonds between particular atoms, so there are almost no free electrons to carry a current, which makes them insulators. The same sea of electrons explains other metal properties. It carries heat quickly through the metal, so metals are good thermal conductors. It reflects light, giving metals their lustre. And because the electrons act like a flexible glue, layers of metal atoms can slide over each other when the metal is hammered or stretched without the metal shattering, which explains malleability and ductility. So one idea about electrons explains almost the whole list of metal properties.

### subtopic_2 — Properties of Non-metals
**Assistant:** How can diamond and graphite both be pure carbon but be so different?

**Tutor:** Because the properties of a substance depend not only on which atoms it contains but also on how those atoms are joined together. In diamond, every carbon atom is joined very strongly to four other carbon atoms, forming a rigid three-dimensional framework that extends throughout the whole crystal, like an endless climbing frame. To scratch or break a diamond, you have to break many of these strong bonds at once, which is why diamond is the hardest natural substance and is used on the tips of drills and saws for cutting rock. All its outer electrons are locked into these bonds, so there are no free electrons and diamond does not conduct electricity. In graphite, each carbon atom is joined strongly to only three others, forming flat sheets of six-sided rings, like chicken wire. The sheets are stacked on top of each other but held together only weakly, so they slide over each other easily, which is why graphite is soft and slippery and leaves a mark on paper as layers rub off. The fourth outer electron of each carbon atom is free to move along the sheets, so graphite conducts electricity. Different forms of the same element are called allotropes. South Africa is famous for diamonds, which formed deep in the Earth under enormous pressure and heat and were carried to the surface in volcanic pipes such as the one mined at the Big Hole in Kimberley.

### subtopic_3 — Semi-metals and Comparing the Three Groups
**Assistant:** How did silicon change the world, when it is made from ordinary sand?

**Tutor:** Because scientists learned to purify it to an extraordinary degree and to control exactly how it conducts electricity, which made modern electronics possible. Silicon is the second most common element in the Earth's crust after oxygen, found joined with oxygen as silica in sand and quartz and in many rocks. To make computer chips, silica is heated with carbon in furnaces to release silicon, which is then purified until it is more than ninety-nine point nine nine nine nine nine nine nine per cent pure, so that fewer than one atom in a thousand million is something else. Large single crystals are grown, sliced into thin discs called wafers, and then treated with tiny controlled amounts of other elements, such as boron or phosphorus, in carefully designed patterns. These patterns make transistors, microscopic switches that can turn a current on or off billions of times a second. In 1947 the first transistors were built, and by the 1970s thousands could be placed on one chip; today a cellphone chip holds many billions, each smaller than a virus. Every computer, cellphone, bank card, modern car and digital clock depends on them. Silicon solar cells use a related effect, in which light knocks electrons free and creates a current. So a semi-metal from sand became the foundation of the information age, which is why the area in California where chip companies grew up is called Silicon Valley.
