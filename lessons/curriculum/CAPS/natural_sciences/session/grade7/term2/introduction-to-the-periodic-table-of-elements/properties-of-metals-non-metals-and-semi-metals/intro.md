Today we open up an electrical plug and find all three kinds of element at work. Copper shows the superpowers of metals: lustre, conducting heat and electricity, being hammered into sheets and drawn into wires, and ringing when struck, with mercury and magnetism as the exceptions. Non-metals are the opposite team: many are gases, and the solids are dull, brittle insulators, apart from the surprise of graphite, the same carbon as diamond. Between them sit the semi-metals, led by silicon, the semiconductor from sand that runs every cellphone and solar panel. We finish with simple tests to sort an unknown element.
