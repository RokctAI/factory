Today a family explores rock pools near Port Alfred and watches the sea come in and go out, and we discover why. The Moon's gravity pulls the oceans into two bulges, so as the Earth spins, each coast gets two high tides and two low tides a day. We learn how the Sun and Moon line up to make extra-big spring tides at new and full Moon and small neap tides at the quarters, and then explore the intertidal zone, where limpets, mussels, anemones and oystercatchers are adapted to life between the tides.
