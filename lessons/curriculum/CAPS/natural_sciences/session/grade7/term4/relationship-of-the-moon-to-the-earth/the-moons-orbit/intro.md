Today a family near Mthatha watches the Moon change over a month, and we explain what they see. We meet the Moon as Earth's natural satellite, a cratered, airless world with one sixth of Earth's gravity, and follow its month-long orbit, discovering why we always see the same face. Then we learn that moonlight is reflected sunlight, model the phases from new Moon through crescent, quarter and gibbous to full Moon with a lamp and a ball, and find out how a lunar eclipse differs from the ordinary phases.
