Today a learner in Pretoria wonders why December evenings stay light so late and June evenings darken so early, and we find the answer in the Earth's 23,5-degree tilt. We model the Earth's daily spin and yearly orbit with a tilted globe and a lamp, use a torch to show how slanting sunlight spreads its energy and lowers its intensity, and prove that the seasons are caused by tilt, not distance. Then we explain the solstices and equinoxes and why day length changes more in Cape Town than in Pretoria.
