Today we boil a pot of mealies and warm a winter room to watch heat travel by convection. When part of a liquid or gas is heated, it expands, becomes less dense and rises, while cooler fluid sinks, setting up a loop called a convection current. We trace the loop with purple dye in a beaker, explain why ceilings are warmer than floors and why heaters go low, and then follow convection outdoors into sea breezes, the thermals vultures ride, and the afternoon thunderstorms of the Highveld summer.
