# Part 1 — Expert

This session asks what really comes out of the machines and appliances we use every day. The anchor is a busy hair salon in Durban: hairdryers roar, a kettle boils, ceiling lights glow, a fan turns, and outside, minibus taxis idle at the rank. The governing idea is that every device changes input energy into useful output energy, the form we want, and wasted output energy, forms we do not want, usually heat and sound that escape into the surroundings, so the useful output is always less than the input. Identifying useful and wasted energy outputs, explaining why output is always less than input, and describing ways to reduce waste are examinable.

## Subtopic: Useful and Wasted Energy Outputs

Every appliance, tool, vehicle or machine has an energy input, the energy it receives, and energy outputs, the forms it changes the energy into.

Useful energy is the output we want from the device. Wasted energy is any output that does not do the job we want. It is not destroyed; it is simply transferred to the surroundings in a form that is not useful to us, usually as heat or sound.

Look around the salon. A hairdryer takes in electrical energy. Its useful outputs are heat, to dry the hair, and kinetic energy of the fan, to blow air; its wasted output is sound, the loud roar. An electric kettle takes in electrical energy; the useful output is heat in the water; wasted outputs are heat that warms the kettle body and the air, and sound. A light bulb takes in electrical energy; the useful output is light; wasted output is heat. A ceiling fan's useful output is kinetic energy of the blades and the air; wasted outputs are heat in the motor and a humming sound.

Notice that the same form of energy can be useful in one device and wasted in another. Heat is useful in a kettle, a toaster, a hairdryer and an iron; heat is wasted in a light bulb, a fan, a phone charger and a computer. Sound is useful in a radio, a speaker and a doorbell; sound is wasted in a hairdryer or a lawnmower.

Vehicles and tools waste energy too. A minibus taxi's useful output is kinetic energy; wasted outputs are heat from the engine, exhaust and brakes, and the sound of the engine. A drill's useful output is the turning bit; wasted outputs are heat and noise.

A simple way to show useful and wasted outputs is an energy-flow diagram drawn with arrows of different widths. The input arrow enters from the left. It splits into a useful arrow pointing forwards and one or more wasted arrows bending away, labelled heat or sound. The width of each arrow shows how much energy it carries, so the widths of the output arrows always add up to the width of the input arrow. For an old light bulb, the light arrow is thin and the heat arrow is very wide.

The questions for this section are with you now: identifying input, useful output and wasted output for appliances, tools and vehicles.

## Subtopic: Output Is Always Less Than Input

The law of conservation of energy says energy cannot be created or destroyed, so the total output always equals the input. But because some of that output is always wasted, the useful output is always less than the input.

Input energy = useful output energy + wasted output energy.

For example, if a light bulb receives 100 joules of electrical energy and gives out 10 joules of light, the remaining 90 joules must have become heat. If an LED bulb receives 100 joules and gives out 40 joules of light, then 60 joules became heat.

No real machine can give out more useful energy than it takes in, and no real machine can turn all of its input into useful output. Wherever parts rub, move air, carry current through wires or vibrate, some energy changes into heat or sound.

Where does wasted energy go? It escapes into the surroundings. The heat from a car engine warms the air and road; the sound of a hairdryer makes the air vibrate, and that energy finally becomes a tiny warming of the air. Wasted energy spreads out so thinly that it cannot be collected and used again.

Energy efficiency describes how good a device is at turning input into useful output. An efficient device wastes little; an inefficient device wastes a lot. Grade 7 does not calculate efficiency, but you should be able to compare devices: an LED bulb is more efficient than a filament bulb, and an electric car's motor is more efficient than a petrol engine, which turns only about a quarter to a third of its fuel's energy into motion.

Electricity is measured in units called kilowatt-hours on the electricity meter, and every unit costs money. Appliances that heat things, such as kettles, geysers, heaters, stoves and hairdryers, use the most energy. A geyser alone can use around a third to half of a household's electricity. That is why wasted heat from appliances matters so much in a family budget, and why reducing it is one of the quickest ways to cut the monthly electricity bill.

## Subtopic: Reducing Wasted Energy

Engineers and users can reduce wasted energy in many ways.

Reduce friction: oiling or greasing moving parts, such as bicycle chains, door hinges, sewing machines and engine parts, lets them move smoothly, so less kinetic energy changes into heat and sound. Ball bearings in wheels reduce friction too.

Streamline shapes: smooth, rounded shapes let cars, taxis, aeroplanes and cyclists move through air with less air resistance, so less fuel is wasted.

Choose efficient appliances: LED bulbs instead of filament bulbs; appliances with good energy-efficiency labels, A or better on the South African energy label; a pressure cooker or a wonder bag, which cooks with less energy than an open pot.

Insulate: a geyser blanket and lagged pipes reduce heat lost from hot water; a lid on a pot keeps heat in so food boils sooner.

Good habits: switch off lights and appliances at the wall when not in use, since many waste power on standby; boil only as much water as you need; maintain vehicles, keeping tyres properly inflated so the car rolls more easily.

Reducing wasted energy saves money, reduces demand on power stations and helps prevent load-shedding, and cuts the coal burned and the pollution and carbon dioxide released.

The error museum, five exhibits. One: saying wasted energy is destroyed; it is transferred to the surroundings. Two: thinking a machine can output more than its input. Three: forgetting sound as a wasted output. Four: saying heat is always wasted; it depends on the device's purpose. Five: confusing using less energy with being efficient.

Engineers also capture some wasted energy and put it to use. In some factories, hot gases from furnaces are used to heat water or produce steam to drive generators. In winter, heat from a car engine is used to warm the inside of the car through the heater. Hybrid and electric vehicles use regenerative braking to turn kinetic energy back into stored electrical energy. Recovering waste heat does not break the conservation law; it simply finds a use for energy that would otherwise spread out uselessly.

The questions for this section are with you now: ways to reduce friction and wasted heat, choosing efficient appliances, and saving energy at home.

## Subtopic: What We Want and What We Don't

Every machine takes energy in and gives energy out. Some of what comes out is what we want: useful energy. The rest is what we don't want: wasted energy.

A hairdryer: we want hot moving air. We don't want the loud noise.

A light bulb: we want light. We don't want the heat.

A kettle: we want hot water. We don't want the heat that warms the kettle and the air.

A taxi: we want it to move. We don't want the engine heat and noise.

Wasted energy usually escapes as heat and sound.

## Subtopic: Never More Out Than In

Energy cannot be made or destroyed, so everything that goes in must come out somewhere.

But some always comes out as wasted heat and sound. So the useful part is always less than what went in.

If 100 units go into an old bulb, maybe 10 units come out as light, and 90 as heat. An LED bulb gives much more light for the same input, so it wastes less.

Wasted energy spreads out into the air and cannot be gathered up again.

Hold the bulb in mind and try this part's questions.

## Subtopic: Waste Less

Oil moving parts so they rub less. Make vehicles smooth and rounded so they slip through air.

Use LED bulbs and appliances with good energy labels. Put a lid on the pot. Wrap the geyser in a blanket.

Switch off at the wall. Even a charger with no phone on it wastes a little energy as heat.

Wasting less saves money, saves coal and helps stop load-shedding.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Every device turns input energy into useful output, what we want, and wasted output, usually heat and sound that spread into the surroundings. Energy is never destroyed, so input equals useful plus wasted output, and the useful output is always less than the input. We can waste less by reducing friction, streamlining, choosing efficient appliances, insulating and switching off.
