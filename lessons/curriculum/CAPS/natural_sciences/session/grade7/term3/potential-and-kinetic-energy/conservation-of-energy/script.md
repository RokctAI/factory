# Part 1 — Expert

This session studies the most important rule about energy, the law of conservation of energy, and follows energy as it moves within and between systems. The anchor is a roller coaster at a theme park in Gauteng: a chain lifts the car to the top of the first hill, it plunges down, climbs smaller hills and finally brakes to a stop at the platform. The governing idea is that energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be changed from one form into another or transferred from one object or system to another, so the total amount stays the same. Stating the law, tracing energy transfers within a system and between systems, and explaining where "lost" energy really goes are examinable.

## Subtopic: The Law of Conservation of Energy

The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be changed from one form to another, or transferred from one place or object to another. The total amount of energy before a change is equal to the total amount of energy after it.

This is one of the most thoroughly tested ideas in all of science. Every time scientists have measured carefully, all the energy has been accounted for. No machine has ever been built that produces more energy than it receives, and no energy has ever been found to vanish.

Follow the roller coaster. At the bottom, an electric motor drives a chain that pulls the car up the first hill. Electrical energy is changed into the gravitational potential energy of the car at the top. As the car races down, its potential energy changes into kinetic energy, and it is moving fastest at the bottom. As it climbs the next hill, kinetic energy changes back into potential energy, and it slows. At every moment, potential energy plus kinetic energy plus the energy already lost as heat and sound adds up to the energy the motor gave it.

That is why each hill after the first must be lower. The wheels rub on the track, and the car pushes through air, so some energy is changed into heat and sound: the rumble and roar of the ride. That energy is not destroyed, but it can no longer lift the car, so the car could never climb a hill higher than the first one on its own. At the end, brakes change the remaining kinetic energy into heat in the brake pads.

Notice the important word "useful". When people say energy is "lost" or "used up", they mean it has been changed into forms, mostly heat spread into the surroundings, that we cannot easily use again. The energy still exists.

The questions for this section are with you now: stating the law, the energy changes on a roller coaster, and why later hills must be lower.

## Subtopic: Energy Transfer Within a System

Energy transfer within a system means energy moving from one part of the system to another, or changing form inside it.

A wind-up radio is a good example. Turning the handle uses kinetic energy from the person's arm, which winds a spring and stores elastic potential energy, or turns a small generator to charge a battery and store chemical potential energy. When the radio plays, stored energy changes into electrical energy in the circuit, then into sound in the speaker, and a little heat everywhere.

Inside a cell phone, chemical potential energy in the battery changes into electrical energy, which becomes light in the screen, sound in the speaker, kinetic energy in the vibrating motor, radio waves to the tower, and heat, which is why a phone gets warm when used a lot.

A useful way to show transfers is an energy-flow diagram: boxes for each form, connected by arrows. For a torch: chemical potential energy in the battery, arrow, electrical energy in the circuit, arrow, light and heat from the bulb. Arrows can be drawn wider for large amounts of energy and narrower for small amounts, so it becomes obvious that most of an old-fashioned bulb's energy becomes heat rather than light.

At every step, the total stays the same. If 100 units of chemical energy leave the battery, 100 units appear as light plus heat, perhaps 10 units of light and 90 units of heat in an old filament bulb, or 40 units of light and 60 units of heat in an LED. The numbers change from bulb to bulb, but they always add up.

The questions for this section are with you now: energy changes inside a wind-up radio and a cell phone, and drawing energy-flow diagrams.

## Subtopic: Energy Transfer Between Systems

Energy also moves from one system to another. When this happens, the energy given out by one system becomes the input of the next.

A long chain links the Sun to a moving taxi. The Sun releases energy from nuclear reactions; light reaches Earth. Ancient plants changed this light into chemical potential energy. Over millions of years, buried plant and animal remains became crude oil. The oil is refined into diesel, a store of chemical potential energy. In the taxi engine, diesel burns, releasing heat; the hot gases push pistons, giving kinetic energy; the taxi moves. Finally, the brakes and tyres change kinetic energy into heat, spread into the road and air. Every system in the chain passed energy on to the next, and no energy was made or destroyed along the way.

A soccer match is full of transfers between systems. A player's leg, a biological system, transfers kinetic energy to the ball; the ball transfers kinetic energy to the net, which stretches and stores elastic potential energy before springing back. A gust of wind transfers energy to the ball, and the ball's bounce makes a sound that transfers energy to the air.

Heating is also a transfer between systems: a hot pot of pap transfers energy to the cooler air and table until they reach the same temperature; this is studied in the next topic, heat transfer.

The error museum, five exhibits. One: saying energy is destroyed when a car stops; it changes into heat. Two: thinking a machine can give out more energy than it takes in. Three: forgetting the heat and sound in an energy-flow diagram. Four: confusing transfer, energy moving to another object, with transformation, energy changing form. Five: thinking renewable energy breaks the law; the Sun's energy also comes from somewhere.

The questions for this section are with you now: the chain from the Sun to a taxi, transfers in a soccer match, and the difference between transfer and transformation.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with a roller coaster, a phone and a taxi — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Big Rule

Here is the biggest rule about energy: you can never make it and you can never destroy it. You can only change it into another form or pass it on.

Ride a roller coaster. A motor pulls you to the top of the first hill, so you gain height energy. Down you zoom, and height energy turns into speed. Up the next hill, speed turns back into height.

But every hill is lower than the one before. Why? Because the wheels rub and the air pushes back, so some energy turns into heat and noise. It is not gone, it just cannot lift you any more.

When we say energy is "used up", it really means it has spread out as heat that we cannot use again.

Keep the roller coaster in mind and try the questions for this part.

## Subtopic: Inside One Gadget

Energy also changes form inside one gadget.

Your phone: the battery stores chemical energy, which becomes electricity, then light from the screen, sound from the speaker, a buzz from the vibrator, and heat, which is why it warms up.

A wind-up radio: your arm turns the handle, the energy is stored, then it comes out as sound. The radio does not make energy; it just stores yours.

We can draw this with boxes and arrows. Whatever goes in must come out somewhere, so if 100 units go in, 100 units come out as light plus heat.

Hold the phone in mind and try this part's questions.

## Subtopic: Passing It Along

Energy passes from one thing to the next, like a relay race.

Sunlight grew ancient plants. They became oil, which became diesel. Diesel burns in a taxi engine and makes the taxi move. When the taxi brakes, the movement becomes heat in the brakes and road. Same energy all the way, just changing form.

On the soccer field, your leg passes energy to the ball, the ball passes it to the net, and the thump passes it to the air as sound.

Nobody can build a machine that runs forever, because rubbing always turns a little energy into heat, and nothing can make new energy.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another or passed from one thing to another, so the total stays the same. A roller coaster swaps height and speed, losing a little as heat and sound each time. Inside a gadget, input energy equals all the outputs added together. Between systems, one system's output is the next one's input, from the Sun to a moving taxi. No machine can run forever without input.

Hold the coaster, the phone and the taxi, and take the final questions of the lesson.
