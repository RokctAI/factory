Today we connect cells together in two different ways and see what happens to the voltage. In series, cells are joined in a line, the positive terminal of one to the negative terminal of the next, and their voltages add: two 1,5 volt cells give 3 volts, three give 4,5 volts, and a bulb connected to them glows more brightly. In parallel, all the positive terminals are joined together and all the negative terminals are joined together, and the voltage stays the same as one cell, 1,5 volts, but the cells share the work, so the bulb stays lit for longer. We will measure voltage with a voltmeter connected across the cells, draw the circuit diagrams with the correct symbols, and see why a cell put in the wrong way round cancels out part of the voltage. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences builds on circuits like these.
