Today we connect resistors and bulbs in parallel, on separate branches side by side, and find three rules that are the mirror image of series. First, the voltage across each branch is the same as the battery voltage, so every bulb gets the full push and glows as brightly as it would on its own. Second, the current splits at a junction and the branch currents add up to the total current, so branches carrying 0,2 amperes and 0,3 amperes together draw 0,5 amperes from the battery. Third, every extra branch adds another path, so the total resistance of the circuit goes down and the total current from the battery goes up. And if one bulb breaks, the others stay on, because their own paths are still complete. That is exactly how the lights and plugs in your home are wired. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences calculates these values with Ohm's law.
