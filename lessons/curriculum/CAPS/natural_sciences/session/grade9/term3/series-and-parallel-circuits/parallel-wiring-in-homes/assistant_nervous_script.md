Hey — this lesson is about the wiring in your own home, the lights and plugs you use every day, so it is very practical. Here is the calm version.

Homes are wired in parallel. That means every appliance gets the full 230 volts, each can be switched independently, and one failure does not stop the others.

South African mains is 230 V alternating current at 50 hertz. Batteries give direct current.

Same voltage for every appliance, so the current depends on resistance. Lower resistance, bigger current.

Heating appliances, like kettles, geysers, stoves, heaters and toasters, have low-resistance elements and draw big currents. A kettle draws about 8,7 A. An LED bulb draws only about 0,04 A.

Currents add up in parallel. Kettle 8,7 plus toaster 3,5 plus microwave 5,2 is 17,4 A. On a 16 A circuit, the breaker trips.

Overloading heats the cables and can start a fire. Do not overload multiplugs. Stoves and geysers have their own circuits.

Open the practice set and add the currents carefully. You've got this!
