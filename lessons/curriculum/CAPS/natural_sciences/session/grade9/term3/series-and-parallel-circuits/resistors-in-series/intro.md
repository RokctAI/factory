Today we connect resistors and bulbs in series, one after another in a single loop, and discover three rules. First, the current is the same everywhere in a series circuit: an ammeter gives the same reading before, between and after the resistors. Second, the voltage of the battery is shared, or divided, between the resistors, so two identical bulbs on a 6 volt battery each get 3 volts, and the voltages across the parts always add up to the battery voltage. Third, every resistor added makes the total resistance bigger, so the current gets smaller and the bulbs get dimmer. We will also see the big drawback of series circuits: if one bulb breaks, the whole circuit is broken and every bulb goes out, which is what used to happen with old strings of festive lights. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences calculates these values with Ohm's law.
