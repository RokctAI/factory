Hey — this lesson has two arrangements and one meter rule. Here is the calm version.

Voltage is the energy given to each bit of charge, measured in volts with a voltmeter. A voltmeter goes in parallel, across a component. An ammeter goes in series, in the loop.

Series: cells end to end, positive to negative. Voltages add.
Two 1,5 V cells: 3 V. Three: 4,5 V. Four: 6 V.
The bulb gets brighter. All the cells run down together.
A reversed cell subtracts: three cells with one reversed give 1,5 V.

Parallel: all positives joined together, all negatives joined together.
Identical cells give the same voltage as one cell: 1,5 V.
The bulb has normal brightness, but the cells share the current and last longer.
Only use identical cells in parallel.

Symbols: cell has a long thin line, positive, and a short thick line, negative. Bulb is a circle with a cross. Voltmeter is a circle with V; ammeter a circle with A.

Open the practice set and start by deciding series or parallel. You've got this!
