Hey — parallel is the mirror image of series, so you can learn them as a pair. Here is the calm version.

Parallel means separate branches, each a complete path across the battery.

Rule one: the voltage across each branch equals the battery voltage. Two bulbs in parallel on 6 V get 6 V each, so each is as bright as a single bulb.

Rule two: the current divides at a junction, and the branch currents add up to the total. If the total is 0,5 A and one branch has 0,2 A, the other has 0,3 A. A branch with less resistance takes more current.

Rule three: adding branches lowers the total resistance and increases the total current. Two 10 Ω resistors in parallel act like 5 Ω. The battery runs down faster.

If one bulb blows, the others stay on. Each branch can have its own switch. That is why homes are wired in parallel.

Too many appliances on one circuit can draw too much current: that is an overload, and circuit breakers protect against it.

Open the practice set and add up the branch currents carefully. You've got this!
