Today we apply series and parallel circuits to the wiring in your home. In South Africa, the mains supply is 230 volts, and every light and plug socket is connected in parallel, so each appliance gets the full 230 volts, can be switched on and off on its own, and keeps working when another one fails. Because the voltage is the same for every appliance, the current each one draws depends on its resistance: a kettle element with a low resistance of about 26 ohms draws a large current of almost 9 amperes, while an LED bulb draws a tiny fraction of an ampere. Every appliance switched on adds another branch, so the total current rises. Kettle, toaster and microwave together on one circuit can draw more than 17 amperes, which is why circuit breakers are there to trip. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences calculates these values with Ohm's law and power.
