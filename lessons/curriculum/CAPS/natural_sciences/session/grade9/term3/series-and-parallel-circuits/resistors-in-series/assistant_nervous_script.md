Hey — series circuits follow three rules, and once you have them, the questions are just applying them. Here is the calm version.

Series means one single loop, one path for the current.

Rule one: the current is the same everywhere. Ammeters anywhere in the loop show the same reading. Current is not used up.

Rule two: the voltage divides. The voltages across the components add up to the battery voltage. Two identical bulbs on 6 V get 3 V each. A bigger resistor gets a bigger share.

Rule three: adding resistors increases the total resistance, so the current decreases and the bulbs get dimmer. Resistances in series add: 10 Ω plus 20 Ω is 30 Ω.

Breaks: if one bulb blows or is removed, the only path is broken, and all the bulbs go out.

Uses: one switch controls everything, and fuses are always in series. Houses are not wired in series.

For missing voltages, subtract: a 12 V battery with 3 V and 4 V across two resistors leaves 5 V for the third.
