Today we look inside the electric cell, the small energy system that powers torches, remotes, clocks and cellphones. A cell contains chemicals that react inside it, and that reaction pushes electric charge around an external circuit, so stored chemical potential energy becomes electrical energy, which a bulb turns into light or a motor into movement. Every cell has two terminals, positive and negative, made of different materials, with a chemical called an electrolyte between them. You can even make a simple cell from a lemon, a galvanised nail and a copper coin. A common torch cell gives 1,5 volts. When cells are connected together, they form a battery: a car battery is six 2 volt cells connected to give 12 volts. We will also see why cells go flat, how rechargeable cells work, and why old batteries must be recycled. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences builds on electric circuits and cells.
