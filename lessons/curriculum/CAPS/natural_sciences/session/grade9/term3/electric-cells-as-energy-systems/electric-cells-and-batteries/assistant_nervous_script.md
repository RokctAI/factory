Hey — this lesson is about something in your pocket right now, so it is easier than it sounds. Here is the calm version.

A cell is an energy system. Input: chemical potential energy. Process: a chemical reaction. Output: electrical energy as a current.

The current only flows in a complete, closed circuit. Components change the electrical energy into light, heat, sound or movement.

Every cell needs two different electrodes and an electrolyte. In a lemon cell: zinc nail, copper coin, lemon juice. It gives nearly 1 volt.

A torch cell gives 1,5 volts. Its size changes how long it lasts, not its voltage.

On a circuit diagram, the long thin line is positive and the short thick line is negative.

A battery is two or more connected cells. In series, the voltages add: two 1,5 V cells give 3 V; six 2 V cells make a 12 V car battery.

A cell goes flat when its chemicals are used up. Rechargeable cells are restored by reversing the reaction. Recycle old batteries; never burn them.
