Today we study contact forces, the forces that act only when objects touch. Friction is the force that opposes sliding between two surfaces: it lets your shoes grip the ground and your brakes stop a bicycle, but it also wears out tyres and wastes energy as heat. Rough surfaces and heavier objects give more friction, while oil, wheels and smooth surfaces reduce it. Tension is the pulling force in a stretched rope, cable or chain, like the cables that hold up the Nelson Mandela Bridge in Johannesburg or the rope in a game of tug of war. Compression is the squashing force in a pillar, a table leg or a pile of bricks, pushing inwards along its length. We will see how engineers design structures so that each part carries tension or compression safely. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences calculates these forces exactly.
