Today we study magnetic force, a field force that acts between magnets and on magnetic materials such as iron, nickel and cobalt. Every magnet has two poles, a north pole and a south pole, where its force is strongest. The rule is simple: like poles repel and unlike poles attract, so north pushes north away but pulls south close. The space around a magnet where its force acts is its magnetic field, which we can map with iron filings or a small compass. Then we look at the biggest magnet you will ever stand on: the Earth itself. The Earth's magnetic field makes a compass needle point north, guides migrating birds and turtles, and shields us from harmful particles from the Sun. South Africa is home to one of the world's important magnetic observatories, in Hermanus. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences builds on magnetism and electromagnetism.
