Hey — forces are something you feel every day, so this lesson is mostly naming what you already know. Here is the calm version.

A force is a push, a pull or a twist. One object exerts it on another.

Forces can start motion, speed up, slow down or stop, change direction, and change shape.

You do not need a force to keep moving at a steady speed. You need a force to change motion. On Earth, friction slows things down.

Force is measured in newtons, N, with a spring balance. A 100 gram apple weighs about 1 N.

Draw forces as arrows: the direction shows which way, the length shows how big.

The big idea: forces come in pairs. If A pushes B, B pushes A with the same size in the opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel.

Examples: you push the ground back, the ground pushes you forward. A swimmer pushes water back. A rocket pushes gas down.

Watch the trap: a book's weight and the table's push both act on the book, so they are not a pair.

Open the practice set and name each pair carefully. You've got this!
