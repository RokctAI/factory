Today we study field forces, which act across a distance without touching, starting with the most familiar one: gravity. Every object with mass attracts every other object with mass. The Earth's gravitational pull on you is your weight, and it is measured in newtons. Your mass, measured in kilograms, is the amount of matter in your body, and it stays the same wherever you go. On Earth, each kilogram is pulled with a force of about 9,8 newtons, so a 60 kilogram learner weighs about 60 × 9,8 = 588 newtons. On the Moon, where gravity is about one sixth as strong, the same learner still has a mass of 60 kilograms but weighs only about 96 newtons. We will see why heavy and light objects fall at the same rate without air, and why astronauts float. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences builds on gravitation.
