Today we study electrostatic force, the field force between electric charges. Rub a balloon on your hair and it can stick to a wall or make your hair stand up. Rubbing transfers tiny negatively charged particles called electrons from one material to the other, so one becomes negatively charged and the other positively charged. The rule will feel familiar from magnets: like charges repel and unlike charges attract. A charged object can even attract small bits of uncharged paper. When enough charge builds up, it can jump across a gap as a spark, and the biggest spark of all is lightning. The South African Highveld has some of the most frequent lightning in the world, and lightning kills people here every summer, so we will finish with the rules that keep you safe in a thunderstorm.
