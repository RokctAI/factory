Hey — magnets are fun to play with, and the rules are short. Here is the calm version.

Magnetic materials: iron, nickel, cobalt and steel. Not aluminium, copper, gold, plastic or wood.

Every magnet has a north pole and a south pole, where the force is strongest. Cut a magnet in half and you get two complete magnets.

The rule: like poles repel, unlike poles attract.

A magnet attracts iron with either pole, so attraction does not prove something is a magnet. Repulsion does.

The magnetic field is the region around a magnet where its force acts. Show it with iron filings or a small compass. Field lines go from north to south outside the magnet, never cross, and are closest at the poles.

The Earth is a giant magnet, made by molten iron moving in its core. A compass needle points to magnetic north. The magnetic pole in the far north is actually a south magnetic pole, because it attracts the compass's north pole.

The Earth's field shields us from particles from the Sun, causes auroras near the poles, and helps animals navigate.
