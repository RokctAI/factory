Hey — you meet contact forces every minute of the day, when you walk, sit or pull a door, so this lesson is mostly about naming them. Here is the calm version.

Contact forces act only when objects touch.

Friction: the grip between surfaces. It always opposes sliding. More friction with rough surfaces and harder pressing. Less friction with oil or grease, wheels or ball bearings, and smooth surfaces. Friction makes heat.

Friction is useful: walking, braking, gripping, tyre treads. Friction is a nuisance: wear and tear, and wasted energy as heat. Air resistance is friction from air; a parachute uses it.

Tension: the pull inside a stretched rope, cable or chain. Ropes can only pull. Examples: tug of war, bridge cables, lift cables.

Compression: the squash inside something pressed from both ends. Examples: table legs, pillars, walls, your leg bones.

Concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension, so steel bars are added. Triangles keep structures rigid.
