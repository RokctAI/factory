Hey — this lesson is mostly about two words that people mix up, and you only need one formula. Here is the calm version.

Gravity is a field force: it acts without touching. Every mass attracts every other mass. Bigger masses pull harder; further apart pulls more weakly. Gravity only pulls.

Mass is the amount of matter in an object. Unit: kilograms. Instrument: a balance. It is the same everywhere.

Weight is the gravitational pull on an object. Unit: newtons. Instrument: a spring balance. It changes from place to place.

The formula: weight = mass × g. On Earth g is about 9,8 newtons per kilogram. On the Moon it is about 1,6.

So 60 kg on Earth weighs 60 × 9,8 = 588 N, and on the Moon 60 × 1,6 = 96 N. The mass stays 60 kg.

Without air, everything falls at the same rate, like the hammer and feather on the Moon. On Earth, air resistance slows light, wide things.

Astronauts float because they are in free fall around the Earth, not because gravity is gone.
