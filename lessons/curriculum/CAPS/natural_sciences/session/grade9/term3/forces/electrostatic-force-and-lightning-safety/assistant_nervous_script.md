Hey — static electricity is something you have felt in your hair and on door handles, so you already know it. Here is the calm version.

Atoms have positive protons in the middle and negative electrons around them. Normally they balance, so objects are neutral.

Rubbing moves electrons from one material to the other. The object that gains electrons becomes negative. The object that loses electrons becomes positive. Only electrons move, and no charge is created.

Examples: polythene rubbed with wool becomes negative. A balloon rubbed on hair becomes negative and the hair positive.

The rule: like charges repel, unlike charges attract. Charged objects also attract neutral bits of paper, so only repulsion proves something is charged.

Conductors, like metals and water, let charge flow. Insulators, like plastic and rubber, hold it. Earthing drains charge to the ground.

Lightning: charge builds up in a thundercloud, with the bottom negative, until a giant spark jumps to the ground. Thunder is the air exploding from the heat.

Safety: when thunder roars, go indoors. Avoid trees, open fields, water and fences. A car with a metal roof is safe.
