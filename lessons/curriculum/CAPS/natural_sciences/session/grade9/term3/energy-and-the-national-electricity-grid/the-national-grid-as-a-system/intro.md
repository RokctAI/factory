Today we follow electricity on its journey from the power station to your home. The national grid is a huge network of power lines, substations and transformers that links power stations in Mpumalanga, Limpopo and the Western Cape to towns, mines, farms and cities across South Africa. Electricity leaves the power station and is stepped up by transformers to hundreds of thousands of volts, carried long distances on tall pylons, then stepped down in stages until it reaches your wall socket at 230 volts. Why go to such high voltages? Because a high voltage lets the same power be carried by a much smaller current, and a smaller current wastes far less energy heating the wires. We will see how transformers make this possible, where energy is still lost, and what happens when the demand for electricity becomes greater than the supply, which is the reason for load shedding.
