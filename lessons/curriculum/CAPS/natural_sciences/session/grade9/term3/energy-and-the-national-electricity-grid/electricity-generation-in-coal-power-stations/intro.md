Today we follow electricity back to where most of South Africa's supply begins: a coal-fired power station. Most of the electricity used in South African homes, schools and factories is generated by burning coal in large power stations, most of them in Mpumalanga and Limpopo, close to the coalfields. Inside, coal is burned in a boiler to heat water into high-pressure steam. The steam spins a turbine, the turbine spins a generator, and the generator changes kinetic energy into electrical energy. We will trace the energy transfers step by step, see why a large part of the energy is lost as heat, find out what cooling towers are for, and weigh up the costs of coal power for the environment and for people's health. By the end you will be able to draw and explain the whole chain from a lump of coal to the light in your room.
