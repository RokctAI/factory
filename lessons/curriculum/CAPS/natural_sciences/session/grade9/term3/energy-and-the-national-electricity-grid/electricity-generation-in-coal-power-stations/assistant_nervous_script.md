Hey, a power station has just a few parts, and each one does one job. Here is the calm version.

Coal stores chemical energy.
Boiler: burning coal heats water into high-pressure steam (chemical → thermal).
Turbine: steam pushes the blades and spins the shaft (thermal → kinetic).
Generator: a magnet spins inside coils of wire and makes electricity (kinetic → electrical).
Condenser and cooling tower: the steam is cooled back into water and reused. The white cloud is water vapour.

Efficiency: only about one third of the coal's energy becomes electricity. Example: 300 MJ in, 100 MJ electrical out, 200 MJ to the surroundings as heat. Energy is never destroyed.

Environment: carbon dioxide (global warming), sulfur dioxide (acid rain), ash (lung damage), mining damage and water use.

Medupi and Kusile are the newest, biggest coal stations, each close to 4 800 MW.

If you forget the order, think: fire, steam, spin, spark. Then add the energy word for each step.
