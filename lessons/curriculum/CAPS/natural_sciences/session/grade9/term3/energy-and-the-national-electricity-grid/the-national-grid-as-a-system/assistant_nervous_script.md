Hey, the grid sounds huge, but the ideas are few. Here is the calm version.

Three parts: generation (power stations), transmission (pylons, long distance, high voltage), distribution (local lines to homes).

Transformers: two coils on an iron core; they change voltage and need alternating current.
Step-up at the power station: to about 400 000 V.
Step-down at substations and on poles: finally 230 V for homes.

Why high voltage? P = V × I. Higher voltage means smaller current for the same power, so less heating loss in the wires.
Example: 100 000 000 W ÷ 400 000 V = 250 A. At 20 000 V it would be 5 000 A.

Losses: heat in lines and transformers, plus theft.

Balance: supply must equal demand every second. If demand is too high, the frequency falls and the grid could collapse, so load shedding cuts demand. Each stage is about 1 000 MW. The evening peak is the busiest.
