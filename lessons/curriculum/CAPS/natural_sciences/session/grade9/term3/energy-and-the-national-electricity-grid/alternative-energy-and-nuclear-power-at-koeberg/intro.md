Today we look beyond coal at the other ways South Africa generates electricity. Some sources are renewable: sunlight, wind and flowing water are replaced naturally and will not run out. Solar farms in the Northern Cape, wind farms along the Eastern and Western Cape coasts, and hydroelectric stations on the Orange River already feed the national grid. Nuclear power is different: Koeberg, near Cape Town, is the only nuclear power station in Africa. It splits uranium atoms to release heat, makes steam and turns turbines just like a coal station, but without burning anything or releasing carbon dioxide while it runs. Nuclear power does, however, produce radioactive waste that must be stored safely for a very long time. We will compare the sources, explain how Koeberg works, and look at how its waste is handled, so that you can weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of each option for South Africa's future.
