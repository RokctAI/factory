Hey — this lesson is four factors and one rule for fair testing. Here is the calm version.

Resistance of a wire depends on:

Material. Copper has very low resistance, so it is used for wiring. Nichrome has about sixty times more, so it is used in heating elements.

Length. Longer wire, more resistance. Double the length, double the resistance.

Thickness. Thicker wire, less resistance. That is why stoves and geysers need thick cables.

Temperature. For metals, hotter means more resistance, because the atoms vibrate more and electrons collide more. Thermistors usually do the opposite.

To investigate: use a cell, switch, ammeter and test wire. With the same cell, a smaller current means a bigger resistance.

Fair test: change one factor only. For length, slide a crocodile clip along a nichrome wire on a metre rule. Keep material, thickness, temperature and cell the same.

Switch off between readings, because the current heats the wire and changes its resistance.
