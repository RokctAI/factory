Hey — resistance is just the idea that wires fight the current a little. Here is the calm version.

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of current. Electrons bump into the atoms of the conductor. Every conductor has some resistance; copper has very little, nichrome more, plastic an enormous amount.

Unit: the ohm, Ω.

For the same cell: more resistance, less current, dimmer bulb. Two bulbs in series are dimmer than one.

Resistance turns electrical energy into heat, and sometimes light.

Resistors: fixed ones limit the current, for example to protect an LED. Variable resistors, like a rheostat, control volume or brightness. An LDR changes with light, used in street lights. A thermistor changes with temperature, used in thermostats.

Useful heating: nichrome elements in kettles, stoves, toasters, irons and geysers. A tungsten filament glows white-hot in an old bulb, but wastes most energy as heat.

Wasted heating: in cables and power lines. Too much current in thin wires is a fire risk, so fuses melt to cut the circuit.
