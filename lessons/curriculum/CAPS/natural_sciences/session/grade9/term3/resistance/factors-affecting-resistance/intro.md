Today we investigate what decides how much resistance a wire has. Four factors matter. The type of material: copper has very low resistance, while nichrome, used in heating elements, has about sixty times more for the same size of wire. The length: a longer wire has more resistance, so doubling the length doubles the resistance. The thickness: a thicker wire has less resistance, because there is more room for the current to flow. And the temperature: for metals, a hotter wire has more resistance. We will see how to test each factor fairly in the laboratory, using a nichrome wire stretched along a metre rule, a cell and an ammeter, and how electricians and engineers use these facts when they choose the cables for a house, a geyser or a power line. Grade 10 to 12 Physical Sciences turns these ideas into calculations.
