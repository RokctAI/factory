Today we study the devices that stop electricity from causing fires and electrocution. When too many appliances are switched on together, or when a fault lets live and neutral wires touch, the current in a circuit becomes too large. That is an overload or a short circuit, and large currents heat cables until their insulation melts and fires start. A fuse is a thin wire that melts and breaks the circuit when the current is too large; it must then be replaced. A circuit breaker is an automatic switch that trips open instead, and can simply be reset once the fault is fixed. The earth leakage unit protects people: it compares the current going out on the live wire with the current returning on the neutral wire, and if even 30 milliamperes is leaking away, perhaps through a person, it switches off in a fraction of a second.
