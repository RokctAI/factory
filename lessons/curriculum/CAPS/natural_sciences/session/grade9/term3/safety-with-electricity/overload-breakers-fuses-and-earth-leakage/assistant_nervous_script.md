Hey — this lesson is about three safety devices, and each has one simple job. Here is the calm version.

Too much current overheats cables and can cause fires. Two causes:
Overload: too many appliances on one circuit, so the currents add up too high.
Short circuit: live touches neutral directly, so a huge current flows.

Fuse: a thin wire that melts when the current is too big, breaking the circuit. Replace it with the same rating after fixing the fault. Never use wire or foil.

Circuit breaker: an automatic switch that trips when the current is too big. Reset it after fixing the fault. Typical ratings: 10 A for lights, 16 or 20 A for plugs.

Both are in the live wire, and both protect the wiring.

Earth leakage unit: protects people. It compares live and neutral currents. If about 30 mA leaks away, perhaps through a person, it switches off in a fraction of a second. Press its test button every few months.

When something trips: unplug, reset, plug back in one at a time. Never bypass protection.
