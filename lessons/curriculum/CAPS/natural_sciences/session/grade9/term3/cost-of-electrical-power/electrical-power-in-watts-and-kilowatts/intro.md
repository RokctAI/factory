Every appliance in your home has a small label or plate that gives a number followed by the letter W. A cellphone charger might say 10 W, a fridge 150 W, and a kettle 2 000 W. That number is the power rating of the appliance, and it tells you how quickly the appliance transfers electrical energy into other forms such as heat, light, sound or movement. Today we find out what electrical power really means, why it is measured in watts and kilowatts, and how to change between the two units. We will compare the power ratings of everyday appliances and discover why the ones that heat things, like kettles, stoves, heaters and geysers, use far more power than the ones that give light or charge your phone. By the end you will be able to read any appliance label and know whether it is a big or a small user of electricity.
