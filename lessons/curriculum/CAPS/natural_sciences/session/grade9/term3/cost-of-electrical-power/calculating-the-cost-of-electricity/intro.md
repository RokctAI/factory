Every month, families across South Africa buy electricity, either by loading prepaid units onto a meter or by paying a bill from Eskom or the municipality. But what exactly are we paying for? We are not paying for volts or amperes, and not even for watts. We pay for the amount of energy we use, counted in units called kilowatt-hours. Today we find out what a kilowatt-hour is and learn a simple three-step calculation: multiply the power rating of an appliance in kilowatts by the number of hours it is used, then multiply by the price of one unit. With this you will be able to work out what it costs to boil the kettle, run the geyser or leave the television on all evening, and you will see why a few small changes at home can save a family a surprising amount of money every month.
