Hey, power is a short idea once you see it. Here is the calm version.

Power = how fast energy is transferred.
Power = energy ÷ time. One watt = one joule every second.

For electrical things: P = V × I (volts × amperes = watts).
Example: 3 V × 0,5 A = 1,5 W.

1 kW = 1 000 W.
W to kW: divide by 1 000 (2 000 W = 2 kW).
kW to W: multiply by 1 000 (3 kW = 3 000 W).

Big users make heat: kettle, iron, heater, stove, geyser (thousands of watts).
Small users: LED lights, chargers, TVs (a few watts up to about 150 W).

Current from power: I = P ÷ V. A 2 300 W kettle on 230 V draws 10 A.

Remember the difference: power is the speed of energy use; energy is the total. A small-power appliance left on all day can still use a lot of energy.
