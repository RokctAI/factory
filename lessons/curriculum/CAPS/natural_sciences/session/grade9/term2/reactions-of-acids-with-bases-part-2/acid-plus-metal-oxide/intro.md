Today we study what happens when an acid reacts with a metal oxide. Because metal oxides are bases, this is a neutralisation, and the general word equation is acid + metal oxide → salt + water. The classic laboratory example uses black copper(II) oxide powder and warm dilute sulfuric acid: the black powder disappears and the liquid turns a clear, bright blue, because copper sulfate forms. The balanced equation is CuO + H2SO4 → CuSO4 + H2O. Evaporate the solution carefully and beautiful blue crystals of copper sulfate appear. We will also balance the reactions of magnesium oxide with hydrochloric acid, MgO + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2O, and of rust with hydrochloric acid, which is how steel is cleaned before it is galvanised. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry builds on preparing salts in this way.
