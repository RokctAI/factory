Hey — this lesson is neutralisation again, with a metal oxide as the base. Here is the calm version.

General equation: acid + metal oxide → salt + water. No gas, no fizzing.

Name the salt: metal from the oxide, then chloride, sulfate or nitrate from the acid.

Key examples to know:
CuO + H2SO4 → CuSO4 + H2O. Black powder disappears; blue solution forms.
MgO + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2O. The 2 is because the salt has two chlorines.
Fe2O3 + 6HCl → 2FeCl3 + 3H2O. This removes rust from steel.

Shortcut: oxides of magnesium, calcium, copper and zinc need 2HCl, but just one H2SO4.

Making copper sulfate crystals:
Warm the acid.
Add copper oxide until some is left over, so all the acid is used.
Filter off the extra.
Evaporate some water, then cool slowly.
Do not boil it dry.

Uses: cleaning steel before galvanising, and copper sulfate as a fungicide for vineyards.
