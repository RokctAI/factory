Hey — this lesson uses everything you know about acids and bases on a real problem. Here is the calm version.

Clean rain is slightly acidic, about pH 5,6, because of carbon dioxide. Acid rain is lower than that, sometimes about pH 4.

Cause: burning coal and fuels. Sulfur in coal burns: S + O2 → SO2. Hot engines make nitrogen oxides. These non-metal oxides form sulfuric and nitric acid in rain. Mpumalanga, with many coal power stations, is badly affected, and wind spreads it far.

Effects: limestone and marble wear away because calcium carbonate reacts with acid; metals rust faster; soil loses nutrients and plants suffer; lakes become too acidic for fish; people's lungs are harmed.

Solution for soil: farmers add agricultural lime, which is calcium carbonate, a base. It neutralises the acid and raises the pH to about 6 to 7 for crops. Test the soil first and lime months before planting.

Solution at the source: scrubbers spray smoke with limestone to trap sulfur dioxide, as at Kusile. Also cleaner fuels, catalytic converters and solar and wind power.
