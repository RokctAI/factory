Today we apply the chemistry of acids and bases to two real problems in South Africa: acid rain and acidic soil. When coal is burnt in power stations and petrol and diesel in vehicles, sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are released. These non-metal oxides dissolve in rainwater and form sulfuric and nitric acids, so rain that is normally about pH 5,6 can drop to pH 4 or lower. Acid rain eats into limestone and marble buildings, corrodes metal, harms plants and kills fish in lakes. The Mpumalanga Highveld, with its many coal power stations, is one of the most affected areas. The remedy for acidic soil is a base: farmers spread crushed limestone, calcium carbonate, which neutralises the acid and raises the pH to suit their crops. Power stations can use the same reaction to scrub sulfur dioxide from their smoke. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry and geography build on this.
