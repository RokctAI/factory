Hey — equations have two kinds of numbers, and once you keep them apart the rest is just counting. Here is the calm version.

The subscript is the small number inside a formula. It says how many atoms are in one particle. In H2O, two hydrogens and one oxygen. Never change it: that would make a different substance.

The coefficient is the big number in front. It says how many particles. 2H2O is two water molecules. You may change coefficients to balance.

To count atoms: coefficient times subscript. 2H2O has four hydrogens and two oxygens. 3CO2 has three carbons and six oxygens. Brackets get multiplied too. If an element appears twice on one side, add.

Make a table: element, left, right. If every row matches, the equation is balanced.

Why must it match? Atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed, so mass is conserved in a closed system. 2,4 grams of magnesium plus 1,6 grams of oxygen gives 4,0 grams of magnesium oxide.

Burning things seem to lose mass because gases escape; magnesium seems to gain because oxygen joins it.
