Today we learn three ways to show what happens in a chemical reaction. In every reaction the starting substances, the reactants, are changed into new substances, the products, because bonds between atoms are broken and new bonds are made. We can show this with particle models, coloured balls joined by sticks, so you can see the atoms being regrouped. We can write it as a word equation, such as magnesium plus oxygen gives magnesium oxide. And we can write it as a chemical equation with symbols and formulas: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO. We will see the signs that tell you a reaction has happened, read the plus sign and the arrow correctly, and practise moving between models, words and symbols for reactions you know, from burning methane in a gas stove to photosynthesis in a leaf.
