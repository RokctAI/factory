Today we learn to balance chemical equations, the skill that turns a correct list of reactants and products into a true description of a reaction. An unbalanced equation such as H2 + O2 → H2O seems to lose an oxygen atom, which the law of conservation of mass forbids. We fix it by changing only the coefficients, the big numbers in front of the formulae, never the subscripts. You will learn a step-by-step method: write the correct formulae, take a stocktake of atoms, balance one element at a time, leave hydrogen and oxygen until late, and check at the end. We will balance the burning of hydrogen, of methane in a gas stove and of propane in a gas braai, and the rusting of iron, 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3. Grade 10 Physical Sciences expects this skill to be automatic.
