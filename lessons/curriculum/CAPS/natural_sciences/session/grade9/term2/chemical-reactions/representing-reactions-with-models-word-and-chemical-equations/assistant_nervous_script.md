Hey — equations can look like a new language, but they are just a short way of saying what happened. Here is the calm version.

In a chemical reaction, reactants turn into new products. Bonds break and new bonds form, but the atoms are only rearranged. None are created or destroyed.

Clues that a reaction happened: bubbles of gas, a colour change, a new solid, heat or light, and it is hard to undo. Melting and dissolving are physical changes, not reactions.

Three ways to show a reaction. A model: coloured balls with a key, carbon black, hydrogen white, oxygen red. A word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide; plus means and, the arrow means produces. A chemical equation: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO, with formulas and numbers in front.

Remember the gases that come in pairs: H2, O2, N2, Cl2. State symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

To check an equation, count each kind of atom on each side. They should match.
