Today we look closely at the two kinds of numbers in a chemical equation and at the law that governs every reaction. A subscript, the small number inside a formula, tells you how many atoms of an element are in one particle, and it can never be changed without changing the substance. A coefficient, the large number in front of a formula, tells you how many particles take part, so in 3CO2 there are three carbon atoms and six oxygen atoms. Using both, we can take a stocktake of every atom on each side of an equation. The reason the stocktake must match is the law of conservation of mass: in a reaction atoms are only rearranged, never created or destroyed, so 2,4 grams of magnesium and 1,6 grams of oxygen make exactly 4,0 grams of magnesium oxide. Grade 10 Physical Sciences builds all quantitative chemistry on this law.
