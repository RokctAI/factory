Today we learn to read a chemical formula as a recipe. A compound is two or more elements chemically joined in a fixed ratio, and its formula records exactly that ratio with symbols and small numbers. H2O says two hydrogen atoms for every one oxygen atom, every time, whether the water comes from a tap in Soweto or a glacier. CO2 says one carbon to two oxygen; NH3, one nitrogen to three hydrogen. We will separate elements, compounds and molecules, learn the rule that a small subscript belongs only to the symbol right before it, unless brackets gather a group, and count the atoms in formulas as large as glucose, C6H12O6. We will also see why a change of a single number makes a completely different substance: H2O is water, H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide. Grade 10 Physical Sciences uses formulas in every calculation.
