Today we learn to name compounds the way chemists do, so that a name tells you the formula and a formula tells you the name. For a compound of a metal and a non-metal, the metal keeps its name and the non-metal's ending changes to -ide: sodium and chlorine make sodium chloride, magnesium and oxygen make magnesium oxide. For a compound of two non-metals, Greek prefixes count the atoms: mono for one, di for two, tri for three, tetra for four. That is why CO is carbon monoxide, CO2 carbon dioxide, SO2 sulfur dioxide and SO3 sulfur trioxide, and why the difference of one prefix can separate a deadly gas from a harmless one. We will also meet the -ate endings of compounds with oxygen groups, such as calcium carbonate, and common names like water and ammonia. Grade 10 Physical Sciences extends these naming rules.
