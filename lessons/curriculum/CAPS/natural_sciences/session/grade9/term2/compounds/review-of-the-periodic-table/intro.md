Today we review the Periodic Table, the map that every chemistry lesson from here to Grade 12 relies on. All matter is built from just 118 known elements, and the table arranges them in order of atomic number, the number of protons in an atom, into 18 vertical groups and 7 horizontal periods. Elements in the same group behave alike, which is why sodium and potassium both fizz in water and why helium, neon and argon hardly react at all. We will read element names and symbols, including the Latin ones such as Fe for iron and Na for sodium, sort the elements into metals, non-metals and semi-metals by their position and properties, and use the atomic number and mass number to count the protons, electrons and neutrons in an atom. Grade 10 Physical Sciences opens with exactly this table.
