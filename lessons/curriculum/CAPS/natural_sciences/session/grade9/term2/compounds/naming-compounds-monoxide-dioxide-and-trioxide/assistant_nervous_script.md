Hey — naming compounds is just a few rules, used again and again. Here is the calm version.

Metal plus non-metal: the metal keeps its name and comes first; the non-metal ends in -ide. NaCl, sodium chloride. MgO, magnesium oxide. CaS, calcium sulfide. Oxygen to oxide, chlorine to chloride, sulfur to sulfide.

Two non-metals: count with prefixes. Mono one, di two, tri three, tetra four, penta five. CO, carbon monoxide. CO2, carbon dioxide. SO2, sulfur dioxide. SO3, sulfur trioxide. Never use mono for the first element, and mono-oxide is shortened to monoxide.

Name to formula: each prefix becomes a subscript. Sulfur trioxide is SO3; dinitrogen monoxide is N2O.

Groups with oxygen usually end in -ate: calcium carbonate, CaCO3; copper sulfate, CuSO4. Hydroxide, OH, is the exception that ends in -ide.

Common names: water, ammonia, methane.

When you see a formula, ask: two elements or more, metal or non-metal, any groups? The answer tells you which rule to use.
