Hey — this lesson has one pattern, and once you have it, every metal follows it. Here is the calm version.

The pattern: metal + oxygen → metal oxide. Two reactants, one product. The name is the metal plus the word oxide.

Magnesium: hold the ribbon with tongs in a flame. It burns with a very bright white light, so do not stare at it. A white powder is left. Word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide. Balanced: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO.

Iron: steel wool glows and sparks orange in a flame because its thin strands have a large surface area. Word equation: iron + oxygen → iron oxide. Balanced at Grade 9: 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3.

Both products are heavier than the metal, because oxygen from the air joined on. For example, 2,4 g of magnesium becomes 4,0 g of magnesium oxide; the extra 1,6 g is oxygen.

Metal oxides are bases. Magnesium oxide in water turns universal indicator blue.

Remember: oxygen gas is O2, and check your balancing with a quick count of each element.
