Today we study rust, the slow reaction of iron with oxygen that costs South Africa billions of rand every year in damaged bridges, cars, roofs and pipes. Rust forms only when iron meets oxygen and water together, and it forms much faster near the sea, where salt spray from the air settles on metal at places like Durban and Gqeberha. In words: iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron(III) oxide. A classic experiment with three iron nails shows that removing either the water or the oxygen stops rusting completely. Because rust is flaky and falls away, it exposes fresh iron and the damage keeps spreading. Then we look at the ways to prevent it: painting, oiling and plastic coatings, and electroplating with chromium or galvanising with zinc. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry explains the electricity behind these methods.
