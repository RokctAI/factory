Hey — rust is a lesson you can see all around you, on gates, roofs and old cars, so the ideas stick quickly. Here is the calm version.

Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide. Only iron and steel rust. Word equation: iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron(III) oxide.

Rust needs two things together: oxygen and water. Salt, acid and warmth make it faster, which is why coastal cars rust sooner.

The experiment: three nails. Tap water open to air rusts. Boiled water under oil, with no oxygen, does not rust. Dry air with calcium chloride, with no water, does not rust. Compare each pair to see which condition is needed.

Rust flakes off and exposes fresh iron, so it keeps spreading.

Prevention: keep oxygen and water out. Paint for bridges and cars. Oil for chains. Plastic for fences. Chromium electroplating for taps. Zinc galvanising for roofs and poles.

Zinc is special. It is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes instead of the iron even when scratched. That is sacrificial protection.

Open the practice set and start with the three nails. You've got this!
