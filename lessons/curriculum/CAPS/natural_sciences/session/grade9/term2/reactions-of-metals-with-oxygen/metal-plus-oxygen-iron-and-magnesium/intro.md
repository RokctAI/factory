Today we study what happens when metals react with oxygen. Every such reaction follows one general pattern: metal + oxygen → metal oxide. The name of the product always comes from the metal, with the ending oxide, so magnesium gives magnesium oxide and iron gives iron oxide. We will look closely at two metals. Magnesium ribbon burns with a blinding white flame and leaves a white powder, 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO. Steel wool, which is mostly iron, glows and throws orange sparks and leaves a dark solid, written in Grade 9 as 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3. We will see why the solid product weighs more than the metal, why some metals react far more readily than others, and why metal oxides behave as bases. Grade 10 Physical Sciences builds on these reactions.
