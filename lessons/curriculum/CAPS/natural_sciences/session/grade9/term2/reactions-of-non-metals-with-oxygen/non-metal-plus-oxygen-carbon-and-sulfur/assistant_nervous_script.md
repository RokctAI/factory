Hey — this is the twin of the metals lesson, so you already know half of it. Here is the calm version.

The pattern: non-metal + oxygen → non-metal oxide. One product, usually a gas.

Carbon: charcoal glows brightly in oxygen and seems to disappear, because the product is a gas. Word equation: carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide. Balanced: C + O2 → CO2. Test: carbon dioxide turns limewater milky.

With too little oxygen, carbon makes carbon monoxide: 2C + O2 → 2CO. It is poisonous and has no smell, so never use a fire or brazier in a closed room.

Sulfur: it melts, then burns with a blue flame and makes a choking gas. Word equation: sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide. Balanced: S + O2 → SO2. Do it in a fume cupboard.

The big idea: non-metal oxides dissolve in water to make acids, so universal indicator turns yellow, orange or red. Metal oxides make bases and turn it blue. Sulfur dioxide from burning coal causes acid rain.
