Today we study what happens when non-metals react with oxygen. The general reaction is non-metal + oxygen → non-metal oxide, and unlike the solid metal oxides of the last topic, the products are usually gases. Carbon, as glowing charcoal, burns to form carbon dioxide, C + O2 → CO2, the gas that turns limewater milky. With too little oxygen it forms poisonous carbon monoxide instead. Sulfur melts and burns with a pale blue flame to form sulfur dioxide, S + O2 → SO2, a choking gas that must be made in a fume cupboard. Both oxides dissolve in water to make acidic solutions, which is the opposite of metal oxides and the reason that burning coal at power stations on the Mpumalanga Highveld causes acid rain. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry builds on these reactions.
