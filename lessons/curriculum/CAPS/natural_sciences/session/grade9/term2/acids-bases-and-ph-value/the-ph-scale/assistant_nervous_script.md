Hey — the pH scale is just a number line, and you already know most of the things on it from your kitchen. Here is the calm version.

The scale runs from 1 to 14. Seven is neutral, like pure water. Below 7 is acidic. Above 7 is basic, also called alkaline.

The further from 7, the stronger. pH 1 is a very strong acid; pH 6 is a weak one. pH 14 is a very strong base; pH 8 is a weak one.

Learn a few anchors: lemon juice about 2, vinegar about 3, milk about 6,5, water 7, blood 7,4, bicarbonate of soda about 8 or 9, ammonia about 11, drain cleaner about 13 or 14.

Each step is ten times. pH 3 is ten times more acidic than pH 4, and a hundred times more acidic than pH 5.

Acids taste sour and bases feel soapy, but never taste anything in the lab. Strong bases are just as dangerous as strong acids.

Safety: goggles on, add acid to water, and rinse any splash with plenty of running water.

Open the practice set and start by sorting substances into acid, neutral and base. You've got this!
