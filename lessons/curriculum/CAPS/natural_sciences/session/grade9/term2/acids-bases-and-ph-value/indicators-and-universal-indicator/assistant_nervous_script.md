Hey — this lesson is mostly about colours, and colours are easy to picture. Here is the calm version.

An indicator is a substance that changes colour depending on pH. We need them because acids and bases usually look like water.

Litmus comes as red and blue paper strips. Acids turn blue litmus red. Bases turn red litmus blue. If neither changes, the solution is neutral. You need both colours.

Phenolphthalein: colourless in acids and neutral solutions, pink in bases.

Universal indicator is a mixture of indicators, so it gives a rainbow and an approximate pH: red for pH 1 to 3, orange 4 to 5, yellow 6, green 7, blue 8 to 10, purple 11 to 14. Compare with the colour chart.

Red cabbage: soak chopped leaves in hot water and strain. It turns pink in acid, stays purple in water, and goes blue-green, green or yellow in bases. Turmeric turns red-brown in bases.

Natural indicators are cheap and safe, but their colours are less sharp, so match carefully.
