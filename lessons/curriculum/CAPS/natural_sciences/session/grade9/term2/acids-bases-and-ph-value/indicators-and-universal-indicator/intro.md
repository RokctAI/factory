Today we learn how to see the invisible. Acids, bases and neutral solutions usually look exactly like water, so chemists use indicators, substances that change colour depending on pH. Litmus is the simplest: acids turn blue litmus red and bases turn red litmus blue. Phenolphthalein stays colourless in acids and turns pink in bases. Universal indicator is a mixture of indicators that gives a whole rainbow: red for strong acids, through orange and yellow for weak acids, green at neutral pH 7, blue for weak bases and purple for strong bases, so it gives an approximate pH value and not just acid or base. We will also make a natural indicator from red cabbage, which turns pink in vinegar and green in bicarbonate of soda solution, and look at others such as beetroot and turmeric. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry uses indicators in titrations.
