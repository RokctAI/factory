Today we meet the pH scale, the number line chemists use to say how acidic or basic a solution is. On the scale used in Grade 9, which runs from 1 to 14, a pH of 7 is neutral, like pure water. Values below 7 are acidic, and the lower the number, the stronger the acid: stomach acid is about 1 to 2, lemon juice about 2, vinegar about 3. Values above 7 are basic, and the higher the number, the stronger the base: bicarbonate of soda is about 8 to 9, household ammonia about 11, and drain cleaner about 13 to 14. Each step on the scale is a tenfold change, so a pH 3 solution is ten times more acidic than a pH 4 solution. We will also compare the properties of acids and bases and learn to handle them safely. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry builds on the pH scale.
