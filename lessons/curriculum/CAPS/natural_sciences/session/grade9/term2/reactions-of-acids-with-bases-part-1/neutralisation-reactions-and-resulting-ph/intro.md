Today we study neutralisation, the reaction in which an acid and a base cancel each other out. The general word equation is acid + base → salt + water, and a typical example is hydrochloric acid reacting with sodium hydroxide to form sodium chloride, ordinary table salt, and water: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O. When exactly the right amounts react, the resulting solution is neutral, with a pH of 7, and universal indicator turns green. With too little base the solution stays acidic, below pH 7; with too much it becomes basic, above pH 7. We will follow a neutralisation drop by drop with universal indicator, learn how to name the salt, notice that the mixture warms up, and see neutralisation at work in indigestion tablets, toothpaste and farmers' fields. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry turns this into precise titrations.
