Hey — neutralisation is one pattern and one colour walk. Here is the calm version.

The pattern: acid + base → salt + water. Every time.

Example: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water. HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O.

Why does it work? The acid gives H+ ions, the base gives OH− ions, and they join to make water. Nothing acidic or basic is left if the amounts are right.

The colour walk with universal indicator: start with acid, red. Add base slowly. Orange, yellow, then green at pH 7, exactly neutral. Keep going and it turns blue and purple, because there is too much base.

So the resulting pH depends on amounts: too little base, below 7; just right, 7; too much base, above 7.

Naming the salt: metal from the base, then chloride from hydrochloric acid, sulfate from sulfuric acid, nitrate from nitric acid.

Everyday uses: antacids for heartburn, toothpaste, lime on acidic soil.

Watch the balancing with sulfuric acid: H2SO4 + 2NaOH → Na2SO4 + 2H2O.
