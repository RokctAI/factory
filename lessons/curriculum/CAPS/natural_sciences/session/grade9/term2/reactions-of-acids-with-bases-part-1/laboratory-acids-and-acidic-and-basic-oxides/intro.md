Today we meet the two acids you will use most in the school laboratory, hydrochloric acid, HCl, and sulfuric acid, H2SO4, and then sort a whole range of compounds into acids and bases. Hydrochloric acid is the acid in your stomach and the pool acid sold at hardware stores. Sulfuric acid fills car batteries and is used in vast amounts to make fertiliser. Both are strong acids, harmless enough when dilute and handled with care, but dangerous when concentrated. Then we bring together everything from this term into one rule: non-metal oxides such as carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide tend to be acidic, while metal oxides, metal hydroxides and metal carbonates, such as magnesium oxide, sodium hydroxide and limestone, are bases. That rule lets you predict how a substance will behave from its formula. Grade 10 to 12 chemistry builds on this classification.
