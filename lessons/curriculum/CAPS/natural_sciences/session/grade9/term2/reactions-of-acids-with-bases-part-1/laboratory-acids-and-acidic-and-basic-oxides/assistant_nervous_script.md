Hey — this lesson is two acids and one sorting rule, and you have already met most of the pieces this term. Here is the calm version.

Hydrochloric acid, HCl: in your stomach for digestion, and sold as pool acid. Concentrated, it gives off stinging fumes.

Sulfuric acid, H2SO4: in car batteries and used to make fertilisers. Concentrated, it is oily, has no smell, and pulls water out of things, so it burns badly.

Both are strong acids. Both turn blue litmus red, react with bases to make a salt and water, fizz with magnesium to give hydrogen, and fizz with carbonates to give carbon dioxide. Always add acid to water.

The sorting rule:
Non-metal oxides tend to be acidic: CO2, SO2.
Metal oxides are bases: MgO, CaO.
Metal hydroxides are bases: NaOH, Ca(OH)2.
Metal carbonates are bases: CaCO3, bicarbonate of soda.

Formula trick: H in front, acid. Metal with O, OH or CO3, base.

Opposites react: carbon dioxide, an acidic oxide, turns limewater, a base, milky.
