South Africa's modern economy was built on mining. The discovery of diamonds near Kimberley and gold on the Witwatersrand in the late 1800s turned a farming region into a centre of world industry, and Johannesburg grew from a mining camp into the country's largest city. Mining still provides hundreds of thousands of jobs, earns much of the country's export income, and supplies the coal that generates most of our electricity. But mining also leaves scars: huge holes and waste dumps, rivers polluted with acid water, dust in the air and in miners' lungs, and communities left struggling when mines close. Today we look at where and what South Africa mines and why it matters, then examine the impacts of mining on the land, water, air and people. Finally, we look at what can be done, by law, by mining companies and by communities, to make mining safer and more responsible.
