# Part 1 — Expert

This session explains how mineral resources are extracted from the lithosphere by mining. It defines an ore, describes the main methods of surface and underground mining and the factors that decide which method is used, and identifies minerals that can be used in their natural form after only simple processing. The anchor facts are that an ore is a rock or mineral deposit containing enough of a valuable mineral or metal to be mined at a profit; that surface mining, including open-pit and opencast strip mining, removes the overlying rock to reach ore near the surface; that underground mining reaches deeper ore through vertical shafts and horizontal tunnels; that alluvial and marine mining recover minerals from river gravels and the sea floor; that mined ore is crushed and the valuable mineral concentrated before it is refined; and that some minerals, such as diamonds, coal, salt, clay, sand, gravel and building stone, can be used in their natural form.

## Subtopic: Ores and Mining Methods

The lithosphere contains many useful minerals, but usually they are spread thinly through ordinary rock. Mining is only worthwhile where geological processes have concentrated a mineral in one place. A rock or deposit that contains enough of a valuable mineral to be mined and processed at a profit is called an ore. Whether a deposit counts as an ore depends not only on how much mineral it contains, but also on the price of the mineral and the cost of mining it. When the price of gold rises, rock with less gold in it can become ore.

The amount of valuable material in an ore is called its grade. Iron ore at Sishen contains about 60 to 65 per cent iron. Gold ore is very low grade by comparison: typical South African gold ore contains about 5 grams of gold in every tonne of rock, so about 200 tonnes of rock must be mined and processed to obtain 1 kilogram of gold.

Before mining begins, geologists search for and investigate deposits. This is called prospecting and exploration. They study maps and satellite images, take rock samples and drill boreholes to find out how large the deposit is, how deep it lies and how rich it is. Mining companies must also obtain a mining right from the government and do an environmental impact assessment before they may start.

The main mining methods are surface mining, underground mining, and alluvial and marine mining. The choice depends on how deep the ore is, its shape, its grade, the strength of the surrounding rock and the cost.

Surface mining is used when the ore is near the surface. The soil and rock above the ore, called overburden, are removed and the ore is dug out. It is usually cheaper and safer than underground mining, but it disturbs a large area of land.

Underground mining is used when the ore lies too deep for surface mining to be economical. Miners reach the ore through shafts and tunnels. It disturbs less of the surface but is more expensive and more dangerous.

Mining is one of the most important parts of South Africa's economy. It employs hundreds of thousands of people, earns a large share of the country's export income, and supports many other industries, from engineering and transport to banking. Johannesburg itself grew from a mining camp after gold was discovered on the Witwatersrand in 1886.

## Subtopic: Surface and Underground Mining

There are several types of surface mining.

Open-pit mining digs a huge, deepening hole in steps called benches, so that trucks can drive down a spiral road. Rock is broken by drilling and blasting, then loaded into enormous trucks by shovels and taken to the crusher. The Sishen iron ore mine near Kathu in the Northern Cape is one of the largest open pits in the world, about 14 kilometres long. The Big Hole at Kimberley was an early open pit dug by hand between 1871 and 1914 to a depth of about 215 metres.

Opencast strip mining is used for flat layers, such as many coal seams in Mpumalanga. The overburden is removed in long strips, the coal is dug out, and the overburden from the next strip is placed into the empty strip behind, so that the land can be rehabilitated as mining moves along.

Quarrying is surface mining of building materials such as granite, limestone, sand and gravel.

Underground mining begins with a vertical shaft sunk from the surface, sometimes more than two kilometres deep in one stage. Lifts called cages carry miners and equipment, and skips carry ore up. Horizontal tunnels lead from the shaft to the ore body. In the gold mines of the Witwatersrand, the gold-bearing layer, called the reef, is often less than a metre thick and slopes deep into the Earth, so miners work in narrow, low spaces called stopes. Mponeng mine, west of Johannesburg, reaches almost four kilometres below the surface, the deepest mine in the world.

Underground mining brings serious challenges. The rock at great depth is hot, about 55 to 60 °C, so mines pump chilled air and ice-cold water underground. Fresh air must be blown through the tunnels to remove dust and gases such as methane in coal mines. The enormous pressure of the rock can cause sudden collapses called rockbursts, so tunnels are supported with steel, timber, concrete and rock bolts. Coal is often mined underground using the bord and pillar method, in which pillars of coal are left standing to hold up the roof.

Alluvial mining recovers minerals such as diamonds and gold from gravels deposited by rivers, as along the Orange and Vaal Rivers. Marine mining uses ships to suck up diamond-bearing gravels from the sea floor off the west coast of South Africa and Namibia.

## Subtopic: Minerals Used in Their Natural Form

Many minerals must be refined, using physical or chemical processes, before they can be used. You will study refining in the next lesson. But some minerals and rocks can be used in their natural form, after only simple physical processing such as washing, crushing, sorting, cutting or polishing.

Diamonds are a form of pure carbon. After they are separated from the rock, they are sorted, and gem-quality diamonds are cut and polished to make jewellery. Industrial diamonds, the hardest natural substance, are used on the tips of drill bits and the edges of cutting saws.

Coal is used in its natural form after being crushed, washed and sorted by size. Most South African coal is burned in power stations to generate electricity, and coal is also exported and used in industry.

Salt, sodium chloride, is obtained by evaporating sea water in shallow pans along the coast, or from salt pans in dry inland areas, and only needs to be washed, dried and crushed.

Clay is used to make bricks, tiles, pottery and cement. Sand and gravel are used in concrete, glass and roads. Granite, sandstone and marble are cut into blocks and slabs for buildings, floors, kitchen counters and monuments. Limestone is crushed and used in cement, in steelmaking and to treat acidic soils. Gypsum is used to make plaster and plasterboard.

Gemstones such as tiger's eye, found in the Northern Cape, are cut and polished for jewellery and ornaments.

Gold is unusual. It is so unreactive that it is found as the pure element, called native gold, rather than combined with other elements. However, in Witwatersrand ore the particles are microscopic, so the ore must still be crushed and the gold extracted by chemical processes. Nuggets of native gold large enough to pick up are rare.

The difference between natural-form minerals and minerals that need refining is mainly chemical. In natural-form minerals, the useful substance is already the substance we want: carbon in a diamond, sodium chloride in salt. In most metal ores, the metal is chemically combined with other elements, such as oxygen or sulfur, so a chemical reaction is needed to separate the metal. Iron ore, for example, contains iron oxides, not iron metal.

## Subtopic: From Ore to Product and the Error Museum

Once ore has been mined, it goes through a series of steps.

Crushing and grinding: the ore is crushed in large crushers and then ground in mills into small pieces or a fine powder, to free the mineral grains from the waste rock.

Concentrating: the valuable mineral is separated from the waste, called gangue, using differences in properties such as density, magnetism or how the mineral sticks to air bubbles in a frothy liquid, called flotation. Diamonds are separated in dense liquids and then by X-ray machines, because diamonds glow under X-rays.

Refining: the concentrated mineral is processed further, physically or chemically, to obtain the pure metal or product.

The waste rock and the finely ground leftovers, called tailings, are stored in waste dumps and in tailings dams. The flat-topped yellow and white mine dumps around Johannesburg are tailings from gold mining.

A simple calculation shows how much waste mining produces. If gold ore contains 5 grams per tonne, then 1 tonne of ore gives 5 grams of gold and 999 995 grams of waste. To produce 1 kilogram, which is 1 000 grams, of gold, 1 000 ÷ 5 = 200 tonnes of ore must be mined.

The error museum for this lesson has five exhibits. One: any rock containing a metal is an ore; it is an ore only if the metal can be extracted at a profit. Two: underground mining is always better because it hides the mine; it is more expensive and dangerous and is used only when the ore is deep. Three: gold ore is mostly gold; South African gold ore contains only a few grams of gold per tonne of rock. Four: coal and diamonds must be chemically refined before use; they can be used in their natural form after simple physical processing. Five: once ore is dug up, mining is finished; the ore still has to be crushed, concentrated and refined, and the waste must be managed.

## Subtopic: Digging for Treasure

Useful minerals are hidden in rock. A rock with enough of a useful mineral to make money from mining it is called an ore.

Some ores are rich, like iron ore, which can be more than half iron. Others are very poor. Gold ore has only about 5 grams of gold in a whole tonne of rock. That means digging up about 200 tonnes of rock to get just 1 kilogram of gold.

Before digging, geologists hunt for ore. They take samples and drill holes to see how big, deep and rich it is.

If the ore is near the surface, miners dig it from above: surface mining. They scrape off the soil and rock on top and dig out the ore. The Sishen iron ore mine is a giant pit about 14 kilometres long.

If the ore is deep, miners go underground through shafts and tunnels.

It is a bit like searching for a few grains of sugar spread through a whole truckload of sand. Only special machines and chemicals make it worth doing.

## Subtopic: Deep Down Under

South Africa has the deepest mine in the world, almost 4 kilometres down.

Miners ride down a shaft in a lift called a cage. Tunnels lead sideways to the ore.

Down there the rock is about 60 °C, as hot as a very hot bath, so the mine pumps in cold air and ice water to cool it.

Fresh air must be blown in all the time to clear dust and gases.

The rock above presses down very hard, so tunnels are held up with steel, concrete and bolts, and in coal mines, pillars of coal are left standing to hold up the roof.

Diamonds are also found in river gravels and under the sea off the west coast, where ships suck them up from the sea floor.

Underground mining is more dangerous and more expensive, which is why it is only used when the ore is deep.

## Subtopic: Ready to Use

Some minerals can be used almost straight from the ground, after just washing, crushing, sorting or polishing.

Diamonds: cut and polished for jewellery, or used on drill bits because they are the hardest natural material.

Coal: crushed and burned to make electricity.

Salt: dried from sea water in shallow pans.

Clay: made into bricks and pots. Sand and gravel: made into concrete and roads. Granite and marble: cut for floors and buildings.

Other ores need a lot more work. They are crushed, the useful mineral is separated from the waste, and then it is refined.

And all that waste rock ends up in dumps, like the mine dumps around Johannesburg.

Pocket summary of the lesson. An ore is rock with enough valuable mineral to mine at a profit. Surface mining is used for ore near the surface; underground mining for deep ore. Ore is crushed, concentrated and refined, leaving waste rock and tailings. Some minerals, such as diamonds, coal, salt, clay, sand and stone, can be used in their natural form. In the next lesson you will see how minerals are refined, including how iron is smelted and steel is made.
