More than a thousand years ago, farming communities in southern Africa were already making their own iron. On hillsides from Limpopo to KwaZulu-Natal, smiths built furnaces of clay, filled them with charcoal and crushed iron ore, and pumped air in with goatskin bellows until the ore gave up its metal. They forged that iron into hoes, axes and spears. Today the same basic chemistry happens on a giant scale in the blast furnaces of Vanderbijlpark and Newcastle. Today we learn how minerals are refined, using physical processes that separate substances and chemical processes that break compounds apart. We follow iron from ore to metal, first in an ancient South African furnace and then in a modern blast furnace, write the chemical equations for what happens, and find out how brittle cast iron is turned into strong, useful steel.
