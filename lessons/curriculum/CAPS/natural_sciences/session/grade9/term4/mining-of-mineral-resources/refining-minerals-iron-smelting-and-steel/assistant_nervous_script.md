Hey, refining sounds technical, but it is a few clear steps. Here is the calm version.

Refining = getting the useful substance out pure.
Physical: nothing new is made (magnets, flotation, panning, dissolve and evaporate).
Chemical: new substances made (smelting with carbon, electrolysis).

Most metals are combined with oxygen. Removing the oxygen = reduction.

Ancient iron smelting in southern Africa (over 1 000 years ago):
Charcoal + ore in a clay furnace, goatskin bellows pump air.
About 1 200 °C: not hot enough to melt iron, so a spongy bloom forms.
Hammer the bloom to remove slag and shape tools.

Blast furnace:
In: iron ore (Fe2O3), coke (carbon), limestone. Hot air blasted in.
Fe2O3 + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO2
Limestone removes impurities as slag (floats on the iron).
Pig iron: about 4% carbon, brittle.

Steel: blow oxygen through to remove carbon, under about 1%. Strong and tough.
Stainless steel: add chromium and nickel.
