Hey, mining is mostly about where the ore is. Here is the calm version.

Ore: rock with enough valuable mineral to mine at a profit.
Grade: how much valuable material is in it.
Gold ore: about 5 g per tonne, so 200 tonnes of rock for 1 kg of gold.

Surface mining (ore near the surface):
Open pit: big stepped hole (Sishen iron ore).
Opencast strip: flat coal seams in Mpumalanga.
Cheaper and safer, but disturbs lots of land.

Underground mining (ore deep down):
Shafts and tunnels (Mponeng gold mine, almost 4 km deep).
Problems: heat, dust, gases, rockbursts. More expensive and dangerous.

Alluvial and marine: diamonds from river gravels and the sea floor.

After mining: crush, concentrate, refine. Waste = gangue, tailings, mine dumps.

Natural form (only washing, crushing, cutting): diamonds, coal, salt, clay, sand, gravel, granite, marble.

Remember: the depth of the ore decides the method. Shallow means surface mining; deep means underground.
