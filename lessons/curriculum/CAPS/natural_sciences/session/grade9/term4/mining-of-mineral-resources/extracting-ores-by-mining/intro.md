In 1871, thousands of diggers rushed to a small hill in the Northern Cape after diamonds were found there. Working with picks and shovels, they dug it away completely and kept going down until they had made one of the largest hand-dug holes on Earth: the Big Hole at Kimberley. Today, mining in South Africa uses enormous machines, open pits several kilometres long and gold mines that reach almost four kilometres underground. Today we find out how minerals are taken out of the ground. We learn what an ore is, compare surface mining with underground mining, and see why the method chosen depends on how deep the ore lies and how it is spread through the rock. We also meet minerals that can be used almost exactly as they come out of the ground, such as diamonds, coal, salt, sand and clay, and follow the first steps that turn rock into useful products.
