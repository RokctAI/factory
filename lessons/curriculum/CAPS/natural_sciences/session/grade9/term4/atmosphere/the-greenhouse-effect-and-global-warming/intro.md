Leave a car parked in the sun in Upington in December, with its windows closed, and within an hour the air inside can be over 60 °C, far hotter than the air outside. Sunlight passes easily through the glass, warms the seats and dashboard, and the heat cannot escape as easily as the light got in. The Earth's atmosphere does something similar for the whole planet, and without it the oceans would freeze. But in the last 150 years, people have been thickening the blanket of heat-trapping gases, and the planet is warming. Today we find out how the greenhouse effect works, which gases cause it, and why it is essential for life. We then look at how burning fossil fuels and other human activities are strengthening it, what global warming means for South Africa, and what can be done to slow it down.
