Hey, air is just a mixture with weight. Here is the calm version.

Dry air:
Nitrogen about 78%.
Oxygen about 21%.
Argon about 0,9%.
Carbon dioxide about 0,04%.
Plus water vapour (changes a lot) and dust.

Air is a MIXTURE, not a compound. The gases can be separated.

Jobs:
Nitrogen: unreactive, plants get it through soil bacteria.
Oxygen: respiration and burning.
Carbon dioxide: photosynthesis, traps heat.
Ozone: blocks ultraviolet light, high up.

Air has mass. Gravity pulls it down. That weight gives air pressure (about 101 kPa at sea level).

Going up:
Less air above, so less squashing.
Pressure and density decrease.
Roughly halves every 5,5 km.
Everest: about a third.

Trap to avoid: the PERCENTAGE of oxygen does not change with height. The air is just thinner, so each breath has fewer molecules.

The atmosphere has no sharp edge. It fades into space.

Easy check: if a question asks why, the answer is usually gravity squashing the air near the ground.
