Every day, the South African Weather Service sends up balloons carrying small instrument packs that radio back the temperature, pressure and humidity of the air as they rise. For the first ten kilometres or so, the readings tell a simple story: the higher the balloon goes, the colder it gets, until the air is colder than any winter night in Sutherland. Then something surprising happens. As the balloon keeps rising, the air starts to get warmer again. Higher still, beyond where any balloon can reach, it turns cold once more, and then, at the edge of space, it becomes hotter than a pizza oven. Today we find out why. We learn how scientists divide the atmosphere into four layers, the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere and thermosphere, by the way the temperature changes with height, and what happens in each layer, from weather and aeroplanes to the ozone layer, shooting stars and the International Space Station.
