Hey, three rock families and one cycle. Here is the calm version.

Igneous (fire): molten rock cools.
Slow cooling underground = big crystals (granite).
Fast cooling at the surface = small crystals (basalt).
No fossils.

Sedimentary (layers): weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, cementation.
Examples: sandstone, shale, limestone, conglomerate, coal.
Often has layers and fossils.

Metamorphic (changed): heat and pressure, without melting.
Shale to slate. Limestone to marble. Sandstone to quartzite. Granite to gneiss.

Rock cycle: any rock can become any other type, by many paths.
Sun: drives weathering and erosion.
Earth's internal heat: drives melting, metamorphism and uplift.
Very slow: millions of years.

Easy way to remember: igneous = fire, sedimentary = settled in layers, metamorphic = morphed, changed.

To tell them apart: crystals usually means igneous; layers and fossils usually means sedimentary; bands or sheets usually means metamorphic.

Trap to avoid: metamorphic rocks do NOT melt. Melting makes magma.
