The deepest mine in the world is in South Africa, west of Johannesburg, where miners dig for gold almost four kilometres below the surface. That sounds incredibly deep, yet it is less than one thousandth of the way to the centre of the Earth, which lies about 6 371 kilometres beneath our feet. Nobody has ever travelled even through the thin outer skin of our planet, so how do we know what lies inside? Today we explore the layers of the Earth, the crust, the mantle and the core, and find out how scientists learned about them from earthquake waves. We will look closely at the lithosphere, the rigid outer shell made of continental crust, oceanic crust and the top of the mantle, and at the minerals that make up the crust, from the common quartz and feldspar in granite to the gold, platinum, diamonds and coal that have shaped South Africa's history.
