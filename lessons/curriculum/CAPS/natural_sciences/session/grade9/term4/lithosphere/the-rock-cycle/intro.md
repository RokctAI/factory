Look up at Table Mountain and you are looking at the rock cycle in action. Its flat top is made of sandstone, formed from sand that was laid down in layers over 400 million years ago, pressed and cemented into rock. Underneath lie even older rocks: granite that cooled from molten magma deep underground, and a dark rock that was once mud, changed by heat and pressure. Rocks may seem permanent, but over millions of years every rock is slowly broken down, carried away, buried, heated, squeezed, melted and formed again. Today we meet the three families of rocks, igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic, and find out how each one forms. Then we connect them in the rock cycle, a never-ending loop driven by energy from the Sun above and heat from the Earth below, and learn how to read the story of South Africa's landscapes from the rocks around us.
