On a clear, dark winter night in the Karoo, look south and you may see a faint, misty patch of light among the stars of the Milky Way, near the constellation of the Southern Cross. It looks like a smudge, but through a telescope it is an enormous glowing cloud of gas and dust called the Carina Nebula, so big that light takes hundreds of years to cross it. Inside it, new stars are being born right now. Every star you can see, including our own Sun, began in a cloud like this. Today we find out how. We learn what a star is, what nebulae are made of, how gravity pulls a cloud together until it becomes hot enough to shine, and how nuclear fusion switches on in a newborn star's core. We also meet the star nurseries that can be seen from South Africa, and the telescopes in the Karoo that study them.
