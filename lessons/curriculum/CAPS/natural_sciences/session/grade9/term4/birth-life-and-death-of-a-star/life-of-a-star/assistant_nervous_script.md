Hey, a star's life is mostly a long, calm balancing act. Here is the calm version.

Main sequence (about 90% of a star's life):
Hydrogen fuses into helium in the core.
Gravity pulls in. Fusion pressure pushes out.
They balance, so the star stays steady.

Colour = surface temperature:
Red: coolest, about 3 000 °C.
Orange, then yellow (Sun, about 5 500 °C).
White: about 10 000 °C.
Blue: hottest, over 20 000 °C.

Mass decides everything:
More mass = hotter, bluer, brighter.
More mass = SHORTER life (burns fuel much faster).
Massive stars: millions of years.
Sun: about 10 billion years.
Red dwarfs: hundreds of billions of years.

The Sun:
Medium-sized yellow star.
About 109 times wider than Earth.
About 4,6 billion years old.
10 − 4,6 = 5,4 billion years left. Halfway.

Trap to avoid: for stars, BLUE is hotter than red. Opposite of the taps!

Easy check: bigger star, brighter star, shorter life.
