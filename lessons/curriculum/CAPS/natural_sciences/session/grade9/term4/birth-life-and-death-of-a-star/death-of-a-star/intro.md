In 2022, the James Webb Space Telescope sent back one of its first pictures: a glowing shell of gas, layered like the rings of an onion, with a tiny, fierce point of light at its centre. It is called the Southern Ring Nebula, and it can be found in the southern sky about 2 500 light-years away. It is what is left of a star a little like our Sun, which has run out of fuel and gently blown its outer layers out into space. In about 5 billion years, the Sun will do the same. Today we find out how stars die. We learn what happens when a star runs out of hydrogen in its core, how a star like the Sun swells into a red giant, sheds its outer layers as a planetary nebula and leaves behind a white dwarf. We also see how the most massive stars die in huge explosions, and why the atoms in your body were once made inside stars.
