Look carefully at the stars on a clear night and you will notice that they are not all the same colour. In the constellation of Orion, the bright star Betelgeuse glows orange-red, while Rigel, on the opposite corner, shines a brilliant blue-white. In Scorpius, which stands overhead in South African winter evenings, the red star Antares marks the scorpion's heart. These colours are not random. They are a thermometer, telling us how hot each star's surface is, and they help us work out how massive a star is and how long it will live. Today we find out what happens during the long, steady life of a star. We learn how a star balances gravity against the energy of fusion for billions of years, how its colour reveals its temperature, why the biggest stars live the shortest lives, and why our Sun is described as a medium-sized yellow star about halfway through its life.
