Hey, a star's death is just what happens when the fuel runs out. Here is the calm version.

Sun-like star:

1. Hydrogen in the core runs out.
2. Core shrinks and heats up.
3. Outer layers swell and cool: RED GIANT.
(Red = cool surface. Giant = huge.)
4. Core reaches about 100 million °C: helium fuses into carbon and oxygen.
5. Outer layers drift off: PLANETARY NEBULA (glowing gas, NOT planets).
6. Core left behind: WHITE DWARF.
Earth-sized, very dense, very hot, no fusion, slowly cools.

The Sun:
Red giant in about 5 billion years.
May grow about 200 times wider.
Swallows Mercury and Venus.

Massive star (more than about 8 Suns):
Red supergiant, then SUPERNOVA explosion.
Leaves a neutron star or black hole.

Recycling: dying stars spread carbon, oxygen, iron and more.
We are made of star stuff.

Trap to avoid: the Sun will NOT explode as a supernova. It is too small.
