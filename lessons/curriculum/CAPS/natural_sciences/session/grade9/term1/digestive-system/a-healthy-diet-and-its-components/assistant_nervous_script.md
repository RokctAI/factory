Hey — lots of nutrients and diseases in one lesson, but it all hangs on one idea: fuel, bricks and spare parts. Here is the calm version.

Fuel: carbohydrates, like pap and bread, and fats, like oil and nuts. Bricks: proteins, like beans, eggs and fish, for growth and repair. Spare parts: vitamins and minerals in tiny amounts. Plus water and fibre.

Food tests: iodine goes blue-black with starch; Benedict's, heated, goes orange or brick-red with glucose; Biuret goes purple with protein; fat leaves a see-through spot on paper.

Energy: 17 kJ per gram of carbohydrate, 17 per gram of protein, 37 per gram of fat. Multiply each mass by its number and add. More energy in than used means fat is stored.

Balanced plate: half vegetables and fruit, a quarter starch, a quarter protein, water to drink.

Deficiencies: no protein, kwashiorkor with a swollen belly; no energy and protein, marasmus; no vitamin C, scurvy; no vitamin D or calcium, rickets; no iron, anaemia; no iodine, goitre. Too much energy: obesity and diabetes.
