Today we follow food through the digestive system as a whole. A meal of pap and beans cannot feed a single cell as it is; it must be taken in, broken down into molecules small enough to pass through a cell membrane, absorbed into the blood, used by the cells, and what cannot be digested must be removed. Those are the five processes: ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion. They happen along a muscular tube about eight to nine metres long, the alimentary canal, helped by organs the food never passes through: the salivary glands, the liver, the gall bladder and the pancreas. We will name every part and its job, see where absorption happens and why, and look at the health issues of the system, from tooth decay and ulcers to diarrhoea, which still kills young children in South Africa. Grade 10 and 11 Life Sciences build on all of it.
