Hey — enzymes and digestion can feel like a lot of names, but there are really just two kinds of digestion and three kinds of food. Here is the calm version.

Mechanical digestion makes pieces smaller without changing the molecules: teeth, tongue, stomach churning, and bile breaking fat into droplets. Incisors cut, canines tear, premolars and molars grind; adults have 32 teeth.

Chemical digestion uses enzymes. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction and is not used up. Each one fits only one substrate, like a key in a lock. It works best at about 37 degrees; cold slows it, high heat denatures it for good.

Mouth: amylase starts on starch. Stomach: acid, about pH 2, kills germs and pepsin starts on protein; amylase stops. Duodenum: bile neutralises acid and emulsifies fat; pancreatic juice brings amylase, trypsin and lipase. Gut wall finishes off.

End products: starch to glucose, protein to amino acids, fat to fatty acids and glycerol.

Investigation: temperature is the independent variable, time for starch to disappear is the dependent variable, and a tube with no amylase is the control.
