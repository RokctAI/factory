Today we go inside the alimentary canal to see how food is actually broken down. Digestion happens in two ways at once. Mechanical digestion breaks food into smaller pieces without changing the molecules: 32 adult teeth cutting, tearing and grinding, the tongue rolling, the stomach churning, and bile breaking fat into droplets. Chemical digestion breaks the molecules themselves, using enzymes, proteins that speed up particular reactions and work best at body temperature: amylase turns starch into sugar, proteases turn proteins into amino acids, and lipases turn fats into fatty acids and glycerol. We will follow each kind of food from the mouth through the acid of the stomach to the small intestine, see why each enzyme works only in its own place, and design the classic experiment with saliva, starch and iodine. Grade 10 and 11 Life Sciences build on enzymes and digestion.
