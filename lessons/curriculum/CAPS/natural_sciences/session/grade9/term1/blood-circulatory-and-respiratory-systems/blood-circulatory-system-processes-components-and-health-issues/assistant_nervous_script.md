Hey — if atria, ventricles, haemoglobin and the pulmonary artery felt like a traffic jam of words, here is the calm version: a fleet, two pumps and three roads.

The fleet is your blood, about five litres. Plasma is the liquid that carries everything dissolved: glucose, salts, hormones, urea, carbon dioxide. Red cells carry oxygen with haemoglobin. White cells fight infection. Platelets clot. Transport, defence, clotting, and carrying heat.

The engine is two pumps side by side, your fist, about 70 beats a minute. Top rooms, the atria, fill; bottom rooms, the ventricles, squeeze; valves stop backflow. Right side sends used blood to the lungs, short trip. Left side sends fresh blood to the whole body, long trip, so its wall is thicker. The route: vena cava, right atrium, right ventricle, pulmonary artery, lungs, pulmonary vein, left atrium, left ventricle, aorta, body.

Three roads. Arteries: thick walls, high pressure, away from the heart. Capillaries: one cell thick, where exchange happens. Veins: thin walls, valves, back to the heart. Define arteries by direction, because the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.

Health: 120 over 80 is normal pressure; exercise, less salt and fat, no smoking.
