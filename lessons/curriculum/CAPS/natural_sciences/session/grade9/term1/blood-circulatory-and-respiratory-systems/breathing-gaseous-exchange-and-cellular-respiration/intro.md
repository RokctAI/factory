Today we join three processes that learners constantly confuse into one chain. Breathing moves air into and out of the lungs. Gaseous exchange is the diffusion of oxygen from the alveoli into the blood and of carbon dioxide the other way, across a surface the size of half a tennis court that is one cell thick. Cellular respiration is the release of energy from glucose inside the mitochondria of every cell, using the oxygen the blood delivered and producing the carbon dioxide the blood carries away. Between the lungs and the cells runs the blood, so the chain is lungs, blood, cell and back. We will see why the alveoli are built as they are, write the respiration equation in words and symbols, and follow a sprint from the first deep breath to the burn of lactic acid. Grade 10 and 11 Life Sciences build gas exchange and respiration into full topics on this foundation.
