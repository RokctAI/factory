Today we follow a breath. Air enters through the nose, where it is filtered, warmed and moistened, passes down the trachea into the two bronchi, branches through ever smaller bronchioles and ends in about 300 million tiny air sacs, the alveoli, whose combined surface would cover half a tennis court. The diaphragm and the muscles between the ribs enlarge the chest to draw air in and relax to push it out, about 15 times a minute at rest. We will name the parts of the respiratory system and their jobs, explain the mechanism of breathing in terms of volume and pressure, compare inhaled and exhaled air, and look at the diseases of the system: asthma, tuberculosis, pneumonia and the damage done by smoking. The next lesson joins this system to the blood and the mitochondria; Grade 10 Life Sciences builds on both.
