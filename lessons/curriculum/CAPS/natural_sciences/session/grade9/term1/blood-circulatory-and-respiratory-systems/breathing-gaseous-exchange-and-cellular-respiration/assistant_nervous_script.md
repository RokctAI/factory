Hey — if breathing, gas exchange and respiration all sounded like the same thing, here is the calm version: three verbs and one taxi.

Fetching is breathing: the diaphragm and ribs move air in and out of the chest. Mechanical. Swapping is gas exchange: oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuses out, and the same swap runs in reverse between blood and cells. Physical, no pushing. Burning is cellular respiration: in the mitochondria of every cell, glucose plus oxygen gives carbon dioxide plus water plus energy. Chemical. Never in the lungs.

The taxi is the blood: oxygen rides on haemoglobin in red cells, carbon dioxide mostly dissolved in plasma. Lungs, blood, cell, blood, lungs.

The alveoli are built for swapping, feature then reason: huge surface area so more gas crosses at once; walls one cell thick so the distance is short; moist so gases dissolve; rich moving blood supply so the difference stays big.

In a sprint, breathing and heart rate climb to fetch more oxygen. When it is not enough, muscles respire without oxygen and make lactic acid, the burn; panting afterwards pays it back.

Open the practice set, fetch, swap, carry, swap, burn. You've got this!
