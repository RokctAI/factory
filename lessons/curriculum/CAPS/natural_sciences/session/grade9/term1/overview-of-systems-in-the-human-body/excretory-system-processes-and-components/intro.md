Today we follow the body's waste. Every cell produces wastes as it works: carbon dioxide from respiration and, in the liver, urea from the breakdown of excess protein. Left in the blood these would poison the cells, so the excretory system removes them. The two kidneys filter about 180 litres of fluid out of the blood every day, take back almost all of the water and every bit of the glucose, and release about one and a half litres of urine, which collects in the bladder. The lungs excrete carbon dioxide and the skin excretes water and salt in sweat. We will name the parts of the urinary system, follow filtration and reabsorption, see how the kidneys balance water on a hot day, and look at kidney stones, infections and kidney failure with dialysis. Grade 10 Life Sciences does not revisit excretion until Grade 11, so this is the foundation.
