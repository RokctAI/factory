Hey — if bones, joints, tendons and ligaments blurred into one list, here is the calm version: scaffolding and ropes.

The scaffolding is the skeleton, 206 bones. Five jobs: support, movement, protection, making blood cells in the marrow, storing calcium. Main bones: skull, spine of 33 vertebrae, 12 pairs of ribs, humerus, radius and ulna in the arm, femur, tibia and fibula in the leg, the pelvis.

Where poles meet is a joint. Skull joints are fixed. Spine joints move a little. Limb joints move freely: hinge at the elbow and knee, one way and back; ball-and-socket at the shoulder and hip, every direction; pivot in the neck, rotation. Cartilage caps the bone ends, synovial fluid oils the joint, ligaments strap bone to bone.

The ropes are muscles, tied on by tendons, muscle to bone. A rope can only pull, so muscles come in antagonistic pairs: biceps contracts and triceps relaxes to bend the elbow; triceps contracts and biceps relaxes to straighten it. Write the contracting muscle, the relaxing muscle, the bone and the joint.

Health: good posture, both bag straps, calcium, sunlight for vitamin D, exercise. Fracture is bone, sprain is ligament, strain is muscle.
