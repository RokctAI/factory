Hey — if filtration, reabsorption, ureters and urea sounded like a chemistry set, here is the calm version: it is a rubbish service with a sieve.

Cells make rubbish by living: carbon dioxide from burning glucose, and urea, which the liver makes from extra protein. Removing rubbish the cells made is excretion. Faeces are different; that is egestion, leftovers that never got into your body.

Three exits: lungs for carbon dioxide, skin for water and salt in sweat, kidneys for urea, extra water and extra salt as urine.

The sieve: the kidney squeezes blood through tiny vessels and everything small falls through, water, glucose, salts, urea. Blood cells and proteins stay. That is filtration, about 180 litres a day. Then it takes back the treasure: all the glucose, 99 percent of the water, the salt you need. That is reabsorption. What is left, about 1,5 litres, is urine.

Plumbing in order: kidney, ureter, bladder, urethra. The bladder stores; it never filters.

Hot day, more sweat, the kidney keeps more water and urine goes dark. Drink water to prevent stones and infections. Failed kidneys need dialysis, about four hours three times a week.

Open the practice set, sieve then reclaim. You've got this!
