Today we look at the human body as one machine made of cooperating systems. The digestive system turns food into glucose, the respiratory system brings in oxygen, the circulatory system delivers both to every cell and carries the wastes away, the excretory system removes those wastes, the nervous system coordinates everything, the musculoskeletal system moves the body and the reproductive system produces the next generation. No system works alone: a single leg muscle cell running for a taxi depends on five of them at once. We will name the seven systems and their jobs, trace a mouthful of food and a breath of air to a working cell and back out, see how heart rate and breathing rise together in exercise, and learn to answer the linking questions examiners love. Every system lesson this term, and Grade 10 Life Sciences, builds on this map.
