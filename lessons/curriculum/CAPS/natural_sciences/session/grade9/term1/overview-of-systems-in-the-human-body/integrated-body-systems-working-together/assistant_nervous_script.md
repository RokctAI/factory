Hey — if seven systems and all their connections felt like too much to hold at once, here is the calm version: it is a taxi rank, and every stall has one job.

Food stall, digestive: turns food into glucose and puts it in the blood. Petrol pump, respiratory: oxygen in, carbon dioxide out. Taxis, circulatory: heart and blood deliver everything to every cell and bring the waste back. Cleaners, excretory: kidneys filter urea out as urine. Marshal, nervous: brain and nerves in charge of all of it. Frames and drivers, musculoskeletal: bones and muscles. And reproductive: the next generation.

The one rule: nothing reaches a cell except through the blood. Lungs to blood to muscle. Gut to blood to muscle. Cell to blood to lungs or kidneys. Never skip the taxi.

For a linking question, say the chain: asthma means less oxygen into the blood, so less oxygen carried to the muscles, so less energy released, so the muscles tire. Name the system, say what it supplies, follow it to the cell.

Sprinting: heart from 70 to 150, breathing from 15 to 40, sweat to stay at 37 degrees. Every change is one system helping another.

Open the practice set, find the stall, follow the blood. You've got this!
