Today we study the frame that holds you up and the engines that move it: the musculoskeletal system. The adult skeleton has 206 bones that support the body, protect the brain, heart and lungs, store calcium and make blood cells in the marrow. Bones meet at joints, and the type of joint decides the movement: a hinge at the elbow and knee, a ball-and-socket at the hip and shoulder, a pivot in the neck. Muscles attached to bones by tendons pull, never push, so they work in opposing pairs like the biceps and triceps. We will name the main bones, classify the joints, explain antagonistic muscle pairs, and look at the health issues the syllabus names: posture, fractures, sprains, osteoporosis and arthritis. Grade 10 Life Sciences returns to support systems in animals with this lesson as its base.
