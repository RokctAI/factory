Today we follow the stages of reproduction in the order they happen. Each month the menstrual cycle prepares the body for a possible pregnancy: in a typical 28-day cycle the lining of the uterus is shed in the first few days, rebuilt over the next week, an egg is released at ovulation around day 14, and the lining is held ready until about day 28. If a sperm meets the egg in the oviduct, fertilisation makes a zygote, which divides into a ball of cells and implants in the uterine lining about a week later. Pregnancy then lasts about 40 weeks, counted from the last period, with the placenta and umbilical cord supplying the baby and carrying its wastes away without the two bloodstreams ever mixing. We finish with birth and with the substances that cross the placenta and harm a baby. Grade 12 Life Sciences develops every stage.
