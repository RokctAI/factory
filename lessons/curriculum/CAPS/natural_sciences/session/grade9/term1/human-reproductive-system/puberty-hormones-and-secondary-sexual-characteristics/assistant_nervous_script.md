Hey — puberty can feel personal and a bit embarrassing to study, and that is fine. Everyone in the room is going through it or has gone through it. Here is the calm version.

Puberty is when the body becomes able to reproduce. It starts at different ages for different people, roughly 8 to 13 in girls and 9 to 14 in boys, and all of that is normal.

The chain: the brain signals the pituitary gland, the pituitary signals the testes or ovaries, and they release sex hormones into the blood. A hormone is a chemical messenger carried in the blood.

Testosterone from the testes: facial hair, deeper voice, broader shoulders, muscle, sperm production. Oestrogen from the ovaries: breasts, wider hips, the start of periods, a thicker uterine lining. Progesterone keeps that lining in place.

Both sexes: growth spurt, pubic and underarm hair, more sweat and body odour, oily skin and acne, bigger feelings.

Primary characteristics are the reproductive organs you are born with; secondary ones appear at puberty.

Habits: wash daily, clean clothes, change pads every four to six hours, do not squeeze pimples.
