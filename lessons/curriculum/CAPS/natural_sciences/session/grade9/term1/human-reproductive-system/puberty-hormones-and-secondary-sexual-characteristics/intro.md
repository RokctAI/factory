Today we look at puberty, the years in which a child's body becomes an adult body able to reproduce. It does not start on a fixed birthday. In girls it usually begins somewhere between about 8 and 13, in boys between about 9 and 14, and either end of that range is normal. Puberty is switched on by the brain and run by hormones, chemical messengers carried in the blood: testosterone from the testes, oestrogen and progesterone from the ovaries. We will see how a hormone works, separate the primary sexual characteristics you are born with from the secondary ones that appear at puberty, list the changes in boys and in girls, explain acne, body odour and a breaking voice, and set out the hygiene habits that go with the new body. Grade 12 Life Sciences returns to these hormones in the endocrine system.
