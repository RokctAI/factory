Hey — if plant cells versus animal cells felt like a list of random parts, here is the calm version: both cells share four things, and the plant adds exactly three.

Shared by both: cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria. The same four as last lesson.

Plant extras, think brick house. One: brick walls, the cell wall, made of cellulose, for support and shape. It is fully permeable, so it does not control what enters; the membrane inside it still does that. Two: a big water balloon, the large permanent vacuole, filled with cell sap. Full balloon means a turgid, firm cell; empty balloon means flaccid, and the lettuce wilts. Three: green solar panels, chloroplasts with chlorophyll, where carbon dioxide and water become glucose and oxygen in sunlight.

Two traps. Onion cells are plant cells but have no chloroplasts, because bulbs grow in the dark. And plant cells still have mitochondria, because every living cell respires.

Shape: plant cells are boxy with the nucleus at the edge; animal cells are round with the nucleus in the middle. Put it in a three-column table, fill every box, and write none where there is nothing.
