Today we open the smallest living unit there is: the cell. Every living thing, from a single bacterium in a pond at Rietvlei to the 30 trillion or so cells of your own body, is built from cells, and every cell shares the same basic plan. We will meet the four structures that Grade 9 must know and describe: the cell membrane that controls what enters and leaves, the cytoplasm where the chemical reactions of life happen, the nucleus that holds the DNA and controls the cell, and the mitochondria that release energy from glucose. We will see how a school microscope magnifies a cheek cell, how to draw and label a cell for full marks, and why this lesson is the foundation of Grade 10 Life Sciences, where the same cell returns in far more detail.
