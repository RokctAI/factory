Hey — if specialised cells, tissues, organs and stem cells felt like four lessons in one, here is the calm version: it is a company, a toolbox and a house.

The company: a one-person spaza shop is a unicellular organism, one cell doing every job, like an Amoeba or a bacterium. A big company is a multicellular organism, trillions of cells with one job each; that is division of labour.

The toolbox: every specialised cell is shaped for its job, and you write the feature then the job. Red blood cell, no nucleus, more room for haemoglobin. Muscle cell, many mitochondria, energy for contraction. Nerve cell, long axon, carries messages far and fast. Root hair cell, long extension, more surface area for absorbing water.

The house: a brick is a cell, a wall of the same bricks is a tissue, a room of different parts is an organ, a house is a system, the street is the organism. Cell, tissue, organ, system, organism.

Stem cells are the blank bricks: unspecialised, able to become other cells, found in embryos and bone marrow. And every cell, blank or not, carries the same 46 chromosomes.

Open the practice set, feature then job, brick to street. You've got this!
