Hey — if cells, organelles and micrometres sounded like a lot of new words, here is the calm version: it is one small shop with four parts, and a ruler.

Think of a spaza shop. The security gate is the cell membrane: it decides what comes in and what goes out, which the test calls selectively permeable. The shop floor is the cytoplasm: mostly water, where the chemical reactions happen. The back office with the recipe book is the nucleus holding the DNA, 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, so it controls the cell. The generator is the mitochondrion, releasing energy from glucose and oxygen; busy muscle cells have thousands.

The ruler: a cheek cell is about 60 micrometres, and 1 000 micrometres make a millimetre, so you need a microscope. Multiply eyepiece by objective: 10 times 40 is 400. Add methylene blue so the nucleus shows.

For a drawing: sharp pencil, no shading, ruled label lines with no arrows, a heading with the magnification, and never a cell wall on an animal cell.

Open the practice set, name the part, say its job. You've got this!
