Today we move from one cell to a whole organism. Some living things, such as Amoeba and bacteria, are a single cell that does everything: feeding, moving, excreting and reproducing. You are made of about 30 trillion cells, and almost every one of them is specialised for a single job: a red blood cell carries oxygen, a muscle cell contracts, a nerve cell carries messages, a root hair cell absorbs water. We will see how each cell's shape fits its function, how specialised cells group into tissues, tissues build organs, organs work together in systems, and systems make up the organism, and we will meet stem cells, the unspecialised cells that can become any of the others. This ladder of organisation is the backbone of every body-system lesson this term and of Grade 10 Life Sciences.
