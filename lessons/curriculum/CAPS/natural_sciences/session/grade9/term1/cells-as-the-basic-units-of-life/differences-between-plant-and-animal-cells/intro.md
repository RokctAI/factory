Today we put a plant cell and an animal cell side by side and find exactly three differences. Both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus and mitochondria, which is why last lesson's four structures apply to a spinach leaf as much as to your cheek. But a plant cell also has a cell wall of cellulose outside its membrane, a single large permanent vacuole filled with cell sap, and, in its green parts, chloroplasts where photosynthesis happens. We will see what each extra structure does for the plant, why a lettuce goes limp in the sun, why onion skin has no chloroplasts, and how to set out the comparison table the examiner expects. The same comparison opens Grade 10 Life Sciences, where plant and animal tissues are studied in detail.
