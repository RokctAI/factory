## Question (5 marks)

In the diagram, O is the centre of the circle. K, T and P lie on the circle (K at the top, T lower left and P lower right; chords KT and KP and radii OT and OP are drawn). Use the diagram above to prove the theorem which states that the angle subtended by a chord (or arc) at the centre of the circle is equal to twice the angle subtended by the same chord (or arc) at the circumference, that is prove that TÔP = 2 TK̂P.

## Method

central angle theorem proof

## Memo working

Construction: draw KO and produce it beyond O; this splits both the centre angle and the circumference angle into two parts, labelled Ô1, Ô2 and K̂1, K̂2 on either side of the line. In triangle KOT: Ô1 = K̂1 + T̂, because the exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles. But OK and OT are radii, so triangle KOT is isosceles and K̂1 = T̂ (angles opposite equal sides). Therefore Ô1 = 2K̂1. By the same argument in triangle KOP: Ô2 = K̂2 + P̂ (exterior angle of triangle) and K̂2 = P̂ (angles opposite equal radii), so Ô2 = 2K̂2. Adding the two results: Ô1 + Ô2 = 2K̂1 + 2K̂2 = 2(K̂1 + K̂2); therefore TÔP = 2 TK̂P. (Alternative memo proof for the reflex case: draw KO; use the isosceles triangles and angle sums, then TÔP = 360° - (KÔT + KÔP) = 2K̂1 + 2K̂2, angles around a point.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

TÔP = 2 TK̂P
