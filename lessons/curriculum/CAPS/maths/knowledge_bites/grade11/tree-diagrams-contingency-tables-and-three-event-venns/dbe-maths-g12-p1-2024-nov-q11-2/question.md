## Question (2 marks)

A certain number of learners are sitting for examinations in Mathematics, Tourism and Geography. All these learners sit for at least one of these examinations. The total number of learners who sit for Mathematics (M), is 22. The total number of learners sitting for Tourism (T), is 16. The total number of learners sitting for Geography (G), is 18. 5 learners sit for Mathematics and Tourism, but not Geography. 4 learners sit for Mathematics and Geography, but not Tourism. 3 learners sit for Tourism and Geography, but not Mathematics. 6 learners sit for only Tourism. (From the completed Venn diagram: M only 11, M and T only 5, T only 6, all three 2, M and G only 4, T and G only 3, G only 9; 40 learners in total.) Calculate the probability that a learner, chosen at random, will sit for examinations in at least TWO of the subjects.

## Method

probability from venn

## Memo working

"At least two subjects" covers the three double-overlap regions and the centre: 5 (M and T only) + 4 (M and G only) + 3 (T and G only) + 2 (all three) = 14 learners. P = 14/40 = 7/20 = 0,35.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

P(at least two subjects) = 14/40 = 7/20 = 0,35
