## Question (5 marks)

Given: f(x) = -6x^2. Determine f'(x) from first principles.

## Method

first principles

## Memo working

Start from the definition f'(x) = lim_{h->0} [f(x + h) - f(x)]/h. f(x + h) = -6(x + h)^2 = -6x^2 - 12xh - 6h^2. So f(x + h) - f(x) = -6x^2 - 12xh - 6h^2 + 6x^2 = -12xh - 6h^2. Divide by h: (-12xh - 6h^2)/h = -12x - 6h. Let h tend to zero: f'(x) = lim_{h->0} (-12x - 6h) = -12x.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

f'(x) = -12x
