## Question (5 marks)

The graphs of f(x) = -x^2 + 4x + 5 and the straight line g(x) = 2x + 2 are drawn. C(3 ; 8) is a point of intersection of f and g. EH is drawn parallel to the y-axis, with E a point on f and H a point on g (concave-down parabola f with x-intercepts A on the negative x-axis and D on the positive x-axis, turning point B, y-intercept 5; increasing line g through A and C(3 ; 8); vertical segment EH between the two graphs, to the left of C). Given: k(x) = f(x + m) = -x^2 - 2mx - m^2 + 4x + 4m + 5. Determine the value of m such that g is a tangent to k.

## Method

tangency condition derivative

## Memo working

For g to be a tangent to k, the gradient of k at the contact point must equal the gradient of g, which is 2. Differentiate: k'(x) = -2x - 2m + 4. Set k'(x) = 2: -2x - 2m + 4 = 2 -> x = 1 - m. At the contact point the graphs also share the same y-value, so set k(1 - m) = g(1 - m): -(1 - m)^2 - 2m(1 - m) - m^2 + 4(1 - m) + 4m + 5 = 2(1 - m) + 2. Expanding and simplifying leaves -2m - 4 = 0, so m = -2. (Memo option 2: equate k and g and require a double root — the discriminant of x^2 + (2m - 2)x + m^2 - 4m - 3 = 0 must be zero, giving 8m + 16 = 0 -> m = -2. Memo option 3: f'(x) = 2 at x = 1, contact point (1 ; 8), so a horizontal translation of 2 units to the right is needed -> m = -2. Memo marking note: the answer alone earns 0/5.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

m = -2
