## Question (3 marks)

M(1 ; 3) is the centre of the circle. The circle cuts the x-axis at N. ST is a tangent to the circle at P(3 ; 9). R(d ; 1), with d > 0, and L lie on the circle. O and V are the x-intercepts of PL and RL respectively. (In the diagram, P(3 ; 9) is at the top right of the circle, R(d ; 1) on the right and L at the bottom left near the y-axis; the tangent ST touches the circle at P; chord PL passes through the centre M and cuts the x-axis at O, the origin; chord RL cuts the x-axis at V; N lies on the x-axis to the right of V.) With R(7 ; 1), TR is a tangent to the circle at R. Prove that PT ⊥ RT.

## Method

perpendicular gradients

## Memo working

Gradient of the radius MR: m_RM = (1 - 3)/(7 - 1) = -1/3. The tangent at R is perpendicular to this radius, so m_RT = 3. The tangent at P has gradient m_PT = -1/3 (from the equation of ST). Then m_RT × m_PT = 3 × (-1/3) = -1, therefore PT ⊥ RT. (Alternative memo methods: PT || MR with angle MRT = 90° and co-interior angles; or the tan-chord theorem with TP = TR, giving angle PTR = 90°.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

m_RT × m_PT = -1, therefore PT ⊥ RT
