## Question (5 marks)

In the diagram, O is the centre of the circle. K, T and P lie on the circle. [Figure: circle with centre O; K at the top, T lower left and P lower right on the circle; chords KT and KP and radii OT and OP drawn.] Use the diagram above to prove the theorem which states that the angle subtended by a chord (or arc) at the centre of the circle is equal to twice the angle subtended by the same chord (or arc) at the circumference, that is prove that TÔP = 2 TK̂P.

## Method

central angle theorem proof

## Memo working

Construction: draw KO produced. Ô1 = K̂1 + T̂ (ext angle of Δ); but K̂1 = T̂ (angles opp equal sides, radii); so Ô1 = 2K̂1. Similarly Ô2 = K̂2 + P̂ (ext angle of Δ) and K̂2 = P̂, so Ô2 = 2K̂2. Ô1 + Ô2 = 2K̂1 + 2K̂2 = 2(K̂1 + K̂2); therefore TÔP = 2 TK̂P

(Memo note: Alternative memo proof (for the reflex case): draw KO; use isosceles triangles and angle sums, then TÔP = 360° - (KÔT + KÔP) = 2K̂1 + 2K̂2 (angles around a point).)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

TÔP = 2 TK̂P
