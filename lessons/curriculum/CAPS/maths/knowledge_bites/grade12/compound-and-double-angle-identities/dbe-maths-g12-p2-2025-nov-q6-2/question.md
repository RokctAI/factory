## Question (7 marks)

It is given that sin^2 x ; cos^2 x and (1/2) sin 2x are the first three terms of an arithmetic sequence. The constant difference of the arithmetic sequence is NOT zero. Determine the general solution for x.

## Method

arithmetic condition trig equation

## Memo working

In an arithmetic sequence consecutive differences are equal, so cos^2 x - sin^2 x = (1/2)sin 2x - cos^2 x. Expand the double angle: (1/2)sin 2x = (1/2)(2 sin x cos x) = sin x cos x. Bring everything to one side: 2cos^2 x - sin x cos x - sin^2 x = 0. This factorises as (2cos x + sin x)(cos x - sin x) = 0. The first bracket gives sin x = -2cos x, so tan x = -2; the second gives tan x = 1. The reference angles are 63,43° and 45°, so x = 116,57° + k.180° or x = 45° + k.180°, k ∈ Z. But at x = 45° + k.180° all three terms equal 1/2, making the constant difference zero — the question says the difference is NOT zero, so this solution is rejected. (Alternative memo factorisation: group as (cos x - sin x)(cos x + sin x) + cos x(cos x - sin x) = 0.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

x = 116,57° + k.180°, k ∈ Z (x = 45° + k.180° gives constant difference zero, excluded)
