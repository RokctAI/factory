## Question (4 marks)

Express in terms of k:  sin 10°  (Given, from QUESTION 5.1: tan 50° = k, so that cos 40° = sin 50° = k/sqrt(k^2 + 1) and sin 40° = cos 50° = 1/sqrt(k^2 + 1).)

## Method

compound angle expansion

## Memo working

Write 10° as a difference of the known angles: sin 10° = sin(50° - 40°) = sin 50° cos 40° - cos 50° sin 40° = (k/sqrt(k^2+1))(k/sqrt(k^2+1)) - (1/sqrt(k^2+1))(1/sqrt(k^2+1)) = (k^2 - 1)/(k^2 + 1). (Memo alternatives: sin 10° = cos 80° = 2 cos^2 40° - 1 = 2k^2/(k^2+1) - 1 = (k^2-1)/(k^2+1); or sin 10° = sin(60° - 50°) = (√3 - k)/(2sqrt(k^2+1)).)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

sin 10° = (k^2 - 1)/(k^2 + 1)
