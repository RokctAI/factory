## Question (5 marks)

A cyclist rode from town P and stopped at town T. The speed (in km/h) at which this cyclist rode, is represented by the equation s'(t) = -3t^2 + 18t, where speed is the rate of change in distance with respect to time. Calculate the distance between town P and town T.

## Method

antiderivative of rate

## Memo working

The ride starts and ends when the speed is zero: -3t^2 + 18t = 0 -> -3t(t - 6) = 0 -> t = 0 or t = 6, so the ride lasts 6 hours. Distance is the function whose derivative is the speed. Try s(t) = at^3 + bt^2, so s'(t) = 3at^2 + 2bt; matching -3t^2 + 18t gives 3a = -3 and 2b = 18, so a = -1 and b = 9. Thus s(t) = -t^3 + 9t^2, and the distance covered is s(6) = -(6)^3 + 9(6)^2 = -216 + 324 = 108.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

108 km
