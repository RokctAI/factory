Today we put enlargement on the co-ordinate plane and discover its rule: with the origin as centre and scale factor k, the point x semicolon y becomes k x semicolon k y. Triangle ABC with A at 1 semicolon 1, B at 3 semicolon 1 and C at 1 semicolon 2 becomes 2 semicolon 2, 6 semicolon 2 and 2 semicolon 4 when k is 2. Reductions use a scale factor between 0 and 1, so halving 4 semicolon 6 gives 2 semicolon 3, and the inverse of scale factor k is 1 over k. We see why the rule works by drawing rays from the origin, where every point slides along its own ray to k times its distance, and we use the same idea to test whether an image is a true enlargement: both co-ordinates of every vertex must give the same quotient. We confirm that area is multiplied by k squared, and finish with the five most common errors.
