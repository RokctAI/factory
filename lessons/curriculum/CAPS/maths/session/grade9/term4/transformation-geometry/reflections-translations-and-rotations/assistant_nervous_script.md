Hey — if all the rules for moving shapes started to swim today, here is the calm version: mirror, slide, turn.

Mirror. In the x-axis, x keeps its number and y changes sign: 1 semicolon 2 goes to 1 semicolon negative 2. In the y-axis, y keeps its number and x changes sign. The axis you use as the mirror keeps its own number. In the slanted line y equals x, just swap the two numbers: 1 semicolon 2 goes to 2 semicolon 1. A mirror flips the shape, so the corners read the other way round.

Slide. A translation moves every point the same amount. Right and up mean add; left and down mean subtract. 3 left and 2 down takes 1 semicolon 2 to negative 2 semicolon 0. Nothing flips or turns.

Turn. Around the origin, half a turn changes both signs. A quarter turn anticlockwise swaps the numbers and makes the first one negative; clockwise swaps and makes the second one negative. If you forget which, check the quadrant: a quarter turn anticlockwise from the top right lands in the top left. If the turning point is somewhere else, count from that point.

Every one of these keeps the shape the same size and shape. Mirror, slide, turn, and check the quadrant. You've got this!
