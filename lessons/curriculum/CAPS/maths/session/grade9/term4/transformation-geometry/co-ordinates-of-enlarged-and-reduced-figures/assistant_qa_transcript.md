### subtopic_1 — Multiplying Co-ordinates by k
**Assistant:** Why does multiplying both co-ordinates by 2 double every side, even the slanted ones?

**Tutor:** Because the length of any side depends only on the horizontal and vertical differences between its endpoints, and both of those differences are doubled. Take a slanted side from 1 semicolon 1 to 3 semicolon 2. Going from the first point to the second, x increases by 2 and y increases by 1, so by the theorem of Pythagoras the length is the square root of 2 squared plus 1 squared, which is the square root of 5. After multiplying all co-ordinates by 2, the endpoints are 2 semicolon 2 and 6 semicolon 4. Now x increases by 4 and y by 2, both doubled, and the length is the square root of 4 squared plus 2 squared, which is the square root of 20. The square root of 20 is the square root of 4 times 5, which is 2 times the square root of 5. The slanted side has doubled exactly, like the horizontal and vertical ones. The algebra works for any side and any scale factor: if the differences are a and b, the new differences are k a and k b, and the new length is the square root of k squared a squared plus k squared b squared, which is k times the original length. This is also why multiplying only one co-ordinate fails. Then only one of the differences changes, and the slanted sides change by an amount that depends on their direction, so different sides grow by different factors and the shape is distorted. Multiplying both co-ordinates by the same k is precisely what makes every length, in every direction, grow by k.

### subtopic_2 — Reductions and Fractional Scale Factors
**Assistant:** If a reduction makes the shape smaller, why do we still multiply instead of divide?

**Tutor:** Because multiplying by a number between 0 and 1 is a way of making something smaller, and it lets one rule cover both enlargements and reductions. Halving 4 semicolon 6 can be described as dividing by 2 or as multiplying by one half; both give 2 semicolon 3. Writing it as multiplication by the scale factor means the same formula, x semicolon y becomes k x semicolon k y, works whether k is 3, or 1, or one half, or 0.4. The size of k alone tells you what happens: greater than 1 enlarges, between 0 and 1 reduces, and exactly 1 leaves the figure unchanged. This matters when the scale factor is not a simple unit fraction. A scale factor of three quarters is awkward to describe as a division, but multiplying by three quarters is straightforward: 8 semicolon 12 becomes 6 semicolon 9. Scale factor 0.4 takes 5 semicolon negative 10 to 2 semicolon negative 4. The same idea appears in percentages, where a 75 percent reduction on a photocopier means multiplying by 0.75, and in maps, where a scale of 1 to 50 000 is a scale factor of one fifty-thousandth. It also makes inverses easy to state. The inverse of multiplying by k is multiplying by 1 over k, so the reduction by one half is undone by the enlargement by 2, and the enlargement by 2.5 is undone by the reduction by 0.4. To find an original from an image, multiply the image co-ordinates by 1 over k.

### subtopic_3 — Rays from the Origin and Checking an Enlargement
**Assistant:** What happens if the centre of enlargement is not the origin?

**Tutor:** Then the rule x semicolon y becomes k x semicolon k y no longer applies directly, but the idea of rays from the centre still does. An enlargement always moves each point along the ray from the centre through that point, to k times its distance from the centre. When the centre is the origin, the co-ordinates themselves measure the position relative to the centre, so multiplying them by k does the job. When the centre is somewhere else, you first work out each point's position relative to the centre, multiply that by k, and then add the centre back. Suppose the centre is 1 semicolon 1 and the scale factor is 2, and you want the image of 4 semicolon 3. Relative to the centre, the point is 3 right and 2 up. Doubling gives 6 right and 4 up. Counting from the centre, the image is 7 semicolon 5. Notice that simply doubling the co-ordinates would give 8 semicolon 6, which is wrong, because it uses the origin as the centre. You can check with the ray: the centre, the point and the image should be in a straight line, with the image twice as far from the centre as the point. From 1 semicolon 1 to 4 semicolon 3 is 3 across and 2 up; from 1 semicolon 1 to 7 semicolon 5 is 6 across and 4 up, the same direction at twice the distance. Grade 9 questions on co-ordinates usually use the origin, but the ray picture works for any centre.

### subtopic_4 — Area of the Image and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** Why is adding the scale factor to the co-ordinates a translation and not an enlargement?

**Tutor:** Because adding the same number to every co-ordinate moves every point by the same amount in the same direction, which is exactly what a translation does, and a translation never changes size. Take the rectangle with corners 1 semicolon 1, 4 semicolon 1, 4 semicolon 3 and 1 semicolon 3. Adding 2 to every co-ordinate gives 3 semicolon 3, 6 semicolon 3, 6 semicolon 5 and 3 semicolon 5. The rectangle is still 3 units by 2 units, area 6 square units, just moved 2 right and 2 up. Multiplying every co-ordinate by 2 instead gives 2 semicolon 2, 8 semicolon 2, 8 semicolon 6 and 2 semicolon 6, a rectangle 6 units by 4 units, area 24 square units, which is 2 squared times the original area. The difference comes from what happens to the distances between points. Adding a constant to both endpoints of a side leaves their difference unchanged, because the constant cancels when you subtract, so every length is preserved. Multiplying both endpoints by k multiplies their difference by k, so every length is scaled. There is a simple check to catch this error: compare a side length before and after. If it has not changed, you have translated, not enlarged. Another check is the origin itself. Under an enlargement about the origin, the origin stays fixed, since k times 0 is 0. Under the mistaken addition, the origin would move to 2 semicolon 2, which shows immediately that something other than an enlargement about the origin has happened.
