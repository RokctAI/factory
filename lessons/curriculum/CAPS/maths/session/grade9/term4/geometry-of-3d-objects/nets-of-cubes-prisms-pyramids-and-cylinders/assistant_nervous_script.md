Hey — if folding shapes in your head felt tricky today, here is the calm version: unfold a box, match the edges, unroll the label.

Unfold a box. A net is the box cut open and laid flat in one piece, every face once. A cube net is 6 squares, and there are 11 real ones. Two quick fake detectors: a row of 5 or more squares, or a 2 by 2 block. In any straight line of 3 squares, the first and third end up opposite, which is how you number a die so opposites add to 7. When in doubt, pick a floor square and fold the walls up in your head.

Match the edges. A tent-shaped prism is two triangles and a strip of rectangles. Every edge must meet an edge of the same length: the triangle's 3 centimetre side sits on a 3 centimetre edge. A square pyramid is a square with a triangle on each side; the triangles must be tall enough to meet at the top.

Unroll the label. A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle. The rectangle is as tall as the tin and as long as the distance round it, the circumference: about 22 centimetres for a radius of 3.5. Never the width of the tin.

For a model, count tabs: edges take away folds. A cube needs 7. You've got this!
