Hey — if five solids with Greek names felt like a lot to remember today, here is the calm version: flat sides, folds and corners; five fair dice; and corners that must fit.

Flat sides are faces, folds are edges, corners are vertices. Count by building the solid in your head, not by poking at a picture, because a drawing hides the back. A box has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 corners. Check every count with the magic 2: corners take away edges add faces. For the box, 8 take away 12 add 6 is 2.

Five fair dice. The 4-sided die is the tetrahedron, 4 triangles. The 6-sided die is the cube. The 8-sided die is the octahedron, 8 triangles. The 12-sided die is the dodecahedron, 12 pentagons. The 20-sided die is the icosahedron, 20 triangles. For edges, count all the sides of all the faces and halve: 20 triangles have 60 sides, so 30 edges.

Corners that must fit. At least three faces meet at a corner, and their angles must add to less than 360 degrees. Triangles fit three, four or five at a corner; squares three; pentagons three. Six triangles, four squares or three hexagons lie flat. That is why there are only five.

Count, check with 2, halve the sides. You've got this!
