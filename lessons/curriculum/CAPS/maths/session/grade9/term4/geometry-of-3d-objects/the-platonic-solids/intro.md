Today geometry leaves the page and stands up in space, starting with the five most symmetrical solids there are. We count faces, edges and vertices on prisms and pyramids, with a method that does not miss the hidden ones, then define a Platonic solid by its two conditions: every face the same regular polygon, and the same number of faces meeting at every vertex. We meet the tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron, the shapes of the four-, six-, eight-, twelve- and twenty-sided dice, and record their counts. We check every count with Euler's formula, V minus E plus F equals 2, and learn to build the counts from the faces alone: the icosahedron's 20 triangles have 60 sides, which make 30 edges. Finally, the angles at a single vertex show why there can be exactly five Platonic solids and never a sixth.
