Hey — if faces, surfaces, edges and slices of round things got tangled today, here is the calm version: the tin can, the ball, and slice it and see.

The tin can. A cylinder has a flat circle on top and on the bottom, one curved surface round the side, two curved rims for edges, and no corners. Say all four: 2 flat faces, 1 curved surface, 2 edges, 0 vertices. Describe it with its radius and height, and remember the diameter is twice the radius: a tin 7 centimetres across has radius 3.5.

The ball. A sphere is every point the same distance from the middle. It has 1 curved surface and nothing else: no flat faces, no edges, no corners. A circle is flat on paper; a sphere is solid in your hand. Half a ball is a hemisphere, with 1 flat face, 1 curved surface and 1 rim.

Slice it and see. Cut a tin across and you get a circle the same as its end, every time; cut it straight down the middle and you get a rectangle; cut at a slant for an oval. Cut a ball anywhere and you get a circle, biggest through the middle. Cut a pipe across and you get a ring; the hole is the outside width minus the wall on both sides.

Name it, list all four parts, slice to check. You've got this!
