Today we describe the two curved solids we meet most often, the cylinder and the sphere. A cylinder has 2 flat circular faces, 1 curved surface, 2 curved edges and no vertices, and it is fixed by its radius and its height, as for a tin 7 centimetres across and 10 centimetres tall. A sphere is every point at one distance from a centre: 1 curved surface, no faces, edges or vertices, and a hemisphere is half of one. We slice both solids to find their cross-sections, circles, rectangles and ovals for the cylinder and only circles for the sphere, including a slice 6 centimetres from the centre of a 10 centimetre sphere. We set them beside prisms, pyramids and cones in one table and see why Euler's formula does not apply. Then we describe real objects, from rainwater tanks and netballs to a drainpipe with walls on both sides, and clear out the five common errors.
