Today we unfold solids into nets and fold nets back into solids. A net is one flat piece made of a solid's faces, joined edge to edge, that folds up with no gaps and no overlaps. We find the eleven nets of a cube and two quick tests that expose the fakes, a row of five squares and a two-by-two block, and we use lines of three squares to find opposite faces, so a die can be numbered with opposites adding to 7. We draw prism nets in which every pair of meeting edges is equal, including a 3, 4, 5 triangular prism whose strip of rectangles is 12 centimetres long. We draw a square pyramid whose 5 centimetre triangles meet 4 centimetres above a 6 centimetre base, and a cylinder whose rectangle is the circumference, about 22 centimetres, not the diameter. Finally we plan real models, counting glue tabs from edges and folds.
