Today the nets pay off. Surface area is the total area of every face of a solid, which is the area of its net, and volume is the number of unit cubes that fit inside. We find the surface area of a cube, of a 4 by 3 by 2 box as twice the sum of three products, of a 3, 4, 5 triangular prism as two ends plus perimeter times length, and of a cylinder as two circles plus the curved surface, 2 pi r h, which comes to about 296.88 square centimetres for a tin of radius 3.5. For volume one rule covers everything: the area of the base times the height. We turn volume into capacity, a cubic centimetre holding a millilitre and a cubic metre holding 1 000 litres, for a fish tank, a tin and a rainwater tank of about 2 120 litres. Then we convert square and cubic units correctly and clear out the five errors that cost the most marks.
