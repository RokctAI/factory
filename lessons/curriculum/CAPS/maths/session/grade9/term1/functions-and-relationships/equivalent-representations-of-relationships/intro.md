Today we take one relationship — "multiply by 3, then add 2" — and describe it five ways: in words, as a flow diagram, as a table, as the formula y equals 3x plus 2, and as a graph on the Cartesian plane. Then we ask the question the CAPS curriculum puts at the centre of this topic: when are two descriptions equivalent, and how do you justify that they are? Two descriptions are equivalent when every input gives the same output in both, and that is tested by substitution and settled by algebra. We will plot the points of a table to see the straight line a constant difference produces, read inputs and outputs off a graph, catch descriptions that look alike but differ, and interpret what the steepness and starting point of a graph mean in a context. This is the picture Grade 10 functions start from.
