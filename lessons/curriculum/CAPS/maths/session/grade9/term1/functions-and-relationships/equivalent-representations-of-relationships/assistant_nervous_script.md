Hey — if words, diagrams, tables, formulas and graphs felt like five topics, here is the calm version: it is one taxi, photographed from five angles.

The taxi charges R2 to get in and R3 a kilometre. Words: that sentence. Flow diagram: times 3, then plus 2. Formula: C equals 3k plus 2. Table: 2, 5, 8, 11. Graph: plot each row as a dot — across first, then up — and the dots line up in a straight line because every kilometre costs the same. Where the line hits the vertical axis is the start, the R2. How steep it is, 3 up for 1 across, is the rate.

Moving between pictures never changes the taxi. If 3 kilometres costs R11 in the formula, it must cost R11 in the table and on the graph. If it does not, you slipped in the translation, and that is easy to fix.

Are two descriptions the same? Two steps. Try a number: if they ever disagree, they are different, done. If they keep agreeing, write both as formulas, multiply out any brackets, and compare. Matching formulas mean the same rule for every input.

And always translate words into a formula before comparing — "three times a number plus two" and "three times the sum of a number and two" are not the same.
