Hey — if HCF and LCM keep swapping places in your head, let me give you the two pictures that stop the swapping.

Every number is built from prime bricks. Break both numbers into bricks first; that part is just halving until you can't, then trying 3, then 5. Now the two questions. Shared bricks — the ones in BOTH rows — multiply to the HCF, the biggest thing that divides both. That's the answer for splitting into the biggest equal packs or the largest tile. Combined bricks — one full row plus whatever the other row still needs — multiply to the LCM, the first number both fit into. That's the answer for "when do the taxis leave together again".

And here's your safety net: the HCF can never be bigger than the smaller number, and the LCM can never be smaller than the bigger number. If your answer breaks one of those, you've simply used the other picture — swap and try again. Even better, multiply your two answers together: they must equal the two original numbers multiplied. That check catches almost every slip.

Open the practice questions, break the numbers into bricks, and ask out loud: shared, or combined? You've got this!
