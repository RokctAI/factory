Hey — if ratio, rate and the two proportions blurred into one long word problem, let me hand you the three pictures that keep them apart.

Sharing in a ratio is piles. Count the parts, price one pile, hand out the piles, add to check. Ten parts of R2 400 means R240 a pile — that's the whole method, every time.

A rate is the word "per". Rand per litre, kilometres per hour. Divide the first thing by the second so the second becomes one, and keep the units stuck to the number. That's how you find the cheaper milk and the taxi's speed with the same move.

And proportion is one question: when the first thing goes up, does the second go up or down? Up with up — bread and money — divide for the unit rate, then multiply. Up with down — painters and days — multiply for the fixed total, then divide. Ask it out loud before you touch a number, and you will never make four workers finish faster than six.

None of this needs clever algebra. It needs the right picture first, then one divide and one multiply. Open the practice questions, name the picture for each one, and let the method follow. You've got this!
