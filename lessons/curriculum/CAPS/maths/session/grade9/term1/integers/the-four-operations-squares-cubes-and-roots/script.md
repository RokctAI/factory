# Part 1 — Expert

This session consolidates every operation on integers — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, squares, cubes, square roots and cube roots — and then governs them with the order of operations. The anchor expression is 5 minus 2 times the quantity negative 3 squared, plus the cube root of negative 8. It evaluates to negative 15, and each step of that evaluation is a rule you must own.

## Subtopic: Adding and Subtracting Integers

Integers live on the number line, with zero at the centre, positives to the right and negatives to the left. Adding a positive moves right; adding a negative moves left.

Negative 7 plus 3: start at negative 7, move 3 to the right, land on negative 4. Negative 7 plus negative 3: start at negative 7, move 3 further left, land on negative 10. When the signs are the same, add the sizes and keep the sign. When the signs differ, subtract the smaller size from the larger and take the sign of the larger: negative 7 and positive 3 differ by 4, and 7 is the larger size, so the answer is negative 4.

Subtraction is adding the opposite. 5 minus negative 3 is 5 plus 3, which is 8: subtracting a negative is adding a positive. Negative 4 minus 6 is negative 4 plus negative 6, which is negative 10. Negative 4 minus negative 6 is negative 4 plus 6, which is 2. Rewrite every subtraction as an addition first; the rules above then finish it.

A context fixes the picture. At dawn in Sutherland the temperature is negative 8 degrees; by midday it is 7 degrees. The rise is 7 minus negative 8, which is 7 plus 8, which is 15 degrees. A bank account at negative R450 — overdrawn — receives a deposit of R300: negative 450 plus 300 is negative 150, still overdrawn.

Take the questions for this section now: same signs add and keep, different signs subtract and take the larger's sign, and subtracting a negative is adding a positive.

## Subtopic: Multiplying and Dividing — The Sign Rules

Multiplication and division share one rule. Same signs give a positive result; different signs give a negative result. The sizes multiply or divide as usual.

Negative 6 times negative 4 is positive 24. Negative 6 times 4 is negative 24. Negative 36 divided by 9 is negative 4. Negative 36 divided by negative 4 is positive 9.

With three or more factors, count the negatives. An even count of negative factors gives a positive product; an odd count gives a negative product. Negative 2 times negative 3 times negative 5: three negatives, odd, so the product is negative; the sizes give 30; the answer is negative 30. Negative 2 times negative 3 times 5: two negatives, even, positive 30.

Why does a negative times a negative give a positive? Follow the pattern. 3 times negative 2 is negative 6. 2 times negative 2 is negative 4. 1 times negative 2 is negative 2. 0 times negative 2 is 0. Each step the first factor drops by one and the product rises by 2. Continue: negative 1 times negative 2 must be 2, and negative 2 times negative 2 must be 4. The rule is forced by the pattern, not invented.

Division by zero remains undefined — negative 8 divided by 0 has no value — while 0 divided by negative 8 is simply 0.

This section's questions are with you now: same signs positive, different signs negative, and the count-the-negatives rule for longer products.

## Subtopic: Squares, Cubes and Their Roots

A square is a number multiplied by itself. Negative 5 squared, with the bracket around negative 5, is negative 5 times negative 5, which is positive 25 — same signs. Every square of a non-zero integer is positive. But the expression negative 5 squared WITHOUT a bracket means the negative of 5 squared: 5 squared is 25, so the value is negative 25. The bracket decides what gets squared. Write the bracket when the negative is included; omit it when only the 5 is squared.

A cube is a number multiplied by itself three times. Negative 3 cubed is negative 3 times negative 3 times negative 3: three negatives, odd, so negative; 27 in size; negative 27. Cubes keep the sign of the base: positive cubed is positive, negative cubed is negative.

Roots reverse these operations, with one asymmetry. The square root of 25 is 5 — the root symbol means the positive root only. Negative 5 also squares to 25, but the square root of 25 is 5, by definition. The square root of negative 25 has no real value, because no real number squared gives a negative. The cube root of 125 is 5, since 5 cubed is 125. And the cube root of negative 27 IS defined: it is negative 3, since negative 3 cubed is negative 27. Cube roots of negatives are negative; square roots of negatives do not exist among the real numbers.

Know the perfect squares to 15 squared, which is 225, and the perfect cubes to 6 cubed, which is 216: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216. Recognising them is what makes roots instant.

Turn to the questions for this section: the bracket that decides a square's sign, the sign a cube keeps, and the asymmetry between square roots and cube roots of negatives.

## Subtopic: Order of Operations with Integers

The order: brackets first; then exponents and roots; then multiplication and division from left to right; then addition and subtraction from left to right. The sign rules operate inside every step.

Build the anchor: 5 minus 2 times the quantity negative 3 squared, plus the cube root of negative 8. Exponents and roots first. Negative 3, bracketed, squared is 9. The cube root of negative 8 is negative 2. The expression becomes 5 minus 2 times 9 plus negative 2. Multiplication next: 2 times 9 is 18. Now 5 minus 18 plus negative 2, left to right: 5 minus 18 is negative 13; negative 13 plus negative 2 is negative 15. The answer is negative 15.

Contrast pairs sharpen the rules. Negative 3 plus 4 times negative 2: multiply first, 4 times negative 2 is negative 8, then negative 3 plus negative 8 is negative 11. But bracketed negative 3 plus 4, times negative 2: the bracket gives 1, times negative 2 is negative 2. Two minus 3 squared: 3 squared is 9, and 2 minus 9 is negative 7. Bracketed 2 minus 3, squared: negative 1 squared is 1. Negative 12 divided by negative 4, plus negative 2 cubed: the division gives 3, the cube gives negative 8, and 3 plus negative 8 is negative 5.

Roots and brackets: the square root of 16 plus 9 means the root of the whole 25, which is 5. The square root of 16 plus the square root of 9 is 4 plus 3, which is 7. The root symbol acts as a bracket over everything beneath it.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: squaring negative 3 without the bracket and writing negative 9 when the bracket was there — or 9 when it was not. Two: reading 5 minus 2 times 9 as 3 times 9. Three: declaring the cube root of negative 8 impossible — that is the square root's restriction, not the cube root's. Four: working addition before multiplication because it came first on the line.

The closing questions of this part are in front of you now: the anchor evaluated to negative 15, the contrast pairs, the root as a bracket, and the four exhibits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the whole lesson again with debts, a frosty morning and a few bricks — the same answers, but built from pictures first.

## Subtopic: Money and Temperature — Adding and Taking Away

A negative number is money you owe, or degrees below zero. That one picture does most of the work.

You owe R7 and you earn R3. You hand the R3 over and still owe R4: negative 7 plus 3 is negative 4. You owe R7 and borrow R3 more: now you owe R10: negative 7 plus negative 3 is negative 10. When both numbers are debts, the debts pile up and the answer is a bigger debt. When one is a debt and one is cash, they fight, and whichever is bigger wins — with its sign.

Taking away is where the surprise lives. You have R5, and someone cancels a R3 debt you owed — they take away a negative. You are now R8 better off: 5 minus negative 3 is 5 plus 3, which is 8. Taking away a debt feels exactly like being given cash. So the rule: subtracting a negative is adding a positive. Every time you see minus-a-minus, turn it into plus and carry on.

Temperature tells the same story. Sutherland at dawn, negative 8 degrees. By midday, plus 7. How far did it climb? From negative 8 up to zero is 8 degrees; from zero up to 7 is 7 more; total 15. In symbols that is 7 minus negative 8 equals 15 — the two minuses became a plus, and the picture shows why.

Your questions for this section are with you now: debts piling up, debt against cash, and taking away a debt.

## Subtopic: Why a Minus Times a Minus Is a Plus

Multiplying is repeated adding. Three debts of R2 is 3 times negative 2, which is negative 6 — you owe R6. A positive times a negative is a negative; that much feels natural.

Now negative times negative. Negative 3 times negative 2 means: take AWAY three debts of R2. Someone removes three R2 debts from your account. Each removal makes you R2 richer, so you end up R6 better off. Negative 3 times negative 2 is positive 6. Taking away debt is gaining money, which is the same picture as subtracting a negative — the two rules are the same rule wearing different clothes.

The pattern proves it too. 3 times negative 2 is negative 6. 2 times negative 2 is negative 4. 1 times negative 2 is negative 2. 0 times negative 2 is 0. Each time the first number drops by one, the answer goes up by 2. Keep going: negative 1 times negative 2 is 2, and negative 2 times negative 2 is 4. The pattern leaves no other choice.

Dividing follows multiplying: same signs give plus, different signs give minus. Negative 36 divided by 9 is negative 4. Negative 36 divided by negative 4 is 9. And for a long chain, just count the minus signs: an even count means plus, an odd count means minus. Negative 2 times negative 3 times negative 5 has three minuses — odd — so negative 30.

One careful picture for squares. Negative 5 in a bracket, squared, is negative 5 times negative 5: two minuses, so positive 25. But negative 5 squared with no bracket means "the negative of 5 squared", which is negative 25. The bracket says whether the minus is inside the squaring or standing outside it.

Your questions on this section are ready: taking away debts, counting the minuses, and the bracket around a squared negative.

## Subtopic: Roots and the Order of Doing Things

Cubes keep their sign. Negative 3 cubed is negative 3 times negative 3 times negative 3: three minuses, odd, so negative 27. That means you can go backwards: the cube root of negative 27 is negative 3. A cube root of a negative is perfectly fine.

Squares cannot be negative. Positive times positive is positive, and negative times negative is positive too, so NOTHING squared gives a negative. The square root of negative 25 therefore has no answer among the numbers we use. And the square root of 25 is 5 — just the positive one, by agreement. Square roots of negatives: no. Cube roots of negatives: yes, and negative.

Now the order of doing things, and the anchor line: 5 minus 2 times negative 3 in a bracket squared, plus the cube root of negative 8. Think of it as jobs with priorities. Powers and roots are urgent: negative 3 squared is 9; the cube root of negative 8 is negative 2. The line is now 5 minus 2 times 9 plus negative 2. Multiplying comes before adding and subtracting: 2 times 9 is 18. Now just walk left to right: 5 minus 18 is negative 13; negative 13 plus negative 2 is negative 15.

Two quick contrasts to keep you honest. Negative 3 plus 4 times negative 2: the multiply first, 4 times negative 2 is negative 8, then negative 3 plus negative 8 is negative 11. Put a bracket around negative 3 plus 4 and it becomes 1 times negative 2, which is negative 2. Different brackets, different answers — the brackets are instructions, not decoration. And the square root of 16 plus 9 is the root of 25, which is 5, because the root sign covers everything under it like a lid; it is not 4 plus 3.

Hold the pictures: debts for adding and subtracting, taking away debts for the double minus, count the minuses for long products, and jobs with priorities for the order. The final questions of the lesson are with you now: the cube root that can be negative, the square root that cannot, and the anchor line walked to negative 15.
