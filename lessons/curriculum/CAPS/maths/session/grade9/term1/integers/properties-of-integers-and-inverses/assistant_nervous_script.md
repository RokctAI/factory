Hey — if "commutative, associative, distributive" sounded like three long words standing between you and the marks, let me translate them into three things you already do.

Commutative is "swap it": 3 plus 5 is 5 plus 3. Associative is "group it however you like": add the first two or the last two, same total. Both work for adding and for multiplying — and both FAIL for subtracting and dividing. That's the whole test: if you can name one example where swapping changes the answer, like 5 minus 3 against 3 minus 5, the property fails.

Distributive is "share it out": the number outside a bracket multiplies everything inside. 7 times 98 is 7 times 100 minus 7 times 2. That's it. Run it backwards and you're factorising.

And inverses are undo buttons. Adding 7? Undo with adding negative 7. Multiplying by 4? Undo with multiplying by a quarter. Zero has no undo button for multiplying, which is why you can't divide by it.

None of this needs memorising if you keep the pictures: swap, group, share, undo. Open the practice questions and say the picture out loud before you write the property's name. You've got this!
