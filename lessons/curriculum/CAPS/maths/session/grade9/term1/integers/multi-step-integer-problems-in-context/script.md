# Part 1 — Expert

This session applies the integer operations to problems set in contexts, where several operations occur in one scenario. The anchor problem is a bank account: opening balance negative R250, a deposit of R1 200, three debit orders of R180 each, and a bank fee of R45. The method has four stages — translate, write one expression, evaluate in order, check — and each stage is worth marks.

## Subtopic: Translating a Context Into Signs

Every context has a zero and two directions, and the first job is to name them. On a thermometer, zero is freezing, above is positive, below is negative. In a bank account, zero is an empty account, money you have is positive, money you owe is negative. For altitude, zero is sea level, above is positive, below is negative. In a lift, the ground floor is zero, upper floors positive, basement levels negative. In golf, par is zero and strokes under par are negative.

The vocabulary maps onto operations. Rise, climb, gain, deposit, earn, receive: add. Fall, drop, lose, withdraw, pay, spend: subtract. "Below", "under", "overdrawn", "in debt": a negative starting value. "Difference between": the higher value minus the lower value, which is always positive. "Change from A to B": B minus A, which carries a sign.

A diver is at negative 12 metres. She rises 5 metres, then descends 9 metres. Her depth is negative 12 plus 5 minus 9. Negative 12 plus 5 is negative 7; negative 7 minus 9 is negative 16. She is at negative 16 metres — 16 metres below the surface.

The Dead Sea shore lies at negative 430 metres; Johannesburg sits at 1 753 metres. The difference in altitude is 1 753 minus negative 430, which is 1 753 plus 430, which is 2 183 metres. "Difference between" a positive and a negative adds the sizes, because you cross zero on the way.

Take the questions for this section now: naming the zero in each context, the word-to-operation map, and the difference that crosses zero.

## Subtopic: Multi-Step Money Problems

The anchor. An account opens the month at negative R250 — overdrawn. A salary of R1 200 is deposited. Three debit orders of R180 each go off. A bank fee of R45 is charged. Find the closing balance.

Translate each event. Opening: negative 250. Deposit: plus 1 200. Debit orders: three of them, each minus 180, so minus 3 times 180. Fee: minus 45. Write ONE expression: negative 250 plus 1 200 minus 3 times 180 minus 45.

Evaluate in order. Multiplication first: 3 times 180 is 540. The expression becomes negative 250 plus 1 200 minus 540 minus 45. Left to right: negative 250 plus 1 200 is 950. 950 minus 540 is 410. 410 minus 45 is 365. The closing balance is positive R365 — the account is no longer overdrawn.

Write the single expression before evaluating. It earns a method mark, it exposes the repeated event that must be multiplied, and it lets the marker follow your reasoning even if a slip occurs later.

A trading problem. A spaza owner buys 24 cans of cooldrink at R8 each and sells 20 of them at R12 each; the remaining 4 pass their expiry date and are thrown away. Profit or loss? Income: 20 times 12 is R240. Cost: 24 times 8 is R192 — all 24 were paid for, including the 4 wasted. Profit: 240 minus 192 is R48. Had only 15 sold, income would be 180 and the result 180 minus 192, which is negative 12 — a loss of R12, written as a negative profit.

This section's questions are with you now: translating each event to a signed term, multiplying repeated events, and the single expression that carries the whole story.

## Subtopic: Temperature, Height and Scores

Temperature problems chain a rate with a time. In Sutherland the temperature at 05:00 is negative 6 degrees. It rises by 3 degrees every hour for 5 hours, then falls by 2 degrees every hour for 4 hours. The final temperature is negative 6 plus 3 times 5 minus 2 times 4. Multiply first: 15 and 8. Then negative 6 plus 15 is 9, and 9 minus 8 is 1. The temperature at 14:00 is 1 degree.

The mean of a set of integers is the sum divided by the count, and the sum must respect the signs. Five night-time readings: negative 4, negative 1, 3, 6 and 1. Sum: negative 4 plus negative 1 is negative 5; plus 3 is negative 2; plus 6 is 4; plus 1 is 5. Mean: 5 divided by 5 is 1 degree. A mean can be negative — readings of negative 4, negative 1 and negative 7 average negative 12 over 3, which is negative 4.

Scoring systems are integer problems in disguise. A quiz awards 5 points for a correct answer and deducts 3 points for a wrong one. A learner answers 7 correctly and 4 wrongly: 7 times 5 minus 4 times 3, which is 35 minus 12, which is 23 points. Another learner answers 3 correctly and 8 wrongly: 15 minus 24, which is negative 9 — a negative score is legitimate under these rules.

Lifts and floors. A lift is at basement level negative 2. It goes up 9 floors, down 4, then up 6. Final floor: negative 2 plus 9 minus 4 plus 6, which is 9. From basement 2 to floor 7 is a journey of 7 minus negative 2, which is 9 floors.

Turn to the questions for this section: rate times time inside a chain, the signed sum behind a mean, and scoring systems with penalties.

## Subtopic: Estimating, Checking and the Error Museum

Before evaluating any multi-step expression, estimate the SIGN of the answer. In the anchor account: the deposit of 1 200 is far larger than the overdraft plus the three debit orders plus the fee, which total 250 plus 540 plus 45, which is 835. So the answer must be positive, and about 365. A negative final answer would signal an error before any checking.

Then check by reversing. From the closing balance of 365, add back the fee and the debit orders and subtract the deposit: 365 plus 45 plus 540 minus 1 200 is negative 250, the opening balance. The reverse walk lands on the start, so the forward walk was correct.

Watch the order-of-operations trap inside stories. "Three debit orders of R180" is 3 times 180, computed before any adding or subtracting; a learner who writes negative 250 plus 1 200 minus 3 and then multiplies the whole result by 180 produces nonsense. Brackets protect: write the repeated event as its own product.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting the wrong way round for "difference between" — 1 753 minus negative 430 is 2 183, not 1 323; the difference between a height and a depth is larger than either. Two: forgetting to multiply a repeated event, so three debit orders are charged once. Three: sign amnesia at the start — treating "overdrawn by R250" as positive 250, which shifts every later line by 500. Four: dropping the wasted stock in a profit problem — the four expired cans were still paid for.

The disciplined layout, four lines. Line one: the single expression. Line two: products evaluated. Line three: the left-to-right walk. Line four: the answer with its unit and its meaning in words — "closing balance R365, in credit". Every line is a mark.

The closing questions of this part are in front of you now: the sign estimate, the reverse check, the single-expression layout and the four exhibits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same stories again — a bank account, a thermometer and a quiz night — told one step at a time with a running total.

## Subtopic: Up Means Plus, Down Means Minus

Every story in this topic has a floor called zero and two directions. Find the zero first. For money it is an empty account. For temperature it is freezing. For a lift it is the ground floor. For a diver it is the surface of the water.

Then listen to the words. Up, rise, gain, deposit, earn — those all mean plus. Down, fall, lose, withdraw, pay, spend — those all mean minus. And words like below, under, overdrawn, owing, in debt tell you that you START on the minus side of zero.

A diver is 12 metres below the surface: negative 12. She rises 5: negative 12 plus 5 is negative 7 — still under water, but closer to the top. She then descends 9: negative 7 minus 9 is negative 16. Sixteen metres down. Each word moved her, and each move was one sign.

A lift sits at basement 2 — negative 2. Up 9 floors: 7. Down 4: 3. Up 6: 9. Floor 9. And if someone asks how many floors from basement 2 to floor 7, you count through zero: two floors up to the ground, then seven more — 9 floors. In symbols, 7 minus negative 2 is 9, and the double minus turned into a plus because you crossed zero.

The biggest word-trap is "difference between". The difference between Johannesburg at 1 753 metres above sea level and the Dead Sea at 430 metres below is NOT 1 753 minus 430. The Dead Sea is on the other side of zero, so you add the two sizes: 1 753 plus 430 is 2 183 metres. Whenever the two places are on opposite sides of zero, their difference is bigger than either of them.

Your questions for this section are with you now: finding the zero, matching words to plus and minus, and the difference that crosses zero.

## Subtopic: Follow the Money Through the Month

Keep a running balance, one event at a time, and the account story becomes a walk.

Start: overdrawn by R250. That is negative 250 — you owe the bank. Salary comes in: R1 200. Negative 250 plus 1 200 is 950. You have paid off the overdraft and have R950 to show. Three debit orders of R180 go off. That is three separate events of minus 180, which together is 3 times 180, which is 540. 950 minus 540 is 410. Then the bank takes a R45 fee: 410 minus 45 is 365. You end the month with R365, in the black.

The same story written as one line is negative 250 plus 1 200 minus 3 times 180 minus 45, and the one-line version is what the examiner wants to see. The running balance is how you build it without losing track; the one line is how you present it. Notice that the three debit orders are MULTIPLIED, because the same thing happened three times — this is where most of the lost marks hide.

Now a spaza problem. You buy 24 cans of cooldrink at R8 each — that is money out, 24 times 8, which is R192. You sell 20 at R12 each — money in, 20 times 12, which is R240. The other 4 expire and go in the bin, but you still paid for them. Profit is money in minus money out: 240 minus 192 is R48. Change one number — only 15 sold — and money in drops to R180, so 180 minus 192 is negative 12: a loss of R12. A negative profit is simply a loss wearing its sign.

Your questions on this section are ready: the running balance, the repeated event that gets multiplied, and the stock you paid for but could not sell.

## Subtopic: Scores, Degrees and the Check at the End

Quiz night. Five points for a right answer, three points taken away for a wrong one. You get 7 right and 4 wrong. Rights: 7 times 5 is 35. Wrongs: 4 times 3 is 12 taken away. Score: 35 minus 12 is 23. Your friend gets 3 right and 8 wrong: 15 minus 24 is negative 9. Below zero on a quiz is perfectly possible when wrong answers cost points.

Sutherland again. Five in the morning, negative 6 degrees. Warming 3 degrees an hour for 5 hours is 3 times 5, which is 15 degrees of warming: negative 6 plus 15 is 9 degrees. Then cooling 2 degrees an hour for 4 hours is 2 times 4, which is 8 degrees of cooling: 9 minus 8 is 1 degree at two in the afternoon. Rate times time first, then move along the thermometer.

Here is the habit that protects every answer: guess the sign before you calculate, and check by walking backwards after. In the bank story, the salary was R1 200 and everything going out — 250 plus 540 plus 45 — adds to R835, so you must end up positive, somewhere around R365. If your calculation says negative, stop; something slipped. Then walk back: 365 plus the 45 fee plus the 540 of debit orders minus the 1 200 salary is negative 250 — exactly where you started. Forward walk and backward walk agree, so the answer stands.

Hold the pictures: find the zero, match the words to plus and minus, multiply anything that repeats, keep a running balance, and guess the sign first. The final questions of the lesson are with you now: the quiz score, the thermometer walk, and the backwards check.
