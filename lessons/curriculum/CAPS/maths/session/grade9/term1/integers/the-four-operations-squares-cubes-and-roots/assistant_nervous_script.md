Hey — if the signs kept flipping on you in that lesson, let me shrink the whole topic to three pictures you already understand.

Picture one: a negative is a debt. Adding a debt makes you poorer; taking a debt away makes you richer. That single picture gives you every addition and subtraction rule, including the famous minus-a-minus-is-a-plus.

Picture two: count the minuses. For any multiplying or dividing, an even number of minus signs means a plus answer, an odd number means a minus answer. Two minuses, plus. Three minuses, minus. You can count — that's all the rule needs.

Picture three: brackets are instructions. Negative 3 in a bracket, squared, is 9. Negative 3 squared with no bracket is minus 9. The bracket tells you whether the minus went into the squaring or stayed outside. And a root sign is a bracket too — it covers everything under it.

Then the order of doing things: powers and roots first, multiply and divide next, add and subtract last, left to right. Say the priority out loud before you touch a number.

Open the practice questions and, for each one, name the picture first. The signs stop flipping the moment you stop guessing them. You've got this!
