Today we study the rules that let you rearrange a calculation without changing its answer — the commutative, associative and distributive properties — and the two kinds of inverse that undo addition and multiplication. These are not decorations on arithmetic; they are the licence behind every shortcut you take in your head, and in a few weeks they become the licence behind every step of algebra, where 3 times the quantity x plus 4 can only be written as 3x plus 12 because the distributive property says so. We will test which operations obey which rules, use them to make ugly integer calculations easy, and meet the additive and multiplicative inverses that solving equations depends on.
