# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the conventions for writing algebraic expressions, the vocabulary of terms, coefficients, constants and exponents, and the distinction between like and unlike terms. The anchor expression is 4x squared minus 3x plus 7, which has three terms, coefficients 4 and negative 3, constant 7 and degree 2. The anchor simplification is 7a minus 3b plus 2a plus b, which collects to 9a minus 2b. Every convention here is a marking convention, and every one of them is enforced in the Term 1 test.

## Subtopic: The Conventions for Writing Expressions

A variable is a letter standing for a number not yet known, and an algebraic expression combines variables and numbers with operations. The conventions compress the writing. Multiplication signs between a number and a letter, or between letters, are omitted: 3 times x is written 3x; x times y is written xy; 2 times a times b is 2ab. The number is written first and the letters in alphabetical order, so y times 3 times x is written 3xy, never y3x.

A coefficient of 1 is not written: 1x is x, and 1ab is ab. The coefficient negative 1 is written as a bare minus sign: negative 1 times x is negative x. Division is written as a fraction: x divided by 2 is x over 2, which may also be written as one half x — the two forms are identical and both are accepted.

Repeated multiplication of a variable uses exponents, exactly as with numbers: x times x is x squared, and x times x times x is x cubed. Note the contrast with addition: x plus x is 2x, not x squared. One is two copies multiplied; the other is two copies added. Confusing them is the most frequent error in Grade 9 algebra.

Numbers multiply together even when letters are present: 2 times 3x is 6x, because 2 times 3 is 6 and the x is carried along. Letters multiply by the exponent laws: x times x squared is x cubed; 2x times 3x is 6x squared — coefficients multiply, exponents add. The quantity 3x, all squared, is 9x squared, since the exponent applies to the 3 as well.

Brackets indicate grouping and an implied multiplication: 3 times the quantity x plus 2 is written 3 open bracket x plus 2 close bracket, and x times the quantity y minus 1 is written x open bracket y minus 1 close bracket. The bracket is not expanded in this lesson; Term 2 does that. For now the convention is that a number or variable written against a bracket multiplies the whole bracket.

Substitution reads the expression with a number in place of the variable, and brackets around the substituted value protect the sign: for 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 at x equals negative 2, write 4 times open bracket negative 2 close bracket squared, minus 3 times open bracket negative 2 close bracket, plus 7, which is 4 times 4 plus 6 plus 7, which is 29.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing products and quotients in standard form, the invisible 1, and x plus x against x times x.

## Subtopic: Terms, Coefficients and Constants

Terms are the parts of an expression separated by plus and minus signs. In 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 the terms are 4x squared, negative 3x, and 7. Multiplication and division do not separate terms: 3xy is one term, and 2x over 5 is one term. The count of terms is the count of pieces between the plus and minus signs, after any brackets have been dealt with.

Each term owns the sign in front of it. The second term of the anchor is negative 3x, not 3x, because the minus sign belongs to it; the expression is the sum 4x squared plus negative 3x plus 7. Reading the sign as part of the term is what makes rearranging safe: negative 3x plus 4x squared plus 7 is the same expression with the terms in a different order, because each term carried its own sign.

The coefficient of a term is the numerical factor multiplying the variable part. The coefficient of 4x squared is 4; the coefficient of negative 3x is negative 3; the coefficient of x over 2 is one half; the coefficient of negative x is negative 1; the coefficient of 3xy is 3. When a question asks for "the coefficient of x" in 4x squared minus 3x plus 7, the answer is negative 3 — sign included — and when it asks for the coefficient of x squared, the answer is 4.

A constant term has no variable: the 7 in the anchor. Its value does not change when x changes, which is what "constant" means. An expression may have no constant term, as in 4x squared minus 3x, or may be only a constant, as in 7 by itself.

In context the vocabulary carries meaning. The taxi fare 3k plus 2 has a term 3k with coefficient 3, the rate per kilometre, and a constant 2, the flag fall. The perimeter 4s has one term with coefficient 4 and no constant. Being able to say which number is the coefficient and which is the constant is being able to say which number is the rate and which is the starting value.

Take this section's questions now: counting terms, reading coefficients with their signs, and identifying the constant.

## Subtopic: Exponents and Degree

The exponent on a variable counts repeated multiplication, as it did for numbers: x squared is x times x, and x to the power 5 is five copies of x multiplied. A variable with no visible exponent has exponent 1: x is x to the power 1. A constant can be regarded as having exponent 0 on the variable, since x to the power 0 is 1 and 7 is 7 times x to the power 0.

The exponent and the coefficient must not be confused. In 4x squared the 4 is a coefficient — how many x squareds — and the 2 is an exponent — how many x's multiplied in each. 2x means two x's added; x squared means two x's multiplied. At x equals 3: 2x is 6, x squared is 9, and 4x squared is 36, since the exponent acts on the x before the coefficient multiplies.

The degree of a term with one variable is its exponent: 4x squared has degree 2, negative 3x has degree 1, and 7 has degree 0. With several variables the degree is the sum of the exponents: 3x squared y has degree 3, and 5ab has degree 2. The degree of an expression is the highest degree among its terms: 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 has degree 2. Next lesson names expressions by their degree and by their number of terms.

The exponent laws apply with variables exactly as with numbers. x cubed times x squared is x to the power 5; x to the power 6 over x squared is x to the power 4; x squared all cubed is x to the power 6; 2x squared, all cubed, is 8x to the power 6. These are the laws from the exponents lessons with the base left as a letter.

Writing exponents correctly is a convention too: 3x squared means 3 times x squared, with only the x squared; the quantity 3x, all squared, needs its bracket and equals 9x squared. Omitting the bracket changes the expression, and the examiner marks what is written.

This section's questions are available now: exponent against coefficient, the degree of a term and of an expression, and the exponent laws with letters.

## Subtopic: Like and Unlike Terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part — the same letters, each with the same exponent. 3x and negative 5x are like terms; 3x and 3x squared are unlike, because the exponents differ; 3x and 3y are unlike, because the letters differ; 2xy and 5yx are like, because xy and yx are the same product by the commutative property; 4a squared b and 4ab squared are unlike, because the exponents sit on different letters. Constants are like terms with each other.

Like terms may be added or subtracted by adding or subtracting their coefficients: 3x plus 5x minus 2x is 6x, because 3 plus 5 minus 2 is 6 and the x is common. This is the distributive property run backwards — 3x plus 5x is the quantity 3 plus 5 times x — and it is the only reason like terms can be combined.

Unlike terms cannot be combined and must be left as they are. 3x plus 2 stays 3x plus 2; it is NOT 5x, because 2 is not a multiple of x. 3x plus 5x squared stays as written. An expression is simplified when every group of like terms has been collected and no further combination is possible.

The anchor: 7a minus 3b plus 2a plus b. Collect the a's: 7a plus 2a is 9a. Collect the b's: negative 3b plus b is negative 2b. Result: 9a minus 2b. Keep each term's sign with it when you regroup, and write the collected expression with the terms in a consistent order.

A longer example: 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 plus 2x squared plus 5x minus 10. The x squared terms: 4 plus 2 is 6, so 6x squared. The x terms: negative 3 plus 5 is 2, so 2x. The constants: 7 minus 10 is negative 3. Simplified: 6x squared plus 2x minus 3. Three groups, three terms, nothing further to combine.

The error museum for this topic has five exhibits. One: 3x plus 2 written as 5x — a constant absorbed into a variable term. Two: x plus x written as x squared — addition confused with multiplication. Three: 2x squared plus 3x squared written as 5x to the power 4 — exponents added when adding like terms; the correct answer is 5x squared, the exponent unchanged. Four: 3x and 3x squared combined as 6x cubed — unlike terms forced together. Five: the sign left behind — 7a minus 3b plus 2a rearranged as 7a plus 2a plus 3b, losing the minus on the b.

Layout for marks: underline or group like terms, write the coefficient arithmetic for each group, and write the simplified expression in descending powers. Check by substituting a value — at x equals 1, the long example gives 4 minus 3 plus 7 plus 2 plus 5 minus 10, which is 5, and 6 plus 2 minus 3 is 5.

The questions for this section are ready: deciding whether terms are like, collecting with signs, and the five exhibits of the museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same grammar again through a shorthand everyone already uses, a fruit bowl and a sentence read aloud — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: The Grammar of Algebra

Algebra is a shorthand, and like any shorthand it has spelling rules. You already use shorthand: "3 km" instead of "three kilometres", "R20" instead of "twenty rand". Algebra does the same with unknown numbers. Call the unknown x. Three of them is 3x — the times sign vanishes and the number goes in front. Two unknowns multiplied, x and y, is xy, letters in alphabetical order. One of something is just the something: 1x is written x, and the minus version, negative 1 times x, is written negative x.

Dividing is written as a fraction: x over 2 means x divided by 2, and it is the same as half of x. Multiplying the same unknown by itself gets a little raised number: x times x is x squared, x times x times x is x cubed. This is where the first trap lives. x plus x is 2x — two of them added. x times x is x squared — two of them multiplied. Say the words "added" and "multiplied" out loud and the trap closes.

Numbers in front multiply together like ordinary numbers: 2 lots of 3x is 6x. And when unknowns multiply, their little numbers add, exactly like last month's exponent laws: x times x squared is x cubed, 2x times 3x is 6x squared. If a bracket is squared, everything inside is squared: 3x in a bracket, all squared, is 9x squared, because the 3 gets squared too.

A number written against a bracket is multiplying the whole bracket: 3 open bracket x plus 2 close bracket means three lots of "x plus 2". We will open those brackets next term; for now, just read them correctly.

And to put a number in place of x, wrap it in brackets so the sign survives. 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 at x equals negative 2: 4 times negative 2 in brackets squared is 16; minus 3 times negative 2 in brackets is plus 6; plus 7. Total 29. The brackets stop negative 2 squared from turning into negative 4.

Keep the shorthand rules in mind and try the questions for this part: writing products the standard way, the vanishing 1, and added versus multiplied.

## Subtopic: Apples and Bananas

Picture a fruit bowl. 3 apples plus 5 apples is 8 apples. 3 apples plus 5 bananas is... 3 apples and 5 bananas. There is no fruit called "apple-banana" to collect them into. That is the whole of like and unlike terms. Like terms are the same fruit — the same letters with the same little numbers. Unlike terms are different fruits and stay separate.

So 3x plus 5x is 8x: same fruit, add the counts. 3x plus 5y stays 3x plus 5y. 3x plus 5x squared ALSO stays — x and x squared are different fruits, because one is x and the other is x times x. And 3x plus 2 stays 3x plus 2, because 2 is a plain number with no x in it; it is not 5x, any more than 3 apples and 2 is 5 apples.

The counts can be negative. 7a minus 3b plus 2a plus b: gather the a's, 7 and 2, making 9a; gather the b's, negative 3 and plus 1, making negative 2b. Answer: 9a minus 2b. The trick is that each count carries its own sign — the b's are negative 3 and positive 1, not 3 and 1.

Why can we add like terms at all? Because 3x plus 5x means "3 lots of x and 5 more lots of x", which is 8 lots of x — that is the sharing-out rule from the integers week, run backwards. It only works when the "lots of" are lots of the same thing.

The little number never changes when you add. 2x squared plus 3x squared is 5x squared — five of the same fruit. Not 5x to the power 4. Adding fruits does not change what kind of fruit they are. Only multiplying changes the little numbers.

Hold the fruit bowl in mind and try this part's questions: same fruit or different, counts with signs, and why the little number stays put when you add.

## Subtopic: Reading an Expression Like a Sentence

An expression is a sentence, and its words are the terms — the pieces between the plus and minus signs. 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 has three words: 4x squared, minus 3x, and 7. The minus belongs to the 3x the way a capital letter belongs to a name; when you move the word, the sign moves with it. Times and divide signs do not start a new word: 3xy is one word, and so is 2x over 5.

Each word has a number in front, called the coefficient, which says how many: 4 lots of x squared, negative 3 lots of x. If no number is showing, it is 1; if only a minus is showing, it is negative 1. A word with no letter at all — the 7 — is a constant, the part that never changes whatever x is. In the taxi fare 3k plus 2, the coefficient 3 is the rate per kilometre and the constant 2 is the flag fall. Coefficient means "how many", constant means "the fixed bit".

The little raised number is the exponent, and it says how many copies of the letter are multiplied together inside the word. Never confuse it with the coefficient. At x equals 3: 2x is two threes added, 6; x squared is two threes multiplied, 9; 4x squared is four lots of 9, which is 36. Exponent first, then coefficient.

The biggest exponent in the sentence is called its degree. 4x squared minus 3x plus 7 has degree 2. Next lesson we give expressions names from their degree and their number of words.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Write numbers first, letters alphabetical, no times signs. 1x is x. x plus x is 2x; x times x is x squared. Terms are the words between plus and minus, and each owns its sign. The coefficient is how many; the constant is the fixed bit; the exponent is how many copies multiplied. Same letters with the same exponents are like terms and can be added by adding their coefficients — the exponent stays. Different fruits stay separate.

Hold the shorthand, the fruit bowl and the sentence, and take the final questions of the lesson: naming the parts of an expression, and reading it aloud without losing a sign.
