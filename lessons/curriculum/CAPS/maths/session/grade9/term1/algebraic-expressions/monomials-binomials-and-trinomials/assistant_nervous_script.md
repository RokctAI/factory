Hey — if monomials, binomials and trinomials sounded like a biology test, here is the calm version: it is counting to three, with one rule about when to count.

The pieces of an expression are separated by plus and minus signs. Times and divide do not make gaps — 3xy is one piece, and so is 6x over 3. One piece is a monomial, two is a binomial, three is a trinomial. Mono, bi, tri — monorail, bicycle, tricycle.

The one rule: tidy before you count. 2x plus 3x looks like two pieces, but they are like terms and collapse into 5x — one piece, a monomial. Collect like terms first, then count.

Then add the first name from the biggest power: 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, 3 is cubic, no letter is constant. 3x squared minus 5x plus 2 is a quadratic trinomial. 4x cubed is a cubic monomial — the 3 is its power, not a count of pieces.

Write it biggest power first, each piece keeping its own sign. And if a letter is underneath a fraction bar or under a square root, it is not a polynomial at all — say so, and say why.

Open the practice set, tidy, count, name. You've got this!
