Hey — if algebra felt like a language everyone else already speaks, here is the calm version: it is a shorthand with about six spelling rules, and you can learn them in one sitting.

Numbers go in front, letters go in alphabetical order, and the times sign disappears: 3x, 2ab. One of something is just the something: x, not 1x. Dividing is a fraction: x over 2. And the big one: x plus x is 2x, two copies added; x times x is x squared, two copies multiplied. Say "added" or "multiplied" out loud and you will never mix them up.

The pieces between plus and minus signs are the terms, and each one owns the sign in front of it. The number in front is the coefficient — how many. A term with no letter is the constant — the fixed bit. The little raised number is the exponent — how many copies multiplied.

Like terms are the same fruit: same letters, same little numbers. Only like terms can be added, and when you add them the count changes and the little number does not. 3x plus 5x is 8x. 3x plus 2 stays 3x plus 2. 2x squared plus 3x squared is 5x squared.

Open the practice set, find the fruit, and keep every sign with its term. You've got this!
