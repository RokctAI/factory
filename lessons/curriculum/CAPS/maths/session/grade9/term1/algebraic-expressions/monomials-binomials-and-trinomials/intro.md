Today we name algebraic expressions by their shape. An expression with one term is a monomial, with two terms a binomial, with three a trinomial, and the name tells you at a glance which Term 2 technique will apply to it: a monomial multiplies straight through, a binomial may be a difference of squares, a trinomial may factorise into two brackets. We will count terms correctly — after simplifying, and never confusing factors with terms — add the degree to the name so that 3x squared minus 5x plus 2 becomes a quadratic trinomial, recognise expressions that are not polynomials at all, and write everything in standard form with descending powers. The vocabulary is small and the marks for it are easy, provided the counting is done after the tidying.
