Today we meet patterns that pass neither of last lesson's tests — no constant difference, no constant ratio — and learn to read them anyway. The square numbers 1, 4, 9, 16 have differences that grow by 2 each time; the triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10 have differences that grow by 1; the Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 are built by adding the two terms before. Each kind has its own fingerprint, and we will learn to take the second difference, compare a pattern with the squares, recognise a pattern built from the previous terms, and tell apart three sequences that start identically. Then we turn the tables: you will invent patterns of your own, describe the rule in words and in algebra, and challenge someone else to find it — which is exactly what the CAPS curriculum asks for.
