Today we move from finding a rule to defending it. A rule that fits the first four terms of a pattern is a guess until you can say why it must keep working, and the difference between "it works for these" and "it works because" is the difference between a conjecture and a justification. We will translate rules between plain words and algebra, justify rules from the structure of a picture — each new square costs three sticks, each table seats two more people — show that two different-looking rules are the same rule, and meet a famous pattern that fits perfectly for five terms and then breaks. The CAPS requirement is to describe and justify general rules in your own words or in algebraic language, and that is the skill Grade 10 proof will build on.
