Hey — if the patterns felt like guesswork, here is the calm version: there are two tests, and each test comes with its own recipe.

Test one: subtract neighbours. If you get the same number every time, it is a step pattern, like a taxi meter. The step goes in front of n. The flag fall is one step back from the first term. 5, 8, 11, 14 has step 3, flag fall 2, rule 3n plus 2.

Test two: divide neighbours. If you get the same number every time, it is a multiplying pattern, like a chain message. The rule is the first term times the multiplier to the power n minus 1. The minus 1 is because the first term has not been multiplied yet — four terms, three jumps.

For picture patterns, look for the part that grows with every step and the part that stays fixed. Matchstick squares grow by 3 and have 1 fixed closing stick: 3n plus 1.

Then the one habit that saves marks: test your rule on two terms before you use it. Put in n equals 1 and n equals 4. If both match, trust it and jump to the 50th term or solve for a position.

Open the practice set, run the two tests, and let the rule do the counting. You've got this!
