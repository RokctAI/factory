Hey — if "justify your rule" sounded like being asked to prove something you only just guessed, here is the calm version: it is one sentence, and the picture writes it for you.

First, say the rule in words a friend could use. "Start at 5 and go up by 3." "Double the position and add 1." If the friend could write the next term, the words are complete. Then turn them into algebra, piece by piece — the position is n, and the words tell you what to do to it.

Second, point every piece of the rule at something in the picture. Tables in a row seat 2n plus 2: the 2n is the two long sides, the plus 2 is the two ends. Matchstick squares use 3n plus 1: 3 new sticks per square, 1 to close the first. When every piece points at something, finish the sentence "this works because..." and you have your justification. "It works for the first three" is only a check.

Third, if your rule looks different from someone else's, do not panic. Put in two values of n. If they match, multiply out the brackets and show they are the same rule.

And remember the circle: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then 31. Numbers that fit are not a reason.

Open the practice set, find the picture, and finish the sentence. You've got this!
