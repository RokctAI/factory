Hey — if the word problems with powers felt like they were written in another language, here is the calm version: there are only two kinds, and each has a recipe.

Kind one is growth. The clue words are every, each and per — "doubles every hour", "triples each round", "8 percent per year". Each repeat multiplies by the same factor, so count the repeats and that number is your exponent. 500 cells doubling every hour for six hours is 500 times 2 to the power 6. Not 500 times 2 times 6 — six doublings, not one multiplication by six. To go backwards in time, divide by the power.

Kind two is big and small numbers. Write every quantity in standard form with its unit. Write the calculation as an expression. Then do the fronts on one line and the powers of ten on another — multiply the fronts, add the exponents; divide the fronts, subtract the exponents. Tidy to one digit before the comma.

Then the safety net: before you calculate, guess the exponent of the answer. Hundreds of thousands times hundreds is hundreds of millions. If your exponent is far from your guess, a power of ten slipped, and you know where to look. Keep the unit on every line.

Open the practice set, find the clue word, and pick the recipe. You've got this!
