Today we take exponents out of the exercise book and into problems: a bacterial culture that doubles every hour, an investment that grows by 8 percent a year, a cube whose side is written in scientific notation, light crossing the gap between the Sun and the Earth, and the number of red blood cells in a human body. Each problem is solved by recognising repeated multiplication and writing it as a power, or by writing enormous and tiny quantities in standard form and letting the exponent laws carry the arithmetic. We will also learn the sanity checks — estimate the size, check the units, test with a small case — that separate a confident answer from a guess. This is where the exponents section of the Term 1 assignment earns its marks, and it is the reasoning Grade 10 exponential growth will assume.
