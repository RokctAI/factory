# Part 1 — Expert

This session performs calculations with numbers in exponential form using addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, including numbers in scientific notation. The governing principle is that the exponent laws apply to multiplication and division of powers with the same base, and to nothing else. The anchor calculations are 2 cubed plus 2 to the power 4, which is 24; 2 to the power 5 times 3 squared divided by 2 cubed, which is 36; and 3 times 10 to the power 4, times 2 times 10 to the power 5, which is 6 times 10 to the power 9.

## Subtopic: Adding and Subtracting Powers

There is no exponent law for addition or subtraction. To add or subtract powers, evaluate each power and then add or subtract the values. 2^3 + 2^4 = 24, because 8 plus 16 is 24. It is NOT 2 to the power 7, which is 128, and it is NOT 4 to the power 7. The exponents are not combined in any way.

Subtraction is the same. 5^2 - 3^2 = 16, because 25 minus 9 is 16. It is not 2 squared, which is 4; 2 squared is the value of the quantity 5 minus 3, all squared, and the bracket makes it a different calculation. The examiner sets both side by side precisely to test whether you respect the bracket: 5 squared minus 3 squared is 16, while 5 minus 3, all squared, is 4.

Mixed operations follow the usual order: powers first, then multiplication, then addition and subtraction. 3 times 2 to the power 4, minus 2 to the power 5: 2 to the power 4 is 16, times 3 is 48; 2 to the power 5 is 32; 48 minus 32 is 16. 7 squared minus 2 times 3 squared: 49 minus 18 is 31.

There is one legitimate shortcut, and it is not an exponent law but the distributive property. Identical powers added together are counted: 2 to the power 5 plus 2 to the power 5 is two copies of 2 to the power 5, which is 2 times 2 to the power 5, and NOW the multiplication law applies: 2 to the power 6. Check: 2^5 + 2^5 = 2^6, since 32 plus 32 is 64. Likewise 3 to the power 4 plus 3 to the power 4 plus 3 to the power 4 is three copies, 3 times 3 to the power 4, which is 3 to the power 5, which is 243.

Common factors can also be taken out before evaluating, which keeps numbers small. 2 to the power 7 minus 2 to the power 5 is 2 to the power 5 times the quantity 2 squared minus 1, which is 32 times 3, which is 96. Check directly: 128 minus 32 is 96. This factorising will matter in Grade 10 and 11 exponential equations; for now it is a convenience.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding and subtracting by evaluating, the bracket that changes a difference of squares into a square of a difference, and the one shortcut for identical powers.

## Subtopic: Multiplying and Dividing Powers

With the same base, use the laws. 2 cubed times 2 to the power 4 is 2 to the power 7; 3 to the power 5 divided by 3 squared is 3 cubed. These were last week's work.

With different bases there are two routes. The first is to evaluate: 2 cubed times 3 squared is 8 times 9, which is 72. The second applies when the exponents are equal: the power-of-a-product law read backwards. 2 cubed times 5 cubed is the quantity 2 times 5, all cubed, which is 10 cubed, which is 1 000. Check: 8 times 125 is 1 000. Similarly 6 to the power 4 divided by 3 to the power 4 is the quantity 6 divided by 3, all to the power 4, which is 2 to the power 4, which is 16. Check: 1 296 divided by 81 is 16. Equal exponents let you combine the bases; equal bases let you combine the exponents; neither equal means evaluate.

The anchor: 2 to the power 5 times 3 squared, divided by 2 cubed. Collect the twos: 2 to the power 5 minus 3 is 2 squared, which is 4. The 3 squared is 9. The product is 36. Check with values: 32 times 9 is 288, divided by 8 is 36.

Composite bases should be factorised before laws are applied. 12 squared is the quantity 2 squared times 3, all squared, which is 2 to the power 4 times 3 squared, which is 16 times 9, which is 144. 12 squared times 3 squared divided by 6 squared: as a single power of a quotient, 12 times 3 over 6 is 6, and 6 squared is 36. Or by prime factors: twos give 2 to the power 4 over 2 squared, which is 2 squared; threes give 3 to the power 4 over 3 squared, which is 3 squared; 4 times 9 is 36.

Negative bases retain their brackets throughout. Negative 2 in brackets cubed times negative 3 in brackets squared is negative 8 times 9, which is negative 72. Negative 6 in brackets to the power 4 divided by 3 to the power 4 is negative 2 in brackets to the power 4, which is 16.

Take this section's questions now: same base, same exponent or neither, and factorising a composite base before the laws are used.

## Subtopic: Order of Operations With Powers

The order is: brackets, then exponents and roots, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. Powers are evaluated before multiplication. 2 + 3 × 2^3 = 26, because 2 cubed is 8, 3 times 8 is 24, and 2 plus 24 is 26. With a bracket the order changes: the quantity 2 plus 3, times 2 cubed, is 5 times 8, which is 40.

Powers bind tighter than a multiplier in front. 2 times 3 squared is 2 times 9, which is 18. The quantity 2 times 3, all squared, is 6 squared, which is 36. The coefficient is not squared unless the bracket says so, which is the same convention as 3x all squared versus 3x squared.

A leading minus is a multiplier of negative 1 and is applied after the power. Negative 3 squared, without brackets, is negative 9. Negative 3 in brackets, squared, is 9. So negative 3 squared plus negative 3 in brackets squared is negative 9 plus 9, which is 0.

Roots are exponents in disguise and sit at the same level. The square root of 2 to the power 4 is 2 squared, which is 4: halve the exponent. The cube root of 2 to the power 6 is 2 squared, which is 4: divide the exponent by 3. So the square root of 2 to the power 4, plus the cube root of 2 to the power 6, is 4 plus 4, which is 8. The square root of 3 to the power 4 times 5 squared is 3 squared times 5, which is 45. Halving an exponent under a square root works only when the exponent is even; the square root of 2 cubed is not an integer and stays as a surd for now.

A longer example in the full order: 2 cubed times 2 squared, plus 2 to the power 5 divided by 2 cubed. Multiplication and division first, using the laws: 2 to the power 5, which is 32, plus 2 squared, which is 4. Then add: 36. Writing 2 to the power 7 by adding every exponent in sight is the error the question is designed to catch.

This section's questions are available now: powers before multiplication, the coefficient outside the bracket, the leading minus, and roots as halved exponents.

## Subtopic: Powers of Ten in Calculations and the Error Museum

Numbers in scientific notation are products: a front number times a power of ten. To multiply two of them, multiply the fronts and multiply the powers of ten separately, then restore standard form. 3 times 10 to the power 4, times 2 times 10 to the power 5: fronts 3 times 2 is 6; powers 10 to the power 4 plus 5 is 10 to the power 9. Result: 6 times 10 to the power 9.

Division is the same with the division law. 8 times 10 to the power 6, divided by 4 times 10 squared: fronts 8 over 4 is 2; powers 10 to the power 6 minus 2 is 10 to the power 4. Result: 2 times 10 to the power 4.

Restore standard form when the front leaves the range from 1 to 10. 4 times 10 cubed, times 5 times 10 squared: fronts 20; powers 10 to the power 5. 20 times 10 to the power 5 is not standard; write 20 as 2 times 10 and the result is 2 times 10 to the power 6. Check: 4 000 times 500 is 2 000 000.

Addition and subtraction have no law, so the powers of ten must be made equal first — the exponent is a place value, and you can only add digits in the same place. 3 times 10 to the power 4, plus 2 times 10 to the power 4, have equal powers: add the fronts, 5 times 10 to the power 4. But 3 times 10 to the power 5, plus 2 times 10 to the power 4, do not. Rewrite the first as 30 times 10 to the power 4; now add fronts: 32 times 10 to the power 4, which in standard form is 3,2 times 10 to the power 5. Check in full: 300 000 plus 20 000 is 320 000. The wrong answer 5 times 10 to the power 9 — adding fronts and adding exponents — is off by a factor of about ten thousand.

The error museum for this topic has five exhibits. One: 2 cubed plus 2 to the power 4 written as 2 to the power 7 — a law applied to a sum. Two: 5 squared minus 3 squared written as 2 squared — the bracket invented. Three: 2 cubed times 5 cubed written as 10 to the power 6 — both bases and exponents combined when only the bases should be. Four: 3 times 10 to the power 5 plus 2 times 10 to the power 4 written as 5 times 10 to the power 9 — exponents added under addition. Five: 2 times 3 squared written as 36 — the coefficient squared without a bracket.

Layout for marks: state which operation you are performing, show the law or the evaluation on its own line, and for scientific notation show the fronts and the powers of ten on separate lines before restoring standard form.

The questions for this section are ready: multiplying and dividing in scientific notation, equalising the powers before adding, and the five exhibits of the museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same calculations again with bags of marbles, matching labels and a shelf of place values — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: Adding Is Not a Law

Think of 2 cubed as a bag with 8 marbles and 2 to the power 4 as a bag with 16. "Add them" means tip both bags onto the table and count: 24 marbles. There is no clever shortcut, because the labels on the bags — cubed, to the fourth — describe how the marbles were produced, not how many you have in total. You must look inside. 8 plus 16 is 24. It is not 2 to the power 7, which would be 128 marbles that nobody owns.

Subtracting is the same. 5 squared is 25 marbles; 3 squared is 9. Take 9 from 25 and 16 remain. Do not be tempted to subtract the labels and write 2 squared, which is 4 — that answer belongs to a different question, where you first find 5 minus 3 and square the result. Brackets tell you which question is being asked.

When the bags are identical there is a shortcut, but it is counting, not an exponent trick. 2 to the power 5 plus 2 to the power 5 is two identical bags: 2 times 2 to the power 5. Now you ARE multiplying, so the law applies and you get 2 to the power 6. Check: 32 plus 32 is 64, and 2 to the power 6 is 64. Three bags of 3 to the power 4 is 3 times 3 to the power 4, which is 3 to the power 5. Only identical bags can be counted like this.

Mixed sums follow the kitchen order: work out every power first, then multiply, then add or subtract. 3 times 2 to the power 4, minus 2 to the power 5: 16 times 3 is 48; 32; 48 minus 32 is 16. If you ever feel the urge to add exponents when there is a plus sign in sight, tip out the bags and count instead.

Keep the marble bags in mind and try the questions for this part: tipping out and counting, the bracket that asks a different question, and identical bags.

## Subtopic: Same Base Shortcut, Different Base Calculate

Multiplying and dividing is where the shortcuts live, but only when something matches. Same base? Use the laws you know: add exponents to multiply, subtract to divide. 2 to the power 5 over 2 cubed is 2 squared. Nothing matching? Just work it out: 2 cubed times 3 squared is 8 times 9, which is 72, and that is final.

There is a third case that catches people out: same EXPONENT, different bases. 2 cubed times 5 cubed. Picture three twos and three fives. Pair each two with a five and you have three tens: 10 cubed, which is 1 000. The exponent stayed 3 — you did not add it to itself — and the bases multiplied. Check: 8 times 125 is 1 000. Dividing works the same way: 6 to the power 4 over 3 to the power 4 is four sixes over four threes, each six over three is a two, so 2 to the power 4, which is 16.

So there are three cases. Same base: combine the exponents, keep the base. Same exponent: combine the bases, keep the exponent. Neither: evaluate and multiply. Never combine both at once — 2 cubed times 5 cubed is 10 cubed, not 10 to the power 6, which would be a million marbles instead of a thousand.

A number like 12 is hiding smaller bases. 12 is 2 times 2 times 3, so 12 squared is 2 to the power 4 times 3 squared, which is 16 times 9, which is 144. Breaking a base into primes lets you match twos with twos and threes with threes across a whole calculation, and that is exactly how the HCF and LCM work from two weeks ago connects to this one.

Hold the three cases and try this part's questions: which thing matches, and what you are allowed to combine when it does.

## Subtopic: BODMAS With Powers and Big Numbers

Order of operations with powers is the kitchen rule: brackets first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then adding and subtracting. 2 plus 3 times 2 cubed: cube first, 8; multiply, 24; add, 26. If a bracket wraps the 2 plus 3, the bracket goes first: 5 times 8 is 40. Same numbers, different question, different answer.

A number in front of a power is not part of the power. 2 times 3 squared is 2 times 9, which is 18. Only a bracket around the 2 times 3 would make it 6 squared, which is 36. And a minus in front behaves like a number in front: negative 3 squared, no bracket, is negative 9, because the squaring happens first and the minus is put on at the end.

Roots are powers running backwards, and they sit on the same step as powers. The square root of 2 to the power 4 is 2 squared, which is 4 — halve the exponent. The cube root of 2 to the power 6 is 2 squared, which is 4 — divide the exponent by 3. Add them: 8.

Now the big numbers. A number in scientific notation is a front times a power of ten — like a price tag and a shelf. To multiply two of them, multiply the fronts and add the shelf numbers: 3 times 10 to the power 4, times 2 times 10 to the power 5, is 6 times 10 to the power 9. To divide, divide the fronts and subtract the shelves: 8 times 10 to the power 6 over 4 times 10 squared is 2 times 10 to the power 4. If the front wanders out of the 1-to-10 range, slide the comma and fix the shelf: 20 times 10 to the power 5 becomes 2 times 10 to the power 6.

To add or subtract, the shelves must match first, because you can only add things on the same shelf. 3 times 10 to the power 5 plus 2 times 10 to the power 4: move the first onto shelf 4 by writing it as 30 times 10 to the power 4, add the fronts to get 32, and tidy to 3,2 times 10 to the power 5. Check the honest way: 300 000 plus 20 000 is 320 000. Adding the shelf numbers to get 10 to the power 9 would be a wildly wrong answer, and the honest check exposes it at once.

Pocket summary. Adding or subtracting powers: tip out the bags and count, unless the bags are identical. Multiplying or dividing: same base, combine exponents; same exponent, combine bases; neither, evaluate. Powers before multiplying; a minus or a coefficient in front waits. Big numbers: fronts and shelves separately, and match the shelves before you add.

Hold the bags, the matching rule and the shelf, and take the final questions of the lesson: the order of operations with powers and roots, and arithmetic in scientific notation.
