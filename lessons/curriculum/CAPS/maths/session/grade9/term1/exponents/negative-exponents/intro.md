Today we extend the laws of exponents to integer exponents — which means making sense of a negative exponent. Last lesson the division law said to subtract exponents; apply it to 2 cubed divided by 2 to the power 5 and you get 2 to the power negative 2, a symbol with no meaning yet. Work the same division with fractions and you get one quarter. For the laws to stay consistent, 2 to the power negative 2 must be one over 2 squared, and that is the new law: a to the power negative m equals 1 over a to the power m. We will derive it, evaluate it with positive, negative and fractional bases, use it to simplify expressions and explain the small numbers of scientific notation, and learn the one picture — flip to the other side of the fraction bar — that makes it obvious.
