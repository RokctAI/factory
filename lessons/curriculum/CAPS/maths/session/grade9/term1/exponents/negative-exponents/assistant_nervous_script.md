Hey — if the minus signs in the exponents made your stomach drop, here is the calm version: a negative exponent is not scary, it is just a passenger standing on the wrong side of a fraction bar.

Start with the pattern you already trust. 8, 4, 2, 1 — each one half the last. Keep halving: a half, a quarter, an eighth. That is 2 to the power negative 1, negative 2, negative 3. Nothing went negative. The numbers only got small.

So the rule in one line: a negative exponent means "one over the positive power". 2 to the power negative 3 is one eighth. Small, not negative.

In a long expression, do the ordinary laws first — add exponents when multiplying, subtract when dividing — and leave the minus signs alone until the last line. Then hop every factor that still has a negative exponent to the other side of the fraction bar and make its exponent positive. Only the ones with a minus hop; a plain coefficient stays where it is.

Not sure about an answer? Put x equal to 1 into the original and into your answer. If they match, you are right.

Open the practice set and let the pattern do the work. You've got this!
