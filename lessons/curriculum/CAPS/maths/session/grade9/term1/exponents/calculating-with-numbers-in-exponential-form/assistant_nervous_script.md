Hey — if the calculations with powers felt like a minefield of rules, here is the calm version: there is really one question to ask before every step. Is there a plus or minus sign, or a times or divide sign?

Plus or minus: no law. Work out each power and count. 2 cubed plus 2 to the fourth is 8 plus 16, which is 24. Tip out the bags and count the marbles. The only exception is identical bags, which you can count as "two of" or "three of" and then multiply.

Times or divide: look for a match. Same base, combine the exponents. Same exponent, combine the bases and keep the exponent. Nothing matches, just work it out. Never combine both at once.

Then the kitchen order: brackets, then powers and roots, then times and divide, then plus and minus. A number or a minus sign in front of a power waits its turn.

Big numbers in scientific notation: handle the fronts and the powers of ten separately when multiplying or dividing. When adding, make the powers of ten match first, like putting things on the same shelf, and slide the comma at the end if the front is out of range.

Open the practice set and ask the one question before each step. You've got this!
