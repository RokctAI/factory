Hey — if the powers and the big-and-small numbers felt like a lot of rules, here is the calm version: there are really only two ideas, and you already know both.

Idea one: an exponent counts how many times you multiply. 2 to the power 5 is five twos multiplied together, 32, not 2 times 5. If the base is negative and sits inside brackets, an even exponent makes the answer positive and an odd one keeps it negative. If the minus is outside the bracket, work out the power first and put the minus on at the end.

Idea two: scientific notation is a comma taking a walk. Put the comma after the first non-zero digit, count the jumps, and the count becomes the exponent on the 10. Big number, comma walked left, positive exponent. Tiny number, comma walked right, negative exponent. And a negative exponent just means small — never negative.

Check yourself with one rule: exactly one digit in front of the comma. If there are two, let the comma step once more and move the exponent by one the other way.

Open the practice questions, count the jumps, and trust the pattern. You've got this!
