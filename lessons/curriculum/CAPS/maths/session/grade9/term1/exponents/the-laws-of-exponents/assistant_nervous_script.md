Hey — if the five laws blurred into one fog of little numbers, here is the calm version: every law is just counting, and you have been counting since Grade 1.

Multiplying powers of the same base? You have more copies of the same thing — add the counts. 2 cubed times 2 to the fourth is seven twos. The twos do not turn into fours; nobody put fours in the box.

Dividing? Pair off the copies top and bottom and cancel the pairs — subtract the counts. Five threes over two threes leaves three threes.

A power wrapped in a bracket with an exponent outside? Boxes of boxes — multiply the counts. Two boxes of three twos is six twos.

A bracket with a product inside? Share the exponent with everyone in there, including the number in front. 3x all squared is 9x squared, because the 3 gets squared too.

And anything non-zero to the power zero is one, because 16, 8, 4, 2 halves once more to 1 — it never crashes to nothing.

When you are stuck, write the twos out. It is slower, but it is never wrong, and after two or three times the law will feel obvious. Open the practice set and count. You've got this!
