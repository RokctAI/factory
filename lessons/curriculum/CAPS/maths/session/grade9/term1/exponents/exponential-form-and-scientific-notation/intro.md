Today we revise exponential form — the shorthand that writes 2 times 2 times 2 times 2 times 2 as 2 to the power 5 — and then use powers of ten to write very large and very small numbers in scientific notation. The distance from the Earth to the Sun, about 149 600 000 kilometres, becomes 1,496 times 10 to the power 8; the width of a red blood cell, 0,0000075 metres, becomes 7,5 times 10 to the power negative 6. This is the first time negative exponents appear in this grade. We will fix the vocabulary of base and exponent, settle the sign traps around negative bases, learn the one rule that makes scientific notation standard, and compare and round numbers in this form — the language physical science and Grade 10 will assume you speak.
