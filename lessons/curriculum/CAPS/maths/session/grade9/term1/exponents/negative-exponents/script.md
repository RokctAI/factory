# Part 1 — Expert

This session extends the laws of exponents to integer exponents by establishing and using the law a to the power negative m equals 1 over a to the power m, for any non-zero base a. The anchor calculations are 2 cubed divided by 2 to the power 5, which is 2 to the power negative 2, which is one quarter; and 3 to the power negative 2, which is one ninth. The law is derived, not assumed, and the derivation is examinable.

## Subtopic: Extending the Division Law

Last lesson the division law, a to the m divided by a to the n equals a to the m minus n, was applied only when m was larger than n. Remove that restriction. 2 cubed divided by 2 to the power 5: the law gives 2 to the power 3 minus 5, which is 2 to the power negative 2. The symbol is new; its value is not yet known.

Now compute the same quotient by cancelling factors. Three twos on top, five twos below; three pairs cancel, leaving 1 on top and two twos below: 1 over 2 squared, which is one quarter. Numerically, 2^3 ÷ 2^5 = 1/4, since 8 over 32 reduces to one quarter.

Two correct calculations of one quantity must agree, so 2 to the power negative 2 must equal 1 over 2 squared. This is forced, exactly as a to the power 0 equals 1 was forced last lesson. The general law: a to the power negative m equals 1 over a to the power m, for a not equal to 0. A negative exponent means a reciprocal: the power moves below the fraction bar and the exponent becomes positive.

The pattern of powers confirms it. 2 cubed is 8, 2 squared is 4, 2 to the power 1 is 2, 2 to the power 0 is 1; each step divides by 2. Continue: 2 to the power negative 1 is a half, 2 to the power negative 2 is a quarter, 2 to the power negative 3 is an eighth. The values shrink towards zero but never reach it and never become negative.

That last sentence is the heart of the topic. A negative exponent does NOT produce a negative number. 2 to the power negative 3 is one eighth, a small positive number. Writing negative 8, or negative one eighth, for 2 to the power negative 3 is the single most frequent error in this section, and it is marked wrong every time. The sign of the exponent controls which side of the fraction bar the power sits on; the sign of the answer is controlled by the base alone.

Base ten is the case that explains scientific notation. 10 to the power negative 3 is 1 over 10 cubed, which is 1 over 1 000, which is 0,001. That is why 5,2 times 10 to the power negative 4 means 5,2 divided by 10 000, which is 0,00052.

The questions for this section are with you now: the derivation from the division law, the pattern of powers, and the sign that does not change.

## Subtopic: Evaluating Negative Exponents

Evaluate in two steps: first apply the law to produce a reciprocal, then evaluate the positive power. 3 to the power negative 2 is 1 over 3 squared, which is 1 over 9. 5 to the power negative 1 is 1 over 5. 6 to the power negative 2 is 1 over 36, and 4 to the power negative 2 is 1 over 16.

A negative base keeps its own sign rules. Negative 2 in brackets to the power negative 3 is 1 over negative 2 in brackets cubed, which is 1 over negative 8, which is negative one eighth. The negative answer here comes from the odd power of a negative base, not from the negative exponent. Negative 2 in brackets to the power negative 2 is 1 over 4, positive, because the power is even.

A fractional base flips. One half to the power negative 3 is 1 over one half cubed, which is 1 over one eighth, which is 8. More directly: a negative exponent on a fraction inverts the fraction and makes the exponent positive. Two thirds to the power negative 2 is three halves squared, which is nine quarters. Three quarters to the power negative 1 is four thirds.

Now the coefficient trap, which has the same shape as last lesson. 5 times 2 to the power negative 3: the exponent applies to the 2 only, so this is 5 times one eighth, which is five eighths. But 5 times 2, all to the power negative 3, is 10 to the power negative 3, which is one thousandth. Read the bracket before you touch the exponent.

Combining with the other laws, which all still hold for integer exponents: 5 to the power negative 2 times 5 to the power 4 is 5 to the power 2, which is 25. 3 to the power negative 1, all squared, is 3 to the power negative 2, which is one ninth. 2 to the power negative 1 plus 2 to the power negative 2 is a half plus a quarter, which is three quarters — and note that addition has no exponent law; the terms are evaluated separately and added.

## Subtopic: Negative Exponents in Simplifying

In algebra the law reads x to the power negative 3 equals 1 over x cubed. The convention for "simplest form" in this course is positive exponents only, so every negative exponent in an answer must be rewritten as a reciprocal.

The law also runs upward. 1 over x to the power negative 2 equals x squared: a power below the fraction bar with a negative exponent moves above with a positive one. The general rule is that any factor may cross the fraction bar provided the sign of its exponent is changed. Crossing the bar and flipping the sign are the same action.

Worked examples. 2 cubed times 2 to the power negative 5: add the exponents, 2 to the power negative 2, which is one quarter. x squared divided by x to the power 6: subtract, x to the power negative 4, which is 1 over x to the power 4. 2x, all to the power negative 2: share the exponent, 2 to the power negative 2 times x to the power negative 2, which is 1 over 4x squared.

The coefficient trap in algebraic form: 3x to the power negative 2, with the exponent on the x only, is 3 over x squared. The 3 is not raised to anything and stays in the numerator. Writing 1 over 3x squared is wrong — it has moved a factor across the bar that never had a negative exponent.

Mixed expressions: 4a to the power negative 3 b squared over 2a b to the power negative 1. Coefficients: 4 over 2 is 2. The a's: negative 3 minus 1 is negative 4, so a to the power negative 4, which goes below the bar as a to the power 4. The b's: 2 minus negative 1 is 3, so b cubed stays above. Result: 2b cubed over a to the power 4. Each base is handled independently, and only the ones with negative exponents move.

Layout for marks: apply the laws with the exponents as they are, negative or positive, in the first lines; convert to positive exponents in the final line only. Converting too early produces fractions within fractions and loses marks for untidy work.

This section's questions are available now: moving factors across the fraction bar, the coefficient that stays on top, and the final conversion to positive exponents.

## Subtopic: Scientific Notation Revisited and the Error Museum

Two lessons ago, small numbers in scientific notation were justified by a pattern. Now the law justifies them. 7,5 times 10 to the power negative 6 is 7,5 times 1 over 10 to the power 6, which is 7,5 divided by 1 000 000, which is 0,0000075. The negative exponent instructs a division by a power of ten, and the number of places the comma moves is the size of the exponent.

Converting the other way is the same law read backwards. 0,00052 is 5,2 divided by 10 000, which is 5,2 times 10 to the power negative 4. Any number between 0 and 1 in scientific notation must carry a negative exponent, and any number of 10 or more must carry a positive one; a number between 1 and 10 has exponent 0, because 10 to the power 0 is 1.

Powers of two give a useful estimate for the sciences and for computing. 2 to the power 10 is 1 024, so 2 to the power negative 10 is 1 over 1 024, which is about 0,001 — a thousandth. Units are written with negative exponents too: metres per second appears as m s to the power negative 1, because "per second" is "divided by seconds", which is "times seconds to the power negative 1".

The error museum for this topic has five exhibits. One: 2 to the power negative 3 written as negative 8 — the sign of the exponent taken as the sign of the answer. Two: 2 to the power negative 3 written as negative one eighth — the reciprocal taken but a stray minus kept. Three: 3x to the power negative 2 written as 1 over 3x squared — the coefficient dragged across the bar. Four: 1 over 2 to the power negative 3 written as one eighth, when it is 8 — the factor already below the bar must come up, not stay down. Five: 2 to the power negative 1 plus 2 to the power negative 2 written as 2 to the power negative 3 — an exponent law applied to a sum.

Every exhibit is caught by one check: substitute a small number and evaluate both the original and the answer. If 2x to the power negative 2 is claimed to be 1 over 2x squared, test x equals 1: the original is 2 to the power negative 2, which is a quarter; the claim gives a half. The claim is wrong. The check costs a few seconds and is available in every examination.

The questions for this section are ready: the law behind small numbers in scientific notation, the units convention, and the five exhibits of the museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same law again from a pattern that will not stop, a passenger on the wrong side of a bar, and the places where negative exponents turn up in real life — plain words, same rule.

## Subtopic: The Pattern Keeps Going Down

Start with what you know and refuse to stop. 2 to the power 3 is 8. 2 to the power 2 is 4. 2 to the power 1 is 2. Each time the little number drops by one, the answer halves. 2 to the power 0: halve again, 1. Now, why stop there? Drop the little number to negative 1 and halve again: a half. Negative 2: a quarter. Negative 3: an eighth. Nothing dramatic happened when the exponent crossed zero. The numbers just kept halving, getting smaller and smaller, always positive.

That is the whole law. 2 to the power negative 3 is one eighth — which is 1 over 2 cubed. A negative exponent means "one over the positive power". Try it with 10: a thousand, a hundred, ten, one, a tenth, a hundredth, a thousandth. 10 to the power negative 3 is 0,001. The pattern never crashes to zero and never goes negative; it only shrinks.

So here is the one sentence to tattoo on your brain: a negative exponent makes a number SMALL, not NEGATIVE. 2 to the power negative 3 is one eighth of a pizza. It is not minus eight pizzas, and it is not minus an eighth. If you ever write a minus sign in your answer because the exponent had one, stop and halve 8 three times instead.

The sign of the answer belongs to the base. Negative 2 in brackets to the power negative 3 is 1 over negative 2 cubed, and negative 2 cubed is negative 8 because three negatives multiply to a negative. So the answer is negative one eighth — but the minus came from the base, not from the exponent. Negative 2 in brackets to the power negative 2 would be 1 over positive 4, because two negatives make a positive.

Keep the halving in your head and try the questions for this part: where the pattern goes below zero in the exponent, and why it never goes below zero in the answer.

## Subtopic: Flip It Over

Picture a fraction bar as a fence with two sides: the top and the bottom. Every power is a passenger standing on one side. A negative exponent is a passenger standing on the WRONG side, and they fix it by hopping over the fence — and when they hop, their exponent's sign flips to positive. 2 to the power negative 3 is standing on top with a negative sign. Hop it over the fence: 1 over 2 cubed. Positive exponent, happy passenger, one eighth.

The hop works both ways. 1 over 2 to the power negative 3 has the passenger standing on the bottom with a negative sign. Hop it up: 2 cubed on top, which is 8. Not one eighth — the passenger was already below, so it comes UP. Anybody with a negative exponent hops; anybody with a positive exponent stays exactly where they are.

That is why 3x to the power negative 2, with the exponent on the x only, is 3 over x squared and not 1 over 3x squared. The x has the negative exponent, so the x hops down. The 3 has no exponent at all — it is a plain positive passenger — so it stays on top. Only the one with the minus hops.

Fractions as bases are the same picture. Two thirds to the power negative 2: the whole fraction is the passenger, and hopping a fraction over the fence turns it upside down. So it becomes three halves squared, which is nine quarters. One half to the power negative 3 flips to 2 cubed, which is 8. A small fraction with a negative exponent turns into a big number, which makes sense: you are dividing by something small.

In a long expression, do the ordinary exponent laws first — add when multiplying, subtract when dividing — and let the negative exponents sit there until the last line. Then hop everyone who needs to hop. 4a to the power negative 3 b squared over 2a b to the power negative 1: coefficients 2; a's give negative 4; b's give 3. Last line: the a hops down. 2b cubed over a to the power 4. Done.

Hold the picture of the fence and try this part's questions: who hops, who stays, and what happens to a fraction when it hops.

## Subtopic: Where Negative Exponents Show Up

You have already been using negative exponents without the name. 0,00052 in scientific notation is 5,2 times 10 to the power negative 4. The negative 4 is a passenger on the wrong side of the fence: hop it and you get 5,2 divided by 10 to the power 4, which is 5,2 divided by 10 000, which is 0,00052. The small-number half of scientific notation is just this law wearing a lab coat.

Science writes units with them. Metres per second is written m s to the power negative 1 in a physics textbook, because "per second" means "divided by a second", and dividing by s is multiplying by s to the power negative 1. When you see a negative exponent on a unit, read it as "per".

Computers use powers of 2, and the negative ones are tiny fractions. 2 to the power 10 is 1 024, so 2 to the power negative 10 is 1 over 1 024, roughly a thousandth. Halve, halve, halve ten times and a whole becomes about one thousandth. That is also why a phone screen brightness or a volume slider, if it halves each step, is almost off after ten steps.

The quick sanity check for any answer in this topic: plug in a small number. If someone claims 2x all to the power negative 2 is 1 over 2x squared, try x equals 1. The original is 2 to the power negative 2, a quarter. The claim gives a half. Not equal, so the claim is wrong; the right answer is 1 over 4x squared, because the 2 inside the bracket gets the exponent too, and 2 squared is 4.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Negative exponent means small, not negative. It means hop to the other side of the fraction bar and make the exponent positive. Only the factor carrying the minus hops; coefficients and positive powers stay put. A fraction that hops turns upside down. And the small numbers of scientific notation are this one law at work.

Hold the fence, the halving pattern and the plug-in check, and take the final questions of the lesson: scientific notation through the law, units read as "per", and the check that catches every wrong hop.
