Today we read graphs critically and compare two sets of data on the same issue. We check a graph's title, axis labels, units, scale and key before trusting it, read values and trends from the electricity graph, and expose the tricks of a truncated axis, uneven spacing, three-dimensional pies and cherry-picked periods. Two classes of ten wrote the same test: we find that their means of 38.0 and 38.6 and medians of 39 and 38.5 are nearly equal while their ranges of 24 and 19 are not, so the classes have similar centres and one is more consistent. We compare two cities' rainfall over six months with two broken lines that cross between April and May, draw a back-to-back stem-and-leaf display, compare schools of different sizes by percentage rather than count, and write a comparison in four moves with a conclusion no larger than the data allows.
