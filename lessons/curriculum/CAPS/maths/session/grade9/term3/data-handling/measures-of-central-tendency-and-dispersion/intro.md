Today we organise and summarise numerical data. We order a jumbled list of tuck shop spending, build a frequency table of shoe sizes with a check that the counts add to 25, draw a stem-and-leaf display of test marks, and sort sixty learners by gender and transport in a two-way table. We calculate the three measures of central tendency, finding a mean of 17.25 rands, a median of 12 and a mode of 12 for the spending data, and the mean of 5.84 from the frequency table. We measure dispersion with the range and the extremes, and we watch what an outlier of 60 rands does: it pulls the mean up and stretches the range while the median and mode stand still. Finally we choose the measure that fits the data, using a firm where eight workers earn R6 000 and the manager R40 000 to show why the median is the honest average.
