Hey — if writing a whole report felt like too much to hold in your head today, here is the calm version: story in order, claim-number-caution, the forecast rule.

Story in order. Eight short chapters: the question; how you got the data; the table and the right graph; the middles and spreads you picked and why; what you found; what you predict; what could have gone wrong; and your answer. Two or three sentences each; skipping one leaves a hole.

Claim-number-caution. Every finding is a sentence with its number and a caution the right size. Walking is the most common mode: 33 of 60, which is 55 percent; a Grade 9 sample, so other grades may differ. Girls use taxis somewhat more: 40 percent against 30; three learners in thirty, so the gap could shrink. For spending, give the median of 12 and say why not the mean: the 60 is an outlier that pulls the mean to 17.25. Say what you did with the outlier; never quietly drop it or quietly include it.

The forecast rule. Predictions carry the word about: about 55 percent of 300, so about 165 walkers. Trend forecasts say their basis and their doubt: up 60 a month means July about 540, but winter peaks. Then list what could have gone wrong, each with the direction it pushes, and what you did right.

Finish with an answer no bigger than the evidence. Story in order, claim-number-caution, the forecast rule. You've got this!
