Today we analyse where data goes wrong. We separate random error, the wobble of chance that averages out and shrinks as a sample grows, from systematic bias, a lean built into the method that no sample size can cure, and we learn that a small fair sample beats a large biased one. We trace bias to who was asked, who answered and how the question was put, comparing a radio SMS poll of 4 000 self-selected listeners reporting 78 percent with a stratified survey of 1 000 adults reporting 41 percent, and a tuck shop owner's counter survey with an anonymous questionnaire. We find the errors that spoil good data afterwards: a 350 typed as 35 that drags a mean down by 31.5 litres, a mean reported where the median was needed, a crooked graph, and a cause claimed from a scatter plot. Finally we audit a report with a fixed list of questions, naming each problem, its direction and its fix.
