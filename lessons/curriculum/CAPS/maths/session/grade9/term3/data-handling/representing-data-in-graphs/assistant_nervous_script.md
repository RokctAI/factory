Hey — if six kinds of graph felt like six separate lessons today, here is the calm version: match the graph to the data, then dress it properly.

Words, like walk, taxi, car: build towers, a bar graph, with gaps between them. Two groups to compare: two towers per word and a key, a double bar graph. Shares of a whole: a pie, but work out the angles first. Each angle is the count over the total times 360, and the angles must add to 360. Walk 33 of 60 gives 198 degrees.

Numbers in bands, like distances to school: a histogram, and the bars touch because the bands run into each other on a number line. If you choose the bands yourself, use five to eight of equal width with round edges, written as 0 to under 1, 1 to under 2, so nothing fits two bands, and check the tallies add to the total.

One thing over time, like monthly electricity: a broken-line graph, dots joined by straight pieces, side scale from zero.

Two numbers per person, like hours studied and marks: a scatter plot, a cloud of dots you do not join. Sloping up means they rise together, but together is not because.

Then dress every graph: title, both axes labelled with units, even scale from zero, key if needed, and one sentence saying what it shows.

Match it, draw it, dress it. You've got this!
