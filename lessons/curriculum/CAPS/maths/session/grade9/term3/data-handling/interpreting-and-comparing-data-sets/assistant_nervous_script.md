Hey — if comparing two sets of data felt like it needed a whole essay, here is the calm version: read the whole picture, same measures for both, middle and spread.

Read the whole picture. Before you believe a graph, check its title, both axis labels with units, where the side scale starts, and the key. A side scale that starts at 300 instead of 0 makes a 50 percent rise look like a rocket. Then read values with their units and trends with their direction.

Same measures for both. Work out the mean, median, lowest, highest and range for each set, side by side in a little table. Class A: mean 38.0, median 39, range 24. Class B: mean 38.6, median 38.5, range 19. If the groups are different sizes, turn counts into percentages before comparing: 240 of 800 is 30 percent, 150 of 400 is 37.5 percent.

Middle and spread. Compare the middles with the actual numbers, then the spreads with the actual numbers, including the lowest and highest. Then one conclusion no bigger than the data: similar middles, B steadier, ten learners too few to judge the teaching.

For two things over time, draw two lines on one graph with one scale and a key, and look for where they cross; that is usually the story.
