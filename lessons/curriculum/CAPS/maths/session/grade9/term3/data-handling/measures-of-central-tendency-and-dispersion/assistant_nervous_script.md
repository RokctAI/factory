Hey — if mean, median, mode and range all blurred into one word today, here is the calm version: line them up, three middles, watch the giant.

Line them up. Before anything, write the numbers from smallest to biggest. If there are many repeats, count them in a frequency table and check the counts add to the total. Sorting by two things at once needs a two-way table.

Three middles. The mean shares it all out: add up, divide by how many. For the tuck shop data that is 207 divided by 12, which is 17.25. The median is the person in the middle of the line; with twelve values take the sixth and seventh, 12 and 12, so the median is 12. The mode is the most common value, 12 again. From a frequency table, multiply each value by its count before adding.

Watch the giant. The range is biggest minus smallest, 55 here, and the extremes are the 5 and the 60. The 60 is an outlier, a giant at the end of the line. Send it away and the mean drops to 13.36 and the range to 20, but the median and mode stay at 12. Giants pull the mean, not the median.

So choose: mean for fair, bunched data; median when there are giants; mode for words or the most common size. Say which one and why.
