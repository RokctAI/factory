Today we begin the data cycle where it really starts, with a question and a plan. We learn to pose statistical questions on social, economic and environmental issues that name a variable, a group and a period, and to recognise whether the data they produce is categorical, discrete or continuous. We distinguish a population from a sample, compare a census such as Census 2022 with sampling, and see how a convenience sample of whoever is nearest biases a result while random, systematic and stratified samples of 60 from a school of 1200 represent it fairly, scaling 42 of 60 up to an estimate of about 840. We choose and justify sources and methods of collection, questionnaires, observation, measurement and the published statistics of Statistics South Africa, design neutral questions with non-overlapping options, and write the plan for an investigation into how Grade 9 learners travel to school.
