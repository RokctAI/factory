Hey — if the words population, sample, stratified and secondary all ran together today, here is the calm version: a countable question, a stirred spoonful, and an honest source.

A countable question. Say what you are measuring, who you are measuring, and when. Hours without power, our street, last week. Then decide the kind of answer: names of groups like walk or taxi are categorical; numbers are numerical, discrete if you count them in whole steps and continuous if you measure them on a scale. Fix the unit before you ask.

A stirred spoonful. The whole group is the population, the pot. The part you ask is the sample, the spoonful. Asking whoever is nearest is a convenience sample and it leans their way. Stir instead: random, names from a hat; systematic, every 20th name, so 1200 learners give 60; stratified, 15 from each of four grades. Then scale up with the word about: 42 of 60 is 70 percent, so about 840 of 1200.

An honest source. Collect it yourself, primary, with a questionnaire, a tally sheet or a meter reading; or borrow it, secondary, from Statistics South Africa or the municipality, and write down who, when and what they counted. Keep questionnaire items neutral and make the ranges not overlap.

Then write the plan: question, population, sample and method, instrument, units, and the bias you are watching for. Countable question, stirred spoonful, honest source. You've got this!
