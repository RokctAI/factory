Today we collect the perimeter and area formulas for every shape Grade 9 meets and use them with correct units. We revise the rectangle, square, triangle, parallelogram, trapezium, rhombus and kite, insisting on the perpendicular height, and find that a trapezium with parallel sides 6 and 10 and height 4 has area 32 square centimetres. We use the circle's circumference and area, finding 44.0 centimetres and 153.9 square centimetres for radius 7, work with semicircles and rings, and work backwards from the area of a pond to its radius. We convert between square millimetres, square centimetres, square metres, hectares and square kilometres with the factor squared, tile a floor with 150 tiles, cost a carpet in rands, and split composite shapes into pieces, discovering that cutting a corner from a rectangle leaves its perimeter unchanged.
