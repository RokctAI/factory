Hey — if the doubling results felt back to front today, here is the calm version: one way, both ways, and k squared.

One way. Stretch a rectangle in one direction only and the carpet doubles, because you now have two of the old rectangle side by side. The fence does not double; it goes up by twice the side you stretched. A 6 by 4 stretched to 12 by 4: carpet 24 to 48, fence 20 to 32.

Both ways. Double every side and the fence doubles while the carpet becomes four times bigger. The 6 by 4 becomes 12 by 8: fence 20 to 40, carpet 24 to 96. Draw it and you will see four copies of the small rectangle inside the big one. Fence is one-dimensional, so doubling hits it once; carpet is two-dimensional, so doubling hits it twice.

k squared. For any scale factor k, lengths and perimeter are multiplied by k and area by k squared. Triple means fence times 3, carpet times 9. Halve means fence times a half, carpet times a quarter. Circles too: radius 3 to 6 doubles the circumference and quadruples the area.

Backwards, take the square root: carpet 9 times bigger means k is 3. The pizza remembers it for you: a 30 centimetre pizza is four 15 centimetre pizzas.

One way, both ways, k squared. You've got this!
