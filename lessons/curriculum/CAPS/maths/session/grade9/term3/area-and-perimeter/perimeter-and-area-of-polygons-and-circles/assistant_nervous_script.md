Hey — if the pile of formulas and units felt heavy today, here is the calm version: fence or carpet, radius first, jump squared.

Fence or carpet. Perimeter is the fence around the shape: add the sides, answer in centimetres or metres. Area is the carpet that covers it: use the formula, answer in square units with the little 2. Rectangle: length times breadth. Triangle: half of base times height. Parallelogram: base times height. Trapezium: average of the two parallel sides, times height. Kite or rhombus: half of one diagonal times the other. The height is always the straight-up distance, never the sloping side.

Radius first. A circle's fence is 2 pi r and its carpet is pi r squared. If you are given the diameter, halve it before anything else; squaring the diameter gives four times too much. A semicircle has half the carpet, and its fence is the curved arc plus the straight diameter.

Jump squared. Lengths change by one factor, 1 metre is 100 centimetres. Areas change by the factor squared, 1 square metre is 10 000 square centimetres, and a hectare is 10 000 square metres. Convert everything to one unit before you touch a formula.

Composite shapes: split them, label each piece, add or take away, and never count an inside join as fence.

Fence or carpet, radius first, jump squared. You've got this!
