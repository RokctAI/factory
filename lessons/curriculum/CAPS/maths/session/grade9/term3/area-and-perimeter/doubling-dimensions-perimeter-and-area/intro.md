Today we investigate what doubling does to perimeter and area. Starting from a 6 by 4 rectangle with perimeter 20 and area 24, we double one dimension and find the area doubles to 48 while the perimeter rises by twice the stretched side, to 32 or 28 depending on which side we stretched. Then we double both dimensions to 12 by 8 and find the perimeter exactly doubles to 40 while the area becomes four times as large, 96, because the new rectangle holds four copies of the old one. We confirm the same pattern for squares, triangles and a circle whose radius goes from 3 to 6, generalise to any scale factor k, with perimeter multiplied by k and area by k squared, run the rule backwards with a square root, and apply it to a fenced farm plot, an enlarged photograph, a map scale and the pizza that is four pizzas.
