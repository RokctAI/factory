Today we classify the four-sided shapes. We define the six special quadrilaterals of Grade 9, the parallelogram, rectangle, square, rhombus, trapezium and kite, each by the minimum condition that makes it what it is, and we read the arrowheads, dashes and right-angle marks that declare a shape on a diagram. We use the interior angle sum of 360 degrees, finding 75 from 90, 85 and 110, and the parallelogram's equal opposite angles and co-interior consecutive angles, solving x and 2x plus 30 to get 50 and 130. We compare what the diagonals do in each shape, which bisect each other, which are equal, which cross at right angles and which bisect the angles, and we arrange the six shapes in a family tree in which a square is both a rectangle and a rhombus, and all three are parallelograms.
