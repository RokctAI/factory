Hey — if twins, photocopies and four sets of letters blurred together today, here is the calm version: twins need a key, photocopies need a scale factor, and the letters go in order.

Twins. Congruent triangles are identical: every side and every angle matches. You prove twins with one of four keys. SSS, three sides. SAS, two sides with the angle between them, the hinge. AAS, two angles and a side. RHS, a right angle, the hypotenuse and one more side. Two fakes to avoid: AAA, three angles, which fixes shape but not size, and SSA, two sides with an angle not between them, which draws two different triangles.

Photocopies. Similar triangles have the same shape at a different size. Prove it with equal angles, two pairs are enough, or by dividing matching sides and getting the same number every time: 6, 8, 10 over 3, 4, 5 gives 2, 2, 2. Find a missing side by multiplying by that scale factor: PQ 5 to XY 15 is times 3, so QR 7 becomes YZ 21.

Letters in order. Triangle ABC congruent to triangle DEF pairs A with D, B with E, C with F, and that order tells you which sides are equal afterwards.

Collect the dashes, arcs, common sides and radii from the diagram before you choose a key. A key, a scale factor, letters in order. You've got this!
