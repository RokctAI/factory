Today we establish by investigation the minimum conditions for two triangles to be congruent or similar. We construct triangles from given measurements and find that three sides, two sides with the included angle, two angles and a side, or a right angle with the hypotenuse and a side each produce exactly one triangle, giving the four congruence conditions SSS, SAS, AAS and RHS. We also construct the failures: three angles give a triangle of any size, and two sides with a non-included angle give two different triangles. Equal angles, or sides all in the same ratio, give similar triangles, the same shape at a different size, and we use a scale factor to find that a 15, 21 triangle matches a 5, 7 triangle, and that a tree with a 12 metre shadow beside a 2 metre pole with a 3 metre shadow is 8 metres tall.
