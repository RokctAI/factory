Today we investigate by construction. We draw a rectangle 8 by 6, a square, a parallelogram, a rhombus and a kite, put in both diagonals, and measure the pieces and the angles at the crossing to confirm which diagonals bisect each other, which are equal, which are perpendicular and which bisect the angles. We record the results in a table, read it backwards to name a shape from its diagonals, and use the properties as reasons, finding that a rectangle whose diagonal makes 35 degrees with a side has angles of 35, 35 and 110 in the triangle at the centre. Then we split polygons into triangles from one vertex to find the interior angle sum, n minus 2 times 180, giving 540 for a pentagon and 720 for a hexagon, use exterior angles that always add to 360, and find that a regular polygon with angles of 150 has twelve sides.
