Hey — if the problems today felt like they needed every rule from the whole term at once, here is the calm version: collect, name, chain, check.

Collect. Before you write anything, read the picture. Dashes are equal sides, arcs are equal angles, arrowheads are parallel sides, a tiny square is 90. Write down what the question says the shape is.

Name. Say the shape's name, because the rules live in the name. Kite: side corners equal. Parallelogram family: opposite corners equal, neighbours add to 180, diagonals halve each other. Rhombus adds diagonals at 90 that halve the corners. Rectangle adds equal diagonals. Trapezium only gives C couples on the slanted sides.

Chain. One fact per line, each with its reason in brackets and the shape named in the reason. A kite with 50 and 110 leaves 200 for two equal corners, so 100 each. A rhombus with a 70 corner has a 110 neighbour, and its diagonal splits that into 55. If a line inside the shape makes triangles, use the triangle rules too: 180 in a triangle, the roof rule, the ramp rule, and twins by SSS, SAS, AAS or RHS.

Check. Add the angles to 360 or 180, or test a second property. If the check fails, go back to the name; most mistakes borrow a rule from the wrong shape.

Collect, name, chain, check. You've got this!
