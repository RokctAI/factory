Hey — if the triangle facts stacked up today, here is the calm version: two boxes, one half-turn, and one ramp.

Two boxes. Every triangle has a sides name, scalene, isosceles or equilateral, and an angles name, acute, right-angled or obtuse. Read the marks on the picture: dashes mean equal sides, a tiny square means 90, matching arcs mean equal angles. The isosceles bonus is the roof rule: equal sides face equal angles.

One half-turn. The three corners of any triangle add to 180. Two corners known, take them from 180 for the third. A roof with 40 at the top has floor angles of 70 each. Letters: x plus 2x plus 3x equals 180 gives x equal to 30, then put x back for 30, 60 and 90.

One ramp. Stretch a side out past a corner. The outside angle equals the two FAR corners added together, not the near one. Far corners 50 and 60 give an outside angle of 110. With letters, set the outside expression equal to the sum of the two far ones.

Every line gets its reason in brackets: sum of angles in a triangle, base angles of an isosceles triangle, exterior angle of a triangle.

Two boxes, one half-turn, one ramp. You've got this!
