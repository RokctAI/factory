Hey — if six shapes and all their properties felt like too much to carry, here is the calm version: a family tree, a full turn, and two sticks.

The family tree. Any four-sided shape at the top. Three children: trapezium with one pair of parallel sides, kite with two pairs of neighbouring equal sides, parallelogram with both pairs of opposite sides parallel. The parallelogram's children are the rectangle, which gained a right angle, and the rhombus, which gained equal sides. Their shared child is the square. Children inherit everything, so a square is a rectangle and a rhombus and a parallelogram, but the arrows only point down.

The full turn. Four corners of any quadrilateral add to 360. In a parallelogram opposite corners are equal and neighbouring corners add to 180. One corner 50 means the others are 130, 50 and 130.

Two sticks. The diagonals halve each other in every parallelogram. They are equal in a rectangle. They cross at right angles in a rhombus. A square has all of it. The kite's middle stick does the work; the trapezium's sticks promise nothing.

Read the arrowheads, dashes and right-angle marks before you name a shape, and write the shape's name in every reason.

Family tree, full turn, two sticks. You've got this!
