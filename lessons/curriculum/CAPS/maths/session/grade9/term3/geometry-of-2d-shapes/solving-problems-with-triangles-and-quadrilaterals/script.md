# Part 1 — Expert

Everything in this topic has been building to today: the triangle facts, the quadrilateral properties, the diagonals, and the conditions for congruence and similarity are now tools, and the task is to use them together on problems with unknown angles and sides. A geometry problem is a chain of statements, each with a reason, from what is given to what is asked, and the skill is choosing the next link. We work through kites, rhombi, trapeziums and parallelograms with numbers and with algebra, triangles hiding inside quadrilaterals, and sides found through congruent and similar triangles, and we finish with the multi-step problems that combine three or four facts. The layout that earned marks in Term 2, statement, equals, value, reason, is the layout throughout.

## Subtopic: Angle Chasing in Quadrilaterals

Start every problem by collecting what the diagram gives: marks for equal sides, arcs for equal angles, arrowheads for parallel sides, a small square for a right angle, and the name of the shape if the question states it. Then choose the property that connects a known angle to the unknown. If no single property reaches the unknown, find an angle that both a known property and the unknown share, and go through it in two steps; most problems need two or three links, rarely more.

Kite ABCD with AB equal to AD and CB equal to CD, angle BAD equal to 50 degrees and angle BCD equal to 110 degrees. The kite's one angle property: the angles where the unequal sides meet, B and D, are equal. Let each be x. Then 50 plus 110 plus x plus x equals 360, sum of angles in a quadrilateral, so 2x equals 200 and x equals 100. Angle ABC is 100 degrees. Check: 50 plus 110 plus 100 plus 100 is 360.

Rhombus ABCD with angle ABC equal to 70 degrees. Angle BAD is 180 minus 70, which is 110 degrees, co-interior angles with AD parallel to BC. The diagonals of a rhombus bisect the angles, so angle BAC is 55 degrees and angle ABD is 35 degrees. The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular, so at the crossing O the angle AOB is 90, and the triangle AOB has angles 55, 35 and 90, which add to 180 as a check.

Trapezium ABCD with AB parallel to DC, angle A equal to 115 degrees, angle B equal to 2x plus 10 and angle D equal to x. The co-interior pair on side AD gives 115 plus x equals 180, so x is 65, and angle D is 65 degrees. Then angle B is 2 times 65 plus 10, which is 140 degrees, and angle C is 180 minus 140, which is 40, co-interior angles on side BC. Check: 115 plus 140 plus 65 plus 40 is 360.

Parallelogram with consecutive angles 3x minus 20 and x plus 40. Consecutive angles add to 180, so 4x plus 20 equals 180, 4x equals 160, x equals 40. The angles are 100 and 80, and the other two are 100 and 80 by opposite angles of a parallelogram. Check: 100 plus 80 is 180.

Each chain names the shape in the reason, since the property belongs to that shape and not to quadrilaterals in general. The questions for this section are with you now: the kite with 50 and 110, the rhombus with 70, the trapezium with x and 2x plus 10, and the parallelogram with 3x minus 20.

## Subtopic: Triangles Inside Quadrilaterals

A diagonal, a midpoint or a line from a vertex creates triangles inside a quadrilateral, and the triangle facts then apply alongside the quadrilateral facts.

Rectangle ABCD with E the midpoint of AB, and ED and EC drawn. Triangles AED and BEC: AE equals EB, E is the midpoint; angle A equals angle B, both 90, angles of a rectangle; AD equals BC, opposite sides of a rectangle. So triangle AED is congruent to triangle BEC, SAS, and ED equals EC, congruent triangles. The triangle DEC is therefore isosceles. If angle ADE is 32 degrees, then angle AED is 58, sum of angles in a triangle; angle BEC is also 58, congruent triangles; and angle DEC is 180 minus 58 minus 58, which is 64 degrees, angles on a straight line.

Isosceles triangle ABC with AB equal to AC, and BC extended to D with angle ACD equal to 124 degrees. Angle ACB is 56, angles on a straight line. Angle ABC is 56, angles opposite equal sides. Angle BAC is 180 minus 112, which is 68 degrees, sum of angles in a triangle. The exterior angle offers a check: 124 should equal angle A plus angle B, and 68 plus 56 is 124.

Parallelogram ABCD with the diagonal AC drawn, angle DAC equal to 35 degrees and angle ABC equal to 80 degrees. Angle ACB equals angle DAC, which is 35, alternate angles with AD parallel to BC. In triangle ABC, angle BAC is 180 minus 80 minus 35, which is 65 degrees. Angle BAD is 35 plus 65, which is 100, and angle BCD is also 100, opposite angles of a parallelogram; 100 plus 80 is 180, consecutive angles, as a check.

Square ABCD with diagonal AC. The diagonal bisects the right angle, so angle BAC is 45 degrees, and triangle ABC is isosceles right-angled with angles 45, 45 and 90. In a rectangle that is not a square the diagonal does not bisect the angle, and a learner who writes 45 in a rectangle has used a square property without a square. Your questions on this section are ready: the rectangle with the midpoint, the isosceles triangle with the 124, the parallelogram with 35 and 80.

## Subtopic: Unknown Sides from Congruent and Similar Triangles

Angles come from angle facts; sides come from congruence, from similarity, or from the equal-sides properties of the shapes.

Congruence gives equal sides. In the rectangle problem, ED equals EC came from congruent triangles. In a kite ABCD with the axis AC, triangles ABC and ADC have AB equal to AD and CB equal to CD, given, and AC common, so they are congruent, SSS, and the angles BAC and DAC are equal, confirming that the axis bisects the angle at A.

Similarity gives sides in proportion. Triangle ABC with D on AB and E on AC and DE parallel to BC, AD equal to 3, DB equal to 5 and DE equal to 4.5. Angle A is common and angle ADE equals angle ABC, corresponding angles with DE parallel to BC, so triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC, AAA. AB is 8, so BC over DE equals AB over AD, and BC is 4.5 times 8 over 3, which is 12.

Shape properties give sides directly. In a parallelogram with AB equal to 2x plus 1 and DC equal to 3x minus 4, opposite sides are equal, so 2x plus 1 equals 3x minus 4 and x is 5; AB is 11. In a rhombus every side equals every other, so one marked side of 7 gives a perimeter of 28. In a rectangle with diagonals meeting at O and AO equal to 6.5, the diagonals are equal and bisect each other, so BD is 13.

The shadow method in a new setting. A person 1.8 metres tall casts a shadow 1.2 metres long while a building casts a shadow 15 metres long. The two triangles are similar, parallel sun rays, so the building's height over 1.8 equals 15 over 1.2, which is 12.5, and the building is 1.8 times 12.5, which is 22.5 metres. The questions for this section are ready: DE parallel to BC with 3, 5 and 4.5, the parallelogram sides in x, and the 22.5 metre building.

## Subtopic: Multi-Step Problems and the Error Museum

Multi-step problems chain several facts, and the order is: collect, name, chain, check. Here is one in full.

ABCD is a rhombus with diagonals meeting at O, and angle ADC is 100 degrees. Find angle OAB and angle ABO. Collect: four equal sides, diagonals bisect each other at right angles and bisect the angles. Name: rhombus. Chain: angle DAB is 180 minus 100, which is 80, co-interior angles with AD parallel to BC, or consecutive angles of a rhombus. Angle OAB is 40, diagonals of a rhombus bisect the angles. Angle AOB is 90, diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular. Angle ABO is 180 minus 40 minus 90, which is 50, sum of angles in a triangle. Check: angle ABC is 100 by opposite angles, and half of it is 50, matching angle ABO.

A second one. In trapezium PQRS with PQ parallel to SR, PS equals QR, angle P equals 70 degrees. An isosceles trapezium has equal base angles, so angle Q is 70, and angles S and R are each 110 by co-interior angles. Draw the diagonal PR with angle QPR equal to 40 degrees. Angle PRS is 40, alternate angles with PQ parallel to SR, and angle SPR is 70 minus 40, which is 30. In triangle PSR the angles are 30, 110 and 40, sum of angles in a triangle, which adds to 180 as the check. Three shapes were used in one problem, the isosceles trapezium, the parallel lines and a triangle, and each line named the one it came from.

The error museum, five exhibits. Exhibit one: using a square property in a rectangle, such as a 45 degree diagonal angle; a rectangle's diagonal bisects the right angle only when the sides are equal. Exhibit two: using the parallelogram property that consecutive angles add to 180 on a kite or a trapezium's non-parallel pair. Exhibit three: a congruence statement without its three facts, or with letters in the wrong order, so that the wrong side is later claimed equal. Exhibit four: a similarity ratio with mismatched sides, dividing BC by AD instead of by DE. Exhibit five: a chain with the right numbers and no reasons, which earns the answer mark and nothing else.

Layout for marks: collect the given facts in a list, name every shape and triangle you use, write each statement as statement, equals, value, reason, with the shape named in the reason, solve any equation with its reason, substitute back, give units, and end with a check against the angle sum or against a second property. The questions for this section are with you now: the rhombus with 100, the isosceles trapezium, and the five exhibits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again through a detective routine, shapes hiding inside other shapes, and letters that stand for angles and sides — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: Collect, Name, Chain

A geometry problem is detective work. Collect the clues: every dash means equal sides, every arc means equal angles, every arrowhead means parallel, every tiny square means 90, and the question's words name the shape. Name the shape, because the shape's name unlocks its rules. Chain: write one fact per line, with its rule in brackets, until you reach the answer. Check at the end with a sum. If the check fails, the error is usually in the first line, where a rule was borrowed from the wrong shape, so re-read the shape's name before re-doing the arithmetic.

A kite with 50 at the top and 110 at the bottom. The kite's rule: the two side corners are equal. Call them x. Four corners make 360: 50 plus 110 plus x plus x is 360, so 2x is 200 and x is 100. Each side corner is 100.

A rhombus with a 70 corner. Neighbouring corners in the parallelogram family add to 180, so the next corner is 110. The rhombus's sticks halve the corners, so the diagonal makes 55 and 35 with the sides, and the sticks cross at 90: triangle 55, 35, 90.

A trapezium with the top and bottom parallel, left corner 115 and the corner below it x. They are a C couple, 180 together, so x is 65. The right-hand corner above is 2x plus 10, which is 140, so the corner below it is 40. Check: 115 plus 140 plus 65 plus 40 is 360.

A parallelogram with neighbouring corners 3x minus 20 and x plus 40: together 180, so 4x plus 20 is 180, x is 40, the corners are 100 and 80. Your questions on this section are ready: collect, name, chain, check, and the kite with 50 and 110.

## Subtopic: Shapes Hiding Inside Shapes

Draw one line inside a quadrilateral and triangles appear. Use the triangle rules on the triangles and the quadrilateral rules on the quadrilateral, both at once.

A rectangle with E halfway along the top side AB, and lines from E down to the bottom corners D and C. The two triangles AED and BEC are twins: AE equals EB because E is the midpoint, the corners at A and B are both 90, and the sides AD and BC are equal because opposite sides of a rectangle match. SAS. So ED equals EC, and the triangle DEC is a roof, isosceles. If angle ADE is 32, angle AED is 58, so is angle BEC, and the angle DEC in the middle is 180 minus 58 minus 58, which is 64.

A roof triangle ABC with AB equal to AC, floor BC stretched to D, and the outside angle at C equal to 124. The inside angle at C is 56, the straight line. The roof rule gives 56 at B too. The top is 180 minus 112, which is 68. Check with the ramp rule: outside 124 equals the two far corners, 68 plus 56.

A square cut by its diagonal gives two triangles of 45, 45, 90, because the square's diagonal halves its right angles. A rectangle's diagonal does not halve the right angle unless the rectangle is a square, which is the commonest hidden-shape mistake.

A parallelogram with a diagonal AC, angle DAC 35 and angle B 80: the Z rule between the parallel sides gives angle ACB 35, the triangle gives angle BAC 65, so the whole corner at A is 100 and the opposite corner is also 100. The questions for this section: the midpoint twins, the roof with the ramp, and the square's 45.

## Subtopic: Letters for Angles and Sides

When a side or angle has a letter in it, the rule becomes an equation, and the equation becomes the answer.

Sides. A parallelogram with AB equal to 2x plus 1 and DC equal to 3x minus 4: opposite sides are equal, so 2x plus 1 equals 3x minus 4, and x is 5, so AB is 11. A rectangle's sticks are equal and cut in half, so AO equal to 6.5 means the other stick BD is 13. A rhombus with one side 7 has perimeter 28.

Photocopies. In triangle ABC, a line DE across the triangle parallel to the base BC makes a small triangle ADE that is a photocopy of the big one, because the angle at A is shared and the parallel lines give matching F angles. AD is 3, DB is 5, so AB is 8 and the scale factor is 8 over 3. DE is 4.5, so BC is 4.5 times 8 over 3, which is 12.

Shadows. A person 1.8 metres tall has a 1.2 metre shadow; a building has a 15 metre shadow. Scale factor 15 over 1.2 is 12.5, and 1.8 times 12.5 is 22.5 metres.

A full chain. Rhombus ABCD, sticks crossing at O, corner D equal to 100. Corner A is 80, neighbours add to 180. The stick from A halves it: angle OAB is 40. The sticks cross at 90. In triangle AOB the last angle, ABO, is 180 minus 40 minus 90, which is 50. Check: corner B is 100 by opposite twins, and half of 100 is 50.

Collect, name, chain, check; twins for equal sides, photocopies for scaled sides, letters into equations. The final questions of the lesson are with you now: x equal to 5 in the parallelogram, BC equal to 12, the 22.5 metre building, and the rhombus chain ending in 50.
