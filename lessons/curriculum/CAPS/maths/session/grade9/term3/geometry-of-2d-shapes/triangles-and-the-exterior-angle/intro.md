Today we begin the geometry of two-dimensional shapes with the triangle. We revise the two independent classifications, by sides as scalene, isosceles or equilateral and by angles as acute, right-angled or obtuse, together with the fact that the angles opposite the equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal. We use the interior angle sum of 180 degrees that last term's parallel lines proved, finding a third angle from 50 and 60, base angles of 70 from an apex of 40, and angles of 30, 60 and 90 from x, 2x and 3x. Then we investigate by construction the exterior angle of a triangle, measure that it equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles, prove it in two lines, and use it to solve problems with numbers and with algebra.
