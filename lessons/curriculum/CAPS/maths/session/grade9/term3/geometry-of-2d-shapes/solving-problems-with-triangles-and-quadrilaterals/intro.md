Today we put the whole topic to work on problems with unknown angles and sides. We chase angles through a kite with angles of 50 and 110, a rhombus with a 70 degree corner, a trapezium with angles 115, x and 2x plus 10, and a parallelogram with consecutive angles 3x minus 20 and x plus 40, naming the shape in every reason. We find triangles hiding inside quadrilaterals, proving two triangles in a rectangle congruent by SAS to show that two lines from a midpoint are equal, and we use an exterior angle of 124 degrees to find the angles of an isosceles triangle. We find sides from congruent triangles, from similar triangles with a line parallel to the base, and from the equal-sides properties of parallelograms and rhombi, and we finish with multi-step chains and the layout, collect, name, chain, check, that earns every mark.
