Today we find probabilities for compound events, where two experiments combine. We list the four equally likely outcomes of two coins and the twelve of a coin and a die, learning that head-tail and tail-head are different outcomes and that the number of outcomes multiplies. We lay out the 36 outcomes of two dice in a two-way table and read events from its cells, finding that a sum of 7 fills six cells for a probability of 1 over 6 while a sum of 2 fills one, so the eleven sums are not equally likely. We draw tree diagrams for experiments in sequence, multiplying along each path and adding across the paths that make an event, for a spinner spun twice and for a bag of 3 red and 2 green marbles drawn without replacement, where the second draw's branches change, and we compare the result of 3 over 10 with the 9 over 25 of drawing with replacement.
