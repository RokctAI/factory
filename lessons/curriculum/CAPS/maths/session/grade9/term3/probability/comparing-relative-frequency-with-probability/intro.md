Today we put probability and relative frequency in the same table and explain why they disagree. A fair coin has a probability of 0.5 for heads, yet 50 tosses gave 27 heads, a relative frequency of 0.54; a fair die should give each face 10 times in 60 rolls, yet our class got anything from 8 to 12. We learn to state the size and direction of each difference, and to name its cause: chance variation, which is unavoidable and goes either way, or bias in the equipment or the method, which pushes the results the same way every time. We watch a running count of 1000 tosses settle towards one half and see why the fraction settles while the count of extra heads does not. Then we use relative frequency to estimate probabilities no formula can give, such as a late taxi on 6 of 40 mornings, and clear out five myths about luck.
