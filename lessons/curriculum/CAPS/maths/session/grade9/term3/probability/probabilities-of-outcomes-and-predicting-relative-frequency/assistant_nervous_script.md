Hey — if chances, tallies and predictions all ran together today, here is the calm version: count the ways that win, tally what happened, chance times tries.

Count the ways that win. When every way is equally likely, the chance is the ways you want over all the ways. A die: a 6 is 1 out of 6, an even number 3 out of 6, which is a half. A bag of 5 red, 3 blue and 2 yellow: red is 5 out of 10. Every chance sits between 0 and 1, and all the chances together make exactly 1. Not blue is 1 take away 3 tenths, which is 7 tenths.

Tally what happened. Do the experiment and count. The relative frequency is the count over the number of tries: 12 fours in 60 rolls is 12 over 60, a fifth. Divide by the tries, never by the number of sides.

Chance times tries. To predict, multiply the chance by the number of tries: a sixth of 120 rolls is about 20 sixes; an eighth of 240 spins is about 30 prizes. Say about, because the real count wanders around the prediction, and the die never remembers what it rolled before.

Run it fairly: same way every time, marbles back in the bag, plenty of tries. Count, tally, multiply. You've got this!
