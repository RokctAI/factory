Today we put the two faces of probability side by side. The first is calculated before anything happens: with equally likely outcomes, the probability of an event is the number of favourable outcomes over the total, so a fair die gives 1 over 6 for a six and a bag of 5 red, 3 blue and 2 yellow marbles gives 1 over 2 for red. The second is counted afterwards: the relative frequency is the number of times an outcome happened divided by the number of trials, as in 12 fours from 60 rolls, which is 0.2. Then we join them, predicting that an outcome with probability P occurs about P times n times in n trials, so 120 rolls should give about 20 sixes and a fun-day spinner played 240 times about 30 winners. We finish with the conditions for a fair experiment and the five classic errors.
