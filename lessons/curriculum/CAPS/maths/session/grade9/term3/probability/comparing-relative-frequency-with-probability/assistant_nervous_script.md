Hey — if the difference between what should happen and what did happen felt slippery today, here is the calm version: should against did, luck evens out slowly, and the data is the judge.

Should against did. Probability is what should happen; relative frequency is what did happen. A fair coin should give 25 heads in 50 tosses; it gave 27. Write both as decimals, 0.5 and 0.54, and say the gap and its direction: 0.04 more heads.

Then say why. A small gap is luck, called chance variation, and it can go either way. A big gap that keeps happening is bias: a weighted die, a sticky spinner, or a sloppy method like not shaking the bag. In 600 rolls a six should come up about 100 times; 108 is luck, 160 means something is wrong.

Luck evens out slowly. The more tries, the closer the fraction gets to the probability: 0.7 after 10 tosses, 0.512 after 1000. But the coin never pays back its extra heads. The fraction settles because the extras get drowned in all the tosses, not because tails catch up. So always say how many tries you did.

The data is the judge. When there is no formula, like a late taxi, record it: 6 late out of 40 days is about 0.15. Should, did, size, cause. You've got this!
