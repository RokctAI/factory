Today we revise the three skills that every piece of Term 2 algebra will lean on: adding and subtracting like terms, including when a minus sign sits in front of a bracket; multiplying and dividing monomials using the exponent laws; and substituting values — whole numbers, common fractions and decimals — into expressions with the brackets that keep the signs honest. The anchor simplification is 3x squared minus 2x plus 5, minus the bracket x squared plus 4x minus 1, which becomes 2x squared minus 6x plus 6. The anchor substitution is 2x squared minus 3x plus 1 at x equals one half, which comes to exactly zero. These are the gears of the machine; the next four lessons build the machine.
