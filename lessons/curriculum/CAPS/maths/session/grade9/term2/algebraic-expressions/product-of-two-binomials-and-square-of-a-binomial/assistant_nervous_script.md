Hey — if four products and three identities felt like too much to hold at once, here is the calm version: one rule, and one check that catches everything.

The rule: every term in the first bracket meets every term in the second. Two terms each, so four meetings, written down with their signs, then sorted. The bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5 is x squared, 5x, 3x, 15, which sorts to x squared plus 8x plus 15. If you drew a grid with four cells and filled every one, you did it right.

A squared bracket is the same rule with two identical brackets. The bracket x plus 5, all squared, is x squared plus 10x plus 25. The middle term is real — two cross products stacking — and leaving it out is the one slip everyone makes. First squared, double the product, last squared. The last term of a squared bracket is always positive.

A sum times a difference loses its middle: x plus 7 times x minus 7 is x squared minus 49.

The check: put x equal to 1 into the original and into your answer. They must match. If they do not, look for a missed meeting, a missing middle, or a minus that only flipped one sign.

Four meetings, sort, check with 1. That is the whole lesson. You've got this!
