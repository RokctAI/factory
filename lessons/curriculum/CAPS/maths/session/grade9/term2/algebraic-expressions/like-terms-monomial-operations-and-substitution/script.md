# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises adding and subtracting like terms, multiplying and dividing monomials, and substitution into algebraic expressions, including common and decimal fractions. The anchor simplification is 3x squared minus 2x plus 5, minus the bracket x squared plus 4x minus 1, which simplifies to 2x squared minus 6x plus 6. The anchor products are 3x squared times 4x cubed, which is 12x to the power 5, and 12x to the power 5 divided by 3x squared, which is 4x cubed. The anchor substitution is 2x squared minus 3x plus 1 at x equals one half, which is 0. Each procedure has a layout that earns marks, and each has a sign trap.

## Subtopic: Adding and Subtracting Like Terms

Like terms have identical variable parts, and only like terms combine: add the coefficients, keep the variable part unchanged. 5x squared minus 2x squared is 3x squared. 4ab plus 7ba is 11ab, since ab and ba are the same product. 3x plus 2y stays 3x plus 2y.

Coefficients may be fractions. One half x plus one third x: common denominator 6, so three sixths plus two sixths is five sixths x. Two fifths a minus a: write a as five fifths a, so negative three fifths a. Decimals behave the same: 0,4y plus 1,25y is 1,65y. The variable is a label on the coefficient; the arithmetic is ordinary fraction arithmetic.

A plus sign before a bracket leaves the contents unchanged. A minus sign before a bracket is a multiplication by negative 1 and changes every sign inside. The anchor: 3x squared minus 2x plus 5, minus the bracket x squared plus 4x minus 1. Remove the bracket: 3x squared minus 2x plus 5 minus x squared minus 4x plus 1. Each term inside flipped: plus x squared became minus x squared, plus 4x became minus 4x, minus 1 became plus 1. Now collect: x squared terms, 3 minus 1 is 2x squared; x terms, negative 2 minus 4 is negative 6x; constants, 5 plus 1 is 6. Result: 2x squared minus 6x plus 6.

Check by substitution at x equals 1. Original: 3 minus 2 plus 5 is 6; bracket: 1 plus 4 minus 1 is 4; 6 minus 4 is 2. Simplified: 2 minus 6 plus 6 is 2. Agreed.

The most common error is to flip only the first sign inside the bracket — writing minus x squared plus 4x minus 1 — which gives 2x squared plus 2x plus 4 and fails the check at x equals 1, where it gives 8 against 2. The minus distributes to every term, and the check catches a partial distribution every time.

A longer example: 2a minus the bracket 3b minus a, plus the bracket a minus 2b. Remove brackets: 2a minus 3b plus a plus a minus 2b. Collect: 4a minus 5b.

Layout: write the expression with brackets removed and all signs resolved on one line, then group like terms, then write the result in descending powers. Three lines, three marks.

The questions for this section are with you now: fraction coefficients, the minus before a bracket, and the substitution check.

## Subtopic: Multiplying and Dividing Monomials

A monomial times a monomial: multiply the coefficients, and for each variable add the exponents. 3x squared times 4x cubed: 3 times 4 is 12; x squared times x cubed is x to the power 5; result 12x to the power 5. Negative 2ab times 5a squared b: negative 2 times 5 is negative 10; a times a squared is a cubed; b times b is b squared; result negative 10a cubed b squared.

The sign follows the integer rules: negative times negative is positive. Negative 3x times negative 2x squared is 6x cubed. Three negative factors give a negative product: negative x times negative 2x times negative 3x is negative 6x cubed.

A monomial divided by a monomial: divide the coefficients, and for each variable subtract the exponents. 12x to the power 5 divided by 3x squared: 12 over 3 is 4; x to the power 5 minus 2 is x cubed; result 4x cubed. 15a cubed b squared divided by negative 5ab: 15 over negative 5 is negative 3; a cubed over a is a squared; b squared over b is b; result negative 3a squared b.

When the exponent below exceeds the exponent above, the result has the variable in the denominator: 6x squared divided by 3x to the power 5 is 2 over x cubed, which is also 2x to the power negative 3. In this course write it with a positive exponent.

A monomial raised to a power: raise the coefficient and multiply each exponent. 2x squared y, all cubed: 2 cubed is 8; x to the power 2 times 3 is x to the power 6; y cubed; result 8x to the power 6 y cubed. Negative 3a squared, all squared, is 9a to the power 4, positive because the exponent is even.

Multiplication and addition must not be confused. 3x times 2x is 6x squared; 3x plus 2x is 5x. In the product the coefficients multiply and the exponents add; in the sum the coefficients add and the exponents are untouched. Check at x equals 2: 3x times 2x is 6 times 4, which is 24, and 6x squared at 2 is 24; 3x plus 2x is 10, and 5x at 2 is 10.

Take this section's questions now: coefficients multiply and exponents add, coefficients divide and exponents subtract, and the sign of a product.

## Subtopic: Substitution With Fractions and Decimals

To substitute, replace every occurrence of the variable with the value in brackets, then evaluate in the order of operations. The anchor: 2x squared minus 3x plus 1 at x equals one half. Write 2 times open bracket one half close bracket squared, minus 3 times open bracket one half close bracket, plus 1. One half squared is one quarter; 2 times one quarter is one half. 3 times one half is three halves. So one half minus three halves plus 1: one half minus three halves is negative 1; negative 1 plus 1 is 0. The expression is 0 at x equals one half.

The same value as a decimal: x equals 0,5. 2 times 0,25 is 0,5; 3 times 0,5 is 1,5; 0,5 minus 1,5 plus 1 is 0. The two routes must agree, and they do.

A negative fraction: x equals negative three quarters. 2 times open bracket negative three quarters close bracket squared: the square is nine sixteenths, positive, and 2 times nine sixteenths is nine eighths. Minus 3 times negative three quarters is plus nine quarters. Plus 1. Common denominator 8: nine eighths plus eighteen eighths plus eight eighths is thirty-five eighths, which is 4 and three eighths, or 4,375.

Two variables with decimals: 3a squared b at a equals 1,5 and b equals negative 2. 1,5 squared is 2,25; 3 times 2,25 is 6,75; times negative 2 is negative 13,5.

A formula in context: the perimeter of a rectangle, P equals 2 times the bracket l plus b, with l equal to 2,5 metres and b equal to 1,25 metres. 2,5 plus 1,25 is 3,75; times 2 is 7,5 metres. The bracket is evaluated first because it is a bracket, not because of any special rule for formulae.

The two traps are sign and exponent. Without brackets, negative three quarters squared is read as the negative of nine sixteenths, and the first term becomes negative nine eighths — wrong. And 2x squared at x equals one half is 2 times one quarter, not the quantity 2 times one half, all squared, which would be 1. The exponent acts on x alone; the coefficient multiplies afterwards.

This section's questions are available now: brackets around every substituted value, fractions and decimals giving the same answer, and the coefficient outside the square.

## Subtopic: Mixed Simplification and the Error Museum

Expressions combine the three skills, and the order of operations decides the sequence: products and quotients of monomials first, then collection of like terms. 3x times 2x, plus 4x squared, minus x times 5x: the products are 6x squared and 5x squared; then 6x squared plus 4x squared minus 5x squared is 5x squared.

12x cubed divided by 4x, plus 2x squared: the quotient is 3x squared; then 3x squared plus 2x squared is 5x squared. A student who adds 12x cubed and 2x squared first has combined unlike terms and violated the order of operations at once.

With brackets: 2x squared minus the bracket 3x times x minus 4x squared. The product inside is 3x squared, so the bracket is 3x squared minus 4x squared, which is negative x squared. Then 2x squared minus negative x squared is 3x squared. Alternatively remove the bracket first: 2x squared minus 3x squared plus 4x squared is 3x squared. Both routes agree.

Check every simplification by substitution at an easy value, x equals 1 or 2, never 0 if there is division. The original and the simplified form must give the same number; if not, the error is in the working, and the size of the discrepancy often points to the line.

The error museum for this topic has six exhibits. One: 3x times 2x written as 5x squared — coefficients added in a product. Two: 3x plus 2x written as 6x, or 5x squared — a sum treated as a product. Three: x squared times x cubed written as x to the power 6 — exponents multiplied instead of added. Four: minus the bracket x minus 3 written as minus x minus 3 — the sign distributed to the first term only. Five: a negative value substituted without brackets, so that negative 2 squared reads as negative 4. Six: one half x plus one third x written as two fifths x — numerators and denominators added separately.

Layout for marks: products and quotients resolved on the first line, brackets removed with every sign resolved on the second, like terms collected on the third, result in descending powers on the fourth, and the substitution check in the margin. The check is unmarked but it protects every other line.

The questions for this section are ready: products before sums, the bracket resolved on one line, and the six exhibits of the museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same three skills again through a fruit bowl with a minus sign in front of it, a rule about numbers and little numbers, and plugging in without blowing a fuse — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: Fruit Again, With Minus Brackets

Like terms are the same fruit, and you add the counts: 5 apples take away 2 apples is 3 apples, so 5x squared minus 2x squared is 3x squared. Counts can be fractions: half an apple plus a third of an apple is five sixths of an apple, so one half x plus one third x is five sixths x — ordinary fraction adding, with the fruit as a label.

Now the new bit: a minus sign in front of a bracket. Think of the bracket as a bag, and the minus as "take away this whole bag". If the bag holds x squared, plus 4x, minus 1, then taking away the bag means taking away the x squared, taking away the 4x, and taking away the minus 1 — which is adding 1. Every sign in the bag flips. 3x squared minus 2x plus 5, take away the bag: 3x squared minus 2x plus 5 minus x squared minus 4x plus 1. Then sort the fruit: 2x squared, minus 6x, plus 6.

The classic slip is flipping only the first thing in the bag. People write minus x squared and then copy plus 4x and minus 1 unchanged. Picture it: you cannot take away a bag and leave half the contents behind. Everything in the bag goes.

Prove it to yourself with x equals 1. Original: 3 minus 2 plus 5 is 6, bag is 1 plus 4 minus 1 is 4, six take away four is 2. Your answer: 2 minus 6 plus 6 is 2. Match. If the check does not match, the sign slip is somewhere in the bag.

Keep the bag picture in mind and try the questions for this part: adding fractions of fruit, and taking away a whole bag.

## Subtopic: Multiply the Numbers, Add the Little Numbers

Multiplying monomials is a two-part rule. Part one: multiply the ordinary numbers. Part two: for each letter, add the little numbers. 3x squared times 4x cubed: 3 times 4 is 12; the little numbers 2 and 3 add to 5. Answer 12x to the power 5. Why add? Because x squared is two x's and x cubed is three x's, and multiplying them together gives five x's in a row — you counted them last term with the exponent laws.

Dividing is the mirror: divide the numbers, subtract the little numbers. 12x to the power 5 over 3x squared: 12 over 3 is 4; 5 minus 2 is 3. Answer 4x cubed. Think of pairing off the x's on top and bottom and cancelling the pairs — five on top, two below, three survive.

Signs ride along with the numbers. Negative 2ab times 5a squared b: negative 2 times 5 is negative 10; a's: one and two make three; b's: one and one make two. Negative 10a cubed b squared. Two negatives make a positive, three make a negative, exactly as with integers.

A bracket with a power outside shares the power with everyone inside, and the little numbers multiply: 2x squared y, all cubed, is 2 cubed times x to the power 2 times 3 times y cubed, which is 8x to the power 6 y cubed.

The thing to tattoo on your brain: multiplying is "numbers times, little numbers add"; adding is "numbers add, little numbers stay". 3x times 2x is 6x squared. 3x plus 2x is 5x. Put x equals 2 into both and you get 24 and 10 — different jobs, different answers.

Hold the two-part rule and try this part's questions: numbers times and little numbers add, numbers divide and little numbers subtract, and the sign that rides along.

## Subtopic: Plug In Carefully

Substituting is plugging a number into every x. The one habit that saves you is to put the number in brackets first, before you do anything else. 2x squared minus 3x plus 1 at x equals one half becomes 2 times open bracket a half close bracket squared, minus 3 times open bracket a half close bracket, plus 1. Now work it: a half squared is a quarter, 2 quarters is a half; 3 halves; a half minus three halves is minus 1; plus 1 is 0. Zero. The same thing in decimals, x equals 0,5: 2 times 0,25 is 0,5; minus 1,5; plus 1; zero again. Fractions and decimals are the same number in different clothes, and they must give the same answer.

Why the brackets? Because of negatives. At x equals negative three quarters, the first term is 2 times negative three quarters in brackets, squared. Squared first: nine sixteenths, positive. Then times 2: nine eighths. Without the brackets your eye reads "negative nine sixteenths" and the whole answer goes wrong. The middle term, minus 3 times negative three quarters, becomes plus nine quarters — minus times minus. Add it all over 8: nine plus eighteen plus eight is thirty-five eighths, four and three eighths.

Second habit: the little number belongs to the x only. 2x squared at x equals a half is 2 times a quarter, which is a half. It is not 2 times a half, squared, which would be 1. Square the x, then multiply by the number in front.

Decimals in a formula are no different. Perimeter equals 2 times the bracket length plus breadth: 2 times the bracket 2,5 plus 1,25 is 2 times 3,75, which is 7,5. Bracket first, always.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Same fruit adds; a minus in front of a bag flips every sign in the bag. Multiplying monomials: numbers times, little numbers add; dividing: numbers divide, little numbers subtract. Plug in with brackets, square before you multiply by the number in front, and check every simplification by plugging in 1 or 2 — the original and your answer must match.

Hold the bag, the two-part rule and the brackets, and take the final questions of the lesson: substituting fractions and decimals, and the check that catches every slip.
