Today we multiply integers and monomials by polynomials, and divide polynomials by integers and monomials. Both are the distributive property at work: the multiplier outside a bracket reaches every term inside, and a divisor under a polynomial divides every term above it. 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4 is 6x cubed minus 2x squared plus 8x; 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x, all over 4x, is 3x squared minus 2x plus 1. The traps are the same in both directions — a multiplier or divisor applied to the first term only, and a negative that fails to flip every sign — and the cure is the same too: one line with every term resolved, then a substitution check. This is the lesson that turns brackets from obstacles into routine.
