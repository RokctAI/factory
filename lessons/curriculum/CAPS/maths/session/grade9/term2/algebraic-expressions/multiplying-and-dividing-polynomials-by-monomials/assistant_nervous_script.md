Hey — if brackets with things in front of them made you freeze, here is the calm version: it is one rule, and you already know it from numbers.

Three times 25 is three times 20 plus three times 5. The 3 goes to both parts. A bracket is the same: the number or letter in front is delivered to every term inside, not just the first. 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5 is 6x minus 15. 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4 is 6x cubed minus 2x squared plus 8x — numbers times, little numbers add, for each term.

If the thing in front is negative, every sign inside flips as it is multiplied. Negative 2 times negative 3x is plus 6x. Say "flip" out loud for each term.

Dividing is the same rule backwards: every term on top gets divided. 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x, over 4x, is 3x squared minus 2x plus 1 — and that 1 is a real term, not nothing.

Then check. Plug x equals 1 into the original and your answer. Or, for a division, multiply your answer back by the divisor and see the original come back.

Open the practice set, deliver to every bag, and check with 1. You've got this!
