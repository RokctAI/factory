Hey — if algebraic fractions felt like a wall of brackets, here is the calm version: three habits from ordinary fractions, plus one rule about crossing out.

The rule: cross out factors, never terms. 10 plus 5 over 5 is 3, not 10, because that 5 is added, not multiplied. So before you cross anything out, factorise the top and the bottom. x squared plus 8x plus 15 over x plus 3 becomes the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5 over the bracket x plus 3, and now the bracket crosses out, leaving x plus 5.

Habit one, multiplying: factorise everything, cross out across both fractions, multiply what is left.

Habit two, dividing: flip the second fraction, then do habit one. The bracket x plus 1 over 4, divided by x squared plus x over 8, becomes 2 over x.

Habit three, adding and subtracting: same bottom first, multiply the tops up to match, and keep the second top in a bag when there is a minus. The bracket x plus 1 over 3 minus the bracket x minus 2 over 4 is the bracket x plus 10 over 12.

Then try a number. If the original and your answer agree at x equals 2, you are right.

Factorise, cross out factors, flip the second, same bottom, try a number. You've got this!
