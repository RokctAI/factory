# Part 1 — Expert

Today we multiply a binomial by a binomial. Last lesson a single term in front of a bracket was distributed to every term inside; now there are two terms in front, and each of them must be distributed. The product of two binomials produces four terms before collecting, and usually three after. The square of a binomial is the special case that Grade 9 learners get wrong more than any other: the middle term exists, and leaving it out is the error that costs the most marks in Term 2. We expand, we check by substitution, and we look at what the result tells us about next lesson, which runs this whole process in reverse.

## Subtopic: Multiplying Two Binomials With the Grid

The rule: every term of the first bracket multiplies every term of the second. With two terms in each bracket that is two times two, four products, no exceptions and no shortcuts. The anchor is the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5. Expand with the first term first: x times x is x squared, x times 5 is 5x. Now the second term: 3 times x is 3x, 3 times 5 is 15. Four products: x squared plus 5x plus 3x plus 15. Collect the like terms: 5x plus 3x is 8x. Result: x squared plus 8x plus 15.

The grid is the layout that makes the four products impossible to skip. Draw a two-by-two table. Write x and plus 3 along the top, x and plus 5 down the side. Each cell holds the product of its row and column: x squared, 5x, 3x, 15. The grid is also an area diagram: a rectangle with sides x plus 3 and x plus 5 has total area equal to the sum of the four pieces, and that is exactly why the expansion is true. If x were 1, the rectangle would be 4 by 6, area 24, and the expanded form gives 1 plus 8 plus 15, also 24.

Signs ride with their terms. The bracket 2x minus 3 times the bracket x plus 4: 2x times x is 2x squared; 2x times 4 is 8x; negative 3 times x is negative 3x; negative 3 times 4 is negative 12. Collect: 8x minus 3x is 5x. Result: 2x squared plus 5x minus 12. The middle term came from two products with different signs, so the sign of the middle term depends on which was bigger. Learners who write all four products with their signs before collecting get the method mark even if the collecting slips.

One more with two negatives: the bracket x plus 2 times the bracket x minus 5. x squared, negative 5x, plus 2x, negative 10. Collect: negative 5x plus 2x is negative 3x. Result: x squared minus 3x minus 10. Notice the pattern that will matter next lesson: the constant term is the product of the two numbers, 2 times negative 5, and the middle coefficient is their sum, 2 plus negative 5. When you factorise a trinomial in the next lesson, you will be hunting for two numbers with exactly that product and that sum. The questions for this section are with you now: the four products with their signs, the grid as an area diagram, and the sum-and-product pattern in the constant and middle terms.

## Subtopic: The Square of a Binomial

The bracket 2x plus 5, all squared, means the bracket 2x plus 5 times the bracket 2x plus 5. Square means multiply by itself, so this is a binomial product like any other. Four products: 2x times 2x is 4x squared; 2x times 5 is 10x; 5 times 2x is 10x; 5 times 5 is 25. Collect: 10x plus 10x is 20x. Result: 4x squared plus 20x plus 25.

Look at the shape of the answer. The first term is the square of the first term of the bracket. The last term is the square of the last term. The middle term is twice the product of the two terms: two times 2x times 5 is 20x. That is the identity: the bracket a plus b, all squared, is a squared plus 2ab plus b squared. The middle term is there because the two cross products in the grid are identical, so they add to double.

With a minus sign: the bracket x minus 4, all squared. x squared; x times negative 4 is negative 4x; negative 4 times x is negative 4x; negative 4 times negative 4 is positive 16. Result: x squared minus 8x plus 16. The last term is positive because a negative times a negative is positive; the middle term is negative because the two cross products are each negative. The identity for a minus: a squared minus 2ab plus b squared. Only the middle sign changes.

The error that dominates this topic: writing the bracket x plus 5, all squared, as x squared plus 25. Check it with x equals 1. The bracket is 6, and 6 squared is 36. The wrong answer gives 1 plus 25, which is 26. Not equal, so the expansion is wrong, and the missing piece is the middle term 10x, which at x equals 1 is exactly the missing 10. Squaring does not distribute over addition: the square of a sum is not the sum of the squares. Learners who have internalised that 3 squared times 4 squared is the same as 12 squared sometimes assume the same for plus, and it is false: 3 squared plus 4 squared is 25, while 7 squared is 49.

The square of a binomial is tested directly, and also hides inside longer questions. Any time a bracket wears a little 2, write it as two brackets side by side before you do anything else. The questions for this section are ready: the square written as two brackets, the doubled middle term, and the sign of the last term.

## Subtopic: The Difference of Two Squares as a Product

A third pattern appears when the two brackets are the same except for the sign in the middle. The bracket x plus 7 times the bracket x minus 7. Four products: x squared; negative 7x; plus 7x; negative 49. The two cross products cancel exactly: negative 7x plus 7x is zero. Result: x squared minus 49. Two terms, not three.

The bracket 3x minus 2 times the bracket 3x plus 2: 9x squared; plus 6x; minus 6x; minus 4. Result: 9x squared minus 4. Again the middle vanishes. The identity: the bracket a plus b times the bracket a minus b is a squared minus b squared. The cross products are equal in size and opposite in sign, so they always cancel.

Why this matters now: next lesson you will meet x squared minus 49 and be asked to factorise it, and the answer is the bracket x plus 7 times the bracket x minus 7, which is this lesson run backwards. Recognising that a two-term answer with a minus between two perfect squares came from a sum-times-difference product is the whole of that skill. Write the three identities on one line in your notes: square of a sum, square of a difference, sum times difference.

Do not confuse the second and third. The bracket x minus 7, all squared, is x squared minus 14x plus 49: three terms, positive constant. The bracket x plus 7 times the bracket x minus 7 is x squared minus 49: two terms, negative constant. One has the same sign in both brackets; the other has opposite signs. Substituting x equals 1 separates them instantly: the square gives 36, the product gives 8 times negative 6, which is negative 48, and x squared minus 49 at x equals 1 is indeed negative 48. Your questions on this section: the cancelling cross products, the two-term result, and telling a sum-times-difference apart from a square of a difference.

## Subtopic: Mixed Expansions and the Error Museum

Examination questions combine the three patterns with the previous lesson's skills. Simplify the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5, minus the bracket x minus 2, all squared. Expand each product on its own line, keeping the second one inside a bracket because a minus sign waits for it. First product: x squared plus 8x plus 15. Second: x squared minus 4x plus 4. Now subtract the whole second bracket: x squared plus 8x plus 15 minus x squared plus 4x minus 4. Collect: the x squared terms cancel; 8x plus 4x is 12x; 15 minus 4 is 11. Result: 12x plus 11. A check at x equals 1: the original is 24 minus 1, which is 23; 12 plus 11 is 23.

A second one: the bracket x minus 3, all squared, minus the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x minus 3. First: x squared minus 6x plus 9. Second: x squared minus 9. Subtract the whole bracket: x squared minus 6x plus 9 minus x squared plus 9. Result: 18 minus 6x, which is also written negative 6x plus 18. The x squared terms cancelled, and that is common in these questions by design, so a cancelled square is not a sign that you have made a mistake.

Now the error museum, four exhibits. Exhibit one: the bracket x plus 5, all squared, written as x squared plus 25. The middle term 10x is missing. Exhibit two: the bracket 2x minus 3 times the bracket x plus 4 written as 2x squared minus 12, multiplying only the firsts and the lasts. The two cross products, 8x and negative 3x, are missing. Exhibit three: the bracket x minus 4, all squared, written as x squared minus 8x minus 16. The last term is negative 4 times negative 4, which is positive 16. Exhibit four: the minus in front of the second expansion flipping only its first sign, writing minus x squared minus 4x plus 4 instead of minus x squared plus 4x minus 4.

Every exhibit is caught by substituting a value into the original and into the answer. The original of exhibit two at x equals 1 is negative 1 times 5, which is negative 5; the wrong answer gives negative 10. Exhibit four happens to agree with the correct answer at x equals 1, so use x equals 2 there: the original gives 35, the correct 12x plus 11 gives 35, and the partial flip gives 27. A check costs twenty seconds; losing the question costs the marks. Layout for marks: brackets rewritten as two brackets when squared, four products shown with signs, like terms collected on a fresh line, second expansion kept in its bracket until the minus is distributed. The questions for this section are with you now: a mixed expansion with a minus in front, the four exhibits, and the substitution check that exposes each one.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same three patterns again through two bags of fruit that all have to meet, a square that is a real square, and a check that takes twenty seconds — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: Every Term Meets Every Term

Picture two bags. Bag one holds x and 3. Bag two holds x and 5. Multiplying the bags means every item in bag one meets every item in bag two: two items times two items is four meetings. x meets x: x squared. x meets 5: 5x. 3 meets x: 3x. 3 meets 5: 15. Four results written down with their signs. Then sort the fruit: 5x and 3x are the same fruit, 8x. Answer: x squared plus 8x plus 15.

Draw the grid if you prefer: a square cut into four cells, bag one's items along the top, bag two's items down the side, each cell filled with its product. A full grid has four full cells. Two full cells and two empty ones means somebody skipped two meetings, and that is the most common slip: multiplying the firsts and the lasts and forgetting the two in the middle.

Minus signs travel with their item. Bag one holds 2x and negative 3. Bag two holds x and 4. The meetings: 2x squared; 8x; negative 3x; negative 12. Sort: 8x and negative 3x make 5x. Answer: 2x squared plus 5x minus 12. Not sure? Let x be 1. The bags become negative 1 and 5, product negative 5. The answer: 2 plus 5 minus 12 is negative 5. Match.

The grid is not a baby method. It is the layout a marker can follow, and it is the same layout that makes the next lesson easy, because factorising is just filling in a grid from the outside in. Your questions on this section are ready: four meetings, the grid with every cell filled, and the minus signs that travel with their items.

## Subtopic: The Middle Term Is Not Zero

Squaring a bracket is the place everyone wants a shortcut, and the shortcut is wrong. The bracket x plus 5, all squared, is NOT x squared plus 25. Test it: x equals 1 makes the bracket 6, and 6 squared is 36. x squared plus 25 gives 26. Ten missing. Where did the 10 go?

The little 2 means the bracket times itself: two identical bags, each holding x and 5. Four meetings: x squared; 5x; 5x; 25. The two middle meetings are the same, so they stack: 10x. Answer: x squared plus 10x plus 25. At x equals 1 that is 1 plus 10 plus 25, which is 36. Found the 10.

Think of a real square floor tile, side x plus 5. Cut it with one vertical line and one horizontal line at the 5 mark. You get a big x by x square, a small 5 by 5 square, and two rectangles each x by 5. The two rectangles are the middle term, and you can see them. Leaving them out is leaving floor uncovered.

With a minus: the bracket x minus 4, all squared. Two bags each holding x and negative 4. x squared; negative 4x; negative 4x; positive 16, because negative times negative is positive. Answer: x squared minus 8x plus 16. The last term of a squared bracket is always positive. If yours is negative, you multiplied negative 4 by negative 4 and got a negative, which is the integer rule going wrong, not the algebra.

The rhythm for any squared bracket: first squared, double the product, last squared. For 2x plus 5: 4x squared, 20x, 25. Two identical bags, four meetings, middle stacks. The questions for this section: the missing middle, the tile picture, and why the last term is always positive.

## Subtopic: Two Terms Vanish, Then Check Everything

One special pair of bags makes the middle disappear. Bag one holds x and 7; bag two holds x and negative 7. The meetings: x squared; negative 7x; plus 7x; negative 49. The two middle meetings are the same size with opposite signs, and they cancel. Answer: x squared minus 49. Two terms only. Same bags except for one sign, and the middle always cancels. Remember this one, because next lesson you will be given x squared minus 49 and asked to find the bags, and the answer is this pair.

Now mixed questions. The bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5, minus the bracket x minus 2, all squared. Do each product on its own line. First: x squared plus 8x plus 15. Second: x squared minus 4x plus 4, and keep it in a bag, because a minus is waiting outside it. Minus the whole bag flips everything: minus x squared plus 4x minus 4. Sort: the x squared terms cancel; 8x plus 4x is 12x; 15 minus 4 is 11. Answer: 12x plus 11.

The check that protects every mark: let x be 1 in the original and in your answer. Original: 4 times 6 is 24, minus negative 1 squared, which is 1, gives 23. Answer: 12 plus 11 is 23. Match. If they do not match, the error is in one of three places: a missed meeting, a squared bracket with no middle, or a minus that flipped only the first sign. Look at those three in order, and you will find it.

Four lines for every question: write a squared bracket as two brackets, show four meetings with signs, keep the second product in a bag until the minus is dealt with, sort on a fresh line. Then the twenty-second check. The final questions of the lesson are with you now: the vanishing middle, the second product kept in its bag, and the twenty-second check.
