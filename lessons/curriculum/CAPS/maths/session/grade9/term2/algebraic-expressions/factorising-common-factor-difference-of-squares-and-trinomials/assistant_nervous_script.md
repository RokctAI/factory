Hey — if factorising felt like three different topics with their own rules, here is the calm version: it is one habit with three shapes, and expanding checks all of them.

The habit: first ask what every term shares, and pull it out to the front. 6x squared plus 9x shares 3x, so it is 3x times the bracket 2x plus 3. Pull out everything shared, not just the number.

Then count the terms left in the bracket. Two terms, a minus in the middle, both squares: that is a square minus a square, and it splits into a plus bracket and a minus bracket. x squared minus 49 is the bracket x plus 7 times the bracket x minus 7. A plus in the middle does not factorise; leave it.

Three terms: play the two-number game. Multiply to the end number, add to the middle number. x squared plus 8x plus 15 wants 3 and 5. If the end number is negative, the two numbers have different signs and you look for a difference instead.

Finally, multiply your brackets back out. If you get the original, you are right. If you do not, you have found your own mistake before the marker does.

Shared part out, count the terms, two-number game, multiply back. You've got this!
