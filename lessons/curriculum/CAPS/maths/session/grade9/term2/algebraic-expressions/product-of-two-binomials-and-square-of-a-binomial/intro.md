Today we multiply a binomial by a binomial: every term of the first bracket meets every term of the second, giving four products that usually collect to three terms. The anchor expansion is the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5, which becomes x squared plus 8x plus 15, and the grid or area diagram shows why. We then study the square of a binomial — the bracket 2x plus 5, all squared, is 4x squared plus 20x plus 25, with a middle term that must never be left out — and the sum-times-difference product whose middle term vanishes, giving x squared minus 49. Everything is checked by substituting x equals 1, and the patterns you see today are exactly what next lesson's factorising reverses.
