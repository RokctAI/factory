# Part 1 — Expert

This session multiplies integers and monomials by polynomials, and divides polynomials by integers and monomials. Both operations are the distributive property: a times the quantity b plus c equals a b plus a c, and the quantity b plus c, over a, equals b over a plus c over a. The anchor product is 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4, which is 6x cubed minus 2x squared plus 8x. The anchor quotient is 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x, all over 4x, which is 3x squared minus 2x plus 1. Every term inside the bracket, or above the bar, receives the operation, and that completeness is what is marked.

## Subtopic: Multiplying a Polynomial by an Integer

An integer in front of a bracket multiplies every term inside. 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5 is 3 times 2x, which is 6x, and 3 times negative 5, which is negative 15: 6x minus 15. The 3 reaches both terms. Writing 6x minus 5 — the multiplier applied to the first term only — is the error this lesson exists to remove.

With three terms the pattern continues. 4 times the bracket x squared plus 3x minus 2 is 4x squared plus 12x minus 8. Each term is multiplied; no term is combined with another, because x squared, x and the constant are unlike.

A negative integer flips every sign as it multiplies. Negative 2 times the bracket x squared minus 3x plus 4: negative 2 times x squared is negative 2x squared; negative 2 times negative 3x is positive 6x; negative 2 times 4 is negative 8. Result: negative 2x squared plus 6x minus 8. The signs inside have each been reversed, because each has been multiplied by a negative.

The bare minus is negative 1. Minus the bracket a minus b is negative 1 times a plus negative 1 times negative b, which is negative a plus b. This is the same rule as last lesson's minus before a bracket, seen now as a multiplication.

After distributing, collect like terms if any exist. 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5, plus 2 times the bracket x plus 7: 6x minus 15 plus 2x plus 14, which is 8x minus 1. 5 times the bracket x minus 2, minus 3 times the bracket x minus 4: 5x minus 10 minus 3x plus 12, which is 2x plus 2 — note that negative 3 times negative 4 is positive 12.

Check by substitution. At x equals 1, 5 times the bracket 1 minus 2, minus 3 times the bracket 1 minus 4, is negative 5 plus 9, which is 4; and 2x plus 2 at 1 is 4. Agreed.

In context, distributing is how a formula is rearranged. The perimeter of a rectangle, 2 times the bracket l plus b, is 2l plus 2b — two lengths and two breadths — and the two forms describe the same perimeter.

The questions for this section are with you now: the multiplier reaching every term, the negative that flips every sign, and collecting after distributing.

## Subtopic: Multiplying a Polynomial by a Monomial

A monomial multiplier does the same thing, with the exponent laws handling the variables. The anchor: 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4. 2x times 3x squared is 6x cubed — coefficients 2 times 3, exponents 1 plus 2. 2x times negative x is negative 2x squared. 2x times 4 is 8x. Result: 6x cubed minus 2x squared plus 8x. Three terms in, three terms out, each one of a different degree.

With two variables: negative 3ab times the bracket 2a minus 5b. Negative 3ab times 2a is negative 6a squared b. Negative 3ab times negative 5b is positive 15ab squared. Result: negative 6a squared b plus 15ab squared.

A monomial with a higher power: x squared times the bracket x cubed minus 2x plus 1 is x to the power 5 minus 2x cubed plus x squared. The exponent on the multiplier adds to the exponent of each term, and the constant 1 becomes x squared.

Combining two distributions: 2x times the bracket x plus 3, minus x times the bracket x minus 1. First: 2x squared plus 6x. Second: minus the quantity x squared minus x, which is minus x squared plus x. Together: 2x squared plus 6x minus x squared plus x, which is x squared plus 7x. The negative in front of the second product must reach both of its terms.

Check at x equals 2: 2 times 2 times 5 is 20; 2 times 1 is 2; 20 minus 2 is 18; and x squared plus 7x at 2 is 4 plus 14, which is 18. Agreed.

The geometric meaning: a rectangle of width 2x and length 3x squared minus x plus 4 can be cut into three rectangles of widths 2x and lengths 3x squared, x and 4; the areas 6x cubed, 2x squared and 8x — with the middle one subtracted because its length was negative in the expression — add to the total area. The area model is the picture behind the distributive property and will carry the product of two binomials next lesson.

Take this section's questions now: coefficients multiply and exponents add term by term, two variables, and two distributions combined.

## Subtopic: Dividing a Polynomial by an Integer or a Monomial

Division by an integer: every term above the bar is divided. 6x squared minus 9x, all over 3, is 6x squared over 3 minus 9x over 3, which is 2x squared minus 3x. Dividing the first term only — writing 2x squared minus 9x — is the mirror image of the multiplication error and is marked the same way.

Check: at x equals 1, the numerator is 6 minus 9, which is negative 3; over 3 is negative 1. The answer at 1 is 2 minus 3, which is negative 1. Agreed.

Division by a monomial: each term's coefficient is divided and each term's exponents are reduced. The anchor: 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x, all over 4x. 12x cubed over 4x is 3x squared. Negative 8x squared over 4x is negative 2x. 4x over 4x is 1. Result: 3x squared minus 2x plus 1. The last term is 1, not 0 and not nothing: a term divided by itself is 1, and it must be written.

With two variables and a negative divisor: 10a squared b minus 15ab squared, all over negative 5ab. 10a squared b over negative 5ab is negative 2a. Negative 15ab squared over negative 5ab is positive 3b. Result: negative 2a plus 3b.

When a term does not divide exactly, the result has a fraction or a variable in the denominator. 6x squared plus 4x plus 3, all over 2x, is 3x plus 2 plus 3 over 2x. In this course such results are left in that form; they are correct, simply not polynomials.

Division undoes multiplication, and that gives a check that needs no substitution: multiply the answer by the divisor and the original must return. 4x times the bracket 3x squared minus 2x plus 1 is 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x. Yes.

This section's questions are available now: every term divided, the term that becomes 1, and the multiplication check.

## Subtopic: Checking by Substitution and the Error Museum

Two checks are available for every answer in this lesson. The substitution check: choose an easy value, evaluate the original and the answer separately, and compare. The reverse check: for a division, multiply back by the divisor; for a multiplication, divide back by the multiplier. The substitution check is universal; the reverse check is faster when the expression is short.

Substitution values: 1 is easiest but hides errors in coefficients of x — 2x and 2x squared both give 2 at x equals 1 — so when exponents are involved use x equals 2 as well. Never use a value that makes a divisor zero; for a divisor of 4x, avoid 0.

The error museum for this topic has six exhibits. One: 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5 written as 6x minus 5 — the multiplier reached the first term only. Two: negative 2 times the bracket x squared minus 3x plus 4 written as negative 2x squared minus 6x minus 8 — the negative multiplied the magnitudes but did not flip the signs. Three: 6x plus 3, all over 3, written as 2x plus 3 — the divisor reached the first term only; the correct result is 2x plus 1. Four: 4x over 4x written as 0, or dropped — a term divided by itself is 1. Five: 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4 written as 6x squared minus 2x plus 8 — the exponents were not added. Six: like terms combined inside the bracket before distributing when they were unlike, or unlike terms combined after.

Layout for marks: write the distribution with every product or quotient shown as a separate term on the first line, with signs resolved; collect like terms on the second line if any exist; write the result in descending powers on the third; write the check in the margin. Markers award the method mark for the first line even when a later slip costs the answer, so the first line must show every term.

A final word on why this matters next lesson: the product of two binomials is two distributions in a row — each term of the first bracket multiplies the whole second bracket — and factorising is this lesson run backwards. Distributing completely, every term and every sign, is the skill both of them rest on.

The questions for this section are ready: choosing a substitution value, the reverse check, and the six exhibits of the museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same operations again through sharing sweets into every bag, a minus sign that flips everything it touches, and dividing that has to share too — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: Sharing Out the Multiplier

Picture a bracket as a row of bags, one bag per term. A number in front of the bracket is a delivery: it goes into EVERY bag, not just the first. 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5 means three of everything in there: three lots of 2x is 6x, three lots of negative 5 is negative 15. 6x minus 15. If the 3 reaches the first bag only and write 6x minus 5, the second bag was short-changed, and the marker notices.

The same with a letter in front. 2x times the bracket 3x squared minus x plus 4: 2x goes to each bag. 2x times 3x squared is 6x cubed — numbers times, little numbers add. 2x times negative x is negative 2x squared. 2x times 4 is 8x. Three bags, three deliveries, 6x cubed minus 2x squared plus 8x. Nothing combines afterwards, because the three results are different fruit.

Check with a number. x equals 1: the bracket is 3 minus 1 plus 4, which is 6, times 2 is 12. Your answer: 6 minus 2 plus 8 is 12. Match.

Why does sharing out work? It is the same reason 3 times 25 is 3 times 20 plus 3 times 5: three lots of a total is three lots of each part. Last term you called it the distributive property. Now the parts have letters in them, and nothing else has changed.

Two deliveries in one problem: 3 times the bracket 2x minus 5, plus 2 times the bracket x plus 7. Deliver the 3: 6x minus 15. Deliver the 2: 2x plus 14. Then sort the fruit: 8x minus 1.

Keep the row of bags in mind and try the questions for this part: every bag gets the delivery, numbers times and little numbers add, and sorting afterwards.

## Subtopic: The Minus Sign Flips Everything

A negative delivery flips the sign of every bag it visits. Negative 2 times the bracket x squared minus 3x plus 4: negative 2 times x squared is negative 2x squared; negative 2 times negative 3x is POSITIVE 6x, because two negatives make a positive; negative 2 times 4 is negative 8. Negative 2x squared plus 6x minus 8. Look at the middle: it was minus, now it is plus. Every sign turned over.

A bare minus in front of a bracket is a delivery of negative 1, so it flips everything and changes nothing else. Minus the bracket a minus b is negative a plus b. That is last lesson's bag rule wearing its multiplication hat.

The mistake to watch for is multiplying the sizes but leaving the signs alone: writing negative 2x squared minus 6x minus 8. Check it with x equals 1. The bracket is 1 minus 3 plus 4, which is 2, times negative 2 is negative 4. The wrong answer gives negative 2 minus 6 minus 8, which is negative 16. The right one gives negative 2 plus 6 minus 8, which is negative 4. The check catches it instantly.

Negatives also lurk between brackets. 5 times the bracket x minus 2, minus 3 times the bracket x minus 4: the first delivery gives 5x minus 10; the second delivery is negative 3, and negative 3 times negative 4 is plus 12, so negative 3x plus 12. Sort: 2x plus 2. Or 2x times the bracket x plus 3, minus x times the bracket x minus 1: 2x squared plus 6x, then minus x squared plus x, sorted to x squared plus 7x.

A rule of thumb: when the delivery is negative, say "flip" out loud as you visit each bag. Minus times plus, flip to minus. Minus times minus, flip to plus. Then check with a number.

Hold the flipping delivery in mind and try this part's questions: the negative multiplier, the bare minus, and the check that exposes an unflipped sign.

## Subtopic: Dividing Shares Too

Dividing a bracket is sharing in the other direction, and it has the same rule: every bag gets divided, not just the first. 6x squared minus 9x, all over 3, means both terms are divided by 3: 2x squared minus 3x. The 9x cannot hide behind the 6x squared and escape the division.

With a letter underneath, use "numbers divide, little numbers subtract" on every bag. 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x, all over 4x. First bag: 12 over 4 is 3, x cubed over x is x squared — 3x squared. Second bag: negative 8 over 4 is negative 2, x squared over x is x — negative 2x. Third bag: 4x over 4x is 1. Exactly 1, not nothing: a thing divided by itself is one. 3x squared minus 2x plus 1.

The reverse check is the quickest in the world here: multiply your answer back by the divisor and the original should reappear. 4x times the bracket 3x squared minus 2x plus 1 is 12x cubed minus 8x squared plus 4x. It does. If you had dropped the 1, multiplying back would give 12x cubed minus 8x squared and the missing 4x would shout at you.

Negatives underneath flip too. 10a squared b minus 15ab squared, all over negative 5ab: 10 over negative 5 is negative 2, leaving a; negative 15 over negative 5 is plus 3, leaving b. Negative 2a plus 3b.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A number or letter in front of a bracket is delivered to every bag — numbers times, little numbers add. A negative delivery flips every sign it touches. Dividing shares out too — numbers divide, little numbers subtract — and a term over itself is 1. Check by plugging in a number, or by multiplying back. And remember the classic: 6x plus 3 over 3 is 2x plus 1, never 2x plus 3.

Hold the row of bags, the flipping delivery and the sharing division, and take the final questions of the lesson: dividing every term, the term that becomes 1, and multiplying back to check.
