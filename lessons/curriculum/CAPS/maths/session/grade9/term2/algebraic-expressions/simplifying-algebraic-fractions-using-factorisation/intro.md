Today factorising earns its keep. An algebraic fraction is simplified by factorising its numerator and denominator and cancelling the factors they share, and the single rule that governs the lesson is that you cancel factors, never terms. The anchor is x squared plus 8x plus 15, all over x plus 3, which factorises to the bracket x plus 3 times the bracket x plus 5 over the bracket x plus 3 and simplifies to x plus 5. We then multiply, divide, add and subtract algebraic fractions using the rules you already know for numerical fractions: factorise before cancelling, flip the second fraction when dividing, and find the lowest common denominator before adding, giving results such as 2 over x and the bracket x plus 10 over 12. Every answer is checked by substituting a value.
