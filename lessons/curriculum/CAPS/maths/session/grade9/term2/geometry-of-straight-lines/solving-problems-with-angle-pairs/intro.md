Today last lesson's angle facts become a method. A geometry problem gives a figure with a few angles marked, as numbers or as expressions, and asks for the rest; the solution is a chain in which each new angle is found from known ones by one named relationship with its reason in brackets. We solve intersecting-line problems such as 2x and x plus 30 on a straight line giving angles of 100 and 80, parallel-line problems where corresponding angles 3x minus 15 and 2x plus 25 give 105 and co-interior angles 2x plus 10 and x plus 20 give 110 and 70, and a two-transversal figure in which alternate angles and a straight line show that the angles of a triangle add to 180. We also run the logic backwards to prove lines parallel, or not.
