Today we move from algebra to geometry, and the algebra comes along. The geometry of straight lines rests on a handful of angle facts: angles on a straight line add to 180 degrees, angles around a point add to 360, perpendicular lines make 90, vertically opposite angles are equal, and when parallel lines are cut by a transversal the corresponding angles are equal, the alternate angles are equal and the co-interior angles add to 180. Each fact has a name, each name is the reason written in brackets after every statement, and the examiner marks the reason as strictly as the number. We find unknowns such as x when x and 2x plus 30 lie on a straight line, and we work round a parallel-line figure from one given angle of 125 degrees.
