Today we draw. We revise the Cartesian plane, its four quadrants and the sign pattern that tells you where a point must land, and we plot ordered pairs accurately: across for x first, then up or down for y, so that 3 semicolon 2 and negative 2 semicolon 4 and 4 semicolon negative 2 each find their own quadrant. We sketch global graphs from stories such as a bath that fills for 10 minutes, is sat in for 20 and drains in 5, matching each section to increasing, constant or decreasing and making the drain steeper than the fill. And we draw from tables, plotting every point before deciding whether to join with a ruler, a smooth curve or not at all. Uniform scales, labelled axes and precise marks are where the accuracy marks live.
