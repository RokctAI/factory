Hey — if the grid felt like a lot of rules today, here is the calm version: the grid is a building, and plotting is just following directions.

The x-axis is the hallway, the y-axis is the lift, and the origin is the front door. A pair is directions: hall first, then lift. 3 semicolon 2 is three right, two up. Negatives change direction: negative 2 semicolon 4 is two left, four up. Before you mark anything, look at the two signs and ask which corner the point belongs in. Right-and-up, left-and-up, left-and-down, right-and-down. Wrong corner means you misread a sign.

Mark a small sharp cross and write its name next to it.

For a story graph, label both axes with units, then draw one chapter at a time: up for increasing, flat for constant, down for decreasing, straight when steady and smooth when not. The bath fills, sits flat, then drains steeper than it filled.

For a table, plot every dot before you join anything. Then decide: ruler if the dots line up, smooth hand if they bend, no join at all if the thing along the bottom comes in whole pieces.

And keep the scale even: if one block is 5 at the bottom, it is 5 all the way up.

Hall then lift, chapter by chapter, dots then decide. You've got this!
