Today the straight line goes both ways. Given an equation such as y equals 2x minus 1, we draw its graph three ways: a table of three points, with the third as a check; the two intercepts, where the line crosses the axes; and the gradient-intercept method, starting at c on the y-axis and stepping the gradient m as a staircase. Given a drawn line, such as the one through 0 semicolon negative 3 and 3 semicolon 3, we recover its equation by reading c from the y-axis and calculating m as rise over run, giving y equals 2x minus 3, and we verify with a third point. Horizontal lines are y equals a number, vertical lines are x equals a number, and when the y-intercept is not visible, c comes from substituting a known point.
