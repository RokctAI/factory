### subtopic_1 — The Cartesian Plane and Its Quadrants
**Assistant:** How does knowing the quadrant help me plot a point correctly?

**Tutor:** The quadrant is a check that costs nothing and catches the most common plotting error, a misread sign. The two signs of an ordered pair fix the quadrant before any counting happens: both positive is quadrant one at the top right, negative x with positive y is quadrant two at the top left, both negative is quadrant three at the bottom left, and positive x with negative y is quadrant four at the bottom right. So when you plot negative 3 semicolon 2 you know, before moving the pencil, that it must land in the top left; if you find yourself in the bottom right, you have moved in the wrong direction for one of the coordinates. The quadrants are numbered anticlockwise starting from the top right, which is worth memorising because questions sometimes ask for the quadrant by number. Points with a zero coordinate lie on an axis and belong to no quadrant: 4 semicolon 0 sits on the x-axis and 0 semicolon negative 5 on the y-axis, which is why the intercepts from last lesson always carry a zero. The origin is the only point with both coordinates zero. The axes themselves are drawn with a ruler, labelled x and y at their positive ends, and given a uniform scale, the same number of units per block along each axis, which may differ between the two axes but never changes partway along one of them.

### subtopic_2 — Plotting Ordered Pairs
**Assistant:** Why does the horizontal movement always come before the vertical one?

**Tutor:** Because an ordered pair is written x first and y second by convention, and the x-coordinate measures horizontal position while the y-coordinate measures vertical position, so following the pair in the order it is written means moving horizontally first. Doing it the other way round for a pair such as 2 semicolon 5 places the point at 5 across and 2 up, which is the point 5 semicolon 2, a different point in a different place. The order is the whole content of the word ordered. The method is mechanical: from the origin, move x units along the horizontal axis, right for positive and left for negative, then y units vertically, up for positive and down for negative, and mark a small precise cross at the grid intersection, labelled with its coordinates or its letter. Negative coordinates are where errors cluster, so negative 2 semicolon 4 goes two left and four up into quadrant two, while 4 semicolon negative 2 goes four right and two down into quadrant four. Plotting a table of pairs, such as the five from y equals 2x plus 1, gives a built-in check: a linear rule produces points that lie on a straight line, so a point that sits off the line has been misplotted or miscalculated and must be rechecked before anything is drawn through it. Mirror images are a second check: 3 semicolon 2 and negative 3 semicolon 2 sit opposite each other across the y-axis.

### subtopic_3 — Drawing a Global Graph From a Story
**Assistant:** How can a sketch earn marks when it does not have numbers on every point?

**Tutor:** Because a global graph is assessed on whether its shape and its labelled key values match the story, not on plotted coordinates. The marks are for the axes being labelled with the right quantities and units, the independent variable along the bottom, for each section of the story being drawn with the right direction and shape, and for the key values that the story gives being marked in the right places and in the right proportion. For the bath that fills steadily for 10 minutes, is sat in for 20 and drains in 5, the horizontal axis is time in minutes with 10, 30 and 35 marked, the vertical axis is volume in litres, and the graph is a straight line increasing from the origin to 10 minutes, a horizontal section to 30 minutes, and a straight line decreasing to zero at 35 minutes. The draining line must be steeper than the filling line, because the same volume leaves in half the time, and a marker checks that. Curved stories are drawn as curves: a thrown ball is a smooth symmetrical hill with a rounded top rather than a pointed one, because the ball slows before turning; cooling tea is a curve that falls steeply and then flattens towards room temperature without crossing it; doubling bacteria is a curve that grows steeper. A sketch that has these features in place is a full-marks answer, and one that has the right numbers but a horizontal section drawn as a slope is not.

### subtopic_4 — Drawing From a Table, Joining and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** Why must I plot every point before joining any of them?

**Tutor:** Because the pattern of the plotted points is both the instruction for how to join them and the check that each point is right, and neither is available until all the points are down. If the points lie on a straight line, the relationship is linear and a ruler is used, extended a little past the end points with an arrowhead if the rule continues; if they bend, the relationship is non-linear and the join is a smooth freehand curve, since a ruler between points on a curve produces a pointed shape that misrepresents it, as a V would misrepresent the U of y equals x squared minus 1 with its lowest point at 0 semicolon negative 1; and if the independent variable is discrete, such as passengers at R15 each, the points are left unjoined. A point that sits off the pattern is a warning that it was miscalculated or misplotted, and that warning disappears the moment a line is drawn through it. The error museum collects what goes wrong when the order is reversed or the standards are skipped: swapped coordinates, a scale that reads 0, 5, 10, 20, 50 at equal spacing and misplaces every point, discrete points joined, a ruler used on a parabola, axes left unlabelled, and an off-pattern point drawn through unchecked. The layout that earns full accuracy marks is the same for every graph in Grade 9 and beyond: ruler-drawn axes with letters, quantities and units, a uniform scale written in, each point a small labelled cross, the joining decision made correctly, and a final glance that each point sits in the quadrant its signs demand.
