Today we measure a straight line. Every linear graph has three features an examiner can ask for by name: the y-intercept, where the line crosses the vertical axis and x is 0; the x-intercept, where it crosses the horizontal axis and y is 0; and the gradient, the rise divided by the run between any two of its points, positive for a rising line and negative for a falling one. For the anchor line y equals 2x plus 1 the y-intercept is 1, the x-intercept is negative one half and the gradient is 2, and all three sit inside the standard form y equals mx plus c. In context the y-intercept is the starting value, such as a taxi's R20 flag fee, and the gradient is the rate, such as R12 per kilometre.
