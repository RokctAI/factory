Today an equation gets a second variable. y equals 2x plus 1 describes a relationship rather than a single answer: for every x you choose there is a y that goes with it, and substituting the standard run x equals negative 2 to 2 produces the table negative 3, negative 1, 1, 3, 5, written as ordered pairs with x first and y second. We build tables for linear rules such as y equals 3x minus 2, where the differences are constant, and for non-linear rules such as y equals x squared minus 1 and y equals 24 over x, where they are not. We run the equation backwards to find x when y is given, and read a rule off a table from its jumps. These pairs are the points that next week's graphs are drawn from.
