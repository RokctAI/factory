Hey — if tables of ordered pairs felt like a lot of little numbers, here is the calm version: it is one machine, run forwards or backwards.

The rule is the machine. y equals 2x plus 1 means double, then add 1. Feed in the standard five inputs, negative 2 to 2, and write what comes out underneath: negative 3, negative 1, 1, 3, 5. Put negatives in brackets so the signs behave. Each column is a pair, input first, output second: 2 semicolon 5.

Forwards is arithmetic: given x, do the rule. Backwards is an equation: given y, undo the rule in reverse order. Rule three times then take away 2, out came 10: add 2, divide by 3, in went 4. Read the question to see which one you were given.

Squaring machines give two answers backwards, 3 and negative 3, because both square to 9.

To find a hidden rule, look at the jumps in the outputs. Same jump every time means times that number; then use any column to find the add-on. Outputs 5, 8, 11, 14 with inputs 0 to 3: jumps of 3, starts at 5, so y equals 3x plus 5.
