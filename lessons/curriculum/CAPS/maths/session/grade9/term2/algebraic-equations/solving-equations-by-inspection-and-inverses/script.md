# Part 1 — Expert

Today we return to equations with the full Term 2 toolkit behind us. An equation is a statement that two expressions are equal for some value of the variable, and solving it means finding that value. Grade 9 revises three methods: inspection, where the answer can be seen; inverses, where each operation done to x is undone in reverse order; and the laws of exponents, where the unknown sits in the exponent or x is raised to a power. We also set up equations from words, which is where the marks are lost, and we check every solution by substitution, which is where the marks are saved.

## Subtopic: Solving by Inspection

Inspection means reading the equation and seeing the answer because the arithmetic is small. x plus 7 equals 12: what number plus 7 is 12? Five. 3x equals 21: three times what is 21? Seven. x over 4 equals 6: what divided by 4 is 6? Twenty-four. Each is a single fact from arithmetic, and writing x equals 5 with a one-line check, 5 plus 7 is 12, earns full marks.

Inspection also works for powers, provided you know the powers from Term 1. x squared equals 49: which numbers square to 49? Seven, and also negative 7, because negative 7 times negative 7 is 49. Both solutions must be written: x equals 7 or x equals negative 7. x cubed equals negative 8: only negative 2 works, because a negative cubed stays negative and a positive cubed cannot be negative. 2 to the power x equals 32: which power of 2 is 32? The fifth, so x equals 5.

Inspection has limits. 3x minus 5 equals 16 can be seen by a strong learner, but the method does not scale to 5 times the bracket x plus 2 equals 3 times the bracket x plus 6, and an examiner who writes "show all working" will not accept a bare answer. Use inspection for one-step equations and as a check, and move to inverses the moment an equation has two or more operations.

One more thing inspection teaches: an equation can have no solution or many. x plus 3 equals x plus 5 has no solution, because no number is 2 more than itself. 2 times the bracket x plus 1 equals 2x plus 2 is true for every x, because both sides are the same expression. Both appear in Grade 10, and recognising them now is a small advantage. The questions for this section are with you now: one-step equations by sight, both roots of x squared equals 49, and the limits of inspection.

## Subtopic: Additive and Multiplicative Inverses

An inverse undoes an operation. The additive inverse of adding 5 is adding negative 5, which is subtracting 5. The multiplicative inverse of multiplying by 3 is multiplying by one third, which is dividing by 3. Solving by inverses means doing the same inverse to both sides of the equation, so the equation stays balanced, until x is alone.

The anchor: 3x minus 5 equals 16. Two things were done to x: multiplied by 3, then 5 was subtracted. Undo them in reverse order. Add 5 to both sides: 3x equals 21. Divide both sides by 3: x equals 7. Check: 3 times 7 is 21, minus 5 is 16. The reverse order matters: undoing the multiplication first means dividing 16 and negative 5 by 3 and dealing with fractions, which is legal but invites errors.

x over 4 plus 3 equals 8. Subtract 3 from both sides: x over 4 equals 5. Multiply both sides by 4: x equals 20. Check: 20 over 4 is 5, plus 3 is 8.

Negative coefficients follow the integer rules. 10 minus 2x equals 4. Subtract 10 from both sides: negative 2x equals negative 6. Divide both sides by negative 2: x equals 3. Check: 10 minus 6 is 4. Learners who divide by 2 instead of negative 2 get negative 3, and the check exposes it: 10 minus negative 6 is 16, not 4.

Layout for marks: one operation per line, the same operation on both sides, the equals signs aligned under each other, and the check written at the end. A marker awards method marks for correct inverse steps even when an arithmetic slip spoils the final value, but only if the steps are visible. Your questions on this section: the inverse of each operation, undoing in reverse order, and dividing by a negative.

## Subtopic: Equations With Brackets, Fractions and Exponents

Brackets are expanded first, using the distributive law from this term. 2 times the bracket x minus 3 equals x plus 5. Expand: 2x minus 6 equals x plus 5. Collect the x terms on one side by subtracting x from both sides: x minus 6 equals 5. Add 6: x equals 11. Check: 2 times 8 is 16, and 11 plus 5 is 16.

Brackets on both sides: 5 times the bracket x plus 2 equals 3 times the bracket x plus 6. Expand: 5x plus 10 equals 3x plus 18. Subtract 3x: 2x plus 10 equals 18. Subtract 10: 2x equals 8. Divide by 2: x equals 4. Check: 5 times 6 is 30, and 3 times 10 is 30.

Fractions are cleared by multiplying every term by the lowest common denominator. x over 2 minus x over 3 equals 2. The LCD is 6. Multiply every term by 6: 3x minus 2x equals 12, so x equals 12. Check: 6 minus 4 is 2. Multiplying only the fraction terms and forgetting the 2 on the right is the standard slip.

Equations with x on both sides are the Grade 9 step up from Grade 8. The strategy is to move all the x terms to one side and all the numbers to the other, by adding or subtracting the same term from both sides. It does not matter which side the x terms end up on, but choosing the side with the larger coefficient keeps the coefficient positive and avoids a division by a negative at the end. Writing each move as its own line is what the method marks are for.

When the unknown is in the exponent, write both sides as powers of the same base and equate the exponents. 3 to the power of the bracket x plus 1 equals 81. 81 is 3 to the fourth. So x plus 1 equals 4, and x equals 3. 2 to the power x equals one eighth: one eighth is 2 to the negative 3, from the negative exponents lesson, so x equals negative 3. When x is raised to a power, take the root, remembering both signs for an even power: x squared equals 49 gives x equals 7 or negative 7, while x cubed equals 27 gives only x equals 3. The questions for this section are ready: expand first, clear fractions with the LCD, and same base then equate exponents.

## Subtopic: Setting Up Equations From Words and the Error Museum

Word problems are solved in four steps: choose a letter and say what it stands for, write the equation, solve it, answer the question in words with units. A taxi charges a flat fee of R20 plus R12 per kilometre, and a trip costs R104. Let k be the number of kilometres. 12k plus 20 equals 104. Subtract 20: 12k equals 84. Divide by 12: k equals 7. The trip was 7 km. Check: 7 times 12 is 84, plus 20 is 104.

Sipho is 3 years older than twice his sister's age, and their ages add to 27. Let s be the sister's age; Sipho is 2s plus 3. Then s plus 2s plus 3 equals 27, so 3s plus 3 equals 27, 3s equals 24, s equals 8. The sister is 8 and Sipho is 19. Check: 8 plus 19 is 27, and 19 is 3 more than 16.

A rectangle's length is 3 cm more than its width and its perimeter is 26 cm. Let the width be w; the length is w plus 3. Perimeter: 2 times the bracket w plus w plus 3 equals 26, so 4w plus 6 equals 26, 4w equals 20, w equals 5. Width 5 cm, length 8 cm. Check: 5 plus 8 plus 5 plus 8 is 26.

The error museum. Exhibit one: 3x minus 5 equals 16 solved by subtracting 5, giving 3x equals 11; the inverse of subtracting 5 is adding 5. Exhibit two: 10 minus 2x equals 4 solved to x equals 3 correctly but written as negative 3 after dividing by positive 2. Exhibit three: 2 times the bracket x minus 3 expanded as 2x minus 3, the distributive law applied to the first term only. Exhibit four: x squared equals 49 answered as x equals 7 only; the negative root is dropped and a mark with it. Exhibit five, in word problems: solving for s equals 8 and answering that Sipho is 8; the question asked for Sipho, and the letter stood for the sister.

Every exhibit is caught by substituting the answer back into the original equation, or back into the original story. A solution that does not satisfy the equation is not a solution, whatever the working looked like. The questions for this section are with you now: the four steps for a word problem, the letter and what it stands for, and the five exhibits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same equation-solving again through a locked box, a row of undo buttons, and a story that has to become a sentence with an equals sign — plain words, same rules.

## Subtopic: See It When You Can

An equation is a locked box with x inside, and the lock is the arithmetic. Some locks you can just see through. x plus 7 is 12: what plus 7 makes 12? Five. 3x is 21: three of something is 21? Seven. x over 4 is 6: something cut into 4 gives 6? Twenty-four. If you can see it, write x equals the number and show the check in one line: 5 plus 7 is 12. That is full marks.

Powers you can see too, if you know your Term 1 squares and cubes. x squared is 49: 7 times 7 is 49. But also negative 7 times negative 7 is 49, so there are TWO answers, and you must write both. x cubed is negative 8: negative 2 times negative 2 times negative 2 is negative 8, and only negative 2 works because a positive number cubed is never negative. 2 to the power x is 32: count up the doubles, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, five of them, so x is 5.

When the lock has two or more steps, seeing through it gets unreliable, and a marker who asks for working will not accept a bare number. So use seeing for one-step locks, and for everything else use undo buttons, which come next. And always use seeing as a check at the end: once you have an answer, put it back in the box and see if the equation is true.

Two odd locks worth knowing. x plus 3 equals x plus 5 has no answer, because nothing is 2 more than itself. 2 times the bracket x plus 1 equals 2x plus 2 is true for every x, because both sides are the same thing written twice. Your questions on this section are ready: one-step locks by sight, the two answers to a square, and seeing as a check.

## Subtopic: Undo Buttons in Reverse Order

Think of an equation as a list of things that were done to x. 3x minus 5 equals 16: x was multiplied by 3, then 5 was taken off, and the result was 16. To get x back, press the undo buttons in REVERSE order, like taking off shoes before socks. The last thing done was take off 5, so undo it first: add 5 to both sides, giving 3x equals 21. The first thing done was times 3, so undo it last: divide both sides by 3, giving x equals 7. Check: 3 times 7 is 21, take off 5 is 16.

Both sides, always. The equation is a scale that balances. Whatever you do to one side you do to the other, or it tips and the answer is wrong.

x over 4 plus 3 equals 8: x was cut into 4, then 3 was added. Undo the add 3: x over 4 equals 5. Undo the cut into 4: multiply by 4, x equals 20. Check: 20 cut into 4 is 5, plus 3 is 8.

Minus x is the one that trips people. 10 minus 2x equals 4. Take 10 off both sides: negative 2x equals negative 6. Now undo times negative 2 by dividing by negative 2: x equals 3. If you divide by 2 instead, you get negative 3, and the check catches it: 10 minus 2 times negative 3 is 16, not 4.

Letters on both sides: get them together first. Take the smaller lot of x's off both sides, so what is left has a plus sign in front of it, and then undo the rest as usual.

Brackets: open them first. 2 times the bracket x minus 3 equals x plus 5 becomes 2x minus 6 equals x plus 5. Get the x's together by taking x off both sides: x minus 6 equals 5. Add 6: x equals 11. Fractions: multiply everything by the number both bottoms fit into. x over 2 minus x over 3 equals 2, times 6 everywhere: 3x minus 2x equals 12, so x is 12. Everything, including the 2 on the right. The questions for this section: reverse order, both sides, dividing by a negative, and opening brackets first.

## Subtopic: Turn the Story Into a Sentence With an Equals Sign

A word problem is a story with a number hidden in it. Four steps turn it into a lock you can open. Step one: pick a letter and write down in words what it stands for. Step two: write the story as a sentence with an equals sign. Step three: press the undo buttons. Step four: answer the actual question, with units.

A taxi charges R20 to get in and R12 for every kilometre, and the trip costs R104. Let k be the kilometres. The story says: 12 times k, plus 20, equals 104. Undo the plus 20: 12k equals 84. Undo the times 12: k equals 7. Answer: the trip was 7 km. Check it against the story: 7 kilometres at R12 is R84, plus R20 is R104.

Sipho is 3 years older than twice his sister's age, and their ages add up to 27. Let s be the sister's age, so Sipho is 2s plus 3. Together: s plus 2s plus 3 equals 27. Collect: 3s plus 3 equals 27. Undo: 3s equals 24, s equals 8. Now step four, carefully: the question might ask for Sipho, who is 2 times 8 plus 3, which is 19. Writing 8 when the question wanted Sipho loses the mark even though every step was right.

A rectangle's length is 3 cm more than its width and the perimeter is 26 cm. Let the width be w, length w plus 3. Perimeter is all four sides: w plus w plus 3 plus w plus w plus 3, which is 4w plus 6, equals 26. Undo: 4w equals 20, w equals 5. Width 5 cm, length 8 cm. Check: 5 plus 8 plus 5 plus 8 is 26.

Then the exponent locks: 3 to the power of the bracket x plus 1 equals 81. Write 81 as 3 to the fourth, so x plus 1 is 4 and x is 3. Same base, then match the little numbers. Every answer in this lesson goes back into the box to be checked. The final questions of the lesson are with you now: pick a letter and say what it means, write the sentence with an equals sign, and answer the question that was actually asked.
