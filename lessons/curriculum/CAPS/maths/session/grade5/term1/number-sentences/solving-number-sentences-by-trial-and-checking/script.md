# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving and completing number sentences by inspection, by trial and improvement, and by checking with substitution. The anchor is a farm near Bethlehem in the Free State that packs eggs in trays of 30, and a school library in Polokwane with 4 300 books, some on the shelves and some on the trolley. The governing idea is that the equals sign means both sides have the same value, so the box must be the number that makes the sentence true, and substituting the answer back into the sentence proves it. Solving by inspection, solving by trial and improvement, using inverse operations, and checking by substitution are examinable.

## Subtopic: Solving by Inspection

Inspection means looking at the sentence and seeing the answer from facts you already know. Box plus 7 equals 15: you know 8 plus 7 is 15, so the box is 8. Box divided by 8 equals 9: you know 9 times 8 is 72, so the box is 72. 1 000 minus box equals 750: 750 and 250 make 1 000, so the box is 250.

Known facts stretch to bigger numbers. 60 times box equals 360. You know 6 times 6 is 36, and 60 is ten times 6, so 60 times 6 is 360 and the box is 6. On the egg farm, box trays of 30 hold 300 eggs: 30 times 10 is 300, so the box is 10.

The equals sign is not a signal that the answer comes next. It means the two sides have the same value, like a balanced scale. 25 plus 15 equals box plus 30. The left side is 40, so the right side must also be 40. Box plus 30 equals 40 gives a box of 10, not 40.

Some sentences can be solved by inspection because of a special number. 45 plus box equals 45: adding has changed nothing, so the box is 0. 37 times box equals 37: multiplying has changed nothing, so the box is 1. You will meet these two special numbers again in the next lesson.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving with facts you know, the equals sign as a balance, and the special numbers 0 and 1.

## Subtopic: Trial and Improvement

When the answer cannot be seen straight away, try a number, check it and improve the next try. 23 times box equals 552. Try 20: 23 times 20 is 460, which is too small. Try 25: 23 times 25 is 575, which is too big. The box is between 20 and 25, and closer to 25. Try 24: 23 times 24 is 552. The box is 24.

Each try gives a clue. If the result is too small, the next try must be bigger; if it is too big, the next try must be smaller. Keeping a small table of tries, with the result and too big or too small, stops you from repeating a try or going the wrong way.

On the egg farm, box trays of 30 hold 840 eggs. Try 20 trays: 600 eggs, too few. Try 30 trays: 900 eggs, too many. Try 28 trays: 30 times 28 is 840. The box is 28. A sensible first try saves time: 840 is a bit less than 900, which is 30 times 30, so start just below 30.

Trial and improvement also works when the same box appears twice. Box times box equals 196: 10 times 10 is 100, too small; 15 times 15 is 225, too big; 14 times 14 is 196. Box plus box plus 15 equals 85: two boxes make 70, so try 35. 35 plus 35 plus 15 is 85, so the box is 35.

The questions for this section are with you now: choosing a sensible first try, using too big and too small to improve, and solving sentences with the same box twice.

## Subtopic: Checking by Substitution and Inverses

An inverse operation undoes another. Subtraction undoes addition, addition undoes subtraction, division undoes multiplication and multiplication undoes division. The library has 4 300 books. 1 875 are on the shelves and the rest are on the trolley: box plus 1 875 equals 4 300. Undo adding 1 875 by subtracting it: 4 300 minus 1 875 is 2 425.

Substitution means putting your answer back into the box and checking that the sentence is true. 2 425 plus 1 875: 5 plus 5 is 10, carry 1; 2 plus 7 plus 1 is 10, carry 1; 4 plus 8 plus 1 is 13, carry 1; 2 plus 1 plus 1 is 4. The total is 4 300, so the sentence is true and the box is 2 425.

Be careful with the direction of the inverse. Box minus 368 equals 1 250: some number had 368 taken away and 1 250 was left. To get back to the start, add the 368 back: 1 250 plus 368 is 1 618. Substitute: 1 618 minus 368 is 1 250, true. Box divided by 12 equals 15: undo the division by multiplying, 15 times 12 is 180. Substitute: 180 divided by 12 is 15, true.

Substitution also catches mistakes. If Lindiwe says the box in box minus 368 equals 1 250 is 882, substitute: 882 minus 368 is 514, not 1 250. The sentence is false, so 882 is wrong. Without the check, the mistake would have gone unnoticed.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: solving box minus 368 equals 1 250 by subtracting 368 again to get 882; the inverse of subtracting is adding, so the box is 1 618. Two: guessing at random without using too big and too small; let each try point the way. Three: skipping the substitution check and handing in a wrong answer; always put the answer back in. Four: reading 25 plus 15 equals box plus 30 as if the box were the answer, and writing 40; both sides must balance, so the box is 10.

The questions for this section are with you now: using inverse operations, substituting to check, and catching wrong answers.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the egg trays and the library books — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Just Look

Some boxes you can see at once. Box plus 7 equals 15: the box is 8.

60 times box equals 360. Six sixes are 36, so 60 times 6 is 360. The box is 6.

The equals sign means both sides are worth the same. 25 plus 15 equals box plus 30. The left side is 40, so the box is 10.

Keep the balance in mind and try the questions for this part: solving by looking.

## Subtopic: Guess, Check, Improve

Box trays of 30 hold 840 eggs. Try 20: 600, too small. Try 30: 900, too big. Try 28: 840. Got it.

Too small? Go bigger. Too big? Go smaller.

23 times box equals 552. Try 20: 460, too small. Try 24: 552. The box is 24.

Hold the too big, too small rule in mind and try this part's questions: trial and improvement.

## Subtopic: Put It Back In

Box plus 1 875 equals 4 300. Undo the plus with a minus: 4 300 minus 1 875 is 2 425.

Put it back in: 2 425 plus 1 875 is 4 300. True, so the box is right.

Box minus 368 equals 1 250. Undo the minus with a plus: 1 250 plus 368 is 1 618.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The equals sign means both sides have the same value. Solve by inspection when you know the fact. Use trial and improvement when you do not, letting too big and too small guide the next try. Undo an operation with its inverse. Always substitute the answer back into the sentence to prove it is true.

Hold the put-it-back habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: inverses and substitution.
