# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the commutative, associative and distributive properties of operations with whole numbers, and the special roles of 0 in addition and 1 in multiplication. The anchor is a school hall in Kimberley being set up for prize-giving, with chairs in rows, tickets sold at R25 each and cool drinks delivered in boxes of packs. The governing idea is that these properties let you change the order, change the grouping or split a number to make a calculation easier without changing the answer, as long as you use each property only where it holds. Recognising and using the three properties, knowing that they do not hold for subtraction and division, and using 0 and 1 correctly are examinable.

## Subtopic: Swapping the Order

The commutative property says that the order of the numbers does not change the answer for addition and multiplication. The caretaker can set out 25 rows of 16 chairs or 16 rows of 25 chairs. Turn the first arrangement around and it becomes the second, so both have the same number of chairs: 400.

Swapping is useful when one order is easier. 25 times 16 is easier to think of as 16 times 25, or as four 4s of 25. Since 4 times 25 is 100, 16 times 25 is 400. For addition, start from the bigger number when you count on: 7 plus 386 is the same as 386 plus 7, which is 393, and counting on 7 is easier than counting on 386.

The property does not hold for subtraction or division. 50 chairs minus 20 chairs leaves 30, but 20 minus 50 cannot give 30, because you cannot take 50 chairs from 20. 20 divided by 4 is 5, but 4 divided by 20 is not 5; it is less than 1. Sharing 20 sweets among 4 friends is not the same as sharing 4 sweets among 20 friends.

So the rule is: swap freely for plus and times, and never swap for minus or divide. In a number sentence this matters. 1 875 plus 2 499 equals 2 499 plus 1 875 is always true. 1 875 minus 499 equals 499 minus 1 875 is never true.

The questions for this section are with you now: swapping the order for addition and multiplication, choosing the easier order, and knowing where swapping fails.

## Subtopic: Changing the Grouping

The associative property says that when you add three or more numbers, or multiply three or more numbers, you can group them in any way. Brackets show the grouping. (37 plus 48) plus 63 gives the same total as 37 plus (48 plus 63). Together with swapping, this lets you pick the friendliest pairs: 37 plus 63 makes 100, and 100 plus 48 is 148.

The cool drinks come in 5 boxes, each holding 6 packs of 4 cans. The total is 5 times 6 times 4. Group 5 and 4 first: 5 times 4 is 20, and 20 times 6 is 120 cans. Grouping 5 and 6 first gives 30 times 4, which is also 120. The friendliest grouping makes a 10, a 20 or a 100.

The same trick works with bigger numbers. 4 times 7 times 25: group 4 and 25 to make 100, then 100 times 7 is 700. Working from left to right, 4 times 7 is 28 and 28 times 25 is 700, which is correct but much harder to do in your head.

Grouping does not work for subtraction or division. (50 minus 20) minus 10 is 30 minus 10, which is 20. But if you work out 20 minus 10 first, you get 50 minus 10, which is 40. (48 divided by 4) divided by 2 is 6, but 48 divided by (4 divided by 2) is 24. When a calculation has minus or divided by, work in the order written unless brackets say otherwise.

The questions for this section are with you now: grouping to make tens and hundreds, using brackets, and knowing where regrouping fails.

## Subtopic: Splitting to Multiply, 0 and 1

The distributive property lets you split a number, multiply each part and add the results. 23 tickets are sold at R25 each. Split 23 into 20 and 3: 20 times 25 is 500 and 3 times 25 is 75, so 23 times 25 is 575. The money taken is R575. 6 times 47 is 6 times 40 plus 6 times 7, which is 240 plus 42, which is 282.

Splitting also works with subtraction. 8 boxes of 99 programmes: 8 times 99 is 8 times 100 minus 8 times 1, which is 800 minus 8, which is 792. For three-digit numbers, split by place value: 7 times 125 is 7 times 100 plus 7 times 25, which is 700 plus 175, which is 875. The key is that every part must be multiplied.

Two numbers have special properties. 0 is special in addition: adding 0 to a number leaves it unchanged, so 1 875 plus 0 is 1 875. 1 is special in multiplication: multiplying a number by 1 leaves it unchanged, so 575 times 1 is 575. In the same way, subtracting 0 and dividing by 1 change nothing.

Do not mix up the two. 575 plus 1 is 576, because adding 1 does change a number. 575 times 0 is 0, because zero groups of 575 is nothing. Only plus 0 and times 1 leave a number exactly as it was.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 50 minus 20 equals 20 minus 50; subtraction is not commutative. Two: regrouping a subtraction, so (50 minus 20) minus 10 becomes 40; work in the order written to get 20. Three: splitting 6 times 47 into 240 plus 7; every part must be multiplied, so it is 240 plus 42, which is 282. Four: thinking 575 times 0 is 575; it is adding 0 that changes nothing, while multiplying by 0 gives 0.

The questions for this section are with you now: splitting to multiply, splitting with subtraction, and the special numbers 0 and 1.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the chairs, the tickets and the cool drinks — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Swap It Round

For plus and times, the order does not matter. 25 rows of 16 chairs is the same as 16 rows of 25. Both are 400.

Start from the bigger number: 7 plus 386 is easier as 386 plus 7, which is 393.

Never swap minus or divide. 50 minus 20 is 30. 20 minus 50 is not.

Keep the swap rule in mind and try the questions for this part: swapping the order.

## Subtopic: Group It Your Way

When you add or multiply three numbers, choose which two to do first. 37 plus 48 plus 63: do 37 plus 63 first to make 100. Then 148.

5 boxes of 6 packs of 4 cans: 5 times 4 is 20, then 20 times 6 is 120 cans.

Look for pairs that make 10 or 100. 4 times 25 is 100.

Hold the friendly pairs in mind and try this part's questions: grouping.

## Subtopic: Split It, and Two Special Numbers

Split a number to multiply. 23 tickets at R25: 20 times 25 is 500, 3 times 25 is 75. Total R575.

Multiply every part. 6 times 47 is 240 plus 42, which is 282.

Plus 0 changes nothing. Times 1 changes nothing. But times 0 gives 0.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The commutative property lets you swap the order for addition and multiplication. The associative property lets you change the grouping for addition and multiplication. The distributive property lets you split a number, multiply each part and add. None of these work for subtraction or division in the same way. Adding 0 and multiplying by 1 leave a number unchanged.

Hold the split-and-add trick in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: splitting, 0 and 1.
