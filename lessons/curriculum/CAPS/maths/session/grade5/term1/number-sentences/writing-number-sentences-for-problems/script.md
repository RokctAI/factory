# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers writing number sentences to describe problem situations. The anchor is a recycling drive at a school in Mthatha where Grade 5A collected 1 245 bottle tops and Grade 5B collected 1 378, a fleet of minibus taxis that each carry 15 passengers, and Lerato, who saves R250 every month. The governing idea is that a number sentence is the story written in the language of mathematics: it shows the operation, the known numbers and a box where the unknown number belongs. Choosing the operation, placing the box where the gap in the story is, and using brackets to show the first step in a two-step problem are examinable.

## Subtopic: From Story to Number Sentence

A number sentence is a complete mathematical statement with an equals sign. 1 245 plus 1 378 equals 2 623 is a true number sentence. 1 245 plus 1 378 equals 2 500 is a false one. 1 245 plus 1 378 equals box is an open number sentence: the box stands for a number we still have to find.

To write a number sentence, first decide what is happening in the story. How many bottle tops did the two classes collect altogether? Two amounts are combined, so add: 1 245 plus 1 378 equals box. How many more did 5B collect than 5A? We are comparing, so subtract the smaller from the bigger: 1 378 minus 1 245 equals box.

Multiplication and division follow the same thinking. 24 taxis each carry 15 passengers: equal groups are being combined, so 24 times 15 equals box. 432 learners must travel in groups of 18: a total is being split into equal groups, so 432 divided by 18 equals box. Words like altogether, total, difference, left, each, per, shared equally and groups of are useful clues.

Clues are not rules, so read the whole story. Thabo has 15 more marbles than Sipho, and Sipho has 40: Thabo's marbles are 40 plus 15. But Thabo has 55 marbles, which is 15 more than Sipho: now Sipho's marbles are 55 minus 15. The words 15 more than appear in both stories, yet one needs adding and the other needs subtracting.

The questions for this section are with you now: true, false and open sentences, and choosing the operation that matches the story.

## Subtopic: Where the Box Goes

The box does not always go at the end. Put it where the gap in the story is. Lerato had some money saved. She saved R250 more, and now she has R1 750. The unknown is the starting amount, so the sentence is box plus 250 equals 1 750.

A water tank in Upington held 5 000 litres. Some water was used during the day and 3 650 litres were left. The unknown is the amount used, so 5 000 minus box equals 3 650. Apples are packed in crates of 24, and there are 360 apples altogether. The unknown is the number of crates, so box times 24 equals 360.

A story can often be written in more than one correct way. Box plus 250 equals 1 750 follows the story in order. 1 750 minus 250 equals box gives the same answer and shows how to find it. Both are correct sentences for the story. The first is a good way to describe the problem; the second is a good way to solve it.

To check a sentence, read it back as a story. Box times 24 equals 360 reads: some number of crates, each with 24 apples, gives 360 apples. If the story you read back matches the problem, the sentence is right.

The questions for this section are with you now: placing the box at the start, in the middle or at the end, and checking a sentence by reading it back.

## Subtopic: Two-Step Sentences and Brackets

Some problems need two steps. Lerato saves R250 a month for 12 months and then spends R1 200 on a bicycle. How much is left? First find her savings, then take away the bicycle. Brackets show the first step: (12 times 250) minus 1 200 equals box. The brackets are worked out first: 12 times 250 is 3 000, and 3 000 minus 1 200 is 1 800. R1 800 is left.

A shopping problem: 3 loaves of bread at R18 each and 2 litres of milk at R24 each. The total is (3 times 18) plus (2 times 24) equals box. Each bracket is worked out first: 54 and 48. Then 54 plus 48 is 102, so the total is R102.

Brackets change the meaning. 2 tickets at R45 and 1 ticket at R30 cost (2 times 45) plus 30, which is 120. But 2 times (45 plus 30) means 2 tickets of each kind, which is 150. The same numbers in a different bracket give a different story, so put the brackets around the step that really comes first.

Writing a sentence is not the same as solving it. If the question says write a number sentence, the answer must have an equals sign and a box. Writing only the answer, R1 800, does not show the sentence.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 1 245 minus 1 378 for how many more; for a difference the bigger number comes first. Two: believing that more than always means add; in the second marble story it means subtract. Three: writing just the answer when a sentence is asked for; write the sentence with its box. Four: writing a two-step sentence with no brackets, so nobody knows which step is first; bracket the first step.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing two-step sentences, using brackets to show the first step, and working them out.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the bottle tops, the taxis and Lerato's savings — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Story to Sentence

Ask what the story is doing. Putting together: add. 1 245 plus 1 378 equals box.

Finding how many more: subtract, bigger number first. 1 378 minus 1 245 equals box.

Equal groups put together: multiply. 24 taxis of 15 is 24 times 15. Sharing or grouping: divide. 432 in groups of 18 is 432 divided by 18.

Keep the four actions in mind and try the questions for this part: choosing the operation.

## Subtopic: Box the Gap

The box goes where the story has a gap. Lerato had some money, saved R250 more and now has R1 750. Box plus 250 equals 1 750.

The tank had 5 000 litres, some were used and 3 650 were left. 5 000 minus box equals 3 650.

Read your sentence back as a story. If it tells the same story, it is right.

Hold the gap in mind and try this part's questions: where the box goes.

## Subtopic: First Step in Brackets

Two steps? Put the first step in brackets. R250 for 12 months, then R1 200 spent: (12 times 250) minus 1 200 equals box.

Work out the brackets first: 3 000. Then 3 000 minus 1 200 is 1 800. R1 800 is left.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A number sentence has an equals sign. Choose the operation from what the story does, not from one word. Put the box where the missing number is in the story. For two steps, bracket the step that comes first and work it out first. Read the sentence back to check that it tells the same story.

Hold the brackets in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: two-step number sentences.
