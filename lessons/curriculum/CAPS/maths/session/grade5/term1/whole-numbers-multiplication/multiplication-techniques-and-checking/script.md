# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers techniques for multiplying and for checking multiplication: estimation, building up and breaking down numbers, doubling and halving, and multiplication and division as inverse operations. The anchor is an inter-schools sports day in Rustenburg, with 16 teams of 25 learners, 35 players each given juice worth R14, 48 taxis each carrying 15 visitors, and family tickets at R199. The governing idea is that a product can be found more easily by changing the numbers into friendlier ones without changing the answer, and that every product is checked with a second technique, usually an estimate or a division. Using any two techniques to perform and check multiplication is examinable.

## Subtopic: Doubling and Halving

If you double one number and halve the other, the product stays the same. 16 teams of 25 learners: halve 16 to get 8 and double 25 to get 50. 8 times 50 is 400. Do it again: 4 times 100 is also 400. The last step is so easy that the whole calculation can be done in your head.

It works because doubling makes the answer twice as big and halving makes it half as big, so together they cancel out. Picture 16 rows of 25 learners. Join every two rows into one long row: now there are 8 rows of 50. Nobody has moved in or out of the field, so the total is still 400.

35 players each get juice worth R14. Double 35 to get 70 and halve 14 to get 7. 70 times 7 is 490, so the juice costs R490. 125 times 24 goes even further: 250 times 12, then 500 times 6, which is 3 000.

Doubling and halving works best when one number is even, so it can be halved neatly, and the other ends in 5 or 25, so that doubling makes a 10, 50 or 100. If both numbers are odd, like 37 times 23, halving makes fractions, so choose a different technique.

The questions for this section are with you now: doubling and halving, why the product stays the same, and when to use the technique.

## Subtopic: Breaking Down, Building Up and Compensating

Breaking down splits one number into easy parts. 48 taxis each carry 15 visitors. Split 15 into 10 and 5. 48 times 10 is 480. 48 times 5 is half of 48 times 10, which is 240. 480 plus 240 is 720 visitors. Every part of 15 must be multiplied by 48.

Building up goes to a friendly number above and then takes away the extra. 15 times 48 is close to 15 times 50, which is 750. But that is 2 fifteens too many, which is 30. 750 minus 30 is 720, the same answer.

Rounding and compensating is building up with a number just below a round number. Family tickets cost R199 and 12 families buy one each. 199 is 1 less than 200. 200 times 12 is 2 400. But we used 12 extra rands, one for each ticket, so take 12 off: 2 388. The tickets cost R2 388.

Be careful with the size of the compensation. Using 200 instead of 199 adds 1 extra for every one of the 12 tickets, so the extra is 1 times 12, which is 12, not just 1. Always ask how many times the extra was counted.

The questions for this section are with you now: breaking down a number, building up to a friendly number, and rounding and compensating.

## Subtopic: Estimating and Checking by Division

Estimate first to know what size of answer to expect. 412 chairs in each section, 19 sections. Round 412 to 400 and 19 to 20: 400 times 20 is 8 000. The exact answer is 412 times 20 minus 412, which is 8 240 minus 412, which is 7 828. It is close to 8 000, so it makes sense.

Multiplication and division are inverse operations: each undoes the other. If 48 times 15 is 720, then 720 divided by 15 must be 48, and 720 divided by 48 must be 15. To check a product, divide it by one of the numbers and see if you get the other. If 125 times 24 is 3 000, then 3 000 divided by 24 should be 125.

Division checks are best done with easy facts. To check 3 000 divided by 24, ask how many 24s make 3 000: 100 twenty-fours make 2 400, and 25 more make 600, so 125 twenty-fours make 3 000. The check uses building up, a different technique from the doubling and halving that found the answer.

CAPS asks for two techniques for each calculation: one to work it out and one to check. A strong pair is a quick technique to work it out, such as doubling and halving, and an estimate or a division to check.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: doubling both numbers, so 16 times 25 becomes 32 times 50, which is 1 600; double one and halve the other to keep 400. Two: compensating by 1 instead of 12, so 199 times 12 becomes 2 399; the extra 1 was counted 12 times, so take off 12 to get 2 388. Three: breaking down only part of the calculation, so 48 times 15 becomes 480 plus 5; both parts of 15 must be multiplied by 48. Four: checking by doing the same calculation again; check with a different technique, such as 720 divided by 15.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating products, checking by division, and choosing two techniques.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the teams, the taxis and the family tickets — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Double One, Halve the Other

16 teams of 25. Halve the 16 and double the 25: 8 times 50. Again: 4 times 100. That is 400.

35 times 14: double 35, halve 14. 70 times 7 is 490.

Double one, halve the other. Never double both.

Keep the double-and-halve trick in mind and try the questions for this part: doubling and halving.

## Subtopic: Split or Round

48 times 15: split 15 into 10 and 5. 480 plus 240 is 720.

R199 is nearly R200. 200 times 12 is 2 400. Take off one 12: R2 388.

When you round up, take off the extra for every group.

Hold the split-or-round choice in mind and try this part's questions: breaking down and compensating.

## Subtopic: Guess and Undo

Guess first: 412 times 19 is about 400 times 20, which is 8 000.

Undo to check: times is undone by divide. 720 divided by 15 is 48. It matches, so 48 times 15 is 720.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Double one number and halve the other to make a friendly product. Break a number into parts and multiply every part. Round to a friendly number and take off the extra for every group. Estimate to know the size of the answer, and divide the product by one number to get the other as a check. Use two techniques every time.

Hold the guess-and-undo habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: estimating and checking by division.
