# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers multiples of two-digit whole numbers to at least 100 and factors of two-digit whole numbers to at least 100. The anchor is a school hall in Mahikeng where 48 chairs are set out in equal rows, a sports day where juice comes in packs of 12 and muffins in packs of 18, and 48 boys and 72 girls who must be split into equal teams. The governing idea is that a multiple of a number is in its times table, while a factor of a number divides into it exactly, and that factors are found in pairs. Listing multiples, finding all the factors of a number, telling multiples and factors apart, and finding common multiples and common factors are examinable.

## Subtopic: Multiples of Two-Digit Numbers

A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply it by a whole number. You find multiples by skip-counting. The multiples of 12 up to 100 are 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84 and 96. The multiples of 15 up to 100 are 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 and 90. The multiples of 25 up to 100 are 25, 50, 75 and 100.

To test whether a number is a multiple, ask whether it is in the times table. Is 84 a multiple of 12? 12 times 7 is 84, so yes. Is 50 a multiple of 15? 15 times 3 is 45 and 15 times 4 is 60, so 50 falls between them and is not a multiple of 15.

Some tests are quick. A multiple of 2 is even. A multiple of 5 ends in 0 or 5. A multiple of 10 ends in 0. A multiple of 3 has digits that add up to a multiple of 3: 72 gives 7 plus 2, which is 9, so 72 is a multiple of 3, and indeed 3 times 24 is 72.

Multiples go on for ever. There is always a next multiple: after 96 comes 108, 120 and so on. Every number is also a multiple of itself, because it is the number times 1. Juice in packs of 12 can only be bought in multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36 and so on, never 30.

The questions for this section are with you now: listing multiples by skip-counting, testing whether a number is a multiple, and quick tests for 2, 3, 5 and 10.

## Subtopic: Factors of Numbers to 100

A factor of a number divides into it exactly, with nothing left over. The caretaker wants to set out 48 chairs in equal rows. He can make 1 row of 48, 2 rows of 24, 3 rows of 16, 4 rows of 12 or 6 rows of 8. Each of these pairs multiplies to 48. So the factors of 48 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 48.

Factors come in pairs, so search in an orderly way. Start at 1 and work upwards, testing each number. For 72: 1 and 72, 2 and 36, 3 and 24, 4 and 18, 6 and 12, 8 and 9. 5 and 7 do not divide 72 exactly, so they are not factors. After 8 and 9 the next pair would be 9 and 8, which you already have, so you can stop. The factors of 72 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36 and 72.

Some numbers have very few factors. 37 has only two: 1 and 37. No other number divides into it exactly, so 37 chairs can only be set out in one long row or in rows of 1. Some numbers have an odd number of factors, like 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 36, because 6 pairs with itself.

Every number has 1 and itself as factors. 1 is a factor of every whole number, and the number itself is always its biggest factor. The factors of 100 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50 and 100.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding factor pairs, listing all the factors of a number, and knowing when to stop.

## Subtopic: Common Multiples and Common Factors

Juice comes in packs of 12 and muffins in packs of 18. What is the smallest number of each you can buy so that every learner gets one juice and one muffin, with none left over? List the multiples: 12, 24, 36, 48 and 18, 36, 54. The first number in both lists is 36. Buy 3 packs of juice and 2 packs of muffins to get 36 of each. 36 is the lowest common multiple of 12 and 18.

12 times 18 is 216, which is also a common multiple, but it is not the smallest. Multiplying the two numbers always gives a common multiple, but often there is a smaller one, so list the multiples and look for the first match.

48 boys and 72 girls must be split into the same number of teams, with the boys shared equally and the girls shared equally. The number of teams must be a factor of 48 and a factor of 72. The common factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24. The biggest, 24, is the highest common factor. With 24 teams, each team has 48 divided by 24, which is 2 boys, and 72 divided by 24, which is 3 girls.

Common multiples answer questions about when things match up, like packs, timetables and repeating events. Common factors answer questions about splitting things into equal groups, like teams, rows and parcels. Deciding which kind of question you have is the first step.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: mixing up the words and saying 4 is a multiple of 48; 4 is a factor of 48, and 48 is a multiple of 4. Two: listing only some factors, such as 2, 4, 6 and 8 for 48; search in pairs from 1 to find all ten. Three: leaving out 1 or the number itself; every number has both as factors. Four: giving 12 times 18, which is 216, as the smallest common multiple; list the multiples to find 36.

The questions for this section are with you now: common multiples, common factors, and choosing which one a problem needs.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the chairs, the juice and muffins, and the teams — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Up the Times Table

Multiples are the times table. Skip-count in 12s: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96.

In 15s: 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90.

Is 84 a multiple of 12? 12 times 7 is 84. Yes.

Keep skip-counting in mind and try the questions for this part: multiples.

## Subtopic: Pairs That Fit

Factors divide in exactly. They come in pairs. 48 chairs: 1 and 48, 2 and 24, 3 and 16, 4 and 12, 6 and 8.

Start at 1 and stop when the pairs meet.

1 is a factor of every number, and every number is a factor of itself.

Hold the pairs in mind and try this part's questions: factors.

## Subtopic: When Things Match

Packs of 12 and packs of 18. Skip-count both until they match: 36. Buy 3 packs and 2 packs.

48 boys and 72 girls in equal teams: 24 teams, each with 2 boys and 3 girls.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A multiple is in the times table of a number; a factor divides into a number exactly. Find multiples by skip-counting and factors in pairs, starting at 1 and stopping when the pairs meet. The first number in two lists of multiples is the lowest common multiple. The biggest factor shared by two numbers is the highest common factor.

Hold the first match in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: common multiples and common factors.
