# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers rounding off whole numbers to the nearest 5, 10, 100 and 1 000. The anchor is 21 367 runners in a fun run in Durban, a fridge costing R6 748 and a stove costing R4 395 at a shop in Pietermaritzburg, and 32 learners each paying R185 for a school outing. The governing idea is that rounding chooses the closest multiple of 5, 10, 100 or 1 000, and that for 10, 100 and 1 000 the digit just to the right of the rounding place decides: 0 to 4 rounds down and 5 to 9 rounds up. Rounding to the nearest 5, 10, 100 and 1 000, rounding with roll-over, and using rounding to estimate are examinable.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 5 and 10

Rounding to the nearest 10 means choosing the multiple of 10 that is closest. 21 367 sits between 21 360 and 21 370. It is 7 away from 21 360 and only 3 away from 21 370, so it rounds to 21 370. The quick rule from Grade 4 still works: look at the units digit; 0 to 4 rounds down, 5 to 9 rounds up.

Rounding to the nearest 5 is new in Grade 5. The multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5, so they come twice as often as the multiples of 10. 21 367 sits between 21 365 and 21 370. It is 2 away from 21 365 and 3 away from 21 370, so to the nearest 5 it is 21 365. R6 748 sits between R6 745 and R6 750; it is 3 away from R6 745 and 2 away from R6 750, so it rounds to R6 750.

A pattern makes this quick. Look at the units digit. If it is 1 or 2, round down to the 0. If it is 3 or 4, round up to the 5. If it is 6 or 7, round down to the 5. If it is 8 or 9, round up to the next 0. If it is already 0 or 5, the number is a multiple of 5 and stays as it is. 2 463 ends in 3, so it rounds to 2 465.

Notice that the nearest 5 and the nearest 10 can give different answers. 21 367 is 21 365 to the nearest 5 but 21 370 to the nearest 10. Both are correct; each answers a different question.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to the nearest 5, rounding to the nearest 10, and the units-digit pattern for fives.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 100 and 1 000

To round to the nearest 100, look at the tens digit, because it is the digit just to the right of the hundreds place. 21 367 sits between 21 300 and 21 400. The tens digit is 6, so round up: 21 400. R6 748 has tens digit 4, so it rounds down to R6 700. 2 463 has tens digit 6, so it rounds up to 2 500.

To round to the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit. 21 367 has hundreds digit 3, so it rounds down to 21 000. R6 748 has hundreds digit 7, so it rounds up to R7 000. Six-digit numbers follow the same rule: 348 952 has hundreds digit 9, so it rounds up to 349 000.

Watch for roll-over. 39 960 to the nearest 100: the tens digit is 6, so round up. 399 hundreds plus 1 hundred is 400 hundreds, which is 40 000. Several digits change at once, just as they do on a car's odometer when it passes a round number.

Always round the original number in one step. 2 449 to the nearest 100 is 2 400, because the tens digit is 4. If you first round to the nearest 10, getting 2 450, and then round 2 450 to the nearest 100, you get 2 500, which is wrong. The extra step pushed the number over the halfway mark.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to the nearest 100 and 1 000, rounding with roll-over, and rounding in one step.

## Subtopic: Rounding to Estimate

Rounding is most useful before a calculation, to know roughly what answer to expect. If 21 367 people run on Saturday and 18 842 on Sunday, round each to the nearest 1 000: 21 000 plus 19 000 is 40 000. The exact total is 40 209, close to the estimate, so it makes sense. If you got 4 020, the estimate would warn you at once.

The fridge and the stove cost R6 748 and R4 395. Rounded to the nearest R100 they are R6 700 and R4 400, so the total is about R11 100. The exact total is R11 143. Rounded to the nearest R1 000 they are R7 000 and R4 000, about R11 000. The finer rounding gives a closer estimate; the rougher rounding is quicker to work out.

Estimates work for multiplication too. 32 learners each pay R185. Round 32 to 30 and R185 to R200: 30 times R200 is R6 000. The exact amount is R5 920. The teacher knows that about R6 000 must be collected, and an answer of R592 or R59 200 would be clearly wrong.

Choose the rounding to suit the job. For checking a calculation, rounding each number so that only one or two digits are not zero keeps the mental work easy. For money you have to pay, use the exact amount; an estimate only tells you roughly what to expect.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: rounding 21 367 to the nearest 100 by looking at the units digit; the deciding digit is the tens digit, 6, so the answer is 21 400. Two: rounding in stages, 2 449 to 2 450 and then to 2 500; round the original in one step to get 2 400. Three: treating the nearest 5 like the nearest 10 and rounding 21 367 to 21 370; the closest multiple of 5 is 21 365. Four: being afraid of the roll-over and leaving 39 960 at 39 900 when rounding to the nearest 100; the tens digit 6 says round up, and the answer is 40 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating sums and products by rounding first, and checking exact answers against estimates.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the fun run, the fridge and the school outing — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Fives and Tens

Multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5. To round to the nearest 5, find the closest one. 21 367 is closer to 21 365 than to 21 370.

The units digit tells you. Ends in 1 or 2: down to the 0. Ends in 3 or 4: up to the 5. Ends in 6 or 7: down to the 5. Ends in 8 or 9: up to the next 0.

For the nearest 10, the units digit decides too: 0 to 4 down, 5 to 9 up. 21 367 is about 21 370.

Keep the 0 or 5 endings in mind and try the questions for this part: rounding to the nearest 5 and 10.

## Subtopic: Hundreds and Thousands

For the nearest 100, look at the tens digit. 21 367 has a 6 in the tens, so round up to 21 400.

For the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit. 21 367 has a 3 in the hundreds, so round down to 21 000.

When nines roll over, many digits change. 39 960 to the nearest 100 is 40 000.

Hold the deciding digit in mind and try this part's questions: rounding to the nearest 100 and 1 000.

## Subtopic: Round First, Then Check

Round first to guess the answer. R6 748 plus R4 395 is about R7 000 plus R4 000, which is R11 000.

The exact answer is R11 143. It is close to the guess, so it makes sense.

32 learners at R185 each is about 30 times R200, which is R6 000.

Pocket summary of the lesson. To round to the nearest 5, choose the closest number ending in 0 or 5. To round to the nearest 10, 100 or 1 000, look at the digit just to the right of that place: 0 to 4 rounds down, 5 to 9 rounds up. Round the original number in one step and let the nines roll over. Round before you calculate to know roughly what answer to expect.

Hold the guess-first habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: estimating by rounding.
