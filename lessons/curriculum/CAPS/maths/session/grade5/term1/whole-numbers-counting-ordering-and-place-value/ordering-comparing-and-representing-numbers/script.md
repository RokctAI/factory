# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers reading, writing, comparing, ordering and representing whole numbers to at least six digits, building on the four-digit numbers of Grade 4. The anchor is the number of visitors to three game reserves in Limpopo in one year, 245 318, 254 138 and 245 381, and the prices of two bakkies at a dealer in Kimberley, R189 950 and R198 590. The governing idea is that a six-digit number is two groups of three digits, read group by group from the left, and that comparison starts at the digit with the greatest place value. Reading and writing six-digit numbers in digits and words, comparing with the greater than and less than signs, ordering in ascending and descending order, and placing numbers on a number line are examinable.

## Subtopic: Reading and Writing Six-Digit Numbers

In Grade 4 you read numbers like 5 318: five thousand three hundred and eighteen. A six-digit number works the same way, it just has more thousands. 245 318 has 245 thousands and 318 more. We leave a space after every three digits, counting from the right, so the number splits into a thousands group, 245, and a units group, 318.

To read it, read the left group as an ordinary three-digit number and add the word thousand, then read the right group. 245 318 is two hundred and forty-five thousand, three hundred and eighteen. 254 138 is two hundred and fifty-four thousand, one hundred and thirty-eight. The word thousand is the bridge between the two groups.

Zeros need care. 407 052 is four hundred and seven thousand and fifty-two. The left group, 407, is four hundred and seven. The right group, 052, has no hundreds, so there is no hundreds word and we simply say and fifty-two. Going the other way, from words to digits, fill every empty place with a zero. Three hundred thousand and six is 300 006, which has six digits, not 3 006 or 30 006.

The space matters. Write 245 318, not 245318. In South Africa a space separates the groups of three, and a comma is kept for decimal numbers, which you will meet in later grades. A quick check: the group on the right must always have exactly three digits.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading six-digit numbers in groups, writing them in words, and writing numbers from words with zeros in the right places.

## Subtopic: Comparing Six-Digit Numbers

To compare two whole numbers, first count the digits. A number with more digits is bigger. 100 000 has six digits and 99 999 has only five, so 100 000 is bigger, even though 99 999 is full of nines.

When both numbers have the same number of digits, start on the left, where the digits are worth the most, and move right until two digits are different. Compare 245 318 and 254 138. The hundred thousands are both 2. The ten thousands are 4 and 5. Five is bigger, so 254 138 is the bigger number, and we do not need to look any further. Compare 245 318 and 245 381. The digits 2, 4, 5 and 3 match. The tens are 1 and 8, so 245 381 is bigger.

The signs save words. The greater than sign and the less than sign both have an open side and a point, and the open side always faces the bigger number. So 254 138 is greater than 245 318, and 245 318 is less than 245 381. For the two bakkies, R189 950 and R198 590, the hundred thousands match, then 8 meets 9 in the ten thousands, so R198 590 is the dearer bakkie.

Notice that the two bakkie prices use the same six digits in a different order, yet they are R8 640 apart. The game reserve numbers 245 318 and 245 381 also use the same digits, but they are only 63 apart. Swapping digits on the left changes a number by a lot; swapping digits on the right changes it by a little.

The questions for this section are with you now: comparing by counting digits, comparing from the left, and choosing the correct sign.

## Subtopic: Ordering and Representing on a Number Line

Ordering means arranging numbers from smallest to biggest, called ascending order, or from biggest to smallest, called descending order. The three reserves in ascending order are 245 318, 245 381, 254 138. In descending order they are 254 138, 245 381, 245 318. Ascending climbs up a staircase; descending comes down it.

When the list mixes five-digit and six-digit numbers, sort by number of digits first. 98 765, 102 345 and 99 999 in ascending order are 98 765, 99 999, 102 345. The two five-digit numbers both start with 9, so the thousands digit decides: 8 thousand is less than 9 thousand.

A number line shows how far apart numbers are, not just their order. On a line from 240 000 to 260 000 marked every 1 000, 245 318 and 245 381 sit almost on top of each other just past the 245 000 mark, while 254 138 sits just past 254 000, about 9 000 further along. To find a number between 245 318 and 245 381, pick any number in the gap, such as 245 350.

Counting on and back by 1, 10, 100 or 1 000 changes one place at a time. 1 000 more than 245 318 is 246 318. 10 more than 245 395 is 245 405, because the tens roll over. 1 less than 300 000 is 299 999.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: comparing 99 999 and 100 000 digit by digit and choosing 99 999 because 9 beats 1; count the digits first. Two: writing three hundred thousand and six as 3 006; fill the empty places with zeros to get 300 006. Three: grouping digits from the left and writing 2 453 18; always group in threes from the right, 245 318. Four: mixing up ascending and descending; ascending starts with the smallest.

The questions for this section are with you now: ascending and descending order, numbers between, and counting on and back on a number line.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the game reserves and the two bakkies — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Say It, Write It

A big number splits into two groups of three. 245 318 is 245 thousand and 318. Read the left group, say thousand, then read the right group: two hundred and forty-five thousand, three hundred and eighteen.

Empty places get a zero. Three hundred thousand and six is 300 006. Count the digits: six.

Group from the right, with a space every three digits. 245 318, not 245318.

Keep the two groups in mind and try the questions for this part: reading and writing six-digit numbers.

## Subtopic: Bigger or Smaller

Count the digits first. More digits wins. 100 000 beats 99 999.

Same number of digits? Start on the left and move right until two digits are different. 245 318 and 254 138: 2 and 2 match, then 4 meets 5. Five wins, so 254 138 is bigger.

The bakkies cost R189 950 and R198 590. 1 and 1 match, then 8 meets 9. R198 590 costs more.

Hold the left-to-right rule in mind and try this part's questions: comparing big numbers.

## Subtopic: Put Them in a Line

Ascending climbs up: smallest first. 245 318, 245 381, 254 138. Descending comes down: biggest first.

On a number line, 245 318 and 245 381 sit almost on top of each other. 254 138 is far along.

1 000 more than 245 318 is 246 318. 1 less than 300 000 is 299 999.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A six-digit number is two groups of three, read thousands first. Empty places hold zeros. Compare by counting digits, then from the left, moving right only when digits match. The sign opens towards the bigger number. Ascending goes up, descending comes down, and a number line shows how far apart numbers are.

Hold the number line in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: ordering and placing numbers.
