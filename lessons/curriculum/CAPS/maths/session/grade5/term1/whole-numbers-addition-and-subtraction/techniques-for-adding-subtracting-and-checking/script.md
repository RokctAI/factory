# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers techniques for adding and subtracting whole numbers of at least five digits and for checking the answers. The anchor is a school book fair in East London that sold R34 567 worth of books, with another R9 998 in orders, and a fundraising drive with a target of R42 000 that has reached R38 750. The governing idea is that a calculation can be made easier by splitting numbers by place value, by rounding to a friendly number and then compensating, or by jumping along a number line, and that a second technique, especially the inverse operation, checks the first. Using any two techniques to perform and check calculations, including estimation, building up and breaking down, the number line, rounding and compensating, and inverse operations, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Breaking Down and Building Up

Breaking down means splitting numbers by place value and adding the like parts. 23 400 plus 15 300: the thousands give 23 000 plus 15 000, which is 38 000, and the hundreds give 400 plus 300, which is 700. Together, 38 700. 46 250 plus 27 380: 46 000 plus 27 000 is 73 000, and 250 plus 380 is 630. The total is 73 630.

For subtraction, it is often easiest to break down only the number being taken away and subtract it in parts. 58 760 minus 23 450: take away 23 000 to get 35 760, then take away 450 to get 35 310. Each step is small enough to do in your head.

Building up means making friendly numbers, usually whole thousands or ten thousands. How much must be added to 27 650 to make 30 000? 650 needs 350 to make 1 000, which takes us to 28 000, and 2 000 more makes 30 000. So 2 350 is needed. Pairs that make 1 000, like 650 and 350 or 250 and 750, are worth knowing well.

These methods use the same place value as the column method, but they start from the biggest parts. That makes them good for mental calculation and for estimating, because the biggest parts already give a close answer.

The questions for this section are with you now: splitting by place value, subtracting in parts, and building up to a friendly number.

## Subtopic: Rounding and Compensating

Some numbers are very close to a friendly number. The book fair took R34 567, and orders added R9 998. 9 998 is only 2 less than 10 000. Add 10 000 instead: 34 567 plus 10 000 is 44 567. But we added 2 too many, so take 2 off: 44 565. Taking off the extra is called compensating.

Compensating also works in subtraction, but the fix goes the other way. 65 432 minus 19 997: subtract 20 000 instead, to get 45 432. We took away 3 too many, so give 3 back: 45 435.

The rule is to think about what you did. If you added too much, take the extra off. If you subtracted too much, give the extra back. In money: R12 485 plus R4 999 is R12 485 plus R5 000, which is R17 485, minus the extra R1, which is R17 484.

Rounding and compensating is most useful when a number ends in 7, 8 or 9 in the last places, such as 9 998, 19 997 or R4 999. For a number like 23 456, rounding to 23 000 and compensating by 456 is no easier than the column method, so choose the technique that fits the numbers.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to a friendly number, compensating in addition and subtraction, and choosing when to use the technique.

## Subtopic: Number Lines and Inverse Checks

A number line turns subtraction into counting up. The fundraising drive has R38 750 and the target is R42 000. How much more is needed? Start at 38 750. Jump 250 to reach 39 000. Jump 3 000 to reach 42 000. The answer is the total of the jumps: 250 plus 3 000 is 3 250. R3 250 is still needed.

The inverse operation is the strongest check, because addition and subtraction undo each other. If 42 000 minus 38 750 is 3 250, then 38 750 plus 3 250 must be 42 000. It is, so the answer is right. If 34 567 plus 9 998 is 44 565, then 44 565 minus 9 998 must be 34 567. 44 565 minus 10 000 is 34 565, plus 2 is 34 567. Correct.

Estimation is the fastest check. Rounded to the nearest 1 000, 34 567 plus 9 998 is about 35 000 plus 10 000, which is 45 000. 44 565 is close. An estimate cannot prove an answer is exactly right, but it catches big slips, like a forgotten carry or a digit in the wrong place.

CAPS asks you to use any two techniques for every calculation: one to work it out and one to check it. A good pair is a quick method to calculate and the inverse to check, or the column method to calculate and an estimate to check.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: compensating the wrong way, so 34 567 plus 9 998 becomes 44 569; you added 2 too many, so take 2 off to get 44 565. Two: in subtraction, taking 3 more instead of giving 3 back, so 65 432 minus 19 997 becomes 45 429; the answer is 45 435. Three: giving the point where you land on the number line, 42 000, as the answer; the answer is the total of the jumps, 3 250. Four: checking with the same method that made the mistake; use a different technique, such as the inverse.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting up on a number line, checking with the inverse, and estimating to check.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the book fair and the fundraising target — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Split It by Place

Thousands with thousands, hundreds with hundreds. 23 400 plus 15 300 is 38 000 plus 700, which is 38 700.

Taking away? Take it off in parts. 58 760 minus 23 000 is 35 760. Minus 450 more is 35 310.

Make friendly numbers. 650 and 350 make 1 000. 27 650 plus 2 350 is 30 000.

Keep the place-by-place split in mind and try the questions for this part: breaking down and building up.

## Subtopic: Round, Then Fix

9 998 is nearly 10 000. 34 567 plus 10 000 is 44 567. You added 2 too many, so take 2 off: 44 565.

19 997 is nearly 20 000. 65 432 minus 20 000 is 45 432. You took 3 too many, so give 3 back: 45 435.

Added too much? Take it off. Took too much? Give it back.

Hold the fix-it rule in mind and try this part's questions: rounding and compensating.

## Subtopic: Jump and Check

From R38 750 to R42 000: jump 250 to 39 000, then 3 000 to 42 000. Add the jumps: R3 250.

Check with the opposite: 38 750 plus 3 250 is 42 000. Correct.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Break numbers down by place value, or build up to friendly numbers. Round a number close to a friendly one and compensate: take off what you added too much, give back what you took too much. Count up on a number line and add the jumps. Use two techniques for every calculation, one to work it out and one to check, and the inverse is the best check.

Hold the jump-and-check habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: number lines and checking.
