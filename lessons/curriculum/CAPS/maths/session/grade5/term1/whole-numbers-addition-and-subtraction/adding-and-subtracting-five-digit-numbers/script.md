# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers addition and subtraction of whole numbers of at least five digits using the column method. The anchor is a music festival in Bloemfontein with 24 586 people on Saturday and 18 937 on Sunday, and a family in Mbombela saving R45 000 towards a car that costs R38 750. The governing idea is that the column method works place by place from the units, carrying when a column passes 9 and exchanging when a top digit is too small, and that every answer is checked by estimation and by the inverse operation. Adding and subtracting five-digit and six-digit numbers in columns, carrying and exchanging, subtracting across zeros, and checking answers are examinable.

## Subtopic: Adding in Columns with Carrying

How many people came to the festival over the two days? Write 24 586 above 18 937, lined up on the right so that units sit under units, tens under tens, and so on. Start with the units, because a carry can only move to the left.

Units: 6 plus 7 is 13. Write 3 and carry 1 ten. Tens: 8 plus 3 plus the carried 1 is 12. Write 2 and carry 1 hundred. Hundreds: 5 plus 9 plus 1 is 15. Write 5 and carry 1 thousand. Thousands: 4 plus 8 plus 1 is 13. Write 3 and carry 1 ten thousand. Ten thousands: 2 plus 1 plus 1 is 4. The total is 43 523 people.

Estimate before or after to make sure the answer is sensible. Rounded to the nearest 1 000, the crowds are 25 000 and 19 000, which make 44 000. The exact answer, 43 523, is close, so it makes sense. An answer like 33 523 would show that a carry had been forgotten.

Six-digit numbers work in exactly the same way, with one more column. 125 648 plus 37 895: 8 plus 5 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 4 plus 9 plus 1 is 14, write 4 carry 1; 6 plus 8 plus 1 is 15, write 5 carry 1; 5 plus 7 plus 1 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 2 plus 3 plus 1 is 6; and the 1 hundred thousand comes down. The total is 163 543.

The questions for this section are with you now: lining up numbers, carrying from column to column, and estimating to check a sum.

## Subtopic: Subtracting in Columns with Exchanging

How many more people came on Saturday than on Sunday? 24 586 minus 18 937. Line up on the right again and start with the units. Units: 6 minus 7 cannot be done, so exchange 1 ten from the tens column for 10 units. The tens drop from 8 to 7, and the units become 16. 16 minus 7 is 9.

Tens: 7 minus 3 is 4. Hundreds: 5 minus 9 cannot be done, so exchange 1 thousand for 10 hundreds. The thousands drop from 4 to 3, and the hundreds become 15. 15 minus 9 is 6. Thousands: 3 minus 8 cannot be done, so exchange 1 ten thousand. The ten thousands drop from 2 to 1, and the thousands become 13. 13 minus 8 is 5. Ten thousands: 1 minus 1 is 0. The answer is 5 649.

Check by adding, because addition undoes subtraction. 18 937 plus 5 649 should give back the number we started with. 7 plus 9 is 16, write 6 carry 1; 3 plus 4 plus 1 is 8; 9 plus 6 is 15, write 5 carry 1; 8 plus 5 plus 1 is 14, write 4 carry 1; 1 plus 1 is 2. That is 24 586, so the answer is correct.

Money works the same way. The family has saved R45 000 and the car costs R38 750. 45 000 minus 38 750: exchange through the columns to get R6 250 left over. Check: R38 750 plus R6 250 is R45 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: exchanging one column at a time, subtracting five-digit numbers, and checking by adding.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Across Zeros and Adding Many Numbers

Zeros make exchanging longer, because there is nothing to exchange from in the next column. 50 000 minus 23 476: the units need a ten, but the tens are 0, and so are the hundreds and the thousands. Exchange 1 ten thousand all the way down: 50 000 becomes 4 ten thousands, 9 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 units. Then 10 minus 6 is 4, 9 minus 7 is 2, 9 minus 4 is 5, 9 minus 3 is 6 and 4 minus 2 is 2. The answer is 26 524.

A shortcut avoids the long exchange. 50 000 is 1 more than 49 999, and 49 999 minus 23 476 needs no exchanging at all: 26 523. Because we took away from a number 1 smaller, add the 1 back: 26 524. Check: 23 476 plus 26 524 is 50 000.

Adding three or more numbers is the same column method with more digits in each column. 12 345 plus 23 456 plus 4 789: line them up on the right, so 4 789 sits under the thousands, hundreds, tens and units only. Units: 5 plus 6 plus 9 is 20, write 0 carry 2. Tens: 4 plus 5 plus 8 plus 2 is 19, write 9 carry 1. Hundreds: 3 plus 4 plus 7 plus 1 is 15, write 5 carry 1. Thousands: 2 plus 3 plus 4 plus 1 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Ten thousands: 1 plus 2 plus 1 is 4. The total is 40 590.

Notice that a column can carry more than 1. In the units, 20 carries 2 tens. This only happens when three or more numbers are added, so take extra care with long lists.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: lining up 4 789 on the left under 12 345; always line up on the right so that units sit under units. Two: taking the smaller digit from the bigger in every column, so 24 586 minus 18 937 becomes 14 451; when the top digit is smaller, exchange. Three: across zeros, writing 0 minus 6 as 6 and getting 33 476 for 50 000 minus 23 476; exchange through the zeros, or use 49 999, to get 26 524. Four: skipping the check; adding the answer to the number taken away shows at once whether a subtraction is right.

The questions for this section are with you now: subtracting across zeros, using the 49 999 shortcut, and adding three numbers in columns.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the festival crowds and the car savings — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Line Up and Carry

Put units under units. Start on the right.

Add each column. If it is more than 9, write the units digit and carry the ten to the next column. 24 586 plus 18 937 is 43 523.

Guess first: 25 000 plus 19 000 is 44 000. 43 523 is close, so it makes sense.

Keep the carry in mind and try the questions for this part: adding in columns.

## Subtopic: Exchange When You Need To

Start on the right. If the top digit is too small, exchange 1 from the next column on the left. 1 ten becomes 10 units.

24 586 minus 18 937 is 5 649.

Check by adding: 18 937 plus 5 649 is 24 586. Back where you started, so it is right.

Hold the exchange in mind and try this part's questions: subtracting in columns.

## Subtopic: Zeros and Long Lists

50 000 minus 23 476 has lots of zeros. Use 49 999 instead: 49 999 minus 23 476 is 26 523. Add the 1 back: 26 524.

For a long list, line all the numbers up on the right. A column can carry 2 or more.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Line numbers up on the right and work from the units. Carry when a column adds up to more than 9. Exchange when a top digit is too small. Across zeros, exchange all the way or use the 49 999 shortcut. Check every sum with an estimate, and every subtraction by adding back.

Hold the 49 999 trick in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: zeros and long lists.
