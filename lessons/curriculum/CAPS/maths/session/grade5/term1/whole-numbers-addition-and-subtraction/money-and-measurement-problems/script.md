# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems with whole numbers in financial and measurement contexts, using addition and subtraction. The anchor is a family budget in Pietermaritzburg with an income of R18 500 a month, a spaza shop's stock and sales, a truck carrying maize and sugar from Bethlehem, and a 25 000 litre water tank on a farm in the Karoo. The governing idea is that every word problem follows the same steps: understand the story, write a number sentence, calculate, check, and answer in a sentence with the correct units. Solving one-step and two-step money and measurement problems, working with profit, change and budgets, and keeping units consistent are examinable.

## Subtopic: Money Problems

A spaza shop owner buys stock for R12 650 and sells all of it for R15 230. The money left after paying for the stock is the profit, so profit is selling price minus cost price. 15 230 minus 12 650 is 2 580. The profit is R2 580. Check: R12 650 plus R2 580 is R15 230.

Saving towards a goal is another subtraction. A laptop costs R8 499 and Thabo has saved R5 750. He still needs R8 499 minus R5 750. Count up: R5 750 plus R250 is R6 000, plus R2 499 is R8 499. The jumps make R2 749. Thabo needs R2 749 more.

Change is money given back. A customer pays R500 for an item costing R367. The change is 500 minus 367, which is R133. A quick check: R367 plus R133 is R500. Shopkeepers often give change by counting up from the price to the amount paid, which is the inverse check in action.

Totals of several amounts are additions. The family pays R6 750 rent, R4 380 for food and R2 465 for transport. R6 750 plus R4 380 is R11 130, and R11 130 plus R2 465 is R13 595. Writing the rand sign on every amount and on the answer keeps it clear that these are money amounts.

The questions for this section are with you now: profit, saving towards a goal, change and totals.

## Subtopic: Measurement Problems

Distances can be found from a car's odometer, which counts every kilometre driven. At the start of a trip it reads 45 678 km and at the end 47 123 km. The distance is the difference: 47 123 minus 45 678 is 1 445. The trip was 1 445 km. Check: 45 678 plus 1 445 is 47 123.

A truck leaving Bethlehem carries 8 450 kg of maize and 4 975 kg of sugar. The total load is 8 450 plus 4 975, which is 13 425 kg. The truck may carry at most 15 000 kg, so it can still take 15 000 minus 13 425, which is 1 575 kg. Both answers are masses, so both are written in kilograms.

Units must match before you add or subtract. 3 km and 250 m cannot be added as 3 plus 250, because a kilometre and a metre are different sizes. In Term 3 you will learn to convert between them. For now, check that the numbers in a problem use the same unit, and carry that unit into the answer.

Measurement answers should make sense in real life. If a calculation says the truck can still take 28 425 kg, that is more than its whole limit, so something has gone wrong; the numbers were added instead of subtracted. Asking whether an answer is possible is a quick and powerful check.

The questions for this section are with you now: distances from odometer readings, total and spare mass, and matching units.

## Subtopic: Multi-Step Problems and Sensible Answers

Many problems need two steps, where the answer to the first step is used in the second. The family earns R18 500 a month. How much is left after rent, food and transport? Step one: total spending is R13 595. Step two: R18 500 minus R13 595 is R4 905. R4 905 is left each month. The total spending, R13 595, is not the final answer; it is only the first step.

The farm tank holds 25 000 litres. In one week 8 745 litres are used, and in the next week 9 368 litres. How much is left? Step one: 8 745 plus 9 368 is 18 113 litres used. Step two: 25 000 minus 18 113 is 6 887 litres. Check: 18 113 plus 6 887 is 25 000.

There is often more than one correct plan. The tank problem could also be done by subtracting each week in turn: 25 000 minus 8 745 is 16 255, and 16 255 minus 9 368 is 6 887. The answer is the same. Choose the plan that makes the calculation easiest, and write the steps so someone else can follow them.

Finish every problem with an answer sentence that uses the words of the question and the correct unit: R4 905 is left each month; 6 887 litres are left in the tank. A bare number like 6 887 does not say whether it is litres, rands or kilometres.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding when the question asks how much is left, so the truck seems able to take 28 425 kg more; a question about what is left needs subtraction. Two: answering with a bare number, such as 1 445; write 1 445 km. Three: adding 3 km and 250 m as 253; units must match before you calculate. Four: stopping after the first step and giving the total spending, R13 595, as the amount left; finish the second step to get R4 905.

The questions for this section are with you now: two-step problems, different plans that give the same answer, and complete answer sentences.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the spaza shop, the truck and the water tank — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Rands In, Rands Out

Profit is selling price minus cost price. R15 230 minus R12 650 is R2 580 profit.

How much more to save? Price minus what you have. R8 499 minus R5 750 is R2 749.

Change from R500 for R367: count up from R367 to R500. That is R133.

Keep the rand sign in mind and try the questions for this part: money problems.

## Subtopic: Kilometres and Kilograms

Distance from an odometer: end reading minus start reading. 47 123 minus 45 678 is 1 445 km.

Maize 8 450 kg and sugar 4 975 kg make 13 425 kg. The limit is 15 000 kg, so 1 575 kg more can go on.

Same units before you add. Write the unit in the answer.

Hold the units in mind and try this part's questions: measurement problems.

## Subtopic: Step by Step

Two steps: first find the total, then use it. Spending is R13 595. Income R18 500 minus R13 595 is R4 905 left.

Tank: 8 745 plus 9 368 is 18 113 litres used. 25 000 minus 18 113 is 6 887 litres left.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Read the story, write a number sentence, calculate, check, and answer in a sentence with units. Profit is selling price minus cost price; change is amount paid minus price. Units must match, and every answer needs its unit. In a two-step problem the first answer is a stepping stone, not the final answer. Ask whether the answer is possible in real life.

Hold the two-step plan in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: multi-step problems.
