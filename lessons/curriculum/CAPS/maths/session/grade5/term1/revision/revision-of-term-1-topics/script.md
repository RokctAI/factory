# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 1 topics: whole numbers to six digits, including ordering, place value and rounding; number sentences and the properties of operations; addition and subtraction of five-digit and six-digit numbers with techniques for checking; multiplication of three-digit by two-digit numbers with techniques for checking; multiples and factors; and ratio and rate. The anchor is a school market day in Upington that raised R148 365 last year and R153 840 this year, with 12 476 visitors on Saturday and 9 858 on Sunday, 24 stalls paying R135 each, and lemonade mixed from syrup and water. The governing idea is that each topic gives you a tool, and that estimation and inverse operations check every tool. The whole of Term 1 is examinable in the Term 1 test.

## Subtopic: Numbers: Place Value, Ordering and Rounding

Place value: R148 365 has 1 hundred thousand, 4 ten thousands, 8 thousands, 3 hundreds, 6 tens and 5 units. The 4 is worth 40 000. In expanded notation it is 100 000 plus 40 000 plus 8 000 plus 300 plus 60 plus 5. In words it is one hundred and forty-eight thousand, three hundred and sixty-five.

Comparing and ordering: count the digits, then compare from the left. R148 365 and R153 840 both have six digits and both start with 1. The ten thousands are 4 and 5, so R153 840 is bigger: this year beat last year. In ascending order, the smaller comes first. Remember that more digits always means a bigger number: 100 000 is bigger than 99 999.

Rounding: look at the digit just to the right of the place you want. 148 365 to the nearest 10: the units digit 5 rounds up, so 148 370. To the nearest 100: the tens digit 6 rounds up, so 148 400. To the nearest 1 000: the hundreds digit 3 rounds down, so 148 000. To the nearest 5, it ends in 5 already, so it stays 148 365.

These number skills are the base for everything else. An estimate is a rounding; a column method is place value; a number line is ordering. If any of these feels shaky, revise it first, because the later topics stand on it.

The questions for this section are with you now: place value and words, comparing and ordering six-digit numbers, and rounding to 5, 10, 100 and 1 000.

## Subtopic: Number Sentences, Adding and Subtracting

Number sentences: how many visitors came altogether? 12 476 plus 9 858 equals box. In columns: 6 plus 8 is 14, write 4 carry 1; 7 plus 5 plus 1 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 4 plus 8 plus 1 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 2 plus 9 plus 1 is 12, write 2 carry 1; 1 plus 1 is 2. 22 334 visitors. Estimate: 12 000 plus 10 000 is 22 000. Close.

The box can go anywhere. If 22 334 visitors came and 9 858 came on Sunday, then box plus 9 858 equals 22 334, and the inverse gives the box: 22 334 minus 9 858 is 12 476. Substitute to check: 12 476 plus 9 858 is 22 334, true.

Subtraction: how much more was raised this year? 153 840 minus 148 365. Exchange where a top digit is too small: 0 minus 5 needs a ten, 3 minus 6 needs a hundred, and so on. The answer is 5 475. Check by adding: 148 365 plus 5 475 is 153 840. Rounding and compensating helps with near-round numbers: R12 476 plus R9 998 is R12 476 plus R10 000, minus R2, which is R22 474.

Properties: swap and group for addition and multiplication, never for subtraction or division. 25 times 13 times 4 is easiest as 25 times 4, which is 100, times 13, which is 1 300. Split to multiply: 7 times 135 is 700 plus 210 plus 35, which is 945. Plus 0 and times 1 change nothing.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing and solving number sentences, adding and subtracting with checks, and using the properties.

## Subtopic: Multiplying, Factors, Ratio and Rate

Multiplication: 24 stalls each pay R135. Split 24 into 20 and 4: 20 times 135 is 2 700, and 4 times 135 is 540. The total is R3 240. In long multiplication the rows are 135 times 4, which is 540, and 135 times 20, written with a 0 first, which is 2 700. Estimate: 25 times 130 is 3 250. Close.

Techniques and checks: double and halve, as in 16 times 25 equals 4 times 100; round and compensate, as in 199 times 12 equals 2 400 minus 12; and check by division, as in 3 240 divided by 24, which is 135.

Multiples and factors: 36 tables in the hall can be set out in equal rows using the factor pairs of 36: 1 and 36, 2 and 18, 3 and 12, 4 and 9, 6 and 6. A raffle is drawn every 15 minutes and a lucky draw every 20 minutes, starting together. The multiples of 15 are 15, 30, 45, 60 and the multiples of 20 are 20, 40, 60, so they next happen together after 60 minutes.

Ratio and rate: lemonade is mixed 1 part syrup to 5 parts water, a ratio of the same kind. For 4 litres of syrup, multiply both parts by 4: 20 litres of water, 24 litres of lemonade. Boerewors rolls sell at R25 each, a rate. 148 rolls bring in 148 times 25, which is R3 700.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: comparing 99 999 and 100 000 from the left and choosing 99 999; count the digits first. Two: rounding in stages, so 2 449 becomes 2 450 and then 2 500; round the original once to get 2 400. Three: taking the smaller digit from the bigger in every column of a subtraction; exchange when the top digit is too small. Four: adding instead of multiplying in a ratio, so 1 to 5 becomes 4 to 8; multiply both parts to get 4 to 20.

The questions for this section are with you now: multiplication with checks, multiples and factors, and ratio and rate.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again, walking through the market day from the takings board to the stalls — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Takings Board

R148 365 last year. The 4 sits in the ten thousands seat, so it is worth R40 000.

R153 840 this year. Compare from the left: 1 and 1 match, then 5 beats 4. This year is bigger.

Round 148 365: nearest 10 is 148 370, nearest 100 is 148 400, nearest 1 000 is 148 000.

Keep the takings board in mind and try the questions for this part: place value, comparing and rounding.

## Subtopic: At the Gate and the Till

Visitors: 12 476 plus 9 858. Line up, carry: 22 334. Guess first: 12 000 plus 10 000 is 22 000. Close.

More money this year: 153 840 minus 148 365 is 5 475. Check: 148 365 plus 5 475 is 153 840.

Write the sentence first. Put the box in the gap. Swap and group only for plus and times.

Hold the gate and the till in mind and try this part's questions: number sentences, adding and subtracting.

## Subtopic: At the Stalls

24 stalls at R135: 2 700 plus 540 is R3 240.

Factors of 36 come in pairs. Raffle every 15 minutes, lucky draw every 20: together at 60.

Lemonade 1 to 5: for 4 litres of syrup, 20 litres of water. Rolls at R25 each: 148 rolls make R3 700.
