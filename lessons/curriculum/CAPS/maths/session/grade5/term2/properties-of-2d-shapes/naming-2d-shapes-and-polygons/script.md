# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising, visualising and naming 2D shapes: regular and irregular polygons, including triangles, squares, rectangles, other quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons and heptagons, and circles. The anchor is a Grade 5 class on an outing to a cultural village in Mpumalanga, where the walls of the houses are painted in bold Ndebele designs full of shapes. The governing idea is that a polygon is named by counting its straight sides, and it is regular only when all its sides are equal and all its angles are equal. Naming polygons, telling regular from irregular, and recognising shapes in any position are examinable.

## Subtopic: Polygons and Circles

A 2D shape is flat: it has length and width but no thickness. The Ndebele designs on the walls are made of 2D shapes painted with black outlines.

A polygon is a closed 2D shape with only straight sides. Closed means the outline has no gaps, so you could colour the inside without the paint leaking out. Each place where two sides meet is a corner, called a vertex. A polygon always has the same number of vertices as sides.

Polygons are named by the number of their sides. A triangle has 3 sides. A quadrilateral has 4 sides. A pentagon has 5, a hexagon has 6 and a heptagon has 7. The word parts help: penta means five, hexa means six and hepta means seven. An octagon, like a stop sign, has 8.

A circle is a 2D shape too, but it is not a polygon, because its outline is one curved line with no straight sides and no vertices. The class finds circles in the painted beads around a doorway. A shape with some straight sides and some curved sides, such as a semicircle, is not a polygon either.

Polygons are all around the cultural village. The doors are rectangles, the roof edges make triangles, and some painted borders are made of pentagons and hexagons. Counting the sides of each shape before naming it is the habit that never fails.

The questions for this section are with you now: deciding what is a polygon and naming polygons by their number of sides.

## Subtopic: Regular and Irregular Polygons

A regular polygon has all its sides the same length and all its angles the same size. A square is a regular quadrilateral. A triangle with three equal sides is a regular triangle. The hexagons in a honeycomb are regular hexagons.

An irregular polygon has sides or angles that are not all equal. A rectangle that is longer than it is wide is an irregular quadrilateral, because its sides are not all the same length, even though its angles are all equal. The painted house shape on one wall, a square with a triangle roof on top, has 5 sides, so it is a pentagon, and it is irregular.

Both conditions matter. A rhombus has 4 equal sides, but two of its angles are pointy and two are wide, so it is not regular. Only a square has 4 equal sides and 4 equal angles.

Irregular polygons are still named by their sides. Any shape with 6 straight sides is a hexagon, whether it looks like a honeycomb cell or like a bent letter L. The letter L shape has 6 sides, so it is an irregular hexagon. Count the sides; do not judge by how familiar the shape looks.

You can test whether a polygon is regular with a ruler and a square corner. Measure every side: if one is different, the polygon is irregular. Then compare the corners. A square passes both tests, a long rectangle fails the side test, and a rhombus with two pointy corners fails the corner test.

The questions for this section are with you now: deciding whether a polygon is regular or irregular, and naming irregular polygons.

## Subtopic: Recognising Shapes in Any Position

A shape keeps its name when it is turned, flipped or moved. A square turned on its corner looks like a diamond, but it still has 4 equal sides and 4 square corners, so it is still a square. A triangle standing on its point is still a triangle.

The quadrilateral family includes squares, rectangles and other 4-sided shapes. A rectangle has 4 square corners and opposite sides equal. A square is a special rectangle with all 4 sides equal. A kite has two pairs of equal sides next to each other. All of them are quadrilaterals because they have 4 straight sides.

Visualising means picturing a shape in your mind from a description. A closed shape with 7 straight sides of equal length and 7 equal angles is a regular heptagon. A shape made from a rectangle with a semicircle on one end has a curved side, so it is not a polygon.

To name any shape, ask three questions. Is it closed? Are all the sides straight? How many sides does it have? Then decide whether all the sides and angles are equal.

Visualising also works with cuts. Picture a regular hexagon and cut it with a straight line through two opposite corners. Each half has 4 sides, so each half is a quadrilateral. Imagining cuts and turns like this builds a strong picture of shapes in your mind.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: calling a turned square a diamond and saying it is a different shape; it is still a square. Two: calling a circle a polygon with many sides; it has no straight sides. Three: saying a rectangle is regular because its corners are all equal; its sides are not all equal. Four: calling the L shape a quadrilateral because it looks like a rectangle; it has 6 sides, so it is a hexagon.

The questions for this section are with you now: recognising shapes in different positions, quadrilaterals, and naming shapes from a description.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the painted Ndebele walls — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Count the Straight Sides

A polygon is closed and has only straight sides.

3 sides: triangle. 4: quadrilateral. 5: pentagon. 6: hexagon. 7: heptagon.

A circle has no straight sides, so it is not a polygon.

Keep counting sides in mind and try the questions for this part: naming polygons.

## Subtopic: Regular or Not

Regular means all sides equal and all angles equal. A square is regular.

A long rectangle is irregular: its sides are not all equal.

The L shape has 6 sides: an irregular hexagon.

Hold the all-equal test in mind and try this part's questions: regular and irregular.
