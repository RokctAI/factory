# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising, describing and using the equivalence of division and fractions, and solving problems with common fractions, including grouping and sharing. The anchor is a family braai in Mamelodi, where 3 pizzas are shared among 4 children, 7 oranges between 2 friends and 5 rotis among 3 cousins, and where flour for amagwinya is measured in quarter cups. The governing idea is that a fraction such as 3/4 also means 3 divided by 4, so a sharing problem can have a fraction answer, a remainder can be shared as a fraction, and grouping asks how many fraction pieces fit into an amount. Writing divisions as fractions, sharing with fraction answers, and grouping with fractions are examinable.

## Subtopic: A Fraction Is a Division

3 pizzas are shared equally among 4 children. Each child cannot have a whole pizza, because there are not enough. Cut each pizza into 4 quarters. That makes 12 quarters. Share the 12 quarters among 4 children: each child gets 3 quarters, one from each pizza. So 3 divided by 4 is 3/4.

This works for every fraction. The top number is the amount being shared and the bottom number is the number of shares. 1 divided by 4 is 1/4: one pizza for four children. 2 divided by 5 is 2/5. Read 3/4 as three quarters, or as 3 shared by 4, or as 3 divided by 4. All three mean the same amount.

It also works the other way round. 12/4 means 12 divided by 4, which is 3. 6/3 means 6 divided by 3, which is 2. When the top number is a multiple of the bottom number, the fraction is a whole number. 8/8 is 8 divided by 8, which is 1, the whole.

Be careful with the order. The amount being shared always goes on top. 3 pizzas for 4 children is 3/4 each, less than one pizza. 4 pizzas for 3 children would be 4/3 each, which is 1 and 1/3, more than one pizza. A quick question settles it: are there more children than pizzas? Then each child gets less than a whole.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing a division as a fraction, writing a fraction as a division, and putting the numbers in the right order.

## Subtopic: Sharing with Fraction Answers

In Term 1, a division with a remainder was written like 7 divided by 2 is 3 remainder 1. When the thing being shared can be cut, the remainder can be shared too. 7 oranges are shared between 2 friends. Each gets 3 whole oranges, and the 1 orange left over is cut in half. So each friend gets 3 and 1/2 oranges.

The leftover is shared by the same number of people, so it goes over the divisor. 10 cakes shared among 4 people: each gets 2 whole cakes, and 2 cakes are left. The 2 leftover cakes shared among 4 is 2/4 each. So each person gets 2 and 2/4 cakes. 23 divided by 4 is 5 remainder 3, and the 3 shared by 4 is 3/4, so 23 divided by 4 is 5 and 3/4.

5 rotis are shared among 3 cousins. Each gets 1 whole roti, and 2 rotis are left over. 2 shared by 3 is 2/3. Each cousin gets 1 and 2/3 rotis. Check: three cousins with 1 and 2/3 each make 3 and 6/3, which is 3 plus 2, which is 5 rotis.

Whether to write a remainder as a fraction depends on the story. Oranges, rotis, cakes and money can be cut or changed into smaller amounts, so a fraction makes sense. Learners on a bus or chairs in a hall cannot be cut, so the remainder stays as a whole number of leftovers.

The questions for this section are with you now: sharing leftovers as fractions, writing divisions as mixed numbers, and deciding when a fraction answer makes sense.

## Subtopic: Grouping Problems with Fractions

Grouping asks how many equal pieces fit into an amount. A recipe for amagwinya needs flour measured with a quarter-cup scoop. How many quarter cups are in 3 cups? Each cup holds 4 quarter cups, so 3 cups hold 3 times 4, which is 12 quarter cups.

A 5 litre bottle of juice is poured into half-litre bottles. Each litre fills 2 half-litre bottles, so 5 litres fill 5 times 2, which is 10 bottles. A braai lasts 2 hours, and games are played in slots of 1/3 of an hour. Each hour has 3 thirds, so 2 hours have 6 slots.

When each piece is more than one unit piece, count the unit pieces first. A 3 m ribbon is cut into pieces that are each 3/4 m long. 3 m is 12 quarter metres. Each piece uses 3 quarter metres, and 12 divided by 3 is 4. So there are 4 pieces. Check: 4 pieces of 3/4 m make 12/4 m, which is 3 m.

Notice that grouping with fractions gives an answer bigger than the amount you started with: 12 quarter cups from 3 cups. That is because the pieces are small, so many of them fit. It is the same as asking how many R5 coins make R100: small pieces, many of them.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 3 pizzas among 4 children as 4/3 each; the amount being shared goes on top, so it is 3/4. Two: leaving 7 oranges between 2 as 3 remainder 1 when the orange can be cut; share the leftover to get 3 and 1/2. Three: writing 23 divided by 4 as 5 and 3/5; the leftover is shared by 4, so it is 5 and 3/4. Four: saying there are 3/4 quarter cups in 3 cups; count the pieces, 4 in each cup, to get 12.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting how many fraction pieces fit into an amount, and grouping with pieces bigger than one unit.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the pizzas, the oranges and the flour scoop — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Top Shared by Bottom

3 pizzas for 4 children: cut every pizza into quarters. Each child gets 3 quarters: 3/4.

So 3/4 also means 3 divided by 4. The top is shared by the bottom.

12/4 is 12 divided by 4, which is 3.

Keep the sharing picture in mind and try the questions for this part: fractions as divisions.

## Subtopic: Share the Leftovers

7 oranges for 2 friends: 3 each and 1 left. Cut the last one in half. 3 and 1/2 each.

The leftover goes over the number of people. 23 divided by 4 is 5 and 3/4.

5 rotis for 3 cousins: 1 and 2/3 each.

Hold the share-the-leftovers idea in mind and try this part's questions: sharing with fractions.

## Subtopic: How Many Pieces Fit

How many quarter cups in 3 cups? 4 in every cup, so 12.

How many half-litre bottles from 5 litres? 2 in every litre, so 10.

Small pieces means lots of them fit.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A fraction is a division: the top number is shared by the bottom number, so 3/4 is 3 divided by 4. When the thing being shared can be cut, share the remainder as a fraction over the divisor: 23 divided by 4 is 5 and 3/4. To find how many fraction pieces fit into an amount, count how many pieces make one whole and multiply.

Hold the how-many-fit question in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: grouping with fractions.
