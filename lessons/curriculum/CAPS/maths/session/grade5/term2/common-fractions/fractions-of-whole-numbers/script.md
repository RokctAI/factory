# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers finding fractions of whole numbers where the answer is a whole number. The anchor is a Grade 5 class of 36 learners in Kroonstad, R150 that Ayanda has saved, a 64 km cycle race, and a jacket priced at R240 in a sale. The governing idea is that to find a fraction of an amount you share the amount into as many equal parts as the bottom number, which gives one part, and then take as many parts as the top number. Finding unit fractions and other fractions of whole numbers, and solving problems that ask for a part or for what is left, are examinable.

## Subtopic: Unit Fractions of Amounts

A unit fraction has 1 on top, like 1/2, 1/4 or 1/12. To find a unit fraction of an amount, share the amount into that many equal groups. 1/4 of the 36 learners means 36 shared into 4 equal groups: 36 divided by 4 is 9. So 1/4 of 36 is 9 learners.

Every unit fraction works this way: divide by the bottom number. Ayanda spends 1/5 of her R150: R150 divided by 5 is R30. 1/3 of a day of 24 hours is 24 divided by 3, which is 8 hours. 1/12 of an hour of 60 minutes is 60 divided by 12, which is 5 minutes.

A drawing makes it clear. Draw 36 dots, then draw 4 equal circles and share the dots into them one at a time. Each circle ends up with 9 dots. One circle is one quarter. The drawing also shows why the bottom number decides the size of each part: more circles means fewer dots in each.

Times tables make this quick. Knowing that 4 times 9 is 36 tells you straight away that a quarter of 36 is 9. Knowing that 12 times 5 is 60 tells you that a twelfth of 60 is 5.

The questions for this section are with you now: unit fractions of amounts, dividing by the bottom number, and using times tables.

## Subtopic: Other Fractions of Amounts

When the top number is more than 1, find one part first, then take as many parts as the top number says. 3/4 of the 36 learners: one quarter is 9, so three quarters is 3 times 9, which is 27. 27 learners walk to school.

Ayanda saves 2/5 of her R150: one fifth is R30, so two fifths is 2 times R30, which is R60. In the cycle race of 64 km, the riders have finished 5/8 of the distance: one eighth of 64 is 8, so five eighths is 5 times 8, which is 40 km.

Minutes in an hour are a good place to practise twelfths and quarters. 7/12 of an hour: one twelfth of 60 minutes is 5 minutes, so seven twelfths is 35 minutes. 3/4 of an hour: one quarter is 15 minutes, so three quarters is 45 minutes.

A fraction less than 1 always gives an answer less than the whole amount. 3/4 of 36 must be less than 36. If your answer is bigger than the amount you started with, you have probably divided by the top number and multiplied by the bottom number by mistake.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding one part first, then several parts, with learners, money, distances and minutes.

## Subtopic: Fraction Problems: The Part and the Rest

Many problems ask about the part that is left. If 3/4 of the 36 learners walk to school, how many do not walk? The rest is 1/4, which is 9 learners. Or work it out as 36 minus 27, which is also 9. The two parts together must make the whole: 27 plus 9 is 36.

In a sale, a fraction off means a fraction is taken away from the price. A jacket costs R240 and has 1/4 off. 1/4 of R240 is R60. That is the discount, not the new price. The new price is R240 minus R60, which is R180. Read the question carefully: does it ask for the discount or for what you pay?

The same fraction of different amounts gives different answers. 1/2 of 40 is 20, but 1/3 of 90 is 30. So 1/3 of a bigger amount can be more than 1/2 of a smaller amount. A half is only bigger than a third when they are fractions of the same whole.

Different fractions of different amounts can even be equal. 2/3 of 36 is 24, because one third is 12 and two thirds is 24. 3/4 of 32 is also 24, because one quarter is 8 and three quarters is 24.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: dividing by the top and multiplying by the bottom, so 3/4 of 36 becomes 48; divide by the bottom, 4, and multiply by the top, 3, to get 27. Two: stopping after one part, so 3/4 of 36 becomes 9; take 3 parts to get 27. Three: giving the discount as the new price, so the jacket costs R60; take the discount off the price to get R180. Four: believing a half is always more than a third; 1/3 of 90 is more than 1/2 of 40.

The questions for this section are with you now: the part that is left, discounts, and comparing fractions of different amounts.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the class, the pocket money and the jacket — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Share It Out

1/4 of 36: share 36 into 4 equal groups. Each group has 9.

Divide by the bottom number. 1/5 of R150 is R30. 1/12 of 60 minutes is 5 minutes.

Times tables help: 4 times 9 is 36, so a quarter of 36 is 9.

Keep the equal groups in mind and try the questions for this part: one part of an amount.

## Subtopic: One Part, Then More

Find one part. Then take as many parts as the top number.

3/4 of 36: one quarter is 9. Three quarters is 27.

2/5 of R150: one fifth is R30. Two fifths is R60.

Hold the one-part-first rule in mind and try this part's questions: fractions of amounts.

## Subtopic: The Part or the Rest

27 learners walk. The rest, 9, do not. 27 plus 9 is 36, the whole class.

1/4 off R240 is R60 off. You pay R180.

1/3 of 90 is more than 1/2 of 40. The amount matters.

Pocket summary of the lesson. To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the bottom number to get one part, then multiply by the top number. The answer for a fraction less than 1 is less than the amount. The part and the rest always add up to the whole. A fraction off is a discount, so take it away to find the new price. The same fraction of different amounts gives different answers.

Hold the part-and-rest idea in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: fraction problems.
