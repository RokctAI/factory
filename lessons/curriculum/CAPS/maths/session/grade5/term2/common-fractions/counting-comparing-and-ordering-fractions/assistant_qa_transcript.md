### subtopic_1 — Counting in Fractions
**Assistant:** Why does counting in quarters go past 1? I thought fractions were always less than 1.

**Tutor:** Fractions can be any size, because they count pieces, and you can have as many pieces as you like. When you count in quarters, every step adds one quarter. After four steps you have 4/4, which is exactly one whole, like four quarter-kilometre markers making one kilometre. Nothing stops you from taking another step. The fun run is 3 km long, so the runners keep going: 5/4, 6/4, 7/4, and so on, up to 12/4 at the finish. We usually write these as mixed numbers, like 1 and 1/4 or 2 and 3/4, because a whole number plus a fraction is easier to picture than a large number of quarters. Fractions such as 3/4, where the top number is smaller than the bottom, are less than 1, and they are the ones you probably met first. Fractions where the top number is equal to or bigger than the bottom are 1 or more. Both kinds are fractions, and you will use both. In real life, recipes ask for 1 and 1/2 cups of flour, races are 2 and 1/2 kilometres long, and a family can eat 1 and 1/4 pizzas. Fractions bigger than 1 are everywhere.

### subtopic_2 — Comparing Fractions
**Assistant:** Why is 1/8 smaller than 1/4 when 8 is bigger than 4?

**Tutor:** Because the bottom number does not tell you how big the fraction is; it tells you how many pieces the whole is cut into, and more cuts make smaller pieces. Picture one slab of chocolate. Cut it into 4 equal pieces and each piece is a quarter. Cut an identical slab into 8 equal pieces and each piece is an eighth. The same amount of chocolate is shared into twice as many pieces, so each eighth is only half the size of a quarter. If someone offered you 1/4 or 1/8 of a slab, you would choose 1/4, because it is the bigger piece. This is one of the most important ideas about fractions, and it feels backwards at first because we are used to bigger numbers meaning more. It helps to say the fraction in words: one quarter means one of four pieces, and one eighth means one of eight pieces. The more people share a cake, the smaller each slice. The rule only works when the top numbers are the same. 3/8 is less than 3/4, but 7/8 is more than 1/4, because 7 big pieces of eighths outweigh 1 piece of quarters. When the top numbers differ too, use a fraction wall or compare both fractions with a half.

### subtopic_3 — Ordering Fractions on a Number Line
**Assistant:** Can I compare half of a pizza with half of a sandwich?

**Tutor:** You can say that both are halves, but you cannot say they are the same amount of food, because they are halves of different wholes. A fraction always means a part of something, and the size of the part depends on the size of the whole. Half of a large family pizza is much more food than half of a sandwich, and even half of a large pizza is more than half of a small pizza. That is why comparing fractions only works fairly when the fractions are of the same whole. In mathematics questions, it is understood that fractions being compared are of the same whole unless the question says otherwise, so 1/2 is always bigger than 1/3 in a test. But in real-life problems, read carefully. If Sipho eats 1/2 of a small loaf and Lerato eats 1/3 of a big loaf, it is possible that Lerato ate more bread, even though 1/3 is less than 1/2. This idea will matter later when you find fractions of amounts. Half of R40 is R20, and half of R100 is R50: the same fraction of a different amount gives a different answer. Always ask what the whole is.
