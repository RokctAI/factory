### subtopic_1 — A Fraction Is a Division
**Assistant:** Why is 3 shared by 4 the same as 3/4?

**Tutor:** Because when you actually do the sharing, each person ends up with three quarters. Try it with 3 pizzas and 4 children. You cannot give anyone a whole pizza without someone missing out, so cut every pizza into 4 equal slices. Now you have 12 slices, each a quarter of a pizza. Hand them out one at a time, round and round, until they are gone. Every child receives 3 slices, which is 3 quarters, or 3/4 of a pizza. The picture shows that sharing 3 among 4 gives three quarters each. There is a neat shortcut in the picture too. Each child gets one quarter from each pizza: a quarter of pizza one, a quarter of pizza two, a quarter of pizza three. One quarter from each of 3 pizzas is 3 quarters. This is why the fraction line is sometimes read as divided by. The fraction 3/4 is the answer to the division 3 divided by 4, written in a compact way. Once you see this, many things fit together: 12/4 must be 3, because 12 divided by 4 is 3, and 5/5 must be 1, because 5 divided by 5 is 1. It will also help you in later grades, when you turn fractions into decimals by dividing.

### subtopic_2 — Sharing with Fraction Answers
**Assistant:** When do I write a remainder as a fraction, and when do I just leave it as a remainder?

**Tutor:** Ask whether the thing being shared can sensibly be cut or split into smaller parts. If it can, the remainder can be shared too, and a fraction answer makes sense. Oranges, rotis, cakes, pizzas, ribbon, juice and money can all be split, so 7 oranges shared between 2 friends gives 3 and 1/2 each, and 23 cakes shared among 4 gives 5 and 3/4 each. If the things cannot be cut, the remainder stays as whole leftovers. 23 learners in groups of 4 make 5 groups with 3 learners left over, not 5 and 3/4 groups, because you cannot have three quarters of a learner. Chairs, buses, eggs in trays and marbles are the same. Sometimes the story wants the remainder rounded up instead, like an extra bus for the leftover learners, as you saw earlier this term. So there are three choices after dividing: share the leftover as a fraction, keep it as a whole-number remainder, or round up or down. The story always decides. In a plain calculation with no story, your teacher may ask for either form, so read the instructions. Both 5 remainder 3 and 5 and 3/4 are correct answers to 23 divided by 4; they just describe the leftover differently.

### subtopic_3 — Grouping Problems with Fractions
**Assistant:** How can I get 12 quarter cups out of only 3 cups? Isn't that more than I started with?

**Tutor:** You get 12 pieces, but the total amount of flour is still exactly 3 cups. The number 12 counts small pieces, not cups. Each piece is only a quarter of a cup, so 12 of them make 12 quarters, which is 3 whole cups. Nothing has been added; the same flour has been counted in a smaller unit. It is like changing R100 into R5 coins. You get 20 coins, which sounds like more than 100 only if you forget that each coin is worth less than R100. In the same way, 5 litres of juice fills 10 half-litre bottles. The number of bottles, 10, is bigger than 5, because each bottle holds only half a litre. Whenever you count how many small pieces fit into an amount, the answer will be bigger than the amount when the pieces are smaller than one whole. A quick check is to put the pieces back together: 10 half litres is 5 litres, and 12 quarter cups is 3 cups. If the pieces add back to the amount you started with, your grouping answer is right.
