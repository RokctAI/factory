# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 1 and Term 2 topics: whole numbers with place value, ordering and rounding; number sentences; addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of whole numbers with checks; common fractions, including adding, subtracting, fractions of amounts, sharing and equivalent fractions; geometric and numeric patterns with flow diagrams and tables; properties of 2D shapes; and symmetry. The anchor is a winter sports day at a primary school in Bloemfontein, with 3 586 spectators on Friday and 4 729 on Saturday, 432 learners in teams, oranges cut into quarters at half-time, a pyramid of cones, and a field marked out with shapes. The governing idea is that every topic is a tool, and estimation and inverse operations check every tool. All the Term 1 and Term 2 topics are examinable in the test.

## Subtopic: Whole Numbers and the Four Operations

Adding: how many spectators came altogether? 3 586 plus 4 729. In columns: 6 plus 9 is 15, write 5 carry 1; 8 plus 2 plus 1 is 11, write 1 carry 1; 5 plus 7 plus 1 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 3 plus 4 plus 1 is 8. The total is 8 315. Estimate: 3 600 plus 4 700 is 8 300. Close.

Subtracting: how many more came on Saturday? 4 729 minus 3 586 is 1 143. Check by adding back: 3 586 plus 1 143 is 4 729.

Place value and rounding: the season's ticket money is R126 450. The 2 is worth R20 000. Rounded to the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit, 4, which rounds down, so it is R126 000. Rounded to the nearest 100, look at the tens digit, 5, which rounds up, so it is R126 500.

Multiplying: 236 adult tickets at R15 each. Split 15 into 10 and 5: 236 times 10 is 2 360, and 236 times 5 is 1 180. The total is R3 540.

Dividing: 432 learners are put into teams of 18. Box times 18 equals 432. Long division: 18 into 43 goes 2 times, which uses 36, leaving 7. Bring down the 2 to make 72. 18 into 72 goes 4 times exactly. So there are 24 teams. Check: 24 times 18 is 432. Remainders need thought: 350 bottles of water packed in crates of 24 gives 14 full crates with 14 bottles left over, so 15 crates are needed to pack them all.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding, subtracting, rounding, multiplying and dividing with checks.

## Subtopic: Common Fractions

At half-time, every orange is cut into quarters. The red team eats 2 and 3/4 oranges and the blue team eats 1 and 2/4 oranges. Together: 2 plus 1 is 3, and 3/4 plus 2/4 is 5/4, which is 1 and 1/4. So they eat 4 and 1/4 oranges. Add the tops and keep the bottom.

Subtracting: 2 and 1/4 oranges are left on one plate and 3/4 is taken away. Break one whole into quarters: 2 and 1/4 is 1 and 5/4. 1 and 5/4 minus 3/4 is 1 and 2/4.

Fractions of amounts: 3/4 of the 24 learners in a team run in the relay. Find 1/4 first: 24 divided by 4 is 6. Then 3 times 6 is 18 learners. 2/5 of R150 is R60, because 1/5 is R30.

Sharing: 3 oranges shared equally among 4 learners gives each 3/4 of an orange. 7 oranges shared between 2 learners gives each 3 and 1/2.

Equivalent fractions: 6/8 of an orange is the same as 3/4, because the top and bottom of 3/4 are both multiplied by 2. To compare 2/3 and 3/4, change both to twelfths: 2/3 is 8/12 and 3/4 is 9/12, so 3/4 is bigger.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding and subtracting fractions and mixed numbers, fractions of amounts, sharing and equivalent fractions.

## Subtopic: Patterns, Shapes and Symmetry

Geometric patterns: the cones are stacked in triangles: 1, 3, 6, 10. The differences are 2, 3 and 4, growing by 1, so the next triangle uses 15 cones.

Numeric patterns: a runner trains 2 km, 4 km, 6 km, 8 km, a constant difference of 2. A message about the results spreads 3, 6, 12, 24, a constant ratio of 2, so the next number is 48.

Flow diagrams and tables: points for the obstacle race use the rule times 5, then minus 2. Input 6 gives 30, then 28. Working backwards, an output of 43 gives 43 plus 2, which is 45, and 45 divided by 5, which is 9. A table with inputs 1, 2, 3, 4 and outputs 3, 7, 11, 15 grows by 4, so the rule is times 4, then minus 1.

Shapes and symmetry: the field is a rectangle, an irregular quadrilateral with 2 lines of symmetry, not its diagonals. The centre circle is not a polygon, and every line through its centre is a line of symmetry. The pennants are triangles with two equal sides, with 1 line of symmetry each. A hexagon has 6 straight sides, and a regular one has 6 lines of symmetry.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: answering 14 crates for 350 bottles; the 14 left-over bottles need a 15th crate. Two: adding the bottoms, so 3/4 plus 2/4 becomes 5/8; keep the bottom and get 5/4. Three: continuing 3, 6, 12, 24 with 30 by adding 6; it doubles, so 48. Four: counting the diagonals of the rectangle as lines of symmetry; folding shows they are not.

The questions for this section are with you now: patterns, flow diagrams and tables, shapes and symmetry.
