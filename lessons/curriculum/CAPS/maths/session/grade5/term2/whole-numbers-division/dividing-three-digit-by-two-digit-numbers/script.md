# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers division of whole three-digit numbers by two-digit numbers. The anchor is a fruit farm in Ceres packing 864 apples into boxes of 24, a school in Welkom sending 576 learners on an outing in buses with 32 seats each, and R945 shared equally among 15 learners. The governing idea is that division asks how many equal groups fit, that it is the inverse of multiplication, and that big chunks of the divisor can be taken away at a time until nothing, or only a small remainder, is left. Dividing by multiples of 10, dividing three-digit numbers by two-digit numbers, finding remainders and checking by multiplication are examinable.

## Subtopic: Dividing by Tens and by One Digit

Every division fact comes from a multiplication fact. 6 times 8 is 48, so 48 divided by 6 is 8. Place value stretches the facts. 6 times 80 is 480, so 480 divided by 6 is 80. 60 times 8 is 480, so 480 divided by 60 is 8. Dividing by 60 gives a smaller answer than dividing by 6, because the groups are ten times bigger, so fewer of them fit.

To divide by a multiple of 10, use the fact and then think about the tens. 840 divided by 20: 84 tens shared into groups of 2 tens gives 42 groups. Check: 42 times 20 is 840. 720 divided by 24 is 30, because 24 times 3 is 72, so 24 times 30 is 720.

To divide a three-digit number by a one-digit number, split it into friendly parts. 864 divided by 4: 800 divided by 4 is 200, and 64 divided by 4 is 16, so the answer is 216. Choose parts that the divisor goes into exactly. For 864 divided by 4, splitting into 800 and 64 works well.

These skills are the building blocks of dividing by two-digit numbers. Knowing that 24 times 10 is 240 and 24 times 30 is 720 lets you take big chunks out of a division at once.

The questions for this section are with you now: division facts from multiplication facts, dividing by multiples of 10, and dividing by one digit.

## Subtopic: Dividing by Taking Away Chunks

The farm has 864 apples to pack into boxes of 24. How many boxes? We want to know how many 24s fit into 864. Instead of taking away one 24 at a time, take away big chunks. 30 boxes hold 30 times 24, which is 720 apples. 864 minus 720 leaves 144 apples.

Now take another chunk. 144 is 6 times 24, because 6 times 20 is 120 and 6 times 4 is 24. Taking away 144 leaves 0. The chunks were 30 boxes and 6 boxes, so the answer is 30 plus 6, which is 36 boxes.

Smaller chunks also work, but they take more steps. Take away 10 times 24, which is 240, three times: 864, 624, 384, 144. Then 6 times 24 takes the last 144. The chunks are 10, 10, 10 and 6, which add up to 36. The bigger your first chunk, the faster you finish, so look for the biggest multiple of 10 that fits.

The school in Welkom has 576 learners and buses with 32 seats. 10 buses carry 320 learners, leaving 256. 256 is 8 times 32, because 8 times 30 is 240 and 8 times 2 is 16. So 10 plus 8 is 18 buses, with every seat filled.

The questions for this section are with you now: taking away big chunks, adding up the groups, and choosing a good first chunk.

## Subtopic: Building a Table and Checking

A short table of multiples of the divisor makes chunking quick. For 24: 1 times 24 is 24, 2 times is 48, 5 times is 120, 10 times is 240, 20 times is 480. Doubling and halving build the table fast: 10 times is 240, so 5 times is half of that, 120, and 20 times is double, 480.

Check every division by multiplying back, because multiplication undoes division. If 864 divided by 24 is 36, then 36 times 24 must be 864. 30 times 24 is 720 and 6 times 24 is 144, and 720 plus 144 is 864. Correct. If R945 shared among 15 learners gives R63 each, then 63 times 15 must be 945: 60 times 15 is 900 and 3 times 15 is 45, so 945. Correct.

Some divisions do not come out exactly. 500 divided by 32: 15 times 32 is 480, and 16 times 32 is 512, which is too many. 500 minus 480 leaves 20, so the answer is 15 remainder 20. The check is 15 times 32 plus 20, which is 480 plus 20, which is 500.

The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor. If it is not, another group fits. 900 divided by 24 is not 36 remainder 36, because 36 is more than 24. One more group fits, so the answer is 37 remainder 12. Check: 37 times 24 is 888, and 888 plus 12 is 900.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: turning the division around and thinking of 24 divided by 864; the number being shared or grouped always comes first. Two: giving 144, the amount left after the first chunk, as the answer; the answer is the number of groups, 36. Three: leaving a remainder bigger than the divisor, such as 36 remainder 36 for 900 divided by 24; another group fits, so it is 37 remainder 12. Four: skipping the check; multiply the answer by the divisor and add the remainder to get back the number you started with.

The questions for this section are with you now: building a table of multiples, checking by multiplying back, and finding remainders.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the apple boxes, the buses and the shared money — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Use Your Times Tables

Division runs times tables backwards. 6 times 80 is 480, so 480 divided by 6 is 80.

Bigger groups mean fewer groups. 480 divided by 60 is only 8.

24 times 30 is 720, so 720 divided by 24 is 30.

Keep the tables in mind and try the questions for this part: division facts.

## Subtopic: Big Bites

864 apples, boxes of 24. Take a big bite: 30 boxes use 720 apples. 144 are left.

Take a small bite: 6 boxes use 144. Nothing is left.

Add the bites: 30 plus 6 is 36 boxes.

Hold the big-bite idea in mind and try this part's questions: dividing by chunks.

## Subtopic: Count, Check, Remainder

Check by multiplying back: 36 times 24 is 864. It matches.

Some divisions leave a little over. 500 divided by 32 is 15 remainder 20. The remainder must be smaller than 32.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Division asks how many equal groups fit. Use times tables and place value to divide by multiples of 10. Take away big chunks of the divisor, then add up the number of groups. Build a short table of the divisor's multiples to choose chunks quickly. The remainder must be smaller than the divisor. Check by multiplying the answer by the divisor and adding the remainder.

Hold the multiply-back check in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: checking and remainders.
