### subtopic_1 — Prisms and Cubes
**Assistant:** Is a cube a rectangular prism?

**Tutor:** Yes. A rectangular prism has two matching rectangle ends joined by rectangles, so all 6 of its faces are rectangles. A square is a special rectangle, because it has four square corners like every rectangle, and its sides just happen to be equal. A cube has 6 square faces, so all its faces are rectangles, and it fits the description of a rectangular prism exactly. What makes the cube special is that all its faces are the same size and all its edges are the same length. So the cube is a member of the rectangular prism family, in the same way that a square is a member of the rectangle family. The reverse is not true. A cereal box is a rectangular prism, but its faces are different sizes, so it is not a cube. When you compare them, list what is the same, 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices, and what is different, equal square faces against faces of different sizes. If a test asks you to name a sugar cube, the best answer is cube, because it is the most exact name, but saying rectangular prism would not be wrong.

### subtopic_2 — Cubes and Rectangular Prisms Compared
**Assistant:** How can I tell a prism from a pyramid quickly?

**Tutor:** Look at the top. A prism has a top face that matches its bottom face, so it looks the same at both ends, like a box, a tent or a pencil. The faces joining the two ends are rectangles. A pyramid has a base at the bottom, but no top face at all. Its side faces are triangles, and they lean in to meet at a single point, the apex. If you could slide a slice off a prism, parallel to its base, the slice would be the same size as the base all the way up. A slice of a pyramid gets smaller and smaller as you go up, until it is just a point. Both are named in the same way, by a face: a prism by its two ends, and a pyramid by its base. So a triangular prism has two triangle ends and three rectangles, which is 5 faces. A triangular-based pyramid has one triangle base and three triangle sides, which is 4 faces. A square-based pyramid has one square base and four triangle sides, which is 5 faces. Counting faces is a good check after you have looked at the top.

### subtopic_3 — Cylinders, Cones and Pyramids
**Assistant:** Why is a tin of beans called a cylinder and not a circle?

**Tutor:** Because a circle is flat and a tin is solid. A circle is a 2D shape: it has length and width but no thickness, so it can be drawn on paper but not picked up. A tin of beans is a 3D object: it has height as well, and it holds the beans inside. The circles are only its two flat faces, one at the top and one at the bottom. Between them is a curved surface, which you could peel off as the label and flatten into a rectangle. The whole object, with its two circle faces and its curved surface, is called a cylinder. The same mix-up happens with other shapes. A die is a cube, not a square; the squares are its faces. A tent is a triangular prism, not a triangle; the triangles are its ends. A good habit is to use 2D names, like circle, square and triangle, for flat shapes and for the faces of objects, and 3D names, like cylinder, cube and prism, for whole solid objects. That way your description always says exactly what you mean.
