# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between millimetres, centimetres, metres and kilometres, with whole numbers and common fractions, and solving problems in contexts involving length. The anchor is a 5 km fun run in Knysna that a Grade 5 class helps to plan: they measure route sections in different units, cut ribbon for medals and place water stations along the way. The governing idea is that 10 mm make 1 cm, 100 cm make 1 m and 1 000 m make 1 km, so you multiply to change to a smaller unit and divide to change to a bigger unit, and you always change to the same unit before you calculate. Converting units, converting fractions of units and solving length problems are examinable.

## Subtopic: Changing Bigger Units to Smaller

The key facts are: 1 cm is 10 mm, 1 m is 100 cm, 1 m is 1 000 mm, and 1 km is 1 000 m. A smaller unit fits into a bigger one many times, so when you change to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger. You multiply.

The fun run is 5 km long. In metres, that is 5 times 1 000, which is 5 000 m. The start banner is 3 m wide, which is 3 times 100, or 300 cm. A medal ribbon is 7 cm wide, which is 70 mm.

Mixed measurements work the same way. A section of the route measures 2 km 350 m. 2 km is 2 000 m, and 2 000 plus 350 is 2 350 m. A table is 1 m 45 cm long: 100 cm plus 45 cm is 145 cm. A ribbon is 12 cm 6 mm: 120 mm plus 6 mm is 126 mm.

Watch out for small parts. 4 m 5 cm is 400 cm plus 5 cm, which is 405 cm, not 45 cm. 3 km 60 m is 3 000 m plus 60 m, which is 3 060 m.

Converting helps you compare. Is a section of 2 km 350 m longer than a section of 2 500 m? Change the first to metres: 2 350 m. 2 350 is less than 2 500, so the 2 500 m section is longer, by 150 m.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing kilometres to metres, metres to centimetres, and centimetres to millimetres.

## Subtopic: Changing Smaller Units to Bigger, and Fractions

To change to a bigger unit, divide. 600 cm divided by 100 is 6, so 600 cm is 6 m. 4 000 m divided by 1 000 is 4, so 4 000 m is 4 km. 90 mm divided by 10 is 9, so 90 mm is 9 cm.

When the division is not exact, write a mixed measurement. 2 500 m is 2 km and 500 m. 375 cm is 3 m and 75 cm.

Fractions of units are common in real life. Half a kilometre is half of 1 000 m, which is 500 m. A quarter of a kilometre is 1 000 divided by 4, which is 250 m. Three quarters of a kilometre is 3 times 250, which is 750 m. Half a metre is 50 cm, a quarter of a metre is 25 cm, and three quarters of a metre is 75 cm. Half a centimetre is 5 mm.

So 2 500 m can also be written as 2 and 1/2 km, because 500 m is half a kilometre. And 1 m 25 cm can be written as 1 and 1/4 m.

Some conversions go between millimetres and metres. 1 m is 1 000 mm, because 1 m is 100 cm and each centimetre is 10 mm. So a banner pole 3 000 mm long is 3 m long. To change millimetres to metres, divide by 1 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing to bigger units, and converting fractions of kilometres, metres and centimetres.

## Subtopic: Solving Length Problems

The route has three sections. The first is 1 km 750 m, the second is 2 and 1/2 km, and the third is still to be measured. The whole route must be 5 km. Change everything to metres: 1 750 m and 2 500 m. Together they make 4 250 m. The third section is 5 000 minus 4 250, which is 750 m, or 3/4 km.

The class has 6 m of ribbon and cuts it into pieces of 25 cm for medals. Change to centimetres: 6 m is 600 cm. 600 divided by 25 is 24, so they can make 24 medals.

Water stations are placed every half kilometre between the start and the finish: at 500 m, 1 000 m, 1 500 m and so on. The last one before the finish is at 4 500 m. That is 9 water stations, because 4 500 divided by 500 is 9.

The steps are always the same: read the problem, change all lengths to the same unit, choose the operation, calculate, and write the answer with its unit. Then check that the answer makes sense.

The finish banner needs 3 m 50 cm of rope, and the start banner needs 2 m 75 cm. Change to centimetres: 350 cm and 275 cm. Together they need 625 cm, which is 6 m 25 cm. The class has a 10 m rope, which is 1 000 cm, so 1 000 minus 625 leaves 375 cm, or 3 m 75 cm.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: multiplying by 100 to change kilometres to metres, so 5 km becomes 500 m; a kilometre is 1 000 m. Two: writing 4 m 5 cm as 45 cm; it is 405 cm. Three: thinking half a kilometre is 50 m; it is 500 m. Four: adding 1 km 750 m and 2 and 1/2 km as 1 plus 2 plus 750 plus 1/2 without changing units.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving route, ribbon and spacing problems by converting first.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Knysna fun run — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Big to Small: Multiply

1 km is 1 000 m. 1 m is 100 cm. 1 cm is 10 mm.

5 km is 5 000 m. 3 m is 300 cm. 4 m 5 cm is 405 cm.

Keep the multiply-for-smaller rule in mind and try the questions for this part: bigger to smaller units.

## Subtopic: Small to Big: Divide

600 cm is 6 m. 2 500 m is 2 and 1/2 km.

Half a kilometre is 500 m. A quarter of a metre is 25 cm.

Hold the divide-for-bigger rule in mind and try this part's questions: smaller to bigger, and fractions.

## Subtopic: Same Unit, Then Solve

6 m of ribbon is 600 cm. 600 divided by 25 is 24 medals.

Pocket summary of the lesson. 10 mm make 1 cm, 100 cm make 1 m, and 1 000 m make 1 km. Multiply to change to a smaller unit and divide to change to a bigger unit. Half a kilometre is 500 m, a quarter is 250 m and three quarters is 750 m; half a metre is 50 cm, a quarter is 25 cm. In a problem, change every length to the same unit before you calculate, and write the unit in your answer.

Hold the same-unit-first rule in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: length problems.
