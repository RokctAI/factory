### subtopic_1 — Changing Bigger Units to Smaller
**Assistant:** How do I remember whether to multiply or divide when I convert?

**Tutor:** Think about the size of the unit and how many of them you need. Small units are like small steps: you need many of them to cover a distance. Big units are like big strides: you need only a few. When you change kilometres to metres, you are changing to a smaller unit, so you will need more of them, and the number must get bigger. That means multiply: 5 km is 5 times 1 000, which is 5 000 m. When you change centimetres to metres, you are changing to a bigger unit, so you need fewer of them, and the number must get smaller. That means divide: 600 cm is 600 divided by 100, which is 6 m. A quick check is to ask whether your answer makes sense. If you changed 5 km to metres and got 5 m, ask yourself whether a 5 km fun run could really be only 5 big strides long. It could not, so you divided when you should have multiplied. Picturing the real length in this way catches most conversion mistakes. Always write the new unit next to your answer, so that you can see what the number means.

### subtopic_2 — Changing Smaller Units to Bigger, and Fractions
**Assistant:** How do I change a fraction of a kilometre into metres?

**Tutor:** Find that fraction of 1 000 m, because 1 km is 1 000 m. For half a kilometre, find half of 1 000, which is 500 m. For a quarter of a kilometre, divide 1 000 by 4, which gives 250 m. For three quarters, find one quarter first, 250 m, and then multiply by 3, which gives 750 m. For a tenth of a kilometre, divide 1 000 by 10, which gives 100 m. Mixed numbers work too. 2 and 1/2 km is 2 000 m plus half of 1 000 m, which is 2 000 plus 500, or 2 500 m. The same method works for metres, using 100 cm. Half a metre is 50 cm, a quarter of a metre is 25 cm, and three quarters of a metre is 75 cm. For centimetres, use 10 mm: half a centimetre is 5 mm. You already know how to find a fraction of an amount from your work on fractions, and this is exactly the same skill. The only extra step is remembering how many of the smaller unit fit into the bigger one.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Length Problems
**Assistant:** Why must I change all the lengths to the same unit before adding?

**Tutor:** Because you can only add amounts of the same kind, just as you cannot add 3 apples and 2 bananas and say you have 5 apples. In the fun run, one section is 1 km 750 m and another is 2 and 1/2 km. If you add the numbers as they are, you might get 1 plus 2 plus 750 plus a half, which makes no sense, because the 1 and the 2 are kilometres and the 750 is metres. Change both to metres: 1 km 750 m is 1 750 m, and 2 and 1/2 km is 2 500 m. Now both are in the same unit, and 1 750 plus 2 500 is 4 250 m. If you want the answer in kilometres, change it back at the end: 4 250 m is 4 km 250 m, or 4 and 1/4 km. The same rule applies to subtracting, comparing and ordering. Usually it is easiest to change everything to the smallest unit in the problem, because then you work with whole numbers and avoid fractions until the very end.
