### subtopic_1 — What Perimeter Is
**Assistant:** What is the difference between perimeter and area?

**Tutor:** Perimeter is the distance around the outside of a shape, and area is the amount of flat space inside it. Think of a sports field. The perimeter is how far you would run if you jogged once around the edge. The area is how much grass there is to mow. The frame around Thandeka's painting measures the perimeter, 100 cm of wooden strip. The paint covering the paper measures the area. Because they measure different things, they use different units. Perimeter is a length, so it is measured in centimetres, metres and so on. Area is measured in square units, such as square centimetres, which you will meet in the next lesson. A quick way to remember: perimeter has rim in it, and a rim goes around the edge, like the rim of a plate or the rim of a wheel. If a question asks for fencing, framing, a border, or a distance around, it is asking for perimeter. If it asks how much carpet, paint or grass covers a shape, it is asking for area.

### subtopic_2 — Measuring Perimeter with Rulers and Tapes
**Assistant:** How can I work out a missing side when I know the perimeter?

**Tutor:** Use what you know about the shape and work backwards. In a rectangle, opposite sides are equal, so the perimeter is made of two lengths and two widths. If the perimeter is 100 cm and the length is 30 cm, the two lengths together are 60 cm. Take that away from the perimeter: 100 minus 60 is 40 cm. That 40 cm is the two widths together, so one width is 40 divided by 2, which is 20 cm. Check by adding all four sides: 30 plus 20 plus 30 plus 20 is 100 cm. For a square, all four sides are equal, so divide the perimeter by 4. A square with a perimeter of 60 cm has sides of 15 cm. For a regular hexagon, divide by 6. For an irregular shape, add the sides you know and subtract from the perimeter to find the one missing side. Always check your answer by adding all the sides again to see if you get the perimeter you started with.

### subtopic_3 — Perimeter Problems and Checks
**Assistant:** Why does an L shape have 6 sides?

**Tutor:** Because the L has a corner cut out, and that cut-out adds two extra sides. Start at the bottom left corner of the garden bed and walk around it. You go along the bottom, 8 m. Then up the right side, 4 m. Then the edge turns inwards, and you walk 3 m to the left. Then it turns up again, and you walk 2 m. Then it turns left along the top, 5 m. Then you walk down the left side, 6 m, back to where you started. Count the turns: you walked along 6 sides. A plain rectangle has only 4 sides, but the step in the L adds two more. If you only measured four sides, you would miss part of the boundary, and the fence would have a gap. Interestingly, the perimeter of this L shape, 28 m, is the same as the perimeter of the full 8 m by 6 m rectangle, because the inward step and the upward step together replace the missing corner exactly. But you should still measure all 6 sides to be sure, because not every shape works out that neatly.
