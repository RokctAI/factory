# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers measuring perimeter using rulers or measuring tapes. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in eMalahleni with two projects: they glue wooden strips around their paintings to make frames, and they put a low wire fence around an L-shaped garden bed behind the classroom. The governing idea is that perimeter is the total length of the boundary of a shape, so you measure every side, write each length with the same unit, and add them all. Explaining perimeter, measuring sides, calculating perimeters of regular and irregular shapes, and solving perimeter problems are examinable.

## Subtopic: What Perimeter Is

Perimeter is the distance all the way around the outside of a shape. Imagine an ant walking along the edge of a painting, starting at one corner and walking until it gets back to the same corner. The distance it walks is the perimeter.

Thandeka's painting is a rectangle 30 cm long and 20 cm wide. Its four sides are 30 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm and 20 cm. The perimeter is 30 plus 20 plus 30 plus 20, which is 100 cm. So she needs 100 cm of wooden strip for the frame.

Perimeter is a length, so it is measured in length units: millimetres, centimetres, metres or kilometres. It is not the space inside the shape; that is area, which is the next lesson.

Every shape with straight sides has a perimeter you can find by adding its sides. A triangle with sides of 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm has a perimeter of 18 cm. A square with sides of 25 cm has a perimeter of 4 times 25, which is 100 cm, because all four sides are equal.

The questions for this section are with you now: what perimeter means, and adding sides to find the perimeter.

## Subtopic: Measuring Perimeter with Rulers and Tapes

To measure a perimeter, measure each side one at a time, write the lengths down, and then add. Use a ruler for small shapes like a painting or a page. Use a measuring tape for big shapes like a garden bed or a classroom floor.

Mark the corner where you start, so you know when you have gone all the way around. Tick each side as you measure it. Record every length in the same unit: if one side is 1 m and another is 45 cm, write both in centimetres, 100 cm and 45 cm, before adding.

For a shape with a curved edge, like a round table or a tree trunk, a measuring tape can bend around the curve. If you only have a ruler, lay a piece of string around the edge, mark it, straighten it, and measure the string.

The class measures a hexagon-shaped tile with a ruler: each side is 8 cm. The perimeter is 6 times 8, which is 48 cm. A regular shape has all sides equal, so you can multiply one side by the number of sides. An irregular shape has sides of different lengths, so you must measure and add each one.

The questions for this section are with you now: measuring sides with rulers and tapes, and finding perimeters of regular and irregular shapes.

## Subtopic: Perimeter Problems and Checks

The garden bed is L-shaped. Its sides, measured with a tape and going around in order, are 8 m, 4 m, 3 m, 2 m, 5 m and 6 m. The perimeter is 8 plus 4 plus 3 plus 2 plus 5 plus 6, which is 28 m. The class buys 28 m of wire for the fence.

Count the sides of an L shape carefully: it has 6 sides, not 4. Missing one side means the fence would have a gap.

Sometimes a side length is not given, but you can work it out. If a rectangle has a perimeter of 100 cm and its length is 30 cm, then the two lengths make 60 cm, leaving 40 cm for the two widths, so each width is 20 cm.

Check that your answer makes sense. The perimeter must be longer than any one side, and for a rectangle it is exactly double the length plus the width.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding only the length and the width, so the painting gets 50 cm of frame instead of 100 cm. Two: missing a side of the L shape and buying only 22 m of wire. Three: adding 1 m and 45 cm as 46; change to the same unit first, 145 cm. Four: counting the squares inside a shape and calling it the perimeter; that is area.

The questions for this section are with you now: L shapes, missing sides and checking perimeter answers.
