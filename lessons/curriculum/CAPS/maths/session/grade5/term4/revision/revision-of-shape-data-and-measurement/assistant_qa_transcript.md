### subtopic_1 — Shapes, Objects and Transformations
**Assistant:** How do I add hours and minutes when the minutes go past 60?

**Tutor:** Add the hours and the minutes separately, then change every 60 minutes into 1 hour. For the bus trip, counting on gave 45 minutes, 3 hours and 40 minutes. The hours make 3. The minutes make 45 plus 40, which is 85. But 85 minutes is more than an hour: 60 of those minutes make 1 hour, and 25 minutes are left over. So 85 minutes is 1 hour 25 minutes. Add that hour to the 3 hours: the trip is 4 hours 25 minutes. Writing 3 hours 85 minutes is like writing 13 units in a column without carrying: the amount is right, but it is not written properly. Time carries at 60, not at 10. The same idea works for seconds, where 60 seconds make 1 minute. A good check is to count on from the start time by your answer: 07:15 plus 4 hours is 11:15, and 25 more minutes is 11:40, which matches the arrival time. If your answer does not land on the end time, look for a mistake in the minutes.

### subtopic_2 — Measurement: Time, Length, Mass and Capacity
**Assistant:** How can I remember the conversions for length, mass and capacity?

**Tutor:** Notice that the big units all use the number 1 000. 1 kilometre is 1 000 metres. 1 kilogram is 1 000 grams. 1 litre is 1 000 millilitres. The word parts help: kilo means a thousand, and milli means a thousandth part. So a kilometre is a thousand metres, a kilogram is a thousand grams, and a millilitre is one thousandth of a litre. The only extra length facts are 100 centimetres in a metre and 10 millimetres in a centimetre. To change to a smaller unit, multiply: 3 and 1/2 km is 3 500 m. To change to a bigger unit, divide: 4 250 g is 4 kg 250 g. For fractions, find the fraction of 1 000: half is 500, a quarter is 250, three quarters is 750. These same steps work for all three measurements, so if you can do one, you can do them all. Time is the odd one out, because it uses 60 and 24, so treat it separately.

### subtopic_3 — Data, Perimeter, Area and Volume
**Assistant:** What is the most important check for a measurement answer?

**Tutor:** Ask whether the answer makes sense in real life, with the unit written next to it. A bus trip from a city to Clarens taking 4 hours 25 minutes sounds reasonable, but 44 hours would not. A hike of 3 500 m sounds right for a walk of a few hours, but 350 m would be over in a few minutes. Each learner carrying 1 500 ml of water fits in a big bottle, but 15 000 ml would be far too heavy to carry. An area of 12 square units for a tent floor drawn on a grid with 12 squares inside makes sense, but if you get 14, check whether you have found the perimeter by mistake. Writing the unit is part of this check, because a number without a unit cannot be judged. Use benchmarks that you know: a metre is about a big stride, a kilogram is a bag of sugar, a litre is a carton of milk, and an hour is about the length of a long lesson. If your answer is far from what the benchmarks suggest, look for a mistake before you move on.
