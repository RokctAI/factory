# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises space and shape, measurement, and data handling from Terms 1 to 4: 2D shapes, symmetry, 3D objects and transformations; time, length, mass and capacity with conversions; collecting, showing and analysing data, and probability; and perimeter, area and volume. The anchor is a two-day Grade 5 camp at a nature reserve near Clarens in the Free State, with tents, a bus trip, a hike, water bottles, rucksacks, a survey around the campfire and a cool box of snacks. The governing idea is that every question starts with looking carefully and choosing the right unit, and ends with checking that the answer makes sense. All of these topics are examinable in the final test.

## Subtopic: Shapes, Objects and Transformations

2D shapes: the camp signs are hexagons, with 6 straight sides. The tent door is a triangle. A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry, but not its diagonals, and a square has 4. A circle is not a polygon, because it has no straight sides.

3D objects: each tent is a triangular prism, with 2 triangle ends and 3 rectangles, 5 faces altogether. The water tank is a cylinder, with 2 circle faces and 1 curved surface. The cool box is a rectangular prism, with 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

Transformations: the floor of the camp kitchen is tiled with regular hexagons that tessellate with no gaps. The path is made of paving bricks translated in rows. A butterfly on a flower shows reflection, with a line of symmetry down its body, and the flower's 6 petals turn into place every sixth of a turn.

Describe shapes with exact words: name the shape or object, count sides or faces, and say how it repeats or whether it has symmetry.

Composite shapes: the camp flag is made by reflecting a right-angled triangle over one of its shorter sides, which gives a bigger triangle with two equal sides and one line of symmetry. The camp map is drawn on grid paper, where shapes are drawn by counting squares along the lines.

The questions for this section are with you now: 2D shapes, symmetry, 3D objects and transformations.

## Subtopic: Measurement: Time, Length, Mass and Capacity

Time: the bus leaves at 07:15 and arrives at 11:40. Count on: 07:15 to 08:00 is 45 minutes, 08:00 to 11:00 is 3 hours, and 11:00 to 11:40 is 40 minutes. 45 plus 40 is 85 minutes, which is 1 hour 25 minutes, so the trip takes 4 hours 25 minutes. The hike starts at 2:30 p.m., which is 14:30 in 24-hour time.

Length: the hike is 3 and 1/2 km, which is 3 000 m plus 500 m, or 3 500 m. Each tent pole is 1 m 20 cm, which is 120 cm.

Capacity: each learner carries 1 and 1/2 l of water, which is 1 500 ml. For 24 learners, that is 24 times 1 500, which is 36 000 ml, or 36 l.

Mass: a rucksack has a mass of 4 kg 250 g, which is 4 250 g. The food for one meal is 2 and 1/2 kg, which is 2 500 g. Together they are 6 750 g, which is 6 kg 750 g.

Always change to the same unit before calculating, and remember that time works in 60s, while length, mass and capacity use 1 000 for km, kg and l.

Calendar and clock: the class counts down to the camp on 26 November from 2 November, and 26 minus 2 is 24 days. On the way, the bus stops for 15 minutes at 09:20, so it leaves again at 09:35.

The questions for this section are with you now: time intervals and 24-hour time, and converting length, mass and capacity.

## Subtopic: Data, Perimeter, Area and Volume

Data: around the campfire, the 24 learners vote for their favourite activity: hiking 9, swimming 7, campfire stories 6 and birdwatching 2. The total is 24. Ordered from smallest to largest: birdwatching 2, campfire 6, swimming 7, hiking 9. The mode is hiking. In a pictograph with one symbol for 2 learners, hiking needs 4 and a half symbols.

Probability: a coin is tossed 20 times to decide which group washes up, giving heads 12 and tails 8. With only 20 trials, a difference like that is normal.

Perimeter and area: the floor of a small tent, drawn on grid paper, is 4 squares by 3 squares. Its area is 3 rows of 4, which is 12 square units. Its perimeter is 4 plus 3 plus 4 plus 3, which is 14 units.

Volume: the cool box is packed with juice boxes shaped like cubes. The bottom layer has 5 rows of 3, which is 15, and there are 2 layers, so it holds 30.

Graphs: a bar graph of the votes needs a scale from 0 in equal steps, such as 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, with the hiking bar halfway between 8 and 10 and all bars the same width.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing the bus trip as 3 hours 85 minutes; 85 minutes is 1 hour 25 minutes, so it is 4 hours 25 minutes. Two: changing 3 and 1/2 km to 350 m; it is 3 500 m. Three: giving the tent floor an area of 14, which is its perimeter; the area is 12 square units. Four: calling the tent a triangle; it is a triangular prism.

The questions for this section are with you now: data, probability, perimeter, area and volume.

## Subtopic: Around the Campfire

Votes: hiking 9, swimming 7, campfire 6, birdwatching 2. Mode: hiking.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Name 2D shapes by their straight sides and 3D objects by their faces, and check for lines of symmetry. Slide, turn and flip shapes, and tessellate with no gaps. Count on in time, remembering 60 minutes in an hour, and add 12 for p.m. in 24-hour time. Multiply or divide by 1 000 for km, kg and l. Tally data, order it and find the mode. Perimeter goes around in length units, area counts squares in square units, and volume counts cubes in cubic units.

Hold the right-unit rule in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: data and measurement.
