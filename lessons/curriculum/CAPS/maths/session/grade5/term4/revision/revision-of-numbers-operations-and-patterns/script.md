# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises numbers, operations and relationships from Terms 1 to 4: whole numbers to six digits with place value, ordering and rounding; addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with checks; common fractions; number sentences; and geometric and numeric patterns with flow diagrams and tables. The anchor is an end-of-year concert at a primary school in George, with ticket money of R136 480, 1 245 people on Friday and 1 878 on Saturday, 48 rows of chairs, programmes packed in bundles, and ticket prices worked out with a flow diagram. The governing idea is that every number skill is a tool, and estimation and inverse operations check every tool. All of these topics are examinable in the final test.

## Subtopic: Whole Numbers, Adding and Subtracting

Place value: the concert raised R136 480. The 1 is worth 100 000, the 3 is worth 30 000, and the 6 is worth 6 000. In expanded notation: 100 000 plus 30 000 plus 6 000 plus 400 plus 80.

Rounding: to the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit, 4, which rounds down, so R136 000. To the nearest 100, look at the tens digit, 8, which rounds up, so R136 500. To the nearest 10 it stays R136 480, because it already ends in 0.

Adding: 1 245 people came on Friday and 1 878 on Saturday. In columns: 5 plus 8 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 4 plus 7 plus 1 is 12, write 2 carry 1; 2 plus 8 plus 1 is 11, write 1 carry 1; 1 plus 1 plus 1 is 3. The total is 3 123 people. Estimate: 1 200 plus 1 900 is 3 100. Close.

Subtracting: 1 878 minus 1 245 is 633, so 633 more people came on Saturday. Check by adding: 1 245 plus 633 is 1 878.

Comparing and ordering: compare from the left. R136 480 is more than last year's R129 950, because 3 ten thousands beat 2 ten thousands.

Ordering: the concert money for four years was R129 950, R136 480, R118 700 and R131 205. Compare the ten thousands first, then the thousands. From smallest to largest: R118 700, R129 950, R131 205, R136 480.

The questions for this section are with you now: place value, rounding, comparing, adding and subtracting.

## Subtopic: Multiplying, Dividing and Fractions

Multiplying: the hall has 48 rows of 26 chairs. Split 26 into 20 and 6: 48 times 20 is 960, and 48 times 6 is 288. 960 plus 288 is 1 248 chairs. Estimate: 50 times 25 is 1 250. Close.

Dividing: the 1 248 chairs are packed away in stacks of 24. 1 248 divided by 24 is 52 stacks. Check: 52 times 24 is 1 248. Remainders need thought: 750 programmes are tied in bundles of 40, which gives 18 bundles with 30 left over. To tie up all the programmes, 19 bundles are needed.

Fractions of amounts: 3/8 of the 48 rows are reserved for parents. 1/8 of 48 is 6, so 3/8 is 18 rows.

Adding and subtracting fractions: 2/5 of the hall is decorated on Friday and 1/5 on Saturday. Together that is 3/5, because the bottoms stay the same. 1 and 3/4 cakes minus 3/4 of a cake leaves 1 whole cake. Equivalent fractions: 6/8 of a pizza is the same as 3/4.

Division can also give fractions. 7 cakes shared equally among 4 tables gives each table 1 cake, with 3 cakes left over. The 3 left-over cakes are cut into quarters, so each table also gets 3/4 of a cake, which makes 1 and 3/4 cakes each.

The questions for this section are with you now: multiplying and dividing with checks, remainders, and common fractions.

## Subtopic: Patterns and Number Sentences

Numeric patterns: the rows at the front have 20 chairs, then 24, 28 and 32. The constant difference is 4, so the next rows have 36 and 40 chairs. A ratio pattern doubles: 3, 6, 12, 24, then 48.

Geometric patterns: cups stacked in a triangle use 1, 3, 6 and 10 cups. The differences grow by 1, so the next stack uses 15.

Flow diagrams: tickets cost R35 each, plus a R5 booking fee per order. The rule is times 35, then plus 5. An order of 4 tickets costs 4 times 35, which is 140, plus 5, which is R145. Working backwards from an order of R180: 180 minus 5 is 175, and 175 divided by 35 is 5 tickets.

Number sentences: box times 26 equals 1 248. Use the inverse: 1 248 divided by 26 is 48, so the box is 48. Substitute to check: 48 times 26 is 1 248.

Tables show the same rule. Inputs of 1, 2, 3 and 4 tickets give outputs of R40, R75, R110 and R145. The outputs go up by 35 each time, and the first output is 35 plus 5. Checking every column against the rule times 35, then plus 5, shows the table is right.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: rounding 136 480 to the nearest 1 000 as 137 000, using the 8 instead of the hundreds digit, 4. Two: answering 18 bundles for 750 programmes and leaving 30 untied. Three: adding 2/5 and 1/5 to get 3/10 by adding the bottoms. Four: adding the booking fee before multiplying, so 4 tickets cost 9 times 35, which is R315.

The questions for this section are with you now: patterns, flow diagrams and number sentences.

Hold the hall in mind and try this part's questions: operations and fractions.

## Subtopic: Price Patterns

Times 35, plus 5: 4 tickets cost R145. R180 means 5 tickets.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Read place value from the left, round by looking at one digit to the right, and compare from the left. Add and subtract in columns and check with the inverse. Multiply by splitting, divide by long division, and decide what a remainder means. Find fractions of amounts by finding one part first, and keep the bottom number when adding fractions with the same bottom. Find pattern rules from the jumps, follow flow diagrams in order, and undo them in reverse. Estimate first and check every time.

Hold the price machine in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: patterns and number sentences.
