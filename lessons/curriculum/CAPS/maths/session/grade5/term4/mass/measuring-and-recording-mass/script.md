# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers estimating and practically measuring mass using bathroom scales, kitchen scales and balances, and recording, comparing and ordering mass in grams and kilograms. The anchor is Rendani, a Grade 5 learner who helps at her family's fruit and vegetable stall in Tzaneen on Saturdays, weighing mangoes, avocados, tomatoes and potatoes for customers. The governing idea is that mass is how heavy something is, measured in grams and kilograms, with 1 000 grams in 1 kilogram, and that every scale must start at zero and be read by working out what each mark is worth. Choosing units and tools, reading scales, and comparing and ordering masses are examinable.

## Subtopic: Grams, Kilograms and Scales

Mass is how heavy something is. Small masses are measured in grams, g, and larger masses in kilograms, kg. 1 kilogram is 1 000 grams.

Benchmarks help you estimate. A paper clip has a mass of about 1 g. A mango is about 300 g to 500 g. A loaf of bread is about 700 g. A bag of sugar is often 1 kg or 2 kg. A Grade 5 learner might be about 35 kg.

Different scales suit different jobs. A kitchen scale measures food in grams, up to a few kilograms. A bathroom scale measures people in kilograms. A balance has two pans: put an object on each side, and the heavier side goes down. A balance can also measure mass by putting known mass pieces, like 100 g and 500 g pieces, on the other side until the pans are level.

Mass is not the same as size. A big bag of chips is light, and a small bag of potatoes is heavy. You cannot judge mass by looking alone; you need to feel it or weigh it.

Kitchen scales and bathroom scales measure different ranges. A kitchen scale might go up to 5 kg, so it cannot weigh a person. A bathroom scale goes up to about 150 kg, but its marks are too far apart to weigh a single mango accurately.

Estimating mass by holding objects helps you build benchmarks. Rendani holds a 1 kg bag of sugar in one hand and a pocket of potatoes in the other, and guesses that the potatoes are about twice as heavy. The scale shows 2 kg, so her estimate was right.

The questions for this section are with you now: grams and kilograms, benchmarks and choosing a scale.

## Subtopic: Reading Scales

Before weighing, check that the scale shows 0 with nothing on it. If the pointer starts at 50 g, every reading will be 50 g too heavy.

Rendani's kitchen scale has a dial with numbers every 100 g: 0, 100, 200 and so on. Between each pair of numbers there are 4 small marks, which make 5 equal spaces. 100 divided by 5 is 20, so each small mark is worth 20 g. A mango makes the pointer stop 2 marks past 300, so it has a mass of 340 g.

The bathroom scale has numbers every 10 kg, with a small mark for every 1 kg. When Rendani stands on it, the pointer stops 7 marks past 30, so her mass is 37 kg.

A digital scale shows the mass as numbers on a screen, such as 450 g or 2 kg. Read the unit next to the number carefully.

With a balance, put the tomatoes in one pan and mass pieces in the other until the pans are level. If a 500 g piece and a 200 g piece balance the tomatoes, the tomatoes have a mass of 700 g.

Some scales have a bowl on top. Rendani puts the empty bowl on the scale first and turns the knob until the pointer is back at 0, so that the scale measures only the fruit. This is called zeroing the scale.

The questions for this section are with you now: zeroing scales, working out what each mark is worth and reading dials and balances.

## Subtopic: Recording, Comparing and Ordering

Always record mass with its unit: 340 g, not 340. Without the unit, a reader cannot tell 340 g of mango from 340 kg, which is about as heavy as five adults.

To compare masses, change them to the same unit. A bag of avocados is 1 kg 200 g, which is 1 200 g. A box of tomatoes is 750 g. A pocket of potatoes is 2 kg, which is 2 000 g. A mango is 450 g. From lightest to heaviest: mango 450 g, tomatoes 750 g, avocados 1 200 g, potatoes 2 000 g.

Be careful with numbers that look small. 2 kg is heavier than 750 g, even though 2 is less than 750, because 2 kg is 2 000 g.

Estimate first, then weigh. A customer's bag of 6 mangoes, each about 400 g, should be about 2 400 g, or 2 kg 400 g. If the scale shows 240 g, something is wrong.

The stall sells fruit by mass. Mangoes cost R40 for each kilogram, so a 1 kg bag costs R40 and a 2 kg bag costs R80. Rendani weighs each bag carefully, because a wrong reading would cost either the customer or the stall money.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: not checking that the scale starts at 0. Two: counting each small mark as 10 g when it is worth 20 g, so 340 g is read as 320 g. Three: thinking 2 kg is lighter than 750 g because 2 is less than 750. Four: judging by size, thinking the big bag of chips is heavier than the small bag of potatoes.

The questions for this section are with you now: recording masses with units, and comparing and ordering them.
