### subtopic_1 — Stopwatches and Timing
**Assistant:** Why does the smallest time win a race?

**Tutor:** Because a stopwatch measures how long the swimmer took to finish, and the fastest swimmer is the one who takes the least time. In the 50 m race, Johan's stopwatch stopped at 55 seconds and Bongani's at 70 seconds. Johan got to the wall 15 seconds before Bongani, so Johan was faster. It is easy to mix this up, because in most games a bigger score wins. In a race, the stopwatch is like a measure of how slow you were, so the smaller the better. This is different from events like the long jump, where the bigger distance wins. When you order race results, put the smallest time first as the winner. Change all the times to the same unit before comparing: 1:02 looks like it might be smaller than 0:58 at first glance, but 1:02 is 62 seconds and 0:58 is only 58 seconds. So Lerato, with 58 seconds, beat Ayesha, with 62 seconds. A quick check is to ask who touched the wall first. That swimmer always has the smallest time.

### subtopic_2 — Reading Calendars
**Assistant:** How can I find the day of the week for any date in a month?

**Tutor:** Start from a date whose day you know, and count on in sevens, because the days of the week repeat every 7 days. In October, 1 October is a Thursday. Add 7 to get the next Thursday, 8 October, then 15, 22 and 29. Now you have all the Thursdays. For any other date, find the closest Thursday before it and count on one day at a time. For 17 October, the closest Thursday before it is 15 October: Friday 16, Saturday 17. So 17 October is a Saturday. For 31 October, the closest Thursday is 29: Friday 30, Saturday 31. You can also count backwards: 14 October is the day before Thursday 15, so it is a Wednesday. To cross into the next month, remember how many days the month has. October has 31 days, so the day after 31 October is 1 November, which here is a Sunday. Using sevens means you never have to count every single day.

### subtopic_3 — Using Clocks, Watches and Calendars Together
**Assistant:** How do I remember how many days are in each month?

**Tutor:** There are two common ways. The first is a rhyme: thirty days has September, April, June and November; all the rest have thirty-one, except February alone, which has twenty-eight days clear and twenty-nine in each leap year. The second is the knuckle method. Make two fists and hold them side by side. Starting with the first knuckle on the left, say the months in order: January on a knuckle, February in the dip between knuckles, March on the next knuckle, April in a dip, and so on. When you run out of knuckles on the first fist, jump to the first knuckle of the other fist for August. Months on a knuckle have 31 days. Months in a dip have 30 days, except February, which has 28, or 29 in a leap year. Both July and August land on knuckles, which is why they both have 31 days. Knowing the number of days in each month is important when you count days between dates, which you will do in the next lesson.
