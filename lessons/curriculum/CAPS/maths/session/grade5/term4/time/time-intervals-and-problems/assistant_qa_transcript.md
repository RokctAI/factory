### subtopic_1 — Units of Time and Converting
**Assistant:** Why can I not just subtract the times like ordinary numbers?

**Tutor:** Because time does not work in tens and hundreds. In ordinary numbers, 100 ones make a hundred, but in time, only 60 minutes make an hour. If you write 10:20 as 1 020 and 06:45 as 645 and subtract, you get 375. That might look like 3 hours 75 minutes, but 75 minutes is more than an hour, so the answer is wrong in a confusing way. The real interval is 3 hours 35 minutes. The safe method is to count on. From 06:45, jump 15 minutes to 07:00, then 3 hours to 10:00, then 20 minutes to 10:20. The jumps add to 3 hours 35 minutes. Each jump is easy because you are always going to a whole hour or from one. You can draw this as a number line with the start time on the left, the whole hours marked in the middle, and the end time on the right. Then write each jump above its arc and add them up. This method works for any two times, even across midnight.

### subtopic_2 — Finding Time Intervals
**Assistant:** How do I change minutes into hours and minutes?

**Tutor:** Divide by 60, because there are 60 minutes in an hour. The answer to the division is the number of whole hours, and the remainder is the minutes left over. For 215 minutes: 60 goes into 215 three times, because 3 times 60 is 180, and 215 minus 180 leaves 35. So 215 minutes is 3 hours 35 minutes. For 150 minutes: 2 times 60 is 120, and 150 minus 120 leaves 30, so it is 2 hours 30 minutes. A common mistake is to treat the minutes like a decimal and say 150 minutes is 1 hour 50 minutes, as if there were 100 minutes in an hour. Check by changing back: 2 hours 30 minutes is 2 times 60, which is 120, plus 30, which is 150 minutes. The same method changes seconds into minutes and seconds, also using 60, and hours into days and hours, using 24. For 96 hours, 96 divided by 24 is exactly 4, so it is 4 days.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Time Problems
**Assistant:** What is a decade, and how do I work with long time intervals?

**Tutor:** A decade is 10 years, and a century is 100 years. Long intervals are found the same way as short ones, by subtracting or counting on, but using years. The Kruger National Park was proclaimed in 1926. From 1926 to 2026 is 2026 minus 1926, which is 100 years. 100 years is 10 decades, because 100 divided by 10 is 10, or 1 century. You can use decades to describe your own life too. A learner who is 11 years old has lived for 1 decade and 1 year. Months and weeks also appear in long intervals. A school year has about 40 weeks of school, and a year has 12 months and just over 52 weeks. When you change between these units, remember the facts: 7 days in a week, 12 months in a year, 10 years in a decade. Be careful with months, because they do not all have the same number of days, so changing months into days needs a calendar. Long intervals help us understand history, like how long ago a park was proclaimed or when a building was built.
