### subtopic_1 — Analogue Clocks: Hours, Minutes and Seconds
**Assistant:** Why do we need a.m. and p.m.?

**Tutor:** Because a 12-hour clock shows each time twice a day, and a.m. and p.m. tell you which one you mean. The clock face only has the numbers 1 to 12, so the hands point to the same place at 5:30 in the early morning and at 5:30 in the late afternoon. If Kagiso's ticket said only 5:30, his family would not know whether to arrive at the station before sunrise or after school. The letters a.m. mean the time is in the first half of the day, from midnight until just before noon. The letters p.m. mean the second half, from noon until just before midnight. So 5:30 a.m. is very early morning, and 5:30 p.m. is late afternoon. Noon is written 12:00 p.m. and midnight is 12:00 a.m., which surprises many people, so it helps to say noon and midnight in words when you can. When you write a 12-hour time, always add a.m. or p.m. unless it is completely clear from the sentence, such as I eat supper at 6:30 in the evening.

### subtopic_2 — 12-Hour Time with a.m. and p.m.
**Assistant:** How do I change 24-hour time to 12-hour time?

**Tutor:** Look at the hour, which is the first two digits. If it is from 01 to 11, the time is in the morning, so keep the hour and write a.m. 09:20 becomes 9:20 a.m. If the hour is 12, it is just after noon, so keep it and write p.m. 12:30 becomes 12:30 p.m. If the hour is 13 or more, subtract 12 and write p.m. 17:30 becomes 5:30 p.m., because 17 minus 12 is 5. 21:05 becomes 9:05 p.m., because 21 minus 12 is 9. If the hour is 00, it is the first hour after midnight, so write 12 and a.m. 00:15 becomes 12:15 a.m. The minutes never change in either direction. A good check is to think about the day. 17:30 is in the late afternoon, when school is out, so 5:30 p.m. makes sense. If you got 7:30 p.m. by taking away 10, ask yourself whether the bus really arrives in the evening darkness; working it out properly shows it does not.

### subtopic_3 — 24-Hour Time
**Assistant:** How do I read the hour hand when it is between two numbers?

**Tutor:** The hour hand moves slowly between the numbers as the hour passes, so look at the number it has just passed, not the one it is getting close to. At 7:00 the hour hand points exactly at the 7. As the minutes go by, it creeps towards the 8. At 7:30, it is halfway between the 7 and the 8. At 7:45, it is three quarters of the way to the 8, so it looks very close to the 8. But it is still seven o'clock and something, because it has not reached the 8 yet. So 7:45 is the right reading, not 8:45. A good check is to read the minute hand first. If the minute hand shows 45 minutes, the hour hand should be well past the halfway point, close to the next number. If the minute hand shows 10 minutes, the hour hand should be only a little past the number. Reading the minute hand first and then asking which number the hour hand has passed makes analogue clocks much easier to read.
