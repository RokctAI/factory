# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers calculating time intervals given in seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years and decades, and solving problems in contexts involving time. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Polokwane on a four-day school tour to the Kruger National Park, with a timetable for the bus, a game drive, and a guide who talks about the history of the park. The governing idea is that time does not work in tens like ordinary numbers: 60 seconds make a minute and 60 minutes make an hour, so you find intervals by counting on in friendly steps to the next hour and then in whole hours. Converting units of time, finding intervals, and finding start and end times are examinable.

## Subtopic: Units of Time and Converting

The units of time build on one another. 60 seconds make 1 minute. 60 minutes make 1 hour. 24 hours make 1 day. 7 days make 1 week. 12 months make 1 year, and a year has 365 days, or 366 in a leap year, which is just over 52 weeks. 10 years make 1 decade, and 100 years make 1 century.

To change to a smaller unit, multiply. The tour lasts 4 days, which is 4 times 24, or 96 hours. 3 weeks is 3 times 7, which is 21 days. 2 decades is 20 years. 5 minutes is 5 times 60, which is 300 seconds.

To change to a bigger unit, divide, and write any remainder in the smaller unit. 150 seconds divided by 60 is 2 remainder 30, so it is 2 minutes 30 seconds. 215 minutes divided by 60 is 3 remainder 35, so it is 3 hours 35 minutes.

Mixed times can be changed into one unit. 3 hours 35 minutes is 3 times 60, which is 180, plus 35, which is 215 minutes.

Weeks, months and years are used for longer plans. The class saved money for the tour for 6 months, which is half a year. They paid a deposit 8 weeks before the trip, which is 8 times 7, or 56 days before.

The questions for this section are with you now: converting between seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, years and decades.

## Subtopic: Finding Time Intervals

A time interval is the time from a start to an end. The bus leaves Polokwane at 06:45 and reaches the park gate at 10:20. How long is the trip?

Count on in friendly steps. From 06:45 to 07:00 is 15 minutes. From 07:00 to 10:00 is 3 hours. From 10:00 to 10:20 is 20 minutes. Add the parts: 3 hours, and 15 plus 20, which is 35 minutes. The trip takes 3 hours 35 minutes.

Do not subtract the times as if they were ordinary numbers. 10:20 is not one thousand and twenty, and there are not 100 minutes in an hour, so subtracting 645 from 1 020 gives 375, which is not 3 hours 75 minutes or anything else that makes sense.

A number line helps. Mark the start time, jump to the next whole hour, jump in whole hours as far as you can, and then jump to the end time. Label each jump and add them.

For longer intervals, use the same idea with bigger units. The tour leaves on Monday morning and returns on Thursday afternoon. From Monday to Thursday is 3 days, plus some hours.

Some intervals cross midnight. On the last night, the teachers stay up from 21:30 until 00:15 to listen for animals. From 21:30 to 22:00 is 30 minutes, from 22:00 to 00:00 is 2 hours, and from 00:00 to 00:15 is 15 minutes. The total is 2 hours 45 minutes.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding the time between a start time and an end time.

## Subtopic: Solving Time Problems

Finding an end time: the afternoon game drive starts at 15:30 and lasts 2 hours 45 minutes. Add the hours first: 15:30 plus 2 hours is 17:30. Then add the minutes: 17:30 plus 30 minutes is 18:00, and 15 more minutes is 18:15. The drive ends at 18:15.

Finding a start time: supper is at 19:00, and the cooks need 1 hour 30 minutes to prepare it. Count back: 19:00 minus 1 hour is 18:00, and 30 minutes earlier is 17:30. They must start at 17:30.

Long intervals: the guide explains that the Kruger National Park was proclaimed a national park in 1926. From 1926 to 2026 is 100 years, which is 10 decades, or 1 century.

Always check: does the answer make sense? A bus trip of 3 hours 35 minutes from Polokwane to the park is reasonable. A game drive ending before it started is not.

Short intervals are measured in seconds. The guide says a cheetah's fast chase lasts about 30 seconds, and a learner's sprint in the 100 m race lasts about 15 seconds, so the chase is about twice as long. He also says a lion can sleep about 20 hours a day, which leaves only about 4 hours awake, because a day has 24 hours.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting 06:45 from 10:20 like ordinary numbers and getting 3 hours 75 minutes. Two: thinking there are 100 minutes in an hour, so 150 minutes is called 1 hour 50 minutes; it is 2 hours 30 minutes. Three: adding 2 hours 45 minutes to 15:30 and writing 17:75; 75 minutes is 1 hour 15 minutes, so the time is 18:15. Four: thinking a decade is 100 years; it is 10 years.

The questions for this section are with you now: end times, start times and long intervals in years and decades.
