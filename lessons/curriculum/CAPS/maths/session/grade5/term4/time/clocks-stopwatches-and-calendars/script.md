# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers using clocks, watches and stopwatches, and reading calendars. The anchor is a primary school in Mossel Bay preparing for its October swimming gala: coaches time each swimmer with stopwatches, the programme lists event start times, and the school calendar shows practice days and the gala date. The governing idea is that a stopwatch measures how long something takes, a clock or watch tells what time it is, and a calendar organises days into weeks and months, so each tool answers a different question about time. Reading stopwatches, comparing times, and reading and using calendars are examinable.

## Subtopic: Stopwatches and Timing

A stopwatch measures how long something takes. Press start when the race begins and stop when the swimmer touches the wall. Press reset to set it back to zero for the next race.

Many stopwatches show minutes and seconds with a colon between them. 0:58 means 0 minutes and 58 seconds. 1:02 means 1 minute and 2 seconds. Some also show parts of a second after a dot, but for this gala the coaches round to the nearest second.

In the 50 m race, the times are: Lerato 0:58, Ayesha 1:02, Johan 0:55 and Bongani 1:10. In a race, the smallest time wins, because that swimmer took the least time. Change all the times to seconds to compare them: 1 minute is 60 seconds, so 1:02 is 62 seconds and 1:10 is 70 seconds. From fastest to slowest: Johan 55 seconds, Lerato 58, Ayesha 62 and Bongani 70.

The difference between two times tells you by how much one swimmer beat another. Johan beat Bongani by 70 minus 55, which is 15 seconds.

Coaches also use stopwatches for longer races. In the 100 m race, one swimmer finishes in 2:15, which is 2 minutes and 15 seconds. 2 minutes is 120 seconds, so 2:15 is 135 seconds. Changing to seconds makes it easy to compare with a time like 2:08, which is 128 seconds.

Stopwatches can also time things other than races. The coach times how long each swimmer rests between lengths, and how long the whole warm-up lasts. A warm-up that shows 12:30 on the stopwatch took 12 minutes and 30 seconds, which is 12 times 60 plus 30, or 750 seconds.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading stopwatches, changing minutes and seconds to seconds, and ordering race times.

## Subtopic: Reading Calendars

A calendar shows the days of a year arranged in months and weeks. A week has 7 days, from Monday to Sunday on most South African calendars. A year has 12 months and 365 days, or 366 days in a leap year.

The months do not all have the same number of days. April, June, September and November have 30 days. February has 28 days, or 29 in a leap year. All the other months have 31 days. Some people use the knuckles of their fists to remember: months that land on a knuckle have 31 days.

In this year's calendar, 1 October is a Thursday. Every 7 days later is also a Thursday, so 8, 15, 22 and 29 October are Thursdays. To find the day for 17 October, count on from Thursday 15 October: Friday 16, Saturday 17. The gala is on Saturday 17 October.

Dates can be written in words, 17 October, or in numbers. In South Africa, the numbers are often written day, month, year, as 17/10, or year first, as in 2026-10-17 on many forms. Always check which order a form uses.

A calendar also shows how the weeks fit into a month. October has 31 days, which is 4 weeks and 3 days, because 4 times 7 is 28 and 31 minus 28 is 3. So in October, three days of the week appear 5 times and the others appear only 4 times. This year, those three days are Thursday, Friday and Saturday.

The questions for this section are with you now: days in each month, finding days of the week, and writing dates.

## Subtopic: Using Clocks, Watches and Calendars Together

The gala programme says the first race starts at 09:00 and a new race starts every 6 minutes. The second race starts at 09:06, the third at 09:12, and the fourth at 09:18. Lerato's race is the fifth, at 09:24, so she checks her watch and gets to the starting block at 09:20.

Practices are on Tuesdays and Thursdays in October. The first Tuesday is 6 October, because Thursday 1 October is followed by Friday 2, Saturday 3, Sunday 4, Monday 5 and Tuesday 6. The Tuesdays are then 6, 13, 20 and 27 October. With the 5 Thursdays, 1, 8, 15, 22 and 29, there are 9 practices in October.

Each tool answers its own question. The calendar answers which day. The clock or watch answers what time. The stopwatch answers how long.

The school calendar also helps with planning backwards. The gala is on Saturday 17 October, and entry forms are due one week before, on Saturday 10 October. A swimmer who wants 3 full weeks of training before the gala must start 21 days before it, on Saturday 26 September.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the biggest stopwatch time wins; in a race, the smallest time wins. Two: reading 1:02 as 102 seconds; it is 1 minute and 2 seconds, which is 62 seconds. Three: saying every month has 30 days, so October ends on the 30th; October has 31 days. Four: counting a week as 5 days, so the Thursday after 1 October is called 6 October; a week is 7 days, so it is 8 October.

The questions for this section are with you now: programmes, watches and planning with a calendar.
