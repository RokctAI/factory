# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers calculating the number of days between any two dates within the same year or in consecutive years. The anchor is Siphokazi, a Grade 5 learner in Komani in the Eastern Cape, who keeps a countdown on the fridge calendar: to her cousin's visit, to the last day of school and to a family trip in January. The governing idea is that you count the days from the first date to the end of its month, add the days in any full months in between, and then add the days in the last month, using the correct number of days for each month. Counting days in the same month, across months and across a new year are examinable.

## Subtopic: Days in the Same Month

When both dates are in the same month, subtract the smaller date from the bigger one. Today is 10 October, and Siphokazi's cousin arrives on 25 October. 25 minus 10 is 15, so the cousin arrives in 15 days.

Think of it as jumps on a number line. From 10 to 11 is one jump, from 11 to 12 is another, and so on until 25. There are 15 jumps, and each jump is one day. So the number of days from 10 October to 25 October is 15.

Some questions count both the first day and the last day, such as how many days does a festival last if it runs from 10 October to 25 October. The festival includes both 10 and 25 October, so it lasts 16 days: the 15 jumps plus the starting day. Read the question carefully to see which kind it is. In this lesson, days between two dates means the number of jumps, unless the question says otherwise.

Check by counting on from the first date: 10 October plus 15 days is 25 October.

Siphokazi also counts down to her birthday on 30 October, starting on 18 October. 30 minus 18 is 12, so her birthday is 12 days away. To check, she counts on from 18 in her head, 19, 20 and so on up to 30, which is 12 jumps.

Counting on is a good way to check. From 10 October, count on 5 days to 15 October, then 10 more days to 25 October. 5 plus 10 is 15, which matches the subtraction. Counting on in friendly jumps like this is also how you will check longer counts.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting days between two dates in the same month.

## Subtopic: Days Across Months

When the dates are in different months, break the count into parts. Siphokazi wants to know how many days there are from 20 October to the last day of school, 9 December.

First, count from 20 October to the end of October. October has 31 days, so 31 minus 20 is 11 days. Next, add any full months in between. All of November is in between, and November has 30 days. Last, add the days in December up to the 9th: 9 days. The total is 11 plus 30 plus 9, which is 50 days.

The number of days in each month matters. April, June, September and November have 30 days. February has 28, or 29 in a leap year. All the others have 31. Using 30 for every month gives wrong answers whenever a 31-day month or February is involved.

Another example: from 25 January to 5 March in a normal year. January: 31 minus 25 is 6 days. February: 28 days. March: 5 days. Total: 6 plus 28 plus 5, which is 39 days. In a leap year, February has 29 days, so the total is 40.

Some gaps cross more than one full month. From 14 August to 3 November: August has 31 days, so 31 minus 14 is 17 days. All of September is 30 days, and all of October is 31 days. Then there are 3 days in November. 17 plus 30 plus 31 plus 3 is 81 days.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting days across one or more months.

## Subtopic: Days Into the Next Year

The same method works when the second date is in the next year. The family trip starts on 15 January, and Siphokazi starts counting on 20 December. December has 31 days, so from 20 December to 31 December is 11 days. Then 15 more days in January. 11 plus 15 is 26 days.

For longer gaps, add more full months. From 15 November to 10 February: November, 30 minus 15 is 15 days; all of December, 31 days; all of January, 31 days; and 10 days in February. 15 plus 31 plus 31 plus 10 is 87 days.

Check with an estimate. From 15 November to 15 February is about 3 months, roughly 90 days. 87 days is a little less, which makes sense because the second date is the 10th, not the 15th.

December and January both have 31 days, so the count from a date in December to the same date in January is always 31 days. From 5 December to 5 January: 31 minus 5 is 26 days in December, and 5 days in January, which makes 31 days.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting dates in different months directly, as in 9 minus 20 for 20 October to 9 December. Two: using 30 days for October, so the count from 20 October to the end of the month becomes 10 instead of 11. Three: adding both the starting day and all the jumps when the question asks only for days between. Four: forgetting that February has only 28 days in a normal year.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting days from one year into the next, and checking with estimates.

## Subtopic: Into the New Year

20 December to 15 January: 11 plus 15 is 26 days.

Pocket summary of the lesson. In the same month, subtract the dates. Across months, count the days left in the first month, add the days in each full month between, and add the days in the last month. Use 30 days for April, June, September and November, 28 or 29 for February, and 31 for the rest. The same method works into the next year.
