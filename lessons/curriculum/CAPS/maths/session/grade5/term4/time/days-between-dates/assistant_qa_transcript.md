### subtopic_1 — Days in the Same Month
**Assistant:** Why do I subtract 20 from 31 to find the days left in October?

**Tutor:** Because October has 31 days, and you want to know how many jumps there are from 20 October to 31 October, the last day of the month. On a number line, the jumps from 20 to 31 are 20 to 21, 21 to 22, and so on, up to 30 to 31. Subtracting tells you how many jumps there are without counting each one: 31 minus 20 is 11. Then the next jump, from 31 October to 1 November, is counted as the first day of November. That is why, when you add a full month or the days in the last month, you count from the 1st. For the last month, the number of days up to 9 December is 9, because the jumps from 30 November to 1 December, 1 to 2 December, and so on up to 8 to 9 December make 9 jumps. If you use 30 instead of 31 for October, you will lose a day. So always check how many days the first month has before you subtract.

### subtopic_2 — Days Across Months
**Assistant:** How do I know whether to count the first day too?

**Tutor:** Read the question carefully and think about what is being counted. If the question asks how many days from one date to another, or how many days until something happens, you count the jumps from the first date to the second: from 10 October to 25 October is 15 days. If you wake up on 10 October and the cousin arrives on 25 October, you have 15 more sleeps. But if the question asks how long something lasts, such as a holiday from 10 October to 25 October with both days included, you count every day in the holiday, including the first. That is 15 jumps plus the first day, which is 16 days. A calendar helps: shade every day of the holiday and count the shaded days. Questions in this lesson use the first meaning unless they say otherwise, such as including both days. When you write your answer, say which you have counted, for example 15 days from 10 to 25 October.

### subtopic_3 — Days Into the Next Year
**Assistant:** How can I check my answer when there are several months?

**Tutor:** Use an estimate based on whole months, and then count again a different way. For 15 November to 10 February, think: from 15 November to 15 February is 3 months. Months have about 30 days, so that is about 90 days. The second date is 5 days before the 15th, so the answer should be about 85 to 90 days. The calculation gave 87, which fits. Another check is to count on from the first date month by month. From 15 November, add 30 days to reach 15 December. Add 31 days to reach 15 January. Add 31 days to reach 15 February. That is 92 days. Then go back 5 days to 10 February: 92 minus 5 is 87. Two different methods giving the same answer makes you sure. Also check the number of days you used for each month, especially February and the 30-day months. A small mistake with a month's length is the most common cause of a wrong answer.
