# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers reading, telling and writing time in 12-hour and 24-hour formats on analogue and digital instruments, in hours, minutes and seconds. The anchor is Kagiso, a Grade 5 learner travelling with his family on a long-distance bus from Gqeberha to Cape Town: the station has a big analogue clock, his ticket and the timetable use 24-hour time, and his digital watch uses a.m. and p.m. The governing idea is that every way of showing time describes the same moment, and you can change between them by knowing that a day has 24 hours, an hour has 60 minutes and a minute has 60 seconds. Reading analogue clocks, using a.m. and p.m., and converting between 12-hour and 24-hour time are examinable.

## Subtopic: Analogue Clocks: Hours, Minutes and Seconds

An analogue clock has a face with the numbers 1 to 12 and hands that move around it. The short hand is the hour hand. The long hand is the minute hand. A thin hand that sweeps around quickly is the second hand.

The minute hand goes all the way around in 60 minutes, which is 1 hour. Each number on the face is 5 minutes apart for the minute hand: on the 1 it shows 5 minutes past, on the 3 it shows 15 minutes past, on the 6 it shows 30 minutes past, and on the 9 it shows 45 minutes past. The second hand goes all the way around in 60 seconds, which is 1 minute.

When the bus is boarding, the station clock shows the minute hand on the 9 and the hour hand three quarters of the way from the 7 to the 8. It is 7:45, which can be said as forty-five minutes past seven or quarter to eight. The hour hand is not yet on the 8, because it is not yet eight o'clock.

Times after half past are often said as minutes to the next hour. 7:50 is ten to eight, and 7:55 is five to eight. Before half past, use past: 8:10 is ten past eight.

The second hand is useful for short times. When the driver checks the engine, he lets it run for 30 seconds, while the second hand goes halfway around. A sign at the station says the doors close 1 minute before the bus leaves, which is one full turn of the second hand.

Seconds matter for short events. At the station, the big clock's second hand goes all the way around in 60 seconds. When it moves from the 12 to the 6, 30 seconds have passed, and from the 12 to the 3, 15 seconds have passed, just like the minutes for the minute hand.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading the hour, minute and second hands, and saying times past and to the hour.

## Subtopic: 12-Hour Time with a.m. and p.m.

The hours of the day repeat twice on a 12-hour clock: once from midnight to noon, and again from noon to midnight. To show which half of the day we mean, we write a.m. for times from midnight to just before noon, and p.m. for times from noon to just before midnight.

The bus leaves at 7:45 a.m., in the morning. It stops for lunch at 12:30 p.m., just after noon. It arrives in Cape Town at 5:30 p.m., in the late afternoon. Without a.m. or p.m., 5:30 could mean early in the morning or late in the afternoon, which is a big difference when catching a bus.

Kagiso's digital watch shows time as numbers, such as 7:45, with a small a.m. or p.m. next to it. Some digital watches also show seconds: 7:45:20 means 7 hours, 45 minutes and 20 seconds, 20 seconds after 7:45.

Noon is 12:00 p.m., the middle of the day. Midnight is 12:00 a.m., the start of a new day.

Writing times correctly matters on forms. Kagiso writes the departure time on his luggage tag as 7:45 a.m.: the hour, a colon, the minutes with two digits, and then a.m. or p.m. Five past eight is written 8:05, not 8:5.

The questions for this section are with you now: choosing a.m. or p.m., and reading digital clocks with seconds.

## Subtopic: 24-Hour Time

Timetables and tickets use 24-hour time, where the hours are numbered from 00 to 23, so there is no need for a.m. or p.m. Midnight is 00:00. The morning hours stay almost the same, but are written with four digits: 7:45 a.m. is 07:45.

From noon onwards, add 12 to the hour. 12:30 p.m. stays 12:30. 1:00 p.m. is 13:00. Kagiso's arrival at 5:30 p.m. is 17:30, because 5 plus 12 is 17. A late bus at 10:15 p.m. would be 22:15.

To change 24-hour time back to 12-hour time, subtract 12 from any hour bigger than 12, and write p.m. 17:30 is 5:30 p.m., because 17 minus 12 is 5. 21:05 is 9:05 p.m. Hours from 01 to 11 are a.m.: 09:20 is 9:20 a.m. The first hour after midnight, 00, is 12 a.m.: 00:15 is 12:15 a.m.

Always write 24-hour time with four digits and a colon or the letter h: 07:45 or 07h45.

The timetable lists other buses too: 06:30, 09:15, 13:40 and 20:05. In 12-hour time these are 6:30 a.m., 9:15 a.m., 1:40 p.m. and 8:05 p.m. Ordering times is easiest in 24-hour time, because the numbers go up through the whole day without starting again at 12.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: changing 17:30 to 7:30 p.m. by taking away 10 instead of 12; it is 5:30 p.m. Two: writing 5:30 p.m. as 05:30, which is early morning; add 12 to get 17:30. Three: reading 7:45 as 8:45 because the hour hand is close to the 8. Four: writing 12:30 p.m. as 24:30; noon hours stay 12.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing between 12-hour and 24-hour time.

Pocket summary of the lesson. On an analogue clock, the short hand shows hours, the long hand shows minutes, and the thin hand shows seconds; there are 60 seconds in a minute and 60 minutes in an hour.
