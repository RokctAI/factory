# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between millilitres and litres, with whole numbers and fractions, and solving problems in contexts involving capacity and volume. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Cape Town, a city that has faced serious water shortages, running a water-saving project: each learner measures water used at home and works out how much can be saved. The governing idea is that 1 litre is 1 000 millilitres, so you multiply by 1 000 to change litres to millilitres and divide by 1 000 to change back, and that every amount in a problem is changed to the same unit before you calculate. Converting whole numbers and fractions of litres, and solving capacity problems, are examinable.

## Subtopic: Litres to Millilitres and Back

1 litre is 1 000 millilitres. To change litres to millilitres, multiply by 1 000. A 3 l kettle holds 3 times 1 000, which is 3 000 ml. A 5 l bottle of drinking water holds 5 000 ml.

Mixed amounts work the same way. A jug holds 2 l 250 ml. 2 l is 2 000 ml, and 2 000 plus 250 is 2 250 ml. A bucket has 1 l 75 ml left in it: 1 000 plus 75 is 1 075 ml, not 175 ml.

To change millilitres to litres, divide by 1 000, and write what is left over in millilitres. 4 000 ml is 4 l. 4 500 ml is 4 l and 500 ml. 12 300 ml is 12 l and 300 ml.

Check the size of your answer. Changing to the smaller unit, millilitres, makes the number bigger. Changing to the bigger unit, litres, makes the number smaller.

Converting helps when shopping. Water is sold in 500 ml bottles and in 5 l bottles. Ten 500 ml bottles hold 5 000 ml, which is 5 l, the same as one big bottle. Knowing this, the class can compare prices fairly.

Conversions also work with bigger amounts. A 25 l container of water for the school garden holds 25 times 1 000, which is 25 000 ml. A tank holding 2 000 l holds 2 000 times 1 000 ml, which is far too many millilitres to picture, so litres are the sensible unit for tanks.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing litres to millilitres and millilitres to litres.

## Subtopic: Fractions of a Litre

Many containers hold a fraction of a litre. To change a fraction of a litre to millilitres, find that fraction of 1 000 ml.

Half a litre is half of 1 000, which is 500 ml. A quarter of a litre is 1 000 divided by 4, which is 250 ml. That is the size of a measuring cup. Three quarters of a litre is 3 times 250, which is 750 ml. A fifth of a litre is 1 000 divided by 5, which is 200 ml. A tenth of a litre is 100 ml.

Mixed numbers work too. 1 and 1/2 l is 1 000 ml plus 500 ml, which is 1 500 ml. 2 and 1/4 l is 2 000 plus 250, which is 2 250 ml.

Going the other way, 4 500 ml is 4 l and 500 ml, and 500 ml is half a litre, so 4 500 ml is 4 and 1/2 l. 750 ml is 3/4 l.

Some jugs are marked in fractions of a litre: 1/4 l, 1/2 l, 3/4 l and 1 l. These marks are the same as 250 ml, 500 ml, 750 ml and 1 000 ml. Lwazi's mother uses the jug to measure 3/4 l of milk, which is 750 ml.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing fractions and mixed numbers of litres to millilitres, and back.

## Subtopic: Solving Capacity Problems

Lwazi measures his shower: the water fills a 10 l bucket every minute. A 2-minute shower uses 2 times 10, which is 20 l. A bath uses 80 l. Showering instead of bathing saves 80 minus 20, which is 60 l.

A dripping tap fills a 250 ml cup every 10 minutes. In 1 hour there are 6 lots of 10 minutes, so it wastes 6 times 250, which is 1 500 ml, or 1 and 1/2 l. In a day of 24 hours, it wastes 24 times 1 and 1/2, which is 36 l. Fixing the tap saves 36 l a day.

The family drinks water from a 5 l bottle, poured into 250 ml glasses. Change to the same unit: 5 l is 5 000 ml. 5 000 divided by 250 is 20, so the bottle fills 20 glasses.

On Monday the family drinks 1 l 750 ml, and on Tuesday 2 and 1/2 l. Change both to millilitres: 1 750 ml and 2 500 ml. Together that is 4 250 ml, which is 4 l 250 ml, or 4 and 1/4 l.

The class also works out a family's daily water for showers and drinking. Lwazi's family of 4 each take a 2-minute shower of 20 l, and they drink 4 l altogether. 4 times 20 is 80 l, and 80 plus 4 is 84 l a day.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: changing 3 l to 300 ml by multiplying by 100; a litre is 1 000 ml, so it is 3 000 ml. Two: saying a quarter of a litre is 25 ml; it is 250 ml. Three: changing 4 500 ml to 45 l; it is 4 l 500 ml. Four: adding 1 l 750 ml and 2 and 1/2 l without changing units, getting a mix of numbers that means nothing.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving water-saving problems with conversions.
