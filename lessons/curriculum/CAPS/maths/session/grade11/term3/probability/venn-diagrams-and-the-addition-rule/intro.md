Today probability grows up: instead of single events we work with how events combine — this or that, this and that, not this, and whether one event changes the chances of another. We revise the two honest ways of assigning a probability, master the Venn diagram and the addition rule that stops you counting anyone twice, and pin down the vocabulary the examiners love to test: mutually exclusive, complementary, and independent. By the end you will have the product rule as a working lie detector for independence, which is the idea the whole of grade eleven probability stands on.
