Hey, it's Bianca — if the cosine rule looks like a wall of letters, slow down for one second, because underneath it is a formula you have trusted since grade eight. It is Pythagoras — square the two sides and add — plus one correction term that adjusts for the corner not being square. That is all. Angle tighter than 90, the ends pull closer. Angle wider than 90, they push apart. The correction term is just the formula being honest about the hinge.

You only need two versions and they are the same sentence twice. Hunting a side: the lonely side squared equals the other two squared and added, minus twice their product times cosine of the angle FACING the lonely side. Hunting an angle: flip it around — the side facing your angle is the one whose square gets subtracted on top.

Choosing between sine rule and cosine rule feels like a trap, but it is a stocktake, not a guess. Look at what you hold. Two sides hugging an angle — cosine rule. All three sides — cosine rule. A side with its opposite angle known — sine rule. Say your inventory out loud before you write, and the formula picks itself.

Sign errors cause most of the tears here, so build the one habit that ends them: type the whole right-hand side into the calculator in one go, brackets around the cosine, one press of equals. And check the answer against sense — a wide angle must give a longer side. If it did not, hunt the minus sign, not your understanding.

The big scary two-triangle questions are two small questions wearing a coat. Find the side the triangles share, solve the easy triangle for it, carry the unrounded value across, and finish. You have every tool this question needs. You've got this!
