Hey, it's Bianca — if the panic hit the moment the right angle disappeared, you are in good company. Grade ten trigonometry gave you a safety rail: there was always a 90 degrees to lean on. Now the triangles are any shape at all, and it feels like the rules changed. They did not. Two formulae cover everything in this lesson, and both of them are built out of things you already own.

Hold on to this: the area rule is just half base times height wearing a disguise. The height you used to need is manufactured by sine — two sides, times the sine of the angle squeezed between them, times a half. And the sine rule is nothing but a fair exchange rate: side over the sine of its opposite angle is the same number all the way around any triangle. Big angles face big sides. That is the whole machine.

When a question stares back at you, do the inventory before anything else. Write down what you know: which sides, which angles, and — this is the key — which side comes with its opposite angle as a complete pair. A complete pair switches on the sine rule. Two sides hugging an angle switches on the area rule. And two known angles always give you the third one free, because they must total 180.

Two small habits, two safe marks. Round only at the very end — keep the long ugly decimals on the calculator until the final line. And whenever inverse sine gives you an angle, test 180 minus that angle too; write one line saying why you keep it or why you throw it away.

Label the triangle yourself, small letter opposite capital letter, every single time. That one habit quietly prevents most of the errors in this topic. Everything else is a calculator and a plan. You've got this!
