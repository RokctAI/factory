Today we complete the triangle toolkit with the cosine rule — the formula built for exactly the two situations where the sine rule stalls: two sides with the angle between them, and three sides with no angle at all. We prove the rule, watch it collapse into Pythagoras at ninety degrees, and drill both of its directions, including the sign trap that obtuse angles set at the calculator. Then we put the whole toolkit to work on the two-dimensional problems that close every trigonometry section in Paper Two — harbours, bearings, and two triangles sharing one side.
