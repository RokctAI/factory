Today trigonometry breaks out of the right-angled triangle. The area rule and the sine rule work in any triangle at all, and between them they measure the area of a wedge-shaped farm, the distance across a river nobody can swim, and half of Paper Two's trigonometry section. Everything rests on one labelling convention — small letter opposite capital letter — and two formulae which we will prove, because the proofs themselves are examinable. We finish with the chained, multi-step problems where the angle sum hands you a free angle and the sine rule builds the side the area rule needs.
