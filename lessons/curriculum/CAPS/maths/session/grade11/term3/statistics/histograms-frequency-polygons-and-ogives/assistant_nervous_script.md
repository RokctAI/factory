Hey, it's Bianca — if the three graphs are blurring into one another right now, breathe. This topic has almost no algebra in it. It is three pictures of the same forty learners, and once you know which question each picture answers, the plotting rules stop being rules and start being obvious.

The histogram answers: how many in each class? Touching bars, heights are frequencies, tallest bar is the modal class. The frequency polygon is the same information as dots at the rooftop midpoints, joined by straight lines and pulled down to zero at both ends — its whole purpose is comparing two datasets without the bars fighting. The ogive answers a different question: how many so far? Running total, plotted at the upper boundary of each class, starting at zero and climbing — only climbing — until it reaches the total.

Keep the chant: polygon, plain frequency, midpoint. Ogive, cumulative frequency, upper boundary. That one line untangles ninety percent of the plotting confusion in this topic.

Reading the ogive is two ruler moves. Given a position — median at half of n, quartiles at a quarter and three quarters — start on the vertical axis, go across, go down, and report a value with the word approximately. Given a value — more than 35 minutes — start on the horizontal axis, go up, go across, and remember the one subtraction: the curve counts who is below, so more-than needs n minus your reading.

Before you write any answer, glance at the cumulative column and ask whether your reading even lands in the right class. That five-second check catches nearly every slip. Sharp pencil, visible dotted lines, the word estimate — and this becomes one of the friendliest sections in the whole paper. You've got this!
