Picture one: the kraal with four fence posts. Opposite corners of a cyclic quadrilateral face two arcs that together make the whole wall, so their angles add to one eighty. Walk past a corner and the outside angle photocopies the opposite inside one. And the converses are just the same sentences read backwards — show one opposite pair adds to one eighty, and the circle through all four points must exist.

Picture two: the peg and the two ropes. A tangent meets its radius at a perfect ninety — that one is free, an axiom, but still needs its written reason. Two ropes from one peg are always twins, and twins make an isosceles triangle that pays out equal base angles. Rope questions with distances are just Pythagoras on the right angle at the touching point.

Picture three: the echo. The angle between the tangent wall and a chord echoes across that chord, landing exact and unchanged in the segment on the far side. Cross the chord — that is the whole rule. If you did not cross, you did not echo.

And keep the golden habit from last time: every statement gets its short reason — opposite angles of cyclic quad, tangent perpendicular to radius, tangents from same point, tangent-chord. The marks sit on those little phrases.
