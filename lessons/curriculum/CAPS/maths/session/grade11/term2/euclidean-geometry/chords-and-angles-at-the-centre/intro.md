Today we enter circle geometry, where the stakes rise: theorems must now be proved, not just used, and every statement in a rider must carry its reason. We cover the first half of the circle work — the chord theorems and their perpendicular-from-the-centre symmetry, the angle at the centre that doubles the angle at the circumference, the right angle hiding in every semicircle, and equal angles in the same segment. Then we practise the discipline the marks actually attach to: writing a rider solution as a chain of claims, each with its reason, that a marker can follow line by line.
