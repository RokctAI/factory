Hey, it's Bianca — if the formula sheet blurred somewhere around the third fraction today, stay with me for two minutes, because analytical geometry is secretly the friendliest topic in Paper 2. It never surprises you. It only ever asks five things, and every one of them starts from the same move: write down the two points.

Thing one: distance. Blocks east, blocks north, then Pythagoras on the diagonal. Thing two: midpoint. Average the x's, average the y's — both of them. Thing three: gradient. Rise over run, subtracting in the same order top and bottom. Those three instruments are the whole of the first half.

Thing four: equations of lines. Every version is the same recipe — get m, then substitute one point to find c. Two points given? Gradient between them. Parallel line given? Steal its gradient. Perpendicular? Flip the fraction, switch the sign. Then one substitution, and ten seconds to check with the unused point. That check has saved more marks than any formula.

Thing five: inclination. One sentence connects this topic to trigonometry: gradient equals tangent of the angle. Rising line, acute angle, straight from the calculator. Falling line, obtuse angle: take the size, inverse tangent, then one eighty minus. Never hand in a negative angle — inclination lives between zero and one eighty.

Here is tonight's ten-minute fix. Take the points minus two, three, and four, minus five. Find the distance, the midpoint, the gradient, the equation through both, and the inclination. That single pair of points rehearses the entire lesson — and it is the same pair we used today, so you can mark yourself. Ten for the distance, one and minus one for the midpoint, minus four thirds for the gradient.

This topic pays out to the learner who is neat and systematic, not the one who is fast. That can absolutely be you. You've got this!
