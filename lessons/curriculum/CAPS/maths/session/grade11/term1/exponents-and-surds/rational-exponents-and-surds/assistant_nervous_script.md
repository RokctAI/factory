Hey, it's Bianca — I know which line made your chest tighten. It is the one where the exponent turns into a fraction, or where a root sign sits on the bottom of a fraction and refuses to leave. It looks like a new subject. It is not. It is last year's exponent laws with one extra sentence attached.

That sentence is this: the bottom of a fractional exponent is the root, the top is the power. Sixteen to the three quarters — bottom is four, so fourth root of sixteen is two; top is three, so two cubed is eight. Done. Practise that reading on five numbers tonight and the fear goes with it.

The second habit that carries the whole topic is turning every base into a prime. Nine becomes three squared, twenty-seven becomes three cubed, eight becomes two cubed, sixteen becomes two to the fourth. Once every base is a prime, the laws you already know do all the work — add exponents when multiplying, subtract when dividing, multiply when raising a power to a power.

For equations, there are only two shapes. If the unknown is upstairs in the exponent, get both sides onto one base and then match the exponents. If you spot the same exponential appearing squared and unsquared in one line, give it a nickname like k, factorise the quadratic that appears, then swap the nickname back — and reject any negative value of k out loud, because a positive base raised to any real power is never negative.

For surds, three moves and one habit. Move one: pull every perfect square out, so root fifty becomes five root two. Move two: only like surds combine. Move three: to clear a two-term bottom, multiply top and bottom by the same expression with the middle sign flipped. And the habit — the one that protects real marks — is that every time you square both sides of an equation, you go back and test each answer in the original. Squaring invents answers; the check throws them out.

Fraction exponents read bottom-then-top. Bases go prime. Equations go to one base or get a nickname. Surds get tidied, and every squared equation gets checked. That is the whole session, and none of it is beyond you. You've got this!
