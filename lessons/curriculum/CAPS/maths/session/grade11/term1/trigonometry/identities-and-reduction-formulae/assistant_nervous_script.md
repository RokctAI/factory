Hey, it's Bianca — I know exactly where this one stings. The board fills up with sines of one eighty plus this and cosines of ninety minus that, and it feels like twelve new formulae landed on you at once. They did not. There are two ideas in this whole lesson, and you can hold two ideas.

Idea one: every ratio is a direction. Sine is the north-south share of a spinning spoke, cosine is the east-west share, and a minus sign only ever means west or south. So the sign of any ratio is never a mystery — it is geography. Draw the little wheel, mark which quarter the angle is in, and the CAST letters tell you the one ratio that stays positive there.

Idea two: every big angle has an acute home angle, and reduction is just walking it home. Three steps, always the same: find the home angle, let the quarter fix the sign, write the sign in front of the acute ratio. Cosine of one twenty becomes minus cosine sixty. That is the entire machine. The only special case is ninety degrees, which swaps sine and cosine before the sign goes on.

For the identities, keep the two mother statements where you can see them: tangent is sine over cosine, and sine squared plus cosine squared is one. Every proof in this topic opens with a common denominator, a tangent rewrite, or a one-minus-a-square swap — try them in that order and one of them will move the mountain.

Tonight, do this: draw the CAST wheel from memory three times, and reduce five angles — one twenty, two ten, three hundred, minus sixty, two twenty-five — writing quarter, sign, home angle for each. Ten minutes, and the fear turns into a checklist. You've got this!
