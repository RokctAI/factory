Hey, it's Bianca — completing the square is the topic that makes people feel like they missed a lesson. All those fractions, that plus-or-minus, and a formula with letters instead of numbers. But there is really only one new instruction in the whole session, and everything else is arithmetic you can already do.

The instruction is this: halve the coefficient of x, square it, and add it. That is the corner tile in the paving picture, and it never changes. Six x gives you three, then nine. Minus ten x gives you minus five, then twenty-five. Practise five of those tonight, out loud, and half the fear is gone by tomorrow.

Two small guards. First, when you add that number to one side of an equation, add it to the other side too — otherwise the equation stops being true. Second, when you finally square-root both sides, write plus or minus. A square of seven has two square roots, one positive and one negative, and the learner who writes only one has thrown away a whole solution and the mark that came with it.

If the equation starts with two x squared instead of x squared, divide every term by two before you begin. Dividing by a number is always safe. It is only dividing by a variable that is forbidden, because the variable might be zero.

The formula is not a new topic to memorise on top of this one — it is exactly this method, done once, in general. So if you can complete the square, you can rebuild the formula from scratch, which is useful on the day you blank. When you do use it, write a, b and c down separately with their signs before you substitute anything. Most formula errors are not formula errors at all; they are a minus sign that got lost on the way in.

And the last habit: round only at the end. If the question says surd form, keep the root sign and stop. If it says two decimal places, do the entire calculation exactly and press the round button once, at the very end.

Halve, square, add to both sides, plus or minus. Divide by a number when you must, never by a variable. Round last. That is the whole session. You've got this!
