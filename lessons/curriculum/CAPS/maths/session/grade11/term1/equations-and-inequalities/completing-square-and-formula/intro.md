Today we solve the quadratics that factorisation cannot touch. Completing the square works on every quadratic equation, and it does something no other method can: it rewrites the expression so the turning point of its graph reads straight off the page. We build the method from the perfect-square pattern, handle the leading coefficient when it is not one, then perform the completion once on the general equation to derive the quadratic formula in full — a derivation examiners ask for by name. The session closes with the sign and rounding discipline that formula questions reward: substitute a, b and c with their signs intact, keep the whole numerator over two a, and round once, at the end.
