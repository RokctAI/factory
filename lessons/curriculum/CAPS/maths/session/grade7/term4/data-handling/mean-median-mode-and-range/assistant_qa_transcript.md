### subtopic_1 — The Mean
**Assistant:** Can the mean be a number nobody actually got?

**Tutor:** Yes. The mean of 6, 7, 8 and 10 is 7,75, and nobody scored 7,75. The mean is a balance point, a fair-share value, not a value that must appear in the data. Famous examples include an average family having 1,8 children. The number is still useful for comparing groups.

### subtopic_2 — The Median and the Mode
**Assistant:** Why must the data be in order for the median?

**Tutor:** Because the median is the middle of the ordered list. In the unordered list 9, 3, 13, 5, 6, 8, 5 the fourth value is 5, which is not the middle in size at all. Only after ordering — 3, 5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 13 — does the fourth value, 6, have three values below it and three above it.

### subtopic_3 — The Range and Choosing an Average
**Assistant:** Is the range an average?

**Tutor:** No. The mean, median and mode tell you about the centre — a typical value. The range tells you about spread — how far apart the smallest and largest values are. You usually need both: the centre says where the data sits, and the spread says how much it varies around that.
