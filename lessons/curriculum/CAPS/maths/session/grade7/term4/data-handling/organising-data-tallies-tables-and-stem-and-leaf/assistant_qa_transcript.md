### subtopic_1 — Tallies and Frequency Tables
**Assistant:** Why cross every fifth tally instead of just drawing lines?

**Tutor:** Because counting a long row of identical strokes is slow and easy to get wrong. Bundles of five can be counted by fives at a glance: three bundles and two strokes is seventeen. It also keeps your place while you are tallying lots of answers, so mistakes are much less likely.

### subtopic_2 — Ordering and Grouping Numerical Data
**Assistant:** How do I choose the interval width?

**Tutor:** Choose a width that gives roughly four to eight groups. With marks from 23 to 47, a width of ten gives three or four groups, which shows the shape clearly. Too narrow, and you get many groups with only one value each; too wide, and everything falls into one group. Widths like 5 or 10 are popular because they are easy to read.

### subtopic_3 — Stem-and-Leaf Displays
**Assistant:** What is the advantage of a stem-and-leaf display over a grouped table?

**Tutor:** A grouped table tells you only that seven marks are in the thirties. A stem-and-leaf display shows the same grouping, so you can see the shape, but it also keeps every single mark, so nothing is lost. You can still find the smallest, largest, middle and most common values directly from it.
