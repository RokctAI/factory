### subtopic_1 — Yes or No and Multiple-Choice Questions
**Assistant:** Why not just let people write whatever they want as an answer?

**Tutor:** You can, and sometimes open answers are useful, but they are hard to count. One learner writes footy, another soccer, another playing ball with friends, and you must decide which belong together. Fixed options make every answer land in a clear category, so tallying is quick and fair. Adding other with a short line lets people give an unusual answer when they need to.

### subtopic_2 — Avoiding Leading Questions
**Assistant:** Why do people answer differently when a question is leading?

**Tutor:** Most people like to agree and do not want to seem rude or different. When a question signals the expected answer, many will give that answer even if they do not fully believe it. The survey then measures the wording, not the real opinions. Neutral wording and balanced options let people answer honestly.

### subtopic_3 — Options That Cover Everything Once
**Assistant:** What happens to the data if options overlap?

**Tutor:** A person whose answer fits two options must pick one, and different people pick differently. The counts in those options become unreliable, because the same kind of answer is split between them. Gaps cause the opposite problem: people with no fitting option skip the question or tick something wrong. Either way, the graph you draw later will not show the truth.
