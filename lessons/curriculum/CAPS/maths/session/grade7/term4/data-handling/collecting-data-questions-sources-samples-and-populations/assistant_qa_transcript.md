### subtopic_1 — Posing a Question Worth Answering
**Assistant:** Why does the question matter so much if we are going to collect data anyway?

**Tutor:** Because the question decides which data you collect. If the question is vague, you may ask the wrong people or record the wrong thing, and the data will not answer anything useful. A clear question names the group and what is measured, so every later step, from the questionnaire to the graph, has a clear target. Time spent sharpening the question saves a lot of wasted collecting.

### subtopic_2 — Sources of Data
**Assistant:** Is data from the internet always reliable?

**Tutor:** No. Some websites publish carefully collected data, such as Statistics South Africa, and some publish numbers with no clear source at all. Check who collected the data, how, when and from whom. If you cannot find those answers, treat the numbers with caution. Old data may also be out of date for today's question.

### subtopic_3 — Populations and Samples
**Assistant:** If a sample is smaller, how can it tell us about everyone?

**Tutor:** If it is chosen fairly, the sample tends to look like a small copy of the population. When names are drawn at random, learners who walk, take taxis and come by car all have a fair chance of being picked, so roughly the same mix appears in the sample. It will not be perfect, but it gives a good estimate. The danger is an unfair sample, which can be completely misleading no matter how big it is.
