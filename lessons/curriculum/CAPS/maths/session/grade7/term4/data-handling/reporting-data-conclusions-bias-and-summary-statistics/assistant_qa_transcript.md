### subtopic_1 — Summarising and Concluding
**Assistant:** Why can't we say why learners walk?

**Tutor:** Because we never asked why. The survey only recorded how learners travel. Some may walk because they live close, some because transport costs money, some because they enjoy it. Any reason we give would be a guess, not a conclusion from the data. To find out why, we would need a new question and new data.

### subtopic_2 — Sources of Bias and Error
**Assistant:** If every survey has some limitations, is any data trustworthy?

**Tutor:** Yes. No survey is perfect, but careful surveys reduce bias and are honest about what remains. A fair random sample, neutral questions, a reasonable sample size and checked totals give data you can rely on for sensible conclusions. Being honest about limitations is a sign of good data work, not bad data.

### subtopic_3 — Choosing the Statistics to Report
**Assistant:** Is it dishonest to report the mean when there is an outlier?

**Tutor:** Not if you explain it, but reporting the mean alone can give a false impression. Saying the average pocket money is R60, when four of five learners get R30 or less, misleads the reader. Honest reporting gives the median as the typical value and points out the outlier, or gives both measures so readers see the full picture.
