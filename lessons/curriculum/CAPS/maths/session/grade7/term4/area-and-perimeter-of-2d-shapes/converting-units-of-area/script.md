# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers using and converting between SI units of area: mm² to cm² and cm² to m², and the reason the conversion factors are squares of the length factors. The anchor is a poster of 60 cm by 40 cm, with an area of 2 400 cm² = 0,24 m².

## Subtopic: Square Millimetres and Square Centimetres

Length conversions are familiar: one centimetre is ten millimetres. Area conversions are different, because area has two dimensions.

Picture one square centimetre: a square one centimetre by one centimetre. Measure it in millimetres: ten millimetres by ten millimetres. Its area in square millimetres is ten times ten, one hundred. So one square centimetre equals one hundred square millimetres.

To convert square centimetres to square millimetres, multiply by one hundred. A stamp of 6 square centimetres is 600 square millimetres.

To convert square millimetres to square centimetres, divide by one hundred. A microchip of 250 square millimetres is 2,5 square centimetres.

The most common error is to use ten, the length factor, instead of one hundred. A rectangle three centimetres by two centimetres has area six square centimetres; in millimetres it is thirty by twenty, 600 square millimetres — not sixty.

The questions for this section are ready: convert between square millimetres and square centimetres using one hundred.

## Subtopic: Square Centimetres and Square Metres

The same reasoning connects square metres and square centimetres. One metre is one hundred centimetres. One square metre is a square one hundred centimetres by one hundred centimetres, so its area is one hundred times one hundred: 10 000 square centimetres.

To convert square metres to square centimetres, multiply by 10 000. To convert square centimetres to square metres, divide by 10 000.

The poster is sixty centimetres by forty centimetres: 2 400 square centimetres. In square metres: 2 400 divided by 10 000 is 0,24 square metres. Check using metres from the start: 0,6 metres times 0,4 metres is 0,24 square metres. The two methods agree.

A classroom of 48 square metres is 480 000 square centimetres — a large number, which is why we use square metres for rooms.

Choosing the unit: square millimetres for very small areas like a phone chip, square centimetres for pages, tiles and stamps, square metres for rooms, walls and gardens. In Grade 8 and beyond, hectares and square kilometres are used for land.

Pause here for this section's questions: convert using 10 000, and check by converting the lengths first.

## Subtopic: Conversions in Problems

Problems often mix units, and the conversion is part of the solution.

A wall of 10,26 square metres is tiled with tiles measuring 15 centimetres by 15 centimetres. One tile is 225 square centimetres. The wall in square centimetres is 10,26 times 10 000, which is 102 600 square centimetres. Number of tiles: 102 600 divided by 225, which is 456 tiles.

Alternatively, convert the tile to square metres first: 0,15 times 0,15 is 0,0225 square metres. Then 10,26 divided by 0,0225 is also 456.

A sheet of A4 paper is 21 centimetres by 29,7 centimetres: about 623,7 square centimetres, or about 0,0624 square metres. How many A4 sheets would cover one square metre? 10 000 divided by 623,7 is about 16.

Conversions between area units can be chained: one square metre is 10 000 square centimetres, and each of those is one hundred square millimetres, so one square metre is one million square millimetres.

Here come the questions for this section: convert as part of tiling and covering problems.

## Subtopic: Counting the Little Squares

Take a square that is one centimetre on each side. Now look at it with a ruler that shows millimetres. Along the bottom there are ten millimetres. Up the side there are ten millimetres.

Draw lines every millimetre across and up. How many tiny squares appear? Ten rows of ten — one hundred tiny squares. So one square centimetre holds one hundred square millimetres.

That surprises people, because one centimetre is only ten millimetres. But area goes in two directions — across AND up — so the ten happens twice. Ten times ten is one hundred.

So a stamp of six square centimetres holds six hundred square millimetres. And going the other way, 250 square millimetres is 2,5 square centimetres — divide by one hundred.

Your questions for this section are with you now: ten across, ten up, one hundred little squares.

## Subtopic: A Poster in Square Metres

Your poster is sixty centimetres wide and forty centimetres tall. Its area is sixty times forty, 2 400 square centimetres. How many square metres is that?

First, how many square centimetres fit in one square metre? A square metre is one hundred centimetres across and one hundred centimetres up. One hundred rows of one hundred: 10 000 little squares. So one square metre is 10 000 square centimetres — a huge number.

Our poster has 2 400 of them. 2 400 out of 10 000 is 0,24. So the poster is 0,24 square metres — about a quarter of a square metre.

Check another way: change the sides to metres first. Sixty centimetres is 0,6 metres; forty centimetres is 0,4 metres. 0,6 times 0,4 is 0,24 square metres. Same answer!

That second way is a great trick: if you are unsure about area units, change the lengths first and then multiply.

You can now convert between square millimetres, square centimetres and square metres. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: one hundred for mm² and cm², ten thousand for cm² and m².
