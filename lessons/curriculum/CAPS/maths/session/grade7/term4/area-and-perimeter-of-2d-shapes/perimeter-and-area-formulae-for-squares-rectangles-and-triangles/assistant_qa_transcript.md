### subtopic_1 — Area of Squares and Rectangles
**Assistant:** Why is area measured in square units?

**Tutor:** Because area counts how many unit squares fit on a surface. A square metre is a square one metre by one metre, and the classroom floor holds forty-eight of them. When you multiply length in metres by breadth in metres, the units multiply too: metres times metres gives square metres. This is also why area and perimeter cannot be compared directly — one counts squares covering the surface, the other measures a distance around the edge.

### subtopic_2 — Area of Triangles
**Assistant:** Why is the area of a triangle half of base times height?

**Tutor:** Because every triangle fits exactly into a rectangle with the same base and height, filling exactly half of it. Draw the rectangle around the triangle and drop the height from the top vertex. It splits the rectangle into two smaller rectangles, and the triangle's sides cut each of those exactly in half along a diagonal. So the triangle takes half of each small rectangle, which is half of the whole rectangle. The rectangle's area is base times height, so the triangle's is half of that.

### subtopic_3 — Using Formulae Carefully
**Assistant:** Why must the units be the same before I use an area formula?

**Tutor:** Because the formula multiplies the numbers, and the answer's unit comes from multiplying the units. Two metres times fifty centimetres is not a number of square metres or square centimetres — the units are mixed, and the number one hundred means nothing. Converting fifty centimetres to 0,5 metres gives two times 0,5, one square metre, which is correct. Alternatively, converting two metres to 200 centimetres gives 200 times fifty, 10 000 square centimetres, which is the same area. Either way, one unit throughout.
