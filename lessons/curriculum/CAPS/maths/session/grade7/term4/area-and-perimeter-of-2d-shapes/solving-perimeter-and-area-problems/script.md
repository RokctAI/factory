# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems involving the perimeter and area of polygons, including composite shapes and shapes with holes, calculating to at least one decimal place. The anchor is a room renovation.

## Subtopic: Composite Shapes: Splitting Into Rectangles and Triangles

A composite shape is made by joining simple shapes. To find its area, split it into rectangles and triangles, find each area, and add.

The L-shaped floor: the outside measures 6,5 metres along the bottom and 5 metres up the left side. The top part is only 3 metres wide, and the right part is only 2,4 metres tall. Split it with one line into two rectangles. The bottom rectangle is 6,5 by 2,4: 15,6 square metres. The top rectangle is 3 metres wide and 5 minus 2,4, which is 2,6 metres tall: 7,8 square metres. Total: 23,4 square metres.

Check by subtraction: the full rectangle around the L is 6,5 by 5, 32,5 square metres. The missing corner is 6,5 minus 3, 3,5 metres wide, by 2,6 metres tall: 9,1 square metres. 32,5 minus 9,1 is 23,4 square metres. Both methods agree.

The front of a house with a triangular gable: a rectangle 8 metres wide and 3 metres tall, with a triangle on top of base 8 metres and height 2,5 metres. Rectangle: twenty-four square metres. Triangle: half of eight times 2,5, ten square metres. Total: thirty-four square metres.

Any composite shape can be split in more than one way; choose the split that uses the given measurements, and label the unknown lengths before calculating.

The questions for this section are ready: split, calculate each part and add.

## Subtopic: Shapes With Holes and Practical Coverings

Many problems involve a shape with a part removed — a wall with a door, a lawn with a pond. Find the area of the whole shape and subtract the area of the hole.

The wall is 4,5 metres wide and 2,7 metres high: 12,15 square metres. The door is 0,9 by 2,1 metres: 1,89 square metres. The area to paint: 12,15 minus 1,89, which is 10,26 square metres.

Coverage problems: a litre of paint covers about eight square metres per coat. Two coats on 10,26 square metres need 20,52 square metres of coverage, which is 20,52 divided by eight, about 2,6 litres. Paint is sold in whole tins, so buy enough — for example a five-litre tin, or three one-litre tins. Context decides how to round, and here it is always up.

Tiling problems: tiles of 0,3 by 0,3 metres cover 0,09 square metres each. A floor of 23,4 square metres needs 23,4 divided by 0,09, which is 260 tiles, plus extra for cutting and breakages.

Costing: carpet at R185 per square metre for 23,4 square metres costs 23,4 times 185, R4 329.

Pause here for this section's questions: subtract holes, then turn area into litres, tiles or rand.

## Subtopic: Perimeter or Area? Choosing and Combining

The first decision in any problem is whether it asks about the boundary — perimeter — or the surface — area.

Perimeter words: fence, border, edging, skirting board, frame, trim, distance around. Area words: cover, paint, tile, carpet, grass, how much space.

Some problems need both. A rectangular lawn 12,5 metres by 8 metres needs grass seed and a border of bricks. Seed covers the area: 12,5 times 8, one hundred square metres. The border runs along the perimeter: two times 12,5 plus two times 8, 41 metres.

Same perimeter, different areas: with twenty metres of fencing, a five by five square pen encloses twenty-five square metres; a nine by one pen encloses only nine. For a fixed perimeter, the closer a rectangle is to a square, the larger its area.

Same area, different perimeters: thirty-six square metres can be six by six with perimeter twenty-four, or twelve by three with perimeter thirty, or thirty-six by one with perimeter seventy-four.

Give answers to at least one decimal place where the measurements require it, and always with units.

Here come the questions for this section: choose perimeter or area, and compare shapes.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same renovation now, as two jobs: painting a wall with a door in it, and carpeting an L-shaped floor. Same calculations, practical steps.

## Subtopic: Painting the Wall

You are painting a bedroom wall. It is 4,5 metres wide and 2,7 metres high. But there is a door in it, 0,9 metres wide and 2,1 metres high, and you do not paint the door.

Step one: the whole wall. 4,5 times 2,7 is 12,15 square metres.

Step two: the door. 0,9 times 2,1 is 1,89 square metres.

Step three: take the door away. 12,15 minus 1,89 is 10,26 square metres to paint.

Now the paint. The tin says one litre covers eight square metres. You want two coats, so you are really painting 10,26 twice: 20,52 square metres. Divided by eight: about 2,6 litres.

Can you buy 2,6 litres? Paint comes in whole tins — so buy three one-litre tins, or a bigger tin. If you bought only two litres, part of the wall would stay unpainted!

Your questions for this section are with you now: whole wall, minus the hole, then turn it into tins.

## Subtopic: The L-Shaped Floor

The bedroom floor is shaped like the letter L. How much carpet is needed?

The trick: cut the L into two rectangles. The bottom part is 6,5 metres long and 2,4 metres wide: 6,5 times 2,4 is 15,6 square metres. The top part is 3 metres wide. How tall? The whole side is 5 metres, and the bottom part used 2,4 of them, so 5 minus 2,4 is 2,6 metres. 3 times 2,6 is 7,8 square metres.

Add them: 15,6 plus 7,8 is 23,4 square metres of carpet.

Check another way: imagine the full rectangle around the L, 6,5 by 5 — 32,5 square metres. The missing corner is 3,5 by 2,6, which is 9,1. 32,5 minus 9,1 is 23,4. Same!

And watch the question words. Carpet covers the floor — that is area. Skirting board goes around the edge — that is perimeter. A fence goes around — perimeter. Paint covers — area.

You can now solve real area and perimeter problems. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: split, add, check, and ask whether it is edge or surface.
