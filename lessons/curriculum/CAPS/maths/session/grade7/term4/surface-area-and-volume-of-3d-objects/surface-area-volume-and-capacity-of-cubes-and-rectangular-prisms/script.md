# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers using formulae to calculate the surface area, volume and capacity of cubes and rectangular prisms. The anchor is a cereal box 20 cm × 7 cm × 30 cm.

## Subtopic: Surface Area: The Net of a Prism

The surface area of a three-dimensional object is the total area of all its faces. It tells you how much material is needed to make or wrap the object, and it is measured in square units.

A rectangular prism has six rectangular faces in three matching pairs: top and bottom, front and back, left and right. Unfolding the prism flat gives its net, which shows all six faces.

The cereal box is twenty centimetres long, seven centimetres wide and thirty centimetres tall. Front and back: twenty by thirty, 600 square centimetres each, 1 200 together. Left and right sides: seven by thirty, 210 each, 420 together. Top and bottom: twenty by seven, 140 each, 280 together. Total surface area: 1 200 plus 420 plus 280, 1 900 square centimetres.

As a formula: surface area equals two times length times breadth, plus two times length times height, plus two times breadth times height.

A cube has six identical square faces, so its surface area is six times the side squared. A cube of side five centimetres has surface area six times twenty-five, 150 square centimetres.

The questions for this section are ready: list the faces in pairs, add, and use square units.

## Subtopic: Volume: Counting Cubes

Volume is the amount of space an object occupies. It is measured in cubic units: cubic centimetres, cubic metres. One cubic centimetre is the space taken by a cube with edges of one centimetre.

A rectangular prism can be filled with layers of unit cubes. The cereal box's base is twenty by seven centimetres, so one layer holds 140 one-centimetre cubes. There are thirty layers, one per centimetre of height. Volume: 140 times thirty, 4 200 cubic centimetres.

That is why the formula is volume equals length times breadth times height, or equally, area of the base times height.

A cube's volume is side cubed: a five-centimetre cube has volume five times five times five, 125 cubic centimetres. This is where the word cubed comes from.

Working backwards: a box has volume 4 200 cubic centimetres, length twenty and breadth seven. Height: 4 200 divided by 140, thirty centimetres. A cube has volume sixty-four cubic centimetres; its side is the cube root, four centimetres.

Do not confuse surface area with volume: surface area is the wrapping, in square units; volume is the filling, in cubic units.

Pause here for this section's questions: layers of cubes, the formula and working backwards.

## Subtopic: Capacity: How Much a Container Holds

Capacity is the amount a container can hold, usually of a liquid. It is measured in millilitres, litres and kilolitres.

Capacity and volume are linked: one cubic centimetre holds exactly one millilitre. So one thousand cubic centimetres hold one litre, and one cubic metre holds one thousand litres, one kilolitre.

A fish tank is fifty centimetres long, thirty centimetres wide and forty centimetres high. Volume: fifty times thirty times forty, 60 000 cubic centimetres. Capacity: 60 000 millilitres, which is sixty litres.

If the tank is filled only to a depth of thirty-five centimetres, the water's volume is fifty times thirty times thirty-five, 52 500 cubic centimetres, which is 52,5 litres.

A rainwater tank shaped as a rectangular prism two metres by 1,5 metres by one metre has volume three cubic metres and holds three kilolitres, 3 000 litres.

Inside measurements give capacity; outside measurements give the total space the object occupies. For thin-walled containers the difference is small.

Here come the questions for this section: link cubic centimetres to millilitres and cubic metres to kilolitres.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same cereal box now, cut open and filled with sugar cubes, and a fish tank filled with water. Same formulae, real objects.

## Subtopic: Wrapping the Cereal Box

How much cardboard is needed to make a cereal box twenty centimetres long, seven centimetres wide and thirty centimetres tall?

Cut the box open along its edges and lay it flat. You see six rectangles, in matching pairs.

The big front and back: twenty by thirty, 600 square centimetres each. Two of them: 1 200.

The thin sides: seven by thirty, 210 each. Two of them: 420.

The top and bottom: twenty by seven, 140 each. Two: 280.

Add all six: 1 200 plus 420 plus 280 — 1 900 square centimetres of cardboard. That total is the surface area, the outside skin of the box.

A dice-shaped box, a cube, is even easier: six identical squares. A cube with five-centimetre sides: each face is twenty-five square centimetres, six faces make 150.

Your questions for this section are with you now: open the box, find each pair, add them all.

## Subtopic: Filling the Fish Tank

Now let us fill things. How much space is inside the cereal box?

Imagine filling the bottom with sugar cubes, one centimetre each. Twenty along, seven across: 140 cubes in the bottom layer. The box is thirty centimetres tall, so thirty layers. 140 times thirty: 4 200 cubes. The volume is 4 200 cubic centimetres.

Now a fish tank: fifty centimetres long, thirty wide and forty tall. Fifty times thirty times forty is 60 000 cubic centimetres.

Here is the magic link: each one-centimetre cube holds exactly one millilitre of water. So the tank holds 60 000 millilitres. A thousand millilitres is a litre, so that is sixty litres of water.

A big rainwater tank measured in metres works the same way: each cubic metre holds a thousand litres — a kilolitre.

So: surface area is the wrapping, volume is the filling, and capacity is how much liquid fits.

You can now wrap a box and fill a tank. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: count the layers, then turn cubes into millilitres.
