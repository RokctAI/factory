# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between SI units of area and volume — mm² and cm², cm² and m², mm³ and cm³, cm³ and m³ — and using the equivalences 1 cm³ = 1 ml and 1 m³ = 1 kl in problems. The anchor is a one-metre cube built from centimetre cubes.

## Subtopic: Area Units Revisited

A quick revision of area conversions, because volume conversions follow the same pattern with one more dimension.

One centimetre is ten millimetres, so one square centimetre is ten times ten, one hundred square millimetres. One metre is one hundred centimetres, so one square metre is one hundred times one hundred, 10 000 square centimetres.

The rule: square the length factor. Length factor ten gives area factor one hundred. Length factor one hundred gives area factor 10 000.

Converting to a smaller unit, multiply; to a larger unit, divide. 3,5 square metres is 35 000 square centimetres. 840 square millimetres is 8,4 square centimetres.

## Subtopic: Volume Units: Cubing the Factor

Volume has three dimensions, so the length factor is used three times — it is cubed.

One cubic centimetre is a cube ten millimetres by ten by ten: 1 000 cubic millimetres.

One cubic metre is a cube one hundred centimetres by one hundred by one hundred: one hundred times one hundred times one hundred, 1 000 000 cubic centimetres — one million. Picture building the metre cube from centimetre cubes: a bottom layer of 10 000, and one hundred such layers.

To convert cubic metres to cubic centimetres, multiply by a million. 0,25 cubic metres is 250 000 cubic centimetres. To convert cubic centimetres to cubic metres, divide by a million: 4 200 cubic centimetres is 0,0042 cubic metres.

Cubic millimetres to cubic centimetres: divide by 1 000. A raindrop of about thirty cubic millimetres is 0,03 cubic centimetres.

Safest method in doubt: convert the lengths first, then calculate the volume in the new unit. A box twenty by seven by thirty centimetres is 0,2 by 0,07 by 0,3 metres: 0,0042 cubic metres.

Pause here for this section's questions: cube the length factor.

## Subtopic: Volume and Capacity Together

Capacity units link to volume units through two equivalences: one cubic centimetre equals one millilitre, and one cubic metre equals one kilolitre.

So one litre, a thousand millilitres, is a thousand cubic centimetres: a cube ten centimetres on each side. One kilolitre, a thousand litres, is one cubic metre.

Conversions: 2,5 litres is 2 500 millilitres, which is 2 500 cubic centimetres. 750 cubic centimetres is 750 millilitres, 0,75 litres. 0,6 cubic metres is 0,6 kilolitres, 600 litres.

Problem: a medicine bottle holds 200 millilitres, and a dose is five millilitres, which is five cubic centimetres. Number of doses: forty.

Problem: a JoJo-style water tank of 5 000 litres is five kilolitres, which is five cubic metres. If the tank is a rectangular prism two metres long and 1,25 metres wide, its height must be five divided by 2,5, two metres.

Units must match before calculating: you cannot combine litres and cubic metres directly; convert first.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same one-metre box now, filled with a million tiny cubes, and then with water. Same conversions, ones you can picture.

## Subtopic: A Million Little Cubes

Imagine a box exactly one metre long, one metre wide and one metre tall. You want to fill it with tiny cubes, each one centimetre on every side — like sugar cubes, but smaller.

Along the bottom edge: one hundred tiny cubes. Across the bottom: one hundred rows. So the bottom layer has one hundred times one hundred: 10 000 cubes.

How many layers to reach the top? One hundred. So the whole box holds 10 000 times one hundred: one million tiny cubes!

That is why one cubic metre is one million cubic centimetres. Length uses the hundred once. Area uses it twice — 10 000. Volume uses it three times — a million.

The same idea with millimetres: one cubic centimetre holds ten by ten by ten, a thousand cubic millimetres.

If you are ever unsure, change the lengths first, then multiply. That trick always works.

## Subtopic: Litres in a Box

Here is the best fact in this whole topic: every one-centimetre cube holds exactly one millilitre of water.

So a box ten by ten by ten centimetres — a thousand cubic centimetres — holds a thousand millilitres. That is one litre.

And the big one-metre box, with its million cubic centimetres, holds a million millilitres. That is a thousand litres — one kilolitre.

So a water tank that is five cubic metres holds five kilolitres, which is 5 000 litres — that is the size of many home rainwater tanks in South Africa.

Going the other way: a cooldrink can of 330 millilitres has a volume of 330 cubic centimetres. A medicine dose of five millilitres is five cubic centimetres.

You can now convert between every unit of area, volume and capacity. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: one cubic centimetre is one millilitre, one cubic metre is one kilolitre.
