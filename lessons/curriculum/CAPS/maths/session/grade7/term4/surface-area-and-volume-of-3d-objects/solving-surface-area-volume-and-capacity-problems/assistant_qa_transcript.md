### subtopic_1 — Volume and Capacity Problems
**Assistant:** Why is the depth of water equal to volume divided by base area?

**Tutor:** Because the volume of water in a rectangular tank is base area times depth, just like any prism. If you know the volume and the base area, dividing undoes the multiplication and gives the depth. With 3,3 cubic metres in a tank whose base covers three square metres, the water forms a layer 1,1 metres deep. This works only for containers with straight vertical sides, where every layer has the same base area.

### subtopic_2 — Surface Area Problems
**Assistant:** How do I decide which faces to include in a surface area problem?

**Tutor:** Picture the real object and ask which faces are actually covered, painted or made of material. A tank standing on a slab has a bottom nobody paints. A box without a lid has five faces, not six. A room being painted includes four walls and perhaps the ceiling but not the floor. Write down the list of faces you are including before calculating, so that the marker can see your decision, and then add only those areas.

### subtopic_3 — Multi-Step Problems With Conversions
**Assistant:** Why is counting along each edge safer than dividing volumes when packing boxes?

**Tutor:** Because boxes are rigid and cannot be squashed into leftover gaps. Dividing volumes assumes every bit of space is used. If a crate is sixty-five centimetres long and cartons are ten long, only six fit along that edge, and the leftover five centimetres is wasted, but volume division would pretend part of a seventh carton fits. Counting how many whole cartons fit along the length, the width and the height, then multiplying, gives the number that really fit.
