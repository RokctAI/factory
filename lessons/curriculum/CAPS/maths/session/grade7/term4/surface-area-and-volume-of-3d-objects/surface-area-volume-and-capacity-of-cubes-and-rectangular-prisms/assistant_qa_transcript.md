### subtopic_1 — Surface Area: The Net of a Prism
**Assistant:** Why does the formula have three pairs of faces?

**Tutor:** Because a rectangular prism's opposite faces are identical rectangles. The front and back both measure length by height, the left and right sides both measure breadth by height, and the top and bottom both measure length by breadth. So you only need to calculate three different areas and double each one. Drawing the net, the box unfolded flat, shows the six faces clearly and helps you check that none has been left out or counted twice.

### subtopic_2 — Volume: Counting Cubes
**Assistant:** Why is volume length times breadth times height?

**Tutor:** Because you can fill the prism with layers of unit cubes. The base, length by breadth, tells you how many cubes fit in one layer, and the height tells you how many layers stack up. For the cereal box, twenty by seven gives 140 cubes per layer, and thirty layers make 4 200 cubes. Multiplying the three dimensions is a shortcut for counting all those cubes. It is also why volume is in cubic units: three lengths multiplied together.

### subtopic_3 — Capacity: How Much a Container Holds
**Assistant:** Why is one cubic centimetre exactly one millilitre?

**Tutor:** Because the metric system was designed that way: the litre was defined as the volume of a cube ten centimetres on each side, which is 1 000 cubic centimetres. Dividing a litre into a thousand millilitres makes each millilitre exactly one cubic centimetre. The same design links one cubic metre to 1 000 litres, a kilolitre. This lets you go straight from a container's measurements in centimetres or metres to how much liquid it holds.
