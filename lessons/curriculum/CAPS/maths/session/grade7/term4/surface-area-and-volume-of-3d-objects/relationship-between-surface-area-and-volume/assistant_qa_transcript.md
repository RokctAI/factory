### subtopic_1 — Same Volume, Different Surface Areas
**Assistant:** Why does a compact shape have less surface area for the same volume?

**Tutor:** Because in a compact shape more of the cubes are hidden inside or pressed against each other, so fewer faces are exposed. In a long row, almost every cube shows four faces to the outside. In a chunky block, many cubes touch neighbours on several sides, and those shared faces are not part of the surface. Each face that is shared instead of exposed removes one square centimetre of wrapping from each of two cubes. The more faces are shared, the smaller the surface area.

### subtopic_2 — Doubling the Side of a Cube
**Assistant:** Why does volume grow faster than surface area when a cube gets bigger?

**Tutor:** Because surface area depends on two dimensions and volume on three. Doubling the side doubles both dimensions of every face, so each face's area is two times two, four times as big. But volume multiplies three dimensions, so it becomes two times two times two, eight times as big. Each time the cube grows, there is relatively more inside compared with the outside. That ratio of surface to volume is why small things heat up and cool down faster than big things.

### subtopic_3 — Surface-to-Volume Comparisons in Context
**Assistant:** If a cube uses the least cardboard, why are most boxes not cubes?

**Tutor:** Because cardboard is only one of several things a designer must consider. A tall, thin cereal box has a large front that is easy to see and print on, it fits on supermarket shelves, and it is easy to hold and pour from. A flat box can be posted through a letterbox. The mathematics tells you the cost in material of each choice: the cube is cheapest in cardboard, and every other shape costs a little more. The designer then decides whether the other benefits are worth that extra cost.
