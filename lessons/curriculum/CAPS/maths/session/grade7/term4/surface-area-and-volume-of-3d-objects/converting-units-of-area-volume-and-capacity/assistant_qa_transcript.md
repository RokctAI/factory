### subtopic_1 — Area Units Revisited
**Assistant:** To convert to a smaller unit, why do I multiply?

**Tutor:** Because smaller units are needed in greater numbers to cover the same area. A square metre is a large square; to cover it with tiny square centimetres you need 10 000 of them. So the number in the smaller unit must be bigger, which means multiplying. Going to a larger unit, fewer are needed, so you divide. A quick sense check: if your converted number moved in the wrong direction, you multiplied when you should have divided.

### subtopic_2 — Volume Units: Cubing the Factor
**Assistant:** Why is the volume factor between cm³ and m³ a million, not a hundred?

**Tutor:** Because volume uses all three dimensions, and each one converts by one hundred. A cubic metre is one hundred centimetres long, one hundred wide and one hundred tall, so it holds one hundred times one hundred times one hundred small cubes: a million. Using only one hundred would convert just one of the three dimensions. If you remember that length uses the factor once, area twice and volume three times, you can always work the factor out.

### subtopic_3 — Volume and Capacity Together
**Assistant:** How can I picture one litre as a volume?

**Tutor:** As a cube ten centimetres on each side, about the size of a large milk carton's base stretched into a cube. Its volume is ten times ten times ten, 1 000 cubic centimetres, and since each cubic centimetre holds one millilitre, it holds 1 000 millilitres, one litre. A thousand of those litre cubes stacked ten by ten by ten make a cube one metre on each side, which holds one kilolitre. Picturing these cubes makes the conversion factors feel natural.
