### subtopic_1 — Equivalent Fractions and Simplest Form
**Assistant:** Why does multiplying the top and bottom by the same number not change the fraction?

**Tutor:** Because multiplying the top and bottom by the same number is the same as multiplying the whole fraction by one. Five over five is one, and any number times one stays the same. In picture terms, cutting each quarter into five equal pieces turns four quarters into twenty pieces, and the three quarters you had become fifteen of those smaller pieces. The amount you hold has not changed; only the size of the pieces has. Simplifying is the same idea in reverse: you are joining small pieces back into bigger ones.

### subtopic_2 — Ordering and Comparing Fractions
**Assistant:** Why is one third bigger than one quarter when four is bigger than three?

**Tutor:** Because the denominator tells you how many pieces the whole is cut into, and more pieces means smaller pieces. Cut a pizza into three and each slice is bigger than when you cut the same pizza into four. So with the same numerator, the fraction with the smaller denominator is the larger one. It feels backwards only if you treat the denominator like an ordinary whole number. Think of it as a size label for the pieces instead.

### subtopic_3 — Fractions on the Number Line and Mixed Numbers
**Assistant:** Why do we compare the whole numbers first in mixed numbers?

**Tutor:** Because the fraction part of a mixed number is always less than one, so it can never make up for a difference in the whole numbers. Two and nine tenths is still less than three, while three and one tenth is more than three. So whenever the whole numbers differ, the comparison is already settled. Only when they are equal does the fraction part matter, and then you compare the fractions the usual way, with a common denominator.
