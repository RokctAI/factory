# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers percentages as fractions with denominator one hundred, calculating a percentage of a whole, expressing a part as a percentage of a whole, and percentage increase and decrease of whole numbers. The anchor is a school of 640 learners.

## Subtopic: Percentage of a Whole

Percent means per hundred, or out of one hundred. Fifteen percent is fifteen hundredths, fifteen over one hundred. One hundred percent is the whole; fifty percent is a half; twenty-five percent is a quarter; ten percent is a tenth.

To find a percentage of an amount, write the percentage as a fraction over one hundred and multiply. Fifteen percent of 640 learners: fifteen over one hundred times 640. Simplify: fifteen over one hundred is three twentieths, and 640 divided by twenty is thirty-two, times three is 96 learners.

A strong mental method builds from ten percent. Ten percent of 640 is sixty-four, found by dividing by ten. Five percent is half of that, thirty-two. Fifteen percent is ten percent plus five percent: sixty-four plus thirty-two, 96. The same answer.

Other building blocks: one percent is the amount divided by one hundred, so one percent of 640 is 6,4. Fifty percent is half, 320. Twenty-five percent is a quarter, 160.

A check: fifteen percent is a bit less than a fifth, and a fifth of 640 is 128, so ninety-six — a bit less — is reasonable.

The questions for this section are ready: percent as hundredths, the multiply method and the ten-percent method.

## Subtopic: Writing a Part as a Percentage

To write one quantity as a percentage of another, write the part as a fraction of the whole, then multiply by one hundred percent.

Of the 640 learners, 240 walk to school. As a fraction: 240 over 640. Simplify: divide both by eighty — three eighths. As a percentage: three eighths times one hundred is three hundred over eight, which is thirty-seven and a half percent. So 37,5 percent of the learners walk.

Some fractions convert easily by making the denominator one hundred: twelve out of twenty-five is forty-eight out of one hundred, forty-eight percent. When the denominator does not go into one hundred neatly, multiply by one hundred and divide.

Always check that the part and whole are in the same units. Forty cents as a percentage of R2 is forty cents out of two hundred cents: twenty percent, not two thousand percent.

Percentages let you compare groups of different sizes fairly. If 240 of 640 learners walk at one school and 150 of 300 walk at another, the counts suggest the first school has more walkers, but the percentages, 37,5 percent and 50 percent, show that walking is more common at the second school.

Pause here for this section's questions: part over whole, times one hundred, and fair comparisons.

## Subtopic: Percentage Increase and Decrease

A percentage increase makes an amount bigger by a percentage OF THE ORIGINAL amount; a percentage decrease makes it smaller.

The school expects its enrolment of 640 to increase by twenty-five percent. Increase: twenty-five percent of 640 is 160. New enrolment: 640 plus 160, which is 800 learners. A one-step method: the new amount is one hundred and twenty-five percent of the original, five quarters of 640, which is also 800.

Now a decrease. If instead enrolment dropped by twenty-five percent: 640 minus 160 is 480 learners. One step: seventy-five percent of 640, three quarters of 640, 480.

To find the percentage change between two amounts, use the change divided by the ORIGINAL, times one hundred. From 640 to 800 the change is 160; 160 over 640 is a quarter, twenty-five percent increase.

Watch the trap: an increase of twenty-five percent followed by a decrease of twenty-five percent does NOT return to the start. 640 rises to 800, but twenty-five percent of 800 is 200, so it falls to 600. The second percentage is taken of a different, larger amount.

Here come the questions for this section: increase, decrease, percentage change and the trap.

## Subtopic: Out of a Hundred

Imagine the school has exactly one hundred learners. If fifteen of them wear glasses, that is fifteen percent. Percent just means out of a hundred.

But our school has 640 learners, not one hundred. How many is fifteen percent of 640?

Start with the easiest piece: ten percent. Ten percent is one tenth, so divide by ten: sixty-four learners. Half of that is five percent: thirty-two learners. Ten percent plus five percent is fifteen percent: sixty-four plus thirty-two, ninety-six learners.

Now the other way round. 240 learners walk to school. What percentage is that? Make a fraction: 240 out of 640. Both divide by eighty: three out of eight. If the school had a hundred learners, how many would that be? Three eighths of one hundred is 37 and a half. So 37,5 percent walk.

Some percentages are worth knowing by heart: fifty percent is half, twenty-five percent is a quarter, ten percent is a tenth, and one hundred percent is everybody.

Your questions for this section are with you now: start with ten percent, build up, and turn a fraction into out-of-a-hundred.

## Subtopic: Up and Down by a Percentage

Next year the school will grow by twenty-five percent. How many learners will there be?

Twenty-five percent is a quarter. A quarter of 640 is 160. Those are the NEW learners joining. Add them on: 640 plus 160, 800 learners.

What if the school shrank by twenty-five percent instead? A quarter of the learners leave: 640 minus 160, 480 learners.

Here is a puzzle that fools many adults. A shop raises a R200 jacket price by twenty-five percent, then later puts it on sale at twenty-five percent off. Is it back to R200?

Up: a quarter of R200 is R50, so R250. Down: a quarter of R250 is R62,50, so R187,50. It is CHEAPER than before! The sale took a quarter of a bigger price.

The lesson: a percentage is always a percentage OF something. When the something changes, so does the amount.

You now know how to find a percentage, write a part as a percentage, and increase or decrease by one. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: find the change, then add or take away.
