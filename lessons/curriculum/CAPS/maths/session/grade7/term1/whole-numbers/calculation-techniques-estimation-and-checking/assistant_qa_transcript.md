### subtopic_1 — Rounding and Estimating Before You Calculate
**Assistant:** Why use 9 100 instead of 9 000 when estimating 9 386 ÷ 13?

**Tutor:** Because the point of an estimate is to make the calculation easy, and 9 000 divided by 13 is not easy — thirteen does not go into 9 000 exactly. Nine thousand one hundred is chosen because it is close to 9 386 and because thirteen times seven hundred is exactly 9 100. Numbers chosen to divide neatly are called compatible numbers. Rounding to the nearest thousand is still a valid estimate, but it leaves you with a harder division. With compatible numbers you get a clean target of about seven hundred, and you know the true answer is a little more, since 9 386 is bigger than 9 100.

### subtopic_2 — Column Methods and Long Division
**Assistant:** Why do we put a zero in the units place before multiplying by the tens digit?

**Tutor:** Because the tens digit is not really three, it is thirty. When you multiply 247 by the three in 38, you are actually multiplying by thirty, and multiplying by thirty is the same as multiplying by three and then by ten. Multiplying by ten moves every digit one place to the left, which is exactly what writing a zero in the units place does. If you forget the zero, the second row becomes 741 instead of 7 410, and the final answer comes out as 2 717 — far smaller than the estimate of ten thousand, which is how the estimate catches the mistake.

### subtopic_3 — Compensating and Checking With Inverse Operations
**Assistant:** If a calculator is so accurate, why should I not just use it for everything?

**Tutor:** Because a calculator is only as accurate as what you type into it, and it gives no warning when you press the wrong key. If you type 274 instead of 247, it confidently shows a wrong answer. Learners who calculate by hand first and use the calculator only to check have two independent answers, and if they agree you can be very confident. Learners who rely on the calculator alone have one answer and no way to know if it is right. There is also the skill itself: estimation, number sense and the written methods are tested in examinations, and they are what let you notice when any answer, from any source, does not make sense.
