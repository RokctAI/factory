# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers prime numbers, prime factorisation of numbers up to at least three digits, and the HCF and LCM of whole numbers found by inspection or by factorisation. The anchor pair is 84 and 120.

## Subtopic: Primes and Prime Factorisation

A prime number has exactly two different factors: one and itself. The first primes are two, three, five, seven, eleven, thirteen, seventeen, nineteen and twenty-three. Two is the only even prime. One is NOT prime, because it has only one factor. A number with more than two factors, such as twelve, is called composite.

Every composite number can be written as a product of primes, and there is only one way to do it apart from the order. That product is the prime factorisation. To find it, divide repeatedly by the smallest prime that goes in, until you reach one. This is the ladder method.

Anchor number one: 84. Two goes in: 42. Two again: 21. Two does not divide 21, so try three: 7. Seven is prime: 1. The primes are two, two, three and seven. So 84 equals two times two times three times seven, which in exponential form is two squared times three times seven.

Anchor number two: 120. Two: 60. Two: 30. Two: 15. Three: 5. Five: 1. So 120 equals two times two times two times three times five, or two cubed times three times five.

Divisibility rules speed up the ladder. A number is divisible by two if it is even, by three if its digit sum is divisible by three, and by five if it ends in zero or five. For 21, the digit sum is three, so three divides it. Always multiply your primes back together to check: two times two times three times seven is four times twenty-one, which is 84.

The questions for this section are ready: spot the primes, run the ladder and check by multiplying back.

## Subtopic: The HCF by Inspection and by Factorisation

A factor of a number divides into it exactly. A common factor of two numbers divides into both. The highest common factor, the HCF, is the largest of them.

By inspection, for small numbers, list the factors. Factors of eighteen: one, two, three, six, nine, eighteen. Factors of twenty-four: one, two, three, four, six, eight, twelve, twenty-four. The common factors are one, two, three and six, and the highest is six.

For larger numbers, factorisation is quicker. Write both factorisations side by side and take only the primes they SHARE. 84 is two, two, three, seven. 120 is two, two, two, three, five. Both have two twos — 84 only has two of them, so take two twos. Both have one three: take it. Seven is only in 84 and five is only in 120, so neither is shared. HCF equals two times two times three: 12.

Check: 84 divided by twelve is seven, and 120 divided by twelve is ten. Both divisions are exact. And seven and ten have no common factor except one, which proves twelve is the HIGHEST common factor.

A problem asks for an HCF when you want the largest thing that divides into both quantities: the most identical gift bags from 84 sweets and 120 stickers with nothing left over, or the longest piece of rope that cuts two lengths exactly.

Pause here for this section's questions: list factors, share primes and check with division.

## Subtopic: The LCM and Choosing Between HCF and LCM

A multiple of a number is that number times a whole number. A common multiple of two numbers is in both times tables. The lowest common multiple, the LCM, is the smallest of them.

By inspection: multiples of six are six, twelve, eighteen, twenty-four; multiples of eight are eight, sixteen, twenty-four. The first number in both lists is twenty-four, so the LCM of six and eight is 24.

By factorisation: the LCM must contain enough of each prime to satisfy BOTH numbers, so take every prime that appears, as many times as the greater number of times it appears in either. 84 is two, two, three, seven; 120 is two, two, two, three, five. Twos: 120 needs three, so take three. Threes: one. Fives: 120 needs one. Sevens: 84 needs one. LCM equals two times two times two times three times five times seven: eight times three is twenty-four, times five is 120, times seven is 840.

Check: 840 divided by 84 is ten and 840 divided by 120 is seven, both exact. There is also a neat check: HCF times LCM equals the product of the two numbers. Twelve times 840 is 10 080, and 84 times 120 is also 10 080.

How to choose. If the answer must go INTO both numbers, it is the HCF and it will be smaller than them. If both numbers must go INTO the answer — two buses that leave every 84 and 120 minutes, when do they leave together again? — it is the LCM and it will be bigger than them.

Here come the questions for this section: find the LCM, run the product check and choose the right tool.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same 84 and 120 now, as brick towers, gift bags and buses. The answers stay the same — HCF 12, LCM 840 — but this time you will see what they are for.

## Subtopic: Breaking Numbers Into Prime Bricks

Think of every whole number as a tower made of special bricks. The bricks are prime numbers — two, three, five, seven, eleven — numbers that cannot be split into smaller whole-number factors. Every tower has exactly one set of bricks.

Let us find the bricks of 84. It is even, so pull off a two: 42 left. Even again: another two, 21 left. Twenty-one is odd, but two plus one is three, so three goes in: 7 left. Seven is a brick on its own. The bricks of 84 are two, two, three and seven.

Now 120. Two: 60. Two: 30. Two: 15. Three: 5. Five is a brick. The bricks of 120 are two, two, two, three and five.

Rebuild the towers to check. Two times two is four, times three is twelve, times seven is 84. Correct! Always rebuild — it takes a few seconds.

Put the two brick piles next to each other. Both piles have two twos and a three. 120 has an extra two and a five; 84 has a seven. That little comparison — what they share and what is extra — is all you need for the HCF and the LCM.

Your questions for this section are with you now: find the bricks, rebuild the tower, compare the piles.

## Subtopic: Gift Bags and Buses

Two real jobs show you when to use each answer.

Job one: gift bags. You have 84 sweets and 120 stickers. You want to make identical gift bags with nothing left over. What is the greatest number of bags? The number of bags must go INTO 84 and INTO 120. So build it only from the bricks both piles share: two, two, three. Two times two times three is twelve bags. Each bag gets 84 divided by twelve — seven sweets — and 120 divided by twelve — ten stickers. That is the HCF: the biggest thing that fits into both.

Job two: buses. One bus leaves the rank every 84 minutes and another every 120 minutes. They leave together at six in the morning. When do they next leave together? Now both numbers must go INTO the answer. So the answer needs every brick that either pile needs, using the bigger count: three twos, a three, a five and a seven. Eight times three times five times seven is 840 minutes — that is fourteen hours, so about eight in the evening. That is the LCM: the smallest thing both fit into.

So keep two pictures in your head. Gift bags: share, so take only common bricks, and the answer is smaller. Buses: need, so take every brick, and the answer is bigger. For small numbers you can skip the bricks and just list factors or multiples.

You now know which tool a problem wants and how to use it. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: bags or buses, share or need.
