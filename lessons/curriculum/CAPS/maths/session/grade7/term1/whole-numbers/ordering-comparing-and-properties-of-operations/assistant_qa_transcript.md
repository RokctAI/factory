### subtopic_1 — Place Value, Ordering and Comparing
**Assistant:** Why do we count the digits before comparing anything else?

**Tutor:** Because the number of digits tells you the highest place value the number reaches. A seven-digit number has a millions digit, and even the smallest seven-digit number, one million, is bigger than the largest six-digit number, 999 999. So the digit count settles the comparison straight away whenever it differs. Only when two numbers have the same number of digits do you need to look inside them, and then you start at the left, because the left-most place carries the most value. The first place where the digits differ decides the whole comparison, and nothing to the right of it can change the answer.

### subtopic_2 — Commutative, Associative and Distributive Properties
**Assistant:** If subtraction is not associative, why can I still say 20 − 8 − 2 = 10 with confidence?

**Tutor:** Because there is an agreed convention: subtraction and division are worked from left to right. Twenty minus eight is twelve, and twelve minus two is ten. The convention removes the ambiguity that the missing associative property would otherwise create. What you may not do is regroup the last two numbers first, because twenty minus the result of eight minus two gives fourteen, a different answer. A useful way to see it is that subtracting eight and then subtracting two is the same as subtracting ten altogether, so if you want to regroup, you must add the numbers being taken away. That is why careful learners rewrite 20 − 8 − 2 as 20 − (8 + 2) rather than 20 − (8 − 2).

### subtopic_3 — Zero, One and the Order of Operations
**Assistant:** Why is dividing by zero undefined instead of just being zero?

**Tutor:** Division asks a question: what number, multiplied by the divisor, gives the dividend? Twelve divided by three is four because four times three is twelve. Now try twelve divided by zero: you need a number that, multiplied by zero, gives twelve. But anything times zero is zero, so no such number exists. That is why the answer is not zero, and not twelve, and not anything else — there is simply no answer, so we say the division is undefined. The answer cannot be zero, because zero times zero is zero, not twelve. Keep the check-by-multiplying habit and you will never be tricked by a division by zero.
