# Part 1 — Expert

This session applies addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of decimal fractions to problems in money, measurement and rate contexts, including choosing operations, converting units and rounding answers sensibly. The anchor is a school trip costing R4 845,60 for 38 learners.

## Subtopic: Money Problems With Decimals

Money is the most common decimal context. Rand and cents are written with two decimal places, and final answers are rounded to the nearest cent.

The trip costs R4 845,60 for 38 learners. Cost per learner: 4 845,60 divided by 38. Estimate: about 4 800 divided by forty, which is 120. Long division: thirty-eight into forty-eight goes once, remainder ten; into 104 goes twice, remainder twenty-eight; into 285 goes seven times, 266, remainder nineteen; place the comma; into 196 goes five times, 190, remainder six; into sixty goes once, remainder twenty-two. So about R127,51 and a bit; to the nearest cent, divide one more place: 220 divided by 38 is five, so 127,515, rounded to R127,52 — but in practice the school would charge R127,52 or round up to R128 so the full cost is covered.

Context decides rounding: when collecting money to cover a cost, round UP, otherwise the total is short. Thirty-eight times R127,51 is R4 845,38, which is twenty-two cents short.

Multi-step money problems combine operations. Each learner also buys lunch for R35,50 and a drink for R12,75. Total per learner: R127,52 plus R35,50 plus R12,75, which is R175,77.

Always write the unit and check the size: about R120 per learner fits the estimate.

The questions for this section are ready: cost per person, sensible rounding and multi-step totals.

## Subtopic: Measurement Problems and Converting Units

Measurement problems use decimals with units of length, mass and capacity. Before calculating, all quantities must be in the same unit.

Length: one kilometre is 1 000 metres, one metre is 100 centimetres, one centimetre is ten millimetres. Mass: one kilogram is 1 000 grams. Capacity: one litre is 1 000 millilitres.

To convert to a smaller unit, multiply; to a larger unit, divide. 184,5 kilometres is 184 500 metres. 750 millilitres is 0,75 litres. 1,2 kilograms is 1 200 grams.

The bus travels 184,5 km to the science centre and the same distance back, plus a 12,75 km detour. Total: 184,5 times two is 369, plus 12,75 is 381,75 km.

Each learner gets a 750 ml bottle of water. For 38 learners: 38 times 0,75 litres is 28,5 litres. Water is sold in five-litre containers: 28,5 divided by five is 5,7, so the school must buy six containers. Once again, the context rounds up.

A sandwich is 0,125 kg. How many grams? 0,125 times 1 000 is 125 grams. Thirty-eight sandwiches weigh 38 times 0,125, which is 4,75 kg.

Pause here for this section's questions: convert first, then calculate, then round for the context.

## Subtopic: Rates and Choosing the Operation

Rates combine two units, such as kilometres per litre or rand per litre. Decimal rates appear constantly in real problems.

The bus uses fuel at a rate of 0,32 litres per kilometre. For 381,75 km: 381,75 times 0,32 is 122,16 litres. At R23,85 per litre, the fuel costs 122,16 times 23,85, about 122 times 24, roughly R2 900; exactly R2 913,52 to the nearest cent.

Choosing the operation is the heart of a word problem. Ask what the units say. Litres per kilometre times kilometres gives litres. Rand per litre times litres gives rand. Rand divided by learners gives rand per learner. If the units do not work out, the operation is wrong.

Comparing rates: Bus company A charges R12,50 per kilometre. Company B charges a fixed R1 200 plus R9,20 per kilometre. For 381,75 km: A costs 381,75 times 12,50, which is R4 771,88; B costs 1 200 plus 381,75 times 9,20, which is 1 200 plus 3 512,10, R4 712,10. Company B is cheaper for this trip, by about R60.

Here come the questions for this section: rates with decimals, units as a guide and comparing options.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same school trip now, through the eyes of the trip organiser: what each learner pays and how much water to buy. Same numbers, real-life decisions.

## Subtopic: Paying for the Bus

The school trip costs R4 845,60 for the whole bus, and 38 learners are going. How much should each learner pay?

First, a rough answer. R4 845 is close to R4 800, and 38 is close to 40. Forty-eight hundred shared by forty is 120. So about R120 each.

Now exactly: R4 845,60 divided by 38 is R127,515 — a bit more than R127,51.

Here is the important question: should each learner pay R127,51 or R127,52? If everyone pays R127,51, the school collects twenty-two cents too little! The bus driver will not accept that. So the school rounds UP to R127,52 — or, to make it simple, asks for R128.

That is real-life rounding: if you need to cover a cost, round up. If you are sharing out money and cannot give away more than you have, round down.

And the extras: lunch R35,50 and a drink R12,75. Add it to R127,52 and each learner needs R175,77 for the day.

Your questions for this section are with you now: rough answer, exact answer, and round the way the situation needs.

## Subtopic: Water Bottles and Kilometres

Every learner gets a 750 millilitre bottle of water. How much water is that for 38 learners?

First change millilitres to litres so the numbers are friendlier: 750 millilitres is three quarters of a litre, 0,75 litres. Thirty-eight times 0,75: thirty-eight times three quarters. A quarter of 38 is 9,5, three quarters is 28,5 litres.

The water comes in five-litre containers. Five, ten, fifteen, twenty, twenty-five — that is not enough. Thirty — that is enough. Six containers. You cannot buy 5,7 containers, and five would leave learners thirsty!

The bus drives 184,5 kilometres there and 184,5 kilometres back. That is 369 kilometres. Add a 12,75 kilometre detour to fetch the lunches: 381,75 kilometres altogether.

In every problem, three questions help: what units are we in, what operation do we need, and does the answer make sense in real life?

You helped plan a whole school trip with decimals. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: change units, calculate, and decide what makes sense.
