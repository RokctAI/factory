# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers adding and subtracting decimal fractions of at least three decimal places, by aligning the decimal commas and using place value, with mental strategies and checks. The anchor is a shopping bill of R16,99 and R24,50, paid with R50.

## Subtopic: Adding Decimals by Place Value

To add decimals in columns, line up the decimal commas so that each place value sits in its own column: units under units, tenths under tenths, hundredths under hundredths. Fill empty places with zeros so every number has the same number of decimal places. Then add exactly as with whole numbers, carrying when a column adds to ten or more, and put the comma in the answer directly below the other commas.

Add R16,99 and R24,5. Write R24,5 as R24,50. Hundredths: nine plus zero is nine. Tenths: nine plus five is fourteen — write four, carry one into the units. Units: six plus four plus the carried one is eleven — write one, carry one. Tens: one plus two plus one is four. Total: R41,49.

Now masses with three decimal places. Apples 1,875 kg plus potatoes 2,35 kg. Write 2,35 as 2,350. Thousandths: five. Hundredths: seven plus five is twelve, write two carry one. Tenths: eight plus three plus one is twelve, write two carry one. Units: one plus two plus one is four. Total: 4,225 kg.

The most common error is lining up the last digits instead of the commas, which adds tenths to hundredths. Lining up the commas is the whole method.

Estimate first: R17 plus R25 is about R42. R41,49 is close, so the column addition is believable.

The questions for this section are ready: line up the commas, fill with zeros, add and estimate.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Decimals and Giving Change

Subtraction of decimals uses the same layout: commas lined up, zeros filling the gaps, and borrowing exactly as with whole numbers.

The learner pays for the R41,49 bill with R50. Change: R50,00 minus R41,49. Hundredths: zero minus nine is impossible, so borrow. There are no tenths or units to borrow from directly, so borrow along: fifty becomes forty-nine units, ten tenths become nine tenths, and the hundredths become ten. Ten minus nine is one. Tenths: nine minus four is five. Units: nine minus one is eight. Tens: four minus four is zero. Change: R8,51.

Check by adding: R41,49 plus R8,51. Hundredths nine plus one is ten, and so on — R50,00. The subtraction is confirmed.

A mental method, counting on, is how cashiers give change: from R41,49 add one cent to make R41,50, fifty cents to make R42, and eight rand to make R50. One cent plus fifty cents plus eight rand is R8,51.

With three decimal places: 5 kg minus 1,875 kg. Write 5 as 5,000. Borrowing along gives 3,125 kg.

The error to watch is subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column regardless of which is on top — for example writing 0,009 for 50 minus 41,49 in the hundredths. The top number must always stay on top.

Pause here for this section's questions: borrow along zeros, count on and check by adding.

## Subtopic: Mental Strategies and Mixed Calculations

Many decimal sums can be done mentally by using whole-number skills and place value.

Compensating: R16,99 is one cent less than R17. So R16,99 plus R24,50 is R17 plus R24,50, minus one cent: R41,50 minus R0,01, R41,49.

Making wholes: 0,875 plus 0,125 makes exactly 1. In 1,875 plus 2,125 the decimal parts combine to a whole: 1 plus 2 plus 1 is 4.

Breaking up by place value: 3,46 plus 2,3 — add the units, three plus two, five; then the tenths, four plus three, seven; then the hundredths, six. Result 5,76.

Calculations can mix addition and subtraction. Work from left to right, or regroup the additions first. A learner's bank card has R120,40. She spends R41,49 and R35,25 and then receives R50 pocket money. Balance: 120,40 minus 41,49 is 78,91; minus 35,25 is 43,66; plus 50 is R93,66. Alternatively: total spent is R76,74, and 120,40 plus 50 minus 76,74 is also R93,66.

Units must match before adding: 1,875 kg plus 350 g is not 351,875. Convert 350 g to 0,35 kg first, giving 2,225 kg.

Here come the questions for this section: compensate, make wholes, and work through mixed calculations.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same bread and milk now, at the till: adding the bill the way coins work and counting out change the way cashiers do. Same answers, real money.

## Subtopic: Adding Up the Bill

You are at the shop with R50. Bread costs R16,99 and milk costs R24,50. Do you have enough?

Think about money the way coins work. Rand go with rand, and cents go with cents. That is what lining up the commas means — rand in one column, ten-cent pieces in the next, one-cent pieces in the next.

R16,99 is nearly R17. R17 plus R24,50 is R41,50. But you added one cent too many, so take it back: R41,49. Yes, you have enough.

If you do it in columns, write the amounts one under the other with the commas in a straight line. Cents first: 99 cents plus 50 cents is 149 cents, which is one rand and 49 cents. Write 49 cents and carry the rand. Rand: 16 plus 24 plus the carried 1 is 41. Total: R41,49.

Both ways give the same answer. And a quick estimate — about R17 plus about R25, about R42 — tells you the answer is sensible.

Your questions for this section are with you now: rand with rand, cents with cents, and check with an estimate.

## Subtopic: Counting Out the Change

You hand the cashier R50 for a R41,49 bill. How much change should you get?

Cashiers do not subtract — they count up. Start at R41,49. One cent makes R41,50. Fifty cents makes R42. Eight rand makes R50. Now add up what you counted: one cent, fifty cents and eight rand — R8,51.

Check by adding the other way: R41,49 plus R8,51. Forty-nine cents plus fifty-one cents is one rand exactly. Forty-one rand plus eight rand plus that one rand is fifty rand. Perfect!

Decimals with three places work the same way. A bag of flour weighs 5 kilograms and you use 1,875 kilograms. How much is left? Count up from 1,875: plus 0,125 makes 2; plus 3 makes 5. 0,125 plus 3 is 3,125 kilograms left.

Making a whole — finding what you add to reach the next whole number — is a super-power with decimals. 0,875 needs 0,125; 0,49 needs 0,51; 0,3 needs 0,7.

You now know how to add a bill and count out the change. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: count up to the next whole, then on to the target.
