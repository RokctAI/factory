### subtopic_1 — Place Value to Three Decimal Places and Counting
**Assistant:** Why is 3,10 not the number after 3,9?

**Tutor:** Because 3,10 means three units, one tenth and zero hundredths, which is the same as 3,1. That is smaller than 3,9, not bigger. When you count tenths, nine tenths plus one tenth makes ten tenths, which is one whole, so the units digit goes up and you get 4,0. The decimal places behave exactly like whole-number places: after 39 comes 40, not 3 10. Treating the digits after the comma as a separate whole number is the root of most decimal mistakes.

### subtopic_2 — Ordering and Comparing Decimals
**Assistant:** Why can I add zeros at the end of a decimal but not in the middle?

**Tutor:** Because a zero at the end of the decimal part adds zero of a smaller place value. 3,5 and 3,500 both mean three units and five tenths — the extra zeros say there are no hundredths and no thousandths, which was already true. A zero in the middle moves digits into different places: 3,05 means five hundredths, not five tenths, so it is a different, smaller number. Adding zeros at the end is a safe way to give decimals the same number of places so you can compare them digit by digit.

### subtopic_3 — Rounding Decimals
**Assistant:** Why is rounding in a chain wrong?

**Tutor:** Because each rounding step can push the number a little further from its true value, and the errors can add up. 3,447 is closer to 3,4 than to 3,5 — it is only 0,047 above 3,4 but 0,053 below 3,5. Rounding to 3,45 first creates a halfway number that then rounds up to 3,5, which is the wrong answer. Rounding directly from the original looks only at the digit right after the place you want, the four hundredths, and correctly gives 3,4. Always go back to the original number.
