### subtopic_1 — Fractions to Decimals
**Assistant:** Why can I always convert a fraction to a decimal by dividing?

**Tutor:** Because a fraction IS a division. Three eighths means three shared equally among eight, and sharing is division. Dividing three by eight with long division, adding zeros after the comma, gives exactly the decimal value of three eighths. The equivalent-fraction method is quicker when the denominator goes neatly into ten, a hundred or a thousand, but division works for every fraction, including those like one third that give recurring decimals.

### subtopic_2 — Decimals to Fractions
**Assistant:** How do I know which power of ten to use as the denominator?

**Tutor:** Look at the place of the LAST digit after the comma. In 0,6 the last digit is in the tenths, so the denominator is ten. In 0,35 it is in the hundredths, so one hundred. In 0,375 it is in the thousandths, so one thousand. The digits after the comma, read as a whole number, form the numerator. Another quick way: the number of decimal places equals the number of zeros in the denominator. Three places, three zeros, one thousand.

### subtopic_3 — Converting To and From Percentages
**Assistant:** Why does multiplying a decimal by one hundred give the percentage?

**Tutor:** Because a percentage counts hundredths. 0,375 is a decimal fraction of one whole; to say how many hundredths that is, you ask how many hundredths fit into it, which is the same as multiplying by one hundred. 0,375 times one hundred is 37,5, so it is 37,5 hundredths, or 37,5 percent. Going the other way, dividing by one hundred turns a count of hundredths back into a fraction of one. The percentage sign is really a short way of writing divided by one hundred.
