### subtopic_1 — Money Problems With Decimals
**Assistant:** Why does the context matter when rounding money?

**Tutor:** Because rounding to the nearest cent can leave a total slightly short or slightly over, and in real life one of those is a problem. If every learner pays R127,51, the school collects R4 845,38 and is twenty-two cents short of the bus bill. When the money must cover a cost, round up so the total is enough. When sharing money out, round down so you do not hand out more than you have. Mathematics gives the exact value; the situation decides which way to round it.

### subtopic_2 — Measurement Problems and Converting Units
**Assistant:** How do I remember whether to multiply or divide when converting units?

**Tutor:** Think about how many of the new unit you will need. Going to a smaller unit — kilometres to metres — you need MORE of them, so the number gets bigger: multiply. Going to a larger unit — millilitres to litres — you need FEWER of them, so the number gets smaller: divide. 184,5 kilometres is a lot of metres, 184 500. 750 millilitres is less than one litre, 0,75. If your converted number moves the wrong way, you have multiplied when you should have divided.

### subtopic_3 — Rates and Choosing the Operation
**Assistant:** How do the units help me choose the right operation?

**Tutor:** Units behave like a small calculation of their own. Litres per kilometre times kilometres: the kilometres cancel and you are left with litres, which is what you wanted, so multiplying was right. If you had divided kilometres by litres per kilometre you would get kilometres squared per litre, which is nonsense — a sign the operation was wrong. Writing the units next to every number and checking that they produce the unit the question asks for is one of the most reliable ways to solve multi-step problems.
