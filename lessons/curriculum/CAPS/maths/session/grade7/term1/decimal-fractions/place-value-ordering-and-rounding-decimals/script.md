# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers place value of decimal fractions to at least three decimal places, counting forwards and backwards in decimal steps, ordering and comparing decimals, and rounding to at least two decimal places. The anchor distances are 3,457 m, 3,5 m and 3,45 m.

## Subtopic: Place Value to Three Decimal Places and Counting

Decimal fractions extend the place-value system to the right of the units. The comma separates the whole number from the fraction part. The first place after the comma is tenths, the second is hundredths and the third is thousandths. Each place is worth one tenth of the place to its left.

In 3,457 the three is three units, the four is four tenths, the five is five hundredths and the seven is seven thousandths. In expanded notation: three plus four tenths plus five hundredths plus seven thousandths. As a common fraction: three and 457 thousandths.

A decimal can be read digit by digit after the comma — three comma four five seven — and that is the clearest way to say it.

Counting in decimal steps follows the same place-value rules. Count on in steps of 0,1 from 3,7: 3,8, 3,9, 4,0, 4,1 — after nine tenths comes a whole one. Count on in steps of 0,01 from 3,47: 3,48, 3,49, 3,50, 3,51. Count back in steps of 0,005 from 3,46: 3,455, 3,45, 3,445. A common error is to count 3,9 then 3,10 — but 3,10 is the same as 3,1, which is LESS than 3,9. After 3,9 comes 4.

The questions for this section are ready: name the place values and count across a boundary.

## Subtopic: Ordering and Comparing Decimals

To compare decimals, line them up by the comma and compare place by place from the left, just as with whole numbers. It helps to write every number with the same number of decimal places by adding zeros at the end, which does not change its value.

Compare the jumps: 3,457, 3,5 and 3,45. Write them with three decimal places: 3,457, 3,500 and 3,450. Units: all three. Tenths: four, five and four. So 3,500 is the largest. Now compare 3,457 and 3,450: tenths equal, hundredths equal, thousandths seven and zero. So 3,457 is larger. Order from longest to shortest: 3,5 m, 3,457 m, 3,45 m.

The classic error is to think that a decimal with more digits is larger, because that rule works for whole numbers. 3,457 has more digits than 3,5 but is smaller. For decimals, more digits only means smaller pieces, not a larger number.

Another way to see it: 3,5 is 3 and 500 thousandths, while 3,457 is 3 and 457 thousandths. 500 thousandths is more than 457 thousandths.

On a number line between 3,4 and 3,5, the hundredths 3,41, 3,42 up to 3,49 sit between the tenths, and 3,457 lies between 3,45 and 3,46.

Pause here for this section's questions: line up the commas, add zeros, compare from the left.

## Subtopic: Rounding Decimals

Rounding a decimal works exactly like rounding a whole number. Decide which place you are rounding to, look at the next digit to the right, and round up if it is five or more, or keep the digit if it is four or less. Then drop all the digits after the rounding place.

Round 3,457 to two decimal places, the nearest hundredth. The hundredths digit is five; the next digit is seven, which is five or more, so round up: 3,46. Round 3,457 to one decimal place, the nearest tenth. The tenths digit is four; the next digit is five, so round up: 3,5. Round to the nearest whole number: the tenths digit is four, so round down: 3.

Rounding up can cause a carry. Round 2,996 to two decimal places: the thousandths digit is six, so the nine hundredths becomes ten hundredths, which carries into the tenths and units: 3,00. Keep both zeros — they show the number was rounded to two decimal places.

Rounding is used to report measurements sensibly and to estimate. Money is usually rounded to two decimal places, the nearest cent: R14,567 becomes R14,57.

A common error is to round in a chain: rounding 3,447 first to 3,45 and then to 3,5. Always round from the original number: 3,447 to one decimal place looks at the hundredths digit four, giving 3,4.

Here come the questions for this section: round to tenths, hundredths and whole numbers, and watch the carry.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same three long jumps now, on a sports field with a scoreboard. Same answers, explained with money and hills.

## Subtopic: Who Jumped Furthest?

Three learners do the long jump. Amahle jumps 3,457 metres, Bongani jumps 3,5 metres and Cara jumps 3,45 metres. Who wins?

Many people say Amahle, because 3,457 has the most digits. But that is a trap!

Think in money instead. Make every distance have three digits after the comma by adding zeros — that changes nothing, just like R3,50 is the same as R3,5. Now we have 3,457, 3,500 and 3,450.

Compare from the left. All three start with 3 metres. Next, the tenths: 4, 5 and 4. Bongani has 5 tenths, the others only 4. Bongani wins!

Amahle against Cara: tenths equal, hundredths equal, thousandths 7 against 0. Amahle beats Cara by a tiny 7 thousandths of a metre — seven millimetres.

So the order is Bongani 3,5, then Amahle 3,457, then Cara 3,45.

Your questions for this section are with you now: add zeros so the digits line up, and compare from the left.

## Subtopic: Rounding Like a Scoreboard

The sports-day scoreboard only shows two digits after the comma. So Amahle's 3,457 has to be rounded.

Look at the place you want to keep: the hundredths, the 5. Now peek at the next digit along: 7. Is it 5 or more? Yes, so the 5 goes up to 6. The scoreboard shows 3,46.

If the scoreboard only showed one decimal place, look at the tenths, the 4, and peek at the next digit, 5. Five or more goes up: 3,5.

Rounding is like deciding which side of a hill you are on. If you are halfway or more up, you roll down the far side to the next number. If you are less than halfway, you roll back.

Watch out for chains of nines. 2,996 to two places: peek at the 6, round the 9 hundredths up — ten hundredths — which bumps everything along: 3,00. The scoreboard shows 3,00, and those zeros matter because they show it was rounded to two places.

You now know how to compare decimals and round them. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: find the place, peek right, decide up or stay.
