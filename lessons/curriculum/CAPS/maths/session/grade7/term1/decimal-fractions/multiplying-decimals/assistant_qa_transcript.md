### subtopic_1 — Multiplying Decimals by Whole Numbers and Powers of Ten
**Assistant:** Why does the answer have the same number of decimal places as the decimal I multiplied?

**Tutor:** Because the decimal places tell you the size of the units you are counting. 1,375 is 1 375 thousandths. Multiplying by a whole number like six counts six lots of those thousandths, so the answer is still in thousandths: 8 250 thousandths, which is 8,250. The whole number has no decimal part, so it does not change the size of the units. When both numbers are decimals, the units get smaller still, which is why we then count the decimal places in both.

### subtopic_2 — Multiplying a Decimal by a Decimal
**Assistant:** Why is 0,3 × 0,2 equal to 0,06 and not 0,6?

**Tutor:** Because three tenths of two tenths is a much smaller amount than either. Think of 0,3 times 0,2 as three tenths OF two tenths. Two tenths is already small; taking three tenths of it gives six hundredths. In fractions: three over ten times two over ten is six over one hundred. Counting decimal places gives the same result: one place plus one place is two places, so six becomes 0,06. If your answer is bigger than both numbers when both are less than one, the comma is in the wrong place.

### subtopic_3 — Positioning the Comma With Estimation
**Assistant:** Why must I count decimal places before removing a zero at the end?

**Tutor:** Because the zero at the end of the whole-number product is a real digit in the calculation, even though it does not change the final value once the comma is placed. 25 times 14 is 350, and with two decimal places 350 hundredths is 3,50. Only after the comma is in place can you safely drop the zero to get 3,5. If you drop it first, you are left with 35 and you put the comma two places in to get 0,35, which is ten times too small. Estimating, about two and a half times one and a half, is about three or four, which catches the mistake.
