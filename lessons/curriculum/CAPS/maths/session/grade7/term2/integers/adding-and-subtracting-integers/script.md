# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers adding and subtracting integers using the number line and the rules that follow from it, including subtracting a negative integer. The anchor is a bank balance: 40 − 65 = −25, then −25 + 30 = 5.

## Subtopic: Adding Integers on the Number Line

On a number line, adding a positive integer means moving right; adding a negative integer means moving left.

Start at minus twenty-five and add thirty: move thirty to the right. Twenty-five steps bring you to zero, and five more bring you to five. So minus twenty-five plus thirty equals five.

Start at four and add minus seven: move seven to the left. Four steps to zero, three more to minus three. So four plus minus seven equals minus three.

Start at minus three and add minus five: move five to the left from minus three, reaching minus eight. Adding two negatives gives a more negative answer.

Two rules follow from the picture. When the signs are the same, add the sizes and keep the sign: minus three plus minus five is minus eight. When the signs are different, subtract the smaller size from the larger and take the sign of the integer with the larger size: minus twenty-five plus thirty — thirty is larger, so the answer is positive, thirty minus twenty-five, five.

Adding an integer and its opposite gives zero: seven plus minus seven is zero. They are additive inverses.

The questions for this section are ready: move right for positive, left for negative, and apply the sign rules.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Integers

Subtraction finds the difference between two integers, or takes an amount away.

Subtracting a positive integer means moving left. Zinhle has R40 and spends R65: forty minus sixty-five. Move sixty-five left from forty: forty steps to zero, twenty-five more to minus twenty-five. Her balance is minus R25 — she is overdrawn by R25.

Another view: forty minus sixty-five is the same as forty plus minus sixty-five. Subtracting an integer gives the same result as adding its opposite.

This also explains subtracting a negative. Three minus minus four equals three plus four, seven. Why? Think of differences: the difference between three and minus four on the number line is seven steps, so three minus minus four is seven. Or think of a bank: if the bank removes a wrongly charged R4 fee, a negative, from your account, your balance goes UP by R4.

The rule: to subtract an integer, add its opposite. Minus two minus five is minus two plus minus five, minus seven. Minus two minus minus five is minus two plus five, three.

The difference in temperature between fourteen degrees and minus six degrees: fourteen minus minus six is fourteen plus six, twenty degrees.

Pause here for this section's questions: subtract by adding the opposite, and find differences.

## Subtopic: Mixed Calculations and Checking

Calculations can combine several additions and subtractions of integers. Work from left to right, or first rewrite every subtraction as adding the opposite and then group.

Zinhle's account over a week: she starts with R40, spends R65, receives R30, spends R12 and receives R20. Forty minus sixty-five is minus twenty-five; plus thirty is five; minus twelve is minus seven; plus twenty is thirteen. Final balance R13.

Grouping method: add all the money in, forty plus thirty plus twenty, ninety. Add all the money out, sixty-five plus twelve, seventy-seven. Ninety minus seventy-seven is thirteen. Same answer.

Checks: does the sign make sense? She received more than she spent overall, so the final balance should be positive. Does the size make sense? She ended thirteen rand ahead.

Common errors: dropping a negative sign halfway through, and treating minus minus as minus. Writing brackets around negative numbers, such as open bracket minus six close bracket, helps keep the signs visible.

Here come the questions for this section: multi-step calculations, grouping and sense checks.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same bank account now, from Zinhle's side: spending more than she has, paying it back, and the strange case of taking away a debt. Same answers, real money.

## Subtopic: Spending More Than You Have

Zinhle has R40 in her bank account. She buys a jacket for R65. What happens?

She does not have enough. The bank lets her go into debt — her balance goes below zero. Count it on a number line: from forty, go back forty steps to reach zero, and she still has twenty-five rand of the jacket to pay. So her balance is minus twenty-five rand. She owes the bank R25.

Next week she gets R30 pocket money. From minus twenty-five, go up twenty-five steps to pay off the debt and reach zero. She still has five rand of the thirty left. Her balance is R5.

So adding money moves you up, spending moves you down, and zero is the line between having money and owing money.

Two minus numbers added together go even further down: owing R3 and then owing R5 more means owing R8. Minus three plus minus five is minus eight.

Your questions for this section are with you now: up for money in, down for money out, and zero is the line.

## Subtopic: Taking Away a Debt

Here is the strangest-looking rule with integers: three minus minus four is seven. How can taking something away make the number bigger?

Imagine your account shows R3, but the bank made a mistake and also charged you a R4 fee that should not be there. That fee is a minus four sitting in your account. When the bank takes the mistake away — subtracts the minus four — your money goes UP by R4. Three minus minus four is three plus four, seven rand.

Taking away a debt is the same as giving money. That is why "minus a minus" becomes "plus".

Here is another way: temperature. Durban is fourteen degrees and Sutherland is minus six. How much warmer is Durban? From minus six up to zero is six degrees; from zero up to fourteen is fourteen degrees. Altogether twenty degrees. Fourteen minus minus six is twenty.

So the rule is: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.

You can now add and subtract with integers, even across zero. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: in and out, up and down, and minus a minus is plus.
