### subtopic_1 — What Integers Are and Where They Live
**Assistant:** Why is zero neither positive nor negative?

**Tutor:** Because positive and negative describe direction away from zero, and zero is the starting point itself. Positive numbers are a distance to the right of zero, negative numbers a distance to the left, but zero is no distance at all in either direction. On a thermometer, zero degrees Celsius is the freezing point of water — the reference — and temperatures are described as above or below it. That is also why zero is its own opposite: it is the only integer that is the same distance, nothing, on both sides.

### subtopic_2 — Counting Forwards and Backwards Across Zero
**Assistant:** Why does counting forwards from minus four go to minus three and not minus five?

**Tutor:** Because forwards means moving to the right on the number line, towards larger numbers, and minus three is to the right of minus four. It is easy to be fooled because the digit three is smaller than four, but on the negative side a smaller digit means closer to zero, which means larger. Think of a thermometer: minus three degrees is warmer than minus four. Counting forwards means getting warmer, so the minus numbers shrink towards zero before the positive numbers begin.

### subtopic_3 — Ordering and Comparing Integers
**Assistant:** Why is minus 100 smaller than minus 1 when 100 is bigger than 1?

**Tutor:** Because minus 100 is much further to the left of zero than minus 1. On the number line, further left means smaller. In money, owing R100 leaves you worse off than owing R1. On a thermometer, minus 100 degrees is far colder than minus one. The size of the number, 100, tells you how far from zero it is; the minus sign tells you which side. On the negative side, being further away means being smaller, which is the opposite of how positive numbers behave.
