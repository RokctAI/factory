# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers mental recall of squares to at least 12² and their square roots, and cubes to at least 6³ and their cube roots, with the geometric meaning of each. The anchors are 12² = 144 and 6³ = 216.

## Subtopic: Square Numbers and Square Roots

A square number is a number multiplied by itself. Twelve squared, written twelve with a small two, means twelve times twelve, which is 144. The name comes from geometry: 144 tiles can be laid out as a perfect square, twelve tiles along each side.

The squares from one to twelve must be known instantly: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121 and 144. These are the perfect squares up to 144.

The square root undoes squaring. The square root of 144 is the number that, multiplied by itself, gives 144: twelve. It is written with the root sign. A square of tiles with area 144 has sides of length twelve; the square root finds the side from the area.

Squares grow quickly. The gaps between them are the odd numbers: four minus one is three, nine minus four is five, sixteen minus nine is seven. This pattern helps you check recall: eleven squared is 121, and adding the next odd number, twenty-three, gives 144, twelve squared.

A common error is to confuse squaring with doubling. Twelve squared is 144, not twenty-four. Squaring multiplies the number by itself; doubling multiplies by two.

The questions for this section are ready: recall the squares, find square roots and avoid the doubling trap.

## Subtopic: Cube Numbers and Cube Roots

A cube number is a number multiplied by itself three times. Six cubed, written six with a small three, means six times six times six. Six times six is thirty-six, times six is 216. The name again comes from geometry: 216 small blocks can be stacked into a perfect cube, six along each edge.

The cubes from one to six must be known instantly: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125 and 216.

The cube root undoes cubing. The cube root of 216 is the number that, used three times as a factor, gives 216: six. It is written with a small three in the root sign. A cube with volume 216 cubic centimetres has edges of six centimetres.

Notice that sixty-four is both a square and a cube: eight squared and four cubed. One is too.

A common error is to confuse cubing with tripling: six cubed is 216, not eighteen. Another is to stop after two multiplications: six times six is only thirty-six, the square.

Pause here for this section's questions: recall the cubes, find cube roots and avoid the tripling trap.

## Subtopic: Using Squares, Cubes and Roots Mentally

Fluent recall makes many calculations quick. Some patterns help.

Squares of multiples of ten: thirty squared is nine hundred, because three squared is nine and ten squared is one hundred. Cubes of multiples of ten: twenty cubed is eight thousand.

Estimating square roots: the square root of fifty lies between seven and eight, because forty-nine is seven squared and sixty-four is eight squared, and fifty is much closer to forty-nine, so it is just over seven.

Combining: the square root of 144 plus the cube root of 125 is twelve plus five, seventeen. Six cubed minus twelve squared is 216 minus 144, seventy-two.

Roots of products: the square root of 36 times 4 equals the square root of 144, twelve, which is also the square root of 36 times the square root of 4, six times two. This works for multiplication but NOT for addition: the square root of 9 plus 16 is the square root of 25, five — not three plus four, seven.

Here come the questions for this section: multiples of ten, estimating roots and combining operations.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same 144 and 216 now, built from tiles and blocks. Same numbers, ones you can picture.

## Subtopic: Tiles in a Square

You have a bag of floor tiles and you want to lay them in a perfect square — the same number along each side.

With nine tiles you can do it: three along, three down. With sixteen: four by four. With 144 tiles: twelve by twelve. These numbers — 9, 16, 144 — are called square numbers, because they make squares.

To find the number of tiles in a square, multiply the side by itself. Twelve along and twelve down: twelve times twelve, 144. We write it as twelve squared.

To go backwards — you know there are 144 tiles and want the side — you ask: what number times itself is 144? Twelve. That is the square root.

Careful: twelve squared is NOT twelve times two. Twelve times two is only twenty-four tiles — that would not even fill two rows!

Learn the first twelve square numbers like a song: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144.

Your questions for this section are with you now: side times side for the square, and backwards for the root.

## Subtopic: Blocks in a Cube

Now stack blocks into a cube — the same number along, across and up.

Two along, two across, two up: two times two is four blocks in the bottom layer, and two layers make eight blocks. Eight is two cubed.

Six along, six across, six up: the bottom layer is six times six, thirty-six blocks. Six layers of thirty-six: 216 blocks. That is six cubed.

To go backwards — you have 216 blocks in a cube and want to know how many along each edge — ask: what number, used three times, multiplies to 216? Six. That is the cube root.

The first six cube numbers are 1, 8, 27, 64, 125 and 216. And here is a fun one: sixty-four is a square — eight by eight — and also a cube — four by four by four.

Careful: six cubed is NOT six times three. Eighteen blocks would not even make one layer!

You can now build squares and cubes and find their roots. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: layers of squares for cubes, and backwards for the cube root.
