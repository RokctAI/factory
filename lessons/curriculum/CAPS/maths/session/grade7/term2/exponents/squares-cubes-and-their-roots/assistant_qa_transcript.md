### subtopic_1 — Square Numbers and Square Roots
**Assistant:** Why are they called square numbers?

**Tutor:** Because you can arrange that number of tiles or dots into a perfect square. Nine dots make a three by three square, sixteen make a four by four square, and 144 make a twelve by twelve square. A number like twelve cannot be arranged into a square — you can make three by four, but that is a rectangle. So the name describes a shape, and the square root is the length of the side of that shape. Thinking of the picture is a good way to remember that squaring means a number times itself.

### subtopic_2 — Cube Numbers and Cube Roots
**Assistant:** Why is 64 both a square number and a cube number?

**Tutor:** Because sixty-four can be built both ways. As a square it is eight times eight: 64 tiles in an eight by eight square. As a cube it is four times four times four: 64 blocks in a four by four by four cube. In prime factors, 64 is two multiplied by itself six times, and six can be split into two equal groups of three or three equal groups of two, which is why it works as both. One is the only smaller number with this property.

### subtopic_3 — Using Squares, Cubes and Roots Mentally
**Assistant:** Why does the square root of 9 plus 16 not equal 3 plus 4?

**Tutor:** Because the root sign covers the whole sum, so you must add first: nine plus sixteen is twenty-five, and the square root of twenty-five is five. Three plus four is seven, and seven squared is forty-nine, not twenty-five, so seven cannot be the square root. Square roots split nicely across multiplication — the root of 36 times 4 is six times two — but not across addition. Checking by squaring your answer always reveals the error.
