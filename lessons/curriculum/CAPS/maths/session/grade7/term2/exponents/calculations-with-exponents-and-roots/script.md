# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the order of operations with exponents and roots, the appropriate laws of operations with numbers involving exponents and square and cube roots, and calculations with all four operations using exponents up to 5. The anchor is 3 + 2³ × √25 = 43.

## Subtopic: Where Powers and Roots Fit in the Order of Operations

The order of operations now has one more level. Brackets first. Then exponents and roots — powers, square roots and cube roots. Then multiplication and division, from left to right. Then addition and subtraction, from left to right. Many learners remember this as BODMAS, where the O stands for orders, meaning powers and roots.

Calculate 3 plus two cubed times the square root of twenty-five. No brackets. Powers and roots: two cubed is eight; the square root of twenty-five is five. The expression is now 3 plus 8 times 5. Multiplication next: forty. Addition last: 43.

The common wrong answer is fifty-five, from working left to right: three plus eight is eleven, times five is fifty-five. The order of operations exists precisely to stop this ambiguity.

A root sign acts like a bracket over everything beneath it. The square root of 16 plus 9 means the square root of twenty-five, five, because the sum under the sign is calculated first.

Powers apply only to the number directly attached. Two times three squared is two times nine, eighteen — not six squared. If you want six squared, you must write brackets: two times three, in brackets, squared.

The questions for this section are ready: powers and roots first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.

## Subtopic: Laws of Operations With Powers and Roots

The commutative, associative and distributive properties still hold when numbers are written as powers, because powers are just numbers.

Commutative: two cubed plus three squared equals three squared plus two cubed: seventeen either way. Associative: two squared times five times five squared can be regrouped as two squared times five squared, times five — four times twenty-five is one hundred, times five is five hundred.

Distributive: four squared times nine plus four squared times one equals four squared times ten, which is sixteen times ten, 160.

Roots and products: the square root of a product equals the product of the square roots. The square root of 4 times 25 equals the square root of 4 times the square root of 25, two times five, ten — and the square root of one hundred is indeed ten. The same works for cube roots: the cube root of 8 times 27 is two times three, six, and the cube root of 216 is six.

But roots do NOT distribute over addition or subtraction: the square root of 36 plus 64 is the square root of one hundred, ten, not six plus eight, fourteen. And powers do not distribute over addition either: three plus two, squared, is twenty-five, not nine plus four, thirteen.

Pause here for this section's questions: which laws hold, which do not, and how to use them to calculate cleverly.

## Subtopic: Multi-Step Calculations

Multi-step calculations combine everything. Work carefully, one level at a time, and write each line.

Example one: open bracket five squared minus three to the power four divided by nine, close bracket, times the cube root of eight. Inside the bracket, powers first: twenty-five and eighty-one. Then division: eighty-one divided by nine is nine. Then subtraction: twenty-five minus nine is sixteen. Outside: the cube root of eight is two. Sixteen times two is 32.

Example two: two to the power five minus four cubed divided by two to the power four. Powers: thirty-two minus sixty-four divided by sixteen. Division: four. Subtraction: twenty-eight.

Example three, with roots of squares: the square root of twelve squared plus five squared. Under the root: 144 plus 25, 169. The square root of 169 is thirteen. This kind of calculation appears in Grade 8 with the Theorem of Pythagoras.

Write each step on a new line with an equals sign, so that a marker can follow it and you can find slips.

## Subtopic: Doing Things in the Right Order

Think of a calculation like getting dressed. Socks before shoes! If you do them in the wrong order, the result is a mess. Maths has an order too.

First: anything in brackets. Second: powers and roots — the little numbers up high and the root signs. Third: times and divide. Last: plus and minus.

Let us try three plus two cubed times the square root of twenty-five. No brackets. Powers and roots: two cubed is eight, and the square root of twenty-five is five. Now we have three plus eight times five. Times next: eight times five is forty. Plus last: three plus forty, 43.

If you went straight from left to right — three plus eight is eleven, times five is fifty-five — you would get the wrong answer. That is putting shoes on before socks.

Another trap: two times three squared. The little two belongs ONLY to the three. Three squared is nine, two times nine is eighteen. If you want six squared, you need brackets around the two times three.

Your questions for this section are with you now: brackets, powers and roots, times and divide, plus and minus.

## Subtopic: Roots Are Like Brackets

A root sign has a long roof. Everything under the roof must be worked out first, just like brackets.

The square root of sixteen plus nine: under the roof is sixteen plus nine, twenty-five. Now the root: five.

Many people get this wrong by taking the roots separately: the square root of sixteen is four, the square root of nine is three, four plus three is seven. But seven squared is forty-nine, not twenty-five! So seven cannot be right.

When the roof covers a multiplication, though, splitting IS allowed. The square root of four times twenty-five: the square root of four is two, the square root of twenty-five is five, and two times five is ten. Check: ten squared is one hundred, and four times twenty-five is one hundred. It works.

So remember: roots split over times, but not over plus. If you are not sure, work out what is under the roof first. That always works.

You now know where powers and roots fit, and how to handle the root roof. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: work under the roof first, then take the root.
