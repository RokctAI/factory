### subtopic_1 — Base, Exponent and Power
**Assistant:** What exactly does the exponent count?

**Tutor:** It counts how many times the base appears as a factor in the multiplication. Two to the power five has five twos multiplied together. It does not count the multiplication signs — there are only four of those — and it is not something you multiply the base by. A useful check is to expand the power and count the factors: if you wrote two times two times two times two times two, that is five twos, so the exponent is five. This becomes important later when you learn the laws of exponents, which are all about counting factors.

### subtopic_2 — Calculating Powers
**Assistant:** Why do powers grow so much faster than multiples?

**Tutor:** Because each step multiplies by the base instead of adding it. Multiples of two go two, four, six, eight, ten — adding two each time. Powers of two go two, four, eight, sixteen, thirty-two — doubling each time. After ten steps the multiples reach twenty, but the powers reach 1 024. That repeated multiplication is why messages, rumours and some infections can spread so quickly, and why exponents are a useful way to describe growth.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing Numbers in Exponential Form
**Assistant:** Why is two to the fifth not the same as five squared?

**Tutor:** Because the base and the exponent play completely different roles. In two to the fifth, two is multiplied five times: two times two times two times two times two, thirty-two. In five squared, five is multiplied twice: five times five, twenty-five. Swapping them changes both the number being multiplied and how many times, so the results differ. The only small whole numbers where swapping gives the same answer are two to the fourth and four squared, both sixteen — a coincidence, not a rule.
