### subtopic_1 — Flow Diagrams: Finding Outputs
**Assistant:** Why does the order of the operators matter?

**Tutor:** Because the second operator acts on the result of the first, not on the original input. Multiplying three by eight first gives twenty-four, and adding twelve gives thirty-six. Adding twelve first gives fifteen, and multiplying that by eight gives 120. In the taxi, the twelve rand is a once-off charge, so it must be added after the per-kilometre amount, not multiplied along with it. The flow diagram's arrows show the order, and following them exactly is what makes the machine give the right output.

### subtopic_2 — Working Backwards to Find Inputs
**Assistant:** Why must I undo the operations in reverse order?

**Tutor:** Because the last thing done to the number is the first thing you must undo, like taking off shoes before socks. The taxi machine multiplied by eight and then added twelve, so the twelve is the outermost layer of the fare. Removing it first gives the per-kilometre amount, and dividing that by eight gives the distance. If you divided first, you would be dividing the twelve rand fixed charge by eight too, which mixes up the two parts of the fare and gives the wrong distance.

### subtopic_3 — Finding the Rule From Inputs and Outputs
**Assistant:** What should I do when the inputs in a table are not consecutive?

**Tutor:** Compare how much the output changes with how much the input changes. In the table with inputs two, five and ten, the input rises by three from two to five and the output rises by nine, so the output rises three for every one the input rises. That tells you the multiplier is three. Then compare three times the input with the output to find what to add or subtract. Always check the finished rule on every pair in the table, because a rule fitted to two pairs might not fit the third.
