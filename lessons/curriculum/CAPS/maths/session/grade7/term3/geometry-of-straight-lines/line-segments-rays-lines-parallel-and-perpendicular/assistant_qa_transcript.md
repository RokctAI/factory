### subtopic_1 — Line Segments, Rays and Straight Lines
**Assistant:** Why are ray AB and ray BA different, but line AB and line BA the same?

**Tutor:** Because a ray has a starting point and a direction, and the name tells you both: the first letter is where it starts, the second shows which way it goes. Ray AB starts at A and heads through B; ray BA starts at B and heads through A, in the opposite direction. They overlap only between A and B. A line has no starting point and goes forever both ways, so naming it from A to B or from B to A describes exactly the same set of points.

### subtopic_2 — Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
**Assistant:** Can two different straight lines meet at more than one point?

**Tutor:** No. Two different straight lines in a plane can meet at most once. If they met at two points, there would be two different straight paths between those two points, but only one straight line can pass through two given points. So two lines either meet at exactly one point, are parallel and never meet, or are actually the same line lying on top of itself. This is why we can speak of the point of intersection of two lines.

### subtopic_3 — Using the Language of Lines
**Assistant:** Why does it matter whether I call something a line or a segment?

**Tutor:** Because they are different objects with different properties. A segment has a length you can measure and use in calculations, such as the perimeter of a shape. A line has no length; it goes on forever. Later, when you work with angles formed by lines, or with distances along segments, using the wrong word can lead to wrong reasoning — for example, trying to measure a line, or assuming two segments meet because their lines would. Precise words keep your geometry arguments correct.
