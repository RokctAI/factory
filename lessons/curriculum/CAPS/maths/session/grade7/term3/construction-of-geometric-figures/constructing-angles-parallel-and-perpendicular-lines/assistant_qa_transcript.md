### subtopic_1 — Constructing Angles With a Protractor
**Assistant:** Why construct a 110 degree angle to get a 250 degree reflex angle?

**Tutor:** Because the protractor only reads up to 180 degrees, so a reflex angle cannot be marked directly. The angle inside and the reflex angle outside together make a full turn, 360 degrees. So drawing the inside angle of 360 minus 250, which is 110 degrees, automatically creates the 250 degree reflex angle on the outside. You then mark the outside with an arc to show which angle you mean. Extending an arm and adding 70 degrees beyond the straight line is a second method that checks the first.

### subtopic_2 — Constructing Perpendicular Lines
**Assistant:** Why does the compass need to be opened to more than half of AB?

**Tutor:** Because the arcs from A and from B must cross each other. If the opening is less than half of AB, the arc from A does not reach as far as the arc from B, and they never meet, so there are no crossing points to join. With more than half, the arcs overlap above and below the segment. Every crossing point is the same distance from A as from B, and the line through those points is exactly the set of points equidistant from A and B, which is why it cuts AB in half at a right angle.

### subtopic_3 — Constructing Parallel Lines
**Assistant:** Why does constructing equal angles on a transversal give parallel lines?

**Tutor:** Because if two lines make the same angle with a line that crosses them, they are heading in exactly the same direction. Picture the transversal as a road and the two lines as side streets turning off it. If both side streets turn off at sixty-five degrees, on the same side, they point the same way, so they can never get closer together or further apart — they are parallel. In Grade 8 you will learn this as corresponding angles, and it becomes a key tool in geometry.
