### subtopic_1 — Angles and Their Types
**Assistant:** Why is the angle between neighbouring hour marks thirty degrees?

**Tutor:** Because a full turn of the clock face is 360 degrees, and the twelve hour marks divide it into twelve equal parts. 360 divided by twelve is thirty. So from twelve to one is thirty degrees, from twelve to three is three of those parts, ninety degrees, and from twelve to six is six parts, 180 degrees. This makes a clock a handy mental model for angles: you can picture an angle's size by imagining the hands.

### subtopic_2 — Measuring With a Protractor
**Assistant:** How do I know which of the two scales to read?

**Tutor:** Read the scale that starts at zero on the arm you lined up with the baseline. If your arm points to the right, the scale that has zero on the right is yours; if your arm points to the left, use the one with zero on the left. Then follow that scale round to the other arm. As a safety check, classify the angle by eye before you measure: an angle that is clearly acute must give a reading below ninety, so if you read 120 you have switched scales.

### subtopic_3 — Measuring Reflex Angles
**Assistant:** Why does subtracting from 360 give the reflex angle?

**Tutor:** Because the inside angle and the reflex angle together go all the way around the vertex, which is one full turn, 360 degrees. Turning from one arm to the other the short way gives the inside angle; turning the long way gives the reflex angle. Since the two turns together make a complete revolution, the reflex angle is whatever is left of 360 after the inside angle. Measuring the inside angle with the protractor is easy, so this is the quickest method.
