### subtopic_1 — Centre, Radius, Diameter and Circumference
**Assistant:** Why are all the radii of a circle equal?

**Tutor:** Because that is what a circle is. A circle is defined as all the points that are a fixed distance from the centre, and the radius is exactly that fixed distance. When you draw a circle with a compass, the fixed opening of the compass is the radius, and every point the pencil marks is that same distance from the point of the compass. That is also why a wheel rolls smoothly: every part of the rim is the same distance from the hub, so the axle stays at the same height.

### subtopic_2 — Chords and Arcs
**Assistant:** Why is the diameter the longest chord?

**Tutor:** Because any other chord can be compared with two radii. Take a chord that does not pass through the centre, and join each of its ends to the centre. You get a triangle with two sides that are radii and a third side that is the chord. In any triangle, one side is shorter than the other two put together, so the chord is shorter than two radii — which is the diameter. Only when the chord passes straight through the centre does the triangle flatten into a line and the chord equal two radii.

### subtopic_3 — Sectors and Segments
**Assistant:** How do I tell a sector from a segment quickly?

**Tutor:** Look at the straight edges. A sector has two straight edges, and both are radii meeting at the centre, like a pizza slice. A segment has only one straight edge, a chord, which does not usually pass through the centre, like the piece of a round loaf you cut off near the edge. If the region touches the centre at a point with two radii, it is a sector; if it is cut off by a single straight line, it is a segment.
