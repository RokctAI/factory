### subtopic_1 — Unknown Angles in Triangles
**Assistant:** Why do I divide by two to find the base angles of an isosceles triangle?

**Tutor:** Because the two base angles are equal, and together they take up whatever is left of 180 degrees after the top angle. With a top angle of forty degrees, 140 degrees is shared between two equal base angles, so each gets half: seventy degrees. If you forgot to divide, you would get 140 for one base angle, and the triangle's angles would add to far more than 180. Checking that all three angles add to 180 catches that error immediately.

### subtopic_2 — Unknown Angles in Quadrilaterals
**Assistant:** Why do neighbouring angles in a parallelogram add up to 180 degrees?

**Tutor:** Because they lie between two parallel sides, on the same side of a line that crosses them. Picture walking along one side of the parallelogram: at each end you turn onto the next side, and because the sides are parallel, the two turns must together make a half-turn, 180 degrees. Another way to see it: opposite angles are equal, so the four angles are two pairs, say a, b, a, b, adding to 360. That means a plus b is 180, so every pair of neighbours adds to 180.

### subtopic_3 — Unknown Sides and Multi-Step Problems
**Assistant:** How should I set out a geometry solution so that it earns full marks?

**Tutor:** Write one fact per line, each with a reason in brackets or after a dash, in an order that builds towards the answer. For example: base angles are equal, because the triangle is isosceles; the base angles add to 140 degrees, because the angles of a triangle add to 180; each base angle is seventy degrees. A marker can then see exactly which property you used at each step. A correct answer with no reasons often loses marks, while clear reasons earn method marks even if a calculation slips.
