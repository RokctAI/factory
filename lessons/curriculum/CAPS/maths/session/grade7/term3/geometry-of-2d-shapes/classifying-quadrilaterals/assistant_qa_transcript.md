### subtopic_1 — Parallelograms, Rectangles, Rhombi and Squares
**Assistant:** Why is a square also a rectangle?

**Tutor:** Because a rectangle is defined as a parallelogram with four right angles, and a square has both pairs of opposite sides parallel and four right angles. It meets every condition. The square simply has an extra property, four equal sides, that a general rectangle does not need. In the same way a square is also a rhombus, because it has four equal sides and both pairs of opposite sides parallel. Definitions say what must be true, not what must be different, so a square belongs to all three families.

### subtopic_2 — Trapeziums and Kites
**Assistant:** What is the real difference between a kite and a parallelogram?

**Tutor:** Which sides are equal. In a parallelogram the OPPOSITE sides are equal — the top equals the bottom and the left equals the right — and they are parallel. In a kite, the ADJACENT sides are equal — two short sides meet at the top and two long sides meet at the bottom — and in general no sides are parallel. A rhombus has all four sides equal, so it is both: its opposite sides are equal and parallel, and its adjacent sides are equal too.

### subtopic_3 — Sorting and Comparing Quadrilaterals
**Assistant:** Why does a parallelogram with just one right angle have to be a rectangle?

**Tutor:** Because the angles of a parallelogram are linked. Opposite angles are equal, so the angle opposite the right angle is also ninety degrees. And neighbouring angles add up to 180 degrees, because they lie between parallel sides, so each neighbour of a ninety-degree angle is also ninety. That gives four right angles, which is exactly the definition of a rectangle. One right angle is enough to force all the others.
