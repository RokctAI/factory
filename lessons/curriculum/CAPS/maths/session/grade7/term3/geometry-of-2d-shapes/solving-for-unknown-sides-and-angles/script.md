# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving simple geometric problems involving unknown sides and angles in triangles and quadrilaterals, using known properties: the angle sums of 180° and 360°, equal sides and angles in isosceles and equilateral triangles, and the properties of special quadrilaterals. The anchor is a stained-glass window design.

## Subtopic: Unknown Angles in Triangles

The key fact: the interior angles of any triangle add up to 180 degrees. If two angles are known, the third is 180 minus their sum.

A triangle has angles of seventy degrees and fifty degrees. The third angle is 180 minus 120, sixty degrees.

Isosceles triangles add a second fact: the angles opposite the equal sides are equal. The top pane of the window is an isosceles triangle with a top angle of forty degrees between its two equal sides. The base angles are equal, and together they make 180 minus forty, 140 degrees. So each base angle is seventy degrees.

Working the other way: an isosceles triangle has a base angle of sixty-five degrees. The other base angle is also sixty-five, and the top angle is 180 minus 130, fifty degrees.

Equilateral triangles have three equal angles of sixty degrees each.

Right-angled triangles: if one angle is ninety degrees, the other two add to ninety. A right-angled triangle with a thirty-five degree angle has a third angle of fifty-five degrees.

Always state the reason: "angles in a triangle add to 180 degrees", "base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal".

The questions for this section are ready: find unknown angles in triangles and give reasons.

## Subtopic: Unknown Angles in Quadrilaterals

The interior angles of any quadrilateral add up to 360 degrees. You can see why by drawing a diagonal, which splits the quadrilateral into two triangles, each with 180 degrees.

A quadrilateral has angles of eighty, ninety-five and 110 degrees. The fourth angle is 360 minus 285, seventy-five degrees.

Special quadrilaterals give extra facts. In a rectangle and a square, every angle is ninety degrees. In a parallelogram and a rhombus, opposite angles are equal and neighbouring angles add to 180. A parallelogram with an angle of sixty-five degrees has angles sixty-five, 115, sixty-five and 115.

In a kite, the angles between the unequal sides — on either side of the line of symmetry — are equal. The window's kite has a top angle of eighty degrees, a bottom angle of sixty degrees, and two equal side angles. Together the side angles are 360 minus 140, 220 degrees, so each is 110 degrees.

Always check that your four angles add to 360.

Pause here for this section's questions: use 360 degrees and the properties of special quadrilaterals.

## Subtopic: Unknown Sides and Multi-Step Problems

Properties also give unknown side lengths.

Equilateral triangle: all sides equal. If one side is twelve centimetres, the perimeter is thirty-six centimetres.

Isosceles triangle: two sides equal. If the perimeter is thirty centimetres and the base is eight, the two equal sides share twenty-two centimetres, eleven each.

Rectangle and parallelogram: opposite sides equal. A rectangle with perimeter fifty centimetres and length fifteen has width 50 minus thirty, twenty, divided by two: ten centimetres.

Rhombus and square: all sides equal. A rhombus with perimeter thirty-six has sides of nine.

Kite: two pairs of equal adjacent sides. A kite with short sides of twenty centimetres and a perimeter of 130 has long sides of 130 minus forty, ninety, divided by two, forty-five centimetres.

Multi-step problems combine angle and side facts. In the window, the triangle sits on top of the rectangle, sharing its base. If the rectangle is sixty by forty centimetres and the triangle's equal sides are fifty centimetres, the total outline of the window, without the shared base, is fifty plus fifty plus forty plus sixty plus forty: 240 centimetres.

Here come the questions for this section: use properties for sides, and combine facts with reasons.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same stained-glass window now, pane by pane: the triangle at the top, the kite below and the rectangular frame. Same facts, one pane at a time.

## Subtopic: The Stained-Glass Window

A church window has a triangle of coloured glass at the top. The triangle has two equal sloping sides, and the angle at the very top is forty degrees. What are the other two angles?

First fact: the three angles of any triangle always add up to 180 degrees. So far we know forty, so the other two angles share 180 minus forty, which is 140 degrees.

Second fact: the triangle has two equal sides, so the two angles at the bottom are equal too. Two equal angles sharing 140 degrees: half each. Seventy degrees and seventy degrees.

Check: forty plus seventy plus seventy is 180. Perfect!

Another window has a right-angled triangle with one angle of thirty-five degrees. The square corner is ninety, so the other two share ninety. Ninety minus thirty-five is fifty-five degrees.

Each time, say WHY: "angles in a triangle add to 180" and "equal sides give equal angles".

Your questions for this section are with you now: 180 for a triangle, equal sides mean equal angles.

## Subtopic: Kite Panes and Rectangle Frames

Below the triangle, the window has a kite-shaped pane. The top corner is eighty degrees and the bottom corner is sixty degrees. The two side corners are equal. What are they?

Four-sided shapes have angles that add up to 360 degrees. Why? Draw a line from one corner to the opposite corner — you get two triangles, and two lots of 180 is 360.

So the two side corners share 360 minus eighty minus sixty, which is 220 degrees. Equal, so half each: 110 degrees.

The window also has a rectangular frame. Its perimeter is fifty centimetres and its length is fifteen centimetres. How wide is it? A rectangle has two lengths and two widths. Two lengths make thirty centimetres, leaving twenty centimetres for the two widths. Ten centimetres each.

Every problem uses two kinds of fact: totals, like 180 and 360, and "equal" facts, like equal sides and equal angles. Use the total to find what is left, then use the equal facts to share it out.

You can now find missing angles and sides with reasons. The last questions of the lesson are with you now: find the total, take away what you know, and share what is left.
