### subtopic_1 — Congruent Figures
**Assistant:** Why does the order of the letters matter when writing that two triangles are congruent?

**Tutor:** Because the order tells you which vertices match. Writing triangle ABC congruent to triangle PQR says that A matches P, B matches Q and C matches R, so side AB equals side PQ, angle B equals angle Q, and so on. If the letters were written in a different order, the statement would claim different matches, which might be false. Getting the order right lets you read off equal sides and angles directly from the statement, which is essential when you solve problems with congruent shapes later.

### subtopic_2 — Similar Figures
**Assistant:** Why are all squares similar but not all rectangles?

**Tutor:** Because a square's shape is completely fixed by its definition: four equal sides and four right angles. The only thing that can change is the side length, which is just a scale factor, so any square is an enlargement or reduction of any other. A rectangle only needs four right angles; its length and width can be in any ratio. A rectangle three by four and one three by nine have different length-to-width ratios, so one cannot be enlarged into the other. They have the same angles but different shapes.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing Figures Systematically
**Assistant:** Why must every side ratio be the same for figures to be similar?

**Tutor:** Because similar means one figure is an exact enlargement or reduction of the other, and an enlargement multiplies every length by the same number. If one side were doubled and another tripled, the figure would be stretched more in one direction than another, and its shape would change. Checking only one pair of sides cannot reveal that, so you must check every pair. If the ratios are all equal, the shape is preserved; if any ratio differs, the shape has been distorted.
