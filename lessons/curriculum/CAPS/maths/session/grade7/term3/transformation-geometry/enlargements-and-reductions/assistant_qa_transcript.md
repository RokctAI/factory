### subtopic_1 — Scale Factors
**Assistant:** Why do the angles not change in an enlargement?

**Tutor:** Because every length is multiplied by the same number, so the shape keeps its proportions. A corner is formed by two sides meeting at a certain slope; when both sides are scaled equally, they meet at the same slope, so the angle is the same. If you look at an enlarged photograph, the corners of a window or the tilt of a roof look exactly the same as in the small photo, only bigger. Only lengths change; directions and angles do not.

### subtopic_2 — Drawing Enlargements and Reductions on Squared Paper
**Assistant:** Why should I count the steps of a sloping line instead of measuring it?

**Tutor:** Because on squared paper, a sloping line's length is not a whole number of squares, so measuring it is awkward and inaccurate. But the slope is fully described by how many squares it goes across and how many up. Multiplying both of those by the scale factor gives the image line exactly, with the same slope and the correctly scaled length. This keeps every angle the same and avoids the distortions that come from guessing where a sloping line should end.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing Perimeter and Area
**Assistant:** Why does doubling the lengths make the area four times bigger?

**Tutor:** Because area depends on two lengths multiplied together, and both are doubled. A rectangle's area is length times width; if the length doubles and the width doubles, the area is two times two, four times as big. You can see it on squared paper: the enlarged rectangle can be cut into four copies of the original, two along and two up. Perimeter is just one length added up all around, so it only doubles. This difference is why a photo enlarged to double the size needs four times as much paper.
