### subtopic_1 — Translations: Sliding a Figure
**Assistant:** Why is a translated image always congruent to the original?

**Tutor:** Because every point moves by exactly the same amount in the same direction. The distance between any two points of the figure stays the same, since both points moved together, and the angles between the sides stay the same, since nothing was turned. So every side length and every angle of the image matches the object. Same shape and same size is exactly what congruent means. The figure has only changed its position.

### subtopic_2 — Reflections: Flipping in a Mirror Line
**Assistant:** How can I tell whether an image was made by a translation or a reflection?

**Tutor:** Look at which way the figure faces. A translation only slides the figure, so the image faces exactly the same way as the object. A reflection flips it, so the image is a mirror version, reversed: a flag pointing right becomes one pointing left. Another test is to trace the object on thin paper: if you can slide the tracing onto the image without turning it over, it was a translation; if you must turn the tracing over, it was a reflection.

### subtopic_3 — Rotations: Turning About a Point
**Assistant:** Why does a half turn give the same image in both directions?

**Tutor:** Because 180 degrees is exactly half of a full turn. Turning half-way round clockwise or half-way round anticlockwise both end at the point directly opposite where you started, the same distance from the centre. Picture the hands of a clock pointing at twelve: a half turn either way lands on six. For any other angle, the two directions end in different places, so the direction must be stated.
