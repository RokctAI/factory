# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers finding the volume of rectangular prisms by packing them with cubes, finding capacity by filling, understanding why the volume of a rectangular prism is length times width times height, and solving packing problems. It builds on area from the previous lesson and on rectangular prisms and cubes from earlier this term. The anchor is a fruit packhouse in Ceres. Learners on a school visit pack 1 cm cubes into a small box 5 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high. Workers pack crates of apples into a truck, 6 crates long, 4 crates wide and 3 crates high, and fill a water tank to wash the fruit. The governing idea is that volume is the amount of space a 3D object takes up, measured in cubic units, and that a rectangular prism can be filled in layers: one layer is length times width, and the height tells you how many layers there are. Finding volume by packing, explaining and using the rule, and solving packing problems are examinable.

## Subtopic: Packing and Filling

Volume is the amount of space a 3D object takes up. Capacity is the amount a container can hold. A box has a volume because it takes up space, and a capacity because you can fill it.

We measure volume by counting how many cubes of a fixed size fit inside, with no gaps. A cube with edges 1 cm long has a volume of 1 cubic centimetre. If 24 of these cubes exactly fill a box, the volume of the box is 24 cubic centimetres. Bigger things use cubic metres: a cube with edges 1 m long, about the size of a large washing machine box.

Volume is always measured in cubic units. Length is in centimetres, area is in square centimetres and volume is in cubic centimetres.

You can also find capacity by filling. Fill a container with sand, rice or water, and then measure how much it held. A cube with edges of 10 cm holds exactly 1 litre of water, and 10 times 10 times 10 is 1 000, so 1 000 cubic centimetres is 1 litre. That is why 1 cubic centimetre of water is 1 millilitre.

At the packhouse, the learners start packing the small box with 1 cm cubes. They put cubes along the bottom first, then build up layers until the box is full. Counting carefully, they find it holds 60 cubes.

## Subtopic: Layers and the Rule

Counting 60 cubes one by one is slow. Look at how they are arranged instead.

The bottom of the box is 5 cm long and 4 cm wide. So the bottom layer has 4 rows of 5 cubes, which is 20 cubes. That is the same as the area of the base: 5 times 4.

The box is 3 cm high, so 3 layers fit, one on top of another. 3 layers of 20 cubes is 60 cubes. So the volume is 60 cubic centimetres.

This shows us the rule. Volume of a rectangular prism equals length times width times height. The length times the width gives the number of cubes in one layer, and multiplying by the height gives the number of layers.

The rule works in any order, because multiplication can be done in any order. 5 times 4 times 3, 3 times 4 times 5 and 4 times 3 times 5 are all 60.

A cube has the same length, width and height. A cube with edges of 4 cm has a volume of 4 times 4 times 4, which is 64 cubic centimetres. A cube with edges of 3 cm has a volume of 27 cubic centimetres.

Compare length, area and volume. A line 3 cm long is a length of 3 cm. A square with sides of 3 cm has an area of 9 square centimetres, which is one layer of squares. A cube with edges of 3 cm has a volume of 27 cubic centimetres, which is 3 layers of 9 cubes. Each step adds one more direction to multiply by.

## Subtopic: Packing Problems

How many crates fit in the truck? Each layer on the floor of the truck is 6 crates long and 4 crates wide, which is 24 crates. The crates are stacked 3 high. So the truck holds 24 times 3, which is 72 crates.

You can work backwards. A box has a volume of 60 cubic centimetres and a base 5 cm by 4 cm. The base layer holds 20 cubes, so the number of layers is 60 divided by 20, which is 3. The box is 3 cm high.

Different boxes can have the same volume. A box 6 cm by 2 cm by 2 cm and a box 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm both have a volume of 24 cubic centimetres, even though they look different.

Before you calculate, estimate. The truck holds a bit more than 20 crates in each layer and 3 layers, so it should hold a bit more than 60 crates. 72 fits.

The washing tank is 100 cm long, 50 cm wide and 40 cm deep. Its volume is 100 times 50 times 40, which is 200 000 cubic centimetres. Since 1 000 cubic centimetres is 1 litre, the tank holds 200 litres.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding the edges, saying the 5 by 4 by 3 box has a volume of 12; volume is length times width times height, 60. Two: finding only one layer, saying the volume is 20; multiply by the height as well. Three: counting only the cubes you can see in a picture; the hidden cubes inside count too. Four: writing the volume in square centimetres; volume needs cubic units.
