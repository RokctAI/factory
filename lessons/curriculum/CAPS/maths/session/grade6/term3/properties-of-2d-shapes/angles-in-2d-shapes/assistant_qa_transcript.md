### subtopic_1 — Naming Angles
**Assistant:** Why is a straight line called an angle when it does not look like a corner?

**Tutor:** Because an angle is an amount of turn, not a corner shape. Picture two arms joined at a vertex, like the hands of a clock. Start with both hands pointing to 12. Now turn one hand. At 3 o'clock it has turned a quarter of the way around, and the hands make a right angle. Keep turning. At 6 o'clock it has turned halfway around. The two hands now point in opposite directions, so together they make a straight line. The hand has still turned, so there is still an angle between the arms; it is a half turn, and we call it a straight angle. It is two right angles put together. If you keep turning past 6, the angle becomes reflex, and when the hand gets back to 12, it has turned a full revolution. Sizwe on his skateboard is the same. If he turns to face the way he came, he has turned through a straight angle, even though he has no corner to show for it. The straight angle is a very useful benchmark, because it separates obtuse angles from reflex angles.

### subtopic_2 — Angles Inside 2D Shapes
**Assistant:** How can an L-shape have a reflex angle inside it?

**Tutor:** Look at the inside corner, where the L turns in, and imagine standing inside the shape at that vertex. The two sides that meet there open out around you by more than a half turn. That is bigger than a straight angle, so it is a reflex angle. From outside the shape, the same corner looks like a neat right angle, because the outside part is a quarter turn. But the angle of the shape is the angle inside it, and inside it is three quarters of a full turn. The other five corners of the L-shaped deck are ordinary right angles, both inside and out. A good check is to use your right-angle tester. Put it in the inside corner, inside the shape. It fills only part of the space, and there is room for two more testers. That means the angle is three right angles, which is bigger than a straight angle. Shapes that have a reflex angle look as if they have been dented or pushed in, like the L-shape, an arrowhead or a star. Shapes with no dents, like squares and regular hexagons, have no reflex angles inside them.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing and Sorting by Angles
**Assistant:** Why can a triangle not have two right angles?

**Tutor:** Because the three angles inside any triangle always add up to exactly a straight angle, a half turn. You can test this. Cut out any paper triangle, tear off its three corners, and put the corners next to each other with their points touching. They always make a straight line. Now think about two right angles. Two right angles together already make a straight angle, a whole half turn. That leaves nothing at all for the third angle, and a triangle must have three angles that are bigger than nothing. So two right angles cannot fit in a triangle. The same is true for obtuse angles. One obtuse angle is more than a right angle, so two obtuse angles would be more than a straight angle, which is too much. That is why a triangle can have at most one right angle or one obtuse angle, and its other angles must be acute. The railings at the skate park are made of right-angled triangles, each with one right angle and two acute angles. An equilateral triangle has three acute angles, all the same size.
