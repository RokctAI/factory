# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising and naming polygons, including triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, heptagons and octagons, deciding whether a polygon is regular or irregular, and describing, sorting and comparing 2D shapes by the number of sides, the length of the sides and the size of the angles. It builds on naming 2D shapes in earlier grades. The anchor is a Grade 6 class on a shape walk through the centre of Durban. They see a stop sign, a yield sign, a rectangular shop window, an L-shaped paving area, a kite in the sky and a honeycomb at a market stall. The governing idea is that a polygon is a closed flat shape made only of straight sides, that its name comes from its number of sides, and that a polygon is regular only when all its sides are equal and all its angles are equal. Naming polygons, deciding whether they are regular or irregular, and sorting and comparing shapes by sides and angles are examinable.

## Subtopic: Naming Polygons

A polygon is a closed 2D shape made only of straight sides. Closed means there are no gaps: you could walk all the way around it and get back to where you started. Straight means no curves. A circle is not a polygon, because its edge is curved. A shape with a gap is not a polygon either.

The corners of a polygon are called vertices. One corner is a vertex. Every polygon has the same number of vertices as sides, and the same number of angles inside it.

Polygons are named by their number of sides. A triangle has 3 sides. A quadrilateral has 4 sides; squares, rectangles, parallelograms, rhombuses, kites and trapeziums are all quadrilaterals. A pentagon has 5 sides. A hexagon has 6 sides. A heptagon has 7 sides. An octagon has 8 sides.

Some memory tricks help. Tri means three, as in a tricycle. Quad means four, as in a quad bike. Octa means eight, as in an octopus with eight arms. Hexagon and heptagon are easy to mix up; remember that hexagon has an x, just like six, so the other one, heptagon, has seven sides.

On the shape walk, the stop sign has 8 sides, so it is an octagon. The yield sign is a triangle. The cells of the honeycomb are hexagons.

You can also see that the number of sides and the number of angles always match. A triangle has 3 sides and 3 angles. A pentagon has 5 sides and 5 angles. That is because every vertex joins two sides and makes one angle inside the shape.

## Subtopic: Regular and Irregular Polygons

A regular polygon has all its sides equal and all its angles equal. Both must be true. If even one side or one angle is different, the polygon is irregular.

The stop sign is a regular octagon. All 8 sides are the same length and all 8 angles are the same size. Each cell of a honeycomb is a regular hexagon. An equilateral triangle, with three equal sides and three equal angles, is a regular triangle. A square is a regular quadrilateral.

The shop window is a rectangle, 6 m wide and 3 m high. Its four angles are all right angles, so the angles are equal. But its sides are not all equal, because 6 m and 3 m are different. So it is an irregular quadrilateral.

A rhombus is the opposite case. All four sides are equal, but if its angles are not right angles, two are smaller and two are bigger. So it is also irregular.

The L-shaped paving area is worth a careful count. Walk around it and count the sides: there are 6. So it is a hexagon, even though it does not look like a honeycomb cell. Its sides are different lengths, so it is an irregular hexagon. Any shape with 6 straight sides is a hexagon, whatever it looks like.

The questions for this section are with you now: deciding whether polygons are regular or irregular.

## Subtopic: Describing, Sorting and Comparing Shapes

To describe a shape fully, say three things: how many sides it has, which sides are equal in length, and what sizes its angles are. In drawings, equal sides are marked with small matching dashes, and right angles are marked with a small square in the corner.

The kite in the sky has 4 sides. It has two pairs of equal sides, with the equal sides next to each other. It has one pair of equal angles opposite each other.

The yield sign is a triangle with three equal sides and three equal angles. A rectangle has two pairs of equal sides, opposite each other, and four right angles. Notice how the same three questions describe every shape.

We can sort shapes in a table. Put them in columns by number of sides and in rows by regular or irregular. The stop sign goes under octagons and regular. The L-shape goes under hexagons and irregular. The window goes under quadrilaterals and irregular.

We can also sort by angles. Which shapes have at least one right angle? The square, the rectangle and the L-shape do. The equilateral triangle, the regular hexagon and the stop sign do not; their angles are all equal, but none of them is a right angle.

When you compare two shapes, say what is the same and what is different. A square and a rhombus both have four equal sides, but the square has four right angles and the rhombus does not.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: calling a circle a polygon; a polygon has only straight sides. Two: calling a rectangle regular because its angles are all equal; the sides must be equal too. Three: saying an L-shape has 4 sides; count every side and you get 6, so it is a hexagon. Four: calling a 7-sided shape an octagon; an octagon has 8 sides, and a 7-sided shape is a heptagon.

The questions for this section are with you now: describing, sorting and comparing shapes.
