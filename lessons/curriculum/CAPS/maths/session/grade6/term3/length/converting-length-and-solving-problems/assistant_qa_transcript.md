### subtopic_1 — Bigger Units to Smaller Units
**Assistant:** How do I remember whether to multiply or divide when I convert?

**Tutor:** Think about the size of the pieces. If you change into a smaller unit, the pieces are smaller, so you need more of them, and the number must get bigger. That means you multiply. If you change into a bigger unit, the pieces are bigger, so you need fewer of them, and the number must get smaller. That means you divide. Picture Lindiwe's 8 750 m ride. A kilometre is a big piece, 1 000 m long. How many of those big pieces fit into 8 750 m? Not many: 8 and three quarters. So you divide by 1 000 and get 8,75 km. Now picture her 12,5 km ride in metres. A metre is a small piece. Many of them fit into 12,5 km: 12 500. So you multiply by 1 000. After every conversion, check that the number moved in the right direction. If you change kilometres to metres and your answer is smaller than what you started with, you divided by mistake. Also check that you used the right number: 10 between mm and cm, 100 between cm and m, and 1 000 between m and km.

### subtopic_2 — Smaller Units to Bigger Units
**Assistant:** How do I change 3/4 km into metres?

**Tutor:** Find three quarters of 1 000 m, because one kilometre is 1 000 m. To find a fraction of a number, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. 1 000 divided by 4 is 250, so one quarter of a kilometre is 250 m. Then 250 times 3 is 750, so three quarters of a kilometre is 750 m. You can also go through decimals. 3/4 is 0,75, which is 75 hundredths. One hundredth of 1 000 m is 10 m, so 75 hundredths is 750 m. Both ways give the same answer. A common mistake is to say 3/4 km is 75 m, because 3/4 is 75 hundredths and the learner forgets that a kilometre is 1 000 m, not 100 m. A quick estimate catches this: half a kilometre is 500 m, and three quarters is more than half, so the answer must be more than 500 m. 75 m is far too small. Learn the common ones by heart, because they come up often: 1/2 km is 500 m, 1/4 km is 250 m, 3/4 km is 750 m and 1/10 km is 100 m.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Length Problems
**Assistant:** Why can I not just add 12,5 km and 8 750 m as they are?

**Tutor:** Because the numbers count different-sized pieces. 12,5 counts kilometres and 8 750 counts metres. Adding them is like adding 12 rand and 50 cents to make 62 of something: the answer has no sensible unit. If you add 12,5 and 8 750, you get 8 762,5, which is neither kilometres nor metres. Lindiwe certainly did not ride 8 762,5 km in two days. Change one of the lengths so that both are in the same unit. You can change 8 750 m into 8,75 km, and then add 12,5 km and 8,75 km to get 21,25 km. Or you can change 12,5 km into 12 500 m, and then add 12 500 m and 8 750 m to get 21 250 m. Both answers are the same distance, since 21 250 m is 21,25 km. Choose the unit the question asks for, or the one that avoids messy decimals. Then estimate to check: about 12 km plus about 9 km is about 21 km, so 21,25 km makes sense.
