# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting lengths between millimetres, centimetres, metres and kilometres, including common fraction and decimal fraction forms to two decimal places, and solving problems in contexts involving length. It builds on measuring and recording length in the previous lesson and on multiplying decimals by 10 and 100 in Term 2. The anchor is Lindiwe, who trains for a cycling race near Upington. Her logbook shows 12,5 km on Monday, 8 750 m on Tuesday and 15,25 km on Wednesday, and her weekly goal is 50 km. She also cuts a 5 m rope into pieces of 25 cm to tie her gear. The governing idea is that when you change a bigger unit into a smaller one you multiply, because you need more of the small units, and when you change a smaller unit into a bigger one you divide, and that you must change lengths into the same unit before adding or subtracting them. Converting in both directions, writing lengths as decimals and fractions, and solving length problems are examinable.

## Subtopic: Bigger Units to Smaller Units

Remember the links. 1 cm is 10 mm. 1 m is 100 cm. 1 km is 1 000 m. And 1 m is 1 000 mm.

To change a bigger unit into a smaller unit, multiply. Monday's ride is 12,5 km. There are 1 000 m in each kilometre, so 12 km is 12 000 m. The 0,5 km is half a kilometre, which is 500 m. Altogether, 12,5 km is 12 500 m.

Decimals with two places work the same way. 2,05 km is 2 km and 5 hundredths of a kilometre. One hundredth of 1 000 m is 10 m, so 5 hundredths is 50 m. So 2,05 km is 2 050 m. And 0,45 km is 45 hundredths of 1 000 m, which is 450 m.

Fractions are often easier. Lindiwe's warm-up is 3/4 km. One quarter of 1 000 m is 250 m, so three quarters is 750 m. Half a metre is 50 cm, and a fifth of a metre is 20 cm.

For metres to centimetres, multiply by 100. Lindiwe's bicycle is 1,75 m long, which is 175 cm. For centimetres to millimetres, multiply by 10. A bolt is 2,4 cm long, which is 24 mm.

Always check that the answer is a bigger number. You are counting the same length in smaller pieces, so there must be more of them.

A conversion table helps you keep track. Write the units from biggest to smallest: km, m, cm, mm. Moving from left to right, you multiply: by 1 000 from km to m, by 100 from m to cm and by 10 from cm to mm. Moving from right to left, you divide by the same numbers.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing bigger units into smaller units.

## Subtopic: Smaller Units to Bigger Units

To change a smaller unit into a bigger unit, divide. Now the answer should be a smaller number, because the pieces are bigger.

Tuesday's ride is 8 750 m. Divide by 1 000 to get kilometres. 8 000 m is 8 km. The 750 m left over is 750 thousandths of a kilometre, which is 3/4 km or 0,75 km. So 8 750 m is 8,75 km, or 8 and 3/4 km.

Some benchmarks to know: 500 m is 1/2 km, or 0,5 km. 250 m is 1/4 km, or 0,25 km. 100 m is 1/10 km, or 0,1 km.

For centimetres to metres, divide by 100. 175 cm is 1,75 m. 60 cm is 0,6 m, which is 6/10 or 3/5 of a metre. For millimetres to centimetres, divide by 10. 35 mm is 3,5 cm. For millimetres to metres, divide by 1 000. A shelf of 1 200 mm is 1,2 m.

You can also write a length using two units, the way the logbook does. 8 750 m is 8 km and 750 m. 175 cm is 1 m and 75 cm. 35 mm is 3 cm and 5 mm. This is useful when you measure, because you read the big units first and then the small ones.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing smaller units into bigger units, as decimals and fractions.

## Subtopic: Solving Length Problems

How far did Lindiwe ride on Monday and Tuesday together? The lengths are 12,5 km and 8 750 m, which are in different units. Change 8 750 m into 8,75 km. Then 12,5 km plus 8,75 km is 21,25 km. Line up the commas when you add.

Add Wednesday's 15,25 km as well: 21,25 plus 15,25 is 36,5 km for the three days. Her goal is 50 km for the week. She still needs 50 minus 36,5, which is 13,5 km.

Lindiwe cuts a 5 m rope into pieces of 25 cm. How many pieces? Change 5 m into 500 cm. Then 500 divided by 25 is 20 pieces.

She rides 2,4 km to school and 2,4 km back each day. In 5 school days, that is 4,8 km a day, and 4,8 times 5 is 24 km.

At the sports ground, one lap of the track is 400 m. Seven laps are 2 800 m, which is 2,8 km.

For every problem, follow the same steps. Read the question and note the units. Change them into the same unit. Choose the operation. Estimate, calculate and check. Then give the answer in the unit the question asks for.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 12,5 km as 125 m; there are 1 000 m in a kilometre, not 10, so it is 12 500 m. Two: writing 8 750 m as 87,5 km by dividing by 100; divide by 1 000 to get 8,75 km. Three: writing 3/4 km as 75 m; three quarters of 1 000 m is 750 m. Four: adding 12,5 km and 8 750 m to get 8 762,5; change to the same unit first, and the answer is 21,25 km.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving length problems.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with Lindiwe's cycling — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Big to Small: Multiply

km to m: times 1 000. 12,5 km is 12 500 m.

m to cm: times 100. 1,75 m is 175 cm.

3/4 km is 750 m.

Keep multiplying in mind and try the questions for this part: big to small.

## Subtopic: Small to Big: Divide

m to km: divide by 1 000. 8 750 m is 8,75 km.

cm to m: divide by 100. 175 cm is 1,75 m.

500 m is half a kilometre.

Hold dividing in mind and try this part's questions: small to big.

## Subtopic: Same Unit, Then Solve

12,5 km plus 8 750 m: first make it 8,75 km.

Then 12,5 plus 8,75 is 21,25 km.

Pocket summary of the lesson. 10 mm is 1 cm, 100 cm is 1 m and 1 000 m is 1 km. Big unit to small unit: multiply, and the number gets bigger. Small unit to big unit: divide, and the number gets smaller. Learn the fractions: 500 m is 1/2 km and 250 m is 1/4 km. Before you add or subtract lengths, change them into the same unit.

Hold the same-unit rule in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: length problems.
