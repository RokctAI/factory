### subtopic_1 — Choosing Units and Instruments
**Assistant:** Why should I start measuring at the 0 mark and not at the end of the ruler?

**Tutor:** Because on many rulers, the 0 mark is not at the very edge. There is often a small gap of a few millimetres before the 0, so that the 0 mark does not wear away when the ruler gets bumped. If you put the end of the eraser at the edge of the ruler, you are actually starting a few millimetres before 0. Then the reading at the other end will be a few millimetres too long. For the eraser, you might read 48 mm instead of 45 mm. That may not sound like much, but in a design or a science experiment it can matter a lot. So always look for the 0 mark and line up the end of the object exactly with it. If the 0 mark is worn away or hard to see, you can start at another mark, such as 1 cm, and then subtract. If the pencil starts at the 1 cm mark and ends at the 15 cm mark, its length is 15 minus 1, which is 14 cm. The same rule applies to a tape measure: check where its 0 is before you start.

### subtopic_2 — Measuring and Recording Accurately
**Assistant:** Which instrument should I use to measure the fun run route?

**Tutor:** Use a trundle wheel. The route is 2,5 km long and goes around corners, along paths and down streets. A ruler or a metre stick would take far too long, because you would have to lift and place it thousands of times, and every placing adds a small error. A tape measure is better, but even a long one is only about 30 m, and you would still need to move it many times. A trundle wheel solves this. It is a wheel that goes around exactly once for every metre it rolls, and it clicks each time. You push it along the route and count the clicks, so 2 500 clicks means 2 500 m, which is 2,5 km. It also follows curves and corners, because the wheel stays on the ground the whole way. Choosing the right instrument is part of measuring well. Small lengths need a ruler, medium straight lengths need a metre stick, curved lengths need a tape measure, and long distances on the ground need a trundle wheel.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing and Ordering Lengths
**Assistant:** How can 1,2 m be longer than 115 cm when 115 is a bigger number?

**Tutor:** Because the two numbers are in different units, so you cannot compare them directly. It is like comparing R1,20 with 115 cents. Which is more money? R1,20 is 120 cents, so it is more than 115 cents, even though 115 looks bigger. Lengths work the same way. A metre is 100 times as long as a centimetre. So 1,2 m is 1,2 times 100 cm, which is 120 cm. Now both lengths are in centimetres, 120 cm and 115 cm, and you can see that the desk at 1,2 m is 5 cm longer than the bookshelf. The rule is to change the lengths into the same unit before you compare or order them. It is usually easiest to change everything into the smallest unit in the list, because then you avoid decimals. For 2 m, 150 cm and 1 800 mm, change them all to millimetres: 2 000 mm, 1 500 mm and 1 800 mm. Now the order from shortest to longest is easy to see: 150 cm, 1 800 mm, 2 m.
