# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 3 topics: measuring and converting length; perimeter, area and volume; the properties of 2D shapes, including polygons, quadrilaterals, angles and circles; lines of symmetry; transformations, tessellations, and enlargements and reductions; and the properties of 3D objects, including faces, vertices, edges and nets. The anchor is a new community park in Mbombela. It has a walking path of 1,25 km, a rectangular sandpit 6 m long and 4 m wide, a round fountain with a radius of 3 m, a stop sign at the car park, hexagonal paving, a slide, a climbing frame shaped like a square-based pyramid, and a map at the gate that is an enlargement of the plan. The governing idea is that every measurement has the right unit, every shape is described by its sides, angles and symmetry, and every object by its faces, vertices and edges. The whole of Term 3 is examinable in the Term 3 test.

## Subtopic: Length, Perimeter, Area and Volume

The walking path is 1,25 km long. In metres, multiply by 1 000: 1 km is 1 000 m and 0,25 km is a quarter of a kilometre, which is 250 m, so the path is 1 250 m. Zodwa walks 3 laps, which is 3,75 km.

The sandpit is 6 m long and 4 m wide. Its perimeter, the distance around it, is 2 times the sum of 6 and 4, which is 2 times 10, or 20 m. Its area, the space inside, is 6 times 4, which is 24 square metres. Perimeter is a length, in metres; area is in square metres.

A square vegetable bed has a perimeter of 16 m, so each side is 4 m and its area is 16 square metres.

A storage box for the park's balls is 2 m long, 1 m wide and 1 m high. Its volume is 2 times 1 times 1, which is 2 cubic metres. In the play area, a block tower is built from 1 m cubes, 3 long, 2 wide and 2 high. One layer has 6 cubes and there are 2 layers, so there are 12 cubes.

Remember the conversions: 10 mm make 1 cm, 100 cm make 1 m and 1 000 m make 1 km. Multiply to change to a smaller unit and divide to change to a bigger unit.

When a problem mixes units, change them first. A long bench is made by joining a 1,5 m plank to an 80 cm plank. In centimetres that is 150 cm plus 80 cm, which is 230 cm, so the bench is 2,3 m long.

The questions for this section are with you now: length, perimeter, area and volume.

## Subtopic: 2D Shapes, Angles and Symmetry

The stop sign is a regular octagon: 8 equal sides, 8 equal obtuse angles and 8 lines of symmetry. A rectangle 6 m by 4 m, like the sandpit, is irregular, because its sides are not all equal. It has 2 lines of symmetry, not 4, because its diagonals are not lines of symmetry.

The slide meets the ground at an acute angle, smaller than a right angle. The corners of the sandpit are right angles. A gate swung wide open makes an obtuse angle. Remember the order: acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, revolution.

A parallelogram that is not a rectangle has two acute angles and two obtuse angles, and no lines of symmetry. A rectangle is a special parallelogram with four right angles, and a square is a special rectangle.

The fountain is a circle with a radius of 3 m, so its diameter is 6 m. To draw it on the plan, open a pair of compasses to the radius.

In capital letters, A and T have a vertical line of symmetry, B has a horizontal one, H has both, and S has none.

The park plan is drawn on grid paper. The sandpit is drawn as a rectangle 6 squares by 4 squares, so its corners are right angles because the grid lines cross at right angles.

The questions for this section are with you now: 2D shapes, angles and symmetry.

## Subtopic: Transformations and 3D Objects

The hexagonal paving is a tessellation: regular hexagons cover the ground with no gaps and no overlaps, because three of their angles fill a full revolution at each point. Regular pentagons and circles cannot do this. The park gate slides along a track, which is a translation. A swing gate turning on its hinges is a rotation. A reflection is a flip across a mirror line.

The map at the gate is an enlargement of a plan that is 20 cm by 15 cm. Every length is multiplied by 5, so the map is 100 cm by 75 cm. The angles stay the same.

The climbing frame is a square-based pyramid. It has 5 faces, 1 square and 4 triangles; 5 vertices, 4 at the base and 1 at the apex; and 8 edges, 4 around the base and 4 up to the apex. So it is built from 8 metal poles and 5 joints. A tetrahedron has 4 triangular faces, 4 vertices and 6 edges. A cube has 6 faces, 8 vertices and 12 edges, and a cross of 6 squares is one of its nets.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 1,25 km as 125 m; there are 1 000 m in a kilometre, so it is 1 250 m. Two: giving the area of the sandpit as 20 square metres; 20 m is the perimeter, and the area is 24 square metres. Three: saying the diagonals of a rectangle are lines of symmetry; fold along a diagonal and the corners stick out. Four: saying a square-based pyramid has 4 faces; count the square base too, so it has 5.

The questions for this section are with you now: transformations and 3D objects.

Hold sides, angles and mirrors in mind and try this part's questions: 2D shapes.

## Subtopic: Moves and Solids

Hexagons tessellate. The map is 5 times bigger.

Climbing frame: 5 faces, 5 vertices, 8 edges.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Convert length by multiplying to smaller units and dividing to bigger ones. Perimeter is the distance around, area is length times width in square units, and volume is length times width times height in cubic units. Name polygons by their sides, check regular shapes by their sides and angles, and name angles by comparing with right and straight angles. Translations slide, reflections flip and rotations turn; enlargements multiply every length by the same number. Describe 3D objects by their faces, vertices and edges.
