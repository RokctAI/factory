# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising, visualising and naming 3D objects, especially rectangular prisms, cubes, triangular prisms, tetrahedrons and other pyramids, and describing the similarities and differences between tetrahedrons and other pyramids. It builds on the properties of 2D shapes from earlier this term. The anchor is a Grade 6 class helping at a recycling depot in Bloemfontein. They sort the packaging into bins: cereal boxes, sugar cubes and dice, tea bags shaped like little pyramids with four triangular faces, a chocolate box shaped like a long triangular tube, and a gift box shaped like a pyramid with a square base. The governing idea is that a 3D object is named by the shapes of its faces: a prism has two matching bases joined by rectangles, while a pyramid has one base and triangles that meet at a single point. Naming 3D objects, describing their faces, and comparing tetrahedrons with other pyramids are examinable.

## Subtopic: Prisms and Cubes

A 2D shape is flat; it has length and width only. A 3D object is solid; it has length, width and height, and takes up space. A square is 2D, but a sugar cube is 3D.

The flat surfaces of a 3D object are called faces. The lines where two faces meet are edges. The corners where edges meet are vertices.

A prism has two identical, parallel faces at its ends, called bases, joined by rectangular faces around the sides. If you slice a prism anywhere parallel to its bases, the slice is the same shape as the base. Prisms are named by the shape of their base.

The cereal box is a rectangular prism. All six of its faces are rectangles, and opposite faces are identical. A brick and a shoebox are rectangular prisms too.

A cube is a special rectangular prism. All six of its faces are identical squares. The sugar cubes and the dice at the depot are cubes.

The chocolate box is a triangular prism. Its two bases are triangles, and three rectangles join them. It has five faces altogether.

Not every 3D object is a prism or a pyramid. A ball is a sphere, with one curved surface. A tin of beans is a cylinder, with two flat circles and a curved surface. An ice-cream cone is a cone. These objects have curved surfaces, so they are not prisms or pyramids, which have only flat faces and straight edges. The class puts the tins and balls in a separate bin.

The questions for this section are with you now: naming prisms and cubes.

## Subtopic: Pyramids and Tetrahedrons

A pyramid has one base, which can be any polygon, and triangular faces that rise from each side of the base and meet at a single point at the top, called the apex. Pyramids are named by the shape of their base.

The gift box is a square-based pyramid. Its base is a square, and four triangles meet at the apex. It has five faces: one square and four triangles. The famous pyramids of Egypt are square-based pyramids.

A pentagonal pyramid has a pentagon for its base and five triangles. A hexagonal pyramid has a hexagon for its base and six triangles. The number of triangles is always the same as the number of sides of the base.

A triangular-based pyramid has a triangle for its base and three triangles meeting at the apex. That is four triangles altogether, so every face is a triangle. This pyramid has its own special name: a tetrahedron. Tetra means four, because it has four faces. The pyramid tea bags at the depot are tetrahedrons.

A tetrahedron has no top or bottom. Whichever face it stands on becomes its base, and the opposite vertex becomes its apex.

You can make a pyramid from a flat net. For a square-based pyramid, draw a square and attach a triangle to each of its four sides. Fold the triangles up until their points meet. For a tetrahedron, draw a large triangle, mark the middle of each side and join those points to make four small triangles. Fold up the three outer triangles until they meet.

The questions for this section are with you now: naming pyramids and tetrahedrons.

## Subtopic: Comparing and Visualising 3D Objects

How are a tetrahedron and other pyramids the same? They all have one base, triangular faces that meet at an apex, and only flat faces and straight edges. How are they different? A tetrahedron's base is a triangle, so all four of its faces are triangles. A square-based pyramid has a square base and four triangles, five faces altogether. Other pyramids have more faces as their bases get more sides.

How do prisms and pyramids differ? A prism has two bases and rectangles around the sides; a pyramid has one base and triangles that meet at a point.

Visualising means seeing an object in your mind from different sides. Look at the square-based pyramid from the front and you see a triangle. Look down on it from directly above and you see a square with four lines running from the corners to the centre, where the apex is. Look at a cube from any side and you see a square. Look at the triangular prism from its end and you see a triangle; from the side you see a rectangle.

A good way to sort the objects at the depot is to ask questions in order. Does it have any curved surfaces? Does it have two matching bases or one base and an apex? What shape is the base? The answers lead you straight to the name.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: calling a dice a square; a square is flat, and a dice is a 3D cube with six square faces. Two: calling a tetrahedron a triangle; it is a 3D object with four triangular faces. Three: calling a square-based pyramid a triangular pyramid because its sides are triangles; pyramids are named by their base, so it is a square-based pyramid. Four: saying a cube is not a rectangular prism; a cube is a special rectangular prism with square faces.

The questions for this section are with you now: comparing and visualising 3D objects.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the recycling depot — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Boxes and Cubes

A cereal box is a rectangular prism: six rectangles.

A dice is a cube: six squares.

A prism has two matching ends.

Keep the boxes in mind and try the questions for this part: prisms.

## Subtopic: Points on Top

A pyramid has one base and triangles meeting at the apex.

Square base: square-based pyramid.

Triangle base: tetrahedron, four triangles.

Hold the pointy tops in mind and try this part's questions: pyramids.

## Subtopic: Same and Different

Tetrahedron: all faces are triangles.

Other pyramids: a base that is not a triangle.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A 3D object has faces, edges and vertices. Prisms have two identical bases joined by rectangles; a rectangular prism has six rectangular faces and a cube has six square faces. Pyramids have one base and triangles meeting at an apex, and are named by their base. A tetrahedron is a triangular-based pyramid with four triangular faces.

Hold the same-and-different list in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: comparing 3D objects.
