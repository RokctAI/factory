# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems in financial and measurement contexts using multiplication, and problems involving ratio and rate. The anchor is a school in Rustenburg that buys 145 tablets at R1 250 each, a builder who mixes cement and sand in the ratio 1 to 3, a cool drink made from 1 cup of concentrate to 4 cups of water, and a delivery van that travels 80 km every hour. The governing idea is that equal groups are found by multiplying, a ratio compares two amounts of the same kind and must stay in the same proportion, and a rate compares two different kinds of amounts, such as rand per hour or kilometres per hour. Multiplying in money and measurement problems, using ratios to scale up mixtures and groups, and using rates to find totals are examinable.

## Subtopic: Multiplication in Money and Measurement Problems

When a problem has many equal groups, multiply. The school in Rustenburg buys 145 tablets at R1 250 each. The total cost is 1 250 times 145. Break up 145: 1 250 times 100 is 125 000, 1 250 times 40 is 50 000, and 1 250 times 5 is 6 250. Add: 125 000 plus 50 000 plus 6 250 is 181 250. The tablets cost R181 250.

Measurement works the same way. A farmer buys 36 rolls of fencing wire, and each roll is 125 m long. The total length is 125 times 36. Double and halve: 250 times 18, then 500 times 9, which is 4 500. The farmer has 4 500 m of wire.

Some problems use multiplication backwards. If the farmer needs 4 500 m and each roll is 125 m, how many rolls must he buy? 4 500 divided by 125 is 36 rolls. Check: 36 times 125 is 4 500.

Always write the unit in the answer, and read the question to see what is being asked. A total cost needs rand. A total length needs metres or kilometres. Estimating helps too: R1 250 times about 150 is about R187 500, so R181 250 is sensible.

The questions for this section are with you now: multiplying to find totals of money and measurement.

## Subtopic: Ratio: Comparing Quantities of the Same Kind

A ratio compares two amounts of the same kind. The builder mixes 1 bucket of cement with 3 buckets of sand. We say the ratio of cement to sand is 1 to 3, and we can write it as 1 : 3. The order matters: cement to sand is 1 : 3, but sand to cement is 3 : 1.

To keep a mixture the same, multiply both parts of the ratio by the same number. If the builder uses 15 bags of cement, the sand must be 15 times 3, which is 45 bags. The ratio is still 1 to 3, because for every 1 bag of cement there are 3 bags of sand.

The cool drink is made from 1 cup of concentrate for every 4 cups of water. For a party, the class uses 6 cups of concentrate. They need 6 times 4, which is 24 cups of water. Together that makes 6 plus 24, which is 30 cups of drink. If they used 24 cups of water and only 3 cups of concentrate, the drink would be too weak, because the ratio would have changed.

Ratios also describe groups of people. In a choir the ratio of boys to girls is 2 to 3. That means for every 2 boys there are 3 girls. If there are 120 boys, there are 60 groups of 2 boys, so there are 60 groups of 3 girls, which is 180 girls. A table helps: 2 and 3, 4 and 6, 20 and 30, 120 and 180.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading ratios, keeping the order, and scaling a ratio up.

## Subtopic: Rate: Comparing Different Kinds of Quantities

A rate compares two different kinds of amounts. The delivery van travels 80 kilometres in each hour. Its rate, or speed, is 80 km per hour. Per means for each. In 3 hours it travels 80 times 3, which is 240 km.

Money rates are everywhere. A builder's assistant earns R185 per hour. In an 8-hour day she earns 185 times 8, which is R1 480. If petrol costs R23 per litre, then 45 litres costs 23 times 45, which is R1 035. A cellphone uses 125 MB of data per day, so in 30 days it uses 3 750 MB.

To find a total from a rate, multiply the rate by the number of units. To find the rate from a total, divide. If the van went 240 km in 3 hours, the rate is 240 divided by 3, which is 80 km per hour. If the assistant earned R1 480 in 8 hours, her rate is R1 480 divided by 8, which is R185 per hour.

The difference between ratio and rate is the kind of amounts. A ratio compares the same kind, like cups to cups or people to people. A rate compares different kinds, like kilometres to hours or rand to litres. A rate always has two units joined by per.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding instead of multiplying, so 6 cups of concentrate needs 6 plus 4, which is 10 cups of water; multiply to get 24. Two: swapping the ratio, so 15 bags of cement get 5 bags of sand; cement to sand is 1 to 3, so 45 bags. Three: forgetting the units of a rate, so the speed is 80 instead of 80 km per hour. Four: multiplying when the problem gives the total and asks for the rate; divide instead.

The questions for this section are with you now: rates, per, and telling ratio and rate apart.

## Subtopic: For Every

A ratio says for every. 1 bucket of cement for every 3 of sand.

Multiply both parts by the same number: 15 bags of cement need 45 bags of sand.

Keep the order the same as the words.

Hold the for every in mind and try this part's questions: ratio.

## Subtopic: Per

A rate says per. 80 km per hour.

Total? Multiply. 3 hours at 80 km per hour is 240 km.

Rate? Divide. R1 480 for 8 hours is R185 per hour.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Multiply to find the total of equal groups of money or measurement. A ratio compares the same kind of amount; multiply both parts by the same number to scale it up, and keep the order. A rate compares different kinds of amounts and uses per; multiply to find a total and divide to find the rate.
