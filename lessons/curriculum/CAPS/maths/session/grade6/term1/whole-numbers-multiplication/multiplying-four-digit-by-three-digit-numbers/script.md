# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers multiplication of whole four-digit numbers by three-digit numbers. It builds on multiplying three-digit by two-digit numbers in Grade 5. The anchor is a factory in Pinetown that makes 1 245 pairs of school shoes every working day and works 236 days a year, a printer that packs 3 408 boxes of 125 exercise books, and a farmer in the Free State who plants 304 rows of 2 006 seedlings. The governing idea is that a three-digit multiplier can be broken into hundreds, tens and units, so a big multiplication becomes three smaller ones that are added together. Breaking up the multiplier, long multiplication in columns, handling zeros, and estimating to check are examinable.

## Subtopic: Breaking Up the Three-Digit Number

How many pairs of shoes does the factory make in a year? We need 1 245 times 236. That looks hard, but 236 is 200 plus 30 plus 6. So we can work out 1 245 times 6, 1 245 times 30 and 1 245 times 200, and add the three answers.

1 245 times 6: 1 000 times 6 is 6 000, 200 times 6 is 1 200, 40 times 6 is 240 and 5 times 6 is 30. Together that is 7 470.

1 245 times 30: first find 1 245 times 3, which is 3 735, then multiply by 10 to get 37 350. Multiplying by 30 is the same as multiplying by 3 and then by 10.

1 245 times 200: find 1 245 times 2, which is 2 490, then multiply by 100 to get 249 000.

Now add: 7 470 plus 37 350 plus 249 000 is 293 820. The factory makes 293 820 pairs of shoes in a year.

This works because of the distributive property: multiplying by 236 is the same as multiplying by each part of 236 and adding. It is a good method to understand first, because the column method does exactly the same thing in a shorter layout.

The questions for this section are with you now: breaking up the three-digit number and multiplying by 10 and 100.

## Subtopic: Long Multiplication in Columns

The printer packs 3 408 boxes, each holding 125 exercise books. How many books is that? Write 3 408 on top and 125 underneath, with the units lined up. We make one row for each digit of 125.

Row 1, multiply by the 5 units: 3 408 times 5. 8 times 5 is 40, write 0 carry 4. 0 times 5 is 0, plus 4 is 4. 4 times 5 is 20, write 0 carry 2. 3 times 5 is 15, plus 2 is 17. Row 1 is 17 040.

Row 2, multiply by the 2 tens: write a 0 in the units column first, because we are multiplying by 20, not 2. Then 3 408 times 2 is 6 816, so row 2 is 68 160.

Row 3, multiply by the 1 hundred: write two 0s first, in the units and tens, because we are multiplying by 100. Then 3 408 times 1 is 3 408, so row 3 is 340 800.

Add the three rows: 17 040 plus 68 160 plus 340 800 is 426 000. The printer packs 426 000 exercise books.

The zeros at the start of rows 2 and 3 are called placeholders. They push the digits into the right places, so each row has its true value. Forgetting them is the most common mistake in long multiplication.

The questions for this section are with you now: setting out long multiplication, carrying, and placeholder zeros.

## Subtopic: Zeros in the Numbers

The farmer plants 304 rows with 2 006 seedlings in each row. How many seedlings is that? Multiply 2 006 by 304. The 0 in 304 means there are no tens, so the tens row would be all zeros. You can skip it, as long as the hundreds row still starts with two placeholder zeros.

Units row: 2 006 times 4 is 8 024. Hundreds row: 2 006 times 3 is 6 018, and with two placeholder zeros that is 601 800. Add: 8 024 plus 601 800 is 609 824 seedlings.

Zeros inside the top number also need care. In 2 006 times 4, the zeros in the hundreds and tens give 0 times 4, which is 0. So the answer has zeros in those places too, unless something has been carried into them.

Always estimate before or after you calculate. 1 245 times 236 is about 1 200 times 240, which is 288 000, close to 293 820. 2 006 times 304 is about 2 000 times 300, which is 600 000, close to 609 824. If an answer is far from the estimate, look for a missing placeholder or a forgotten carry.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: leaving out the placeholder zero, so the tens row of 3 408 times 125 is 6 816 instead of 68 160. Two: forgetting to add a carried digit, so 3 408 times 5 becomes 15 040 instead of 17 040. Three: skipping the tens row of 2 006 times 304 and also forgetting the two zeros in the hundreds row. Four: adding the rows wrongly because the digits are not lined up in columns.

The questions for this section are with you now: zeros in the multiplier, zeros in the top number, and estimating to check.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the shoe factory, the exercise books and the seedlings — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Three Smaller Sums

Split 236 into 200, 30 and 6.

Multiply 1 245 by each part: 249 000, 37 350 and 7 470.

Add them up: 293 820 pairs of shoes.

Keep the three parts in mind and try the questions for this part: breaking up.

## Subtopic: One Row per Digit

One row for the units, one for the tens, one for the hundreds.

The tens row starts with one 0. The hundreds row starts with two 0s.

3 408 times 125 is 426 000.

Hold the placeholders in mind and try this part's questions: long multiplication.

## Subtopic: Mind the Zeros

A 0 in the multiplier means you can skip that row.

Keep the placeholder zeros in the next row.

2 006 times 304 is 609 824. Estimate: about 2 000 times 300 is 600 000.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Break the three-digit number into hundreds, tens and units. Multiply the top number by each part, one row at a time. Put one placeholder zero at the start of the tens row and two at the start of the hundreds row. Add the rows. Estimate to check that the answer is sensible.

Hold the zeros in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: zeros and estimates.
