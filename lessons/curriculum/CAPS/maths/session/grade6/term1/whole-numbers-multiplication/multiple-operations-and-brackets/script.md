# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers multiple operations on whole numbers, with or without brackets. The anchor is a family buying tickets for a show on the Durban beachfront, where adult tickets cost R125 and child tickets cost R85, a Grade 6 class sharing the cost of a R1 200 bus trip, and a fruit stall at a market working out its takings. The governing idea is that when a calculation has more than one operation, everyone must use the same order so that everyone gets the same answer: brackets first, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. Using the order of operations, using brackets to change the order, and writing a number sentence with several operations for a problem are examinable.

## Subtopic: Multiplication and Division Before Addition and Subtraction

What is 25 plus 15 times 4? If you work from left to right, you get 40 times 4, which is 160. If you multiply first, you get 25 plus 60, which is 85. Two different answers to the same question cannot both be right, so mathematicians agreed on a rule: multiplication and division are done before addition and subtraction. The correct answer is 85.

The rule makes sense when you think of a story. The fruit stall has R25 in its float and then sells 15 bags of oranges at R4 each. The takings are R25 plus 15 times R4. You must find the cost of the oranges first, 15 times 4 is 60, and then add the float: R85. The multiplication is a group of things that belongs together.

The same goes for division. 100 take away 36 divided by 4 is 100 take away 9, which is 91. Do the division first, then the subtraction.

When there are only multiplication and division, or only addition and subtraction, work from left to right. 48 divided by 6 times 2: first 48 divided by 6 is 8, then 8 times 2 is 16. 200 take away 50 plus 30: first 200 take away 50 is 150, then 150 plus 30 is 180. Working from the right would give 48 divided by 12, which is 4, and 200 take away 80, which is 120, both wrong.

The questions for this section are with you now: multiplying and dividing before adding and subtracting, and working from left to right.

## Subtopic: Brackets First

Brackets change the order. Whatever is inside brackets is worked out first, before anything else. 25 plus 15 times 4 is 85, but 25 plus 15, in brackets, times 4 is 40 times 4, which is 160. The brackets tell you to add first.

Brackets are useful when a story needs adding before multiplying. Mrs Naidoo buys 4 meals, each with a burger for R25 and a drink for R15. The cost of one meal is R25 plus R15, and she buys 4 meals, so the number sentence is (25 plus 15) times 4. Without the brackets, the sentence would mean something else.

The Grade 6 class has R1 200 for a bus trip, and R450 is paid by the school. The rest is shared equally by 5 groups. Each group pays (1 200 take away 450) divided by 5. Brackets first: 1 200 take away 450 is 750. Then 750 divided by 5 is 150. Each group pays R150. Without brackets, 1 200 take away 450 divided by 5 would be 1 200 take away 90, which is 1 110, which does not make sense for the story.

A calculation can have operations inside and outside the brackets. 1 000 take away 4 times (120 plus 35): brackets first give 155. Then multiply: 4 times 155 is 620. Then subtract: 1 000 take away 620 is 380.

The questions for this section are with you now: working out brackets first and seeing how brackets change the answer.

## Subtopic: Number Sentences with Several Operations

Problems with several steps can be written as one number sentence. The family buys 3 adult tickets at R125 and 2 child tickets at R85. The total cost is 3 times 125 plus 2 times 85. Multiply first: 375 plus 170 is 545. The tickets cost R545.

If they pay with R600, the change is 600 take away (3 times 125 plus 2 times 85). The brackets keep the total cost together. Inside the brackets: 545. Then 600 take away 545 is 55. Their change is R55.

To write a number sentence, first decide what each part of the story means. Each group of equal things is a multiplication. Joining is addition, and taking away is subtraction. If a total must be found before it is shared or taken away, put it in brackets. Then work out the sentence in the correct order.

Check by reading your sentence back as a story. 3 times 125 plus 2 times 85 reads three tickets at R125 and two tickets at R85. That matches the problem, so the sentence is right.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: working from left to right, so 25 plus 15 times 4 becomes 160 instead of 85. Two: ignoring brackets, so (1 200 take away 450) divided by 5 becomes 1 110 instead of 150. Three: working right to left with division and multiplication, so 48 divided by 6 times 2 becomes 4 instead of 16. Four: leaving out the brackets in the change sentence, so 600 take away 3 times 125 plus 2 times 85 is worked out as 395, which is not the change.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing and working out number sentences with several operations.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the tickets, the bus trip and the fruit stall — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Times Before Plus

Multiply and divide before you add and subtract.

25 plus 15 times 4 is 25 plus 60, which is 85.

Only times and divide? Go left to right. Only plus and minus? Go left to right.

Keep the order in mind and try the questions for this part: times before plus.

## Subtopic: Brackets Go First

Brackets always go first.

(25 plus 15) times 4 is 40 times 4, which is 160.

(1 200 take away 450) divided by 5 is 150.

Hold the brackets in mind and try this part's questions: brackets.

## Subtopic: Build the Sentence

Groups of equal things: multiply. Joining: add. Taking away: subtract.

3 times 125 plus 2 times 85 is 545.

Put a total in brackets if you need it first: 600 take away 545 is R55 change.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Work out brackets first. Then do multiplication and division, from left to right. Then do addition and subtraction, from left to right. Brackets change the order. Write a story as one number sentence, using brackets to keep a total together, and read it back to check it.

Hold the order in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: number sentences.
