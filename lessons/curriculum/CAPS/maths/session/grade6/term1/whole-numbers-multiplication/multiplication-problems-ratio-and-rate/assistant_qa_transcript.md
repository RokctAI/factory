### subtopic_1 — Multiplication in Money and Measurement Problems
**Assistant:** Why do I multiply both parts of a ratio and not add the same amount to both?

**Tutor:** Because a ratio is about how many of one thing there are for every one or more of the other, and adding the same amount changes that relationship. The cool drink is 1 cup of concentrate for every 4 cups of water. Suppose you added 5 to both parts to make a bigger batch: 6 cups of concentrate and 9 cups of water. Now there is only 1 and a half cups of water for each cup of concentrate, so the drink is much stronger than before. It would taste completely different. If you multiply both parts by 6 instead, you get 6 cups of concentrate and 24 cups of water. There are still 4 cups of water for every cup of concentrate, so it tastes exactly the same, just more of it. Builders know this well. If the cement and sand are not mixed in the right ratio, the concrete can be too weak and crack. So when you scale a ratio, think of making copies of the small mixture. 6 cups of concentrate means 6 copies of the recipe, and each copy has 4 cups of water.

### subtopic_2 — Ratio: Comparing Quantities of the Same Kind
**Assistant:** What does per mean, and how is a rate different from a ratio?

**Tutor:** Per means for each or in each. 80 km per hour means 80 kilometres in each hour. R185 per hour means R185 for each hour worked. R23 per litre means R23 for each litre. A rate always joins two different kinds of quantity: distance and time, money and time, money and volume. A ratio joins two quantities of the same kind: cups and cups, bags and bags, boys and girls. That is the main difference. Because a rate has two different units, you must always write both units in the answer. Saying the van's speed is 80 means nothing, because 80 what? 80 km per hour is complete. In a ratio, the units are the same and they cancel out, so a ratio is often written just as numbers, like 1 : 3. Here is a test. Can you say for every with the same kind of thing on both sides? Then it is a ratio: for every 1 bag of cement, 3 bags of sand. Do you need two different units joined by per? Then it is a rate: 80 km per hour. Both use multiplication to scale up and division to scale down.

### subtopic_3 — Rate: Comparing Different Kinds of Quantities
**Assistant:** How do I know whether to multiply or divide in a rate problem?

**Tutor:** Look at what you are given and what you are asked for. If you know the rate and the number of units, and you want the total, multiply. The van goes 80 km per hour for 3 hours, so the distance is 80 times 3, which is 240 km. The assistant earns R185 per hour for 8 hours, so she earns 185 times 8, which is R1 480. If you know the total and the number of units, and you want the rate, divide. The van went 240 km in 3 hours, so its rate is 240 divided by 3, which is 80 km per hour. And if you know the total and the rate, and you want the number of units, also divide. The van must travel 240 km at 80 km per hour, so it needs 240 divided by 80, which is 3 hours. A quick check is to think about size. The total should be bigger than the rate when there is more than one unit. If you divide and get an answer bigger than the total, or multiply and get a tiny answer, swap the operation.
