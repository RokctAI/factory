### subtopic_1 — Multiplication and Division Before Addition and Subtraction
**Assistant:** Why can't I just work from left to right like I read?

**Tutor:** Because then the same calculation could mean two different things, and different people would get different answers. Look at 25 plus 15 times 4. One person gets 160 and another gets 85. In maths, a calculation must have only one correct answer, otherwise nobody could trust it. So mathematicians all over the world agreed on one order, and now 25 plus 15 times 4 means 85 everywhere. There is also a good reason behind the rule. Multiplication is a short way of writing repeated addition. 15 times 4 means 4 plus 4 plus 4 and so on, fifteen times. It is a bundle of additions that belongs together, so it makes sense to finish the bundle before adding anything else. In a shopping story, 15 times R4 is the price of 15 bags of oranges. You would never add the float to just one bag and then multiply the float as well. Calculators that work scientifically also follow this order. If you type 25 plus 15 times 4 into a scientific calculator, it shows 85. A very basic calculator might show 160, which is why you must know the rule yourself.

### subtopic_2 — Brackets First
**Assistant:** When do I need to put brackets into a number sentence?

**Tutor:** Whenever part of the story must be worked out first, but the normal order would do something else. The normal order already does multiplication before addition, so 3 times 125 plus 2 times 85 needs no brackets: the two ticket costs are found first and then added, which is exactly what the story wants. But if the story adds first and then multiplies, you need brackets. Each meal is a R25 burger plus a R15 drink, and there are 4 meals. The meal price must be added first, so you write (25 plus 15) times 4. Without brackets, 25 plus 15 times 4 would multiply only the drink by 4. The same happens when a total is shared or taken away. The bus trip costs R1 200, the school pays R450 and the rest is shared by 5 groups. You need the rest first, so the subtraction goes in brackets: (1 200 take away 450) divided by 5. A good test is to work out your sentence and see if the answer fits the story. R1 110 per group would be more than the whole bus trip, so you know brackets are missing.

### subtopic_3 — Number Sentences with Several Operations
**Assistant:** What do I do if there are two multiplications and an addition, like 7 times 8 take away 6 times 5?

**Tutor:** Do both multiplications first, then the subtraction. 7 times 8 is 56. 6 times 5 is 30. Now 56 take away 30 is 26. It does not matter which multiplication you do first, because neither one depends on the other. What matters is that both are finished before you subtract. A helpful habit is to underline each multiplication or division and work it out, writing its answer underneath. Then the line below has only addition and subtraction left, and you can do those from left to right. For example, 50 plus 50 divided by 5 times 2. Underline 50 divided by 5 times 2 as one bundle, because division and multiplication sit next to each other. Work it from left to right: 50 divided by 5 is 10, and 10 times 2 is 20. Now the line is 50 plus 20, which is 70. Writing a new line after each step keeps things tidy and makes it easy to check. If brackets appear, work them out first, on their own line, and then carry on with the rest in the same order.
