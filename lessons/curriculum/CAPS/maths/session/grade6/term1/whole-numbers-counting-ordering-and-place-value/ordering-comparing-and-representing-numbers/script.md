# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers comparing, ordering and representing whole numbers up to at least nine digits. It builds on the six-digit numbers of Grade 5 and on the place value of nine-digit numbers. The anchor is three towns in the Eastern Cape whose budgets for the year are R245 380 000, R245 308 000 and R254 038 000, a cellphone company that sends 108 750 000 text messages in one week, and a number line from 245 000 000 to 246 000 000. The governing idea is that you compare numbers place by place from the left, starting with the biggest place, and that a number line shows the size of a number by where it sits. Using the signs for bigger and smaller, ordering lists from smallest to biggest and from biggest to smallest, counting on and back in big steps, and placing numbers on a number line are examinable.

## Subtopic: Comparing Nine-Digit Numbers

To compare two whole numbers, first count the digits. A number with more digits is always bigger. 108 750 000 has nine digits and 99 999 999 has eight digits, so 108 750 000 is bigger, even though 99 999 999 is full of nines.

If the numbers have the same number of digits, compare them place by place from the left. Look at the town budgets R245 380 000 and R245 308 000. The hundred millions are both 2. The ten millions are both 4. The millions are both 5. The hundred thousands are both 3. The ten thousands are 8 and 0. Eight is bigger than zero, so R245 380 000 is the bigger budget. We stop at the first place where the digits are different. The digits after that do not matter.

We use signs to write comparisons. The sign > means is greater than, and the sign < means is less than. The wide, open side always faces the bigger number. So 245 380 000 > 245 308 000, and 245 308 000 < 245 380 000. Both sentences say the same thing from opposite ends. When two numbers are exactly the same, we use the equals sign.

Now compare R245 380 000 with R254 038 000. The hundred millions are both 2. The ten millions are 4 and 5. Five is bigger, so 254 038 000 > 245 380 000. Be careful: the two numbers use some of the same digits, 2, 4 and 5, but the order of the digits changes their value completely.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting digits, comparing place by place, and using the signs > and <.

## Subtopic: Ordering Big Numbers

Ordering means putting a list of numbers in a row by size. Ascending order goes from the smallest to the biggest, like climbing stairs. Descending order goes from the biggest to the smallest, like coming down.

Order the three budgets in ascending order: R245 380 000, R245 308 000 and R254 038 000. First compare the biggest place. All three have 2 hundred millions. In the ten millions, two have 4 and one has 5, so R254 038 000 is the biggest and goes last. Now compare the two that start with 245. In the ten thousands place one has 8 and one has 0, so R245 308 000 is smaller. The ascending order is R245 308 000, R245 380 000, R254 038 000.

A neat method is to write the numbers under each other with the digits in their correct columns. Then you can run your finger down each column from the left. For numbers with different lengths, line them up on the right, so that the units are under the units. A shorter number has an empty space at the front, which shows that it is smaller.

Descending order is the same list turned around: R254 038 000, R245 380 000, R245 308 000. Always check the question to see which order is asked for. A good check is to read your list and ask, is each number bigger than the one before it?

The questions for this section are with you now: ascending and descending order, and lining up digits in columns.

## Subtopic: Representing Numbers on a Number Line

A number line shows numbers as points in order. On a number line from 245 000 000 to 246 000 000, the marks go up in steps of 100 000: 245 100 000, 245 200 000, 245 300 000 and so on, up to 246 000 000. There are ten equal jumps between the two ends.

Where does R245 380 000 go? It is more than 245 300 000 and less than 245 400 000, so it sits between those two marks. Because 380 000 is closer to 400 000 than to 300 000, it sits closer to the 245 400 000 mark. R245 308 000 sits just after the 245 300 000 mark, because it is only 8 000 more.

Counting on and back also uses big steps. Count on in 100 000s from 245 800 000: 245 900 000, 246 000 000, 246 100 000. Watch what happens when the hundred thousands go past 9: they become 0 and the millions go up by one. Count back in 1 000 000s from 108 750 000: 107 750 000, 106 750 000, 105 750 000. Only the millions digit changes.

You can also represent a number in other ways. 108 750 000 text messages can be described as 108 million, 750 thousand, or as 108 750 thousands. All of these name the same number.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying 99 999 999 is bigger than 108 750 000 because it has more nines; the number with more digits is bigger. Two: comparing from the right and deciding by the last digits; always compare from the left. Three: writing 245 380 000 < 245 308 000 because the sign looks right; the open side faces the bigger number. Four: counting on 100 000 from 245 980 000 and writing 245 1 080 000; the hundred thousands pass 9, so the answer is 246 080 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: placing numbers on a number line, and counting on and back in big steps.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the town budgets, the text messages and the number line — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Count the Digits First

More digits means a bigger number.

Same number of digits? Start on the left and find the first place where the digits are different.

245 380 000 and 245 308 000 differ at the ten thousands: 8 beats 0.

Keep the left-hand side in mind and try the questions for this part: comparing.

## Subtopic: Smallest to Biggest

Ascending: smallest first. Descending: biggest first.

Line the numbers up in columns, units under units.

R245 308 000, R245 380 000, R254 038 000.

Hold the columns in mind and try this part's questions: ordering.

## Subtopic: Find Its Spot

On the number line, find the two marks a number sits between.

245 380 000 sits between 245 300 000 and 245 400 000, closer to 245 400 000.

When you count on past 9, carry into the next place: 245 980 000 plus 100 000 is 246 080 000.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Count the digits first: more digits means bigger. With the same number of digits, compare from the left and stop at the first difference. The open side of > and < faces the bigger number. Ascending goes up, descending goes down. On a number line, find the two marks a number sits between.

Hold the number line in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: placing and counting.
