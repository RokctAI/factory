# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers rounding off whole numbers to the nearest 5, 10, 100 and 1 000, using numbers of up to seven digits and more. It builds on rounding in Grade 5. The anchor is a crowd of 54 687 people at a rugby match in Durban, a taxi that has driven 1 248 350 km, and prices at a spaza shop in Soweto. The governing idea is that rounding replaces a number with the nearest friendly number, and that you decide by looking at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. Rounding to each of the four places, rounding numbers that carry into the next place, and using rounding to estimate are examinable.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 10 and 5

To round to the nearest 10, look at the units digit. If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, round down: keep the tens and make the units 0. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, round up: add one ten and make the units 0. The crowd of 54 687 has 7 units, so it rounds up to 54 690. The halfway case, 5, always rounds up by agreement, so 54 685 rounds to 54 690 as well.

Sometimes rounding up makes the tens carry. 39 995 to the nearest 10 has 5 units, so it rounds up. 99 tens plus one more ten is 100 tens, so 39 995 becomes 40 000. Write the answer carefully and check that it is close to the starting number.

Rounding to the nearest 5 means finding the closest number that ends in 0 or 5. Look only at the units digit. Units of 1 or 2 round down to 0. Units of 3, 4, 6 or 7 go to 5. Units of 8 or 9 round up to the next ten. So 2 341 and 2 342 round to 2 340, while 2 343 and 2 347 round to 2 345, and 2 348 rounds to 2 350.

Rounding to the nearest 5 is useful with money and with coins. A loaf of bread costs R18 at the spaza shop. To the nearest R5 that is R20, because 8 is closer to 10 than to 5. A pack of chips costs R12, which is R10 to the nearest R5, and a cool drink costs R13, which is R15.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to the nearest 10, rounding to the nearest 5, and rounding that carries.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 100 and 1 000

To round to the nearest 100, look at the tens digit. 54 687 has 8 tens, which is 5 or more, so round up: 54 687 becomes 54 700. To round to the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit. 54 687 has 6 hundreds, so round up again: 54 687 becomes 55 000. Every digit after the place you are rounding to becomes 0.

The taxi has driven 1 248 350 km. To the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit, which is 3. Three is less than 5, so round down: 1 248 000 km. To the nearest 100, look at the tens digit, which is 5, so round up: 1 248 400 km. To the nearest 10, the units digit is 0, so the number stays 1 248 350.

Always look at only one digit: the one just to the right of the rounding place. Do not round in steps. 1 449 to the nearest 1 000 is 1 000, because the hundreds digit is 4. Some people round 1 449 to 1 450, then to 1 500, then to 2 000, but that is wrong, because 1 449 is much closer to 1 000 than to 2 000.

A number line helps you see it. 54 687 sits between 54 000 and 55 000. The halfway mark is 54 500. 54 687 is past halfway, so it is nearer 55 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to the nearest 100 and 1 000, and looking at only one digit.

## Subtopic: Using Rounding to Estimate

Rounding helps you estimate, which means finding a quick, close answer. On Saturday 54 687 people came to the rugby, and on Sunday 38 214 came to a soccer match at the same stadium. About how many came altogether? Round each to the nearest 1 000: 55 000 plus 38 000 is 93 000. The exact answer is 92 901, which is close to 93 000. The estimate tells us the exact answer is sensible.

Estimating catches mistakes. If someone worked out 54 687 plus 38 214 and got 929 010, the estimate of 93 000 shows at once that the answer is ten times too big.

Choose the rounding place to suit the question. For a shopping list at the spaza shop, round to the nearest R5 or R10. For a crowd, round to the nearest 100 or 1 000. For a distance of 1 248 350 km, the nearest 1 000 is plenty. A newspaper might say about 55 000 fans, but the ticket office needs the exact number.

When rounding, remember that the answer is never exact. Use words like about or approximately, and the sign that looks like a wavy equals sign, which means is approximately equal to.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: rounding 54 687 to the nearest 100 as 54 600 because the hundreds digit is 6; look at the tens digit, 8, and round up to 54 700. Two: rounding in steps, so 1 449 becomes 2 000; look at only the hundreds digit and get 1 000. Three: leaving digits after the rounding place, so 54 687 becomes 55 687; every digit after the rounding place becomes 0. Four: rounding 2 343 to the nearest 5 as 2 340; 3 is closer to 5 than to 0, so it is 2 345.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating with rounded numbers, and choosing a sensible rounding place.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the rugby crowd, the taxi and the spaza shop — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Look Next Door

Look at the digit next door, on the right.

5 or more: round up. 4 or less: round down.

54 687 to the nearest 10 is 54 690. To the nearest 5 it is 54 685.

Keep the next-door digit in mind and try the questions for this part: rounding to 10 and 5.

## Subtopic: Bigger Steps

To the nearest 100, look at the tens. To the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds.

54 687 is 54 700 to the nearest 100 and 55 000 to the nearest 1 000.

Look at one digit only. Do not round in steps.

Hold the one digit in mind and try this part's questions: rounding to 100 and 1 000.

## Subtopic: Round, Then Work

Round first, then add for a quick answer.

55 000 plus 38 000 is about 93 000. The exact answer is 92 901.

If an answer is far from the estimate, check it again.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Find the place you are rounding to and look at the digit just to its right. 5 or more rounds up; 4 or less rounds down. Everything after the rounding place becomes 0. To the nearest 5, units of 3 to 7 go to 5. Round to estimate, and use the estimate to check exact answers.

Hold the estimate in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: estimating with rounding.
