### subtopic_1 — Rounding to the Nearest 10 and 5
**Assistant:** Why does 5 round up when it is exactly in the middle?

**Tutor:** It is a rule that everyone agrees to use so that we all get the same answer. A number like 54 685, rounded to the nearest 10, is exactly halfway between 54 680 and 54 690. Neither is closer, so we need a tie-breaker, and the tie-breaker in school mathematics is to round up. It is like a soccer match that ends in a draw: the rules say what happens next, and everyone follows the same rule. There is also a sensible reason. When the units digit is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, that is five digits that round up, and when it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, that is five digits that round down. So the digits are shared out fairly, five each way. Be careful with numbers that look like halfway but are not. 54 687 to the nearest 100 is not a halfway case, because the tens digit is 8 and 87 is past the halfway point of 50. Only when the digits after the rounding place are exactly 5, 50, 500 and so on is it truly halfway. And remember: halfway always goes up.

### subtopic_2 — Rounding to the Nearest 100 and 1 000
**Assistant:** Why is rounding in steps wrong? It feels like it should work.

**Tutor:** Because every step can push the number a little further from where it started, and the errors add up. Take 1 449 and round it to the nearest 1 000. The honest question is: is 1 449 closer to 1 000 or to 2 000? It is 449 away from 1 000 and 551 away from 2 000, so 1 000 is closer. The correct answer is 1 000, and you get it straight away by looking at the hundreds digit, 4. Now see what happens in steps. 1 449 to the nearest 10 is 1 450, because of the 9. 1 450 to the nearest 100 is 1 500, because of the 5. 1 500 to the nearest 1 000 is 2 000, because of the 5. Each step rounded up a tiny amount, and by the end the number had jumped to the wrong thousand. It is like a game of broken telephone, where a message changes a little each time it is passed on. To avoid it, always go back to the original number and look at only the one digit just to the right of the place you want. The other digits do not get a vote.

### subtopic_3 — Using Rounding to Estimate
**Assistant:** How do I choose what to round to when I estimate?

**Tutor:** Choose the place that makes the sum easy to do in your head but still keeps the answer close. For two crowds of 54 687 and 38 214, rounding to the nearest 1 000 gives 55 000 plus 38 000, which you can add in your head: 93 000. Rounding to the nearest 10 gives 54 690 plus 38 210, which is still hard to add mentally, so it does not save much work. Rounding to the nearest 10 000 gives 50 000 plus 40 000, which is 90 000, and that is a bit rough. A good habit is to round each number to its biggest place or the place just after it, so that each rounded number has only one or two non-zero digits. Also think about why you are estimating. If you are checking a calculator answer, a rough estimate is enough to spot a wrong number of digits. If you are working out whether R200 is enough for your shopping at the spaza shop, round each price up to the nearest R5 or R10, so that your estimate is a little too big and you will not run short at the till.
