# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the place value of digits in whole numbers up to at least nine digits, which is up to the hundred millions. It builds on the six-digit numbers of Grade 5. The anchor is the 2022 census, which counted 62 027 503 people in South Africa, the distance from the Earth to the Sun, which is about 149 600 000 km, and a lotto jackpot of R125 000 000. The governing idea is that every digit sits in a place, and the place decides what the digit is worth, and that big numbers are read in groups of three. Naming the place and value of a digit, writing numbers in expanded notation, and changing between digits and words are examinable.

## Subtopic: Place Value to Hundred Millions

In Grade 5 you met six places: units, tens and hundreds, then thousands, ten thousands and hundred thousands. In Grade 6 we add three more: millions, ten millions and hundred millions. That gives nine places. Each place is worth ten times the place on its right. Ten hundred thousands make one million, and ten millions make one ten million.

The places come in groups of three. Reading from the right, the first group is units, tens and hundreds. The next group is the thousands group: units of thousands, ten thousands and hundred thousands. The next is the millions group: millions, ten millions and hundred millions. We leave a small space between groups, so 62 027 503 is easy to read.

Take 62 027 503. The 6 is in the ten millions place, so it is worth 60 000 000. The first 2 is in the millions place, worth 2 000 000. The 0 is in the hundred thousands place, worth nothing. The next 2 is in the ten thousands place, worth 20 000. The 7 is in the thousands place, worth 7 000. The 5 is worth 500, the 0 is worth nothing and the 3 is worth 3.

Notice the difference between the digit and its value. The digit is just the symbol, like 6. Its value depends on its seat. In the jackpot R125 000 000, the 1 is worth R100 000 000, the 2 is worth R20 000 000 and the 5 is worth R5 000 000. Move a digit one place to the left and it is worth ten times as much.

The questions for this section are with you now: naming the place of a digit, and saying what a digit is worth.

## Subtopic: Expanded Notation

Expanded notation breaks a number into the values of its digits and adds them. The distance to the Sun, 149 600 000 km, has 1 hundred million, 4 ten millions, 9 millions and 6 hundred thousands. In expanded notation that is 100 000 000 plus 40 000 000 plus 9 000 000 plus 600 000. The zeros at the end are worth nothing, so we leave them out of the sum.

The census number expands like this: 62 027 503 is 60 000 000 plus 2 000 000 plus 20 000 plus 7 000 plus 500 plus 3. Check by adding: 60 000 000 and 2 000 000 make 62 000 000, then 20 000 and 7 000 make 27 000, and 500 and 3 make 503. Put the groups together: 62 027 503.

You can also go the other way. Write 300 000 000 plus 5 000 000 plus 40 000 plus 9 as one number. Fill the seats: 3 hundred millions, 0 ten millions, 5 millions, 0 hundred thousands, 4 ten thousands, 0 thousands, 0 hundreds, 0 tens and 9 units. The number is 305 040 009. The zeros are placeholders. Without them the 3 would slide into the wrong seat.

A place value table helps. Draw nine columns, label them from hundred millions on the left to units on the right, and write one digit in each column. Every empty column gets a 0, except at the very front of the number.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing numbers in expanded notation, and building a number from its parts.

## Subtopic: Reading and Writing Nine-Digit Numbers

To read a big number, split it into groups of three from the right, then read each group from the left, saying million or thousand after the group. 62 027 503 has the groups 62, 027 and 503. Read 62 as sixty-two million, 027 as twenty-seven thousand, and 503 as five hundred and three. Altogether: sixty-two million, twenty-seven thousand, five hundred and three.

149 600 000 has the groups 149, 600 and 000. It is one hundred and forty-nine million, six hundred thousand. A group of three zeros is not read at all.

To write words as digits, write one group of three for each part. Eight million, six thousand and twelve has a millions group of 8, a thousands group of 006 and a units group of 012. So the number is 8 006 012. Every group after the first must have exactly three digits, so fill with zeros.

Use a check: count the digits. A number in the millions has seven, eight or nine digits. 8 006 012 has seven digits, which is right for eight million.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying the 6 in 62 027 503 is worth 6; it sits in the ten millions place, so it is worth 60 000 000. Two: writing 300 000 000 plus 5 000 000 plus 40 000 plus 9 as 354 009; the zeros hold the seats, so it is 305 040 009. Three: writing eight million, six thousand and twelve as 8 612; each group needs three digits, so it is 8 006 012. Four: grouping from the left, so 62 027 503 is read as 620 275 03; always group in threes from the right.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading numbers aloud, and writing words as digits.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the census, the Sun and the jackpot — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Three Seats in Every Group

Big numbers sit in groups of three: units, thousands, millions.

In 62 027 503 the 6 sits in the ten millions seat. It is worth 60 000 000.

In R125 000 000 the 2 is worth R20 000 000.

Keep the seats in mind and try the questions for this part: place and value.

## Subtopic: Break It Up

Break the number into the value of each digit. 149 600 000 is 100 000 000 plus 40 000 000 plus 9 000 000 plus 600 000.

Going back, fill every empty seat with a 0. 300 000 000 plus 5 000 000 plus 40 000 plus 9 is 305 040 009.

Hold the seats and the zeros in mind and try this part's questions: expanded notation.

## Subtopic: Say It in Groups

Group in threes from the right. Read each group and say million or thousand.

62 027 503 is sixty-two million, twenty-seven thousand, five hundred and three.

Eight million, six thousand and twelve is 8 006 012.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Nine places come in three groups of three: units, thousands and millions. Each place is worth ten times the place on its right. A digit's value depends on its seat. Expanded notation adds the values of the digits. Read and write big numbers in groups of three from the right, and use zeros to hold empty seats.

Hold the groups in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: reading and writing big numbers.
