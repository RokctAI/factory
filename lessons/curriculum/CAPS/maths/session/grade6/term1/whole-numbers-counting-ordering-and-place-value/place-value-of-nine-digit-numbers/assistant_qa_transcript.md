### subtopic_1 — Place Value to Hundred Millions
**Assistant:** Why does the same digit have a different value in different places?

**Tutor:** Because our number system is built on tens, and the place tells you how many lots of ten have been grouped together. A 2 on its own in the units place means two ones. Move it one place left, into the tens, and it means two tens, which is 20. Move it again and it is two hundreds. Every step to the left multiplies the value by ten. So in 62 027 503, the 2 in the ten thousands place is worth 20 000, but the 2 in the millions place is worth 2 000 000, which is a hundred times as much, because it is two places further left. Think of a tall building in Johannesburg where every floor up holds ten times as much as the floor below. The same 2 boxes are worth far more on a higher floor. That is why we always ask two questions about a digit: which digit is it, and where does it sit? The digit tells you how many; the place tells you how many of what. When you say the value, you need both: 2 lots of a million is 2 000 000.

### subtopic_2 — Expanded Notation
**Assistant:** Why do I need to write the zeros if they are worth nothing?

**Tutor:** The zeros are worth nothing themselves, but they push the other digits into the right seats. Look at 305 040 009. If you drop the zeros you get 3549, which is three thousand five hundred and forty-nine, a completely different number. The 3 that should mean three hundred million now means only three thousand. A zero is like an empty chair at a wedding table that still keeps everyone else in their right places. Without the chair, the guests shuffle along and sit at the wrong place cards. In expanded notation you may leave out the parts worth zero, because adding nothing changes nothing: 300 000 000 plus 5 000 000 plus 40 000 plus 9 is correct. But when you write the number as one string of digits, every place from the first digit to the units must be filled. A good habit is to count the digits at the end. A number in the hundred millions must have nine digits. 305 040 009 has nine, so it is complete.

### subtopic_3 — Reading and Writing Nine-Digit Numbers
**Assistant:** Where do I put the word and when I read a big number?

**Tutor:** In South African English we usually say and inside a group of three, between the hundreds and the rest of that group, and sometimes before the last small part of the number. So 62 027 503 is sixty-two million, twenty-seven thousand, five hundred and three. The and sits between five hundred and three. In 149 600 000 we say one hundred and forty-nine million, six hundred thousand, with an and inside the 149. The words million and thousand tell you which group you have just read, so you never need to name the other places one by one. If a whole group is zeros, you say nothing for it: 8 000 012 is eight million and twelve. Do not worry too much about the and; markers mostly check that the groups are right. The real test is whether someone could write your words back into exactly the same digits. Try it with a friend: one of you reads a number aloud and the other writes it, and then you compare.
