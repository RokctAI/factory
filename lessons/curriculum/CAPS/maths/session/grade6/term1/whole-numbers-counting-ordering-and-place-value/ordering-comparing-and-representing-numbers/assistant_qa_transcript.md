### subtopic_1 — Comparing Nine-Digit Numbers
**Assistant:** Why do I compare from the left and not from the right?

**Tutor:** Because the digits on the left are worth the most. In a nine-digit number the first digit is in the hundred millions place, and the last digit is only in the units place. Imagine two people with R245 380 000 and R245 308 000. If you looked at the units first, you would see 0 and 0 and learn nothing. Even if the units were different, a difference of a few rand cannot beat a difference in the ten thousands. Here the ten thousands are 8 and 0, a difference of 80 000 rand, which is far more than anything the places to the right can make up. That is why the first place where the digits differ decides the answer, and you can stop looking there. Think of a soccer log: teams are first ranked by points, and only if the points are equal do we look at goal difference. The left-hand digits are the points; the right-hand digits are like goal difference, only used when everything before them is level. Comparing from the right is like ranking teams by goal difference before looking at points, and it gives the wrong winner.

### subtopic_2 — Ordering Big Numbers
**Assistant:** How do I stop mixing up the greater than and less than signs?

**Tutor:** Look at the shape of the sign. It is wide open on one side and closed to a point on the other. The wide side always faces the bigger number, and the point always faces the smaller number. Some learners imagine a hungry crocodile whose open mouth always wants to eat the bigger number. So in 254 038 000 > 245 380 000 the mouth opens towards 254 038 000. Another trick is to read the sign from left to right. If the first thing you meet is the wide side, read is greater than. If the first thing you meet is the point, read is less than. So 245 308 000 < 245 380 000 reads 245 308 000 is less than 245 380 000. Once you have written your sentence, check it by reading it aloud and asking whether it is true. If you wrote 245 380 000 < 245 308 000, you would be saying that the bigger budget is less, which you know is false, so you would flip the sign. Remember that both sentences, one with > and one with <, can describe the same pair of numbers. You only need to make the mouth face the right way.

### subtopic_3 — Representing Numbers on a Number Line
**Assistant:** How do I know where to put a number on a number line if the marks do not show it?

**Tutor:** First read the marks to find the size of each jump. If the line runs from 245 000 000 to 246 000 000 and has ten equal jumps, each jump is 1 000 000 divided by 10, which is 100 000. So the marks are 245 100 000, 245 200 000, 245 300 000 and so on. Next, find the two marks your number sits between. For 245 380 000, the hundred thousands digit is 3, so it sits after 245 300 000, and it has not yet reached 245 400 000. Then decide whether it is nearer the left mark or the right mark. The part after 245 300 000 is 80 000, which is more than half of a jump of 100 000, so the point goes past the halfway point, nearer to 245 400 000. You do not need to be exact. The number line is a picture of size, and what matters is that the point is in the right gap and on the right side of the middle. This is also the first step of rounding: 245 380 000 rounds to 245 400 000 to the nearest 100 000, because it is nearer that mark.
