### subtopic_1 — What Is a Prime Number
**Assistant:** Why does a prime need exactly two factors? Why not just one?

**Tutor:** Because the definition is built on the idea of a number that cannot be broken into smaller factors, and every number bigger than 1 already has two factors for free: 1 and itself. 13 is 1 times 13, and 12 is 1 times 12. Those two factors tell us nothing special, because every number has them. What makes 12 different is that it has extra factors in between, like 3 and 4. A prime is a number that has the two free factors and nothing else. So exactly two is the smallest number of factors a whole number bigger than 1 can have. Now think about 1. It is 1 times 1, so its only factor is 1. It does not even have two different factors. If we called 1 prime, lots of rules in mathematics would break. For example, we say every whole number can be broken into primes in exactly one way: 12 is 2 times 2 times 3. If 1 were prime, you could also write 1 times 2 times 2 times 3, or 1 times 1 times 2 times 2 times 3, and there would be endless ways. Mathematicians agreed long ago that 1 is not prime, to keep things neat.

### subtopic_2 — Finding Primes to 100
**Assistant:** Why can I stop crossing out after 7 on the hundred square?

**Tutor:** Because any composite number up to 100 must have a small factor, 7 or less. Think about how factors come in pairs. If a number is composite, it is a small factor times a big factor. If both factors were 11 or more, the number would be at least 11 times 11, which is 121. That is more than 100. So every composite number up to 100 has at least one factor of 10 or less, and the only primes that small are 2, 3, 5 and 7. Any composite number with a factor like 4, 6, 8, 9 or 10 has already been crossed out by 2 or 3, because those numbers are themselves made of 2s and 3s. So by the time you have crossed out the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7, every composite number has gone, and whatever is left must be prime. Try it with 77. It is 7 times 11, and it was crossed out when you did the multiples of 7. Or 91, which is 7 times 13, also crossed out by 7. If you used the sieve up to 200, you would have to go further, up to 13, because 13 times 13 is 169, which is less than 200.

### subtopic_3 — Prime and Composite Numbers
**Assistant:** What is the quickest way to tell if a number like 87 is prime?

**Tutor:** Use quick divisibility tests with 2, 3, 5 and 7, in that order. Test for 2: is the number even? 87 is odd, so 2 does not divide it. Test for 3: add the digits. 8 plus 7 is 15, and 15 is in the 3 times table, so 3 divides 87 exactly. 87 divided by 3 is 29. So 87 is 3 times 29, and it is composite. You can stop as soon as one test works. Test for 5: does it end in 0 or 5? Test for 7: there is no easy digit rule, so just think of the 7 times table near the number. For 91, 7 times 13 is 91, so it is composite. For 89, it is odd, 8 plus 9 is 17, which is not in the 3 times table, it does not end in 0 or 5, and 7 times 12 is 84 and 7 times 13 is 91, so 7 does not divide it. None of the four tests work, so 89 is prime. With practice you will remember the 25 primes to 100, and you will only need the tests for checking.
