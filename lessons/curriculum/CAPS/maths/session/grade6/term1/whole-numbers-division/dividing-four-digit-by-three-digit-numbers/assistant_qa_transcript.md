### subtopic_1 — Estimating and Building a Multiples Table
**Assistant:** Why should I write out a table of multiples before I start?

**Tutor:** Because dividing by a three-digit number is hard to do in your head, and the table turns every step into an easy look-up. In 8 432 divided by 124, the first step asks how many times 124 fits into 843. Without a table you would have to guess: is it 6, 7 or 8? You might try 124 times 7, which is 868, find it is too big, and then try 6. With the table, you just run your finger down the list, 124, 248, 372, 496, 620, 744, 868, and stop at the last number that is not more than 843. That is 744, which is 124 times 6. The table takes a minute to write, but it saves time and mistakes in every step after that. You can build it quickly by adding the divisor each time, and you can check it, because the tenth multiple should be the divisor with a zero on the end. 124 times 9 is 1 116, and adding 124 more gives 1 240, which is 124 times 10, so the table is right. In a test, write the table down the side of your page.

### subtopic_2 — Long Division Step by Step
**Assistant:** How do I know where to write the first digit of the answer?

**Tutor:** Write it above the last digit of the part you first divided into. In 8 432 divided by 124, you look at 8, then 84, then 843. 843 is the first part that 124 fits into, and the last digit of 843 is the 3, which is in the tens place of 8 432. So the 6 goes above the 3, and it means 6 tens, or 60. That is why the answer is 68 and not 680. Put another way, 124 cannot fit into 8 thousands or 84 hundreds even once, so the answer has no thousands and no hundreds. It starts with tens. A good habit is to put a small dot or a 0 above the 8 and the 4 to show that 124 did not fit there, then write the 6 above the 3. Now every digit of 8 432 has something above it, so the places line up perfectly. Your estimate is also a strong check. You estimated about 70, which is a two-digit number. If you write an answer with three or four digits, you know the first digit is in the wrong place.

### subtopic_3 — Zeros and Remainders
**Assistant:** What do I do with the remainder in a real problem?

**Tutor:** It depends on the story, so always read the question again. In the print shop, 5 000 pages are shared among 135 learners, and the answer is 37 remainder 5. Each learner gets 37 pages, and the 5 extra pages might go back on the shelf. The remainder is simply left over. But think about a different question: a hall has 5 000 chairs to move and each trolley carries 135 chairs. How many trips are needed? 37 trips move 4 995 chairs, but 5 chairs are still waiting. So you need one more trip: 38 trips. Here the remainder makes you round up. Another story: tickets cost R135 and you have R5 000. How many tickets can you buy? You can buy 37 tickets and have R5 change. You cannot buy a 38th ticket, so you ignore the remainder and keep 37. So there are three ways to use a remainder: leave it over, round up to fit everything, or ignore it because a part is not enough. The numbers are the same, but the story decides the answer.
