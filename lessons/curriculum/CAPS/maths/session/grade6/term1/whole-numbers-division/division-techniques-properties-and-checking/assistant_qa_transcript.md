### subtopic_1 — Breaking Down the Dividend
**Assistant:** How do I choose good parts when I break down a number for division?

**Tutor:** Look for the biggest part that is a multiple of the divisor and close to the number. For 3 648 divided by 12, ask: what multiple of 12 is close to 3 648? You know 12 times 3 is 36, so 12 times 300 is 3 600. That is very close, and it leaves only 48, which is 12 times 4. So 3 648 splits nicely into 3 600 and 48. If you had split by place value, into 3 000, 600, 40 and 8, some of those parts would not divide by 12 exactly. 3 000 divided by 12 is 250, which is fine, but 40 divided by 12 does not work out, and you would get stuck. So think of the times table of the divisor, then multiply by 10, 100 or 1 000 to get close to the number. For 9 636 divided by 12, 12 times 8 is 96, so 12 times 800 is 9 600, which leaves 36. Sometimes you need three parts. 7 245 divided by 15 could be 6 000, 1 200 and 45, which are 15 times 400, 15 times 80 and 15 times 3. The parts must add up to the original number, so check that first.

### subtopic_2 — Building Up with Chunks
**Assistant:** Is chunking really the same as long division?

**Tutor:** Yes, it is the same idea set out differently. In both methods you are asking how many groups of the divisor fit into the number, and you take them away in steps. Long division takes away chunks place by place: first hundreds of the divisor, then tens, then units, and it writes each answer digit in its place. Chunking lets you choose any chunk size you like, such as 400 groups or 80 groups or even 50 groups, and you add the chunks at the end. For 7 245 divided by 15, long division would first find that 15 fits into 72 four times, which is really 400 times into 7 200. Chunking took away 15 times 400 straight away. Then long division finds 8 tens, and chunking took away 15 times 80. So the steps match. Chunking is more flexible and helps you see what is happening, but it can take more lines if you choose small chunks. Long division is shorter once you know it. It is good to know both, because one can check the other. If both give 483, you can be very confident.

### subtopic_3 — Checking: Inverse, Estimation and Dividing by 1
**Assistant:** Why can't I divide by zero?

**Tutor:** Because division asks a question that has no answer when the divisor is zero. 12 divided by 3 asks: how many groups of 3 make 12? The answer is 4. Or it asks: if 12 is shared among 3, how many does each get? Again 4. Now try 12 divided by 0. How many groups of 0 make 12? Even a million groups of 0 make only 0, so no number of groups ever reaches 12. Or share 12 cans among 0 bags: there are no bags to put them in, so the question makes no sense. You can also use the inverse check. If 12 divided by 0 were some number, then that number times 0 would have to be 12. But any number times 0 is 0, never 12. So there is no answer, and we say that division by zero is undefined. Zero divided by a number is fine, though: 0 divided by 12 is 0, because if there are no cans, each of 12 bags gets 0. Many calculators show an error message when you try to divide by 0, which is a good reminder.
