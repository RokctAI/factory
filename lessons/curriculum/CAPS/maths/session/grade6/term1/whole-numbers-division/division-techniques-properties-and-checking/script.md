# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers techniques for dividing whole numbers and checking the answers, the distributive property in division, and 1 in terms of its multiplicative property. The curriculum asks you to use at least two techniques: one to calculate and one to check. The anchor is a recycling club in eMalahleni that collected 3 648 cans and packs them into bags of 12, a soccer academy that shares 7 245 litres of water among 15 tanks, and 2 880 bibs and balls shared among 24 teams. The governing idea is that a number can be broken into friendly parts that are each easy to divide, that division can be done by taking away big chunks, and that every division can be checked by multiplying back. Breaking down, building up with chunks, halving, the distributive property, dividing by 1, estimating, inverse operations and using a calculator only to check are examinable.

## Subtopic: Breaking Down the Dividend

The recycling club collected 3 648 cans and packs them into bags of 12. How many bags? Break 3 648 into parts that 12 divides easily: 3 600 and 48. 3 600 divided by 12 is 300, because 36 divided by 12 is 3. 48 divided by 12 is 4. Add the answers: 300 plus 4 is 304 bags.

This works because of the distributive property. Dividing a sum is the same as dividing each part and adding the answers. 3 648 divided by 12 is 3 600 divided by 12 plus 48 divided by 12. The trick is to choose parts that are multiples of the divisor. For 12, look for parts like 1 200, 3 600, 120, 48 and 60.

Another example: 9 636 divided by 12. Break it into 9 600 and 36. 9 600 divided by 12 is 800, and 36 divided by 12 is 3. The answer is 803. Notice that the parts do not have to be split by place value. They just have to be easy to divide.

Halving is a special kind of breaking down. To divide by 4, halve twice. 5 368 divided by 4: half of 5 368 is 2 684, and half of 2 684 is 1 342. To divide by 8, halve three times.

The questions for this section are with you now: breaking a number into friendly parts and halving to divide by 4 and 8.

## Subtopic: Building Up with Chunks

The soccer academy has 7 245 litres of water to share equally among 15 tanks. Instead of long division, take away big chunks of 15. 15 times 400 is 6 000. Take that away: 7 245 take away 6 000 leaves 1 245. 15 times 80 is 1 200. Take that away: 1 245 take away 1 200 leaves 45. 15 times 3 is 45. Take that away: nothing is left.

Now add up the chunks: 400 plus 80 plus 3 is 483. Each tank gets 483 litres. This method is called chunking, or building up, because you build the answer from big friendly pieces.

The bigger your chunks, the fewer steps you need. Use multiples like 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 and 500 of the divisor, which are easy to work out. If you use small chunks, you still get the right answer, but it takes longer.

You can also build up by counting forward. 24 teams share 2 880 bibs and balls. 24 times 100 is 2 400. How much more to reach 2 880? 480. And 24 times 20 is 480. So 100 plus 20 is 120 for each team.

Chunking also shows remainders clearly. If the academy had 7 250 litres instead, the chunks 6 000, 1 200 and 45 would leave 5 litres over. The answer would be 483 remainder 5, because 5 is less than 15 and no more full chunk fits.

The questions for this section are with you now: dividing by taking away chunks and by building up.

## Subtopic: Checking: Inverse, Estimation and Dividing by 1

Division and multiplication are inverse operations: each one undoes the other. So check a division by multiplying the answer by the divisor. 483 times 15 should be 7 245. Work it out: 483 times 10 is 4 830, and 483 times 5 is 2 415. 4 830 plus 2 415 is 7 245. It matches.

Estimate before you divide. 7 245 divided by 15 is about 7 500 divided by 15, which is 500. 483 is close to 500, so it is sensible. If you had got 48 or 4 830, the estimate would warn you.

The number 1 has special division properties. Any number divided by 1 stays the same: 4 875 divided by 1 is 4 875. Any number except 0 divided by itself is 1: 4 875 divided by 4 875 is 1. And 0 divided by any number is 0: 0 cans shared among 12 bags gives 0 in each. But you cannot divide by 0. Sharing among 0 bags makes no sense.

Division can appear with other operations. 1 500 take away 2 880 divided by 24: divide first, 120, then subtract: 1 380. Use a calculator only to check your working.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: splitting 3 648 by place value into 3 000, 600, 40 and 8, and getting stuck, because 40 and 8 cannot be divided by 12 exactly; choose parts that are multiples of 12. Two: forgetting to add up the chunks, so 7 245 divided by 15 is given as 3, the last chunk; add 400, 80 and 3. Three: saying 4 875 divided by 1 is 1; dividing by 1 leaves the number the same. Four: working out 1 500 take away 2 880 first in 1 500 take away 2 880 divided by 24; divide first.

The questions for this section are with you now: inverse operations, estimating, and the special role of 1.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the cans, the water tanks and the soccer kit — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Split It Up

Split the big number into easy parts. 3 648 is 3 600 plus 48.

Divide each part: 300 and 4. Add: 304 bags.

Divide by 4? Halve twice.

Keep the easy parts in mind and try the questions for this part: breaking down.

## Subtopic: Take Away Chunks

Take away big chunks of the divisor: 15 times 400, then 15 times 80, then 15 times 3.

Add the chunks: 400 plus 80 plus 3 is 483.

Bigger chunks mean fewer steps.

Hold the chunks in mind and try this part's questions: building up.

## Subtopic: Multiply Back

Check a division by multiplying back. 483 times 15 is 7 245.

Divide by 1: no change. Divide a number by itself: 1.

You cannot divide by 0.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Break the number into parts that the divisor fits easily, divide each part and add. Take away big chunks of the divisor and add up the chunks. Halve to divide by 2, 4 and 8. Dividing by 1 leaves a number unchanged. Estimate first and check by multiplying back.

Hold the check in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: checking division.
