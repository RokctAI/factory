### subtopic_1 — Equal Sharing and Grouping with Remainders
**Assistant:** How do I know whether to round the answer up or ignore the remainder?

**Tutor:** Think about what the remainder stands for in the story and whether leaving it out would cause a problem. With the buses, the remainder is 50 learners. If you ignore them, 50 learners have no seat, which is a real problem, so you must round up and book 21 buses. Rounding up happens when everything must be fitted in: people in buses, eggs in boxes, books on shelves. With the chips, the remainder is R5. You cannot buy a packet for R5 when it costs R45, so the remainder cannot buy anything more, and you ignore it: 11 packets. Ignoring happens when you need a full group to count, like buying whole items or making whole costumes. With the sweets, the remainder is 16 sweets that are not shared. Nothing goes wrong if they are left over, so the answer is 41 each with 16 left. A good habit is to write the division answer with the remainder first, 20 remainder 50, then write a short sentence that answers the question in the story, like the school needs 21 buses. That sentence is where you show you understood the remainder.

### subtopic_2 — Division in Money and Measurement
**Assistant:** Why do I add the parts of a ratio before dividing?

**Tutor:** Because the ratio tells you how many equal parts the whole amount is split into, and adding gives you that number. In the ratio 1 to 3, Thabo gets 1 part and Lindiwe gets 3 parts. Altogether there are 4 parts, and those 4 parts must make up the whole R2 400. So each part is R2 400 divided by 4, which is R600. Then Thabo's 1 part is R600, and Lindiwe's 3 parts are R1 800. If you divided R2 400 by 3, you would get R800, and then Thabo would get R800 and Lindiwe R2 400, which adds up to R3 200, more than the prize. That is how you can tell it is wrong. Imagine sharing a slab of chocolate in the ratio 1 to 3. You would break it into 4 equal blocks, give 1 block to one person and 3 blocks to the other. The ratio does not tell you the size of the blocks; dividing the total by the number of parts does. Always check by adding the shares, which must equal the total, and by checking that the bigger share is 3 times the smaller one.

### subtopic_3 — Ratio and Rate with Division
**Assistant:** What is a unit price, and why is it useful?

**Tutor:** A unit price is the cost of one item, or of one unit such as one litre or one kilogram. You find it by dividing the total price by the number of items or units. 144 pens cost R1 008, so one pen costs R1 008 divided by 144, which is R7. Unit prices are useful because shops sell the same thing in different sizes, and the bigger pack is not always the cheaper choice. A pack of 6 juices for R54 works out to R9 per juice. A pack of 4 for R40 works out to R10 per juice. Even though R40 is less money to pay, each juice costs more. So if you need 12 juices, two packs of 6 cost R108, while three packs of 4 cost R120, and the packs of 6 save you R12. Grocery shops in South Africa often print the unit price on the shelf label in small letters, such as per 100 g or per litre, so that shoppers can compare. Learning to work out unit prices now will help you make every rand go further when you shop for yourself one day.
