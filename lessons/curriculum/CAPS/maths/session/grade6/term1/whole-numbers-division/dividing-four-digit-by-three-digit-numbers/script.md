# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers division of whole four-digit numbers by three-digit numbers. It builds on dividing three-digit numbers by two-digit numbers in Grade 5. The anchor is a concert hall in East London with 8 432 seats arranged in 124 equal rows, an egg farm near Bloemfontein that packs 9 375 eggs into boxes of 125, and a print shop that shares 5 000 pages among 135 learners. The governing idea is that long division asks the same question again and again, how many times does the divisor fit, and that a table of multiples of the divisor and a good estimate make each step easy. Estimating the answer, long division step by step, zeros in the answer, remainders, and checking by multiplying are examinable.

## Subtopic: Estimating and Building a Multiples Table

The concert hall has 8 432 seats in 124 equal rows. How many seats are in each row? We need 8 432 divided by 124. First estimate: 8 432 is about 8 400 and 124 is about 120. 8 400 divided by 120 is the same as 840 divided by 12, which is 70. So each row has about 70 seats.

The estimate tells you the answer has two digits and is close to 70. It also helps you catch mistakes later. If you get an answer like 6 or 680, you know something is wrong.

Next, build a table of multiples of the divisor. This saves guessing during the division. Start with 124 and keep adding 124: 124, 248, 372, 496, 620, 744, 868, 992 and 1 116. That is 124 times 1 up to 124 times 9. Check the table: 124 times 5 should be half of 124 times 10, which is half of 1 240, so 620. It is.

With the table, every step of the division is a matter of looking up the biggest multiple that fits. You do not have to do any hard multiplication in your head.

For the egg farm, the divisor is 125, and its multiples are even friendlier: 125, 250, 375, 500, 625, 750, 875, 1 000 and 1 125. Estimate 9 375 divided by 125 by thinking of 1 000 divided by 125, which is 8. So 9 000 divided by 125 is about 72, and the answer should be in the seventies.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating a division and building a table of multiples.

## Subtopic: Long Division Step by Step

Now divide 8 432 by 124. Look at the front of 8 432. Does 124 fit into 8? No. Into 84? No. Into 843? Yes. From the table, the biggest multiple of 124 that is not more than 843 is 744, which is 124 times 6. Write 6 above the 3, in the tens place of the answer. Subtract: 843 take away 744 is 99.

Bring down the next digit, 2, to make 992. How many times does 124 fit into 992? The table shows 992 is exactly 124 times 8. Write 8 above the 2, in the units place. Subtract: 992 take away 992 is 0. There are no more digits to bring down, so the answer is 68. Each row has 68 seats.

Check by multiplying: 68 times 124. 68 times 100 is 6 800, 68 times 20 is 1 360 and 68 times 4 is 272. Together: 6 800 plus 1 360 plus 272 is 8 432. That matches, so the answer is right. It is also close to our estimate of 70.

The egg farm packs 9 375 eggs in boxes of 125. 125 fits into 937 seven times, because 125 times 7 is 875. 937 take away 875 is 62. Bring down 5 to make 625, which is exactly 125 times 5. The answer is 75 boxes.

The steps repeat: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. After each subtraction, check that what is left is smaller than the divisor, otherwise the divisor could have fitted one more time.

The questions for this section are with you now: the steps of long division and checking by multiplying.

## Subtopic: Zeros and Remainders

The print shop shares 5 000 pages among 135 learners. 135 does not fit into 50, but it fits into 500 three times, because 135 times 3 is 405. 500 take away 405 is 95. Bring down the last 0 to make 950. 135 times 7 is 945, so 135 fits 7 times. 950 take away 945 is 5. There are no more digits, so the answer is 37 remainder 5. Each learner gets 37 pages and 5 pages are left over.

Check a division with a remainder by multiplying and then adding the remainder: 37 times 135 is 4 995, and 4 995 plus 5 is 5 000. The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor. If your remainder is 135 or more, the divisor could have fitted one more time.

Sometimes the answer has a zero in it. 6 150 divided by 205: 205 fits into 615 three times exactly, with nothing left over. Bring down the 0. 205 does not fit into 0, so write 0 in the answer. The answer is 30, not 3. Never skip a place: every digit you bring down gets a digit in the answer.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing the first answer digit above the wrong place, so 8 432 divided by 124 becomes 680; the 6 belongs above the 3. Two: forgetting the zero, so 6 150 divided by 205 becomes 3 instead of 30. Three: leaving a remainder bigger than the divisor. Four: checking a remainder answer by multiplying only, so 37 times 135 is 4 995 and the answer is marked wrong; add the remainder to get 5 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: remainders, zeros in the answer, and checking.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the concert hall, the eggs and the print shop — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Guess, Then List

Estimate first. 8 432 divided by 124 is about 8 400 divided by 120, which is about 70.

Make a list of the divisor's multiples: 124, 248, 372 and so on.

Look up the biggest one that fits.

Keep the estimate in mind and try the questions for this part: estimating and tables.

## Subtopic: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring Down

Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Then do it again.

843 take away 744 is 99. Bring down 2: 992. That is 124 times 8.

8 432 divided by 124 is 68. Check: 68 times 124 is 8 432.

Hold the four steps in mind and try this part's questions: long division.

## Subtopic: Leftovers and Zeros

What is left at the end is the remainder. It must be smaller than the divisor.

5 000 divided by 135 is 37 remainder 5.

Every digit you bring down gets a digit in the answer: 6 150 divided by 205 is 30.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Estimate first. Make a table of multiples of the divisor. Then repeat: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Write each answer digit above the digit you brought down. The remainder must be smaller than the divisor. Check by multiplying the answer by the divisor and adding the remainder.

Hold the check in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: remainders and zeros.
