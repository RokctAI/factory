# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems involving whole numbers in financial and measurement contexts, using addition and subtraction. The anchor is the Dlamini family in Mthatha who plan a holiday to Cape Town with a budget of R25 000, a road trip of 1 268 km done in two days, and a farmer near Komani who harvested 48 750 kg of maize. The governing idea is that every word problem tells a story about joining or separating amounts, and that you find the answer by deciding what is being asked, choosing the operation, calculating and then checking the answer against the story. Budgets, change, profit and loss, distances and masses, and problems with more than one step are examinable.

## Subtopic: Money Problems: Budgets and Change

The Dlamini family has a budget of R25 000 for the holiday. The bus tickets cost R4 680, the guest house costs R12 450 and they plan to spend R5 375 on food. How much will they spend? The word spend over several items means add: R4 680 plus R12 450 plus R5 375 is R22 505.

How much of the budget will be left? Left means take away: R25 000 take away R22 505 is R2 495. A quick way is to count on from R22 505: R95 more makes R22 600, R400 more makes R23 000, and R2 000 more makes R25 000. The jumps add up to R2 495.

Money problems use some special words. Income is money that comes in. Expenses are money that goes out. If income is more than expenses, the difference is called a profit. If expenses are more than income, the difference is called a loss. A tuck shop that buys stock for R3 450 and sells it all for R6 235 makes a profit of R6 235 take away R3 450, which is R2 785.

Change works the same way. If Gogo pays for a R6 235 fridge with R10 000, the change is R10 000 take away R6 235, which is R3 765. Check by adding: R6 235 plus R3 765 is R10 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: budgets, what is left over, profit and loss, and change.

## Subtopic: Measurement Problems: Distance and Mass

The family drives from Mthatha to Cape Town, a trip of 1 268 km. On the first day they drive 645 km. How far is left for the second day? 1 268 take away 645 is 623 km. Check: 645 plus 623 is 1 268. If they drive home the same way, the whole return trip is 1 268 plus 1 268, which is 2 536 km.

Measurement problems always have units, like km, m, kg or g. Write the unit with every answer. An answer of 623 means nothing on its own; 623 km tells the reader exactly what you found.

Before adding or subtracting, make sure the amounts are in the same unit. You cannot add 3 km and 500 m by adding 3 and 500. Change 3 km into 3 000 m first, then add: 3 000 m plus 500 m is 3 500 m.

The farmer near Komani harvested 48 750 kg of maize and sold 29 860 kg to a mill. How much is left? 48 750 take away 29 860 is 18 890 kg. A sensible check is an estimate: about 49 000 take away about 30 000 is about 19 000, and 18 890 is close.

The mill sends a truck that can carry 12 000 kg on each trip. After the first trip, how much of the 29 860 kg still has to be fetched? 29 860 take away 12 000 is 17 860 kg. After the second trip, 17 860 take away 12 000 is 5 860 kg. The third trip carries the last 5 860 kg, so the truck needs three trips. Notice how a measurement story can be solved with repeated subtraction, one trip at a time.

The questions for this section are with you now: distances, masses, and keeping the units the same.

## Subtopic: Multi-Step Problems

Some problems need more than one step. Read the whole problem first, then break it into steps. Sipho has saved R18 650. He earns R6 375 from a holiday job and then buys a laptop for R9 990. How much does he have now?

Step 1: he earns money, so add. R18 650 plus R6 375 is R25 025. Step 2: he spends money, so take away. R25 025 take away R9 990 is R15 035. You can use rounding and compensating here: take away R10 000 to get R15 025, then give back R10, because you took R10 too much. That gives R15 035.

A good plan for every word problem has four parts. First, read it and find the question. Second, decide what to do: add, subtract or both. Third, calculate and write each step clearly. Fourth, check the answer and write it in a sentence with the unit: Sipho now has R15 035.

Look out for extra information that is not needed, and for questions that ask for a difference rather than a total. How much more did the guest house cost than the bus tickets? R12 450 take away R4 680, which is R7 770.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding when the problem asks how much is left; left means subtract. Two: adding 3 km and 500 m to get 503 km; change to the same unit first. Three: stopping after the first step of a two-step problem, so Sipho has R25 025; finish every step. Four: writing an answer without its unit, like 623 instead of 623 km.

The questions for this section are with you now: two-step problems, finding differences and writing answers in sentences.

1 268 km take away 645 km is 623 km.

Always write the unit with the answer.

## Subtopic: One Step at a Time

Break a long problem into steps.

Sipho: R18 650 plus R6 375 is R25 025. Then take away R9 990: R15 035.

Answer in a sentence with the unit.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Read the problem and find the question. Joining amounts means add; taking away, finding what is left or finding a difference means subtract. Use the same units, and write the unit in the answer. Break long problems into steps. Check the answer with an estimate or by adding back.
