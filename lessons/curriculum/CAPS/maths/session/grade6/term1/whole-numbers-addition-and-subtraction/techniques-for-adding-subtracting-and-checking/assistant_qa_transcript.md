### subtopic_1 — Breaking Down and Building Up
**Assistant:** Why should I learn other methods if the column method always works?

**Tutor:** The column method is reliable, but it is slow for some sums and easy to slip up in when there are lots of zeros or nines. Other methods are faster in the right situation and give you a way to check. Think about 248 635 plus 99 999. In columns you would carry in almost every place. With rounding and compensating you just add 100 000 and take away 1, which you can do in your head in a few seconds. Or think about R50 000 take away R37 850. In columns you would borrow across three zeros. Counting on from 37 850 to 38 000, to 40 000, to 50 000 is quicker and you are less likely to make a mistake. Knowing more than one method also means you can check your work properly. If you check a column sum with the same column method, you might make the same mistake twice. If you check with a different method, a mistake usually shows up as two different answers. It is like a builder who measures a wall with a tape and then checks it with a second tape: two tools are better than one.

### subtopic_2 — Rounding Off and Compensating
**Assistant:** When I compensate, how do I remember whether to add or subtract the difference?

**Tutor:** Ask yourself what you did too much of, and undo exactly that. For addition: 248 635 plus 99 999. You added 100 000, which is 1 more than you should have. You added too much, so take the extra 1 away: 348 635 take away 1 is 348 634. For subtraction: 76 412 take away 19 998. You took away 20 000, which is 2 more than you should have. You took away too much, so give the 2 back: 56 412 plus 2 is 56 414. Here is a money story that helps. You owe your friend R19 998, but you only have R20 000 notes, so you give R20 000. Your friend has to give you R2 change. The change comes back to you, so you add it back. If you are unsure, use an estimate to see which answer is sensible. 76 412 take away about 20 000 is about 56 400. Both 56 410 and 56 414 are near that, so the estimate cannot decide. Then use the inverse check: 56 414 plus 19 998 is 76 412, so 56 414 is the right answer.

### subtopic_3 — Checking with Inverse Operations and Estimation
**Assistant:** Why can I use a calculator only to check, and not to work out the answer?

**Tutor:** Because the curriculum wants you to understand the numbers, and because a calculator is only as good as the buttons you press. If you press 7 instead of 4, or miss a zero, the calculator gives a wrong answer with total confidence, and if you did not know roughly what to expect, you would believe it. In tests and in many everyday situations, like counting change at a spaza shop or checking a bill, you need to be able to work it out yourself. So the calculator's job is to be a second opinion. Work out 34 526 plus 23 251 yourself and get 57 777. Then type it into the calculator. If it also says 57 777, you can be confident. If it says something different, do not just copy the calculator. Check both: maybe you made a mistake, or maybe you pressed a wrong key. An estimate is your referee. About 35 000 plus about 23 000 is about 58 000, so if the calculator says 577 770, you know at once that a key went wrong.
