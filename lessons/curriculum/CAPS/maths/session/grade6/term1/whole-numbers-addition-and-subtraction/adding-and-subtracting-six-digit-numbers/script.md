# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers addition and subtraction of whole numbers with at least five and six digits, using the column method. It builds on the five-digit work of Grade 5. The anchor is a theme park near Johannesburg that had 486 357 visitors in the first half of the year and 275 896 in the second half, a reservoir in Bloemfontein that held 604 020 kilolitres of water before 285 467 kilolitres were used, and three schools in Mangaung that collected books for a library. The governing idea is that you line up the digits by place value, work from the units to the left, carry when a column makes ten or more, and borrow when the top digit is too small. Adding with carrying, subtracting with borrowing, including across zeros, and adding three numbers are examinable.

## Subtopic: Adding in Columns with Carrying

How many people visited the theme park in the whole year? We add 486 357 and 275 896. Write the numbers under each other, units under units, tens under tens, all the way to the hundred thousands. Always start on the right.

Units: 7 plus 6 is 13. Write 3 and carry 1 ten to the tens column. Tens: 5 plus 9 plus the carried 1 is 15. Write 5 and carry 1 to the hundreds. Hundreds: 3 plus 8 plus 1 is 12. Write 2 and carry 1. Thousands: 6 plus 5 plus 1 is 12. Write 2 and carry 1. Ten thousands: 8 plus 7 plus 1 is 16. Write 6 and carry 1. Hundred thousands: 4 plus 2 plus 1 is 7. The total is 762 253 visitors.

Carrying works because ten of one place make one of the next place. Thirteen units are one ten and three units, so the ten moves into the tens column. Write the carried digit small at the top of the next column so you do not forget it.

Check with an estimate. 486 357 is about 490 000 and 275 896 is about 280 000. 490 000 plus 280 000 is 770 000. Our answer, 762 253, is close to that, so it is sensible.

A five-digit number and a six-digit number are added in the same way. 823 450 plus 56 789: line up the units, and leave the hundred thousands column of 56 789 empty. Units 0 plus 9 is 9. Tens 5 plus 8 is 13, write 3 and carry 1. Hundreds 4 plus 7 plus 1 is 12, write 2 and carry 1. Thousands 3 plus 6 plus 1 is 10, write 0 and carry 1. Ten thousands 2 plus 5 plus 1 is 8. Hundred thousands 8. The total is 880 239.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding six-digit numbers in columns, and carrying.

## Subtopic: Subtracting in Columns with Borrowing

The reservoir held 604 020 kilolitres and 285 467 kilolitres were used. How much is left? We subtract 285 467 from 604 020. Write the bigger number on top and line up the places. Start on the right.

Units: 0 take away 7 cannot be done, so borrow one ten from the tens. The tens digit 2 becomes 1, and the units become 10. 10 take away 7 is 3. Tens: 1 take away 6 cannot be done. The hundreds digit is 0, so there is nothing to borrow there. Go to the thousands: the 4 becomes 3, the hundreds become 10, then borrow one hundred so the hundreds become 9 and the tens become 11. 11 take away 6 is 5. Hundreds: 9 take away 4 is 5.

Thousands: 3 take away 5 cannot be done. The ten thousands digit is 0, so go to the hundred thousands: the 6 becomes 5, the ten thousands become 10, then borrow one ten thousand so the ten thousands become 9 and the thousands become 13. 13 take away 5 is 8. Ten thousands: 9 take away 8 is 1. Hundred thousands: 5 take away 2 is 3. The answer is 318 553 kilolitres.

Borrowing across zeros is where most mistakes happen. When you meet a zero, keep going left until you find a digit you can borrow from. Every zero you pass becomes 9. Check by adding: 318 553 plus 285 467 is 604 020, the number we started with, so the subtraction is right.

The questions for this section are with you now: subtracting with borrowing, borrowing across zeros, and checking by adding.

## Subtopic: Adding and Subtracting Several Numbers

Three schools collect books for a library: 125 408, 67 395 and 209 087. Add all three in one column sum. Be careful: 67 395 has only five digits, so its first digit goes under the ten thousands, not under the hundred thousands.

Units: 8 plus 5 plus 7 is 20. Write 0 and carry 2. With three numbers you can carry 2, not just 1. Tens: 0 plus 9 plus 8 plus 2 is 19. Write 9 and carry 1. Hundreds: 4 plus 3 plus 0 plus 1 is 8. Thousands: 5 plus 7 plus 9 is 21. Write 1 and carry 2. Ten thousands: 2 plus 6 plus 0 plus 2 is 10. Write 0 and carry 1. Hundred thousands: 1 plus 2 plus 1 is 4. Altogether: 401 890 books.

Some problems mix adding and subtracting. If the library gives away 56 789 of the 401 890 books, then 401 890 take away 56 789 is 345 101 books left. Read the question carefully: words like altogether and total mean add, and words like left, difference and how many more mean subtract.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: forgetting to add the carried digit, so 486 357 plus 275 896 becomes 651 143; add the carry every time. Two: subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger in each column, so 604 020 take away 285 467 becomes 481 447; borrow instead. Three: borrowing across a zero and leaving the zero as 0 instead of 9. Four: lining up 67 395 on the left, under the hundred thousands; line up the units.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding three numbers, mixed problems, and choosing add or subtract.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the theme park, the reservoir and the library books — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Carry the Ten

Line up the units. Start on the right.

10 or more in a column? Write the units and carry the ten.

486 357 plus 275 896 is 762 253.

Keep the carry in mind and try the questions for this part: adding.

## Subtopic: Borrow from Next Door

Top digit too small? Borrow one from next door.

Next door is 0? Keep going left. Every 0 you pass becomes 9.

604 020 take away 285 467 is 318 553. Add back to check.

Hold the borrowing in mind and try this part's questions: subtracting.

## Subtopic: Line Them Up

Three numbers? Line up the units, then add down each column.

You may carry 2.

125 408 plus 67 395 plus 209 087 is 401 890.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Line up the digits by place value, units under units. Work from right to left. When a column adds to 10 or more, carry. When the top digit is too small, borrow, and every zero you pass becomes 9. Check addition with an estimate and check subtraction by adding back.

Hold the columns in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: several numbers.
