### subtopic_1 — Adding in Columns with Carrying
**Assistant:** Why do we always start adding from the right and not the left?

**Tutor:** Because carrying moves to the left. When you add the units and get 13, the extra ten has to go into the tens column. If you had already finished the tens column, you would have to go back and change it. Starting on the right means every carry lands in a column you have not done yet, so you never have to rub anything out. Look at 486 357 plus 275 896. If you started on the left you would write 6 in the hundred thousands, because 4 plus 2 is 6. Then later, when the ten thousands give you 16, you would have to change the 6 to a 7. And the thousands would change the ten thousands, and so on down the line. It gets messy fast. Some people do add from the left in their heads, by adding the big parts first, which is a good mental method: 480 000 plus 270 000 is 750 000, and so on. That works because you keep track of all the parts and add them at the end. But for the written column method, right to left keeps the work neat and makes carrying automatic.

### subtopic_2 — Subtracting in Columns with Borrowing
**Assistant:** What happens when I have to borrow and there are two zeros next to each other?

**Tutor:** You keep going left until you reach a digit that is not zero, borrow one from there, and then every zero you passed becomes 9. Think of 7 000 take away 1. The units are 0, the tens are 0 and the hundreds are 0. You go all the way to the thousands and change 7 to 6. Then the hundreds become 10, but you borrow one of them, so they become 9. The tens become 10, then 9 when you borrow. The units become 10. Now 10 take away 1 is 9, and the answer is 6 999. It makes sense: 7 000 take away 1 should be just under 7 000. In real life it is like changing money. If you have a R100 note and you need to pay R1, the shop changes your R100 into nine R10 coins, nine R1 coins and one more R1. In 604 020 take away 285 467, the same thing happens twice: once for the hundreds zero and once for the ten thousands zero. Cross out each digit as you change it and write the new digit above it, so you can see exactly what you have.

### subtopic_3 — Adding and Subtracting Several Numbers
**Assistant:** How can I check a subtraction answer quickly?

**Tutor:** Add your answer to the number you subtracted. If you get the number you started with, the answer is right. This works because subtraction and addition undo each other: they are inverse operations. For the reservoir, 604 020 take away 285 467 gave 318 553. Now add 318 553 and 285 467. Units: 3 plus 7 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Tens: 5 plus 6 plus 1 is 12, write 2 carry 1. Hundreds: 5 plus 4 plus 1 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Thousands: 8 plus 5 plus 1 is 14, write 4 carry 1. Ten thousands: 1 plus 8 plus 1 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Hundred thousands: 3 plus 2 plus 1 is 6. You get 604 020, which is where you started. If the check gives a different number, find the column where it goes wrong, because that is usually where the mistake is in the subtraction too. For a quicker rough check, use an estimate: about 600 000 take away about 300 000 is about 300 000, and 318 553 is near that.
