### subtopic_1 — Money Problems: Budgets and Change
**Assistant:** How do I know whether to add or subtract in a word problem?

**Tutor:** Picture what is happening in the story. If amounts are being put together, like several costs on a holiday or two days of driving, you add. If an amount is being reduced, like money being spent from a budget or maize being sold, you subtract. If the question asks how much more, how much less, how far is left, or what the difference is, you also subtract, because you are comparing two amounts. Words can help, but be careful, because the same word can mean different things. Altogether, total and sum usually mean add. Left, remain, change, difference and how many more usually mean subtract. But a sentence like he has R500 more than his sister might mean you add R500 to the sister's amount to find his. So always come back to the picture. A good test is to guess roughly what the answer should be before you calculate. If the family has R25 000 and spends R22 505, the money left must be less than R25 000. If your answer is bigger than R25 000, you added when you should have subtracted.

### subtopic_2 — Measurement Problems: Distance and Mass
**Assistant:** Why do I have to change the units before I add?

**Tutor:** Because you can only add things that are the same kind of thing and the same size of unit. Think of money. If you have R3 and 50c, you do not have R53 or 53c. You have R3 and 50c, which is 350c, or R3,50. The 3 counts rands and the 50 counts cents, and a rand is a hundred times bigger than a cent. Distances work the same way. In 3 km plus 500 m, the 3 counts kilometres and the 500 counts metres. A kilometre is 1 000 metres, so 3 km is 3 000 m. Now both numbers count metres, and you can add: 3 000 m plus 500 m is 3 500 m. If you just added 3 and 500 and wrote 503 km, you would be saying the distance is more than a hundred times longer than it really is. So before you add or subtract measurements, look at the units. If they are different, change the bigger unit into the smaller one, because then you are working with whole numbers. Then add or subtract, and write the unit with your answer.

### subtopic_3 — Multi-Step Problems
**Assistant:** What is the best way to set out the working for a two-step problem?

**Tutor:** Write each step on its own line with a few words saying what it is. For Sipho, write: Step 1, money after the job, R18 650 plus R6 375 equals R25 025. Step 2, money after buying the laptop, R25 025 take away R9 990 equals R15 035. Then write the answer as a sentence: Sipho now has R15 035. Setting it out like this has three big benefits. First, you can see that you have answered every part of the question, so you do not stop at R25 025 by accident. Second, if you make a mistake, a teacher can see exactly where it happened and you can still earn marks for the steps that are right. Third, it is much easier to check your own work. You can go back and ask of each step: does this make sense? For example, after buying a laptop, Sipho should have less money than R25 025, and R15 035 is less. You can also check the whole thing with an estimate: about R19 000 plus about R6 000 is about R25 000, take away about R10 000 leaves about R15 000.
