# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the commutative, associative and distributive properties of whole numbers, and 0 in terms of its additive property. The anchor is a tuck shop in Kimberley that adds up sweets, chips and juice in its stock room, and a school concert where tickets are sold in rows of seats. The governing idea is that addition lets you change the order and the grouping of numbers without changing the answer, that multiplying a sum can be done part by part, and that adding 0 changes nothing. Recognising and using each property, knowing which properties do not work for subtraction, and using the properties to calculate quickly are examinable.

## Subtopic: Changing the Order: The Commutative Property

The tuck shop has 3 456 sweets in one box and 12 544 in another. Whether you add 3 456 plus 12 544 or 12 544 plus 3 456, the total is 16 000. Changing the order of the numbers in an addition does not change the answer. This is called the commutative property of addition. The word comes from commute, which means to travel or move around.

The commutative property is useful because you can start with the number that is easier to count on from. 8 plus 25 678 is easier as 25 678 plus 8, which is 25 686. In the same way, multiplication is commutative: 4 times 250 is the same as 250 times 4, which is 1 000.

Subtraction is not commutative. 500 take away 200 is 300, but 200 take away 500 does not give a whole number answer at all. If the tuck shop has 500 packets of chips and sells 200, it has 300 left. You cannot swap the numbers round. Division is not commutative either: 12 divided by 4 is 3, but 4 divided by 12 is not 3.

So when you see an addition or multiplication, you may swap the numbers. When you see a subtraction or division, keep them in the order given.

The commutative property also helps you check. If you add a column of numbers from the top down, check it by adding from the bottom up. The order has changed, so if both totals agree, you can trust the answer.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing the order in addition and multiplication, and why subtraction is different.

## Subtopic: Changing the Grouping: The Associative Property

When you add three numbers, you add two of them first and then add the third. Brackets show which two go first. The tuck shop has 2 375 sweets, 1 625 chips and 4 890 juices. Grouping the first two: 2 375 plus 1 625 is 4 000, and 4 000 plus 4 890 is 8 890. Grouping the last two: 1 625 plus 4 890 is 6 515, and 2 375 plus 6 515 is 8 890. The answer is the same. This is the associative property of addition: you can change the grouping without changing the answer.

The associative property and the commutative property together let you add a long list in any order you like. Look for pairs that make friendly numbers. 1 998 plus 5 467 plus 2 002: group 1 998 and 2 002 first, because they make 4 000. Then 4 000 plus 5 467 is 9 467. That is much easier than adding from left to right.

Multiplication is associative too. 2 times 7 times 50 can be grouped as 2 times 50 first, which is 100, and then 100 times 7 is 700.

Subtraction is not associative. 100 take away 50, then take away 20, is 30. But 100 take away the answer to 50 take away 20 is 100 take away 30, which is 70. The grouping changed the answer, so with subtraction you must work from left to right.

The questions for this section are with you now: regrouping additions, looking for friendly pairs, and why subtraction is different.

## Subtopic: The Distributive Property and Zero

At the concert, 4 rows each have R1 250 worth of tickets. 4 times 1 250 can be broken up: 1 250 is 1 000 plus 200 plus 50. Multiply each part by 4 and add: 4 000 plus 800 plus 200 is 5 000. The concert made R5 000. This is the distributive property: to multiply a sum, multiply each part and add the answers.

The distributive property is the reason the breaking down method works for multiplication. 6 times 235 is 6 times 200 plus 6 times 30 plus 6 times 5. That is 1 200 plus 180 plus 30, which is 1 410. It also works backwards: 7 times 48 plus 7 times 52 is 7 times 100, because 48 plus 52 is 100. The answer is 700.

Zero has a special job in addition. Adding 0 to a number leaves it exactly the same: 45 678 plus 0 is 45 678. Taking 0 away also leaves it the same. This is the additive property of 0: 0 is the number that changes nothing when you add it. And any number take away itself is 0: 45 678 take away 45 678 is 0, which means nothing is left.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying 200 take away 500 is 300 because subtraction can be swapped; it cannot. Two: working out 100 take away 50 take away 20 as 100 take away 30; work from left to right to get 30. Three: multiplying only the first part of a sum, so 4 times 1 250 becomes 4 000 plus 250; multiply every part. Four: saying 45 678 plus 0 is 0; adding 0 changes nothing, so it is 45 678.

The questions for this section are with you now: the distributive property and the role of 0.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

## Subtopic: Swap It

In adding, the order does not matter. 3 456 plus 12 544 is the same as 12 544 plus 3 456.

In multiplying, the order does not matter either.

In subtracting, the order matters. Do not swap.

Keep the swap in mind and try the questions for this part: the commutative property.

## Subtopic: Group It

In adding, you can group the numbers any way.

Find friendly pairs. 1 998 plus 2 002 is 4 000. Then add 5 467 to get 9 467.

Subtracting? Work left to right.

Hold the friendly pairs in mind and try this part's questions: the associative property.

## Subtopic: Share It Out

To multiply a sum, multiply each part. 4 times 1 250 is 4 000 plus 800 plus 200, which is 5 000.

Adding 0 changes nothing.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The commutative property: you can swap the order in addition and multiplication, but not in subtraction or division. The associative property: you can change the grouping in addition and multiplication, but not in subtraction. The distributive property: multiply each part of a sum and add the answers. Adding 0 leaves a number unchanged.
