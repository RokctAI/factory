### subtopic_1 — Money Problems
**Assistant:** How do I know which operation to use in a word problem?

**Tutor:** Look at what the story is asking, not just at the numbers. If you are putting amounts together to find a total, such as the cost of flour, oil and mince, you add: R89 plus R65 plus R146 is R300. If you are finding what is left or the difference between two amounts, such as change or profit, you subtract: R600 minus R300 is a profit of R300. If you have many groups of the same size, such as 120 vetkoek at R5 each, you multiply: 120 times R5 is R600. If you are sharing equally or finding how many groups fit, you divide: R232 shared among 4 helpers is R58 each. Many problems need more than one step. Finding the total profit for the day meant subtracting twice, once for vetkoek and once for juice, and then adding the two profits. Write down each step with a short label, like costs, income and profit, so that you do not lose track. At the end, read the question again and check that your answer is the thing it asked for, with the right unit.

### subtopic_2 — Measurement Problems with Fractions
**Assistant:** Why should I change kilograms to grams before finding a fraction?

**Tutor:** Because it lets you work with whole numbers, which is easier and safer. Three quarters of 2 kg sounds tricky, but 2 kg is 2 000 g, and three quarters of 2 000 g is easy. Divide by the denominator: 2 000 divided by 4 is 500. Multiply by the numerator: 3 times 500 is 1 500. So the answer is 1 500 g, which you can change back to one and a half kilograms if the question wants kilograms. The same trick works for capacity. Three fifths of 2 litres becomes three fifths of 2 000 ml, which is 1 200 ml. A common mistake is to think three quarters of 2 kg is three quarters of a kilogram, which forgets about the 2. Changing to grams first makes that mistake much harder to make. When you divide one measurement by another, as with 5 litres poured into 250 ml cups, changing to the same unit is not just easier, it is necessary: 5 000 ml divided by 250 ml is 20 cups.

### subtopic_3 — Grouping and Equal Sharing
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a sharing problem and a grouping problem?

**Tutor:** In a sharing problem, you know how many shares there are, and you find how big each share is. Three melktert tarts shared equally among 4 helpers is a sharing problem: there are 4 shares, and each share is three quarters of a tart. In a grouping problem, you know how big each group is, and you find how many groups there are. Pouring 3 litres of juice into quarter-litre cups is a grouping problem: each cup holds a quarter of a litre, and we want to know how many cups we can fill. Each litre fills 4 cups, so 3 litres fill 12 cups. Both kinds of problem are division, but they ask different questions. A good way to check which one you have is to ask yourself whether you know the number of groups or the size of each group. Drawing a quick picture also helps: draw 3 tarts and cut each into quarters, then hand the pieces out to 4 helpers one at a time. Each helper ends up with 3 quarters.
