### subtopic_1 — Asking Questions and Collecting Data
**Assistant:** What makes a good survey question?

**Tutor:** A good survey question is clear, fair and easy to answer. Clear means everyone understands it in the same way. How do you usually travel to school is clear, because it asks about the usual way, not about one unusual day. Adding choices helps even more: walk, taxi, bus, car or bicycle. Then every learner picks exactly one, and the answers are easy to tally. Fair means the question does not push people towards a particular answer. If you ask, soccer is the best sport, isn't it, many people will just agree, even if they prefer netball, because the question suggests that agreeing is correct. A fair version is: which sport do you like best, and then list the choices. Easy to answer means the question is short and asks about one thing at a time. Do you eat breakfast and lunch every day is confusing, because someone might eat breakfast but not lunch. Split it into two questions. Yes or no questions are the easiest of all to answer and count, but they give less detail. Always try your question on one or two people first, to check that it works.

### subtopic_2 — Tally Marks and Frequency Tables
**Assistant:** Why do we draw tally marks in groups of five?

**Tutor:** Because groups of five are much quicker and safer to count than a long row of single lines. Imagine 13 upright lines in a row. To count them, you would have to point at each one, and it would be easy to lose your place. Now imagine the same 13 drawn as two bundles of five and three more lines. You can see at a glance that the bundles make 10, and 10 plus 3 is 13. To make a bundle, draw four upright lines and then draw the fifth line across them, like a little gate. The line across tells you the bundle is full, so you start a new one with the next tally. This also helps when you are tallying quickly during a survey, because you only have to keep track of the bundle you are working on. When you have finished, count the bundles in fives and add the extra lines. Write the total in the frequency column of your table. Then add up all the frequencies and check that the grand total matches the number of people you asked. That final check catches mistakes like counting someone twice.

### subtopic_3 — Organising and Ordering Data
**Assistant:** Why must intervals not overlap?

**Tutor:** Because every value must belong to exactly one group, or it will be counted twice or in the wrong place. Suppose the class groups travel times into 0 to 10 minutes, 10 to 20 minutes and 20 to 30 minutes. A learner who takes exactly 10 minutes fits in the first group and in the second group. Some people would put her in one group and some in the other, or she might be counted in both, and then the total would be wrong. Now use 0 to 9 minutes, 10 to 19 minutes and 20 to 29 minutes. A learner who takes 10 minutes goes only in the 10 to 19 group. A learner who takes 9 minutes goes only in the 0 to 9 group. There are no gaps and no overlaps, so every whole number of minutes has one home. The intervals should also be the same size, here 10 minutes each, so that the groups can be compared fairly. With these intervals, the class found 7, 13, 10 and 6 learners, which adds up to 36. Intervals make large sets of different numbers easy to organise and show on a graph.
