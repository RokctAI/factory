### subtopic_1 — The Mode
**Assistant:** Why must I put the data in order before finding the median?

**Tutor:** Because the median is the middle value of the ordered data, not the middle of the list as someone happened to write it. The netball scores were written down in the order the matches were played: 12, 15, 9, 15, 18, 11, 15, 10, 14. The middle of that list is the fifth value, 18. But 18 is the team's best score, not a typical one, so it would be a silly answer. When you put the scores in order, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 15, 15, 18, the fifth value is 14. Now there are four scores below it and four above it, which is exactly what the median should mean: a value right in the middle of the spread. The order of the matches has nothing to do with how big the scores are, so the median must ignore it. A good habit is to rewrite the data in order first, and then cross off the smallest and largest values in pairs, one from each end, until one value is left in the middle, or two. That method is quick and it is hard to make a mistake with it.

### subtopic_2 — The Median
**Assistant:** What is the difference between the mode and the median?

**Tutor:** Both describe a typical value, but in different ways. The mode is the value that occurs most often. To find it, you count. In the netball scores, 15 appears three times, more than any other score, so the mode is 15. The median is the value in the middle when the data is in order. To find it, you order the data and find the centre. In the netball scores, the middle value is 14. So the mode and the median can be different, and here they are close but not the same. Each has its uses. The mode is the only one you can use for categories, like favourite sport, where soccer is the mode, because you cannot put sports in order of size. The mode is also useful for things like shoe sizes, where a shop wants to know the most common size to stock. The median is useful when there are a few unusual values, because they do not pull it far. If the team scored 40 in one match, the median would still be 14. When you summarise data, it often helps to give both.

### subtopic_3 — Summarising Data
**Assistant:** How do I make a sensible prediction from data?

**Tutor:** Look at what usually happens in the data, and say what is likely to happen next, using careful words like probably or about. The netball team's median score is 14 and its mode is 15, and most scores are between 10 and 15. So a sensible prediction is that in the next match the team will probably score about 14 or 15 goals. It would not be sensible to predict 40 goals, because the team has never scored close to that, or 0 goals, because the lowest score was 9. You can also mention the spread: the team could score as few as 9 or as many as 18, based on what has happened before. Remember that a prediction is not a promise. The next match might be against a much stronger or weaker team, or a key player might be sick, and the score could be very different. Predictions get better when there is more data. Nine matches give a fair idea, but a whole season of matches would give a better one. That is why weather forecasters use many years of records.
