# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers analysing data by finding the mode and the median, and summarising data in words and short written paragraphs, drawing conclusions and making predictions. It builds on collecting, organising and reading data in the previous lessons. The anchor is the Grade 6 netball team at a school in Kroonstad. Over nine matches, the team scores 12, 15, 9, 15, 18, 11, 15, 10 and 14 goals. The coach also has six learners' fitness test marks out of 20, and the class's favourite-sport data. The governing idea is that the mode and the median are two ways of describing a typical value in a set of data: the mode is the value that occurs most often, and the median is the middle value once the data is in order. Finding the mode and the median, and writing a clear summary with a conclusion and a prediction, are examinable.

## Subtopic: The Mode

The mode is the value that occurs most often. To find it, count how many times each value appears.

In the netball scores, 12 appears once, 15 appears three times, 9 once, 18 once, 11 once, 10 once and 14 once. So the mode is 15 goals. The mode is the score, 15, not the number of times it appears, 3.

The mode works for categories too, not just numbers. In the class's favourite-sport data, soccer had 14 votes, more than any other sport, so soccer is the mode. You cannot find a middle sport, but you can find the most popular one.

Some data has more than one mode. In 3, 5, 5, 7, 7, 9, both 5 and 7 appear twice, so there are two modes. Some data has no mode at all: in 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, every value appears once.

Putting the data in order first makes the mode easy to spot, because equal values sit next to each other.

## Subtopic: The Median

The median is the middle value when the data is arranged in order from smallest to largest. The first step is always to order the data.

The netball scores in order are 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 15, 15, 18. There are 9 values. The middle one has 4 values on each side, so it is the fifth value. The median is 14 goals.

When there is an even number of values, there are two middle values, and the median is halfway between them. The six fitness marks in order are 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19. The two middle values are 15 and 17. Halfway between them is 16, so the median is 16. You can find halfway by adding the two values and halving: 15 plus 17 is 32, and half of 32 is 16.

If both middle values are the same, the median is that value. Shoe sizes of 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 8 have middle values of 6 and 6, so the median is 6. The mode is also 6.

The median is useful because a single very big or very small value does not pull it far. If the team scored 40 in one match instead of 18, the median would still be 14.

A quick way to find the middle is to cross off values in pairs, one from each end of the ordered list. Cross off 9 and 18, then 10 and 15, then 11 and 15, then 12 and 15. Only 14 is left, so 14 is the median. With the six fitness marks, crossing off 12 and 19, then 14 and 18, leaves two values, 15 and 17, so the median is halfway between them.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding the median.

## Subtopic: Summarising Data

A summary describes the data in a few clear sentences. Include the smallest and largest values, a typical value such as the mode or median, and anything interesting you notice. Then draw a conclusion and, if it makes sense, make a prediction.

Here is a summary of the netball scores. In nine matches, the team scored between 9 and 18 goals. The most common score was 15 and the median was 14. The team scored 15 or more goals in 4 of the 9 matches. We conclude that the team usually scores about 14 or 15 goals. We predict that in its next match it will probably score about 14 or 15 goals, though it could score as few as 9 or as many as 18.

A prediction is a sensible guess based on the data, not a promise. The more data we have, the better our predictions.

Here is a second summary, for the fitness marks. Six learners scored between 12 and 19 out of 20. The median mark was 16, and no mark appeared more than once, so there is no mode. Every learner scored more than half of 20. We conclude that the group is fairly fit. If a seventh learner did the same test, we would predict a mark of about 15 to 17. Notice how each summary uses the actual numbers from the data, so a reader can check every sentence.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: finding the median without ordering the data; the middle of 12, 15, 9, 15, 18, 11, 15, 10, 14 as written is 18, but in order the median is 14. Two: saying the mode is the biggest value, 18; the mode is the most common value, 15. Three: giving the mode as 3, which is how many times 15 appears; the mode is the value, 15. Four: choosing one of the two middle values for an even number of values; the median of 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19 is halfway between 15 and 17, which is 16.

## Subtopic: Tell the Story

Scores from 9 to 18. Usually about 14 or 15.

Next match: probably about 14 or 15.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The mode is the value that occurs most often, and there may be one mode, more than one, or none. The median is the middle value once the data is in order; for an even number of values, it is halfway between the two middle ones. A summary gives the smallest and largest values, a typical value, and what you notice, and ends with a conclusion and a sensible prediction.
