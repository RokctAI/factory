### subtopic_1 — Mass, Capacity and Time
**Assistant:** How do I find a time interval like 08:45 to 14:15 without mistakes?

**Tutor:** Count on in easy jumps instead of subtracting. Start at 08:45 and jump in whole hours as far as you can without passing 14:15. From 08:45, five whole hours takes you to 13:45. One more hour would take you to 14:45, which is too far. Then count the minutes from 13:45 to 14:15: 15 minutes takes you to 14:00 and another 15 minutes takes you to 14:15, which is 30 minutes. So the interval is 5 hours 30 minutes. The reason not to subtract the times like decimals is that a clock does not work in tens. If you subtract 8,45 from 14,15 as if they were decimals, you get 5,70, which looks like 5 hours 70 minutes. But there are only 60 minutes in an hour, so that answer cannot be right. A number line with the hours marked on it makes counting on even easier. You can also check your answer by adding it back on: 08:45 plus 5 hours is 13:45, and plus 30 minutes is 14:15.

### subtopic_2 — Data Handling
**Assistant:** How do I know whether to use 1 000 or 60 when I convert?

**Tutor:** It depends on what you are measuring. Mass, capacity and length in the metric system work in thousands. 1 kg is 1 000 g, 1 litre is 1 000 ml, 1 kl is 1 000 litres, and 1 km is 1 000 m. So for these, you multiply or divide by 1 000. To change 2,5 kg to grams, multiply by 1 000 to get 2 500 g. To change 20 000 ml to litres, divide by 1 000 to get 20 litres. Time is different, because it works in sixties for hours, minutes and seconds. 1 hour is 60 minutes and 1 minute is 60 seconds. So 1 minute 25 seconds is 60 plus 25, which is 85 seconds. Time also has other jumps: 24 hours in a day, 7 days in a week, 10 years in a decade and 100 years in a century. A good check after converting to a smaller unit is that the number should get bigger, because you need more of the small units. 2,5 kg became 2 500 g, which is a bigger number, so that makes sense.

### subtopic_3 — Number Fundamentals
**Assistant:** What should I do to get ready for the end-of-year test?

**Tutor:** Start by making a list of the topics, such as whole numbers, fractions, decimals, measurement and data, and tick the ones you feel sure about. Spend most of your time on the ones you are not sure about. For each topic, learn the key facts first, like 1 kg is 1 000 g, 1 hour is 60 minutes, and one fifth is 0,2, which is 20 per cent. Then practise a few questions of each kind. Do not only read your notes; working out answers yourself is how you really learn. When you get something wrong, find the mistake and fix it, because that is the best kind of practice. During the test, read each question carefully and underline what it asks. Estimate first, so you know roughly what the answer should be. After calculating, check with the inverse operation: if R12 485 minus R4 790 is R7 695, then R7 695 plus R4 790 should be R12 485. Always write the unit. Finally, get a good night's sleep before the test. A rested brain works much better than a tired one.
