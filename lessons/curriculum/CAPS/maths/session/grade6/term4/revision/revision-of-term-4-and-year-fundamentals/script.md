# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 4 topics: mass, capacity and volume, time, and data handling, together with the number skills of the whole year, including place value, rounding, the four operations, fractions, decimals and percentages, and ratio and rate. The anchor is the year-end fun day at a primary school in eMalahleni. The day has a fruit stall, a water cooler, a sack race with a stopwatch, a tally board of favourite events and a money box for the profit. The governing idea is that each skill this year has a few key facts and a clear method, and that measurement, data and number all work together when we solve problems. Converting measurements, calculating time intervals, reading and summarising data, and calculating with whole numbers, fractions and decimals are all examinable.

## Subtopic: Mass, Capacity and Time

For mass, remember that 1 kg is 1 000 g. A bag of oranges at the fruit stall has a mass of 2,5 kg. To change kilograms to grams, multiply by 1 000, so 2,5 kg is 2 500 g. Three bags have a mass of 3 times 2,5 kg, which is 7,5 kg. A kitchen scale shows a bunch of bananas at 750 g, which is 0,75 kg, or three quarters of a kilogram.

For capacity, remember that 1 litre is 1 000 ml and 1 kilolitre is 1 000 litres. The water cooler holds 20 litres, which is 20 000 ml. Each paper cup holds 200 ml, so the cooler fills 20 000 divided by 200, which is 100 cups. A bottle of juice holds 1,5 litres, which is 1 500 ml. The school's water tank holds 5 kl, which is 5 000 litres.

To compare or order measurements, change them to the same unit first. Which is more, 1,2 kg or 1 050 g? 1,2 kg is 1 200 g, so 1,2 kg is more.

For time, remember that 1 hour is 60 minutes and 1 minute is 60 seconds. The fun day starts at 08:45 and ends at 14:15. In 12-hour time, 14:15 is 2:15 p.m. To find how long the day lasts, count on: from 08:45 to 13:45 is 5 hours, and from 13:45 to 14:15 is 30 minutes. So the day lasts 5 hours 30 minutes.

The stopwatch shows that the winner of the sack race took 1 minute 25 seconds. In seconds, that is 60 plus 25, which is 85 seconds.

Calendars count days. The fun day is on 4 December. Christmas Day, 25 December, is 25 minus 4, which is 21 days later, or exactly 3 weeks.

The questions for this section are with you now: mass, capacity and time.

## Subtopic: Data Handling

Data handling follows a cycle: ask a question, collect the data, organise it, represent it in a graph, and then analyse and summarise it.

On the tally board, 40 learners chose their favourite event. Tug of war got 10 votes, the sack race got 16, the egg-and-spoon race got 8 and the three-legged race got 6. Check the total: 10 plus 16 plus 8 plus 6 is 40. In tally marks, each group of five is four lines with a line across, so 16 is three groups of five and one more line.

A bar graph shows this data with one bar for each event. With a scale of 2 votes per block, the sack race bar is 8 blocks tall. A pie chart shows each event as a fraction of the whole circle. Tug of war got 10 out of 40 votes, which is one quarter, or 25 per cent, of the pie. The sack race got 16 out of 40, which is two fifths, or 40 per cent. The egg-and-spoon race is 20 per cent and the three-legged race is 15 per cent. The percentages add up to 100.

The sack race times of seven runners, in seconds, are 14, 18, 12, 15, 18, 20 and 16. In order they are 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 18, 20. The mode is 18 seconds, because it appears twice. The median is the middle value, the fourth one, which is 16 seconds.

A short summary of the event votes: the sack race was the most popular event, with 40 per cent of the votes, and the three-legged race was the least popular. We predict that the sack race will be popular again next year.

The questions for this section are with you now: data handling.

## Subtopic: Number Fundamentals

Place value and rounding come first. The fun day raised R12 485. The digit 2 is in the thousands place, so its value is 2 000. Rounded to the nearest 100, the amount is R12 500. Rounded to the nearest 1 000, it is R12 000, because 485 is less than 500.

The four operations solve most problems. The costs for the day were R4 790, so the profit was R12 485 minus R4 790, which is R7 695. Check by adding: R7 695 plus R4 790 is R12 485. The hall has 24 tables with 8 chairs each, which is 24 times 8, or 192 chairs.

Fractions, decimals and percentages are different ways of writing parts of a whole. One fifth of the profit goes to the sports fund. One fifth of R7 695 is R7 695 divided by 5, which is R1 539. One fifth is the same as 0,2 and as 20 per cent. A hot dog costs R12,50, and 4 hot dogs cost 4 times R12,50, which is R50. One half is 0,5, which is 50 per cent.

Ratio and rate come up too. The juice is mixed 1 : 4, concentrate to water. Three hot dogs for R36 is a rate of R12 per hot dog, which is a better buy than R12,50 each.

To prepare for the test, practise a few questions from each topic, check your answers using inverse operations, and always write the unit.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: changing 2,5 kg to 250 g; multiply by 1 000 to get 2 500 g. Two: subtracting the times 08:45 and 14:15 like decimals and getting 5 hours 70 minutes; there are 60 minutes in an hour, so the day lasts 5 hours 30 minutes. Three: taking the middle of the unordered sack race times, 15; in order the median is 16 seconds. Four: rounding R12 485 to the nearest 1 000 as R13 000 by rounding in steps; look at the hundreds digit, 4, so it rounds down to R12 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: number fundamentals.
