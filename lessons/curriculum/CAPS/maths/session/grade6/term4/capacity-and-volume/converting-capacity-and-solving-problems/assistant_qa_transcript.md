### subtopic_1 — Bigger Units to Smaller Units
**Assistant:** How is converting capacity like converting mass?

**Tutor:** It follows exactly the same pattern, because both use the number 1 000 and the same word parts. Kilo means one thousand, so a kilolitre is 1 000 litres, just as a kilogram is 1 000 grams. Milli means one thousandth, so a millilitre is one thousandth of a litre, and 1 000 millilitres make a litre. To change from a bigger unit to a smaller unit, multiply by 1 000, because you need more of the small pieces. 1,5 l is 1 500 ml, just as 1,5 kg is 1 500 g. To change from a smaller unit to a bigger unit, divide by 1 000. 2 500 ml is 2,5 l, just as 2 500 g is 2,5 kg. Even the fractions match: 250 ml is a quarter of a litre and 250 g is a quarter of a kilogram. In fact, water links the two. One litre of water has a mass of about 1 kg, and one millilitre of water has a mass of about 1 g. So once you know how to convert mass, you already know how to convert capacity. Only length is different, because centimetres and metres are linked by 100.

### subtopic_2 — Smaller Units to Bigger Units
**Assistant:** How long will 5 kl of water last if the school uses 1 000 l a day?

**Tutor:** First change both amounts into the same unit. The tank holds 5 kl, and 1 kl is 1 000 l, so the tank holds 5 000 l. Now the question is how many lots of 1 000 l fit into 5 000 l. 5 000 divided by 1 000 is 5, so the water lasts 5 days. Check by multiplying: 5 days times 1 000 l a day is 5 000 l, which is the whole tank. If the school saves water and uses only 800 l a day, the tank lasts longer. 5 000 divided by 800 is 6 with 200 l left over. So it lasts 6 full days, and there is a little water for a seventh morning. This shows why saving water matters during a drought. Every litre saved makes the tank last longer. Small things add up: a dripping tap wastes 120 l a day, which is more than a tenth of the school's daily use. Fixing it, and turning taps off while brushing teeth, can give the school extra days of water before it rains again.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Water Problems
**Assistant:** Why do I divide 1 500 by 250 to find how many cups a bottle fills?

**Tutor:** Because you are finding how many cups of 250 ml fit into the bottle, and that is a grouping question: how many groups of 250 are in 1 500? First, make sure the units match. The bottle holds 1,5 l and the cups hold 250 ml, so change the bottle to millilitres: 1,5 l is 1 500 ml. Now divide: 1 500 divided by 250 is 6. So one bottle fills 6 cups. You can check this with a benchmark. 4 cups of 250 ml make 1 l, and half a litre is 2 more cups. 4 plus 2 is 6. Both ways agree. If you forgot to change the units and divided 1,5 by 250, you would get a tiny number that makes no sense, because a bottle obviously fills more than a small part of one cup. If you need to serve 30 learners, you need 30 divided by 6, which is 5 bottles. Always ask whether your answer is sensible in the real situation, and check that the units match before you divide.
