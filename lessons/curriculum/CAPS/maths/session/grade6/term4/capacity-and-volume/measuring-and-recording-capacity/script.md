# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers estimating and measuring capacity and volume using measuring spoons, cups and jugs, reading the scales on measuring jugs, and recording, comparing and ordering amounts in millilitres, litres and kilolitres. It builds on measuring mass in the previous lessons and on volume from Term 3. The anchor is the Mokoena family in Welkom making ginger beer for a family celebration. They measure ginger with tablespoons of 15 ml and sugar with cups of 250 ml, measure water in a 2 l jug, pour the ginger beer into 1,5 l bottles, and fill a pot from a garden water tank that holds 2,5 kl. The governing idea is that capacity is how much a container can hold and volume is how much space a liquid or object takes up, that 1 000 ml make 1 l and 1 000 l make 1 kl, that every jug must be read at eye level after working out what each mark is worth, and that amounts must be in the same unit before you compare them. Choosing units and instruments, reading measuring jugs, and comparing and ordering capacities are examinable.

## Subtopic: Units and Benchmarks

Capacity is the amount a container can hold. Volume is the amount of space that something takes up, such as the liquid inside the container. A 2 l jug has a capacity of 2 l, but if it is only half full, the volume of water in it is 1 l.

We measure capacity and the volume of liquids in three units. Millilitres, written ml, are for small amounts, like a spoon of medicine. Litres, written l, are for bottles, buckets and jugs. Kilolitres, written kl, are for very large amounts, like a water tank or a swimming pool.

The units are linked by 1 000. 1 000 ml make 1 l. 1 000 l make 1 kl.

Benchmarks help you estimate. A teaspoon holds 5 ml. A tablespoon holds 15 ml. A cup holds about 250 ml, so 4 cups make 1 l. A small cooldrink can holds about 330 ml. A large cooldrink bottle holds 2 l. A bucket holds about 10 l. A bath holds about 150 l. The garden tank holds 2,5 kl, which is 2 500 l.

Before measuring, the Mokoenas estimate. The big pot looks as if it holds about two large cooldrink bottles, so they estimate 4 l.

Labels help too. Look at the containers in a kitchen cupboard and you will see their capacity printed on them: 500 ml on a bottle of oil, 1 l on a carton of milk, 2 l on a cooldrink bottle.

## Subtopic: Measuring with Spoons, Cups and Jugs

Measuring spoons and cups hold fixed amounts. The recipe needs 3 tablespoons of grated ginger, which is 3 times 15 ml, or 45 ml. It needs 2 cups of sugar, which is 2 times 250 ml, or 500 ml.

A measuring jug has a scale on its side. First, work out what each small mark is worth. The Mokoenas' 2 l jug has numbers every 200 ml, and there are 3 small marks between each pair of numbers. The 3 marks split the 200 ml into 4 equal spaces, so each mark is 200 divided by 4, which is 50 ml. If the water reaches the second mark after 400 ml, the reading is 400 plus 100, which is 500 ml.

Read the jug correctly. Put it on a flat table and bend down so that your eyes are level with the top of the liquid. If you look from above or below, the reading will be wrong. The surface of water curves slightly where it touches the jug, so read the level at the flat middle part.

Some jugs show both millilitres and litres. On the 2 l jug, the 1 000 ml mark is also labelled 1 l.

To measure more than the jug holds, fill it several times and add. The pot needs 4 l of water. The jug holds 2 l, so Mr Mokoena fills it to the 2 l mark twice. To measure an amount like 2,5 l, he fills the jug once to 2 l and then again to the 500 ml mark.

## Subtopic: Recording, Comparing and Ordering

Always write the unit. 2 on its own could mean 2 ml, 2 l or 2 kl, which are very different amounts.

To compare, change into the same unit. Which holds more, a 1,5 l bottle or a 1 250 ml jug? 1,5 l is 1 500 ml, which is more than 1 250 ml, so the bottle holds more.

Half a litre is 500 ml, a quarter of a litre is 250 ml and three quarters is 750 ml.

To order, change all the amounts to millilitres. Order 750 ml, 2 l and 1 800 ml from least to most. In millilitres they are 750 ml, 2 000 ml and 1 800 ml. So the order is 750 ml, 1 800 ml, 2 l.

The recipe uses 4 l of water and 250 ml of lemon juice. In millilitres that is 4 000 ml plus 250 ml, which is 4 250 ml.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: reading a jug from above; bend down and read it at eye level. Two: saying 1 250 ml is more than 1,5 l because 1 250 is bigger than 1,5; change to the same unit first. Three: saying 3 tablespoons is 3 ml; each tablespoon is 15 ml, so it is 45 ml. Four: saying a half-full 2 l bottle contains 2 l; its capacity is 2 l, but the volume of liquid in it is 1 l.
