# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between grams and kilograms, including common fraction and decimal fraction forms to two decimal places, and solving problems in contexts involving mass. It builds on measuring and recording mass in the previous lesson and on converting length earlier this year. The anchor is Mrs Ramathuba's farm stall near Tzaneen. She packs boxes of 12 avocados of about 250 g each, sells mangoes at R24 per kilogram, weighs out bags of macadamia nuts, and loads crates of 18 kg onto a bakkie that can carry 500 kg. The governing idea is that 1 kg is 1 000 g, so we multiply by 1 000 to change kilograms into grams and divide by 1 000 to change grams into kilograms, and that masses must be in the same unit before we add, subtract or compare them. Converting in both directions, writing masses as decimals and fractions, and solving mass problems are examinable.

## Subtopic: Kilograms to Grams

To change kilograms to grams, multiply by 1 000, because each kilogram is 1 000 grams. The answer must be a bigger number, because grams are smaller pieces.

A bag of mangoes is 3,5 kg. 3 kg is 3 000 g, and 0,5 kg is half a kilogram, which is 500 g. So 3,5 kg is 3 500 g.

Decimals with two places work the same way. 1,25 kg is 1 kg and 25 hundredths of a kilogram. One hundredth of 1 000 g is 10 g, so 25 hundredths is 250 g. So 1,25 kg is 1 250 g. In the same way, 0,75 kg is 750 g and 0,05 kg is 50 g.

Fractions of a kilogram are easy if you know the common ones. 1/2 kg is 500 g. 1/4 kg is 250 g. 3/4 kg is 750 g. 1/10 kg is 100 g. 1/5 kg is 200 g. And 2 and 1/2 kg is 2 500 g.

On a place value chart, multiplying by 1 000 moves every digit three places to the left. You already know that times 10 moves one place and times 100 moves two places, so times 1 000 moves three. In 3,5 the 3 units become 3 thousands and the 5 tenths become 5 hundreds, giving 3 500.

## Subtopic: Grams to Kilograms

To change grams to kilograms, divide by 1 000. The answer must be a smaller number, because kilograms are bigger pieces.

A crate of avocados has a mass of 4 750 g. 4 000 g is 4 kg. The other 750 g is 750 thousandths of a kilogram, which is 3/4 kg or 0,75 kg. So 4 750 g is 4,75 kg, or 4 and 3/4 kg.

A bag of nuts is 600 g. That is 600 thousandths of a kilogram, which is 6 tenths, so it is 0,6 kg, or 3/5 kg in simplest form. A bag of 250 g is 0,25 kg, which is 1/4 kg. 1 200 g is 1,2 kg, and 3 400 g is 3,4 kg, which is 3 and 2/5 kg.

Writing a mass in two units is also useful: 4 750 g is 4 kg 750 g.

Check each conversion by going back the other way. 4,75 kg times 1 000 should give 4 750 g again, and it does. If your answer to a grams-to-kilograms question is bigger than the number you started with, you multiplied instead of dividing.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing grams into kilograms, as decimals and fractions.

## Subtopic: Solving Mass Problems

How heavy is a box of 12 avocados at 250 g each? 12 times 250 g is 3 000 g, which is 3 kg.

A customer buys 2,5 kg of mangoes, a 3 kg box of avocados and 750 g of nuts. What is the total mass? The units are different, so change them first. 750 g is 0,75 kg. Then 2,5 plus 3 plus 0,75 is 6,25 kg. Or work in grams: 2 500 plus 3 000 plus 750 is 6 250 g, which is the same.

The mangoes cost R24 per kilogram. 2,5 kg costs 2 times R24, which is R48, plus half of R24, which is R12. That is R60.

A bag of macadamia nuts costs R45 for 250 g. A kilogram is 4 times 250 g, so a kilogram of nuts costs 4 times R45, which is R180.

Mrs Ramathuba shares 6 kg of nuts equally into 8 bags. 6 kg is 6 000 g, and 6 000 divided by 8 is 750 g per bag.

The bakkie can carry 500 kg, and each crate has a mass of 18 kg. How many full crates can it carry? 500 divided by 18 is 27 with 14 left over. So it can carry 27 crates, with a mass of 27 times 18, which is 486 kg. A 28th crate would take the load over 500 kg. Here the remainder tells us to round down.

A 5 kg bag of flour has 1,75 kg used. 5 minus 1,75 leaves 3,25 kg.

For every mass problem, follow the same steps. Read the question and note the units. Change them into the same unit. Choose the operation. Estimate, calculate, and check that the answer makes sense.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 3,5 kg as 350 g by multiplying by 100; there are 1 000 g in a kilogram, so it is 3 500 g. Two: writing 4 750 g as 47,5 kg by dividing by 100; divide by 1 000 to get 4,75 kg. Three: writing 3/4 kg as 75 g; three quarters of 1 000 g is 750 g. Four: adding 2,5 kg and 750 g to get 752,5; change to the same unit first, and the total is 3,25 kg.
