### subtopic_1 — Kilograms to Grams
**Assistant:** Why do I multiply by 1 000 and not by 100 to change kilograms into grams?

**Tutor:** Because the word kilo means one thousand. A kilogram is one thousand grams, just as a kilometre is one thousand metres. Some learners mix this up with centimetres, where there are 100 in a metre. But grams and kilograms are linked by 1 000. So 3,5 kg is 3,5 times 1 000, which is 3 500 g. If you multiply by 100 instead, you get 350 g, which is only about the mass of a can of beans. A quick reality check catches this mistake. 3,5 kg of mangoes is a big, heavy bag, a bit more than three bags of sugar. 350 g would be only a couple of mangoes. Always ask whether your answer makes sense. A good memory trick is to link the units in a table: kilo means 1 000 for kilometres, kilograms and later kilolitres. Centi means one hundredth, as in centimetres, where 100 make a metre. Milli means one thousandth, as in millimetres and millilitres. Knowing what the start of each unit name means helps you choose the right number every time.

### subtopic_2 — Grams to Kilograms
**Assistant:** How do I change 4 750 g into kilograms as a fraction?

**Tutor:** Split the grams into whole kilograms and what is left over, then turn the left-over part into a fraction of 1 000 g. 4 750 g has 4 000 g, which is 4 whole kilograms. That leaves 750 g. As a fraction of a kilogram, 750 g is 750 out of 1 000, which is 750/1 000. Simplify it: divide the top and bottom by 250 to get 3/4. Or simply remember the benchmark: 750 g is three quarters of a kilogram, because a quarter is 250 g and three quarters is 3 times 250 g. So 4 750 g is 4 and 3/4 kg. As a decimal, 750 g is 0,75 kg, so it is also 4,75 kg. Check that the decimal and the fraction agree: 3/4 is 0,75, so they do. The benchmarks are worth learning by heart because they come up so often on labels and in recipes: 250 g is a quarter of a kilogram, 500 g is a half and 750 g is three quarters. 100 g is a tenth and 200 g is a fifth.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Mass Problems
**Assistant:** Why does the bakkie carry 27 crates and not 28 when 500 divided by 18 is nearly 28?

**Tutor:** Because the question asks how many full crates can be loaded without going over 500 kg, and a crate cannot be cut in half. 500 divided by 18 is 27 with 14 left over. That means 27 crates have a mass of 27 times 18, which is 486 kg, and there are 14 kg of space left. One more crate weighs 18 kg, and 18 kg will not fit into 14 kg of space. If Mrs Ramathuba loaded a 28th crate, the load would be 504 kg, which is over the limit and unsafe. So the answer is 27, and we round down. Other problems need you to round up instead. If she needs to deliver 500 kg of fruit in crates that hold 18 kg each, she needs 28 crates, because 27 crates would only hold 486 kg and some fruit would be left behind. So the remainder does not have a single rule. Read the question carefully and think about the real situation: is it asking how many fit, or how many are needed? That tells you whether to round down or up.
