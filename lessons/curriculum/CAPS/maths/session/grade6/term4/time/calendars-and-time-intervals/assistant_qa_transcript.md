### subtopic_1 — Reading Calendars
**Assistant:** Why can I not just subtract times like ordinary numbers?

**Tutor:** Because the clock does not count in hundreds. When you subtract ordinary numbers, every column is worth ten times the one to its right, so 100 is the next step after 99. But an hour has only 60 minutes. After 59 minutes, the clock goes to the next hour, not to 60 or 99. If you subtract 07:30 from 14:15 as if they were the numbers 730 and 1 415, you get 685, which you might read as 6 hours and 85 minutes. That is impossible, because 85 minutes is more than an hour. The real answer is 6 hours and 45 minutes. The safest way is to count on, using the clock. Start at 07:30 and jump to the next whole hour, 08:00: that is 30 minutes. Then jump in whole hours to 14:00: that is 6 hours. Then add the last 15 minutes. Add up the jumps: 6 hours and 30 plus 15 minutes, which is 6 hours 45 minutes. A number line drawn like a timeline makes this easy to see. You can check by counting forward: 07:30 plus 6 hours is 13:30, plus 45 minutes is 14:15.

### subtopic_2 — Intervals in Hours, Minutes and Seconds
**Assistant:** How do I find what day of the week a date falls on?

**Tutor:** Use a date you already know and jump in weeks. The same weekday comes around every 7 days, so if you add 7, 14, 21 or 28 days, you land on the same weekday. The class knows that 1 September 2025 is a Monday. Adding 7 days gives 8 September, also a Monday. Adding 7 again gives 15 September, and again gives 22 September, both Mondays. Heritage Day is 24 September, which is 2 days after 22 September. Monday plus 2 days is Wednesday, so Heritage Day 2025 is on a Wednesday. If the date is in the next month, remember how many days the month has. September has 30 days, so 29 September is a Monday, and 30 September is a Tuesday. That means 1 October is a Wednesday. From there you can keep jumping in sevens through October. Calendars printed on the wall do this for you, but knowing how it works helps you plan ahead, like checking whether a birthday or a public holiday falls on a weekend. It also helps you spot mistakes on a home-made calendar.

### subtopic_3 — Years, Decades and Centuries
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a decade, a century and a millennium?

**Tutor:** They are three units for long stretches of time, and each is ten times the one before. A decade is 10 years. A century is 100 years, which is 10 decades. A millennium is 1 000 years, which is 10 centuries. They work a bit like tens, hundreds and thousands on a place value chart. We use them because counting very long periods in single years becomes awkward. For example, South Africa's first democratic election was in 1994, and 2024 was 30 years later, so we can say three decades of democracy. Nelson Mandela was born in 1918, so 2018 was one hundred years later, his centenary, or one century. People often talk about the 1990s, which means the decade of years from 1990 to 1999. The twentieth century means the years from 1901 to 2000, which is a bit confusing because its years mostly start with 19. To work out how many decades or centuries have passed, subtract the years first, and then divide by 10 for decades or by 100 for centuries. 72 years is 7 decades and 2 years.
