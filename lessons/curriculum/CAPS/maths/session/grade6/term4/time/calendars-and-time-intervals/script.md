# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers reading calendars, calculating time intervals in seconds, minutes and hours, and calculating longer intervals in days, weeks, months, years, decades and centuries. It builds on reading 12-hour and 24-hour time in the previous lesson. The anchor is a Grade 6 class planning its Term 3 and Term 4 calendar for 2025, from Heritage Day on Wednesday 24 September to the Day of Reconciliation on 16 December, and making a history timeline that includes Nelson Mandela's birth in 1918, his release from prison in 1990, and the first democratic election in 1994. The governing idea is that time does not work in tens and hundreds like ordinary numbers: there are 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day and 7 days in a week, so we count on in steps that fit the clock and the calendar. Reading calendars, calculating intervals with hours and minutes, and converting between long units of time are examinable.

## Subtopic: Reading Calendars

A calendar shows the days, weeks and months of a year. There are 7 days in a week and 12 months in a year. Most years have 365 days, about 52 weeks. Every fourth year is a leap year with 366 days, when February has 29 days instead of 28.

The months have different lengths. April, June, September and November have 30 days. February has 28 or 29. All the others have 31. A rhyme helps: thirty days has September, April, June and November.

On the class calendar, 1 September 2025 is a Monday. To find the day of the week for another date, add 7 days at a time, because the same weekday comes around every 7 days. So 8, 15 and 22 September are also Mondays. Then 23 September is a Tuesday and 24 September, Heritage Day, is a Wednesday.

Dates can be written in different ways: 24 September 2025, or in numbers as 2025-09-24, with the year first, then the month, then the day.

To find how many days there are between two dates, count on month by month. From 24 September to 16 December: there are 6 more days in September, then 31 days in October, 30 in November and 16 in December. 6 plus 31 plus 30 plus 16 is 83 days.

Be careful about whether to count the first day. From Monday 1 September to Wednesday 3 September is 2 days later, because you count the jumps, not the dates. But if a school trip runs from Monday 1 September to Wednesday 3 September and includes both days, it lasts 3 days. Read the question to decide.

## Subtopic: Intervals in Hours, Minutes and Seconds

A time interval is how long something lasts, from a start time to an end time.

The school day runs from 07:30 to 14:15. You cannot just subtract the numbers, because an hour has 60 minutes, not 100. Count on instead. From 07:30 to 08:00 is 30 minutes. From 08:00 to 14:00 is 6 hours. From 14:00 to 14:15 is 15 minutes. Add them: 6 hours and 45 minutes.

A class outing starts at 08:45 and the bus returns at 10:20. From 08:45 to 09:00 is 15 minutes. From 09:00 to 10:00 is 1 hour. From 10:00 to 10:20 is 20 minutes. That is 1 hour and 35 minutes.

Check by counting forward: 08:45 plus 1 hour is 09:45, and 35 minutes more is 10:20.

To find an end time, count on from the start. A movie starts at 14:50 and lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. 14:50 plus 1 hour is 15:50. Plus 10 minutes is 16:00. Plus the last 35 minutes is 16:35.

Crossing midnight works the same way. The overnight bus leaves at 21:45 and arrives at 06:30. From 21:45 to 22:00 is 15 minutes, from 22:00 to 06:00 is 8 hours, and from 06:00 to 06:30 is 30 minutes. That is 8 hours and 45 minutes.

For seconds: 3 minutes and 20 seconds is 3 times 60 plus 20, which is 200 seconds.

A timeline, drawn like a number line with the hours marked on it, makes these jumps easy to see. Draw an arrow for each jump and write its length above it, then add the jumps.

## Subtopic: Years, Decades and Centuries

For long stretches of time, we use bigger units. A decade is 10 years. A century is 100 years. A millennium is 1 000 years.

On the history timeline, Nelson Mandela was born in 1918 and released from prison in 1990. 1990 minus 1918 is 72, so he was released 72 years after he was born. The year 2018 marked 100 years since his birth: one century, which is why it was celebrated as his centenary.

South Africa's first democratic election was in 1994. From 1994 to 2024 is 30 years, which is 3 decades.

To change between units, multiply or divide. 2 hours is 120 minutes. 3 weeks is 21 days. Half a century is 50 years. 4 decades is 40 years.

Your own age can be measured in all these units. A learner who is 12 years old has lived for more than one decade, for 144 months and for about 4 380 days.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting 07:30 from 14:15 as ordinary numbers and getting 6 hours 85 minutes; an hour has only 60 minutes, so count on to get 6 hours 45 minutes. Two: forgetting that months have different lengths, and counting every month as 30 days. Three: saying a decade is 100 years; a decade is 10 years and a century is 100. Four: saying February always has 28 days; in a leap year it has 29.
