### subtopic_1 — Why We Have Time Zones
**Assistant:** Why are places to the east ahead of us in time?

**Tutor:** Because the Earth spins from west to east, so places to the east turn towards the sun before we do. Imagine standing on a spinning ball with a lamp shining on it. The side turning towards the lamp sees morning first. Beijing is far to the east of South Africa, so it reaches morning, noon and evening about 6 hours before we do. That is why, when it is 14:00 in Johannesburg, it is already 20:00 in Beijing, and people there are having supper. Places to the west, like São Paulo in Brazil, turn towards the sun later than we do. When it is 14:00 here, it is only 09:00 there, and people are just starting work. Each time zone sets its clocks so that noon happens when the sun is roughly at its highest. A quick memory trick: the sun rises in the east, so the east gets the day first and is ahead. To find a time in the east, add hours. To find a time in the west, subtract hours. Look at the time zone map each time, because the number of hours is different for each place.

### subtopic_2 — Calculating the Time Somewhere Else
**Assistant:** How do I find the arrival time of a flight in another time zone?

**Tutor:** Do it in two steps, and do not mix them up. Step one: find the landing time in your own time zone by adding the flying time to the departure time. Ayanda's uncle leaves Johannesburg at 10:30 and flies for 8 hours. 10:30 plus 8 hours is 18:30, South African time. At that moment, the clocks back home in Johannesburg say 18:30. Step two: change that time into the local time of the place where you land. Dubai is 2 hours ahead of South Africa, so add 2 hours: 20:30, Dubai time. That is what the clocks at Dubai airport show when he walks out. Notice that the flight itself still took exactly 8 hours. It only looks like 10 hours if you compare the Johannesburg clock at take-off with the Dubai clock at landing. Coming home, the opposite happens. If he leaves Dubai at 09:00 Dubai time and flies 8 hours, it is 17:00 in Dubai when he lands, which is 15:00 in Johannesburg. So the trip home looks shorter on the clocks, even though the flight is just as long.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Time Problems
**Assistant:** What happens when adding hours takes me past midnight?

**Tutor:** The time starts again from 00:00, and the date moves to the next day. When it is 20:00 in Johannesburg, Tokyo is 7 hours ahead. If you add 7 to 20 you get 27, but there is no 27:00 on a clock. Instead, count on: from 20:00 to midnight is 4 hours. That leaves 3 more hours to add, so the time is 03:00. Because you went past midnight, it is now the next day in Tokyo. If it is Saturday evening in Johannesburg, it is early Sunday morning in Tokyo. You can also subtract 24 from 27 to get 3, which gives the same answer. Going backwards works the same way. At 02:00 on Saturday in Johannesburg, São Paulo is 5 hours behind. Count back 2 hours to midnight, then 3 more hours into Friday evening: 21:00 on Friday. Forgetting to change the day can cause real problems, for example if you are planning to phone someone or to watch a live match. Always check whether your answer crosses midnight, and write the day as well as the time when it does.
