# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers reading, telling and writing time on analogue and digital clocks and watches, in 12-hour format with a.m. and p.m. and in 24-hour format, and reading stopwatches. It builds on telling the time in earlier grades. The anchor is the Mthembu family travelling from Johannesburg to Durban. Their bus ticket says it departs at 21:45. Gogo's old watch has hands, while Sipho's watch is digital. School usually starts at 07:30, and on the beach, Sipho's sister times a race with a stopwatch that shows 01:05,32. The governing idea is that the 12-hour clock uses a.m. and p.m. to tell morning from afternoon and night, while the 24-hour clock counts the hours of the whole day from 00:00 to 23:59, so that after noon we add 12 to the hour. Reading analogue clocks, changing between 12-hour and 24-hour time, and reading digital clocks and stopwatches are examinable.

## Subtopic: Reading Analogue Clocks

An analogue clock has hands. The short hand is the hour hand. The long hand is the minute hand. Many clocks also have a thin second hand that sweeps around once every minute.

The minute hand goes all the way around in one hour, which is 60 minutes. There are 12 numbers on the clock, so each number stands for 5 minutes for the minute hand. When the minute hand points to 3, it is 15 minutes past the hour; at 6, it is 30 minutes past; at 9, it is 45 minutes past. The small marks between the numbers are single minutes.

The hour hand moves slowly from one number to the next during the hour. At 7:30 it is halfway between 7 and 8. Just before 8 o'clock, it is nearly on 8, but it is still 7-something.

We can say times in words. 7:15 is quarter past 7. 7:30 is half past 7. 7:45 is quarter to 8, because it is 15 minutes before 8. 7:50 is ten to 8.

Gogo's watch shows the minute hand on 10 and the hour hand nearly on 8. The minute hand on 10 means 50 minutes past. The hour hand has not reached 8 yet, so the hour is still 7. The time is 7:50, or ten to 8.

The second hand helps with short times. It goes around once a minute, so each number is 5 seconds for the second hand, just as it is 5 minutes for the minute hand. When Gogo boils an egg for 4 minutes, she watches the minute hand move from one mark to the fourth mark after it.

Some clocks use Roman numerals instead of ordinary numbers. XII is 12, III is 3, VI is 6 and IX is 9. The hands work in exactly the same way.

## Subtopic: 12-Hour and 24-Hour Time

The 12-hour clock goes around twice a day. To show which half of the day we mean, we write a.m. for times from midnight until noon, and p.m. for times from noon until midnight. School starts at 7:30 a.m. The bus leaves at 9:45 p.m.

The 24-hour clock counts straight through the day, from 00:00 at midnight to 23:59 one minute before the next midnight. It always uses four digits, with a colon between the hours and the minutes. Bus, train and flight timetables use it, because there is no chance of mixing up morning and evening.

For morning times, the 24-hour time looks like the 12-hour time with a zero in front if needed: 7:30 a.m. is 07:30. Noon is 12:00.

For afternoon and evening times, add 12 to the hour. 2:15 p.m. is 14:15. 6:05 p.m. is 18:05. 9:45 p.m. is 21:45. 11:30 p.m. is 23:30.

To change back, subtract 12 from any hour bigger than 12 and write p.m. 16:20 is 4:20 p.m. 21:45 is 9:45 p.m.

Midnight is 00:00. Half an hour after midnight is 00:30, which is 12:30 a.m.

Here is the Mthembu family's evening in both formats. They eat supper at 6:30 p.m., which is 18:30. They leave home at 8:15 p.m., which is 20:15. The bus departs at 21:45, which is 9:45 p.m. They fall asleep on the bus at 23:10, which is 11:10 p.m.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing between 12-hour and 24-hour time.

## Subtopic: Digital Clocks, Watches and Stopwatches

A digital clock shows the time in numbers. Some show 12-hour time with a small a.m. or p.m. sign; others show 24-hour time. Sipho's digital watch shows 21:45 as the bus pulls out.

A stopwatch measures how long something takes. It usually shows minutes, then seconds, then hundredths of a second. Sipho's sister times a race on the beach, and the stopwatch shows 01:05,32. That means 1 minute, 5 seconds and 32 hundredths of a second. Since 1 minute is 60 seconds, the race took 65,32 seconds altogether.

Remember that there are 60 seconds in a minute and 60 minutes in an hour, not 100. So 65 seconds is 1 minute and 5 seconds, not 65 minutes or 6,5 minutes.

When comparing stopwatch times, the smaller time is faster. 12,45 seconds is faster than 12,54 seconds.

Many phones and microwaves show digital time. A microwave timer showing 2:30 means 2 minutes and 30 seconds, which is two and a half minutes, not 2,3 minutes. Always check whether the numbers show hours and minutes or minutes and seconds.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: reading 7:50 as 8:50 because the hour hand is nearly on 8; the hour has not changed until the minute hand reaches 12. Two: writing 2:15 p.m. as 02:15; afternoon times need 12 added, so it is 14:15. Three: writing half past midnight as 12:30 in 24-hour time; in 24-hour time, the hour after midnight is 00, so it is 00:30. Four: reading 21:45 as 11:45 p.m. by subtracting 10; subtract 12, and it is 9:45 p.m.

The questions for this section are with you now: digital clocks and stopwatches.
