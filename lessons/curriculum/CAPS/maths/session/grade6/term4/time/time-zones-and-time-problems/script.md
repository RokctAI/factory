## Subtopic: Why We Have Time Zones

The Earth spins around once every 24 hours, turning from west to east. As it turns, different parts of the world face the sun. When it is noon in South Africa, with the sun high in the sky, it is already evening in China and still early morning in Brazil.

If everyone in the world used the same clock, noon would be in the middle of the night for some people. So the world is divided into time zones, strips that run roughly from the North Pole to the South Pole. Each zone sets its clocks so that noon is roughly when the sun is highest.

Moving east, each zone is usually an hour ahead of the one before. Moving west, each zone is an hour behind. A time zone map shows each zone with a number, telling you how many hours it is ahead or behind.

South Africa uses one time zone for the whole country, called South African Standard Time. So Cape Town, Johannesburg and Durban all show the same time, even though the sun rises a little earlier in Durban.

Ghana is to the west of us, and its clocks are 2 hours behind ours. Kenya is to the north-east, and Nairobi is 1 hour ahead.

On a time zone map, the zones are usually shown as coloured strips with a number on each, such as plus 2 or minus 3, comparing them with a starting zone that passes through Greenwich in London. South Africa is plus 2. To compare two places, find the difference between their numbers. Beijing is plus 8 and South Africa is plus 2, so Beijing is 6 hours ahead of us.

## Subtopic: Calculating the Time Somewhere Else

To find the time in another place, start with our time, then add the hours if the place is ahead, or subtract them if it is behind.

It is 14:00 in Johannesburg. Dubai is 2 hours ahead, so add 2: it is 16:00 in Dubai. Beijing is 6 hours ahead, so it is 20:00 there. Accra is 2 hours behind, so subtract 2: it is 12:00 in Accra. São Paulo is 5 hours behind, so it is 09:00 there.

To find the difference between two other places, compare their differences from us. Dubai is 2 hours ahead of us and Beijing is 6 hours ahead, so Beijing is 4 hours ahead of Dubai.

Watch out for midnight. At 20:00 in Johannesburg, what time is it in Tokyo, 7 hours ahead? Count on: 4 hours takes you to midnight, and 3 more hours takes you to 03:00. But you have crossed midnight, so it is 03:00 the next day in Tokyo. Going backwards can also cross midnight. At 02:00 on Saturday in Johannesburg, it is 21:00 on Friday in São Paulo.

It also works the other way round. If it is 21:00 in Beijing, subtract 6 hours to get the time in Johannesburg: 15:00.

## Subtopic: Solving Time Problems

Ayanda's uncle flies from Johannesburg to Dubai. The flight leaves at 10:30 South African time and takes 8 hours. What is the local time in Dubai when he lands? First, find the landing time in South African time: 10:30 plus 8 hours is 18:30. Then change it to Dubai time by adding 2 hours: 20:30. The flight took 8 hours, but the clocks in Dubai say he arrived 10 hours after he left. The extra 2 hours are the time zone difference, not extra flying.

The soccer match in Tokyo kicks off at 19:00 Tokyo time. Tokyo is 7 hours ahead, so subtract 7 to get South African time: 12:00. The family can watch it at lunchtime.

Ayanda wants to phone her aunt in Beijing at 18:00 Beijing time, after the aunt finishes work. Subtract 6 hours: Ayanda must call at 12:00 South African time. She also wants to call her uncle in Accra at 19:00 South African time. That is 17:00 in Accra, which is fine.

Before you phone someone in another country, it is polite to check the time there. A call at 20:00 in Johannesburg reaches Beijing at 02:00 the next morning, when the aunt would be fast asleep.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting for a place that is ahead, saying Beijing is at 08:00 when it is 14:00 here; Beijing is east and ahead, so add 6 to get 20:00. Two: forgetting to change the day when the time passes midnight; 20:00 here is 03:00 the next day in Tokyo. Three: giving the landing time in Dubai as 18:30; that is South African time, so add 2 hours for Dubai time, 20:30. Four: thinking the time zone difference makes the flight longer; the flight still takes 8 hours.
