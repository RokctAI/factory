### subtopic_1 — Reading Analogue Clocks
**Assistant:** Why do we need the 24-hour clock when we already have a.m. and p.m.?

**Tutor:** Because the 24-hour clock makes it impossible to mix up morning and evening, which matters a lot when people are travelling. On a 12-hour clock, 9:45 can be in the morning or at night, and the only clue is the small a.m. or p.m. label. If someone forgets the label, or reads it wrongly, they could arrive at the bus station twelve hours early or twelve hours late. The 24-hour clock gives every minute of the day its own number. 09:45 is always the morning, and 21:45 is always the evening. That is why the Mthembu family's bus ticket says 21:45, and why train timetables, flight bookings, hospitals and the army all use 24-hour time. It also makes it easier to work out how long something takes, because you do not have to cross from a.m. to p.m. Phones and computers can usually be set to show either format. It is useful to be able to read both, because you will meet both in everyday life: the 12-hour clock in conversation and on many wall clocks, and the 24-hour clock on tickets and timetables.

### subtopic_2 — 12-Hour and 24-Hour Time
**Assistant:** How do I change 21:45 into 12-hour time?

**Tutor:** Look at the hour. If it is bigger than 12, the time is after noon, so subtract 12 from the hour and write p.m. 21 minus 12 is 9, so 21:45 is 9:45 p.m. The minutes do not change at all. A common mistake is to subtract 10 instead of 12, because it seems easier: 21 minus 10 is 11, giving 11:45 p.m. That would make the family two hours late for their bus. Always subtract 12, because the 24-hour clock carries on counting past 12 instead of starting again at 1. It helps to learn a few by heart: 13:00 is 1 p.m., 15:00 is 3 p.m., 18:00 is 6 p.m., 20:00 is 8 p.m. and 22:00 is 10 p.m. If the hour is 12, the time is just after noon, so 12:30 is 12:30 p.m. If the hour is 00, the time is just after midnight, so 00:30 is 12:30 a.m. And if the hour is from 01 to 11, it is a morning time: just drop the zero in front and write a.m., so 07:30 is 7:30 a.m.

### subtopic_3 — Digital Clocks, Watches and Stopwatches
**Assistant:** How do I read a stopwatch time like 01:05,32?

**Tutor:** Read it from left to right: minutes, then seconds, then hundredths of a second. The first part, 01, is the minutes: 1 minute. After the colon, 05 is the seconds: 5 seconds. After the comma, 32 is the hundredths of a second: 32 hundredths. So the race took 1 minute, 5 seconds and 32 hundredths of a second. To write it all in seconds, remember that 1 minute is 60 seconds, so add 60 and 5 to get 65, then add the hundredths: 65,32 seconds. Be careful: time does not work in hundreds like money. There are 60 seconds in a minute, not 100. So 65 seconds is 1 minute and 5 seconds, not 0,65 of a minute or 6,5 minutes. Hundredths of a second are used because races can be very close. If two runners finish in 12,45 seconds and 12,54 seconds, the stopwatch shows that the first runner won by 9 hundredths of a second, which is less than the blink of an eye. When you compare stopwatch times, remember that the smaller time is the faster one.
