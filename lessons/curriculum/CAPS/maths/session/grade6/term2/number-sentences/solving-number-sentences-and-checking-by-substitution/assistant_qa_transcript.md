### subtopic_1 — Solving by Inspection
**Assistant:** Is trial and improvement just guessing? Will I lose marks for it?

**Tutor:** It is not just guessing, and it is a method the curriculum asks you to use. Random guessing would be trying any number at all, like 3, then 91, then 18, with no plan. Trial and improvement uses each result to choose a better next try. For box times 24 equals 1 128, you start with a sensible guess, like 40, because 40 times 24 is easy to work out. The result, 960, is too small, which tells you to go higher. 50 gives 1 200, too big, so the answer is between 40 and 50. You can even notice that 1 128 is much closer to 1 200, so the answer is probably in the high 40s. That is clever reasoning, not luck. To earn marks, show your tries. A neat table with the try, the result, and whether it was too big or too small shows the teacher your thinking. Then finish with a substitution check to prove your answer. If you only write the final answer with no working, a teacher cannot see your method. With the table, even if you make a small slip, you can still earn marks for the method.

### subtopic_2 — Trial and Improvement
**Assistant:** How do I choose a good first guess?

**Tutor:** Use rounding and easy numbers. For box times 24 equals 1 128, think of 24 as about 25, and 1 128 as about 1 100 or 1 125. 25 times 40 is 1 000 and 25 times 45 is 1 125, so somewhere around 45 is a good start. Multiples of 10 are good first guesses because they are easy to work out: 24 times 40 is 960 and 24 times 50 is 1 200, which quickly shows the answer is between 40 and 50. Then look at the last digit. 1 128 ends in 8. Which numbers times 24 end in 8? 24 times 2 ends in 8, and 24 times 7 is 168, which also ends in 8. So the answer probably ends in 2 or 7. Between 40 and 50, that means 42 or 47. And because 1 128 is close to 1 200, 47 is the better choice. Test it: 47 times 24 is 1 128. Two or three tries is normal. The more you practise, the better your first guesses become, because you start to feel the size of numbers. It is a bit like guessing how many sweets are in a jar: experience makes you closer.

### subtopic_3 — Checking by Substitution
**Assistant:** Why should I check by substitution if I am already sure of my answer?

**Tutor:** Because being sure is not the same as being right, and a check takes only a few seconds. When you solve in your head, it is easy to slip, especially with inverse operations. In box minus 2 480 equals 3 520, a common slip is to subtract 2 480 from 3 520 and get 1 040. If you substitute 1 040, you work out 1 040 minus 2 480, which does not even give a whole number answer, so you know at once that something is wrong. Substituting 6 000 gives 6 000 minus 2 480, which is 3 520, so 6 000 is proved correct. Substitution is also how mathematicians prove that an answer works. It changes I think the answer is 47 into I know the answer is 47, because 47 times 24 really is 1 128. In high school you will solve harder equations with letters instead of boxes, and checking by substitution will be just as useful there. Get into the habit now. Write your check underneath your answer, with both sides worked out, and put a tick next to it when they match.
