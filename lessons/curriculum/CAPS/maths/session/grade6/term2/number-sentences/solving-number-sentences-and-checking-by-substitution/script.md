# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving and completing number sentences by inspection and by trial and improvement, and checking solutions by substitution. The anchor is a spaza shop in Tembisa that packs 1 128 sweets into bags of 24, buys stock in boxes and keeps a float in the till, and a class game where learners guess a mystery number. The governing idea is that the box stands for one number that makes the sentence true, that you can find it by thinking, by inverse operations or by sensible guessing that gets closer each time, and that you prove it by putting it back in. Solving by inspection, solving by trial and improvement, and checking by substitution are examinable.

## Subtopic: Solving by Inspection

Inspection means looking at a number sentence and working out the box by thinking about the numbers you know. Box plus 250 equals 1 000. What do you add to 250 to make 1 000? You know that 250 and 750 make 1 000, so the box is 750.

Many sentences can be solved from your bonds and times tables. 7 times box equals 63: you know 7 times 9 is 63, so the box is 9. Box divided by 4 equals 25: four 25s make 100, so the box is 100. 1 000 minus box equals 375: what do you take from 1 000 to leave 375? 625, because 375 plus 625 is 1 000.

Inverse operations help when the numbers are bigger. The spaza shop had some sweets, sold 2 480 and has 3 520 left. Box minus 2 480 equals 3 520. To undo the subtraction, add: 3 520 plus 2 480 is 6 000. The box is 6 000. In the same way, if box times 12 equals 432, undo the multiplication by dividing: 432 divided by 12 is 36.

Sometimes the box sits in a sentence with numbers on both sides. 250 plus 350 equals box plus 400. The left side is 600, so box plus 400 must also be 600. The box is 200. Work out the side you can, then make the other side balance.

Inspection works best when you can see a number fact hiding in the sentence. If the numbers are awkward, there is another method.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving by inspection and by using inverse operations.

## Subtopic: Trial and Improvement

Trial and improvement means making a sensible guess, testing it, and using the result to make a better guess. The spaza shop packs 1 128 sweets into bags of 24. How many bags? Box times 24 equals 1 128.

Try 40: 40 times 24 is 960. That is too small. Try 50: 50 times 24 is 1 200. That is too big, so the box is between 40 and 50. 1 128 is closer to 1 200 than to 960, so try a number near 50. Try 47: 47 times 24 is 1 128. That is exactly right, so the box is 47.

A table keeps your tries neat. Write three columns: the try, the result, and too big or too small. Each row helps you choose the next try. Notice that the last digit can help too. 24 times the box ends in 8. 24 times a number ending in 2 or 7 ends in 8, so 47 was a good guess.

Trial and improvement is useful for sentences that are hard to undo. Box times box equals 576. Try 20: 400, too small. Try 25: 625, too big. Try 24: 576. The box is 24. Another one: 15 times box plus 30 equals 480. Try 20: 300 plus 30 is 330, too small. Try 30: 450 plus 30 is 480. The box is 30.

The questions for this section are with you now: making a good first guess, improving it, and recording tries in a table.

## Subtopic: Checking by Substitution

Substitution means putting your answer into the box and working out whether the sentence is true. It is the best way to prove that an answer is right. Check box equals 47 in box times 24 equals 1 128: 47 times 24 is 1 128, and 1 128 equals 1 128, so the sentence is true.

Substitution also shows when an answer is wrong. A learner says the box in 3 times box plus 5 equals 32 is 10. Substitute: 3 times 10 is 30, plus 5 is 35. 35 is not 32, so 10 is wrong. Try 9: 3 times 9 is 27, plus 5 is 32. That is true, so the box is 9.

When you substitute, work out the side with the box using the order of operations, then compare it with the other side. If the two sides are equal, your answer is correct. If they are not, use the difference to improve your guess.

Substitution works for every method. Even if you solved by inspection, a quick substitution catches slips. In the class game, the mystery number doubled and then increased by 15 gives 85: 2 times box plus 15 equals 85. Undo: 85 minus 15 is 70, and half of 70 is 35. Substitute: 2 times 35 is 70, plus 15 is 85. Correct.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: in box minus 2 480 equals 3 520, subtracting to get 1 040; undo subtraction by adding to get 6 000. Two: guessing at random instead of using too big or too small to improve. Three: in 3 times box plus 5 equals 32, adding 5 before multiplying when substituting; multiply first. Four: not checking at all, so a wrong answer like 10 is handed in.

The questions for this section are with you now: substituting to check and using a check to fix a wrong answer.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the spaza shop and the mystery number — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Just Look

Look for a fact you know. 7 times box equals 63: the box is 9.

Undo the operation. Box minus 2 480 equals 3 520: add back to get 6 000.

1 000 minus box equals 375: the box is 625.

Keep the known facts in mind and try the questions for this part: inspection.

## Subtopic: Try, Then Get Closer

Guess, test, then improve.

Box times 24 equals 1 128. 40 is too small. 50 is too big. 47 is just right.

Use a table: try, result, too big or too small.

Hold the table in mind and try this part's questions: trial and improvement.

## Subtopic: Put It Back In

Put your answer in the box and work it out.

Both sides the same? Correct. Different? Try again.

3 times 9 plus 5 is 32, so 9 is right.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Solve by inspection when you can see a number fact, or undo the operation with its inverse. When that is hard, use trial and improvement: guess, test, and use too big or too small to improve. Always check by substitution: put the answer back in and make sure both sides are equal.

Hold the check in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: substitution.
