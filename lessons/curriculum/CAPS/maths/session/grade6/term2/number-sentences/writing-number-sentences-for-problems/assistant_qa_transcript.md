### subtopic_1 — Choosing the Operation
**Assistant:** Why can't I just always put the box at the end of the sentence?

**Tutor:** Because the box shows exactly which number in the story is missing, and that is not always the answer to a sum. In the money story, the club had some money, spent R3 750 and has R8 250 left. The number we do not know is the money it started with. If you write box minus 3 750 equals 8 250, the sentence tells the story exactly as it happened, in the same order. Then you can solve it: what number, take away 3 750, leaves 8 250? It must be 12 000. You can also rearrange the sentence to make it easier to work out, by writing 8 250 plus 3 750 equals box, and get 12 000 again. That is fine, because adding is the inverse of subtracting. But if you had written 8 250 minus 3 750 equals box, you would get 4 500, which is not the starting amount at all. The box at the end tempted you into the wrong calculation. So write the sentence the way the story happens first, with the box in its true place, and only then decide how to find the box.

### subtopic_2 — Where the Box Goes
**Assistant:** What does the equals sign really mean?

**Tutor:** The equals sign means that the two sides have exactly the same value. It does not mean here comes the answer. Many learners get used to sums like 25 plus 15 equals 40, where the answer always sits on the right, so they think equals means work it out. But look at 1 480 plus 2 365 equals 2 365 plus box. Both sides are additions, and the sentence is saying they must balance. The box must be 1 480 to make the two sides equal. Think of an old-fashioned balance scale at a market. Whatever is on the left pan must weigh the same as whatever is on the right pan for the scale to be level. You can have several things on each side, and the scale is still level as long as the totals match. That idea is very important in high school, where you will solve equations by keeping both sides balanced. So whenever you see an equals sign, ask: are both sides the same value? If they are, the sentence is true. If they are not, it is false. If there is a box, find the number that makes them balance.

### subtopic_3 — Two-Step Number Sentences
**Assistant:** How do I know if my two-step number sentence needs brackets?

**Tutor:** Work out your sentence using the normal order, and see whether the result matches the story. The normal order is brackets first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. For the tickets, 4 times 85 plus 6 times 45 multiplies first, giving the cost of the adult tickets and the cost of the child tickets, and then adds them. That is exactly what the story wants, so no brackets are needed. For the cake sale, the story says find the money left after paying for ingredients, then share it among 6 teams. The subtraction must come first. But the normal order would do the division first, because division comes before subtraction. 2 000 minus 560 divided by 6 would mean 2 000 minus about 93, which is not the story at all. So you put brackets around 2 000 minus 560 to force it to happen first. A simple test: underline the step the story does first. If the normal order would not do it first, put it in brackets. And always read the finished sentence back as a story to make sure it says what you mean.
