# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the work of Terms 1 and 2: whole numbers and the four operations, with estimation and checking; number sentences solved by inspection and checked by substitution; numeric and geometric patterns described with rules, flow diagrams and tables; common fractions, including comparing, adding, subtracting and finding fractions of whole numbers; and decimal fractions and percentages. The anchor is a greening day at a school in Polokwane. The school buys 1 344 tree seedlings at R18 each from a budget of R30 000. The seedlings arrive in trays of 24. Learners plant them in a growing pattern, mark out flower beds with poles and string, and measure how much the seedlings grow. Of the seedlings, 3/8 are fruit trees and 25% are indigenous trees. The governing idea is that every topic gives you a tool, and that an estimate before and a check after keep each tool honest. All of this work is examinable in the test that covers Terms 1 and 2.

## Subtopic: Whole Numbers and Number Sentences

Start with the cost. 1 344 seedlings at R18 each is 1 344 times 18. Estimate first: about 1 300 times 20 is 26 000. Now break 18 into 10 and 8. 1 344 times 10 is 13 440. 1 344 times 8 is 10 752. Add them: 13 440 plus 10 752 is 24 192. The seedlings cost R24 192, which is close to the estimate.

The budget is R30 000. The money left over is R30 000 minus R24 192, which is R5 808. Check by adding: R5 808 plus R24 192 gives R30 000.

The seedlings arrive in trays of 24. How many trays? Write a number sentence with a box for the unknown: box times 24 equals 1 344. To solve it, use the inverse operation: 1 344 divided by 24. Estimate: 1 200 divided by 24 is 50, so the answer is a bit more than 50. Work it out and you get 56. Now check by substitution: put 56 in the box. 56 times 24 is 1 120 plus 224, which is 1 344. The number sentence is true, so there are 56 trays.

Remember the other Term 1 skills too. The school raised R245 368 at last year's fun fair. Rounded to the nearest 1 000, that is R245 000, because the hundreds digit is 3, which is less than 5. And 53 is a prime number, because its only factors are 1 and 53.

The questions for this section are with you now: calculating, estimating and solving number sentences.

## Subtopic: Numeric and Geometric Patterns

On the first day of planting, the class plants 5 seedlings in a row. On day 2 the row has 9, on day 3 it has 13, and on day 4 it has 17. The pattern goes up by 4 each time. The next term is 21.

To find a far-off term, find a rule that links the position to the term. Since the pattern goes up in fours, try multiplying the position by 4. Position 1 gives 4, but the term is 5, so add 1. The rule is: multiply the position by 4 and add 1. Check position 3: 3 times 4 is 12, plus 1 is 13. Correct. The 10th term is 41 and the 20th term is 81.

A flow diagram shows the same rule: input, then times 4, then plus 1, then output. You can also run it backwards. Which position gives 81? Take away 1 to get 80, then divide by 4 to get 20.

The flower beds are marked out with poles and string in a row of squares. One square uses 4 strings, two squares side by side use 7, and three squares use 10. Each new square adds 3 strings, because it shares one side with the square before it. The rule is: multiply the number of squares by 3 and add 1. Ten squares need 31 strings and 25 squares need 76 strings. Working backwards, 61 strings make 20 squares, because 61 minus 1 is 60, and 60 divided by 3 is 20.

The questions for this section are with you now: extending patterns and using rules.

## Subtopic: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

Of the 1 344 seedlings, 3/8 are fruit trees. To find 3/8 of 1 344, divide by 8 to find one eighth, which is 168, then multiply by 3 to get 504 fruit trees. 25% are indigenous trees. 25% is one quarter, and 1 344 divided by 4 is 336 indigenous trees.

What fraction is left? Add 3/8 and 1/4. Change 1/4 to 2/8, because they need the same denominator. 3/8 plus 2/8 is 5/8. So 3/8 of the seedlings are other trees. 3/8 of 1 344 is 504, and 504 plus 336 plus 504 is 1 344, the whole.

Which is bigger, 3/8 or 1/4? Since 1/4 is 2/8, 3/8 is bigger.

A seedling grows 0,35 m in the first month and 0,6 m in the second month. Line up the commas: 0,35 plus 0,60 is 0,95 m. That is 5 hundredths short of a metre. Ten such seedlings standing on top of each other would be 0,95 times 10, which is 9,5 m tall.

Remember the three forms of the same number. 3/4 is 75/100, which is 0,75 and 75%. To compare 3/4, 0,7, 0,8 and 72%, write them all as decimals: 0,75, 0,7, 0,8 and 0,72. The biggest is 0,8.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding 3/8 and 1/4 to get 4/12 by adding tops and bottoms; make the denominators the same, and the answer is 5/8. Two: adding 0,35 and 0,6 to get 0,41 by lining up the last digits; line up the commas, and the answer is 0,95. Three: saying the 20th term of 5, 9, 13 is 80 by forgetting the plus 1 in the rule; it is 81. Four: rounding 245 368 to 246 000; the hundreds digit is 3, so it rounds down to 245 000.

The questions for this section are with you now: fractions, decimals and percentages.

5, 9, 13, 17: times 4, plus 1.

Squares in a row: times 3, plus 1.

Work backwards to find the input.

Hold the rules in mind and try this part's questions: patterns.

## Subtopic: Parts of the Whole

3/8 of 1 344 is 504. 25% of 1 344 is 336.

0,35 plus 0,6 is 0,95. 3/4 is 0,75 and 75%.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Estimate before you calculate and check after, using the inverse operation or substitution. Find a pattern's rule by linking the position to the term, and test it on a term you know. To find a fraction of a number, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. Add fractions only when the denominators are the same. Line up the commas for decimals, and use 100 to switch between fractions, decimals and percentages.

Hold every tool in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: Terms 1 and 2 revision.
