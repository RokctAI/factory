### subtopic_1 — What a Percentage Is
**Assistant:** Why do we use percentages when we already have fractions?

**Tutor:** Because percentages put everything out of the same number, 100, which makes comparing quick and fair. Suppose Sipho scored 17 out of 20 in maths and 21 out of 25 in English. Which is the better result? As fractions, 17/20 and 21/25 are hard to compare in your head. As percentages, 17/20 is 85 percent and 21/25 is 84 percent, so the maths mark is a tiny bit better. Shops, banks, the news and sports all use percentages for the same reason. A sale of 25 percent off, a phone battery at 40 percent, a team that wins 60 percent of its games, the chance of rain at 80 percent: in every case, you can picture a hundred square and see the size straight away. Percentages are also easy to say and remember. Twenty-five percent off sounds simpler than one quarter off, though they mean the same thing. But fractions are still useful, especially for working out an answer. To find 25 percent of R480, it is easiest to think of a quarter of R480, which is R120. So the two forms work together: percentages for comparing and talking, fractions for calculating.

### subtopic_2 — Changing Between Fractions and Percentages
**Assistant:** Why is finding 10 percent first such a useful trick?

**Tutor:** Because 10 percent is very easy to find, and almost every other percentage can be built from it. 10 percent is one tenth, so you just divide by 10. 10 percent of R350 is R35, and 10 percent of R640 is R64. Once you have 10 percent, you can double it for 20 percent, or multiply by 3 for 30 percent, and so on. Half of 10 percent gives 5 percent. Add 10 percent and 5 percent to get 15 percent, which is the VAT rate in South Africa. On a R1 200 television, 10 percent is R120, 5 percent is R60, so the VAT is R180. You can even find 1 percent by dividing 10 percent by 10, and then build percentages like 12 percent from 10 percent plus 2 percent. This method is useful in a shop when you do not have a calculator. If a sign says 30 percent off a R250 jacket, 10 percent is R25, so 30 percent is R75 off, and you pay R175. A good check is to make sure the percentage you find is the right size. 30 percent is less than half, so the discount must be less than half of R250.

### subtopic_3 — Finding Percentages of Whole Numbers
**Assistant:** How do I change a percentage like 35 percent into a fraction in simplest form?

**Tutor:** Write it as a fraction over 100, and then simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number. 35 percent is 35/100. Both 35 and 100 end in 5 or 0, so both divide by 5. 35 divided by 5 is 7, and 100 divided by 5 is 20. So 35 percent is 7/20. Can 7/20 be simplified further? 7 is prime, and 7 does not divide into 20, so 7/20 is in simplest form. Another example: 64 percent is 64/100. Both are even, so divide by 2 to get 32/50, and again by 2 to get 16/25. 16 and 25 have no common factor except 1, so 16/25 is the simplest form. Or divide by 4 in one step, because 4 is the highest common factor of 64 and 100. Some percentages are worth knowing as fractions by heart: 50 percent is 1/2, 25 percent is 1/4, 75 percent is 3/4, 20 percent is 1/5, 10 percent is 1/10 and 1 percent is 1/100. With those, many questions become mental maths.
