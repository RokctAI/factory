### subtopic_1 — Making Equivalent Fractions by Multiplying
**Assistant:** Why can't I add the same number to the top and bottom to make an equivalent fraction?

**Tutor:** Because adding does not keep the pieces in the same proportion, so the amount changes. Take 2/3 and add 2 to the top and the bottom to get 4/5. Is 4/5 the same as 2/3? Picture them. 2/3 means 2 out of every 3 pieces, which leaves one third unshaded. 4/5 means 4 out of every 5, which leaves only one fifth unshaded. A fifth is smaller than a third, so 4/5 is more shaded than 2/3. They are not the same. Now multiply instead. 2/3 times 2 over 2 is 4/6. Picture a strip cut into thirds with 2 shaded, then cut each third in half. The shading has not moved at all, but now you can count 4 shaded pieces out of 6. Multiplying the top and bottom by the same number is like zooming in on a picture: you see more pieces, but the picture is the same. Adding to the top and bottom is like changing the picture. A quick check: if you think two fractions are equivalent, simplify both. 4/6 simplifies to 2/3, but 4/5 cannot be simplified, so 4/5 is not equivalent to 2/3.

### subtopic_2 — Simplifying Fractions by Dividing
**Assistant:** How do I know when a fraction is in its simplest form?

**Tutor:** A fraction is in its simplest form when the only whole number that divides exactly into both the numerator and the denominator is 1. To check, think of the factors of the numerator and ask whether any of them, except 1, is also a factor of the denominator. Take 3/4. The factors of 3 are 1 and 3. 3 does not divide into 4, so 3/4 is in simplest form. Take 9/12. The factors of 9 are 1, 3 and 9. 3 divides into 12, so 9/12 is not in simplest form. Divide both by 3 to get 3/4. A quick test is to try the small primes 2, 3, 5 and 7. If both numbers are even, divide by 2. If both have digits that add to a multiple of 3, divide by 3. If both end in 0 or 5, divide by 5. Keep going until none of these work. If you know the highest common factor of the two numbers, you can divide by it once and land on the simplest form straight away. For 18/24 the highest common factor is 6, so 18/24 is 3/4 in one step.

### subtopic_3 — Equivalent Fractions with Two-Digit Denominators
**Assistant:** Why are hundredths so useful?

**Tutor:** Because hundredths connect fractions to percentages, decimals and money, which you meet every day. A hundred square is easy to picture, and many fractions fit neatly into it: a half is 50 squares, a quarter is 25, three quarters is 75, a fifth is 20, a tenth is 10. Once a fraction is written in hundredths, comparing is easy. Which is bigger, 3/4 or 7/10? In hundredths, they are 75/100 and 70/100, so 3/4 is bigger. Hundredths are also how our money works. One rand is 100 cents, so 75 cents is 75/100 of a rand, which is 3/4 of a rand. Later this term you will learn that 75/100 can be written as 75 percent and as the decimal 0,75. All three mean the same amount. So when you learn to write fractions as hundredths, you are building a bridge to percentages and decimals. A useful set to know by heart: 1/2 is 50/100, 1/4 is 25/100, 3/4 is 75/100, 1/5 is 20/100, 1/10 is 10/100 and 1/20 is 5/100. With those, you can work out many others quickly.
