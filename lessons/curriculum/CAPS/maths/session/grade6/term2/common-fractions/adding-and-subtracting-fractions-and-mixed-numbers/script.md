# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers addition and subtraction of common fractions in which one denominator is a multiple of the other, and addition and subtraction of mixed numbers. It builds on adding fractions with the same denominator in Grade 5 and on equivalent fractions in the last lesson. The anchor is the Jacobs family in Paarl who bake koeksisters and measure flour and syrup in cups, and a running club in Gqeberha whose members run distances such as 2 and 1/4 km and 1 and 3/8 km. The governing idea is that you can only add or subtract pieces of the same size, so you first change one fraction into an equivalent fraction with the same denominator as the other, and then add or subtract the numerators. Adding and subtracting fractions with related denominators, simplifying answers, changing answers bigger than 1 into mixed numbers, and adding and subtracting mixed numbers are examinable.

## Subtopic: Adding Fractions with Related Denominators

The Jacobs family uses 1/2 a cup of sugar in the dough and 1/4 of a cup in the syrup. How much sugar is that altogether? Halves and quarters are different-sized pieces, so we cannot just add the tops. But 4 is a multiple of 2, so change halves into quarters: 1/2 is 2/4. Now 2/4 plus 1/4 is 3/4. They use 3/4 of a cup of sugar.

The rule is: find the bigger denominator, check that the smaller denominator divides into it, and change the other fraction to match. Then add the numerators and keep the denominator. 2/3 plus 1/6: 6 is a multiple of 3, so 2/3 is 4/6. 4/6 plus 1/6 is 5/6.

Sometimes the answer is bigger than 1. 3/4 plus 5/8: change 3/4 to 6/8. 6/8 plus 5/8 is 11/8. Eight eighths make 1 whole, and 3 eighths are left over, so 11/8 is 1 and 3/8. A fraction like 11/8, with a top bigger than the bottom, is called an improper fraction. Usually we write the answer as a mixed number.

Tenths and fifths work the same way. 3/5 plus 3/10: change 3/5 to 6/10. 6/10 plus 3/10 is 9/10. Always look at your answer to see if it can be simplified. 1/3 plus 5/12 is 4/12 plus 5/12, which is 9/12, and 9/12 simplifies to 3/4.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding fractions with related denominators, and improper fraction answers.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Fractions with Related Denominators

Subtraction follows the same steps: make the denominators match, then subtract the numerators. The family has 5/6 of a bottle of syrup and uses 1/3 of a bottle. How much is left? Change 1/3 into sixths: 2/6. 5/6 minus 2/6 is 3/6, which simplifies to 1/2. Half a bottle is left.

7/8 minus 1/2: change 1/2 to 4/8. 7/8 minus 4/8 is 3/8. 9/10 minus 2/5: change 2/5 to 4/10. 9/10 minus 4/10 is 5/10, which is 1/2.

Taking a fraction away from 1 whole: write the whole as a fraction with the same denominator. A runner has run 5/12 of a race. What fraction is still to go? 1 is 12/12, and 12/12 minus 5/12 is 7/12.

Word problems often ask how much more. Thabo ran 11/12 of a lap and Kagiso ran 1/4 of a lap. How much further did Thabo run? Change 1/4 to 3/12. 11/12 minus 3/12 is 8/12, which simplifies to 2/3 of a lap. Simplify by dividing the top and bottom by 4, the biggest number that divides both.

Check subtraction by adding. If 5/6 minus 1/3 is 1/2, then 1/2 plus 1/3 should be 5/6. In sixths, 3/6 plus 2/6 is 5/6. It is.

The questions for this section are with you now: subtracting fractions with related denominators, subtracting from 1 and checking.

## Subtopic: Adding and Subtracting Mixed Numbers

A mixed number has a whole number part and a fraction part. Thabo runs 2 and 1/4 km on Monday and 1 and 3/8 km on Tuesday. How far altogether? Add the wholes: 2 plus 1 is 3. Add the fractions: 1/4 is 2/8, and 2/8 plus 3/8 is 5/8. Together: 3 and 5/8 km.

If the fractions add up to more than 1, carry a whole. 4 and 2/3 plus 2 and 5/6: wholes, 6. Fractions: 4/6 plus 5/6 is 9/6, which is 1 and 3/6. So the total is 7 and 3/6, which simplifies to 7 and 1/2.

Subtracting mixed numbers can need a whole to be broken into pieces. Lerato plans to run 3 and 1/2 km and has run 1 and 3/4 km. How far is left? Change the halves to quarters: 3 and 2/4 minus 1 and 3/4. You cannot take 3/4 from 2/4, so break one of the 3 wholes into 4 quarters: 3 and 2/4 becomes 2 and 6/4. Now 2 and 6/4 minus 1 and 3/4 is 1 and 3/4 km. Check: 1 and 3/4 plus 1 and 3/4 is 2 and 6/4, which is 3 and 2/4, which is 3 and 1/2.

Taking a mixed number from a whole number works the same way. A runner plans 5 km and has run 2 and 1/8 km. Break one of the 5 wholes into eighths: 5 is 4 and 8/8. Then 4 and 8/8 minus 2 and 1/8 is 2 and 7/8 km still to run.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding the tops and the bottoms, so 1/2 plus 1/4 becomes 2/6; make the pieces the same size first. Two: changing only the bottom number, so 1/2 becomes 1/4; multiply the top too, to get 2/4. Three: leaving 11/8 as the answer when a mixed number is asked for; it is 1 and 3/8. Four: in 3 and 2/4 minus 1 and 3/4, taking 2/4 from 3/4 instead of breaking a whole.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding and subtracting mixed numbers.

## Subtopic: Match, Then Add

Make the pieces the same size first.

1/2 is 2/4. 2/4 plus 1/4 is 3/4.

Top bigger than bottom? Change to a mixed number: 11/8 is 1 and 3/8.

## Subtopic: Match, Then Take Away

Match the pieces, then take away the tops.

5/6 minus 2/6 is 3/6, which is 1/2.

1 whole is 12/12. 12/12 minus 5/12 is 7/12.

Hold the matching in mind and try this part's questions: subtracting.

## Subtopic: Wholes and Parts

Add wholes to wholes and parts to parts.

2 and 1/4 plus 1 and 3/8 is 3 and 5/8.

Not enough parts to take away? Break a whole into pieces.

Pocket summary of the lesson. You can only add or subtract pieces of the same size. Change one fraction so the denominators match, then add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator. Simplify the answer, and change an improper fraction into a mixed number. With mixed numbers, work with the wholes and the fractions separately, carrying or breaking a whole when you need to.
