# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers finding fractions of whole numbers and solving problems in contexts involving common fractions, including grouping and equal sharing. It builds on fractions of whole numbers in Grade 5. The anchor is a sheep farmer near Graaff-Reinet with a flock of 240 sheep, a group of friends sharing 7 pizzas and 5 litres of juice, and a clothing shop in Thohoyandou that takes 1/5 off its prices in a sale. The governing idea is that to find a fraction of an amount you divide by the denominator to find one part and multiply by the numerator to find how many parts, and that when things are shared equally, the answer is often a fraction or a mixed number. Finding fractions of whole numbers, sharing so that each person gets a fraction, grouping in fraction-sized portions, and solving money and measurement problems with fractions are examinable.

## Subtopic: Finding a Fraction of a Whole Number

The farmer has 240 sheep. One quarter of them are black. How many is that? To find 1/4 of 240, divide 240 into 4 equal groups: 240 divided by 4 is 60. So 60 sheep are black.

Three quarters of the sheep are white. 3/4 of 240 means 3 of those 4 equal groups. One group is 60, so three groups are 3 times 60, which is 180. The rule is: divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to find the number of parts you need. Check: 60 black plus 180 white is 240, the whole flock.

More examples: 2/5 of 350. Divide by 5: 70. Multiply by 2: 140. 5/8 of 96. Divide by 8: 12. Multiply by 5: 60. 3/8 of 1 200. Divide by 8: 150. Multiply by 3: 450.

You can also think of the word of as multiply. 2/5 of 350 is the same as 2/5 times 350. Dividing by 5 and multiplying by 2 is how you work it out. Doing the division first keeps the numbers small, which makes the calculation easier. A quick size check helps too. A fraction less than 1 of a number must give an answer smaller than that number, so 5/8 of 96 must be less than 96, and since 5/8 is more than a half, it must be more than 48. Our answer, 60, fits.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding unit fractions and other fractions of whole numbers.

## Subtopic: Fractions in Sharing and Grouping Problems

Four friends share 7 pizzas equally. How much does each friend get? Each friend first gets 1 whole pizza, using 4 pizzas. There are 3 pizzas left. Cut each of them into 4 quarters, which makes 12 quarters, and give each friend 3 quarters. Each friend gets 1 and 3/4 pizzas. Notice that 7 divided by 4 is 7/4, which is 1 and 3/4. Sharing a whole number among a group gives a fraction: the number being shared goes on top, and the number of people goes underneath.

Eight learners share 5 litres of juice. Each learner gets 5 divided by 8, which is 5/8 of a litre. That is less than 1 litre each, which makes sense, because there are more learners than litres.

Grouping problems ask how many fraction-sized portions fit into an amount. A recipe needs 1/4 of a cup of oil for each batch of vetkoek. How many batches can you make with 3 cups of oil? Each cup holds 4 quarters, so 3 cups hold 12 quarters. You can make 12 batches.

Be clear about which question is being asked. Sharing asks how much each person gets. Grouping asks how many groups or portions you can make. Drawing the wholes and cutting them into pieces is always a good way to check.

The questions for this section are with you now: sharing that gives fractions, and grouping in fraction-sized portions.

## Subtopic: Fraction Problems with Money and Measurement

Money: the shop in Thohoyandou takes 1/5 off a jacket that costs R450. How much is taken off? 1/5 of 450 is 450 divided by 5, which is R90. The sale price is R450 minus R90, which is R360. Read carefully: does the question ask for the discount or for the new price?

Thandeka earns R360 for a weekend job and spends 2/3 of it on a school bag. How much does she spend, and how much is left? 360 divided by 3 is 120, and 2 times 120 is 240. She spends R240 and has R120 left. Another way to find what is left: she spends 2/3, so 1/3 is left, and 1/3 of R360 is R120.

Measurement: a hiking trail is 2 km long, and you have walked 3/4 of it. How far is that in metres? 2 km is 2 000 m. 1/4 of 2 000 is 500, so 3/4 is 1 500 m. Time: how many minutes are in 2/3 of an hour? An hour has 60 minutes. 60 divided by 3 is 20, and 2 times 20 is 40 minutes.

Changing to a smaller unit first, like metres or minutes, often turns a fraction problem into a whole number problem.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: finding 3/4 of 240 by dividing by 3 and multiplying by 4, which gives 320; divide by the denominator, 4, and multiply by the numerator, 3. Two: sharing 7 pizzas among 4 and saying each gets 4/7; the pizzas go on top: 7/4. Three: giving R90 as the sale price when it is the discount; the sale price is R360. Four: forgetting that the trail is 2 km, not 1 km, and answering 750 m; 3/4 of 2 000 m is 1 500 m.

The questions for this section are with you now: fractions of money, discounts, lengths and time.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the sheep, the pizzas and the sale — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Divide, Then Multiply

Divide by the bottom number. Multiply by the top number.

1/4 of 240 is 60. 3/4 of 240 is 180.

2/5 of 350: 350 divided by 5 is 70, times 2 is 140.

Keep divide-then-multiply in mind and try the questions for this part: fractions of whole numbers.

## Subtopic: Share It Fairly

7 pizzas for 4 friends: 7/4, which is 1 and 3/4 each.

5 litres for 8 learners: 5/8 of a litre each.

How many quarter cups in 3 cups? 12.

Hold the fair shares in mind and try this part's questions: sharing and grouping.

## Subtopic: Fractions in Real Life

1/5 off R450 is R90 off. The new price is R360.

3/4 of 2 km is 1 500 m. 2/3 of an hour is 40 minutes.

Check whether the question wants the part or what is left.

Pocket summary of the lesson. To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator. Sharing a number of things among a number of people gives things over people, which may be a mixed number. Grouping asks how many fraction portions fit. Change to smaller units to make measurement problems easier, and read carefully to see whether the question asks for the part or the rest.

Hold the real-life fractions in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: money and measurement.
