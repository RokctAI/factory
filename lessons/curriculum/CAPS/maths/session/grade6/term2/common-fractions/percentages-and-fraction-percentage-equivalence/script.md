# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers finding percentages of whole numbers and recognising the equivalence between common fraction and percentage forms of the same number. It builds on hundredths and equivalent fractions earlier this term. The anchor is Sipho in Durban, who scored 17 out of 20 in a maths test, a shop that takes 10 percent and 25 percent off its prices, and the 15 percent VAT that is added to most things we buy in South Africa. The governing idea is that percent means out of a hundred, so a percentage is just a fraction with a denominator of 100, and that you can find a percentage of an amount by finding 10 percent or 1 percent first. Writing fractions as percentages, writing percentages as fractions, and finding percentages of whole numbers are examinable.

## Subtopic: What a Percentage Is

The word percent comes from Latin and means for every hundred, or out of a hundred. The sign % means percent. 25 percent means 25 out of 100, which is the fraction 25/100. On a hundred square, 25 percent is 25 of the 100 small squares shaded.

Because percentages are hundredths, the whole is always 100 percent. If a cellphone battery is full, it is at 100 percent. If it shows 25 percent, a quarter of the charge is left, because 25/100 simplifies to 1/4. If it shows 50 percent, half is left.

Some percentages are worth knowing by heart. 100 percent is the whole. 50 percent is a half. 25 percent is a quarter. 75 percent is three quarters. 10 percent is one tenth. 20 percent is one fifth. 1 percent is one hundredth.

Percentages can never be more than 100 percent of the whole you started with, unless something grows. If a school says 104 percent of its learners came on a trip, someone has made a mistake.

Percentages make comparing easy, because everything is out of the same number, 100. If one class has 18 learners and 9 walk to school, that is half, or 50 percent. If another class has 40 learners and 14 walk, that is 35 percent. Even though more learners walk in the second class, a smaller percentage of that class walks.

The questions for this section are with you now: what percent means, and knowing the common percentages.

## Subtopic: Changing Between Fractions and Percentages

To change a fraction into a percentage, write it as an equivalent fraction with a denominator of 100. The numerator is then the percentage. Sipho scored 17 out of 20, which is 17/20. Multiply the top and bottom by 5 to get 85/100. So he scored 85 percent.

More examples: 3/10 is 30/100, which is 30 percent. 7/20 is 35/100, which is 35 percent. 9/25: multiply the top and bottom by 4 to get 36/100, which is 36 percent. 3/5 is 60/100, which is 60 percent.

To change a percentage into a fraction, write it over 100 and simplify. 40 percent is 40/100. Divide the top and bottom by 20 to get 2/5. 75 percent is 75/100, which simplifies to 3/4. 12 percent is 12/100, which simplifies to 3/25.

This works neatly whenever the denominator divides into 100, like 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25 and 50. A test out of 50 is easy too: multiply the top and bottom by 2. 43 out of 50 is 86/100, which is 86 percent. A test out of 25: multiply by 4, so 21 out of 25 is 84/100, or 84 percent. For a test out of 40, change to a denominator of 100 in two steps: 24 out of 40 simplifies to 3/5, and 3/5 is 60/100, so 24 out of 40 is 60 percent.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing fractions into percentages, and percentages into fractions.

## Subtopic: Finding Percentages of Whole Numbers

To find a percentage of an amount, a quick method is to find 10 percent first. 10 percent is one tenth, so divide by 10. A pair of shoes costs R350 and the shop takes 10 percent off. 10 percent of 350 is 35, so the discount is R35. 20 percent is double that: R70. 30 percent is three times: R105.

Five percent is half of 10 percent. 10 percent of R360 is R36, so 5 percent is R18. Then 15 percent is 10 percent plus 5 percent: R36 plus R18 is R54.

For friendly percentages, use the fraction. 25 percent of 480 is 1/4 of 480, which is 120. 75 percent of 480 is three times that, 360. 50 percent of 86 is half of 86, which is 43.

VAT in South Africa is 15 percent. On an item that costs R200 before VAT, the VAT is 10 percent, which is R20, plus 5 percent, which is R10. That makes R30, so the item costs R230 with VAT.

Always check that your answer is a sensible size. 10 percent of an amount is small, a tenth of it. 50 percent is half. If you work out 15 percent of R360 and get more than R180, you know something is wrong, because 15 percent is much less than half.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying 17 out of 20 is 17 percent; change the fraction to hundredths to get 85 percent. Two: saying 40 percent is 4/10 and stopping there; it is correct but not simplest form, which is 2/5. Three: finding 10 percent of 350 by dividing by 100 to get 3,5; divide by 10 to get 35. Four: finding 15 percent of R360 as R36 plus R15; 5 percent is half of 10 percent, which is R18, so the answer is R54.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding percentages of amounts, discounts and VAT.

## Subtopic: Out of a Hundred

Percent means out of 100.

25 percent is 25/100, which is 1/4. 50 percent is a half. 100 percent is the whole.

The whole is always 100 percent.

## Subtopic: Same Amount, Two Names

Fraction to percent: make the bottom 100. 17/20 is 85/100, which is 85 percent.

Percent to fraction: put it over 100 and simplify. 40 percent is 2/5.

A fraction and a percentage can name the same amount.

Hold the hundredths in mind and try this part's questions: fractions and percentages.

## Subtopic: Ten Percent First

10 percent: divide by 10. 10 percent of R350 is R35.

5 percent is half of 10 percent. 15 percent is 10 percent plus 5 percent.

25 percent is a quarter. 50 percent is a half.
