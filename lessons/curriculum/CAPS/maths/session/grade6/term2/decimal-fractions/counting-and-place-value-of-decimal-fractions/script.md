# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising decimal fractions to at least two decimal places, stating the place value of their digits, and counting forwards and backwards in decimal fractions. It builds on tenths and hundredths as common fractions earlier this term. The anchor is an athletics day in Bloemfontein where Naledi jumps 3,68 m in the long jump and Johan runs the 100 m in 12,45 seconds, and coins and prices at a tuck shop. The governing idea is that a decimal is another way to write tenths and hundredths, that the decimal comma separates the whole number from the parts smaller than one, and that the places after the comma are worth ten times less each step to the right. Writing tenths and hundredths as decimals, naming the place and value of decimal digits, writing decimals in expanded form, and counting in steps such as 0,1, 0,01 and 0,05 are examinable.

## Subtopic: Tenths and Hundredths as Decimals

In South Africa we write decimals with a comma. 0,1 is read as nought comma one, and it means one tenth, or 1/10. 0,7 means seven tenths, 7/10. The first place after the comma is the tenths place.

The second place after the comma is the hundredths place. 0,01 means one hundredth, or 1/100. 0,25 means 25 hundredths, 25/100, which is the same as a quarter. Our money works this way: R1 is 100 cents, so 1 cent is R0,01 and 25 cents is R0,25.

A decimal can have a whole number part too. Naledi's jump of 3,68 m means 3 whole metres and 68 hundredths of a metre: 3 and 68/100 m. Johan's time of 12,45 seconds means 12 whole seconds and 45 hundredths of a second: 12 and 45/100 seconds.

To change a common fraction with a denominator of 10 or 100 into a decimal, write the numerator in the right places. 3/10 is 0,3. 7/100 is 0,07, because the 7 must sit in the hundredths place, so a 0 holds the tenths place. 35/100 is 0,35. 2 and 9/10 is 2,9.

You can also read a decimal aloud in two ways. 0,35 can be read as nought comma three five, or as 35 hundredths. Reading it as hundredths reminds you what it means. At the tuck shop, a packet of chips costs R6,75. That is 6 whole rands and 75 hundredths of a rand, which is 75 cents. A juice costs R8,50, which is 8 rands and 50 cents.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading decimals, and writing tenths and hundredths as decimals.

## Subtopic: Place Value of Decimal Digits

Place value continues to the right of the comma. To the left are the units, tens, hundreds and so on. To the right are the tenths, then the hundredths. Each place is ten times smaller than the place on its left: a tenth is a unit cut into 10, and a hundredth is a tenth cut into 10.

In 3,68, the 3 is in the units place and is worth 3. The 6 is in the tenths place and is worth 0,6, which is 6/10. The 8 is in the hundredths place and is worth 0,08, which is 8/100. In expanded form, 3,68 is 3 plus 0,6 plus 0,08.

In Johan's time, 12,45, the 1 is worth 10, the 2 is worth 2, the 4 is worth 0,4 and the 5 is worth 0,05. In expanded form: 10 plus 2 plus 0,4 plus 0,05.

Zeros hold places in decimals just as they do in whole numbers. 4,05 has 0 tenths and 5 hundredths, so it is 4 and 5/100. 4,5 has 5 tenths, which is 4 and 50/100. These are very different numbers. And 4,50 is the same as 4,5, because 50 hundredths is the same as 5 tenths. A zero at the very end of a decimal does not change its value, which is why prices are written as R4,50.

We can also write a decimal using place value names. 7,39 is 7 units, 3 tenths and 9 hundredths. It is also 739 hundredths, because 7 units is 700 hundredths. Thinking this way will help later when we compare and add decimals.

The questions for this section are with you now: naming the place and value of decimal digits, and expanded form.

## Subtopic: Counting Forwards and Backwards in Decimals

Count forwards in tenths from 2,7: 2,8, 2,9, 3,0, 3,1. After 9 tenths comes 10 tenths, which is one whole, so 2,9 plus 0,1 is 3,0, which we usually write as 3. The tenths go back to 0 and the units go up by one.

Count in hundredths from 0,97: 0,98, 0,99, 1,00, 1,01. After 99 hundredths comes 100 hundredths, which is one whole. Count in steps of 0,05 like five-cent coins from 4,85: 4,90, 4,95, 5,00, 5,05.

Count backwards in steps of 0,2 from 1,4: 1,2, 1,0, 0,8, 0,6. When you count back past a whole number, the units go down by one and the tenths start again from a big number: 1,0 take away 0,2 is 0,8.

A number line helps. Between 3 and 4 there are 10 tenths: 3,1, 3,2, up to 3,9. Between 3,6 and 3,7 there are 10 hundredths: 3,61, 3,62, up to 3,69. Naledi's 3,68 sits between 3,6 and 3,7, very close to 3,7.

You can also count in steps of 0,25, like quarters. From 0: 0,25, 0,5, 0,75, 1. Four quarters make one whole, just as four 25 cent coins make R1.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: counting 2,8, 2,9, 2,10 in tenths; 9 tenths plus 1 tenth is a whole, so the next number is 3,0. Two: writing 7/100 as 0,7; the 7 belongs in the hundredths place, so it is 0,07. Three: saying the 6 in 3,68 is worth 6 or 60; it is in the tenths place, so it is worth 0,6. Four: thinking 4,05 and 4,5 are the same; the zero holds the tenths place, so 4,05 is much smaller.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting forwards and backwards in tenths, hundredths and other decimal steps.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the long jump, the sprint and the coins — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: After the Comma

The comma splits wholes from parts.

First place after the comma: tenths. Second place: hundredths.

7/10 is 0,7. 7/100 is 0,07. 25 cents is R0,25.

Keep the comma in mind and try the questions for this part: tenths and hundredths.

## Subtopic: Each Digit Has a Seat

In 3,68 the 3 is units, the 6 is tenths and the 8 is hundredths.

3,68 is 3 plus 0,6 plus 0,08.

A 0 holds an empty seat: 4,05 is not 4,5.

Hold the seats in mind and try this part's questions: place value.

## Subtopic: Count in Small Steps

Counting in tenths: 2,8, 2,9, 3,0.

Ten tenths make a whole. A hundred hundredths make a whole.

Counting back: 1,2, 1,0, 0,8.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The decimal comma separates whole numbers from parts. The first place after the comma is tenths and the second is hundredths. Each place is ten times smaller than the one on its left. Write 7/100 as 0,07, with a zero holding the tenths. When counting, ten tenths or a hundred hundredths make a whole, so the units go up by one.

Hold the small steps in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: counting in decimals.
