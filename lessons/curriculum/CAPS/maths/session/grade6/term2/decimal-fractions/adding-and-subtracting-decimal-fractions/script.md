# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers adding and subtracting decimal fractions with up to two decimal places, including whole numbers mixed with decimals, and estimating and checking answers. It builds on place value of decimals and on column addition and subtraction of whole numbers from Term 1. The anchor is a Grade 6 camp in the Drakensberg. On the way, the class buys bread for R16,99, milk for R24,50 and apples for R32,75 and pays with R100. At camp they hike 3,45 km to a waterfall and then 2,8 km to a cave, on a trail that is 10 km long. The governing idea is that we may only add or subtract digits that are in the same place, so the decimal commas must be lined up underneath each other, and we carry and borrow across the comma exactly as we do with whole numbers. Adding and subtracting decimals, filling empty places with zeros, solving money and measurement problems, and estimating and checking are examinable.

## Subtopic: Adding Decimals

When you add decimals in columns, write the numbers so that the commas are in a straight line. This puts units under units, tenths under tenths and hundredths under hundredths. If one number has fewer decimal places, fill the gap with a zero.

The hike to the waterfall and then to the cave is 3,45 km plus 2,8 km. Write 2,8 as 2,80 and put it under 3,45 with the commas lined up. Start on the right. Hundredths: 5 plus 0 is 5. Tenths: 4 plus 8 is 12 tenths. 12 tenths is 1 whole and 2 tenths, so write 2 in the tenths column and carry 1 to the units. Units: 3 plus 2 plus the carried 1 is 6. Put the comma in the answer directly under the other commas. The total is 6,25 km.

Now the shopping. R16,99 plus R24,50 plus R32,75. Line up the commas. Hundredths: 9 plus 0 plus 5 is 14, so write 4 and carry 1. Tenths: 9 plus 5 plus 7 plus 1 is 22, so write 2 and carry 2. Units: 6 plus 4 plus 2 plus 2 is 14, so write 4 and carry 1. Tens: 1 plus 2 plus 3 plus 1 is 7. The total bill is R74,24.

You can also add decimals in your head by splitting them. 3,45 plus 2,8: add the wholes, 3 plus 2 is 5. Add the parts, 0,45 plus 0,8 is 1,25. Then 5 plus 1,25 is 6,25. The same answer as the column method.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding decimals in columns and in your head.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Decimals

Subtraction works the same way. Line up the commas, fill empty places with zeros and subtract from the right, borrowing when you need to.

The trail is 10 km long and the class has walked 6,25 km. How far is left? Write 10 as 10,00, so that it has the same places as 6,25. Hundredths: 0 take away 5 cannot be done, and the tenths and units next door are also 0, so we must borrow from the tens. 10,00 becomes 9 units, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths. Now 10 take away 5 is 5 hundredths, 9 take away 2 is 7 tenths, and 9 take away 6 is 3 units. There are 3,75 km left to walk.

The change from R100 is R100 minus R74,24. Write R100 as R100,00. After borrowing, hundredths: 10 take away 4 is 6. Tenths: 9 take away 2 is 7. Units: 9 take away 4 is 5. Tens: 9 take away 7 is 2. The change is R25,76.

One more: 5,3 minus 2,46. Write 5,3 as 5,30. Hundredths: 0 take away 6 needs a borrow, so take 1 tenth from the 3 tenths, leaving 2 tenths, and the 0 hundredths becomes 10 hundredths. 10 take away 6 is 4. Tenths: 2 take away 4 needs a borrow from the units, so 5 units becomes 4 and the 2 tenths becomes 12 tenths. 12 take away 4 is 8. Units: 4 take away 2 is 2. The answer is 2,84.

Counting on works well for change. From R74,24, add 76 cents to reach R75, then R25 to reach R100. The change is R25,76.

The questions for this section are with you now: subtracting decimals, including from whole numbers.

## Subtopic: Estimating and Checking

Before you calculate, estimate. Round each decimal to the nearest whole number and do the easy sum. For the shopping, R16,99 is about R17, R24,50 is about R25 and R32,75 is about R33. The estimate is R75. Our exact answer, R74,24, is close to R75, so it is sensible. If you had got R7,42 or R742,40, the estimate would warn you that the comma is in the wrong place.

After you calculate, check with the inverse operation. Addition undoes subtraction. If the change is R25,76, then R25,76 plus R74,24 must give back R100,00. Add them: hundredths, 6 plus 4 is 10, write 0 and carry 1; tenths, 7 plus 2 plus 1 is 10, write 0 and carry 1; units, 5 plus 4 plus 1 is 10, write 0 and carry 1; tens, 2 plus 7 plus 1 is 10. The total is R100,00, so the subtraction was right.

To check an addition, subtract one of the numbers from the total. The hike total was 6,25 km. 6,25 minus 2,8 should give 3,45, and it does.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 3,45 plus 2,8 as 3,73, by lining up the last digits instead of the commas; line up the commas and the answer is 6,25. Two: writing 5,3 minus 2,46 as 3,16, by taking the smaller digit from the bigger digit in each column; fill the gap with a zero and borrow, and the answer is 2,84. Three: writing 0,8 plus 0,5 as 0,13; 13 tenths is 1 whole and 3 tenths, so the answer is 1,3. Four: writing 10 minus 6,25 as 4,25, by only subtracting the wholes; write 10 as 10,00 and the answer is 3,75.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating and checking decimal calculations.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the Drakensberg camp — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Commas in a Line

Put the commas under each other.

Fill empty places with zeros: 2,8 becomes 2,80.

3,45 plus 2,80 is 6,25. Carry just like whole numbers.

Keep the straight line of commas in mind and try the questions for this part: adding decimals.

## Subtopic: Take Away with Zeros

10 becomes 10,00. Then borrow.

10,00 minus 6,25 is 3,75.

R100,00 minus R74,24 is R25,76 change.

Hold the zeros in mind and try this part's questions: subtracting decimals.

## Subtopic: Guess, Then Check

Estimate first: about R17 plus R25 plus R33 is R75.

Check after: add the change back on and get R100.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Line up the commas so that the same places are under each other. Fill empty places with zeros; this does not change the value. Carry and borrow exactly as with whole numbers, and put the comma in the answer under the other commas. Estimate by rounding to whole numbers, and check with the inverse operation.

Hold the guess-and-check habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: decimal problems.
