### subtopic_1 — Adding Decimals
**Assistant:** Why must I line up the commas and not the last digits?

**Tutor:** Because you may only add digits that have the same value, and the comma is what tells you the value of each digit. In whole numbers, lining up the last digits works because the last digit is always the units. In decimals, the last digit might be tenths in one number and hundredths in another. Take 3,45 plus 2,8. In 3,45 the last digit, 5, is hundredths. In 2,8 the last digit, 8, is tenths. If you line up the last digits, you add 8 tenths to 5 hundredths, which is like adding 8 rand to 5 cents and calling the answer 13 of something. You would get 3,73, which is wrong. If you line up the commas, the 8 tenths sits under the 4 tenths, the 2 units sits under the 3 units, and the empty hundredths place under the 5 gets a zero. Now each column holds one kind of piece, and the answer is 6,25. A quick estimate warns you as well: about 3 km plus about 3 km is about 6 km, so 3,73 cannot be right.

### subtopic_2 — Subtracting Decimals
**Assistant:** Why can I write 10 as 10,00 when I subtract 6,25?

**Tutor:** Because 10 and 10,00 are the same number. 10,00 means 10 wholes, 0 tenths and 0 hundredths, and zero tenths and zero hundredths add nothing. In money, R10 and R10,00 are exactly the same amount. We write the zeros so that the places line up and so that we have something to borrow from. If you write 10 with nothing after it and try to subtract 6,25, it is easy to forget the decimal part and say 10 minus 6 is 4, giving 4,25. That is wrong. When you write 10,00, the hundredths column shows 0 take away 5, so you know you must borrow. You borrow from the tens: 10,00 is the same as 9 units, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths. Then 10 hundredths take away 5 is 5, 9 tenths take away 2 is 7, and 9 units take away 6 is 3. The answer is 3,75. Check it by adding: 3,75 plus 6,25 gives 10,00, which is the 10 km trail.

### subtopic_3 — Estimating and Checking
**Assistant:** How does estimating help me with decimals?

**Tutor:** It catches mistakes, especially a comma in the wrong place, before you hand in your answer. To estimate, round each decimal to the nearest whole number, or to a friendly number like a multiple of 5 or 10, and work out the easy sum. For the camp shopping, R16,99 is about R17, R24,50 is about R25 and R32,75 is about R33. 17 plus 25 plus 33 is 75, so the bill should be about R75. When you work it out exactly and get R74,24, you can trust it, because it is close to R75. But suppose you forgot to line up the commas and got R40,64, or you dropped the comma and got R7 424. Your estimate of R75 would tell you at once that something has gone wrong. Estimating also helps when you shop in real life. Before you reach the till, you can add up rounded prices in your head to make sure you have enough money. Then, after the calculation, check with the inverse operation as well. An estimate tells you the answer is about right; a check tells you it is exactly right.
