# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers comparing and ordering decimal fractions with up to two decimal places, using place value and the number line, and rounding decimal fractions to the nearest whole number and the nearest tenth. It builds on reading decimals and naming the place value of their digits from the previous lesson. The anchor is a school swimming gala in Durban, where five swimmers race 50 m freestyle: Megan swims 34,98 seconds, Lerato 35,09 seconds, Sipho 35,4 seconds, Kabelo 35,40 seconds and Aisha 36,1 seconds. The governing idea is that decimals are compared place by place from the left, starting with the whole numbers, then the tenths, then the hundredths, and that the number of digits after the comma does not tell you which decimal is bigger. Comparing two decimals, ordering a list of decimals from smallest to biggest or biggest to smallest, placing decimals on a number line, and rounding to the nearest whole number or tenth are examinable.

## Subtopic: Comparing Decimals by Place Value

To compare two decimals, start on the left, exactly as you do with whole numbers. First compare the whole number parts. Megan's 34,98 has 34 whole seconds and Lerato's 35,09 has 35 whole seconds. Since 34 is less than 35, 34,98 is less than 35,09. We do not even need to look at the digits after the comma.

If the whole numbers are the same, compare the tenths. Lerato's 35,09 and Sipho's 35,4 both have 35 wholes. Lerato has 0 tenths and Sipho has 4 tenths, so 35,09 is less than 35,4. Many learners think 35,09 is bigger because 9 is bigger than 4, but the 9 is only 9 hundredths. Tenths are worth more than hundredths, so the tenths decide first.

If the tenths are the same as well, compare the hundredths. 2,47 and 2,43 both have 2 wholes and 4 tenths. 7 hundredths is more than 3 hundredths, so 2,47 is greater.

A helpful trick is to give both decimals the same number of places by adding a zero at the end. Sipho's 35,4 can be written as 35,40. Now compare 35,40 with 35,09: it is like comparing 3 540 hundredths with 3 509 hundredths, and 3 540 is bigger. Adding a zero at the end does not change the value, so Sipho's 35,4 and Kabelo's 35,40 are equal times. They touched the wall at the same moment.

Here is another pair. Is 0,5 or 0,25 bigger? Write 0,5 as 0,50. Now it is 50 hundredths against 25 hundredths, so 0,5 is bigger. In money, 50 cents is more than 25 cents.

The questions for this section are with you now: comparing pairs of decimals and saying which is greater.

## Subtopic: Ordering Decimals

To order a list, compare the numbers in pairs, or line them up by their decimal commas so that the places sit underneath each other. Then fill the empty places with zeros so every number has two decimal places.

Let us order all five swimming times. Written with two places they are 34,98; 35,09; 35,40; 35,40 and 36,10. The smallest whole number is 34, so Megan's 34,98 comes first. Next are the three times with 35 wholes. Compare their tenths: 0, 4 and 4. So 35,09 comes next, then the two equal times of 35,40. Last is 36,10, which has the most wholes. From smallest to biggest: 34,98; 35,09; 35,4; 35,40; 36,1.

In a race, the smallest time is the fastest. So the order from smallest to biggest is also the order from first place to last place. Megan wins, Lerato is second, Sipho and Kabelo tie for third, and Aisha is fifth. This is one of the few times where the smallest number is the best.

To order from biggest to smallest, simply turn the list around. For 2,5; 2,05 and 2,45, write them as 2,50; 2,05 and 2,45. Compare tenths: 5, 0 and 4. Biggest to smallest: 2,5; 2,45; 2,05.

A number line also helps with ordering. Numbers further to the right are bigger. Between 35 and 36, Lerato's 35,09 sits just after 35, while Sipho's 35,4 sits a little before the halfway mark at 35,5.

The questions for this section are with you now: putting lists of decimals in order.

## Subtopic: Number Lines and Rounding Decimals

Rounding a decimal works just like rounding a whole number. Decide which two numbers it lies between, then decide which one it is closer to.

To round to the nearest whole number, look at the tenths digit. If it is 5 or more, round up to the next whole number. If it is 4 or less, keep the whole number. Megan's 34,98 has 9 tenths, so it rounds up to 35 seconds. Aisha's 36,1 has 1 tenth, so it rounds down to 36 seconds. And 7,5 is exactly halfway between 7 and 8; the rule says round up, so it becomes 8.

To round to the nearest tenth, look at the hundredths digit. Naledi's long jump of 3,68 m lies between 3,6 and 3,7. The hundredths digit is 8, which is 5 or more, so we round up to 3,7 m. Johan's 12,45 seconds lies exactly halfway between 12,4 and 12,5, so we round up to 12,5 seconds. Lerato's 35,09 rounds to 35,1, because 9 hundredths is 5 or more.

On a number line from 0 to 1, the halfway mark is 0,5, a quarter of the way is 0,25 and three quarters of the way is 0,75. Marking these helps you see where a decimal sits.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying 0,25 is bigger than 0,5 because 25 is bigger than 5; write 0,5 as 0,50 and compare 50 hundredths with 25 hundredths. Two: saying 3,09 is bigger than 3,1 because 9 is bigger than 1; compare the tenths first, and 1 tenth beats 0 tenths. Three: thinking 4,70 is bigger than 4,7 because it has more digits; a zero at the end changes nothing, so they are equal. Four: rounding 2,45 to the nearest tenth as 2,4; the hundredths digit is 5, so we round up to 2,5.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding decimals and placing them on a number line.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the swimming gala — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Which Is Bigger?

Start on the left. Wholes first, then tenths, then hundredths.

35,09 is less than 35,4, because 0 tenths is less than 4 tenths.

Add a zero to make the places match: 0,5 is 0,50, which beats 0,25.

Keep the left-to-right rule in mind and try the questions for this part: comparing decimals.

## Subtopic: Line Them Up

Line up the commas and fill gaps with zeros.

34,98; 35,09; 35,40; 36,10 is smallest to biggest.

In a race, the smallest time wins.

Hold the lined-up commas in mind and try this part's questions: ordering decimals.

## Subtopic: Round to the Nearest

To the nearest whole: look at the tenths.

To the nearest tenth: look at the hundredths.

5 or more, round up. 4 or less, stay.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Compare decimals from the left: wholes, then tenths, then hundredths. More digits does not mean bigger. Add zeros at the end so every number has the same number of places; this does not change the value. To order, line up the commas. To round, look one place to the right of where you are rounding and round up if that digit is 5 or more.

Hold the rounding rule in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: ordering and rounding decimals.
