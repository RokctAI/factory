# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising the equivalence between common fractions, decimal fractions and percentages, converting between the three forms, comparing numbers written in different forms, and solving problems in context involving decimal fractions. It builds on percentages from earlier this term and on adding, subtracting and multiplying decimals by 10 and 100. The anchor is a market day at a school in Mahikeng. The class sells cupcakes at R4,50 each and trays of popcorn at R12,75 each, a poster says that 25% of the money raised goes to an animal shelter, and a pie chart shows that half of the customers were parents. The governing idea is that 1/2, 0,5 and 50% are three names for exactly the same amount, and the key that unlocks all three is the denominator 100: hundredths are easy to write as decimals and as percentages. Converting between the three forms, comparing and ordering mixed forms, and solving money problems with decimals are examinable.

## Subtopic: Three Names for One Number

A percentage means out of 100. A decimal with two places counts hundredths. So any fraction with a denominator of 100 can be written in all three forms at once. 25/100 is 0,25 and 25%. 50/100 is 0,5 and 50%.

Here are the forms you should know by heart. One half: 1/2 is 50/100, so it is 0,5 and 50%. One quarter: 1/4 is 25/100, so it is 0,25 and 25%. Three quarters: 3/4 is 75/100, so it is 0,75 and 75%. One tenth: 1/10 is 0,1 and 10%. One fifth: 1/5 is 2/10, so it is 0,2 and 20%. One hundredth: 1/100 is 0,01 and 1%. And one whole is 1, which is 100%.

Tenths make an easy pattern. 0,1 is 10%, 0,2 is 20%, 0,3 is 30%, and so on, up to 0,9, which is 90%. Each tenth is worth ten per cent, because one tenth of 100 is 10.

The market day poster says 25% goes to the animal shelter. That is 0,25 of the money, or one quarter. Half of the customers were parents: that is 1/2, 0,5 or 50% of the customers.

To change a fraction into the other forms, find an equivalent fraction with denominator 10 or 100. For 3/5, multiply the numerator and the denominator by 2 to get 6/10. That is 0,6, which is 60/100 or 60%. For 7/20, multiply by 5 to get 35/100, which is 0,35 and 35%.

A hundred square grid shows this clearly. Shade 75 of the 100 small squares. You have shaded 75/100 of the grid, which is three quarters. As a decimal that is 0,75, and as a percentage it is 75%. The shaded part does not change; only the way we write it changes. In the same way, a pie chart that is half shaded shows 1/2, 0,5 or 50% of the circle.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing fractions, decimals and percentages that are equal.

## Subtopic: Converting and Comparing Forms

To change a decimal into a fraction, read its place value. 0,45 is 45 hundredths, so it is 45/100 and 45%. 0,4 is 4 tenths, so it is 4/10, which simplifies to 2/5. It is also 40%. 0,07 is 7 hundredths, so it is 7/100 and only 7%, not 70%.

To change a percentage into a decimal, write it out of 100 and then as hundredths. 62% is 62/100, which is 0,62. 8% is 8/100, which is 0,08.

Some fractions need simplifying after you convert. 0,75 is 75/100. Both 75 and 100 can be divided by 25, so 75/100 simplifies to 3/4. 0,2 is 2/10, which simplifies to 1/5. 35% is 35/100, which simplifies to 7/20.

To compare numbers written in different forms, change them all into the same form. Decimals are usually easiest. Which is the biggest: 3/5, 0,58 or 62%? 3/5 is 0,6, so write it as 0,60. 62% is 0,62. Now compare 0,60, 0,58 and 0,62 place by place. All have 0 wholes. Tenths: 6, 5 and 6, so 0,58 is the smallest. Hundredths of the other two: 0 and 2, so 0,62 is the biggest. In order from smallest to biggest: 0,58; 3/5; 62%.

Another example: order 0,3, 1/4 and 28%. As decimals they are 0,30, 0,25 and 0,28. From smallest to biggest: 1/4; 28%; 0,3.

## Subtopic: Solving Decimal Problems

On market day the class sells 100 cupcakes at R4,50 each. Multiply by 100: the digits move two places to the left, so the cupcakes bring in R450. They sell 10 trays of popcorn at R12,75 each, which brings in R127,50. The total takings are R450 plus R127,50, which is R577,50.

The ingredients cost R185,40. To find the profit, subtract. Line up the commas: R577,50 minus R185,40 is R392,10. Check by adding the profit and the costs: R392,10 plus R185,40 gives back R577,50.

The class fund tops the profit up to a round R400, and 25% of that R400 goes to the shelter. 25% is one quarter, and a quarter of R400 is R100.

A learner buys a juice for R6,50 and pays with R20. Count on: R6,50 plus 50 cents is R7, and R7 plus R13 is R20. The change is R13,50.

The popcorn table also measured its ingredients with decimals. They used 2,5 kg of mealies on Friday and 1,75 kg on Saturday. Altogether that is 4,25 kg. From a 5 kg bag, 0,75 kg is left over, which is three quarters of a kilogram.

Use the same steps for every word problem: read carefully, decide on the operation, estimate, calculate with the commas lined up, and check that your answer makes sense in the story.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 0,5 as 5%; 0,5 is 5 tenths, which is 50 hundredths, so it is 50%. Two: writing 1/4 as 0,4; a quarter is 25 hundredths, so it is 0,25. Three: writing 7/20 as 0,7 or 0,20; make the denominator 100 first, and 35/100 is 0,35. Four: writing 100 cupcakes at R4,50 as R4 500; times 100 moves the digits two places, so it is R450.

The questions for this section are with you now: solving market day problems with decimals.
