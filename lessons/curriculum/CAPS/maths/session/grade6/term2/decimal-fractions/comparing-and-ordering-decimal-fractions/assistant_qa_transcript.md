### subtopic_1 — Comparing Decimals by Place Value
**Assistant:** Why is 0,5 bigger than 0,25 when 25 is bigger than 5?

**Tutor:** Because the digits after the comma are not whole numbers; each one sits in a place with its own value. In 0,5 the 5 is in the tenths place, so it means 5 tenths, which is half. In 0,25 the 2 is in the tenths place and the 5 is in the hundredths place, so it means 2 tenths and 5 hundredths, which is a quarter. Half is more than a quarter. The easiest way to see this is to give both numbers the same number of decimal places. Add a zero to the end of 0,5 to get 0,50. Adding a zero at the end does not change the value, because 5 tenths is the same as 50 hundredths. Now you are comparing 50 hundredths with 25 hundredths, and 50 is clearly bigger. Money helps too: 0,5 of a rand is 50 cents and 0,25 of a rand is 25 cents. Would you rather have 50 cents or 25 cents? So the rule is never to compare decimals by counting how many digits they have. Compare place by place from the left, and the first place where the digits differ decides which number is bigger.

### subtopic_2 — Ordering Decimals
**Assistant:** In the swimming race, why does the smallest number win?

**Tutor:** Because the number is a time, and a smaller time means the swimmer reached the wall sooner. Megan swam 50 m in 34,98 seconds and Lerato swam the same distance in 35,09 seconds. Megan needed less time, so she was faster. If they had started together, Megan would have touched the wall first, about a tenth of a second before Lerato. So when you order race times from smallest to biggest, you are also ordering the swimmers from first place to last place. This is different from the long jump or the shot put, where the biggest distance wins. In a jump, a bigger number means the athlete went further. So before you decide who wins, always ask what the number measures. If it measures how long something took, smaller is better. If it measures how far or how high, bigger is better. The maths of comparing does not change, though. 34,98 is still smaller than 35,09, because 34 wholes is less than 35 wholes. Only the meaning of smaller changes, from less good to better.

### subtopic_3 — Number Lines and Rounding Decimals
**Assistant:** How do I know which digit to look at when I round a decimal?

**Tutor:** Look at the digit one place to the right of the place you are rounding to. If you are rounding to the nearest whole number, you keep the units, so you look at the tenths digit. If you are rounding to the nearest tenth, you keep the tenths, so you look at the hundredths digit. Then use the same rule as for whole numbers: if that digit is 5 or more, round up; if it is 4 or less, keep what you have and drop the rest. Take 3,68. To the nearest whole number, look at the tenths digit, which is 6. That is 5 or more, so 3,68 rounds up to 4. To the nearest tenth, look at the hundredths digit, which is 8. That is 5 or more, so 3,68 rounds up to 3,7. Check on a number line: 3,68 is between 3,6 and 3,7, and it is much closer to 3,7. Be careful when rounding up makes a new whole. 4,96 to the nearest tenth goes up from 9 tenths to 10 tenths, which is a whole, so the answer is 5,0.
