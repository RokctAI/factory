### subtopic_1 — Tenths and Hundredths as Decimals
**Assistant:** Why do we write a comma in decimals when some books and calculators use a dot?

**Tutor:** Both are correct ways to show the same thing, and different countries use different signs. In South Africa, as in many countries in Europe and Africa, the official way to write a decimal is with a comma: 3,68. In other countries, such as the United States and the United Kingdom, people use a point: 3.68. Most calculators and computers show a point, because they were designed for those countries. So if your calculator shows 12.45, read it as 12,45. The important thing is what the sign does: it separates the whole numbers on the left from the parts smaller than one on the right. Because we use the comma for decimals, South Africans group big whole numbers with a space, not a comma, like 62 027 503. That way a comma never means two different things. In your schoolwork, use the comma. When you read a price on a shop label, it might say R34,99 or R34.99, and both mean 34 rand and 99 cents. Whatever sign you see, the place values after it are always tenths and then hundredths.

### subtopic_2 — Place Value of Decimal Digits
**Assistant:** Why is 0,07 not the same as 0,7?

**Tutor:** Because the 7 is in a different place, and places after the comma work just like places before it: position decides value. In 0,7 the 7 is in the tenths place. It means 7 tenths, which is 7/10, or 70 hundredths. In 0,07 the 7 is in the hundredths place, and the 0 in front of it holds the tenths place empty. It means 7 hundredths, which is 7/100. So 0,7 is ten times bigger than 0,07. Money makes this easy to see. R0,70 is 70 cents, but R0,07 is only 7 cents. Would you rather have 70 cents or 7 cents? The zero after the comma in 0,07 is not decoration; it is holding a seat, just as the zero in 305 holds the tens seat. A good habit is to read decimals in words using the places: 0,07 is seven hundredths, and 0,7 is seven tenths. If you write 7/100 as a decimal, ask: which place is hundredths? It is the second place after the comma. So the 7 must go second, and something must fill the first place: a zero.

### subtopic_3 — Counting Forwards and Backwards in Decimals
**Assistant:** What happens when I count forwards past 9 tenths or 99 hundredths?

**Tutor:** You make a new whole, just like when you count past 9 units you make a new ten. Ten tenths make 1 whole. So counting in tenths from 2,7 goes 2,8, 2,9, and then the next tenth makes 10 tenths, which is 1 whole. The tenths place goes back to 0 and the units go up from 2 to 3, giving 3,0. Some learners write 2,10, but that would mean 2 and 10 hundredths, which is 2,1, a number we already passed. In the same way, counting in hundredths from 0,97 goes 0,98, 0,99, and then 100 hundredths make 1 whole, so the next number is 1,00. Counting backwards works the other way. From 1,0 taking away 0,2, there are no tenths to take, so you break the whole into 10 tenths and take 2 of them, leaving 0,8. A number line or a set of coins helps you see this. Count five-cent coins: R4,85, R4,90, R4,95, and the next five cents makes R5,00. You have exactly five rand. The carrying is the same as in whole numbers, just in smaller pieces.
