Hey, it's Thandi — if k, k squared and k cubed are swimming around your head right now, let me shrink this whole lesson to one picture: the brick.

Build a double-size brick out of normal bricks. You need two along, two across, two up — eight bricks. That's the entire topic. One k for every direction the object grows: lengths get one k, flat surfaces get two, volumes get three. Paint scales by k squared, water by k cubed. Everything else in the lesson was just that sentence wearing different numbers.

And reverse questions? Same wall, walked backwards. Volume times twenty-seven — cube root — k is three. Stop at k, then go wherever the question points.

Here's the honest part: this topic has almost no arithmetic in it. The marks are for counting directions, and you can count to three. So open the practice questions, and for each one just ask: how many directions changed? Say the power out loud before you calculate anything. Two or three questions in, you'll feel the pattern click, and this becomes one of the fastest full-mark questions on the paper. You've got this!
