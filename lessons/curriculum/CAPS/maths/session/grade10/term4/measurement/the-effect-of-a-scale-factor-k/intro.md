Today we answer the question examiners love because it tests understanding rather than arithmetic: what happens to volume and surface area when a dimension is multiplied by a scale factor k? The whole topic compresses into one sentence — lengths scale by k, areas by k squared, volumes by k cubed — one copy of k for every dimension of space. We stretch a box one direction at a time, watch a cylinder's radius count twice, then drive the machine in reverse, extracting k from a given change in volume or area. Own this pattern and these become the fastest marks on the paper, no rebuilding from scratch required.
