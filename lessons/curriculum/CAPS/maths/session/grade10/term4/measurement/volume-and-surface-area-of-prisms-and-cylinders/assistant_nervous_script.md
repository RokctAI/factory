Hey, it's Thandi — if formulas with pi in them make your shoulders tense up, breathe. This whole topic is two ideas wearing different outfits.

Filling and wrapping. That's it. Volume is filling: find the floor, count the floors — base area times height, for every prism and every cylinder that will ever appear on your paper. Surface area is wrapping: flatten the solid into its net and add up the panels, like opening a cereal box for recycling.

And the scary cylinder formula? You've held it in your hands a hundred times. The label on a can peels off as a rectangle — height of the can, once around the circle. Two pi r h isn't memory work; it's a peeled label.

You already reproduced the box, the can and the triangular prism in this lesson — the same six numbers, twice, in two different styles. The formulas agreed with the stories every single time. So go to the practice questions and start with the one that looks most like a box. Write r equals first, keep pi to the end, check your unit. One solid at a time, and this topic becomes the easiest marks in Paper 2. You've got this!
