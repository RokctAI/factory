Hey, it's Thandi — three topics in one revision lesson sounds heavy, but each of these runs on one tiny engine, and you already own all three.

Patterns: one constant difference. It becomes the coefficient of n, the first term pins down the constant, and the formula does all the climbing — forwards to term fifty, backwards to check whether a number is on the staircase at all. Fractional n means no. That's the whole topic.

Finance: two formulas, ladder and snowball. Interest on the original amount is the ladder — simple, hire purchase included. Interest on the growing total is the snowball — compound, inflation included. Read the story, pick the machine, and the arithmetic is grade eight work.

Measurement: a short catalogue. Base area times height for prisms and tins, one third of that for anything pointed, four thirds pi r cubed for the sphere. Inside height fills, leaning height wraps. And the scale factor law in six words: length k, area k squared, volume k cubed.

You've used every one of these this year, for marks, successfully. So open the practice set, say the engine out loud before each question — staircase, ladder or snowball, catalogue — and test every answer before moving on. Small checks stack straight into November. You've got this!
