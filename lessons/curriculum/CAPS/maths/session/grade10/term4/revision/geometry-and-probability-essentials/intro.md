Today is the revision sweep for the geometry and data half of the year: quadrilaterals and the midpoint theorem, analytical geometry, probability, and statistics. Four topics compress into four small toolkits — a family tree of shapes where each generation inherits every property before it, three coordinate formulas that are all Pythagoras and averages underneath, one addition rule that stops double counting, and the five-number portrait that draws any data set as a box. Everything is the exam-day version, worked through the classic examples and aimed straight at the traps that harvest marks every November: proving too much, flipping the gradient, counting the overlap twice, and taking quartiles from an unordered list.
