Hey, it's Thandi — four topics in one lesson looks like a mountain, but every one of them is a picture you can draw in a few pen strokes, and pictures are hard to forget.

The quadrilaterals are a family tree: parallelogram at the root, rectangle and rhombus as its children, square inheriting from both. Downwards it hands you properties for free; upwards you only ever prove the cheapest ticket — diagonals bisecting, one right angle, one pair of adjacent equal sides. Never buy what inheritance gives free.

Analytical geometry is three formulas about two points: how far, where's the middle, how steep. All three are Pythagoras and averages wearing coordinates. Parallel means equal gradients; perpendicular means flip and negate.

Probability is counting that refuses to double-count: overlap first in every Venn, subtract it once in every rule, and the outside catches the rest. Sanity-check against the class size — the totals will confess any error.

Statistics is one ordering, then five numbers, then a box. Order the data before touching a quartile, and choose your average out loud: mean for balanced data, median when something extreme is dragging.

You've drawn every one of these pictures this year, for marks. So open the practice set, sketch before you calculate, and let each picture check its own totals. Small wins stack straight into November. You've got this!
