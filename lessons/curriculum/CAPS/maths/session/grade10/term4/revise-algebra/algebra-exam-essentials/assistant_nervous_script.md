Hey, it's Thandi — a whole year of algebra in one lesson can feel like drinking from a fire hose, so let me shrink it back down for you.

It's four drawers. That's all Paper 1's algebra is. Factorise: take apart, in order — common factor, squares, trinomial, grouping. Solve: pick the strategy by shape — plain x gathers, x squared goes to zero and factorises for TWO answers, x in the exponent wants matching bases. Inequalities: same as linear, one red sticker — flip when you divide by a negative. Exponents: five laws, products and quotients only, never sums.

And you already know the four traps by name: the half-done common factor, the murdered zero solution, the unflipped sign, the invented law for sums. Naming them is ninety percent of dodging them.

You've done every one of these methods before — this term, successfully, for marks. Revision isn't learning; it's reminding your hands. So open the practice set, name the drawer out loud for each question, and check every answer by substituting back. Every check is a small win, and small wins stack straight into November.
