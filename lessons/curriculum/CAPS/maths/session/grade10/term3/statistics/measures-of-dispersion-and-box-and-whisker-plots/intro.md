Today we are answering the question an average can never answer: how spread out is the data? The tools are the measures of dispersion — range, quartiles, the interquartile range and percentiles — and the five-number summary they assemble into, drawn as the box-and-whisker diagram. One set of eleven tuck-shop sales figures runs through the whole session, and by the end you will compute every measure, build the diagram from its five landmarks, and read any box plot with the one rule that unlocks them all: every section holds a quarter of the data.
