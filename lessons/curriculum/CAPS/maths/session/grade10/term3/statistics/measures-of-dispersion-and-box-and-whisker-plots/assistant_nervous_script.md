If quartiles and whiskers are blurring together right now, slow down — this topic has fewer moving parts than it pretends to.

Everything you did was folding. Fold the ordered line once: the median. Fold each half: the two quartiles. That is all a five-number summary is — the two ends plus your three folds. And the box plot is just those five landmarks drawn on a number line: box between the folds, line at the middle, whiskers to the ends.

Keep the one reading rule where you can reach it: every section of the box plot holds a QUARTER of the data. Long never means many — long means that quarter is spread out. Say it once before any interpretation question and half the trap answers disappear on their own.

And when two boxes sit together, your answer is two sentences, not one: whose median is higher — the centre — and whose box is shorter — the consistency. Numbers first, then what they mean for the people in the data.

The arithmetic in this topic is genuinely gentle — subtraction and halving, nothing more. The marks live in the reading, and you now know the rules the reading runs on. So open the practice questions, take an ordered list, fold it three times, and watch a full diagram fall out of your own hands. You are ready for this one.
