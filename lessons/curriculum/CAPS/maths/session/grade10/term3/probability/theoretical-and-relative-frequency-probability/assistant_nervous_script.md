If probability feels like a topic where answers appear out of thin air, breathe — you have just seen that every answer came from plain counting or plain dividing. Nothing else happened in this whole lesson.

Keep the washing line hanging in your mind: impossible at 0, certain at 1, everything else pegged between. It is your error alarm forever — any answer below 0 or above 1 is the maths telling you to go back, and that check costs nothing.

Then remember there are only two engines, and the object tells you which to start. Fair and symmetrical — dice, coins, shaken bags? Count what could happen, count what you want, divide. Lopsided or real-world — bottle tops, weather, taxis? Divide what DID happen by how many times you tried. Two engines, one divide each.

The trap to step around is counting types instead of things: ten sweets means a denominator of ten, even though there are only three colours. Whisper "count things, never types" whenever a bag appears.

And the friendliest tool in the topic: the word "not" means one subtraction — 1 minus the probability you already have. No recounting, no stress.

So take these four anchors straight into the practice questions while they are warm. Start with a die, then a bag, then a bottle top. Each one you solve pegs your own confidence a little further right along the line — and it belongs there.
