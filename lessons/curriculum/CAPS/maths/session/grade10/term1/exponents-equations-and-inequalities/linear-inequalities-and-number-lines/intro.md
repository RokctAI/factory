Today we are solving linear inequalities — the questions whose answers are whole stretches of the number line rather than a single point. The algebra is the algebra you already own, with exactly one rule of its own: multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the sign, and we will see precisely why. Around that rule sit two skills the examination marks separately — the number-line sketch with its solid and open dots, and interval notation with its square and round brackets. Four worked inequalities carry the session, from the simplest one-step solve to a compound inequality and a market-stall profit problem, so the words, the algebra and the picture all land together.
