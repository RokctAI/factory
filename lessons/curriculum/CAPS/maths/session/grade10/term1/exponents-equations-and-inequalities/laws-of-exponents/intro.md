Today we take full command of the laws of exponents — the grammar every formula from here to matric is written in. Two tasks organise the whole session: simplifying one bracket-heavy expression with negative exponents, and solving the exponential equation three to the power x plus one equals twenty-seven. Each law will be justified from the single fact that an exponent counts repeated multiplication, so the rules become common sense rather than memory work. We will also tour the four classic mark-losing errors — the unsquared coefficient, mixed bases, the misread negative exponent, and the base multiplied by its counter — so you never repeat them in Paper 1.
