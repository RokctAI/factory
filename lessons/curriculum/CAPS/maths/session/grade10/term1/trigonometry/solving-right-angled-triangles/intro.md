Today the three trig ratios go to work: we are solving right-angled triangles — finding every unknown side and angle — and then pointing that skill at the problems the examination actually sets: masts, ladders, cliffs and cables, reached through angles of elevation and depression. Four worked problems carry the session, and each one turns on the same decision — which ratio, and whether the unknown sits on top of the fraction or underneath it. Along the way we bank the sense checks that catch errors before the marker does: the hypotenuse must be the longest side, and every observer's eye height must be added back.
