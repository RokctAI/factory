**Student:** I labelled the opposite and adjacent sides, but the memo swapped them. My triangle looked exactly the same as theirs!

**Thandi:** Same triangle, different angle — that's the trick! Opposite and adjacent aren't fixed to the triangle; they're fixed to *the angle you're using*. The side facing YOUR angle is its opposite; the arm touching it (that isn't the hypotenuse) is its adjacent. Switch to the other acute angle and the two labels swap places instantly — only the hypotenuse never moves. So make this your ritual: first mark the angle with a big arc, THEN label the sides from that angle's point of view. Angle first, labels second, every single time.

**Student:** Why do I have to know the special angle values by heart? The calculator knows them perfectly!

**Thandi:** Two reasons — one practical, one sneaky. Practical: exam questions literally say "without using a calculator," and they award marks for showing exact values like $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ — a decimal 0,866 earns nothing there, because it's rounded, not exact. Sneaky: in Grade 11 and 12, special angles hide inside bigger problems where recognising that $\sin 60° \times \cos 30° = \frac{3}{4}$ *exactly* keeps your algebra clean. But here's the relief: don't memorise — *rebuild*. The two special triangles take ten seconds to sketch, and Pythagoras hands you every value. Memory fades; triangles don't.

**Student:** When do I press shift-sin versus just sin? I keep doing the wrong one.

**Thandi:** Ask one question: what am I hunting — a SIDE or an ANGLE? Know the angle, want a side? Plain sin/cos/tan: you're feeding an angle in and getting a ratio out, like $13 \times \sin 22{,}6°$ to find the opposite side. Know two sides, want the angle? That's the reverse direction — shift-sin (inverse): you're feeding the ratio $\frac{5}{13}$ in and asking "which angle made this?" Think of sin as a machine and shift-sin as the same machine in reverse gear. Hunting sides: forward gear. Hunting angles: reverse gear.

**Student:** My calculator said sin 30° = −0,988 and I used it in a whole question before noticing. What is going on?

**Thandi:** Ouch — the radian trap claimed another victim! Your calculator was in radian mode: it read "30" as 30 *radians* (a totally different way of measuring angles that you'll meet much later), not 30 degrees. In degree mode, sin 30° = 0,5 exactly. The fix: look for the little D (or DEG) on your screen before every trig session — if you see R or RAD, change the mode in setup. And build in the tripwire: you KNOW sin 30° should be 0,5, so test it whenever you pick up your calculator. One two-second test protects a whole paper.
