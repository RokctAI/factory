Hey, it's Thandi — if that lesson felt like a lot of hunting and splitting and grouping for one little expression, take a breath. Here's the honest shape of it.

The entire technique is one hunt: two numbers, two conditions. They must multiply to a-times-c and add to b. Everything after the hunt — splitting the middle term, grouping, pulling out the bracket — is just careful unpacking that follows automatically once the right pair is found.

And you have TWO safety nets, which is more than most maths gives you. Net one: if your grouped brackets don't match, the method itself taps you on the shoulder before you can write a wrong answer. Net two: expanding your final brackets takes thirty seconds and proves you right beyond argument.

So start the practice questions with the sign-reading habit: product negative? Opposite signs. Sum negative? Big one's negative. Say it before you list any pairs. Three or four questions in, the hunt becomes a reflex — and trinomials show up in every algebra paper until matric, so this reflex pays rent forever. You've got this!
