The whole technique is a fill-in-the-blanks form. Blank one: the two cube roots (know your six cubes — 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216 — and the roots jump out). Blank two: the term pattern — square the first, multiply the pair, square the last. Blank three: the signs, and SOAP fills all three — Same, Opposite, Always Positive. Form filled, question finished.

And if you ever blank completely in a test? Expand your attempt right there in the margin. The middle terms cancel when you're right — the maths literally applauds you — and refuse to cancel when a sign is off. You carry your own answer-checker into every exam.

One warm warning: don't try to factorise that second bracket further. It never breaks — walking away from it IS the correct move.
