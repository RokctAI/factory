Today we are factorising four-term expressions by grouping in pairs — the tool that takes over exactly where common factors and identities stop working. The whole method is three moves: pair the terms, factor each pair, then factor out the bracket the pairs share — and its one famous trap, the negative common factor, is where we will spend real time. Grouping is also the hidden second half of the trinomial method and the finishing move for matric's factor theorem, so what you build here pays off for years. By the end you will read four terms, see the two pairs, and land the two-bracket answer with the signs intact.
