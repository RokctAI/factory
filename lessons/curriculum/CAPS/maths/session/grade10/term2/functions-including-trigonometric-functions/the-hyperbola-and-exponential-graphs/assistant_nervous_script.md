Hey, it's Thandi — two new graphs in one lesson is a lot, and I know the word "asymptote" sounds like something from a different subject. It is not. It just means a line the curve leans towards and never touches, and you already understand it from real life.

You cannot split a taxi fare among zero people. And no matter how many people squeeze in, somebody still pays something — the share never actually becomes nothing. Those two impossible things are the two asymptotes of the hyperbola. Same with doubling: halve a number forever and you always have something left, so that curve never lands on the bottom line either. The maths is just being honest about what cannot happen.

Now the mistake that costs the most marks in this whole topic, and it is a small one. The line the curve avoids MOVES. If the equation subtracts four at the end, the line is four below zero, not at zero. If it adds eight, the line is eight above. Read that last number first, draw the dashed line first, and then everything else fits around it.

Two more little ones worth guarding. A hyperbola has no y-intercept at all — do not go hunting for one, because x equals zero is not allowed. And for the doubling curve, the range is strictly above the line, never "on or below". One symbol, one mark.

And when the two graphs blur together, ask yourself one question: is the x on the ground floor or upstairs? Ground floor and squared, like x squared, means the curve turns around and comes back. Upstairs in the exponent, like two to the x, means it never turns — it just keeps going.

So tonight, sketch two graphs only: one shared-fare curve and one doubling curve, both with a number added at the end. Draw the dashed lines first. Once you see the curve bending toward a line it will never reach, this whole topic clicks and stays clicked. You've got this!
