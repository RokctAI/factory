Hey, it's Thandi — if the words "sketch the graph" make you want to close the book, I promise this one is smaller than it looks. There are only two numbers to read, and each one does exactly one job.

The number in front of the squared part decides which way the bowl faces and how narrow it is. Positive, it smiles. Negative, it frowns. Big number, narrow. Fraction, wide. That is the whole of it.

The number on its own at the end just lifts the bowl up or drops it down. And it is generous: it hands you the y-intercept and the height of the turning point in one go, no working needed. Subtract eight, and the bottom of the curve is sitting at eight below zero. Done, in two seconds.

Now the two mistakes I would most like to save you from. First: when you solve for the x-intercepts and get x squared equals four, the answer is x equals two AND x equals negative two. Both. Every time. Say "plus or minus" out loud as you write it — that habit alone is worth a mark in most papers. Second: the range is about how HIGH the curve goes, so it is written with y, not x. Range is heights, domain is widths.

And if the working ever hands you x squared equals a negative number, do not panic and do not change any signs. That is the maths telling you the truth: there are no x-intercepts. An upright bowl already sitting above the line was never going to reach it. Write that sentence and take the mark.

So tonight, do just three sketches. Shape, turn, crossings, one extra point — four moves, in that order, out loud. By the third one your hand will start moving before your brain does, and that is exactly the feeling you want walking into the paper. You've got this!
