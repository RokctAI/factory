Today we begin analytical geometry — the topic that puts Euclid's shapes onto a grid and replaces arguments about pictures with arithmetic about coordinates. Three formulae do almost all the work: distance, which is Pythagoras in disguise; midpoint, the only one of the three that adds; and gradient, rise over run, with its parallel and perpendicular conditions. We derive each one, work them on a single pair of points so the numbers stay familiar, and then use all three together to classify a quadrilateral and prove every claim — which is exactly how Paper 2 examines this topic.
