Hey, it's Mandy — I know grade twelve geometry has a reputation, and I know the proofs in this lesson look like walls of letters. Here is the secret the letters are hiding: this whole topic is two pictures, and you have seen both in real life a thousand times.

Picture one is the ladder and its shadow. A line parallel to one side of a triangle shares out the other two sides in the same fraction — climb a third of the ladder, use a third of the shadow. That is the proportionality theorem. Its proof is five moves, and the reasons are short: construction, same height, same height, same base between the same parallels, conclusion. Learn the five reasons, not the paragraphs; the paragraphs grow back from the reasons every time.

Picture two is the photograph and its enlargement. Equal angles mean same shape, and for triangles same shape forces one shared stretch factor on all three sides. When you use it, do one thing with religious care: write the vertex letters in the order of the equal angles. The order IS the pairing, and every ratio reads straight off it — first letters over first letters, and so on down the line.

The Pythagoras proof is those same two pictures stacked: fold the right angle down to the hypotenuse, notice each small triangle is a photograph of the whole, cross-multiply twice, add. Each leg squared is the hypotenuse times the piece under that leg — the shadow rule keeps them straight.

For the numeric questions, one discipline: call every length a part or a whole before you build a fraction, and never let a part-to-part ratio face a part-to-whole ratio across an equals sign.

Ladder, photograph, fold. Five reasons, one letter-order rule, one part-whole discipline. That is the entire topic, and it is learnable in an evening. You've got this!
