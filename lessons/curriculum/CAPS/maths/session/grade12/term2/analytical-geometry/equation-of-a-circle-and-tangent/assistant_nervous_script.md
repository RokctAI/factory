Hey, it's Mandy — I know the circle equation arrives with brackets, squares and sign flips, and the word tangent sounds like it belongs in a harder book. Both are gentler than they look. This lesson is one goat, one wheel, and two routines.

The goat first. A circle is every point one rope-length from a peg. The equation just writes that with the distance formula: x minus a squared, plus y minus b squared, equals r squared. Reading it is two questions: what makes each bracket zero? That is the centre — so x minus 3 means centre at positive 3, signs flipped. And what is the square root of the right side? That is the radius — 25 stores a rope of 5.

When the equation arrives multiplied out and messy, completing the square tidies it: group the x terms, group the y terms, halve the middle number, square it, and declare every sneaked-in number on the other side. The declaring is the whole skill — each bracket smuggles in a number, and the right side must be paid. Check by expanding back.

The wheel second. A tangent touches at one point, and the spoke to that point stands square to the road — always. Square means gradients multiply to minus 1. So the tangent routine is four small stops: confirm the point is on the circle, find the radius gradient with rise over run, flip and negate it, then write the line through the point with that gradient. Finish by substituting the touching point back in and watching it balance — a ten-second certificate.

Nothing in this topic is new machinery. It is the distance formula, rise over run, and perpendicular gradients — three old friends in one question. You've got this!
