## Subtopic: Question One — Algebra and Equations

The question, five parts. One point one: solve x times x minus four equals zero, for two marks. One point two: solve three x squared minus five x minus one equals zero, correct to two decimal places, for four marks. One point three: solve x minus four times x plus two greater than zero, for three marks. One point four: solve the square root of x plus five equals x minus one, for five marks. One point five: solve simultaneously, y equals x plus two and x squared plus y squared equals ten, for six marks. Twenty marks in total.

The attack plan: this is the routine opener, so the plan is speed with checks. Already-factorised means read the roots. Two decimal places means formula. An inequality means a parabola sketch. A surd means square and then vet. Simultaneous means substitute the linear into the quadratic.

One point one: the product is zero, so x equals nought or x equals four — one mark for each answer, and no expanding of brackets, because expanding a factorised equation is undoing the examiner's gift.

One point two: the formula. X equals five plus or minus the square root of, twenty-five plus twelve, all over six. The substitution into the formula is the first mark. The discriminant, thirty-seven, is the second. Then the two rounded answers: one comma eight five, and minus nought comma one eight — a mark each, paid only at two decimals.

One point three: roots minus two and four, parabola opening upward, positive outside the roots. One mark for the critical values, one for the sketch or sign analysis, one for the answer: x less than minus two or x greater than four.

One point four: square both sides — x plus five equals x squared minus two x plus one. That line is a mark. Rearrange to x squared minus three x minus four equals zero, factorise as x minus four times x plus one: two more marks. Candidates four and minus one: a mark. Then the vetting: x equals four gives three equals three, valid; x equals minus one gives two equals minus two, rejected. The selection with its check is the fifth mark.

One point five: substitute — x squared plus, x plus two squared, equals ten. One mark. Expand and simplify to two x squared plus four x minus six equals zero, then x squared plus two x minus three equals zero: two marks. Factorise, x plus three times x minus one: one mark. The paired answers: x equals one with y equals three, x equals minus three with y equals minus one — two marks, and unpaired answers forfeit them.

## Subtopic: Question Two — the Quadratic Number Pattern

The question: given the pattern three; eight; fifteen; twenty-four. Two point one: write the next term, one mark. Two point two: determine the general term T n, four marks. Two point three: which term of the pattern equals one hundred and sixty-eight, three marks. Eight marks in total.

The attack plan: differences first, always. First differences five, seven, nine — second difference constant at two, so the pattern is quadratic and the machinery is a n squared plus b n plus c.

Two point one: the next first difference is eleven, so the next term is thirty-five. One mark.

Two point two: two a equals the second difference, so a equals one — first mark. Then three a plus b equals the first of the first differences, five, giving b equals two — second mark. Then a plus b plus c equals the first term, three, giving c equals nought — third mark. The general term: T n equals n squared plus two n, and the final mark is for the formula stated and checked against a known term: four plus four is eight, confirmed.

Two point three: set n squared plus two n equal to one hundred and sixty-eight. Rearranged: n squared plus two n minus one hundred and sixty-eight equals zero — one mark. Factorised: n plus fourteen times n minus twelve — one mark. Then n equals twelve, with n equals minus fourteen rejected because term numbers are natural numbers — the rejection sentence shares the final mark with the answer.

## Subtopic: Question Three — Geometric Series

The question: consider the series one hundred and twenty-eight plus sixty-four plus thirty-two and so on. Three point one: explain why the sum to infinity exists, and calculate it, for three marks. Three point two: determine the smallest value of n for which S n exceeds two hundred and fifty-five, for four marks. Three point three: evaluate the sum from k equals one to twenty of three k minus one, for three marks. Ten marks in total.

The attack plan: identify a and r, quote the convergence condition, and for the inequality let the powers do the arguing. The sigma part is arithmetic wearing sigma clothing — read off the first term and the common difference.

Three point one: a is one hundred and twenty-eight, r is one half, and since r lies strictly between minus one and one the sum converges — that sentence is the first mark. S infinity equals a over one minus r: one hundred and twenty-eight over a half — substitution mark — equals two hundred and fifty-six, answer mark.

Three point two: S n equals two hundred and fifty-six times, one minus a half to the n. Setting up the inequality, two hundred and fifty-six times one minus a half to the n, greater than two hundred and fifty-five, is the first mark. Isolating the power gives a half to the n less than one over two hundred and fifty-six — second mark. Recognising one over two hundred and fifty-six as a half to the eighth: third mark. Since halving shrinks, n must exceed eight, so the smallest n is nine — final mark, with the check that S nine equals two hundred and fifty-five comma five confirming it.

Three point three: the terms run two, five, eight, up to fifty-nine — arithmetic, a equals two, d equals three, twenty terms; identifying that is the first mark. The sum formula: twenty over two, times, two times two plus nineteen times three — substitution mark. Ten times sixty-one: six hundred and ten — answer mark.

## Subtopic: Question Four — Functions and Inverses

The question: given f of x equals two over x minus one, plus three. Four point one: write the equations of the asymptotes, two marks. Four point two: determine the x and y intercepts, three marks. Four point three: sketch f, showing all asymptotes and intercepts, three marks. Four point four: given g of x equals three to the power x, determine g inverse in the form y equals, two marks; and if the point two semicolon nine lies on g, write the corresponding point on g inverse, one mark. Eleven marks in total.

The attack plan: hyperbola checklist — asymptotes from the structure, intercepts from substitution, sketch last so it inherits everything. For the inverse: swap, solve, and swap the point.

Four point one: vertical asymptote where the denominator is zero, x equals one; horizontal from the vertical shift, y equals three. A mark each.

Four point two: y-intercept, substitute x equals nought: two over minus one, plus three, equals one — one mark. X-intercept, set f to zero: two over x minus one equals minus three, so x minus one equals minus two thirds, x equals one third — two marks, one for the setup, one for the value.

Four point three: two branches hugging the asymptote cross, passing through nought semicolon one and one third semicolon nought. One mark for correct branch positions, one for asymptotes drawn and labelled, one for intercepts labelled.

Four point four: swap x and y in y equals three to the x, giving x equals three to the y; making y the subject delivers y equals log base three of x — the swap is one mark, the log form the second. The point two semicolon nine reflects to nine semicolon two — one mark.

## Subtopic: Question Five — Finance, Growth and Decay

The question. Five point one: convert a nominal rate of eight percent per annum, compounded quarterly, to the effective annual rate, for three marks. Five point two: a loan of nine hundred thousand rand is repaid over two hundred and forty equal monthly payments at ten comma five percent per annum compounded monthly, payments starting one month after the loan is granted; calculate the monthly payment, for four marks. Five point three: calculate the total amount repaid over the full term, and the total interest paid, for two marks. Nine marks in total.

The attack plan: part one is the effective rate identity. Part two is the present value annuity formula off the information sheet, with i as the monthly rate. A quick timeline confirms the standard setup: loan today, first payment one month later, two hundred and forty payments.

Five point one: one plus i effective equals, one plus nought comma zero eight over four, to the power four — writing that identity is two marks. Evaluating: one comma zero two to the fourth is one comma zero eight two four, so the effective rate is eight comma two four percent — final mark, quoted as a percentage.

Five point two: the formula — P equals x times, one minus one plus i to the minus n, over i. Identifying i as nought comma one zero five over twelve, which is nought comma zero zero eight seven five, and n as two hundred and forty, earns the first mark. Substituting nine hundred thousand on the left: second mark. Rearranging for x: third mark. The payment: eight thousand nine hundred and eighty-five rand and forty-two cents — final mark, sensibly rounded to the cent.

Five point three: total repaid is two hundred and forty times the payment — about two million, one hundred and fifty-six thousand, five hundred rand — one mark. Interest is that total minus the nine hundred thousand borrowed: about one comma two six million rand — one mark. More interest than loan: say it, because examiners reward the observation when they ask for comment.

## Subtopic: Question Six — Differential Calculus

The question. Six point one: determine f prime of x from first principles if f of x equals three x squared minus two, for five marks. Six point two: determine d y d x if y equals four x cubed minus the square root of x plus two over x, for four marks. Six point three: given f of x equals x cubed minus six x squared plus nine x, determine the intercepts and turning points and sketch the graph, for eight marks. Seventeen marks in total.

The attack plan: first principles is a recited ritual — definition, expansion, division, limit. The rules question is a rewriting question in disguise. The cubic is the full anatomy: factorise, differentiate, classify.

Six point one: write the definition — f prime of x equals the limit as h tends to nought of f of x plus h minus f of x over h — one mark for the definition with the limit present. F of x plus h: three x squared plus six x h plus three h squared minus two — one mark for correct expansion. The difference: six x h plus three h squared — one mark. Divide by h: six x plus three h — one mark. Take the limit: f prime of x equals six x — final mark, and the limit symbol must have survived until this line.

Six point two: rewrite — y equals four x cubed minus x to the half plus two x to the minus one; the rewrite is the first mark. Differentiate term by term: twelve x squared — mark; minus a half x to the minus a half — mark; minus two x to the minus two — mark.

Six point three: factorise f as x times x minus three squared — one mark. Intercepts: x equals nought and x equals three, the origin and a touching double root — two marks. Derivative: three x squared minus twelve x plus nine, zero at x equals one and x equals three — two marks. Turning points: one semicolon four, a local maximum, and three semicolon nought, a local minimum — two marks with coordinates. The sketch, rising through the origin to the maximum, dipping to touch the axis at three, then rising: one mark for correct shape with all points labelled.

## Subtopic: Question Seven — Probability and Counting

The question. Seven point one: events A and B are independent with P of A equal to nought comma four and P of B equal to nought comma five; calculate P of A and B, and P of A or B, for four marks. Seven point two: how many different arrangements can be made of all the letters of the word REVISION, where the two letter I's are treated as identical, for two marks. Seven point three: what is the probability that such an arrangement begins with the letter R, for two marks. Eight marks in total. The seven questions together follow the guideline blueprint — every Paper 1 topic visited, with the heavier topics carrying the heavier questions.

The attack plan: independence unlocks the product rule; the addition rule then delivers the union. Repeated letters divide the factorial. For the final part, count the favourable arrangements the same way and divide.

Seven point one: independence means P of A and B equals P of A times P of B — stating that meaning is the first mark. The product: nought comma two — second mark. The addition rule: P of A or B equals nought comma four plus nought comma five minus nought comma two — third mark. The answer: nought comma seven — fourth mark.

Seven point two: eight letters with a repeated pair arrange in eight factorial over two factorial ways — the setup is one mark. Forty thousand three hundred and twenty over two: twenty thousand one hundred and sixty — answer mark.

Seven point three: fix R in front; the remaining seven letters, still carrying the repeated I pair, arrange in seven factorial over two factorial ways — two thousand five hundred and twenty; that count is one mark. The probability: two thousand five hundred and twenty over twenty thousand one hundred and sixty, which is one eighth, or nought comma one two five — final mark.
