## Subtopic: Question One — Statistics and the Box and Whisker

The question: the marks of ten learners are twelve, forty-five, fifty-two, fifty-eight, sixty-one, sixty-four, sixty-eight, seventy-two, seventy-five, ninety-four. One point three: write the five-number summary and draw the box and whisker diagram, four marks. One point four: determine, with calculation, whether the data contains an outlier, three marks. One point five: how many of the marks lie within one standard deviation of the mean, two marks. Thirteen marks in total.

The attack plan: calculator statistics mode for the first two parts, ordered-list reading for the quartiles, the fence formula for the outlier, and an interval count at the end.

One point one: the sum is six hundred and one, over ten — the mean is sixty comma one. Setup mark, answer mark. One point two: the calculator delivers the standard deviation as twenty comma five — method mark for the mode entry, answer mark. One point three: minimum twelve, lower quartile fifty-two, median sixty-two comma five — the mean of sixty-one and sixty-four — upper quartile seventy-two, maximum ninety-four. Two marks for the summary, two for a box correctly spanning the quartiles with whiskers to the extremes. One point four: the interquartile range is twenty, so the lower fence is fifty-two minus one comma five times twenty — twenty-two. Twelve lies below twenty-two, so twelve is an outlier. Formula mark, calculation mark, conclusion mark — the eye alone earns nothing here. One point five: the interval runs from thirty-nine comma six to eighty comma six; counting the ordered list places eight of the ten marks inside. Interval mark, count mark.

## Subtopic: Question Two — Regression and the Fenced Prediction

The question: a shop records temperature against cans of cooldrink sold — fifteen degrees, twelve cans; eighteen degrees, fifteen; twenty-one degrees, nineteen; twenty-four degrees, twenty-two; twenty-seven degrees, twenty-six; thirty degrees, twenty-nine. The attack plan: everything before the criticism is calculator craft — enter the six pairs once, check the entry, then read off a, b, and r. The last part is a fence argument.

## Subtopic: Question Three — the Analytical Triangle

The question: in the Cartesian plane, A is minus two semicolon five, B is six semicolon one, C is four semicolon minus three. Three point one: calculate the gradient of A B, two marks. ## Subtopic: Question Six — Triangles in Two and Three Dimensions

The question. Six point one: in triangle P Q R, side p equals twelve units, angle P equals sixty-four degrees, angle Q equals fifty-two degrees; calculate side q, three marks. Six point two: a triangle has sides seven and nine enclosing an angle of sixty degrees; calculate the third side and the area of the triangle, five marks. Six point three: from a point forty-five metres from the base of a vertical tower, the angle of elevation of the top is thirty-eight degrees; calculate the height of the tower, three marks. Eleven marks in total.

The attack plan: a side facing a known angle summons the sine rule; two sides trapping an angle summon the cosine rule and the area rule from the same data; the tower is one right triangle with tan.

Six point one: the sine rule — q over sin Q equals p over sin P, one mark. Substitute: q equals twelve sin fifty-two over sin sixty-four — one mark. The answer: approximately ten comma five two units — one mark. A quick sanity glance: the smaller angle faces the shorter side, fifty-two against sixty-four, ten and a half against twelve — consistent. Six point two: the cosine rule — the third side squared equals forty-nine plus eighty-one, minus two times seven times nine times cos sixty — formula and substitution, two marks. Cos sixty is a half, so the expression is one hundred and thirty minus sixty-three — sixty-seven, and the side is root sixty-seven, approximately eight comma one nine — one mark. The area rule: a half times seven times nine times sin sixty — one mark — giving approximately twenty-seven comma two eight square units — one mark. Six point three: the tower is opposite the thirty-eight degree angle with the forty-five metre distance adjacent, so the height is forty-five tan thirty-eight — setup and tan statement, two marks. Approximately thirty-five comma one six metres — answer mark, with units, because a length without units is an incomplete answer in Paper 2.

## Subtopic: Question Seven — the Euclidean Rider

The question: S A T is a tangent to a circle at A. Chords A B and A C are drawn, with B and C on the circle so that the angle between the tangent and chord A B — angle T A B — equals forty degrees, and the angle between the tangent and chord A C on the other side — angle S A C — equals sixty-five degrees. Seven point one: write down, with a reason, the size of angle A C B, two marks. Seven point two: write down, with a reason, the size of angle A B C, two marks. Seven point three: calculate the size of angle B A C, two marks. Seven point four: prove the theorem that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment, five marks. Eleven marks in total.

The attack plan: the tan-chord theorem twice — once on each side of the tangent — then the angle sum of a triangle. The proof is one of the seven examinable performances, delivered with its construction.

Seven point one: the angle in the alternate segment to angle T A B is angle A C B, so angle A C B equals forty degrees — statement mark, and the reason, tan chord theorem, is the second mark. Seven point two: on the other side, angle S A C corresponds to the angle in its alternate segment, angle A B C, so angle A B C equals sixty-five degrees — again statement mark plus the tan chord reason mark. Seven point three: the angles of triangle A B C sum to one hundred and eighty degrees, so angle B A C equals one hundred and eighty minus forty minus sixty-five — seventy-five degrees. Calculation mark, and the reason — angle sum in triangle — the second mark. Notice the check: seventy-five plus forty plus sixty-five restores one hundred and eighty exactly.

Seven point four, the examinable proof, delivered as a performance. Construction: draw the diameter A O D from the point of contact, and join D to B — construction mark. The angle between the tangent and the diameter is ninety degrees, since the tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact — one mark. The angle A B D, subtended by the diameter, is ninety degrees, angle in a semicircle — one mark. So the angle D A B equals ninety minus angle A D B, from the angle sum in triangle A B D; and the angle between the tangent and chord A B equals ninety minus angle D A B — which makes it equal to angle A D B — one mark. But angle A D B and angle A C B are angles in the same segment on chord A B, therefore equal — and so the tangent-chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment — final mark.
