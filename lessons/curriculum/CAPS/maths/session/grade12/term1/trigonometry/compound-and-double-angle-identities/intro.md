Today we add the last major tools to the trigonometry identity toolbox: the compound-angle identities for the sine and cosine of a sum or difference, and their famous special case, the double angle. These identities open the door to exact values without a calculator, to identity proofs, and to the equations that Paper 2 loves — and the very first lesson is that sine of a sum is never the sum of the sines. We will master the four expansions, manufacture the exact value of fifteen degrees, meet the three faces of cos two alpha, and use them to crack a full trigonometric equation.
