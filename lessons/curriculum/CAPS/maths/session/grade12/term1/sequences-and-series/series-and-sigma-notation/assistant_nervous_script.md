Hey, it's Mandy — I know the sigma symbol looks like the moment maths stopped being for you. It is not a new operation. It is a packing slip: start at the bottom number, stop at the top number, build each item from the recipe on the right, add them up. Read three sigma expressions out loud tonight and the alphabet fear is gone by the fourth.

Then there are only two sum formulas in the whole topic, and each comes with a story that rebuilds it if your memory blanks. Arithmetic: fold the queue. First plus last, second plus second-last, every pair worth the same, n over 2 pairs. That IS the formula. Geometric: write the sum, multiply the line by r, subtract — the middle collapses and one minus r to the n is what survives. Practise each derivation twice on paper; both are askable and both are free marks once the move is in your hands.

The how-many-terms questions always end in an equation — a quadratic for arithmetic, a same-base exponent hunt for geometric. Solve it fully, then remember what n is: a count. Reject anything negative or broken in one written sentence, because that sentence is a mark.

And the sum to infinity is the friendliest thing here, not the scariest. Ratio strictly between minus one and one? Then the series creeps to a wall and stops: a over one minus r. Check the condition first, every time — and if x lives inside the ratio, the condition becomes the question: solve minus one less than the ratio less than one.

Packing slip, fold, collapse, creep to the wall. Four pictures, two formulas, and every question in this topic opens to one of them. You've got this!
