The test: divide instead of subtract. Neighbour over neighbour, along the whole list, and a constant answer certifies the sequence geometric. Fractions shrink, negatives alternate the sign, and both are fine — only zero is banned.

The general term: start times ratio to the power of the jumps. The n minus one is the same off-by-one as before — term one is the start, so term seven has six multiplications behind it. Build the formula, then test it on a term you can see. If it reproduces term four, it will not lie about term ten.

Two terms given: divide the bigger fact by the smaller and the start cancels, leaving r to the power of the steps between them. Take the root, watch for the plus-or-minus when the root is even, then recover a. Three terms with an x inside: middle squared equals the product of the neighbours — one equation, and the x squared usually cancels to leave something linear. Substitute back and divide to see the constant ratio with your own eyes.
