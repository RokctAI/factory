Today we move from listing numbers to adding them: series, sigma notation, and the sum formulas that add hundreds of terms in one line. This is the machinery the examiners test hardest in the sequences section of Paper 1 — reading and writing the sigma instruction, the arithmetic sum with its folding proof, the geometric sum with its collapsing proof, and the strangest result of the year: an infinite list of numbers adding up to a single finite value. By the end you will fold queues like Gauss, collapse geometric sums, and turn any recurring decimal into the fraction it has been all along.
