Hey, it's Mandy — I know the moment the letters a, d and T n started raining down, this felt like a formula-memorising contest. It is not. An arithmetic sequence is a staircase with equal steps, and everything in this lesson is one of three questions about that staircase.

Question one: is it really a staircase? Subtract each term from the next. Same gap everywhere means arithmetic; one different gap means not. Do the subtraction in order — later term minus earlier term — so a downward staircase shows its negative d honestly.

Question two: what is on step n? The general term, T n equals a plus n minus one times d. The n minus one is just the fact that the bottom step needs no climbing — term four took three jumps, not four. Build the formula, then test it on a term you can already see. If the formula reproduces term four correctly, trust it all the way to term two hundred.

Question three: which step holds a given number? Set T n equal to the number and solve for n. If n comes out a natural number, that is the position. If n comes out a decimal, the number is not in the sequence — say so in a sentence, because the sentence is the answer. Only the reaching-a-level word problems round up, and only because a salary passes a target mid-staircase.

For the x-inside-the-terms questions, one line: middle minus first equals last minus middle. Solve, substitute back, and look at the real numbers to see the equal gaps with your own eyes. And for inserted means, count spaces like fence posts — three posts between two corners make four spaces, always one more jump than the means you planted.

Same gap, start plus jumps, and n must be whole. That is the entire lesson, and every mark in it is reachable. You've got this!
