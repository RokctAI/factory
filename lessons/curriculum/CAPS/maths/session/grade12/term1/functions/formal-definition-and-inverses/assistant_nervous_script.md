Hey, it's Mandy — I know the words formal definition and restricted domain make this lesson sound like a legal document. It is not. The whole lesson is one vending machine and one mirror, and every mark in it comes from three questions you can always answer.

Question one: is it a function? One input, one output, every time. Slide a vertical line across the graph — if it never cuts twice, the machine works. And remember the promise only runs one way: two inputs sharing an output is fine; one input with two outputs is not.

Question two: what is the inverse? Swap x and y, solve for y again, done. Then spend ten seconds on the check that saves everything: the original sent 1 to 5, so your inverse must send 5 back to 1. If it does, your algebra is certified. On the graph, the inverse is just the original reflected in the diagonal line y equals x — swap the coordinates of the points you know and redraw.

Question three: is the inverse a FUNCTION? Draw a horizontal line on the original. If it cuts twice, two inputs share an output, and the reversed machine will not know which one to give back — that is the plus or minus square root problem with the parabola. The fix is one clean move: keep one arm. Restrict to x greater than or equal to 0 or to x less than or equal to 0, and let the old domain choose the sign of the root, because the old domain becomes the new range.

That is the entire lesson: vertical line for function, swap and check for inverse, horizontal line and one arm for the parabola. Nothing here is beyond you. You've got this!
