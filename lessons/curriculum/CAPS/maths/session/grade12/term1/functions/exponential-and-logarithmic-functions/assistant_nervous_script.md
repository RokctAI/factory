Hey, it's Mandy — I know the word logarithm sounds like it was invented to intimidate. It was actually invented to save time, and this whole lesson is two graphs and one sentence.

The sentence first: a log is an exponent. Log base 2 of 8 is just the answer to the question, 2 to the power what gives 8? Three. That is all a log ever is. Every scary-looking conversion — y equals log base b of x means x equals b to the y — is that one sentence wearing algebra. When a conversion confuses you, ask which number is the exponent, and the other two numbers place themselves.

Graph one: the exponential, y equals b to the x. Through the point 0 and 1, always positive, hugging the x axis on one side. Base bigger than 1 rises; base between 0 and 1 falls. Domain everything, range y greater than 0.

Graph two: the log, which is graph one reflected in the diagonal y equals x. So swap everything: through 1 and 0, asymptote standing up the y axis, domain x greater than 0, range everything. Two anchor points and a dashed wall and the sketch is done — just never let the curve touch the y axis, the same way the exponential never lands on the x axis.

And solving for an exponent is the sentence again, in action. Isolate the power first. If both sides share a base, match the exponents — 2 to the x equals 16 gives x equals 4. If not, call the log: 3 to the x equals 20 means x equals log 20 over log 3, about 2,73, and you can even check it, because 20 sits between 3 squared and 3 cubed.

One sentence, two mirror graphs, one solving routine. That is the entire lesson. You've got this!
