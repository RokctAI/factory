Hey, it's Mandy — I know factorials and counting principles can make a page look like it is shouting at you, exclamation marks everywhere. The truth is gentler: this is the topic where you count clothes and photographs, and the whole thing runs on dashes.

The core habit: draw a dash for every decision, in order. Write above each dash how many options are available at that moment. Multiply along. That is the fundamental counting principle, and it answers most questions on its own — 4 shirts, 3 trousers, 2 shoes: 24 outfits. Multiply along a chain of AND decisions; add only between OR worlds that cannot both happen.

When things get used up — people in a photo row, letters of a word — the dash numbers shrink by one each time: 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. That product is 6 factorial, 720. The exclamation mark is nothing but shrink-and-multiply written small.

Restrictions have exactly three cures, and every question uses one of them. Fussy first: a forced position gets filled before anything else. Glue: people who must stand together become one unit — arrange the units, then times 2 for the swap inside the knot; the times 2 is the step everyone forgets, so say knot inside out loud. Complement: for must-not-be-together, count together and subtract from the total.

Probability is the same machine run twice: count the whole world for the bottom, count your wish for the top, divide. A random PIN with all different digits: 5 040 over 10 000, which is 0,504. Friends together in the photo: 240 over 720, one third. Just make sure top and bottom live in the same world — define what one outcome is before counting either.

Dashes, shrink, three cures, one fraction. Nothing here needs talent — only neat slots. You've got this!
