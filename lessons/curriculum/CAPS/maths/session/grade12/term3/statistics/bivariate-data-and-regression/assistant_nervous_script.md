Hey, it's Mandy — I know regression and correlation sound like a pair of diseases, and the calculator's statistics mode looks like the cockpit of a plane. Relax: this is the most routine-driven topic in the whole paper, and the routine has five stops.

Stop one: the picture. Dots on a board, driver variable across, outcome up, never joined. Three judgements by eye — which way does the cloud lean, is the lane straight, is the queue tight? Say all three in words. That is already marks.

Stop two: the line. The calculator does ALL the fitting — enter the pairs, read off a and b for y hat equals a plus b x. Your job is only the sentence: b is the price of one unit of x, in units of y. One more hour of study, about 4,57 more percentage points. Units plus context, and the word predicted.

Stop three: the number r, between minus 1 and 1. Sign is the lean, size is the hug. Report both in words: very strong positive linear correlation. And glance at the picture first — r cannot see curves.

Stop four: prediction, inside the fence. Substitute an x from within the data range and trust the answer. Outside the range, calculate if asked, then flag it: extrapolation, unreliable — and if it predicts more than 100 percent, say it is impossible. The refusal IS the answer.

Stop five: the sentence you must never write — one variable causes the other. Association yes, causation never, and the ice cream and drowning story is your shield.

Picture, line, r, fenced prediction, no cause. Five stops, all of them drillable, most of them words rather than algebra. This topic hands out marks to anyone with the checklist. You've got this!
