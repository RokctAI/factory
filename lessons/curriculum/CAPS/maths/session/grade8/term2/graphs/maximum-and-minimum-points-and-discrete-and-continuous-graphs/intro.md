Today we add two more features to reading graphs: the highest and lowest points — the maximum and minimum — and whether the graph is made of separate dots or a joined line — discrete or continuous. A thrown ball's height has a maximum at the top of its flight; the temperature through a day has a maximum in the afternoon and a minimum before dawn. The number of learners in a class can only be a whole number, so its graph is dots; the depth of water in a bath takes every value in between, so its graph is a line. By the end you will locate and describe maxima and minima, decide whether a graph should be dots or a line, and explain why.
