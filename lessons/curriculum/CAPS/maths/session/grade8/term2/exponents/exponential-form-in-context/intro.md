Today we use everything from the exponents topic to solve problems set in real situations: bacteria doubling, areas of squares and volumes of cubes, populations and distances written in scientific notation, and money that grows by a fixed factor. A colony of bacteria that doubles every hour and starts with one cell has two to the power six, sixty-four cells after six hours; a cube with volume three hundred and forty-three cubic centimetres has edge seven centimetres. By the end you will read a problem, decide whether it needs a power or a root, set up the exponential form, calculate, and give the answer with units.
