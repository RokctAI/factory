# Part 1 — Expert

This session develops the calculation of squares, cubes, square roots and cube roots of integers and rational numbers. A power of a fraction is found by raising numerator and denominator separately, and a root of a fraction by taking the root of numerator and denominator separately. A power of a decimal is found either through its fraction form or by counting decimal places, and a root of a decimal through its fraction form or by halving or thirding the number of decimal places. The anchor values are two thirds squared, four ninths; the square root of twenty-five over sixty-four, five eighths; nought comma three squared, nought comma zero nine; and the cube root of nought comma zero zero eight, nought comma two.

## Subtopic: Squares and Cubes of Integers Revised

Begin with integers, because the fraction and decimal methods depend on them. The square of an integer is the integer multiplied by itself. Twelve squared is one hundred and forty-four. Negative twelve squared, with brackets, is also one hundred and forty-four, because two negatives multiply to a positive. The cube of an integer is three factors. Five cubed is one hundred and twenty-five. Negative five cubed, with brackets, is negative one hundred and twenty-five, because three negatives leave one unpaired.

Squares you must know: one to fifteen, giving one, four, nine, sixteen, twenty-five, thirty-six, forty-nine, sixty-four, eighty-one, one hundred, one hundred and twenty-one, one hundred and forty-four, one hundred and sixty-nine, one hundred and ninety-six, two hundred and twenty-five. Twenty squared is four hundred and twenty-five squared is six hundred and twenty-five. Cubes you must know: one to ten, giving one, eight, twenty-seven, sixty-four, one hundred and twenty-five, two hundred and sixteen, three hundred and forty-three, five hundred and twelve, seven hundred and twenty-nine, one thousand.

Square roots of integers reverse the list. The square root of one hundred and sixty-nine is thirteen. The square root of one hundred and ninety-six is fourteen. Every positive perfect square has two numbers whose square it is — thirteen and negative thirteen both square to one hundred and sixty-nine — but the square root sign means the non-negative one. If a question wants both, it says so.

Cube roots: the cube root of three hundred and forty-three is seven; the cube root of negative seven hundred and twenty-nine is negative nine. Cube roots keep the sign of what is inside.

For larger numbers, use prime factors. The square root of three hundred and twenty-four: three hundred and twenty-four is two squared times three to the power four; halve the exponents, two times three squared, eighteen. The cube root of one thousand seven hundred and twenty-eight: two to the power six times three cubed; third the exponents, two squared times three, twelve. Prime factorisation turns an unfamiliar root into exponent arithmetic from the earlier lessons. Questions on integer powers and roots follow now.

## Subtopic: Squares and Cubes of Fractions

To square a fraction, square the numerator and square the denominator. Two thirds squared is two squared over three squared, four over nine. The reason is the product law for exponents applied to a quotient: bracket a over b bracket to the n is a to the n over b to the n, because n copies of a over b multiply to n copies of a on top and n copies of b underneath.

Cubes the same way. Three quarters cubed is twenty-seven over sixty-four. One half cubed is one over eight. Negative two fifths cubed, with brackets, is negative eight over one hundred and twenty-five — the sign follows the odd-exponent rule exactly as for integers. Negative two fifths squared is positive four over twenty-five.

Notice what happens to the size. Two thirds is less than one, and four ninths is smaller still; four ninths is about nought comma four four, less than nought comma six seven. Squaring a proper fraction makes it smaller, the opposite of what squaring does to a whole number bigger than one. Cubing makes it smaller again. Eight over twenty-seven is less than four ninths. This surprises learners who expect powers always to grow, and it is tested.

Mixed numbers must be converted first. One and a half squared is not one and a quarter. Convert: three halves, squared, is nine quarters, which is two and a quarter. A learner who squares the whole number and the fraction separately has used the sum error from the laws lesson — the exponent does not distribute over the plus inside a mixed number.

Simplify before squaring when you can. Six eighths squared: simplify six eighths to three quarters first, then square to nine sixteenths. Squaring first gives thirty-six over sixty-four, which simplifies to the same nine sixteenths but with bigger numbers to handle.

Write fractions in lowest terms and never as decimals unless asked, because the exact fraction is the mark. Questions on powers of fractions follow.

## Subtopic: Square Roots and Cube Roots of Fractions

To take the square root of a fraction, take the square root of the numerator and the square root of the denominator. The square root of twenty-five over sixty-four is five over eight. Check: five eighths squared is twenty-five over sixty-four. The cube root of eight over twenty-seven is two thirds. Check: two thirds cubed is eight over twenty-seven.

The rule is the root law for quotients: the root of a over b is the root of a over the root of b, which is the power-of-a-quotient law run backwards. It requires both the numerator and the denominator to be perfect squares, or perfect cubes, for the answer to be a whole-number fraction. The square root of two over nine is not a nice fraction, because two is not a perfect square; in Grade 8 you leave it or estimate it.

Simplify first when the fraction is not in lowest terms, because simplifying can reveal perfect squares. The square root of eighteen over fifty: neither is a perfect square, but eighteen over fifty simplifies to nine over twenty-five, whose root is three fifths. The square root of twelve over twenty-seven: simplify to four over nine, root two thirds.

Mixed numbers again convert first. The square root of two and a quarter: nine quarters, root three halves, one and a half. The square root of one and seven ninths: sixteen over nine, root four thirds, one and a third.

Signs: the square root sign gives the non-negative root, so the square root of twenty-five over sixty-four is positive five eighths. The cube root follows the sign inside: the cube root of negative one over one thousand is negative one tenth.

A useful check on size. The square root of a proper fraction is bigger than the fraction, because squaring made it smaller and the root undoes that. The square root of a quarter is a half, and a half is bigger than a quarter. If your root of a proper fraction comes out smaller than the fraction, you have squared instead of rooted. Questions on roots of fractions follow.

## Subtopic: The Pizza Slice Squared

Here is a fraction squared that you can see. A pizza is cut into three equal strips and you take two of them: two thirds of the pizza. Now someone says: take two thirds of what you have. Two thirds of two thirds. Cut your two strips crossways into three and take two of those three rows. You now have a two-by-two block of small pieces out of a three-by-three grid of the whole pizza: four pieces out of nine. Two thirds squared is four ninths.

The picture explains the rule. Squaring the numerator counts the small pieces you have — two rows of two, four. Squaring the denominator counts the small pieces in the whole — three rows of three, nine. Top squared over bottom squared. And it explains why the answer is smaller than two thirds: taking a fraction of a fraction leaves you with less than you started with. Four ninths of a pizza is less than two thirds of one.

A cube is the same trick in three dimensions. A block of cheese is three units each way, and you cut out a two-by-two-by-two corner: eight small cubes out of twenty-seven. Two thirds cubed is eight twenty-sevenths. Smaller again.

The root goes backwards. If a square of garden is twenty-five sixty-fourths of a square metre — a strange number, but suppose — how long is its side? The side times itself is twenty-five over sixty-four. Five eighths times five eighths is twenty-five sixty-fourths, so the side is five eighths of a metre. Root of top, root of bottom.

Now a recipe. It calls for one and a half cups of flour and you want to scale it by one and a half — one and a half times one and a half cups. Do not say one and a quarter; that squares the one and the half separately and forgets the cross pieces. Convert: three halves times three halves is nine quarters, two and a quarter cups. You can check with a measuring cup: one and a half cups, plus half of one and a half cups, which is three quarters, gives two and a quarter. The mixed number must become a single fraction before it is squared, every time. Questions on pizza, cheese and recipes follow.

## Subtopic: Method Summary and Examination Technique

One method covers every number type. For a power: convert mixed numbers to improper fractions and decimals to fractions if unsure; decide the sign from the exponent if the base is negative; raise numerator and denominator separately; simplify. For a root: simplify the fraction first; decide the sign — square roots non-negative, cube roots follow the inside; take the root of numerator and denominator separately; convert back to a mixed number or decimal if the question used one.

A typical question: evaluate negative three quarters squared, with brackets. Even exponent, positive. Nine over sixteen.

Another: evaluate the square root of one and nine sixteenths. Convert: twenty-five over sixteen. Roots: five over four. Back to a mixed number: one and a quarter.

Another: evaluate the square root of four ninths times the cube root of twenty-seven over eight. Two thirds times three halves. Simplify: one.

Errors to avoid. Squaring a mixed number piece by piece. Forgetting that squaring a proper fraction makes it smaller. Keeping the same number of decimal places under a root. Giving a negative square root. Rooting only the numerator. Not simplifying a fraction before rooting and missing the perfect squares.
