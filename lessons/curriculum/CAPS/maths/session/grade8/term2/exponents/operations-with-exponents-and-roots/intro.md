Today we bring the laws of exponents together with square and cube roots in calculations that mix them all. The square root of a product is the product of the square roots, so the square root of four times nine is two times three, six — but the square root of a sum is not the sum of the square roots, and the square root of nine plus sixteen is five, not seven. By the end you will evaluate expressions such as the square root of sixty-four plus the cube root of twenty-seven, simplify the square root of two to the power six as two cubed, and apply the order of operations when powers and roots sit inside the same expression.
