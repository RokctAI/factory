### subtopic_1 — Repeated Doubling and Tripling
**Assistant:** How do I know whether the exponent should be the number of hours or one less?

**Tutor:** Ask whether the first period has already happened at the moment the problem calls the start. If a colony starts with one cell and doubles every hour, then after one hour one doubling has happened and there are two cells, after six hours six doublings and sixty-four cells: the exponent equals the number of hours because the start is at time zero, before any doubling. The chessboard story is different because the first square already holds the start, one grain, so square two is after one doubling and square n is after n minus one doublings: the exponent is one less than the square number. The chain message is the same as the chessboard: day one is the start with three people, day d is three to the power d only if the first three are counted as the first tripling. Read the wording — after h hours means h periods have passed; on day d or on square n usually means d minus one or n minus one have passed — and write the first two or three values out to check before you commit to the formula.

### subtopic_3 — Scientific Notation in Context
**Assistant:** When I multiply two numbers in scientific notation, what do I do if the first factor comes out bigger than ten?

**Tutor:** Normalise it by moving the comma one place to the left and adding one to the exponent, exactly as you did for a first factor like twelve in the scientific notation lesson. Six comma two times ten to the power seven times two times ten squared gives first factor twelve comma four and exponent nine; twelve comma four is not less than ten, so it becomes one comma two four and the exponent becomes ten. The value has not changed — twelve comma four billion and one comma two four times ten to the power ten are the same number — but only the second is in scientific notation. The reverse happens in division: one comma five over three gives nought comma five, which is less than one, so move the comma one place right and subtract one from the exponent, turning nought comma five times ten cubed into five times ten squared. Always do the normalising as the final step, and check that the first factor has exactly one non-zero digit before the comma.

### subtopic_4 — Growth by a Fixed Factor and Mixed Problems
**Assistant:** Is growth by a factor like 1,1 the same mathematics as doubling?

**Tutor:** Yes, it is a power with a decimal base instead of a whole-number base. Doubling multiplies by two each period, so after three periods you multiply by two cubed. Growing by ten percent multiplies by one comma one each period, so after three periods you multiply by one comma one cubed, which is one comma three three one. One thousand rand becomes one thousand three hundred and thirty-one rand. The only extra skill is cubing a decimal, which you did in the previous lesson: eleven cubed is one thousand three hundred and thirty-one, and three decimal places make it one comma three three one. Grade 9 gives this a name, compound interest, and a formula, but the formula is just start times factor to the power of periods, which is the same shape as the bacteria problem. Halving is the same again with the factor one half, or equivalently dividing by two to the power of the periods, which is how the medicine dose problem works.

### subtopic_6 — The Cube-Shaped Water Tank
**Assistant:** Why does eight times the volume give only twice the edge?

**Tutor:** Because volume is the edge cubed, so changing the edge by some factor changes the volume by that factor cubed, and going backwards means taking a cube root. Doubling the edge multiplies the volume by two cubed, which is eight; so eight times the volume is undone by the cube root of eight, which is two. The product law for roots does the calculation: the cube root of eight times ten to the power six is the cube root of eight times the cube root of ten to the power six, two times one hundred, two hundred centimetres against the original one hundred. The same logic in two dimensions says four times the area gives twice the side, since the square root of four is two, and nine times the area gives three times the side. Learners who expect eight times the volume to give eight times the edge are thinking of a straight-line relationship, but lengths, areas and volumes are linked by powers, and powers are undone by roots, not by division.
