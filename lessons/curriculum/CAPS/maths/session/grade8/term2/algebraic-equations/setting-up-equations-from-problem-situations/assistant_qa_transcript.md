### subtopic_1 — Expression, Equation and the Equals Sign
**Assistant:** What is the difference between an equation and a formula, if both have equals signs?

**Tutor:** A formula is a rule that holds in every case of its kind, while an equation describing a situation is a statement about one particular case that is true for particular values of the variable. A equals l times b is true for every rectangle; eight k plus twelve equals sixty is true only for the one trip that cost sixty rand, and only when k is six. You use a formula by substituting known values to find an unknown — put in length and breadth and read off area. You use an equation by solving it to find the value that makes it true. The two can be connected: putting a known total into a formula produces an equation, as when the perimeter formula two l plus two b with a perimeter of forty-two and a length of b plus five becomes four b plus ten equals forty-two. The equals sign is the same symbol in both, but in a formula it says always, and in an equation it says in this case.

### subtopic_2 — Setting Up One-Step and Two-Step Equations
**Assistant:** How do I decide which quantity gets the letter?

**Tutor:** Give the letter to the quantity the question asks you to find, unless another choice makes the other quantities simpler to write. If the question asks how many kilometres, let k be the kilometres, because then the answer to the equation is the answer to the question and there is no extra step. When two unknowns are related, choose the one the other is built from: if the mother is three times the daughter's age, let the daughter's age be d so that the mother's is three d, a multiple, rather than letting the mother's be m and the daughter's m over three, a fraction. If one number is six more than another, let the smaller be n and the larger n plus six. The test is that every other quantity in the problem can be written as a simple expression in your letter without fractions where possible. Then define the letter in a sentence, including its unit — let k be the number of kilometres travelled — so that the final answer carries the right unit automatically.

### subtopic_3 — Interpreting an Equation — Reading It Back
**Assistant:** What should I look for when asked to interpret an equation I did not write?

**Tutor:** Identify the roles of the parts: the variable, the coefficient, the constant and the right-hand side. The coefficient of the variable is a rate — so much per kilometre, per guest, per hour — because it multiplies a count. A constant added to it is a once-off amount that does not depend on the count, such as a flag fee, a hire charge or a call-out fee. A constant that the variable term is subtracted from is a starting amount that decreases, like a prepaid balance. The right-hand side, if it is a number, is the total that the situation reached. Then check the units: every term must be in the same unit, so if the total is in rand, the rate must be rand per unit of the variable. Finally, say the whole equation as a sentence about the situation — the fare for k kilometres at eight rand each plus a twelve rand flag fee came to sixty rand — and if the sentence is sensible, the interpretation is complete.

### subtopic_6 — Marbles and the Magician
**Assistant:** Why does the magician's trick work for every number, when most equations have one answer?

**Tutor:** Because the magician's instructions build an expression that simplifies to a constant, so the equation it produces is true for every value of the letter rather than for one. Double the number, add six, halve, subtract the original: two n plus six, over two, is n plus three, and subtracting n leaves three, whatever n was. Setting that up in algebra turns a mystery into a one-line simplification, which is exactly why algebra is worth learning — it sees through tricks. An equation like three n minus five equals sixteen is different: the left side is not a constant, it changes as n changes, and it equals sixteen for exactly one value, seven. Grade 9 gives names to the two kinds: an identity, true for all values, and a conditional equation, true for some. For now, notice that the same tools — define a letter, write each step as an expression, simplify — tell you which kind you have, and that setting up the algebra is the first move in both cases.
