# Part 1 — Expert

This session develops the determination of the numerical value of algebraic expressions by substitution. Each variable is replaced by its given value, written in brackets, and the resulting numerical expression is evaluated using the order of operations, with powers before multiplication and multiplication before addition. Negative, fractional and decimal values are substituted with the same care, and formulas from measurement and science are evaluated in the same way. The anchor evaluation is three x squared minus five x plus seven at x equal to three, giving nineteen, and at x equal to negative two, giving thirty-nine.

## Subtopic: Substituting a Positive Whole Number

To evaluate an expression, replace each letter by its value and work out the number. The anchor: three x squared minus five x plus seven, with x equal to three. Write the substitution with brackets where the letter was: three bracket three bracket squared minus five bracket three bracket plus seven. Now the order of operations. Powers first: bracket three bracket squared is nine. Multiplications: three times nine is twenty-seven; five times three is fifteen. Then left to right: twenty-seven minus fifteen plus seven, twelve plus seven, nineteen.

The brackets are not decoration. Three x squared means three times x squared, so the x is squared first and then multiplied by three. Writing three bracket three bracket squared keeps that order visible: the square belongs to the three inside the bracket only. A learner who writes thirty-three squared, or who squares three times three to get eighty-one, has lost the structure of the term.

More examples at x equal to three. Two x plus one: six plus one, seven. x squared minus x: nine minus three, six. Ten minus two x: ten minus six, four. x cubed: twenty-seven. Bracket two x bracket squared: bracket six bracket squared, thirty-six — compare two x squared, which is two times nine, eighteen. The bracket changes what gets squared, exactly as in the exponents topic.

Two variables: for a equal to two and b equal to five, three a plus two b is six plus ten, sixteen; a b squared is two times twenty-five, fifty; bracket a b bracket squared is a hundred; a squared minus b is four minus five, negative one.

The habit to build is the written substitution line: copy the expression with each letter replaced by its number in brackets, then evaluate on the next line. That first line is where method marks live, and it is the line that stops you doing two things at once. Questions on substituting whole numbers follow now.

## Subtopic: Substituting a Negative Number — Brackets as Seatbelt

Negative values are where marks are lost, and brackets are the protection. The anchor at x equal to negative two: three bracket negative two bracket squared minus five bracket negative two bracket plus seven. Powers first: bracket negative two bracket squared is positive four, because a negative squared is positive. Multiplications: three times four is twelve; five times negative two is negative ten, so minus five x becomes minus negative ten, which is plus ten. Twelve plus ten plus seven: twenty-nine. Let me recount: twelve plus ten is twenty-two, plus seven is twenty-nine.

The two danger points are both in that calculation. First, bracket negative two bracket squared is four, not negative four; without brackets, negative two squared reads as the negative of four. Second, minus five times negative two is plus ten; the two negatives make a positive. Learners who write twelve minus ten plus seven, nine, have slipped at the second point.

More at x equal to negative two. Two x plus one: negative four plus one, negative three. x squared minus x: four minus negative two, four plus two, six. x cubed: bracket negative two bracket cubed, negative eight — odd power keeps the sign. Negative x: negative bracket negative two bracket, positive two. Ten minus two x: ten minus negative four, fourteen.

Two variables with a negative: for a equal to negative three and b equal to two, a b is negative six; a squared b is nine times two, eighteen; a b squared is negative three times four, negative twelve; bracket a plus b bracket squared is bracket negative one bracket squared, one; a squared plus b squared is nine plus four, thirteen — and once again the square of a sum is not the sum of the squares.

Write every negative in brackets when you substitute, every time, even when it seems unnecessary. The bracket is a seatbelt: it costs a second and prevents the two commonest sign errors in the whole topic. Questions on negatives follow.

## Subtopic: Fractions, Decimals and Formulas

Fractions substitute like any other number, with the fraction rules from Term 1. For x equal to a half: two x plus one is one plus one, two. x squared is a quarter. Four x squared is four times a quarter, one. Six x minus one is three minus one, two. One over x is two — dividing by a half is multiplying by two.

For x equal to two thirds: three x is two; x squared is four ninths; nine x squared is four; x minus one is negative a third.

Decimals: for x equal to nought comma five, x squared is nought comma two five, two x squared is nought comma five, and bracket two x bracket squared is one. For x equal to one comma five: x squared is two comma two five; four x minus three is three; x cubed is three comma three seven five.

Formulas are expressions with names, and substituting into them is the most useful thing in this lesson. Area of a rectangle, A equals l times b: for l equal to seven comma five and b equal to four, A is thirty square units. Perimeter, P equals two l plus two b: twenty-three. Area of a triangle, A equals a half b h: for b equal to ten and h equal to seven, thirty-five. Volume of a cube, V equals e cubed: for e equal to nought comma three, nought comma zero two seven. Distance, d equals s t: speed eighty kilometres per hour for two and a half hours, two hundred kilometres. Temperature conversion, F equals nine fifths C plus thirty-two: for C equal to twenty-five, forty-five plus thirty-two, seventy-seven degrees Fahrenheit.

Simple interest from Term 1: I equals P r t over a hundred. For P equal to two thousand rand, r equal to six and t equal to three: two thousand times six times three over a hundred, three hundred and sixty rand.

Units matter in formulas: write them in the answer. The order of operations is the same as ever — in nine fifths C plus thirty-two, the multiplication happens before the thirty-two is added. Questions on fractions, decimals and formulas follow.

## Subtopic: Substitution as a Check on Algebra

Substitution is the universal check for every algebra skill you have learned this term. If two expressions are supposed to be equal — an expression and its simplified form, a product and its expansion, a quotient and its result — then they must give the same value for every number you substitute. If they disagree for even one number, the algebra is wrong.

Check a simplification: five x squared minus three x plus two x squared plus x minus four was simplified to seven x squared minus two x minus four. At x equal to two: the original is twenty minus six plus eight plus two minus four, twenty; the simplified is twenty-eight minus four minus four, twenty. Agreement supports the simplification.

Check an expansion: two x bracket three x minus five bracket against six x squared minus ten x. At x equal to two: two times two times one is four; twenty-four minus twenty is four. Agreement.

Check a division: bracket nine a cubed minus six a squared plus three a bracket over three a against three a squared minus two a plus one. At a equal to two: fifty-four over six, nine; twelve minus four plus one, nine. Had the plus one been dropped, the right side would be eight, and the check fails.

Check a root: the square root of sixteen x squared plus nine x squared against five x and against seven x. At x equal to one: the root of twenty-five is five. The five x is confirmed and the seven x is refuted.

Choose the value wisely. Zero kills every variable term and tests only constants. One makes every power equal to one and hides exponent errors: three x squared and three x cubed agree at one. Two is the smallest value that separates powers, and it is easy to compute with. For expressions with two letters, use two different values, such as a equal to two and b equal to three, so that a b and b a both agree but a squared b and a b squared differ.

A substitution check is not a proof — two different expressions can agree at one value by coincidence — but a disagreement is a certain sign of error, and agreement at two or three values is very strong evidence. In an examination, the check takes thirty seconds and saves the marks on the question. Questions on checking follow.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same substituting as stories about a till that only knows the formula until the prices arrive, a recipe scaled for a known number of guests, and a pair of brackets as a seatbelt for a negative number — stories in which the letter was a placeholder all along, and substitution is the moment the real number takes its seat.

## Subtopic: The Till That Knows Only the Formula

A corner shop till is programmed with a formula for a lunch special: three p plus two c plus eight, where p is the price of a pie, c the price of a cold drink and eight rand is the fixed cost of the paper bag and serviettes. Prices change weekly, so the till stores the formula and waits for the numbers. On Monday a pie is fifteen rand and a cold drink twelve. The till substitutes: three bracket fifteen bracket plus two bracket twelve bracket plus eight, which is forty-five plus twenty-four plus eight, seventy-seven rand. On Friday the pie goes up to eighteen and the cold drink down to ten: fifty-four plus twenty plus eight, eighty-two rand.

The till never changes the formula; it only changes the numbers it feeds in. That is substitution. The letter was a placeholder for a price not yet known, and the moment the price is known, the number takes the letter's seat.

Order of operations is built into the till. It multiplies three by the pie price before adding anything, because three p means three pies. A till that added first — three plus fifteen, eighteen — would charge wrongly. Multiplication before addition is not a school rule; it is what the words mean.

Now the special includes a square tray of samoosas with side s, priced at two rand per square centimetre of tray — two s squared. If s is five, that is two bracket five bracket squared, two times twenty-five, fifty rand. Not bracket two times five bracket squared, which is a hundred: the two is the rate per square centimetre and the s squared is the area, and the area is worked out first. Two s squared squares the s; bracket two s bracket squared would square the two as well. The till knows the difference because the programmer put the brackets in the right place.

A refund is a negative. If a customer returns a pie, the till substitutes negative one for the number of pies in a line that reads fifteen n: fifteen bracket negative one bracket, negative fifteen rand, money going back. Had the line been fifteen n squared for some reason, the refund would come out positive — the square of a negative — which is nonsense for a refund and a reminder that squares eat signs.

Every expression you evaluate is a till waiting for its prices. Write the formula, write the substitution line with brackets, then press the buttons in the right order. Questions on the till follow.

## Subtopic: Scaling the Recipe and the Thermometer Abroad

A recipe for pancakes says: flour in cups is a half n, eggs are n over four, milk in millilitres is one hundred and fifty n over four, where n is the number of pancakes. For twelve pancakes: flour, a half of twelve, six cups; eggs, twelve over four, three; milk, one hundred and fifty times twelve over four, one hundred and fifty times three, four hundred and fifty millilitres. For twenty pancakes: ten cups, five eggs, seven hundred and fifty millilitres. The recipe is three expressions in n, and cooking for a known number of guests is substitution.

Fractions arrive naturally. For ten pancakes the eggs are ten over four, two and a half — which you round up to three in the kitchen but leave as two and a half in the mathematics. Substitution gives the exact value; the kitchen decides what to do with it.

Now a thermometer on holiday. A South African in a country that uses Fahrenheit reads thirty degrees Celsius on the forecast and wants Fahrenheit: F equals nine fifths C plus thirty-two. Nine fifths of thirty is fifty-four; plus thirty-two, eighty-six degrees Fahrenheit. A chilly morning at five degrees Celsius: nine plus thirty-two, forty-one. A freezer at negative twenty: nine fifths of negative twenty is negative thirty-six; plus thirty-two, negative four degrees Fahrenheit. The negative substitutes in brackets: nine fifths bracket negative twenty bracket plus thirty-two.

The order matters in the formula. Multiply by nine fifths first, then add thirty-two. A traveller who adds thirty-two first and then multiplies gets nine fifths of sixty-two, over a hundred and eleven, and dresses for the wrong weather.

Going the other way is Grade 9 work — solving the formula for C — but you can check a conversion by substituting back: does nine fifths of thirty plus thirty-two give eighty-six? Yes. Substitution checks the formula the same way it checks algebra.

The pattern in all of these: a rule written once with letters, used many times with numbers. The letters are not mysterious; they are the blanks on a form, and substitution is filling in the form. Questions on recipes and thermometers follow.

## Subtopic: Method Summary and Examination Technique

The method for substitution. One: write the expression. Two: rewrite it with each letter replaced by its value in brackets — the substitution line. Three: evaluate using the order of operations: brackets, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. Four: write the answer with units if the expression came from a formula. Five: if the question asked you to check some algebra, compare the two values and state the conclusion.

A typical question: evaluate two x squared minus three x y plus y squared for x equal to negative one and y equal to three. Two bracket negative one bracket squared minus three bracket negative one bracket bracket three bracket plus bracket three bracket squared. Two times one, two; minus three times negative three, which is minus negative nine, plus nine; plus nine. Two plus nine plus nine, twenty.

Another: evaluate the square root of a squared plus b squared for a equal to six and b equal to eight. Thirty-six plus sixty-four, one hundred, root ten. The Pythagoras calculation of Term 4, in advance; and not six plus eight.

Another: the surface area of a cube is six e squared. For e equal to two comma five, six times six comma two five, thirty-seven comma five square units.

Another: for which of x equal to two or x equal to negative two is x squared minus x larger? At two: four minus two, two. At negative two: four plus two, six. Negative two.

Another: a learner claims bracket x plus three bracket squared equals x squared plus nine. Test at x equal to one: sixteen against ten. The claim is false.

Errors to avoid. Squaring three x when the expression says three x squared. Dropping brackets around a negative and getting negative four for bracket negative two bracket squared. Turning minus five times negative two into minus ten. Adding before multiplying in a formula. Mixing up which value goes with which letter when there are two. Forgetting units.
