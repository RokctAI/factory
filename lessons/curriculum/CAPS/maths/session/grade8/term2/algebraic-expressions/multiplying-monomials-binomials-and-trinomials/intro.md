Today we multiply in algebra: a number or a single term times a single term, times a two-term bracket, and times a three-term bracket. Three x times four x squared is twelve x cubed, by multiplying the coefficients and adding the exponents. Two x times bracket three x minus five bracket is six x squared minus ten x, by the distributive law. By the end you will multiply any monomial into any bracket, keep the signs right when the monomial is negative, and recognise the two errors that cost most: forgetting to multiply every term in the bracket, and adding exponents when you should multiply coefficients.
