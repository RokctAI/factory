### subtopic_1 — Monomial Divided by Monomial
**Assistant:** Why do exponents subtract when I divide, and what if the bottom exponent is bigger?

**Tutor:** Exponents subtract because dividing removes factors: twelve x cubed has three factors of x, dividing by three x removes one of them, and two remain, which is x squared. That is the division law from the exponents topic, a to the m over a to the n is a to the m minus n, and it is the same thing as cancelling one x from top and bottom. When the bottom exponent is bigger, more factors are removed from the top than it has, and the leftovers sit underneath: four x over two x cubed cancels one x from each and leaves two x's below, giving two over x squared. In Grade 9 you will write that as two x to the power negative two, and the two notations mean the same thing. For Grade 8, write the fraction with the letter underneath and make sure the coefficient is reduced to lowest terms. A quick check in either case is to multiply your answer by the divisor and see whether the original top returns.

### subtopic_2 — Binomial Divided by a Monomial
**Assistant:** What is the connection between this lesson and the multiplying lesson before it?

**Tutor:** They are exact inverses, and each checks the other. Multiplying two x into bracket three x minus five bracket gave six x squared minus ten x by the distributive law; dividing six x squared minus ten x by two x gives back three x minus five by the same law applied to division. Every expansion in the previous lesson can be turned into a division question by putting the answer over the monomial, and every division in this lesson can be verified by multiplying the quotient by the divisor. That is why multiplying back is the standard check: it is not a separate skill but the previous lesson run in reverse. The two errors mirror each other too — multiplying only the first term of a bracket and dividing only the first term of a numerator are the same lapse of attention, and the arc-to-every-term habit cures both. Learners who see the pair as one idea seen from two sides find both lessons easier.

### subtopic_3 — Trinomial Divided by a Monomial and the Term That Becomes One
**Assistant:** Why does a term divided by itself become one and not disappear?

**Tutor:** Because division asks how many times the divisor fits into the dividend, and anything fits into itself exactly once. Three a over three a is one in the same way that seven over seven is one or a hundred over a hundred is one. The term feels as if it disappears because the letters cancel completely, but what remains after cancelling all the factors is the number one, not nothing, and the one is a genuine term of the quotient. The multiplying-back check makes this unavoidable: three a times bracket three a squared minus two a plus one bracket produces the original third term three a only because of the plus one, and without it the product would be two terms short of the original. In the vans picture, three a crates shared among three a vans is one crate per van, and a van that receives one crate has not received nothing. Count terms in and terms out: a trinomial divided by a monomial yields three terms, always.

### subtopic_4 — Simplifying Fractions with Letters and Checking
**Assistant:** When am I allowed to cancel, and when is cancelling a mistake?

**Tutor:** You may cancel a common factor that multiplies the whole of the top and the whole of the bottom. In six x squared minus ten x over two x, the two x is a factor of every term on top — six x squared is two x times three x and ten x is two x times five — so it can be cancelled from each, leaving three x minus five. You may not cancel something that is only a term of a sum. In bracket x plus five bracket over x, the x on top is added to five, not multiplied through the whole top, so cancelling it against the x below is illegal; the correct simplification splits the fraction into x over x plus five over x, which is one plus five over x. The slogan is cancel factors, not terms. A substitution with x equal to two exposes the illegal cancel immediately: the original is seven over two, three and a half, whereas the illegal answer six is different. Grade 9 will have you factorise a top before cancelling for exactly this reason.
