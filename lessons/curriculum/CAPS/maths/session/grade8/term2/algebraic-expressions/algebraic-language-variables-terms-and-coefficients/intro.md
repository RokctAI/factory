Today we learn to read algebra the way you read a sentence: knowing what each part is called and what it does. In the expression three x squared minus five x plus seven, there are three terms; x is the variable, seven is the constant, three and negative five are coefficients, and the two on the x is an exponent. By the end you will name every part of an expression, tell like terms from unlike terms, translate word rules into symbols and back, and avoid the three errors that cost the most marks: miscounting terms, losing the sign of a coefficient, and calling x squared and x like terms.
