### subtopic_1 — Collecting Like Terms — The Distributive Law Backwards
**Assistant:** Why is collecting like terms described as the distributive law backwards?

**Tutor:** Because the distributive law, written forwards, says a times bracket b plus c bracket equals a b plus a c, spreading a multiplication over a sum, and collecting like terms reads the same equation from right to left. Three x plus four x is three times x plus four times x, and the law, read backwards, pulls the common factor x out: bracket three plus four bracket times x, which is seven x. That is why only like terms can be collected — the law needs a common factor to pull out, and the variable part is that common factor. Three x plus four y has no common factor to pull, so nothing can be collected. It is also why the variable part never changes during collecting: the x is factored out untouched and only the numbers inside the bracket are added. Seeing collecting as factoring also prepares you for Grade 9, where you will pull common factors out of expressions deliberately and call it factorisation.

### subtopic_2 — Rearranging and Regrouping — Commutative and Associative Laws
**Assistant:** If subtraction is not commutative, how can I move terms around freely?

**Tutor:** Because every subtraction can be read as the addition of a negative, and addition is commutative and associative. Five x squared minus three x plus two x squared is five x squared plus negative three x plus two x squared, a sum of three signed terms, and a sum can be reordered and regrouped at will. The term is negative three x, not three x, and it stays negative three x wherever you place it. The reason five minus three cannot be swapped to three minus five is that the minus sign there is an operation between two numbers; in an expression, you treat each minus as the sign of the following term instead, and then the only operation left is addition. Practically, mark every term with the sign in front of it before you move anything, and if a term has no sign because it comes first, it is positive. Learners who lose marks here almost always did so by moving a term and leaving its minus sign behind.

### subtopic_4 — Expressions with Brackets and a Subtracted Group
**Assistant:** What is the most common error with a subtracted bracket, and how do I avoid it?

**Tutor:** Flipping the sign of the first term inside and leaving the rest alone — writing bracket three x plus two bracket minus bracket five x minus seven bracket as three x plus two minus five x minus seven, and so getting negative two x minus five instead of negative two x plus nine. The cure is to see the minus in front of the bracket as a coefficient of negative one and to multiply every term inside by it, which is the distributive law: negative one times five x is negative five x, and negative one times negative seven is positive seven. Writing the negative one explicitly for a while, as negative one bracket five x minus seven bracket, forces you to visit every term. A second cure is to check with a number: for x equal to one, the original is five minus negative two, seven, and negative two x plus nine gives seven while negative two x minus five gives negative seven. The check takes ten seconds and catches the error every time.

### subtopic_5 — Sorting the Sock Drawer
**Assistant:** Why can three pairs of socks and two loose socks not be combined, if they are all socks?

**Tutor:** Because a pair and a loose sock are different units, and algebra only lets you add quantities measured in the same unit. If b stands for one black sock, then b squared is b times b, a different quantity — the picture of a pair is loose, but the point is exact: b squared is not a multiple of b, any more than a square metre is a multiple of a metre. Three b squared plus two b therefore has two kinds of term and stays as two terms. The laws of exponents are what make the distinction precise: like terms must have identical exponents on every letter, and b squared and b have exponents two and one. The trap this protects you from is writing three x squared plus two x as five x cubed, or five x squared, both of which add the exponents or ignore them. Test with x equal to two: three times four plus two times two is sixteen, while five times eight is forty and five times four is twenty. Only the uncollected form is right.
