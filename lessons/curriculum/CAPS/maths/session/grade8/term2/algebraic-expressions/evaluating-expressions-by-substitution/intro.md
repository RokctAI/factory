Today we find the value of an algebraic expression by substituting numbers for its letters. If x is three, then three x squared minus five x plus seven is three times nine minus fifteen plus seven, which is nineteen. If x is negative two, the same expression is three times four plus ten plus seven, thirty-nine — and the brackets around the negative two are what make that right. By the end you will substitute whole numbers, negatives, fractions and decimals into expressions and formulas, follow the order of operations without slipping, and use substitution to check your algebra.
