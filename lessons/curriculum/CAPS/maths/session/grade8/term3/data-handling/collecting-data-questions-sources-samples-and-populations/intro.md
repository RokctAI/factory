Today we start the data cycle at its beginning: deciding what to ask, whom to ask, and where the answers will come from. A statistics question about water use in a town, taxi fares across a province or recycling at a school only produces useful numbers if the question is sharp, the population is named, and the sample that stands in for that population is chosen fairly. By the end you will pose a focused question on a social, economic or environmental issue, distinguish a population from a sample, recognise biased samples, choose between primary and secondary sources, and justify every choice in the language an examiner expects.
