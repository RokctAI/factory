Today we do what the last lesson only calculated: we run the trials. Toss a coin sixty times and count the heads; roll a die a hundred times and count the sixes; the fraction of trials in which an event happened is its relative frequency, and the question is how it compares with the probability we predicted. By the end you will calculate relative frequency from a tally of trials, predict the expected number of occurrences from a probability, explain why relative frequency wanders in small experiments and settles towards the probability in large ones, and use relative frequency to estimate probability when outcomes are not equally likely.
