# Part 1 — Expert

This session establishes the sign rules for multiplication and division of integers, extends them to products of several factors, and applies the order of operations to mixed calculations. The anchor is negative three times bracket two minus seven bracket, plus twelve divided by negative four, which equals twelve. Every sign rule is derived from patterns you can check, and the examination layout is modelled line by line.

## Subtopic: Multiplying Integers — Where the Sign Rules Come From

Multiplication of integers rests on two facts you already own: a positive times a positive is positive, and multiplication is repeated addition. Three times negative four means three lots of negative four: negative four plus negative four plus negative four, which is negative twelve. So a positive times a negative is negative. By the commutative property, negative four times three is also negative twelve, so a negative times a positive is negative. Two of the four cases are done.

The remaining case is a negative times a negative, and here is the honest derivation rather than a slogan. Look at the pattern: three times negative four is negative twelve; two times negative four is negative eight; one times negative four is negative four; zero times negative four is zero. Each time the first factor drops by one, the product rises by four. Continue the pattern: negative one times negative four must be four; negative two times negative four must be eight. A negative times a negative is positive, because the pattern of products demands it. Any other answer would break the arithmetic you already trust.

Summarise the four cases. Same signs — both positive or both negative — give a positive product. Different signs give a negative product. Then multiply the absolute values as usual. Negative six times negative four: same signs, positive; six times four is twenty-four; the product is positive twenty-four. Negative six times four: different signs, negative twenty-four. Six times negative four: negative twenty-four. Six times four: twenty-four.

Note the contrast with addition, because this is where learners blur the rules. For addition, two negatives give a negative: negative six plus negative four is negative ten. For multiplication, two negatives give a positive: negative six times negative four is positive twenty-four. Different operations, different rules. Before you apply a sign rule, name the operation.

Two certainties to keep: any integer multiplied by zero is zero, and any integer multiplied by one is itself. Negative seven times one is negative seven; negative seven times zero is zero. Questions on single products are with you now.

## Subtopic: Dividing Integers and Products of Several Factors

Division inherits its sign rules directly from multiplication, because division is multiplication asked backwards. Twenty-four divided by negative four asks: what number times negative four gives twenty-four? Negative six, because negative six times negative four is twenty-four. So twenty-four divided by negative four is negative six. Negative twenty-four divided by negative four asks what times negative four gives negative twenty-four: positive six. Negative twenty-four divided by four: negative six.

The same summary therefore holds for division. Same signs give a positive quotient; different signs give a negative quotient; then divide the absolute values. Negative forty-five divided by nine is negative five. Negative forty-five divided by negative nine is five. And the zero cases from the previous topic still apply: zero divided by negative five is zero, while negative five divided by zero is undefined — a sign on the dividend does not rescue a zero divisor.

Now products of three or more factors. Negative two times negative three times negative four. Work in pairs from the left: negative two times negative three is six; six times negative four is negative twenty-four. Alternatively, count the negative signs. Each pair of negatives multiplies to a positive, so an even number of negative factors gives a positive product and an odd number gives a negative product. Three negatives: odd, so the product is negative; two times three times four is twenty-four; the answer is negative twenty-four. Four negative factors, negative one times negative two times negative three times negative four: even, positive twenty-four.

This counting rule explains powers of negative one, which you will meet throughout algebra. Negative one to the power of an even number is one; to an odd number, negative one. Keep it in mind for the squares and cubes lesson that follows.

A worked example in examination layout. Evaluate negative sixty divided by bracket negative three times four bracket. Brackets first: negative three times four is negative twelve. Then negative sixty divided by negative twelve: same signs, positive; sixty divided by twelve is five. Answer five, with the two lines of working written out. Questions on division and multiple factors follow now.

## Subtopic: All Four Operations — Order of Operations with Integers

The order of operations does not change when negatives arrive: brackets first, then exponents, then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right. What changes is the number of places a sign can go wrong, so the layout becomes more important, not less.

The anchor. Negative three times bracket two minus seven bracket, plus twelve divided by negative four. Line one: copy the question exactly. Line two: brackets. Two minus seven is negative five, so the expression becomes negative three times negative five plus twelve divided by negative four. Line three: multiplication and division, left to right. Negative three times negative five is fifteen; twelve divided by negative four is negative three. The expression is now fifteen plus negative three. Line four: fifteen plus negative three is twelve. Four lines, one operation type per line, and every sign visible.

Here is the slip to watch for. Some learners see fifteen and negative three and subtract the wrong way, writing negative twelve. Fifteen is the heavier number and it is positive, so the answer is positive twelve. The addition rules from the last lesson do not switch off because a multiplication came first.

A second example with a fraction bar. Bracket negative eight plus two bracket, all over negative three, minus four times negative two. A fraction bar acts as a bracket around everything above it and everything below it. Top: negative eight plus two is negative six. Negative six divided by negative three is two. Then four times negative two is negative eight. Two minus negative eight is two plus eight, which is ten. Write each of those lines.

A third, with a negative inside a bracket being multiplied: five minus two times bracket negative three minus one bracket. Bracket: negative three minus one is negative four. Multiplication: two times negative four is negative eight. Then five minus negative eight is five plus eight, thirteen. Learners who do five minus two first get three times negative four, which is negative twelve, and they lose every mark after the first line. Multiplication before subtraction, always. Questions on mixed calculations are with you now.

## Subtopic: Examination Technique — Signs, Brackets and Checking

Three habits separate a distinction candidate from the rest on this topic. First, brackets around every negative that follows an operation sign. Write six times bracket negative two bracket, never six times minus two with the signs touching. It costs a second and it prevents the classic misread of a minus sign as subtraction. Second, one operation type per line. Third, a sign-and-size estimate before you write the final answer.

The estimate works like this. In the anchor, negative three times negative five must be positive and about fifteen; twelve divided by negative four must be negative and about three. A positive fifteen and a negative three combine to something positive and near twelve. If your written answer is negative, or near forty, you have made an error and you know it before the marker does.

Watch the difference between the opposite of a product and a product of opposites. Negative bracket three times four bracket is the opposite of twelve, which is negative twelve. Bracket negative three bracket times bracket negative four bracket is positive twelve. The bracket placement changes the answer, and Grade 8 examinations test exactly this distinction. Read the brackets as they are printed, not as you expect them.

Division written as a fraction needs the same care. Negative eighteen over six is negative three. Eighteen over negative six is also negative three. Negative eighteen over negative six is three. The sign of a fraction can be moved between the numerator, the denominator and the front of the fraction without changing its value, which is a fact you will use heavily in Grade 9 algebraic fractions.

Finally, a full mixed example at examination standard. Evaluate bracket negative two bracket times bracket negative nine bracket minus bracket negative thirty bracket divided by five. Products and quotients first: negative two times negative nine is eighteen; negative thirty divided by five is negative six. Then eighteen minus negative six is eighteen plus six, twenty-four. Estimate: positive eighteen, and subtracting a negative makes it bigger, so an answer above eighteen is expected. Twenty-four fits. Distinction-level takeaway: name the operation, apply its sign rule, bracket every negative, and estimate the sign before you commit. Questions on technique follow now.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same rules told through debts you owe to friends, temperatures that drop every hour, and a loss shared across a team. Multiplying by a negative stops being a rule and starts being a sentence you can say out loud.

## Subtopic: Debts You Owe to Friends

Let us make the sign rules into sentences. Suppose you owe twenty rand to each of three friends. Owing twenty rand is negative twenty. Three friends, so three lots of negative twenty: three times negative twenty is negative sixty. You are sixty rand down. A positive number of debts gives a bigger debt — positive times negative is negative, and you did not need a rule to know it.

Now the interesting one. Your gran hears about the three debts and pays them all off for you. Removing three debts of twenty rand each. Removing is negative, a debt is negative: negative three times negative twenty. What happened to your position? You were sixty rand down, and now you are not — you are sixty rand better off than you were. Negative three times negative twenty is positive sixty. Taking away debts improves your position, and that is the whole meaning of negative times negative equals positive.

Division tells the same story shared out. Your soccer team buys new kit and ends up with a loss of four hundred and fifty rand, which is negative four hundred and fifty. Five players agree to share the loss equally. Negative four hundred and fifty divided by five: each player's share is negative ninety, a loss of ninety rand each. A loss shared among people is a smaller loss each — negative divided by positive is negative.

And the backwards question. The team is down four hundred and fifty rand and each player is down ninety. How many players shared it? Negative four hundred and fifty divided by negative ninety. Same signs, positive: five players. A number of people can only ever be positive, and the signs agree with common sense.

So here is the sentence version of the rules. Lots of debts make a bigger debt. Taking away debts makes you richer. A loss shared out is a smaller loss each. You have been using multiplication of integers since the first time you borrowed lunch money. The questions now are all money sentences; translate each into a product or a quotient before you answer.

## Subtopic: Temperatures Dropping by the Hour

Here is a picture for multiplying by a negative that also explains time running backwards. In Sutherland the temperature is falling steadily at two degrees every hour. Falling two degrees is negative two. Five hours from now, the change will be five times negative two, which is negative ten: ten degrees colder. Positive hours into the future, a negative change per hour, a negative total change. Nothing surprising yet.

Now ask about the past. What was the temperature five hours ago, compared with now? Five hours ago is negative five hours. The change per hour is still negative two. Negative five times negative two is positive ten: five hours ago it was ten degrees warmer. Of course it was — if it has been falling all afternoon, earlier was warmer. Negative times negative is positive, and a thermometer and a clock just proved it.

Run it as a table in your head. Three hours ahead: three times negative two, negative six, six degrees colder. One hour ahead: negative two. Now: zero times negative two, zero change. One hour ago: negative one times negative two, positive two, two degrees warmer. Three hours ago: six degrees warmer. The products climb steadily as you go back in time, exactly the pattern Part 1 used to derive the rule.

Division appears when you reverse the question. The temperature dropped twelve degrees in total, at two degrees per hour. How long did that take? Negative twelve divided by negative two: same signs, positive, six hours. Time taken is positive, so the signs had to agree. And if you were told it dropped twelve degrees over four hours and asked for the hourly change: negative twelve divided by four is negative three, a drop of three degrees each hour.

Whenever a product of two negatives feels strange, come back to this: falling temperature, hours in the past, warmer. The questions use the clock and the thermometer; decide first whether each number is a future or a past, a rise or a fall.

## Subtopic: Putting All Four Operations Together

Last section, and we do the whole thing with a shopping story so the order of operations has a reason. You start the day with no money. You buy three cold drinks at twelve rand each, so three times negative twelve is negative thirty-six. Then your aunt gives you forty rand. Then you split a fifty-rand taxi fare with a friend: negative fifty divided by two is negative twenty-five. Where do you stand?

Write it as one line: three times negative twelve, plus forty, plus negative fifty divided by two. Multiplication and division first, because the drinks and the taxi share are each their own little calculation that has to be finished before you can add it to the pile. Negative thirty-six, plus forty, plus negative twenty-five. Now add from the left: negative thirty-six plus forty is four; four plus negative twenty-five is negative twenty-one. You are twenty-one rand in the red.

Notice why multiplication comes before addition: you cannot add forty rand to three drinks. You have to turn the drinks into rand first. That is all the order of operations is — finish each small story before combining the stories.

Now a bracket. Suppose instead that your aunt gave you forty rand before you went shopping, and you then bought three drinks with it at twelve rand each, and the question is how much of her gift is left. Forty plus three times negative twelve: forty plus negative thirty-six, which is four rand left. Compare with bracket forty plus three bracket times negative twelve, which would be forty-three times negative twelve, a huge debt of five hundred and sixteen rand — nonsense for this story. Brackets change the story, so copy them exactly.

Here is your routine for every mixed integer calculation, and it works just as well on tomorrow's algebra. Brackets first. Then multiply and divide, left to right, with the sign rule for each pair: same signs positive, different signs negative. Then add and subtract, left to right, with the heavier-number rule. Then ask whether the sign and size make sense. You have moved from adding sweets to working out your bank balance after a day at the mall — that is a real step up. The final questions mix money, drinks and taxis; finish each little story before you combine them.
