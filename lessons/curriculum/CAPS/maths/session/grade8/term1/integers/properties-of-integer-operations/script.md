# Part 1 — Expert

This session verifies the commutative, associative and distributive properties of addition and multiplication for integers, shows that subtraction and division do not share them, and applies the properties as tools for mental calculation and for algebra. The anchor is negative four times bracket seven plus negative three bracket, which equals negative sixteen whether the bracket is evaluated first or the multiplication is distributed.

## Subtopic: Commutative Property — Order Does Not Matter, Except When It Does

The commutative property of addition says that a plus b equals b plus a: the order of the two numbers does not change the sum. For whole numbers this is obvious. The question for today is whether it survives negatives. Test it: negative five plus eight. Different signs, eight is heavier and positive, so three. Now eight plus negative five: again different signs, eight heavier and positive, three. The order changed; the sum did not. Try two negatives: negative seven plus negative two is negative nine, and negative two plus negative seven is negative nine. Addition of integers is commutative.

The commutative property of multiplication says a times b equals b times a. Negative six times four is negative twenty-four; four times negative six is negative twenty-four. Negative six times negative four is twenty-four, and so is negative four times negative six. The sign rule depends only on which signs are present, not on their order, so multiplication of integers is commutative.

Now the operations that fail. Subtraction: five minus negative two is five plus two, seven. Negative two minus five is negative two plus negative five, negative seven. Different answers. Subtraction is not commutative, and with integers the two results are opposites of each other — a useful check. Division: twelve divided by negative three is negative four; negative three divided by twelve is negative one quarter. Division is not commutative either.

Why does this matter beyond a definition? Because the commutative property is the licence to rearrange. In a long addition such as negative three plus eight plus negative seven plus two, you may collect the positives, eight plus two is ten, and the negatives, negative three plus negative seven is negative ten, and finish with ten plus negative ten, zero. That rearrangement is legal only because addition is commutative. Convert subtractions to adding the opposite first, and then rearrange; never rearrange around a subtraction sign.

In an examination you may be asked to name the property used in a given line, or to complete a statement such as negative nine plus box equals four plus negative nine. The box is four, and the property is the commutative property of addition. Questions on the commutative property are with you now.

## Subtopic: Associative Property — Grouping Does Not Matter, Except When It Does

The associative property is about grouping three or more numbers. For addition it says bracket a plus b bracket plus c equals a plus bracket b plus c bracket. Test it with integers. Bracket negative six plus four bracket plus negative nine: the bracket is negative two, and negative two plus negative nine is negative eleven. Now negative six plus bracket four plus negative nine bracket: the bracket is negative five, and negative six plus negative five is negative eleven. Same result, different grouping. Addition of integers is associative.

For multiplication: bracket negative two times three bracket times negative five. The bracket is negative six; negative six times negative five is thirty. Now negative two times bracket three times negative five bracket: the bracket is negative fifteen; negative two times negative fifteen is thirty. Multiplication of integers is associative.

Subtraction and division fail again. Bracket ten minus four bracket minus negative three: six minus negative three is nine. Ten minus bracket four minus negative three bracket: four minus negative three is seven, and ten minus seven is three. Nine and three — the grouping changed the answer. Division: bracket twenty-four divided by negative four bracket divided by two is negative six divided by two, negative three. Twenty-four divided by bracket negative four divided by two bracket is twenty-four divided by negative two, negative twelve. Not associative.

The practical consequence is a rule for layout. In a chain of additions you may bracket any convenient pair; in a chain of subtractions you may not, so convert them to additions first. In a chain of multiplications you may group freely — for instance, negative twenty-five times negative seven times four is easier as negative twenty-five times four, which is negative one hundred, then times negative seven, which is seven hundred. Spotting a pair that multiplies to a power of ten and grouping it first is a mark-saving habit, and the associative and commutative properties together are what make it legal.

Be able to name the property from a line of working. If the numbers keep their order but the brackets move, it is associative. If the numbers swap places, it is commutative. Many lines use both, and you may name both. Questions on the associative property follow.

## Subtopic: Distributive Property — Multiplication Over Addition

The distributive property links the two operations: a times bracket b plus c bracket equals a times b plus a times c. Multiplication distributes over addition. It is the single most important property in this lesson, because it becomes the engine of algebra in Term 2 — expanding brackets and factorising are the distributive property read left to right and right to left.

Verify it with the anchor. Negative four times bracket seven plus negative three bracket. Route one, bracket first: seven plus negative three is four; negative four times four is negative sixteen. Route two, distribute: negative four times seven is negative twenty-eight; negative four times negative three is positive twelve; negative twenty-eight plus twelve is negative sixteen. Both routes give negative sixteen. The property holds with negatives, provided each product carries its correct sign.

That proviso is where marks are lost. When the multiplier is negative, every term inside the bracket changes sign as it comes out. Negative four times negative three is plus twelve, not minus twelve. Learners who write negative twenty-eight minus twelve get negative forty, and the bracket-first route would have told them they were wrong. Distribute carefully, and when in doubt evaluate the bracket first as a check.

The property also distributes over subtraction, because subtraction is adding the opposite. Negative three times bracket five minus negative two bracket. Bracket first: five minus negative two is seven; negative three times seven is negative twenty-one. Distribute: negative three times five is negative fifteen; negative three times negative two is plus six; negative fifteen plus six is negative nine. Those disagree — so check the distribution. The bracket is five minus negative two, which is five plus two. Distributing gives negative three times five plus negative three times two: negative fifteen plus negative six, negative twenty-one. The error was treating minus negative two as a single negative term. Convert the subtraction to addition inside the bracket before distributing, and the routes agree.

Used backwards, the property factorises. Negative three times seventeen plus negative three times three: both terms share the factor negative three, so the sum is negative three times bracket seventeen plus three bracket, negative three times twenty, negative sixty. Check directly: negative fifty-one plus negative nine is negative sixty. Questions on the distributive property are with you now.

## Subtopic: Using the Properties — Mental Maths and Examination Technique

The properties are not decoration; they are the reason a strong learner calculates faster and with fewer errors. Here are the three uses the examiner expects.

Use one: distributing to make a hard product easy. Seven times negative ninety-eight. Write negative ninety-eight as negative one hundred plus two. Seven times negative one hundred is negative seven hundred; seven times two is fourteen; negative seven hundred plus fourteen is negative six hundred and eighty-six. One line of working, no long multiplication. Similarly negative six times forty-one is negative six times forty plus negative six times one: negative two hundred and forty plus negative six, negative two hundred and forty-six.

Use two: regrouping a sum. Negative forty-seven plus eighty-three plus negative fifty-three. The commutative and associative properties let you pair eighty-three with negative fifty-three first, which is thirty, and then negative forty-seven plus thirty is negative seventeen. Choosing the pair that cancels or rounds is the whole skill.

Use three: naming the property in a justification question. Given the line negative four times bracket seven plus negative three bracket equals negative four times seven plus negative four times negative three, the property is the distributive property. Given negative nine plus four equals four plus negative nine, commutative property of addition. Given bracket negative two times three bracket times negative five equals negative two times bracket three times negative five bracket, associative property of multiplication. Answer with the full name, including the operation.

Two warnings that protect marks. First, the properties are for addition and multiplication only. Never write a divided by bracket b plus c bracket as a divided by b plus a divided by c; it is false. Twelve divided by bracket two plus four bracket is two, but twelve divided by two plus twelve divided by four is six plus three, nine. Second, when distributing a negative multiplier over a bracket with a subtraction, convert to addition of the opposite inside the bracket first, as the previous section showed.

Distinction-level takeaway: commutative swaps, associative regroups, distributive spreads a multiplier across a bracket — and all three are licences that apply to addition and multiplication, not to subtraction and division. Questions on applying the properties follow.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same three properties as things you already do: paying for items in any order, grouping a taxi fare whichever way is convenient, and sharing out a debt across friends. The names are new; the habits are not.

## Subtopic: Paying in Any Order — Commutative in Real Life

Let us see the commutative property in a shop. You have a voucher worth fifty rand, which is plus fifty, and you owe the shop eighty rand for airtime, which is negative eighty. Does it matter whether the cashier scans the voucher first or the airtime first? Fifty plus negative eighty is negative thirty; negative eighty plus fifty is negative thirty. You owe thirty rand either way. Order does not matter for adding, and the till agrees.

Now imagine a different cashier who says: I will take the eighty, then give you the fifty. That is eighty minus fifty, which is thirty — you pay thirty. Compare the other order: fifty minus eighty, which is negative thirty. Same size, opposite meaning: in one, you pay thirty; in the other, the shop would owe you thirty. Subtraction cares about order, and in money terms the order decides who pays whom. That is why we say subtraction is not commutative.

Multiplication in real life: three friends each owe you twenty rand. Three times negative twenty from their point of view is negative sixty. Or twenty rand owed by each of three friends, negative twenty times three, negative sixty. The total debt is sixty rand regardless of which number you say first.

Here is why the property is worth knowing rather than just true. Suppose you add up a long list of small wins and losses from a board game: minus four, plus nine, minus six, plus two, minus one. Instead of plodding left to right, collect the wins: nine plus two is eleven. Collect the losses: negative four plus negative six plus negative one is negative eleven. Eleven plus negative eleven is zero — you broke even. You just rearranged five numbers and the commutative property guaranteed the answer stayed the same. Learners who skip that step make sign slips in the middle of the chain.

So: adding and multiplying, order is free; subtracting and dividing, order is law. The questions now are till slips and game scores; decide first whether the operation is one you are allowed to reorder.

## Subtopic: Grouping the Taxi Fare — Associative in Real Life

Three of you share a taxi. The fare adjustments on your group account over the week are: you owe twelve rand, negative twelve; your friend is owed twenty rand after paying for everyone once, plus twenty; the third person owes eight rand, negative eight. What is the group's position? Group the first two: negative twelve plus twenty is eight; eight plus negative eight is zero. Or group the last two: twenty plus negative eight is twelve; negative twelve plus twelve is zero. Whichever pair you settle first, the group ends at zero. That is the associative property of addition, and it means you can settle debts in whatever grouping is convenient.

Multiplication groups just as freely. A tuck shop loses two rand on every cold drink it sells at a discount, negative two per drink. It sells five drinks a day for twenty days. You can do two times five first — negative two times five is negative ten per day, times twenty days, negative two hundred rand. Or five times twenty first — one hundred drinks, times negative two, negative two hundred rand. Same loss, two groupings. Choose the grouping that gives a round number: here five times twenty is one hundred, which makes the final step trivial.

Where grouping is not free: subtraction. Say you have one hundred rand, spend forty, and then a friend cancels a debt of ten that you owed, which is subtracting a negative ten. Bracket one hundred minus forty bracket minus negative ten: sixty plus ten is seventy. But one hundred minus bracket forty minus negative ten bracket: forty plus ten is fifty, and one hundred minus fifty is fifty. Seventy or fifty — the brackets changed your money. With subtraction you must respect the order the events happened, or convert everything to additions and then group freely: one hundred plus negative forty plus ten is seventy, in any grouping.

The lesson for calculations: when a line is all additions, or all multiplications, bracket the pair that is easiest and do it first. When a line has subtractions or divisions, either go strictly left to right or convert to additions first. The questions now are about taxi accounts and tuck-shop losses; look for the pair that makes a round number.

## Subtopic: Sharing a Debt — Distributive in Real Life, and the Takeaway

The distributive property is the one with the longest name and the simplest picture. Four friends each owe seven rand for pizza, and each one gets three rand back because the delivery was late. What is the change in the group's money? Per friend: negative seven plus three is negative four. Four friends: four times negative four, negative sixteen. The group is sixteen rand down.

Or do it the other way. All the pizza money first: four times negative seven is negative twenty-eight. All the refunds: four times three is twelve. Negative twenty-eight plus twelve is negative sixteen. Same answer. You have just shown that four times bracket negative seven plus three bracket equals four times negative seven plus four times three. That is the distributive property, with the multiplier spread across each thing inside the bracket.

The property also works when the multiplier is the negative one. Suppose instead the pizza place charges a group of negative four — a cancellation of four orders, say — at seven rand with three rand off each. Negative four times bracket seven plus negative three bracket. Bracket first, seven plus negative three is four; negative four times four is negative sixteen. Spread it: negative four times seven is negative twenty-eight; negative four times negative three is plus twelve, because removing a discount from a removed order puts money back; negative twenty-eight plus twelve, negative sixteen. The sign of each small product has to be right, and the story helps you keep it right.

Why bother spreading, if the bracket-first route works? Because sometimes the bracket contains a letter, as in four times bracket x plus three bracket, and then you cannot add first. Next term, the distributive property is how you will expand that bracket to four x plus twelve. Everything you practise today with numbers is rehearsal for that.

Here is the takeaway, in one breath. Adding and multiplying: you may swap the order, you may group as you like, and a multiplier may be spread over a bracket. Subtracting and dividing: none of that is allowed, so convert to adding the opposite first and then enjoy the freedom. You came in knowing three names from Grade 7; you are leaving knowing exactly where each one is safe to use with negatives, which is a real step towards algebra. The final questions mix all three properties; name the property to yourself before you calculate.
