# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises counting, ordering and comparing integers, then rebuilds addition and subtraction with integers from the number line. The anchor calculation is negative three plus eight minus negative two minus ten, which equals negative three, and every rule used to reach it is justified rather than memorised. Integers are the ground on which the whole of Grade 8 algebra stands, so this revision is not optional.

## Subtopic: Integers on the Number Line — Ordering and Comparing

Begin with the definition. The integers are the whole numbers, their opposites, and zero: dot dot dot, negative three, negative two, negative one, zero, one, two, three, dot dot dot. The set is written with the symbol Z, and it stretches without end in both directions. Every natural number is an integer; the new members are zero and the negatives. A negative integer is written with a sign in front, and the sign is part of the number, not an operation.

Draw the number line and the whole topic becomes visible. Zero in the middle, positives to the right, negatives to the left, equal spacing. The single most important fact about the number line is this: further right means larger. Three is to the right of one, so three is greater than one. Zero is to the right of negative four, so zero is greater than negative four. Negative two is to the right of negative seven, so negative two is greater than negative seven. That last one catches learners every year — seven is bigger than two, so surely negative seven is bigger than negative two? No. Negative seven is further from zero on the cold side, so it is smaller. Owing seven rand is worse than owing two rand.

The symbols: less than, with the small end pointing at the smaller number, and greater than, with the open end facing the larger number. Write negative seven is less than negative two. Write negative two is greater than negative seven. Both say the same thing. Order a set in ascending order and you simply read the number line from left to right: negative nine, negative four, negative one, zero, three, eight.

Counting with integers means counting across zero without stumbling. Count up in twos from negative five: negative five, negative three, negative one, one, three, five. Count down in threes from four: four, one, negative two, negative five, negative eight. Notice there is no gap at zero and no special step — zero is just another integer in the sequence.

Finally, the distance of an integer from zero, ignoring direction, is called its absolute value: the absolute value of negative six is six, and the absolute value of six is also six. You will use this idea in the sign rules very soon. Questions on ordering and comparing are with you now.

## Subtopic: Adding Integers — Same Signs and Different Signs

Addition on the number line is movement. Start at the first number; a positive addend moves you right, a negative addend moves you left. Five plus three: start at five, move three right, land on eight. Five plus negative three: start at five, move three left, land on two. Negative five plus three: start at negative five, move three right, land on negative two. Negative five plus negative three: start at negative five, move three left, land on negative eight.

Now extract the rules, because you cannot draw a number line for every question in an examination. Rule one, same signs: add the absolute values and keep the common sign. Negative five plus negative three — both negative, five and three make eight, keep the negative: negative eight. Two debts of five rand and three rand make a debt of eight rand. Rule two, different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, and take the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. Negative five plus three — the absolute values are five and three, five minus three is two, and the five was negative, so the answer is negative two. Five plus negative three — two again, but the five is positive, so positive two.

Test on a larger pair. Negative forty-seven plus twenty-nine. Different signs. Forty-seven minus twenty-nine is eighteen. The bigger absolute value, forty-seven, carried a negative sign, so the answer is negative eighteen. Negative forty-seven plus negative twenty-nine: same signs, seventy-six, negative. Write negative seventy-six.

Two special results you must know cold. Any integer plus its opposite is zero: nine plus negative nine is zero. And adding zero changes nothing: negative four plus zero is negative four. The first will be named the additive inverse later in this topic, and it is the whole reason subtraction can be turned into addition in the next section.

A word on layout. When a negative number follows an operation sign, put it in brackets: five plus bracket negative three bracket. Two signs standing side by side with no bracket is not an error in the mathematics, but it is an error in presentation, and markers notice. Questions on adding integers follow now.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Integers — Adding the Opposite

Subtraction is where integers earn their reputation, and one idea removes the difficulty entirely: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite. Eight minus three is five; eight plus negative three is also five. So a minus b equals a plus the opposite of b, always. This means you never need a separate set of subtraction rules — convert every subtraction to an addition and use the rules from the last section.

Apply it. Four minus negative three. The opposite of negative three is three, so this is four plus three, which is seven. Negative four minus three: the opposite of three is negative three, so negative four plus negative three, same signs, negative seven. Negative two minus negative six: the opposite of negative six is six, so negative two plus six, different signs, positive four. Three minus nine: three plus negative nine, which is negative six.

Why does subtracting a negative give a bigger answer? On the number line, subtracting means moving in the opposite direction to the sign of the number. Subtracting a positive moves you left; subtracting a negative therefore moves you right. Four minus negative three starts at four and moves three right, to seven. In money: removing a debt of three rand from your account leaves you three rand better off.

The examiner's favourite phrasing is a minus sign directly before a bracketed negative: minus bracket negative five bracket. Read it as minus negative five, which is plus five. Two negatives, side by side as subtraction of a negative, become a positive. Do not extend that slogan to addition — negative five plus negative three is negative eight, two negatives, and the answer is negative. The slogan applies to the operation sign meeting the number sign, nothing else.

For a longer calculation, the method mark comes from showing the conversion step. Write the original line, write the line with every subtraction converted to adding the opposite, then compute. Three lines, full marks, and almost no room for sign slips. Questions on subtraction are with you now.

## Subtopic: Mixed Chains and Examination Technique

Now combine everything in the anchor calculation: negative three plus eight minus negative two minus ten. Work left to right, one operation at a time, converting as you go. Negative three plus eight: different signs, eight minus three is five, the eight is positive, so five. Five minus negative two: adding the opposite, five plus two, seven. Seven minus ten: seven plus negative ten, different signs, ten minus seven is three, the ten is negative, so negative three. The chain equals negative three.

The examination layout. Line one, the question copied correctly. Line two, every subtraction converted: negative three plus eight plus two plus negative ten. Line three, group the positives and the negatives if you prefer: eight plus two is ten; negative three plus negative ten is negative thirteen. Line four, ten plus negative thirteen is negative three. Either the left-to-right route or the grouping route is acceptable, and both are worth full marks when the working is visible.

Grouping is powerful on long chains. Negative six plus eleven minus four plus negative nine minus negative two. Convert: negative six plus eleven plus negative four plus negative nine plus two. Positives: eleven plus two is thirteen. Negatives: negative six plus negative four plus negative nine is negative nineteen. Thirteen plus negative nineteen is negative six. You are allowed to regroup because addition of integers is commutative and associative, which the next lesson in this topic makes formal.

The common errors, so you can avoid them. Treating minus negative as negative: four minus negative three written as one. Dropping a sign when copying the line. Subtracting absolute values when the signs are the same: negative five plus negative three written as negative two. And the subtle one — reading negative three squared when the question meant the opposite of three squared; that belongs to a later lesson, but the bracket habit you build now protects you then.

Check every answer with a quick estimate of sign and size. In the anchor, the negatives were three and ten, total thirteen; the positives were eight and two, total ten. Negatives win by three, so the answer must be negative and small. Negative three fits. Questions on mixed chains follow.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same integers, but as temperatures, bank balances and basement parking levels. The rules do not change; they just start to feel obvious, because you have been adding and subtracting negatives in real life for years without calling it that.

## Subtopic: Temperatures — Negatives You Can Feel

Let us leave the symbols and go to Sutherland in the Northern Cape, the coldest town in South Africa. One winter night the temperature is negative seven degrees. By midday it is five degrees. How much did it warm up? Picture a thermometer, which is just a number line standing upright. From negative seven to zero is seven degrees of climbing. From zero to five is five more. Twelve degrees altogether. Notice what you did: you found the gap between negative seven and five, and the gap was seven plus five, not five minus seven.

That is subtraction of integers in disguise. Change in temperature is final minus start: five minus negative seven. Our rule says adding the opposite: five plus seven, twelve. The thermometer and the rule agree, and that agreement is the point — the rule is not a trick, it is a description of what thermometers do.

Go the other way. It is three degrees in the evening and the forecast says a drop of eight degrees overnight. Start at three, go down eight: three, two, one, zero, negative one, negative two, negative three, negative four, negative five. Negative five degrees — frost on the windscreen. In symbols, three minus eight is negative five, or three plus negative eight.

A colder one. Midnight is negative four. By three in the morning it has dropped another six. Negative four, down six more: negative ten. Two drops together: negative four plus negative six, same signs, keep the cold, negative ten. If the question had said it rose six from negative four, you would climb to two: negative four plus six is positive two, because six beats four.

Here is the habit to build: whenever a sum has negatives in it, imagine the thermometer. Adding a positive climbs, adding a negative drops, subtracting flips the direction. You cannot get negative five plus three wrong if you see five below zero climbing three — you land two below. A few temperature questions for you now; feel the thermometer as you answer.

## Subtopic: Bank Balances and Basement Parking

Money is the other place where you already understand integers. A positive balance is money you have; a negative balance is money you owe — an overdraft. Say your account shows negative one hundred and fifty rand. Your friend pays back the two hundred rand she borrowed. Negative one hundred and fifty plus two hundred: the deposit beats the debt by fifty, so the balance is positive fifty rand. Different signs, bigger one wins, difference is the answer.

Now a debit order of three hundred rand goes off while the balance is negative one hundred and fifty. Negative one hundred and fifty plus negative three hundred: two debts, same sign, pile them up — negative four hundred and fifty rand. Then the bank reverses a wrong charge of eighty rand. Reversing a charge is subtracting a negative: negative four hundred and fifty minus negative eighty, which is negative four hundred and fifty plus eighty, which is negative three hundred and seventy rand. Removing a debt makes you better off, and that is exactly why subtracting a negative moves you up.

Shopping-mall parking gives the same picture vertically. The ground floor is zero, shops are floors one to three, and parking is in basements negative one to negative four. You park on level negative three and take the lift up five floors. Negative three plus five is two: you step out on the second floor. Later you are on the first floor and go down four floors: one minus four is negative three, back at your car.

The lift also shows the gap idea. How many floors apart are level two and level negative three? Two minus negative three is two plus three, five floors. Count them: negative three to zero is three, zero to two is two, five in total. Whenever a question asks for the difference between two integers, subtract the lower from the higher and expect a positive answer.

Every one of these is a sum you have already done in Part 1 — just dressed in rands and lift buttons. The questions now use money and lifts; translate each into a plus or a minus before you answer.

## Subtopic: The Traps, and the Takeaway

Last stretch, and this is where the marks are kept or lost. Here are the three traps that catch Grade 8 learners, each with its fix.

Trap one: thinking the bigger-looking negative is bigger. Negative twelve looks bigger than negative five, but it is further left, so it is smaller. Fix: picture debt. Owing twelve rand is worse than owing five, so negative twelve is less than negative five. In an ordering question, put the most negative number first when ascending.

Trap two: two signs in a row. Six minus negative four. Learners write two, because they see a six, a four and a minus. Fix: say it out loud as six take away a debt of four. Taking away a debt helps you, so you go up: ten. Six minus negative four is ten. And the mirror: negative six plus negative four is not plus ten and not negative two; two debts make a bigger debt, negative ten.

Trap three: losing the sign on the way. Negative eight plus three — a learner correctly works out eight minus three is five, then writes five and moves on. Fix: before you write the answer, ask which number was heavier. The eight was heavier and it was negative, so the answer is negative five. Always ask: who wins, and what sign are they wearing?

A tiny routine to finish with. Read the sum. Change any subtraction to adding the opposite. Decide: same signs or different? Same signs: add and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract and take the heavier sign. Check with the thermometer in your head. Four steps, and every integer addition and subtraction in Grade 8 and Grade 9 goes through them.

You started with a cold number line and you are finishing with a method you can run without a picture — that is a real win. Next lesson you will multiply and divide integers, where the rules are different again and, honestly, easier. The final questions mix everything from this lesson, so run the four steps on each one.
