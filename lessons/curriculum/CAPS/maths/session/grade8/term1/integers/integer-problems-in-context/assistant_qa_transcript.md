### subtopic_1 — The Method — Sign Convention, Expression, Evaluate, Interpret
**Assistant:** Why is a change always calculated as finish minus start, even when the start is negative?

**Tutor:** Because a change measures how far and in which direction you moved from where you began, and subtraction is the operation that measures a gap with direction. Finish minus start gives a positive result when the finish is higher, warmer or richer, and a negative result when it is lower, colder or poorer, which is exactly the meaning you want. When the start is negative, subtracting it becomes adding its absolute value, so the change is larger than either number: from negative nine degrees to six degrees is six minus negative nine, fifteen degrees, because you climbed nine to reach zero and then six more. Learners who write six minus nine get negative three, which describes no part of the story. Keeping the formula fixed and bracketing the negative start is what makes the sign come out right every time, and writing the formula down is what earns the method mark.

### subtopic_2 — Money — Balances, Debit Orders and Deposits
**Assistant:** In the bank problem, why write the three debit orders as a multiplication rather than subtracting three times?

**Tutor:** Both are correct, but the multiplication is faster, less error-prone and shows the structure the examiner wants to see. Three equal changes of negative one hundred and eighty rand are a repeated addition, and repeated addition is multiplication: three times negative one hundred and eighty, which is negative five hundred and forty. Writing it as a product also puts the order of operations to work for you — the product is evaluated first, then the additions run left to right — and it makes the expression a single line that contains the whole story, including the overdrawn balance of negative two hundred and ninety rand that appears before the deposit. Subtracting one hundred and eighty three separate times gives the same final answer but triples the chances of a sign slip and hides the fact that the three payments are alike. In any context where the same signed change happens several times, reach for a product.

### subtopic_3 — Scoring Systems and Time Zones
**Assistant:** How do time-zone differences fit the same pattern as altitude and temperature?

**Tutor:** They are all differences between two signed positions, found as the higher value minus the lower value. Time zones are given as offsets from Universal Time, with places ahead of it positive and places behind it negative, so South Africa is plus two and New York in winter is negative five. The difference is two minus negative five, which is two plus five, seven hours, just as the height difference between a summit above sea level and a shore below it is the summit's height minus the shore's negative depth. The convention step is what makes the three contexts identical: once you have decided that ahead, above and warmer are positive, every difference question becomes higher minus lower, and subtracting the negative lower value adds the two distances from zero. Reporting the difference as a positive number with a unit, seven hours, finishes the answer.

### subtopic_5 — The Quiz Night — Scores That Go Negative
**Assistant:** Can a score really be negative, and what does that mean?

**Tutor:** Under a system with penalties it can, and it means the penalties have outweighed the points earned, so the team is in points debt. In the quiz, four right answers earn sixteen points and sixteen wrong answers cost thirty-two, giving sixteen plus negative thirty-two, which is negative sixteen. The team is sixteen points below zero, and if the organisers award a bonus later, that bonus first has to pay off the debt before the score becomes positive. This is exactly the behaviour of a bank balance that has gone overdrawn, and it is the reason integers were invented: to record positions on the far side of zero. The gap between a team on thirty-eight and a team on negative sixteen is thirty-eight minus negative sixteen, fifty-four points, which is larger than either score — the same subtracting-a-negative pattern as the altitude and temperature problems.
