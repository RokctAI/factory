### subtopic_1 — Commutative Property — Order Does Not Matter, Except When It Does
**Assistant:** If subtraction is not commutative, why do the two answers come out as opposites?

**Tutor:** Because swapping the order of a subtraction is the same as multiplying the result by negative one. The expression a minus b and the expression b minus a are additive inverses of each other: add them and you get zero, since a minus b plus b minus a collapses to nothing. So five minus negative two is seven, and negative two minus five is negative seven, and the pair always has this mirror form. That is useful as a check — if you swap a subtraction by accident and your answer is the opposite of the expected one, you know exactly what happened. It also explains why rearranging around a minus sign is dangerous: the size might survive but the sign flips, and a sign flip is a wrong answer. The safe habit is to convert each subtraction to adding the opposite, after which the commutative property applies and any order is legal.

### subtopic_3 — Distributive Property — Multiplication Over Addition
**Assistant:** When distributing a negative multiplier, how do I keep the signs right?

**Tutor:** Treat the bracket as a sum of signed terms before you distribute, and multiply the multiplier by each term, sign included, as its own little product. In negative four times bracket seven plus negative three bracket, the terms are positive seven and negative three. Negative four times positive seven is negative twenty-eight, different signs. Negative four times negative three is positive twelve, same signs. The sum is negative sixteen. The error is to see the minus in front of the three, write minus twelve, and reach negative forty. If the bracket contains a subtraction, convert it first: five minus negative two becomes five plus two, and only then distribute. Finally, use the bracket-first route as a check whenever the bracket contains only numbers; if the two routes disagree, a sign has been dropped in the distribution.

### subtopic_4 — Using the Properties — Mental Maths and Examination Technique
**Assistant:** Why does the distributive property not work for division over a bracket?

**Tutor:** Because division from the left does not spread across a sum. Twelve divided by bracket two plus four bracket means twelve shared into six parts, which is two. Twelve divided by two plus twelve divided by four means six plus three, which is nine. Those are different, so a divided by bracket b plus c bracket is not a divided by b plus a divided by c. What does hold is division of a sum by a single number from the right: bracket twelve plus six bracket divided by three equals twelve divided by three plus six divided by three, since dividing by three is multiplying by one third, and multiplication does distribute. Examiners test the false version precisely because it looks like the distributive property. The rule to keep is that a multiplier in front of a bracket spreads, but a divisor underneath a bracket does not.

### subtopic_6 — Grouping the Taxi Fare — Associative in Real Life
**Assistant:** Does it ever matter which pair I group first in a long multiplication?

**Tutor:** For the answer, no; for the ease of getting it, very much. Multiplication of integers is associative and commutative, so any grouping and any order gives the same product, and you should choose the grouping that produces round numbers. In negative twenty-five times negative seven times four, pairing negative twenty-five with four gives negative one hundred immediately, and negative one hundred times negative seven is seven hundred with no effort. Pairing negative twenty-five with negative seven first gives one hundred and seventy-five, which then has to be multiplied by four — correct but slower and more error-prone. Two warnings. Count the negative signs before you start so that you know the sign of the final answer, and if the line contains a division anywhere, do not regroup across it, because division is not associative; work left to right or rewrite the division as multiplication by a reciprocal.
