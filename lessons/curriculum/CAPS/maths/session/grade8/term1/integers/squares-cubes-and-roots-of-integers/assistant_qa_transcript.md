### subtopic_1 — Squares of Integers and the Bracket That Changes Everything
**Assistant:** Why does −3² give −9 when the number being squared looks negative?

**Tutor:** Because the number being squared is not negative three; it is three. Written without brackets, negative three squared is read as the opposite of three squared, since an exponent is applied before the negative sign in the order of operations. The negative sign in front is really a multiplication by negative one, and multiplication waits until the power has been evaluated. Three squared is nine, and the opposite of nine is negative nine. To square the integer negative three itself, you must wrap it in brackets: bracket negative three bracket squared is negative three times negative three, which is positive nine. Examiners set both forms side by side precisely because learners read them as the same thing. The reflex to build is to read the expression aloud exactly as printed — the square of negative three for the bracketed form, and the opposite of three squared for the unbracketed form — before writing a single digit.

### subtopic_3 — Square Roots — Undoing the Square
**Assistant:** If both 7 and −7 square to 49, why is the square root of 49 just 7?

**Tutor:** Because the root sign is defined by convention to mean the non-negative root, so that every expression has a single value. It is perfectly true that both seven and negative seven have a square of forty-nine, and if a question asks which integers square to forty-nine you must give both. But the symbol root of forty-nine is a specific instruction: give the non-negative number whose square is forty-nine, which is seven. When a question wants both roots it signals this, usually with a plus-or-minus sign written in front of the root. Keep three expressions apart in your mind: root of sixteen is four, the negative of root sixteen is negative four, and root of negative sixteen is not a real number at all. The position of the sign — in front of the root or underneath it — changes the meaning completely, exactly as brackets did for squaring.

### subtopic_4 — Cube Roots and Mixed Calculations
**Assistant:** Does the root sign act like a bracket in the order of operations?

**Tutor:** Yes. Everything written under a root sign is evaluated first, as if it were inside a bracket, and then the root is taken. Only after that does the result join the rest of the calculation, and at that point it behaves like any other number. So root of bracket one hundred and sixty-nine minus twenty-five bracket means work out one hundred and forty-four first and then take the root, giving twelve. It does not mean root of one hundred and sixty-nine minus root of twenty-five, which would be thirteen minus five, eight. Roots do not distribute over addition or subtraction, and that is a common error. Roots and powers sit at the same level as exponents, so they are evaluated before multiplication and division. In the example two times root of one hundred and forty-four divided by negative four, the root gives twelve, then two times twelve is twenty-four, then dividing by negative four gives negative six.

### subtopic_6 — Sugar Cubes and Deep Holes — Cubes Keep Their Sign
**Assistant:** Why is it fine to take the cube root of a negative but not the square root of a negative?

**Tutor:** Because cubing keeps the sign of the base and squaring destroys it. A square is a product of two equal factors, so the two signs are always the same and the product is positive or zero; nothing you square can come out negative, so a negative number has no real square root. A cube is a product of three equal factors; two of them pair off to a positive, and the third keeps its sign, so the cube of a negative is negative. That means negative numbers do have cube roots: the cube root of negative twenty-seven is negative three, because negative three cubed is negative twenty-seven. The physical picture helps. A square is a count of tiles on a square floor, and no floor has a negative number of tiles. A cube can be a stack of sugar cubes going up or a hole dug down, and holes are allowed.
