### subtopic_1 — Adding and Subtracting Decimals — Line Up the Commas
**Assistant:** Why is lining up the commas so important when adding decimals?

**Tutor:** Because addition only works between digits of the same place value, and the comma is the marker that shows where each place is. When the commas are in a vertical line, tenths sit under tenths and hundredths under hundredths, so each column adds like-sized pieces. If instead you right-align the numbers as you would whole numbers, zero comma nine ends up under the eight of zero comma four eight, nine tenths are added to eight hundredths, and the result zero comma five seven is wrong by a factor of nearly three. Padding with zeros — writing zero comma nine as zero comma nine zero — makes the alignment automatic and also makes the error visible, because nine zero over four eight is plainly larger than forty-eight. A quick estimate gives a second guard: about a half plus about one is about one and a half, which one comma three eight satisfies and zero comma five seven does not.

### subtopic_2 — Estimating by Rounding to One Significant Figure
**Assistant:** Why round to one significant figure rather than to the nearest whole number?

**Tutor:** Because the purpose of the estimate is to be quick and to fix the size of the answer, and one significant figure does both for numbers of any size. Rounding to the nearest whole number works for four comma eight seven but fails for zero comma zero four eight, which would round to zero and give a useless estimate, and for four hundred and eighty-seven, which would not simplify at all. Rounding to the first non-zero digit — five, zero comma zero five, five hundred — always produces a number you can multiply or divide mentally, and the result is within a factor of about two of the true answer, which is enough to catch a comma in the wrong place, since a comma slip changes the answer by a factor of ten. Two significant figures gives a closer estimate but costs more effort, and the extra closeness rarely matters because the errors we are hunting are large ones.

### subtopic_3 — Predicting Decimal Places from Place Value
**Assistant:** How does place value give an independent check on the comma?

**Tutor:** Because place value fixes the exact number of decimal places an answer must have, regardless of the digits, while estimation fixes the rough size regardless of the places; two different pieces of information that must both be satisfied. For a product the places add — tenths times hundredths are thousandths — so four comma eight seven times two comma one must have three decimal places. For a sum or difference the answer has as many places as the longest number. For a quotient, the scaling step produces a whole-number divisor and the comma in the quotient sits above the comma in the scaled dividend. If the digit string is one zero two two seven, the place rule allows only ten comma two two seven among the candidates near the estimate of ten, so the two checks agree and the comma is certain. When they disagree, one of the two has been misapplied and the calculation must be revisited before anything is written.

### subtopic_7 — The Pharmacy Dose — The Takeaway
**Assistant:** Is checking really necessary when I have a calculator?

**Tutor:** It is more necessary, not less, because a calculator removes arithmetic errors and leaves only entry errors, and entry errors are large. A slipped comma, a doubled digit or a missed key changes the display by a factor of ten or more, and the calculator reports the wrong result with complete confidence. The estimate is the only thing that notices. In the pharmacy example a dose of zero comma two five millilitres per kilogram for an eighteen-kilogram child is about a quarter of eighteen, four and a half millilitres; a display reading forty-five would be accepted by someone who did not estimate and rejected instantly by someone who did. The inverse check adds a second layer: multiplying a quotient back, or adding a difference back, uses different keys and catches different slips. Markers reward both habits, but the real reward is that an answer you have checked is one you can trust.
