### subtopic_1 — Why Counting Decimal Places Works
**Assistant:** Why do the decimal places add when I multiply, rather than staying the same?

**Tutor:** Because a decimal with one decimal place is a whole number of tenths, and a tenth of a tenth is a hundredth. Zero comma six is six tenths and zero comma four is four tenths; multiplying gives six times four, twenty-four, over ten times ten, one hundred, so twenty-four hundredths, which is zero comma two four with two decimal places. In general a number with m decimal places is a whole number over ten to the power m, and a number with n places is a whole number over ten to the power n; their product is over ten to the power m plus n, which is a decimal with m plus n places. The rule is therefore not a convention but a consequence of how fractions multiply. It also explains why multiplying by a decimal less than one makes the answer smaller: you are taking a part of a part, exactly as when you multiply two proper fractions.

### subtopic_2 — Multi-Digit Products — The Anchor, Laid Out
**Assistant:** Why is estimating written down as part of the answer rather than done in my head?

**Tutor:** Because the estimate is a second, independent method for placing the comma, and showing it earns a method mark while protecting the final mark. The only way to get a decimal product wrong, once the digit multiplication is right, is to place the comma in the wrong position, which changes the answer by a factor of ten or a hundred. Counting the decimal places gives one answer for the position; rounding each factor to one significant figure and multiplying gives a rough size the answer must have. When the two agree, the comma is almost certainly correct. When they disagree, you have caught an error before the marker does. Many memoranda award a mark for a sensible estimate, and a written estimate also makes a calculator slip visible, since a missed comma key produces an answer the estimate rejects. An estimate in your head does neither of those things if you forget to do it under pressure.

### subtopic_3 — Multiplying by Powers of Ten and by Tenths
**Assistant:** What is the connection between multiplying by 0,1 and dividing by 10?

**Tutor:** They are the same operation, because zero comma one is the fraction one tenth, and multiplying by one tenth is dividing by ten. Three comma four seven times zero comma one is three comma four seven divided by ten, which is zero comma three four seven: every digit has moved one place to the right into a position worth a tenth as much, so the comma appears to have moved one place to the left. Similarly multiplying by zero comma zero one divides by one hundred and multiplying by zero comma zero zero one divides by one thousand. The count-the-places rule gives the same result — three comma four seven times zero comma one has two plus one, three decimal places, and three hundred and forty-seven with three places is zero comma three four seven — so the two views agree. This connection is the clearest reason that multiplying by a decimal smaller than one makes a number smaller, and it previews dividing by decimals in the next lesson.
