Today we revise the three laws that govern every addition and multiplication you will ever do — the commutative, associative and distributive properties — together with the two numbers that change nothing when you use them: zero for addition and one for multiplication. These laws are the reason a calculation like four times thirty-seven times twenty-five can be done in your head in two seconds, and they are the exact rules you will use to expand brackets and collect like terms when algebra arrives in Term 2. By the end you will name each law, use it deliberately, and know the two places it does not apply.
