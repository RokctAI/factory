Hey — it's me, your session host. If ratios feel like a pile of colons and fractions that could go either way, take a breath; there are really only three moves in this whole lesson, and you have done all three at a kitchen table.

Sharing is stacks. Count the stacks — four plus five is nine — find what one stack is worth, hand them out, and check the table is empty. Scaling is a fraction with the new number on top and the old number underneath; if you were told to make more and your answer got smaller, just flip the fraction. Comparing is the price for ONE — divide down to one egg, one litre, one hour, and the better buy shows itself.

The only mistake that really costs marks is dividing by the number of people instead of the number of stacks. Say it once: stacks, not people.

Go try the practice questions now. Write the three lines every time — parts, one part, multiply out — even when the numbers are easy. That layout is where the marks live. You've got this.
