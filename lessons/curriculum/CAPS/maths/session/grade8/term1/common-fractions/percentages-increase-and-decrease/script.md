# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises percentage of a whole number and of a part of a whole, calculates one quantity as a percentage of another, applies percentage increase and decrease using multipliers, and solves the reverse problem of finding an original amount from a changed amount. The anchor results are that eight hundred increased by twelve percent is eight hundred and ninety-six, and that a price of three hundred and sixty rand after a twenty percent discount came from an original price of four hundred and fifty rand.

## Subtopic: Percent Means Out of a Hundred

Percent means per hundred. Forty percent is forty out of every hundred, the fraction forty over one hundred, which simplifies to two fifths, and the decimal zero comma four. Every percentage is a fraction with denominator one hundred, so every percentage calculation is a fraction calculation, and you already have the tools.

A percentage of an amount is a fraction of an amount: multiply. Fifteen percent of two hundred and forty is fifteen over one hundred times two hundred and forty. Divide by the denominator first: two hundred and forty divided by one hundred is two comma four; times fifteen is thirty-six. Fifteen percent of two hundred and forty is thirty-six. Alternatively, build from ten percent: ten percent of two hundred and forty is twenty-four, five percent is twelve, so fifteen percent is thirty-six. The ten-percent method is excellent for mental work and for checking.

Percentage of a part of a whole: the same multiplication, applied twice. Three quarters of a class are girls, and forty percent of the girls wear glasses. What percentage of the class are girls who wear glasses? Forty percent of three quarters: two fifths times three quarters is six twentieths, three tenths, thirty percent. Thirty percent of the class. Percent of a part means multiply the fractions, exactly as a fraction of a fraction did.

A quantity as a percentage of another: write the fraction and convert it to hundredths. Thirty-five out of fifty: thirty-five over fifty, multiply top and bottom by two, seventy over one hundred, seventy percent. Twenty-four out of forty: twenty-four over forty is three fifths, which is sixty over one hundred, sixty percent. When the denominator does not go neatly into one hundred, multiply the fraction by one hundred: eighteen out of twenty-four is eighteen over twenty-four times one hundred, which is seventy-five percent.

Order matters in the wording. Twenty as a percentage of eighty is twenty-five percent; eighty as a percentage of twenty is four hundred percent. The quantity after of is the denominator. Questions on the meaning of percent follow now.

## Subtopic: Percentage Increase and Decrease — The Multiplier

To increase an amount by a percentage, you can find the percentage and add it: twelve percent of eight hundred is ninety-six, and eight hundred plus ninety-six is eight hundred and ninety-six. Two steps. The multiplier method does it in one. After a twelve percent increase you have one hundred percent plus twelve percent, which is one hundred and twelve percent of the original, and one hundred and twelve percent is the decimal one comma one two. Eight hundred times one comma one two is eight hundred and ninety-six. One multiplication.

To decrease by a percentage, subtract from one hundred percent. A twenty-five percent decrease leaves seventy-five percent, multiplier zero comma seven five. Eight hundred times zero comma seven five is six hundred. Check the two-step way: twenty-five percent of eight hundred is two hundred, and eight hundred minus two hundred is six hundred. Agreed.

The multiplier is the key to everything that follows, so practise reading it. Increase by eight percent: one comma zero eight. Increase by fifteen percent: one comma one five. Decrease by thirty percent: zero comma seven. Decrease by five percent: zero comma nine five. Increase by one hundred percent — doubling: two. An increase gives a multiplier above one; a decrease gives a multiplier below one; and a multiplier is never negative.

Why the multiplier matters beyond speed. It makes the reverse problem possible, it chains cleanly — two successive changes are two multiplications — and it is exactly the growth factor you will meet in simple and compound interest in later grades. In Grade 8 financial maths, adding fifteen percent VAT to a price is multiplying by one comma one five, and that one fact saves a line in every VAT question.

Worked example, examination layout. A school's enrolment of one thousand two hundred rises by eight percent. New enrolment: one thousand two hundred times one comma zero eight is one thousand two hundred and ninety-six. Line one, the multiplier with a sentence: one hundred percent plus eight percent is one hundred and eight percent, so multiply by one comma zero eight. Line two, the product. Full marks. Questions on multipliers follow.

## Subtopic: Percentage Change — Comparing Before and After

Given an old value and a new value, the percentage change is the change divided by the original, times one hundred. Always the original — the value before the change — in the denominator. A price rises from forty rand to fifty rand. Change: ten rand. Percentage increase: ten over forty times one hundred, twenty-five percent.

Now the same two numbers the other way. A price falls from fifty rand to forty rand. Change: ten rand. Percentage decrease: ten over fifty times one hundred, twenty percent. Same ten rand, same two prices, but twenty-five percent up and twenty percent down, because the original is different in each direction. This asymmetry is the most tested idea in the section: a twenty-five percent rise is undone by a twenty percent fall, not by a twenty-five percent fall.

A worked example. A learner's mark improves from sixty to sixty-nine. Percentage increase: nine over sixty times one hundred, fifteen percent. A town's population falls from twelve thousand to ten thousand eight hundred. Change: one thousand two hundred. Percentage decrease: one thousand two hundred over twelve thousand times one hundred, ten percent.

Layout for marks: line one, the change, found by subtraction. Line two, the fraction change over original. Line three, times one hundred, with the percent sign and the word increase or decrease. Writing change over new value instead of over original is the standard error, and it gives a wrong answer that is often close enough to look plausible, so build the habit of underlining the original value in the question.

Percentage change also lets you check a multiplier calculation backwards. Eight hundred rose to eight hundred and ninety-six: change ninety-six, ninety-six over eight hundred is zero comma one two, twelve percent. The multiplier one comma one two was correct. Questions on percentage change follow.

## Subtopic: Finding the Original Amount — Reverse Percentages

The reverse problem gives the amount after a change and asks for the amount before. A jacket costs three hundred and sixty rand after a twenty percent discount. What was the original price? The tempting error is to add twenty percent of three hundred and sixty, giving four hundred and thirty-two rand. Wrong, because the twenty percent was taken off the original price, not off the sale price. Use the multiplier. A twenty percent discount is multiplying by zero comma eight, so original times zero comma eight equals three hundred and sixty. Original is three hundred and sixty divided by zero comma eight, which is four hundred and fifty rand. Check: twenty percent of four hundred and fifty is ninety, and four hundred and fifty minus ninety is three hundred and sixty. Correct.

The same logic for an increase. A price including fifteen percent VAT is three hundred and forty-five rand. The price before VAT is three hundred and forty-five divided by one comma one five, which is three hundred rand. Check: fifteen percent of three hundred is forty-five, and three hundred plus forty-five is three hundred and forty-five. Never subtract fifteen percent of the VAT-inclusive price; that gives two hundred and ninety-three rand twenty-five, which is wrong.

The unitary method gives the same answers and some learners prefer it. After the twenty percent discount, the three hundred and sixty rand is eighty percent of the original. One percent is three hundred and sixty divided by eighty, four rand fifty. One hundred percent is four hundred and fifty rand. Both methods earn full marks; the multiplier method is one line, the unitary method is two.

The rule to carry: to undo a percentage change, divide by the multiplier. Increase of twelve percent, divide by one comma one two. Decrease of thirty percent, divide by zero comma seven. Eight hundred and ninety-six divided by one comma one two is eight hundred — the anchor, reversed.

Distinction-level takeaway: percent is a fraction over one hundred; a percentage of an amount is multiplication; percentage change is change over original; a change by a percentage is multiplication by a multiplier; and undoing a change is division by that same multiplier. # Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same percentages at the till, in the test results and on the sale rail. The multiplier idea — one point one two for a twelve percent rise — is the one trick that makes shop maths fast, and we will build it slowly enough that it sticks.

## Subtopic: Ten Percent First — Shop Maths in Your Head

Here is the trick that makes you the fastest person at the till. Find ten percent first. Ten percent of anything is that thing divided by ten — just move the digits one place. Ten percent of two hundred and forty rand is twenty-four rand. Once you have ten percent, you can build almost any percentage out of it. Five percent is half of that: twelve rand. Fifteen percent is ten plus five: thirty-six rand. Twenty percent is double: forty-eight rand. Thirty percent is triple: seventy-two rand. One percent is a tenth of ten percent: two rand forty.

Try it on a jacket marked four hundred and fifty rand with twenty percent off. Ten percent is forty-five. Twenty percent is ninety. The discount is ninety rand, so you pay three hundred and sixty. Done before the cashier has scanned it.

Now the other direction — a percentage from two numbers. You got thirty-five out of fifty on a test. What percentage? Out of fifty means out of a half of a hundred, so double both: seventy out of a hundred. Seventy percent. Twenty-four out of forty: forty into a hundred is two and a half, so multiply both by two and a half — or simplify first: twenty-four out of forty is three fifths, and a fifth is twenty percent, so three fifths is sixty percent. Knowing the common fractions as percentages — a half is fifty, a quarter is twenty-five, a fifth is twenty, a tenth is ten, three quarters is seventy-five — makes most test marks instant.

A percent of a part. Three quarters of the class are girls, and forty percent of those girls wear glasses. Picture one hundred learners. Seventy-five are girls. Forty percent of seventy-five: ten percent is seven and a half, so forty percent is thirty. Thirty learners out of one hundred, thirty percent of the class. Working out of one hundred turns a two-fraction problem into counting.

So: ten percent first, then build; know the fraction-percent pairs; and when a percentage of a percentage appears, imagine a hundred people. The questions are till and test questions; find ten percent first.

## Subtopic: One Point One Two — Growing and Shrinking in One Step

Here is the idea that turns two steps into one. A rent of eight hundred rand goes up by twelve percent. The slow way: twelve percent of eight hundred is ninety-six, add it on, eight hundred and ninety-six. Fine. But think about what you end up with: all of the old rent, which is one hundred percent, plus another twelve percent. One hundred and twelve percent of the old rent. And one hundred and twelve percent as a decimal is one comma one two. So the new rent is simply eight hundred times one comma one two, which is eight hundred and ninety-six. One multiplication, same answer.

Now a sale. Twenty-five percent off eight hundred. You keep seventy-five percent of the price, and seventy-five percent is zero comma seven five. Eight hundred times zero comma seven five is six hundred. The number you multiply by is called the multiplier, and it is just the percentage you end up with, written as a decimal.

Let us practise reading them, because this is a skill of its own. Up by eight percent: you end with one hundred and eight percent, multiply by one comma zero eight. Down by thirty percent: you end with seventy percent, multiply by zero comma seven. Up by fifty percent: one comma five. Down by half: zero comma five. Add fifteen percent VAT: one comma one five. A multiplier above one means growing; below one means shrinking; and it is never negative, because you can never end up with less than nothing of a price.

Why bother, when the slow way works? Two reasons. First, VAT: every shop price in South Africa includes fifteen percent VAT, and the price before VAT is the shelf price divided by one comma one five — which you can only do if you think in multipliers. Second, the reverse question, which is where the multiplier really earns its keep, and which is the last section.

So: work out what percentage you end up with, write it as a decimal, and multiply once. The questions are rent, sales and VAT; find the multiplier first.

## Subtopic: Working Backwards from the Sale Price — The Takeaway

The sale rail says: jacket, three hundred and sixty rand, twenty percent off. What was it before the sale? Nearly everyone's first instinct is to add twenty percent of three hundred and sixty, which is seventy-two, and say four hundred and thirty-two. Let us test that. Twenty percent off four hundred and thirty-two is eighty-six rand forty, which leaves three hundred and forty-five rand sixty. Not three hundred and sixty. The instinct is wrong, because the twenty percent was taken off the original price, which was bigger, so the discount was bigger than seventy-two.

Here is the right way, with the multiplier. Twenty percent off means the sale price is eighty percent of the original: original times zero comma eight is three hundred and sixty. So the original is three hundred and sixty divided by zero comma eight. Dividing by zero comma eight is the same as dividing by eight and multiplying by ten: three hundred and sixty divided by eight is forty-five, times ten is four hundred and fifty. Four hundred and fifty rand. Test it: twenty percent of four hundred and fifty is ninety, and four hundred and fifty minus ninety is three hundred and sixty. Perfect.

Same idea with VAT. A phone costs three hundred and forty-five rand including fifteen percent VAT. Before VAT it was three hundred and forty-five divided by one comma one five, which is three hundred rand. If you had instead taken fifteen percent off three hundred and forty-five you would get two hundred and ninety-three rand twenty-five, and the shop's accountant would not be impressed.

If dividing by a decimal feels uncomfortable, use blocks. The sale price is eighty percent — eighty blocks out of a hundred. Eighty blocks are three hundred and sixty rand, so one block is four rand fifty, and a hundred blocks are four hundred and fifty rand. Same answer, no decimals.

So here is the whole lesson. Percent is out of a hundred. Ten percent first, then build. A change by a percentage is one multiplication by the multiplier. Going backwards is one division by the same multiplier, or blocks if you prefer. You walked in able to find a percentage of a number and you are walking out able to undo a discount in your head — a real step up, and exactly what next lesson's work on fractions, decimals and percentages together will lean on. The final questions mix everything; find the multiplier and decide whether to multiply or divide.
