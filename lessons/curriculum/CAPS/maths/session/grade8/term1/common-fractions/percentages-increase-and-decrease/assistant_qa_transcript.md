### subtopic_1 — Percent Means Out of a Hundred
**Assistant:** Why is a percentage of a percentage found by multiplying rather than adding?

**Tutor:** Because the second percentage is taken of the part produced by the first, which is exactly a fraction of a fraction, and a fraction of a fraction is a product. If three quarters of the class are girls and forty percent of the girls wear glasses, the girls with glasses are forty percent of seventy-five percent of the class. Forty over one hundred times seventy-five over one hundred is thirty over one hundred, thirty percent. Adding would give one hundred and fifteen percent, more than the whole class, which is absurd. The hundred-people picture makes it concrete: seventy-five girls, and forty percent of seventy-five is thirty, so thirty of the hundred learners. ### subtopic_2 — Percentage Increase and Decrease — The Multiplier
**Assistant:** How do I know whether a multiplier should be above or below one?

**Tutor:** By asking what percentage of the original you end up with. An increase leaves you with more than one hundred percent of the original, so the multiplier is above one: a twelve percent increase ends at one hundred and twelve percent, multiplier one comma one two. A decrease leaves you with less than one hundred percent, so the multiplier is below one: a twenty-five percent decrease ends at seventy-five percent, multiplier zero comma seven five. The percentage in the question is never itself the multiplier; it is the amount by which one hundred percent is adjusted. A multiplier is never negative, because even a one hundred percent decrease only takes you to zero. A useful check is to glance at your answer: multiplying by a number above one must give a bigger result and by a number below one a smaller one, so a decrease question that produced a larger value means the multiplier was built the wrong way.

### subtopic_3 — Percentage Change — Comparing Before and After
**Assistant:** Why does a 25% increase get undone by a 20% decrease and not a 25% decrease?

**Tutor:** Because the two percentages are taken of different originals. Rising from forty to fifty is a change of ten on an original of forty, which is twenty-five percent. Falling from fifty back to forty is a change of ten on an original of fifty, which is twenty percent. ### subtopic_4 — Finding the Original Amount — Reverse Percentages
**Assistant:** In a reverse percentage problem, why divide by the multiplier instead of subtracting the percentage?

**Tutor:** Because the percentage was applied to the original amount, which you do not yet know, and not to the final amount, which you do.
