## Subtopic: Fraction to Decimal to Percentage

A common fraction, a decimal fraction and a percentage are three notations for one number. Three eighths means three divided by eight; carry out the division and you get zero comma three seven five; multiply by one hundred and you get thirty-seven comma five percent. The three forms are not three numbers; they are one point on the number line with three labels, and the examination tests whether you can read all three.

Fraction to decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator. One quarter is one divided by four, zero comma two five. Three fifths is zero comma six. Seven twentieths: seven divided by twenty is zero comma three five. When the denominator is a factor of a power of ten, you can also build an equivalent fraction with denominator ten, one hundred or one thousand: seven twentieths is thirty-five hundredths, which is zero comma three five directly.

Decimal to percentage: multiply by one hundred, which moves the digits two places. Zero comma three seven five becomes thirty-seven comma five percent. Zero comma six becomes sixty percent. Zero comma zero eight becomes eight percent. One comma two becomes one hundred and twenty percent — percentages above one hundred are normal when the number is above one.

Fraction to percentage directly: multiply by one hundred and simplify. Three eighths times one hundred is three hundred over eight, thirty-seven and a half percent. Two fifths times one hundred is forty percent. This route avoids the decimal entirely and is often quicker when the fraction is simple.

Some fractions give recurring decimals. One third is zero comma three recurring, written with a dot over the three; two thirds is zero comma six recurring; one sixth is zero comma one six recurring with the dot over the six only. As percentages these are thirty-three and a third percent and sixty-six and two thirds percent. ## Subtopic: Choosing the Right Form and Examination Technique

Each form has a job. Fractions are exact and best for calculation: one third of ninety is thirty without any recurring decimals. Decimals are best for measurement, money and calculators: a length of zero comma three seven five metres is a reading, not a fraction. Percentages are best for comparison and communication: a mark of eighty-five percent means the same thing whatever the test was out of. A strong candidate converts to the form that makes the current step easiest, and converts back at the end if the question specifies a form.

Worked example. A learner scores seventeen out of twenty on one test and forty-two out of fifty on another. Which is better, and by how many percentage points? Convert to percentages: seventeen over twenty is eighty-five percent; forty-two over fifty is eighty-four percent. The first is better by one percentage point. Fractions would have required a common denominator of one hundred anyway, so percentages were the natural form.

Another. Find three eighths of two hundred and forty. In fraction form: two hundred and forty divided by eight is thirty, times three is ninety. In decimal form: zero comma three seven five times two hundred and forty is ninety. Same answer; the fraction route avoided a decimal multiplication. Choose the fraction when the denominator divides the amount.

Technique for conversion questions. Show the operation used — divide by the denominator, multiply or divide by one hundred, write over a power of ten — on one line, and the result on the next. Simplify every fraction. Use the recurring dot correctly: a dot over each digit that recurs, and never a rounded decimal when the question asks for an exact value. Give percentages with a percent sign, and keep fractional percentages as fractions, thirty-seven and a half percent, unless a decimal percentage is asked for.

Distinction-level takeaway: the three forms are one number; convert with divide-by-the-denominator, times-or-divide-by-one-hundred, and place value; order by converting everything to one form; and choose the form that makes the next step easy. Questions on choosing the form follow.
