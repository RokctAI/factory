# Part 1 — Expert

This session converts between the SI units of area: square millimetres, square centimetres, square metres and square kilometres. Each area conversion factor is the square of the matching length factor. Since 1 centimetre is 10 millimetres, 1 square centimetre is 10 times 10, which is 100 square millimetres. Since 1 metre is 100 centimetres, 1 square metre is 10 000 square centimetres. Since 1 kilometre is 1 000 metres, 1 square kilometre is 1 000 000 square metres. Converting to a smaller unit multiplies by the factor; converting to a larger unit divides. When the dimensions of a shape are known, converting the lengths first and then multiplying avoids the area factors altogether. The anchor examples are a 30-centimetre floor tile, a phone screen 70 by 150 millimetres and a soccer pitch of 7 140 square metres.

## Subtopic: Why Area Factors Are Squared

Length conversions are familiar. 1 centimetre is 10 millimetres. 1 metre is 100 centimetres. 1 kilometre is 1 000 metres. Area conversions are different, and the reason is visible in a single square.

Draw a square 1 centimetre on each side. Its area is 1 square centimetre. Now measure the same square in millimetres: each side is 10 millimetres. Divide it into squares 1 millimetre on each side: there are 10 rows of 10, which is 100 small squares. So 1 square centimetre equals 100 square millimetres. The length factor is 10, and the area factor is 10 times 10, because the square is 10 times as long in both directions.

The same reasoning gives every factor. A square 1 metre on each side is 100 centimetres on each side, so it holds 100 times 100, which is 10 000 square centimetres. 1 square metre equals 10 000 square centimetres. And since each square centimetre is 100 square millimetres, 1 square metre is 10 000 times 100, which is 1 000 000 square millimetres — which is also 1 000 times 1 000, as the 1 000 millimetres in a metre predict.

A square 1 kilometre on each side is 1 000 metres on each side, so it holds 1 000 times 1 000, which is 1 000 000 square metres. 1 square kilometre equals one million square metres.

So the area factors are the length factors squared. Length: 10, 100, 1 000. Area: 100, 10 000, 1 000 000.

This is the scale-factor rule from the transformation lessons in another form. Changing units is like enlarging the measuring grid: a length factor k gives an area factor k squared.

The most common error in the topic is using the length factor for an area: saying 1 square metre is 100 square centimetres. Picture a square metre on the floor — big enough to stand in — and a square centimetre, about the size of a fingernail. A hundred fingernails would cover only a small patch, about the size of a phone screen. Ten thousand is right.

A useful landmark for each unit. A square millimetre is a pinhead. A square centimetre is a fingernail. A square metre is a large floor tile or a single desk top and chair. A square kilometre is roughly 140 soccer pitches. A hectare, which is not in the syllabus list but appears on farms and in property adverts, is a square 100 metres on each side: 10 000 square metres, about one and a half soccer pitches. ## Subtopic: Converting Down and Converting Up

With the factors known, every conversion is a single multiplication or division. The direction decides which.

Converting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number, so multiply. Converting to a larger unit gives a smaller number, so divide. A square metre is a big unit; the same floor measured in square centimetres needs many more of the small units.

Square centimetres to square millimetres: multiply by 100. 3,5 square centimetres is 350 square millimetres. 0,8 square centimetres is 80 square millimetres.

Square millimetres to square centimetres: divide by 100. 2 500 square millimetres is 25 square centimetres. 64 square millimetres is 0,64 square centimetres.

Square metres to square centimetres: multiply by 10 000. 4,2 square metres is 42 000 square centimetres. 0,09 square metres — the area of a 30-centimetre tile — is 900 square centimetres.

Square centimetres to square metres: divide by 10 000. 15 000 square centimetres is 1,5 square metres. 900 square centimetres is 0,09 square metres.

Square kilometres to square metres: multiply by 1 000 000. 2,3 square kilometres is 2 300 000 square metres.

Square metres to square kilometres: divide by 1 000 000. 750 000 square metres is 0,75 square kilometres. 7 140 square metres, a soccer pitch, is 0,00714 square kilometres.

Multiplying or dividing by 100, 10 000 or 1 000 000 moves the decimal comma 2, 4 or 6 places. Notice that these are twice the 1, 2 or 3 places of the length conversions — again the squaring. To multiply 4,2 by 10 000, move the comma 4 places right, filling with zeros: 42 000. To divide 64 by 100, move it 2 places left: 0,64.

For steps through more than one unit, chain the factors. Square millimetres to square metres: divide by 100 to get square centimetres, then by 10 000 to get square metres, a total of dividing by 1 000 000. 10 500 square millimetres is 105 square centimetres, which is 0,0105 square metres.

A check: after converting, ask whether the number should have grown or shrunk. Going to a smaller unit, the number must grow. If 4,2 square metres became 0,00042 square centimetres, the division was done instead of the multiplication. Questions on converting down and up follow now.

## Subtopic: Convert the Lengths First

When the dimensions of a shape are known, there is a route that avoids the area factors entirely: convert each length to the required unit, then calculate the area.

A phone screen is 70 millimetres wide and 150 millimetres tall. In square millimetres: 70 times 150, which is 10 500. In square centimetres, route one: 10 500 divided by 100, which is 105. Route two: convert the lengths, 7 centimetres by 15 centimetres, then multiply: 105 square centimetres. The same answer, and route two needed only the familiar length factor of 10.

A floor is 4,2 metres by 3,6 metres. In square centimetres: convert the lengths to 420 and 360 centimetres, then multiply: 151 200 square centimetres. Check with the area factor: the floor is 15,12 square metres, times 10 000 is 151 200.

A field is 850 metres by 600 metres. In square kilometres: 0,85 kilometres times 0,6 kilometres, which is 0,51 square kilometres. Check: 850 times 600 is 510 000 square metres, divided by 1 000 000 is 0,51.

Route two is especially useful when the dimensions come in different units. A tile is 30 centimetres square and a floor is 4,2 by 3,6 metres. Convert the tile to 0,3 metres: area 0,09 square metres. The floor is 15,12 square metres. Number of tiles: 15,12 divided by 0,09, which is 168. Or convert the floor to centimetres: 151 200 square centimetres, divided by the tile's 900 square centimetres, is 168. Either way works, as long as both areas are in the same unit before dividing.

The two routes also give a way to check the area factors: if you forget whether a square metre is 10 000 or 100 square centimetres, picture a 1 metre by 1 metre square, convert each side to 100 centimetres, and multiply: 10 000.

There is one caution. Route two works for any shape whose area comes from lengths — rectangles, triangles, circles — but a circle's radius must be converted, not its area. A circle of radius 5 centimetres has area 25 pi square centimetres, about 78,54. In square millimetres, either multiply by 100 to get about 7 854, or convert the radius to 50 millimetres and calculate pi times 2 500, about 7 854. The answers agree. Questions on converting the lengths first follow now.

## Subtopic: Choosing a Sensible Unit

Every area can be written in any of the four units, but one is usually sensible, giving a number that is neither tiny nor enormous.

A postage stamp: about 6 square centimetres, or 600 square millimetres. Either is fine; square millimetres suit very small, precise items such as computer chips or the cross-section of a wire, and square centimetres suit things the size of a hand.

A sheet of A4 paper: 21 centimetres by 29,7 centimetres, about 623,7 square centimetres, or about 0,062 square metres. Square centimetres are more natural.

A classroom floor: about 60 square metres. In square centimetres it would be 600 000, which is hard to picture.

A suburb, a farm or a national park: square kilometres. A farm of 3,6 square kilometres is 3 600 000 square metres; the smaller number is clearer.

Choosing well matters for communication. A news report that a fire burned 12 000 000 square metres is accurate but hard to grasp; 12 square kilometres is immediately meaningful.

Rounding must suit the unit too. An area of 15,12 square metres is precise enough for a floor. Converting it to 151 200 square centimetres does not make it more accurate, it just adds zeros.

Comparing areas requires the same unit. Which is larger: 0,05 square metres or 450 square centimetres? Convert one: 0,05 square metres is 500 square centimetres, so 0,05 square metres is larger. Which is larger: 2,5 square kilometres or 3 000 000 square metres? 2,5 square kilometres is 2 500 000 square metres, so the second is larger.

Ordering a list of areas: convert all to one unit first. For 1,2 square metres, 9 000 square centimetres and 1 500 000 square millimetres, convert to square centimetres: 12 000, 9 000 and 15 000. In order from smallest: 9 000 square centimetres, then 1,2 square metres, then 1 500 000 square millimetres.

Rates are areas too. A paint tin that covers 12 square metres, a fertiliser that needs 30 grams per square metre, a property price of R4 500 per square metre: when the area of the job is in another unit, convert it to the unit in the rate before multiplying. A lawn of 85 000 square centimetres is 8,5 square metres, which needs 8,5 times 30, which is 255 grams of fertiliser. Questions on choosing units follow now.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same conversions as stories about a news report that shrank a veld fire by a factor of a thousand, a family fitting solar panels measured in millimetres onto a roof measured in metres, and a learner who tried to count how many soccer pitches fit in a national park — stories about why a square unit must be squared.

## Subtopic: The Fire That Shrank in the News

A community radio station in Mpumalanga reports on a veld fire near a farming town. The fire services say it burned 12 square kilometres of grassland. The news reader, converting for listeners who might find square kilometres unfamiliar, announces that the fire burned 12 000 square metres.

A Grade 8 learner listening at home is puzzled. 12 000 square metres is less than two soccer pitches — a large fire for a back yard, but hardly worth a news bulletin. She works it out properly.

A square kilometre is a square 1 000 metres on each side, so it is 1 000 times 1 000, which is 1 000 000 square metres. Twelve of them is 12 000 000 square metres. The news reader had used the length factor, 1 000, instead of the area factor, 1 000 000. The fire was a thousand times bigger than the bulletin said.

To make it real, she compares it with soccer pitches. A pitch is 105 by 68 metres, 7 140 square metres. 12 000 000 divided by 7 140 is about 1 681 pitches. The bulletin's figure would have been under 2 pitches.

She also checks the size another way. 12 square kilometres could be a rectangle 4 kilometres by 3 kilometres — a walk of about an hour along one edge. That matches the fire services' description of the fire front stretching from the main road almost to the river.

She sends a message to the station. The next bulletin corrects the figure: 12 square kilometres, which is 12 million square metres, or about 1 200 hectares. She checks the hectares too: a hectare is 10 000 square metres, so 12 000 000 divided by 10 000 is 1 200. Correct.

Her teacher uses the incident in class. The class lists every conversion with its length factor and its area factor side by side: millimetres to centimetres, 10 and 100; centimetres to metres, 100 and 10 000; metres to kilometres, 1 000 and 1 000 000. Then they practise spotting the error in five made-up news headlines. One says a new shopping centre covers 0,05 square kilometres, or 5 000 square metres; the class corrects it to 50 000. Another says a farm of 450 hectares covers 4,5 square kilometres — and that one is right, because 450 times 10 000 is 4 500 000 square metres. Questions on the news report follow.

## Subtopic: Solar Panels on the Roof

After a winter of power cuts, a family in Polokwane decides to install solar panels. The installer's brochure gives each panel's size as 1 722 millimetres by 1 134 millimetres. The usable, north-facing part of their roof measures 8 metres by 5 metres. The learner in the family is asked how many panels could fit.

Two units are in play, millimetres and metres, and they must be matched. She converts the panel's lengths to metres: 1,722 metres by 1,134 metres. Its area is 1,722 times 1,134, which is about 1,95 square metres. Check by the other route: 1 722 times 1 134 is 1 952 748 square millimetres, and dividing by 1 000 000 gives about 1,95 square metres. The same.

The roof area is 8 times 5, which is 40 square metres. Dividing, 40 by about 1,95 is about 20,5, so at most 20 panels by area.

But panels are rectangles that cannot be cut, so she checks how they actually fit. With the long side of each panel along the 8-metre edge: 8 divided by 1,722 is about 4,6, so 4 panels fit along; 5 divided by 1,134 is about 4,4, so 4 rows fit. That is 16 panels. With the panels turned: 8 divided by 1,134 is about 7,05, so 7 fit along; 5 divided by 1,722 is about 2,9, so 2 rows. That is 14 panels. The best layout fits 16 panels, not the 20 that the area alone suggests, because of the gaps at the edges.

The installer also needs a gap of about 20 millimetres between panels for the clamps. She checks the 16-panel layout: 4 panels along is 4 times 1 722, plus 3 gaps of 20, which is 6 888 plus 60, which is 6 948 millimetres, about 6,95 metres, within 8 metres; 4 rows is 4 times 1 134 plus 60, which is 4 596 millimetres, about 4,6 metres, within 5. It fits.

Each panel produces about 400 watts in full sun. The family's area calculation says the panels cover 16 times 1,95, which is about 31,2 square metres of the 40 available. The brochure claims about 200 watts per square metre of panel, which the learner checks: 400 watts divided by 1,95 square metres is about 205 watts per square metre. The claim holds.

The order is placed for 16 panels. The installer remarks that most mistakes he sees come from customers dividing roof area by panel area and forgetting that panels do not bend around corners. Questions on the solar panels follow.

## Subtopic: Method Summary and Examination Technique

The facts. 1 square centimetre is 100 square millimetres. 1 square metre is 10 000 square centimetres, or 1 000 000 square millimetres. 1 square kilometre is 1 000 000 square metres. Each area factor is the square of the length factor.

The method for converting an area. One: identify the starting unit and the target unit. Two: decide the direction — to a smaller unit, multiply; to a larger unit, divide. Three: use the area factor, 100, 10 000 or 1 000 000, or chain factors across several units. Four: move the decimal comma 2, 4 or 6 places. Five: check that the number grew when the unit shrank and shrank when the unit grew.

The alternative when dimensions are known: convert the lengths to the target unit first, then calculate the area.

A typical examination item asks for a conversion in a table: fill in 3,5 square centimetres in square millimetres, 2 500 square millimetres in square centimetres, and so on. Multiply or divide by the right factor and show it.

Another gives a shape with dimensions in one unit and asks for the area in another. Convert the lengths first, or calculate and then convert, showing which.

Another asks how many tiles, panels or sheets cover an area, with the item in centimetres or millimetres and the area in metres. Put both in one unit, divide, round up for whole items, and check the fit if the shapes are rectangles.

Another asks which of two areas is larger, or to order several. Convert all to one unit.

Another asks you to explain why 1 square metre is 10 000 square centimetres. Draw a 1-metre square, mark each side as 100 centimetres, and multiply: 100 rows of 100.

Errors to avoid. Using the length factor for an area: 100 instead of 10 000. Multiplying when you should divide. Converting the area of a circle by converting only the pi. Dividing areas in different units. Forgetting to round items up.

Looking ahead: the next topic, surface area and volume, uses the square units for surfaces and introduces cubic units for volume, where the factors are the length factors cubed: 1 cubic centimetre is 1 000 cubic millimetres. The squaring reasoning of this lesson extends to cubing in three dimensions. Final questions follow.
