Today we find the area of shapes that have no single formula — L-shaped rooms, trapezium-shaped plots, house-shaped badges, irregular yards — by cutting them into rectangles and triangles whose areas we already know. A polygon can be split into pieces and the pieces added, or enclosed in a rectangle and the extra pieces subtracted, and either way the answer must be the same. By the end you will choose a good decomposition, find each piece's dimensions from the measurements given, work with decimal lengths and round the final area to two decimal places, and find areas of polygons drawn on a grid by enclosing them in a rectangle.
