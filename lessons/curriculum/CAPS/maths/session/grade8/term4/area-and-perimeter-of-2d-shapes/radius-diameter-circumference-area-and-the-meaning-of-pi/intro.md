Today we ask where the circle formulae come from. Measure any round object and divide the distance around it by the distance across: the answer is always a little more than 3, the same for a coin as for a bicycle wheel, and that constant is pi. From that one fact follow the circumference formula and, by cutting a circle into slices and rearranging them, the area formula. By the end you will explain what pi means and why it cannot be written exactly as a fraction or terminating decimal, move freely between radius, diameter, circumference and area, see why doubling the radius doubles the circumference but quadruples the area, and work backwards from a circumference or an area to the radius.
