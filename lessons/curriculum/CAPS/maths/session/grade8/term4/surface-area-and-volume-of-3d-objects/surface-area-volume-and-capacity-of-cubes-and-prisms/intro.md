Today we move from flat shapes to solid ones — cubes, rectangular boxes and triangular prisms such as tents and roof spaces — and measure them in two ways: the surface area, the total area of all the faces, which tells you how much material wraps the outside; and the volume, the space inside, which tells you how much fits in. Capacity is volume for liquids, and one cubic centimetre holds exactly one millilitre. By the end you will draw the net of a prism and find its surface area face by face, calculate the volume of any prism as the area of its cross-section times its length, convert volumes to millilitres and litres, and handle the triangular prism, whose faces are not all rectangles.
