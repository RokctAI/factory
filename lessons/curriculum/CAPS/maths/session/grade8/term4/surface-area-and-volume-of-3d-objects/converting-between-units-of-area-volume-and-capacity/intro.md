Today we complete the set of conversions: areas in square units, volumes in cubic units, and capacities in millilitres, litres and kilolitres. Just as area factors are the length factors squared, volume factors are the length factors cubed — one cubic centimetre is a thousand cubic millimetres, and one cubic metre is a million cubic centimetres. The bridge between volume and capacity is exact: a cubic centimetre is a millilitre, and a cubic metre is a kilolitre. By the end you will convert any volume or capacity in either direction, explain where every factor comes from, move between cubic units and litres in the middle of a problem, and read real quantities — a water bill, a medicine dose, a farm dam — in the unit that makes sense.
