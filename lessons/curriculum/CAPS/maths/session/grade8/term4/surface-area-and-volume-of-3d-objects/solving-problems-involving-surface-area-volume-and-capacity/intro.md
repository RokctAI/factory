Today surface area, volume and capacity go to work on real jobs: filling a water tank and timing the tap, catching rain from a roof, packing boxes into a carton, painting a room, and pouring concrete for a ramp. Each problem has to be read for what it is really asking — the outside of something, the inside of something, or how fast it fills — and then the answer must be turned into litres, tins, minutes or rand. By the end you will plan multi-step problems with prisms, convert between cubic units and litres in the middle of a calculation, check whether objects really fit and not only whether their volumes divide, and present a solution that a reader can follow, with or without a calculator.
