Today we ask how the outside of a solid is related to its inside. Two boxes can hold exactly the same amount yet need very different amounts of cardboard; two boxes made from the same cardboard can hold different amounts; and when a box is enlarged, its volume grows much faster than its surface. By the end you will build different boxes from the same number of cubes and compare their surface areas, recognise that compact, cube-like shapes have the least surface for their volume, describe how surface area and volume change when every length is multiplied by a scale factor, and use the surface-area-to-volume ratio to explain why packaging, ice and living things behave the way they do.
