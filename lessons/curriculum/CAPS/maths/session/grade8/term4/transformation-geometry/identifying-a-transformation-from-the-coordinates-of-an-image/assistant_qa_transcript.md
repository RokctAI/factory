### subtopic_1 — Reading the Clues: Signs and Swaps
**Assistant:** Both the 90-degree rotations and the reflection in y equals x swap the coordinates. How do I keep them apart quickly?

**Tutor:** Count the sign changes after the swap. Write the original as x semicolon y and the image with the coordinates swapped, then ask how many of the two numbers changed sign. None: reflection in the line y equals x, rule y semicolon x. Exactly one: a 90-degree rotation about the origin — and which one changed tells the direction: the new first coordinate negated, rule negative y semicolon x, is anticlockwise; the new second coordinate negated, rule y semicolon negative x, is clockwise. Both changed after a swap is not one of the Grade 8 transformations; it is reflection in the line y equals negative x, which comes later. For the point 3 semicolon 2: image 2 semicolon 3, no sign change, reflection; image negative 2 semicolon 3, first negated, anticlockwise; image 2 semicolon negative 3, second negated, clockwise. When the original has a negative coordinate, be careful about what changed: from negative 2 semicolon 5 to negative 5 semicolon negative 2, the swapped pair would be 5 semicolon negative 2, and the image differs from that in the first coordinate only, so it is the anticlockwise rotation. If in doubt, sketch the two arrows from the origin and see whether the turn is a quarter anticlockwise, a quarter clockwise, or a flip across the diagonal.

### subtopic_2 — Describing the Transformation Precisely
**Assistant:** If I write translation 4 right and 3 up, do I also need the rule?

**Tutor:** No; either form is a complete description, and examiners accept both unless the question specifically asks for the rule or for a description in words. The words form — 4 units to the right and 3 units up — is clear and is the natural way to speak about a slide. The rule form — x semicolon y maps to x plus 4 semicolon y plus 3 — is more compact and is the one needed when the translation is to be applied to many points or combined with other transformations, because it can be applied mechanically. What is not acceptable is an incomplete description: translation on its own, or 4 and 3 without directions, or 7 as if the two parts could be added into one number. Also take care with signs in the rule: a shift of 2 left and 5 down is x minus 2 semicolon y minus 5, and writing plus by habit reverses the direction. A good practice is to give both forms when there is time, words first and rule second; it costs a few seconds and guards against a slip in either.

### subtopic_3 — Checks: Distances from Axes, the Origin and the Mirror
**Assistant:** Why does checking the distance from the origin not separate a reflection from a rotation?

**Tutor:** Because all three Grade 8 mirrors — the x-axis, the y-axis and the line y equals x — pass through the origin, and any reflection in a line through a point keeps distances from that point unchanged, exactly as a rotation about the point does. The point 3 semicolon 2 and all of its images under those five transformations lie on the same circle of radius root 13 centred at the origin. So the distance test can only say: this is a rigid motion that fixes the origin, which means a reflection or a rotation and not a translation. To go further you need the pattern of signs and swaps, or a second kind of check. One such check is orientation, which a single point cannot show but a triangle can: reflections reverse the order of the vertices around the figure and rotations keep it. Another is the midpoint test for reflections: the midpoint of a point and its reflected image lies on the mirror, whereas for a rotation of 90 degrees the midpoint lies nowhere special. If the mirror were a line not through the origin, which Grade 9 introduces, the distance from the origin would change under the reflection and the test would catch it; for now, treat the distance test as the translation filter and nothing more.

### subtopic_5 — The Scooter's GPS Log
**Assistant:** The fourth scooter's move was fixed by a mid-trip point. What if no extra point is available?

**Tutor:** Then you report both possibilities, because the data genuinely does not decide, and you say what would. For start 6 semicolon 0 and end negative 6 semicolon 0, write: the transformation is either a reflection in the y-axis or a rotation of 180 degrees about the origin; a point off the x-axis would distinguish them, since the reflection keeps y and the rotation negates it. That is a complete and correct answer. What you should not do is pick one at random and present it as certain, because it may be marked wrong if the examiner's intended answer was the other, and because it misrepresents what the coordinates show. In practice, examination questions avoid this trap by choosing points off the axes and off the line y equals x, or by saying could, which invites one plausible answer with its justification. In the real world, as with the scooter, the fix is to get more data — a second point, a time stamp, a direction of travel. Being able to say the information is insufficient, and exactly what information would suffice, is a mathematical skill in its own right, and it is how the dispatcher's software ended up asking for a mid-point whenever a start lay on an axis.
