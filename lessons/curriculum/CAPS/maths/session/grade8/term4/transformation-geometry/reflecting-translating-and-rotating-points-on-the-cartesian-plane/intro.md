Today we move points around the Cartesian plane by rule. A reflection flips a point across a mirror line — the x-axis, the y-axis or the line y equals x; a translation slides it a fixed number of units across and up; a rotation turns it about a centre through a quarter-turn, a half-turn or three-quarters. Each transformation has a coordinate rule, and by the end you will apply every rule to any point in any quadrant, write the rule in the form x semicolon y maps to its image, plot the point and its image to check, and name the image with the prime notation that the rest of the topic uses.
