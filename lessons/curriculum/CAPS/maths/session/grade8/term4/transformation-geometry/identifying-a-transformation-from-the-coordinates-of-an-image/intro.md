Today we work backwards: a point went in, an image came out, and the question is which transformation did it. The clues are in the coordinates — a sign changed, the two numbers swapped, a constant was added — and reading them correctly names the reflection, translation or rotation and, where it matters, the mirror line, the shift or the angle and direction. By the end you will compare a point with its image and name the transformation precisely, recognise when more than one transformation fits a single point and what extra information settles it, use distances from the axes and the origin as checks, and describe your answer in the exact language the examiner expects.
