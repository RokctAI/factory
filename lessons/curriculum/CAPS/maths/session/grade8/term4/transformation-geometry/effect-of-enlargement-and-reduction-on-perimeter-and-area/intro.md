Today a shape changes size but not shape, and the question is what happens to its perimeter and its area. When every length is multiplied by a scale factor, the perimeter is multiplied by that same factor, but the area is multiplied by the scale factor squared — so doubling the sides of a garden doubles the fence and quadruples the ground. By the end you will describe an enlargement or reduction by its scale factor, predict the new perimeter and area without redrawing the shape, work backwards from an area ratio to the scale factor, and use these proportions to compare real things such as pizzas, photographs and gardens.
