Today we discover the most famous fact in geometry by measuring, drawing and counting squares, and then we state it precisely. In every right-angled triangle, the square on the longest side — the hypotenuse — has exactly the same area as the two squares on the other sides put together, so that the side lengths satisfy a squared plus b squared equals c squared. By the end you will construct right-angled triangles and test the relationship, draw squares on a grid to see it as areas, know the standard triples such as 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13, state the theorem in words and symbols with the hypotenuse correctly identified, and use it to find a hypotenuse from the two shorter sides.
