Today the theorem becomes a tool for finding lengths, including the ones Term 3 had to leave: the height of an equilateral triangle, the diagonal of a square, the reach of a ladder. Finding a hypotenuse means adding squares; finding a leg means subtracting them; and when the answer is not a whole number it is written exactly as a square root — a surd — simplified where a square factor allows, with a decimal only if the question asks. By the end you will choose between adding and subtracting correctly, write the three-line solution, simplify surds such as the square root of 50 to five root two, decide when to leave a surd and when to round, and apply the method to rectangles, isosceles triangles and real situations.
