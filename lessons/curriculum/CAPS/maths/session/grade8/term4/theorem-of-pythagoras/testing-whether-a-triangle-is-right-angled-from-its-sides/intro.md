Today we run the Theorem of Pythagoras in reverse. Given three side lengths and no angles at all, the test is to square the longest side and compare it with the sum of the squares of the other two: equal means the triangle is right-angled, with the right angle opposite the longest side; unequal means it is not. By the end you will apply the test correctly — longest side alone on one side of the comparison — to whole-number, decimal and scaled sides, state the converse of the theorem as the reason, locate the right angle when there is one, and read the direction of the inequality to say whether a non-right triangle is acute- or obtuse-angled.
