# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the place value of digits in whole numbers to at least four digits. The anchor is a fund-raising total of R4 736 at a school in Kimberley, shown as notes and coins, and a jar of 2 305 marbles packed into boxes of a thousand, a hundred and ten. The governing idea is that a digit has a face value, the digit itself, and a place value, what it is worth because of where it stands, and that each place is worth ten times the place to its right. Naming the place of a digit, stating its value, writing numbers in expanded notation and reading numbers with zeros in the middle are examinable.

## Subtopic: Thousands, Hundreds, Tens and Units

A four-digit number has four places. From the right they are units, tens, hundreds and thousands. In 4 736 the 6 is in the units place, the 3 is in the tens place, the 7 is in the hundreds place and the 4 is in the thousands place. The small space between the 4 and the 736 is how South African numbers show the thousands group.

Each place is worth ten of the place to its right. Ten units make a ten. Ten tens make a hundred. Ten hundreds make a thousand. So the 4 in 4 736 is not worth 4; it is worth 4 thousands, which is 4 000. The 7 is worth 700, the 3 is worth 30 and the 6 is worth 6.

The fund-raising money shows this with notes and coins. R4 736 can be laid out as four R1 000 piles, seven R100 notes, three R10 notes and six R1 coins. Count them: 4 000 plus 700 plus 30 plus 6 is 4 736. The same digits in a different order would mean different money. R6 374 has six thousands and only four units.

The marble jar holds 2 305 marbles. Packed into boxes of a thousand there are 2 full boxes. The rest, 305, fills 3 boxes of a hundred, 0 boxes of ten and leaves 5 loose marbles. The digits 2, 3, 0 and 5 are the box counts. Place value is simply a way of counting in boxes of ten, a hundred and a thousand.

The questions for this section are with you now: naming the place of each digit in a four-digit number and saying what it is worth.

## Subtopic: Face Value, Place Value and Expanded Notation

Every digit has two values. The face value is the digit itself. The place value is what the digit is worth where it stands. In 4 736 the digit 3 has a face value of 3 and a place value of 30, because it stands in the tens place. In 3 476 the same digit 3 has a place value of 3 000. The face value never changes; the place value depends entirely on position.

Expanded notation writes a number as the sum of its place values. 4 736 equals 4 000 plus 700 plus 30 plus 6. 2 305 equals 2 000 plus 300 plus 0 plus 5, which we can shorten to 2 000 plus 300 plus 5. Going the other way, 5 000 plus 20 plus 8 is 5 028, with a 0 holding the hundreds place.

Expanded notation can also be written with multiplication: 4 736 equals 4 times 1 000 plus 7 times 100 plus 3 times 10 plus 6 times 1. This form shows most clearly that a digit is multiplied by its place. The 7 is 7 times 100, which is 700.

Changing one digit changes the number by that digit's place value. If the fund-raising total of R4 736 grows to R4 836, the hundreds digit went from 7 to 8, so R100 was added. If it becomes R5 736, the thousands digit went from 4 to 5, so R1 000 was added. Reading the digit that changed tells you the amount of change at once.

The questions for this section are with you now: telling face value from place value, writing expanded notation both ways and reading the change when one digit changes.

## Subtopic: The Job of Zero

A zero in a number is a placeholder. It says that a place has nothing in it, but it keeps every other digit in its correct position. In 2 305 the 0 says there are no tens. Without it the number would read 235, which is a completely different amount with only 2 hundreds.

Zeros can stand in any place. 4 036 has no hundreds. 4 706 has no tens. 4 730 has no units. 4 000 has no hundreds, no tens and no units. Each of these is a different four-digit number, and the zeros are what make the 4 worth 4 000 in every case.

Reading numbers with zeros out loud needs care. 2 305 is two thousand three hundred and five. 4 036 is four thousand and thirty-six. 5 008 is five thousand and eight. Say only the places that have something in them, and let the zeros stay silent.

The distance from Cape Town to Musina is about 2 000 kilometres. The 2 is worth 2 000 only because three zeros hold the hundreds, tens and units places. Take one zero away and the distance drops to 200 kilometres, which would not even reach Beaufort West.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying the 7 in 4 736 is worth 7; its face value is 7, but its place value is 700. Two: writing five thousand and eight as 58 or 508; it needs zeros in the hundreds and tens places, 5 008. Three: dropping the zero in 2 305 and reading it as 235; the zero holds the tens place. Four: thinking the space in 4 736 separates two numbers; it only marks the thousands group of one number.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a zero does in the middle or at the end of a number and how to read numbers with zeros.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the fund-raising money and the marble jar — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Four Places

A four-digit number has four places: units, tens, hundreds, thousands. Read them from the right. In 4 736 the 6 is units, the 3 is tens, the 7 is hundreds, the 4 is thousands.

Each place is worth ten of the place next door on the right. Ten units make a ten. Ten tens make a hundred. Ten hundreds make a thousand.

The school raised R4 736. Picture the money: four piles of R1 000, seven R100 notes, three R10 notes, six R1 coins. The digits tell you how many of each.

Keep the piles of money in mind and try the questions for this part: naming the place of a digit.

## Subtopic: What Is the Digit Worth

A digit has a face and a place. The face value of 7 is 7. In 4 736 its place value is 700, because it stands in the hundreds place.

Expanded notation spells out the worth of each digit. 4 736 is 4 000 plus 700 plus 30 plus 6. Put it back together and you get 4 736 again.

Change one digit and the number changes by that place. R4 736 to R4 836 is R100 more. R4 736 to R5 736 is R1 000 more.

Hold the face and the place in mind and try this part's questions: the value of a digit and expanded notation.

## Subtopic: Zero Keeps the Place

Zero means nothing in that place, but it holds the place open. In 2 305 the 0 says no tens. Take it away and you get 235, a much smaller number.

Reading zeros: say only the places that have something. 2 305 is two thousand three hundred and five. 5 008 is five thousand and eight.

Cape Town to Musina is about 2 000 kilometres. Three zeros hold the places so the 2 can be worth 2 000.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Four-digit numbers have units, tens, hundreds and thousands, each place worth ten of the one to its right. A digit has a face value and a place value. Expanded notation writes the number as the sum of its place values. Zero holds a place open and stays silent when the number is read.

Hold the silent zero in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: numbers with zeros in them.
