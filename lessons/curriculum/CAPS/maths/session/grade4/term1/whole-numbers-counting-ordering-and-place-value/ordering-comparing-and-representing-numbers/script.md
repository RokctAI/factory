# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers ordering, comparing and representing whole numbers to at least four digits, and recognising odd and even numbers to at least 1 000. The anchor is three bicycles priced at R1 850, R1 580 and R1 805 at a market in Polokwane, and the heights of Mafadi at 3 450 metres, Table Mountain at 1 085 metres and the Compassberg at 2 502 metres. The governing idea is that comparison starts from the digit with the greatest place value, and that a number is even when its last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 and odd otherwise. Ordering four-digit numbers, using the symbols for greater than and less than, writing numbers in words and expanded form and classifying odd and even numbers are examinable.

## Subtopic: Comparing from the Left

To compare two whole numbers, first count the digits. A number with more digits is larger: 1 000 is larger than 999 because four digits beat three. When both numbers have the same number of digits, compare the leftmost digits first, because they carry the greatest place value.

The three bicycles cost R1 850, R1 580 and R1 805. All have four digits and all start with 1 thousand. Move to the hundreds: 8, 5 and 8. R1 580 has only 5 hundreds, so it is the cheapest. R1 850 and R1 805 both have 8 hundreds, so move to the tens: 5 and 0. R1 850 has 5 tens and R1 805 has 0 tens, so R1 850 is the most expensive.

The signs save words. The symbol with the open side to the left means greater than, and the symbol with the open side to the right means less than. The open side always faces the bigger number. So 1 850 is greater than 1 805, and 1 580 is less than 1 805. Equal numbers use the equals sign.

The mountains make the same point with bigger gaps. Mafadi is 3 450 metres, Compassberg is 2 502 metres, Table Mountain is 1 085 metres. Thousands digits 3, 2 and 1 settle the order at once: Mafadi is the highest and Table Mountain the lowest. You only move to the next place when the digits you are looking at are equal.

The questions for this section are with you now: comparing by number of digits, then by the leftmost digit, and choosing the correct comparison sign.

## Subtopic: Ordering and Representing Numbers

Ordering means arranging numbers from smallest to largest, called ascending order, or from largest to smallest, called descending order. The bicycle prices in ascending order are R1 580, R1 805, R1 850. In descending order they are R1 850, R1 805, R1 580.

A number can be shown in several ways. In digits: 3 450. In words: three thousand four hundred and fifty. In expanded form: 3 000 plus 400 plus 50 plus 0. Each form says the same thing. Expanded form is the most useful for comparing, because it shows exactly how many thousands, hundreds, tens and units the number holds.

Writing in words needs care with the word and. In South African English we write two thousand five hundred and two for 2 502, and one thousand and eighty-five for 1 085. The and sits before the tens and units. Watch for zeros: 1 085 has 0 hundreds, so there is no hundreds word at all.

Numbers can also sit on a number line. Between 1 000 and 2 000 the line is marked in hundreds: 1 100, 1 200 and so on. 1 580 sits a little past the 1 500 mark, 1 805 sits just past 1 800 and 1 850 sits halfway between 1 800 and 1 900. Placing numbers on a line is a picture of their order.

The questions for this section are with you now: ascending and descending order, writing numbers in words and expanded form, and placing them on a number line.

## Subtopic: Odd and Even to 1 000

An even number can be shared into two equal groups with nothing left over. An odd number always leaves one over. 8 counters make two groups of 4, so 8 is even. 9 counters make two groups of 4 with one left, so 9 is odd.

You never need to share out a big number to test it. Look only at the last digit. If the last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8, the number is even. If the last digit is 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9, the number is odd. 346 is even because it ends in 6. 1 085 is odd because it ends in 5. 1 000 is even because it ends in 0.

The house numbers in a street show the pattern. One side of the road has the even numbers 2, 4, 6, 8 and the other has the odd numbers 1, 3, 5, 7. Even and odd numbers alternate: every even number is followed by an odd one, and every odd number by an even one. Counting in 2s from an even number keeps you on the even side of the street.

Odd and even combine in a predictable way. Even plus even is even: 4 plus 6 is 10. Odd plus odd is even: 3 plus 5 is 8. Even plus odd is odd: 4 plus 3 is 7. This gives a fast check on addition: if you add two odd numbers and get an odd answer, something has gone wrong.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: comparing 1 850 and 999 digit by digit from the left and deciding 999 is bigger because 9 beats 1; count the digits first, and four digits always beat three. Two: writing 1 085 as one thousand eight hundred and five; the 0 means there are no hundreds, so it is one thousand and eighty-five. Three: testing odd or even by adding the digits; only the last digit matters. Four: thinking a number ending in 0 is neither odd nor even; 0 is an even ending, so 1 000 and 350 are even.

The questions for this section are with you now: the last-digit test for odd and even, house numbers, and what happens when odd and even numbers are added.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the three bicycles and the mountains — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Which Is Bigger

Count the digits first. More digits means a bigger number. 1 000 beats 999.

Same number of digits? Compare from the left. The bikes cost R1 850, R1 580 and R1 805. All start with 1. Hundreds: 8, 5, 8. The 5 loses, so R1 580 is cheapest. Then tens: 5 against 0. R1 850 wins.

The sign points its open side at the bigger number. 1 850 is greater than 1 805. 1 580 is less than 1 805.

Keep the bicycles in mind and try the questions for this part: comparing from the left and choosing the sign.

## Subtopic: Lining Them Up

Ascending means going up: smallest first. Descending means going down: biggest first. The bikes ascending: R1 580, R1 805, R1 850.

One number, three ways. Digits: 3 450. Words: three thousand four hundred and fifty. Expanded: 3 000 plus 400 plus 50.

Zeros need care. 1 085 is one thousand and eighty-five. No hundreds word, because there are no hundreds.

Hold the three ways of writing a number and try this part's questions: ordering, words and expanded form.

## Subtopic: Odd Side, Even Side

Even numbers share into two equal groups. Odd numbers leave one over. 8 is even, 9 is odd.

The quick test: look at the last digit only. 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 means even. 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 means odd. 346 is even. 1 085 is odd.

House numbers: even on one side of the street, odd on the other. Odd and even take turns as you count.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Compare numbers by counting digits, then by the leftmost digit, moving right only when digits match. The comparison sign opens towards the bigger number. Ascending goes up, descending goes down. A number can be written in digits, in words and in expanded form. Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8, odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9.

Hold the last-digit test and take the final questions of the lesson: odd and even numbers up to 1 000.
