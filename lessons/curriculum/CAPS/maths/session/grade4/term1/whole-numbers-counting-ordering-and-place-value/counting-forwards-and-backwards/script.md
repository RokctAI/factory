# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises Grade 3 counting and extends it to numbers up to at least 10 000. The anchor is a minibus taxi collecting fares of R25 at a rank in Durban, a stadium with rows of 50 seats, and a shop shelf stacked in packs of 3. The governing idea is that counting in steps means adding or subtracting the same number each time, and that the pattern in the last digits tells you the step. Counting forwards and backwards in 2s, 3s, 5s, 10s, 25s, 50s and 100s from any starting number, and completing a sequence with missing numbers, are examinable.

## Subtopic: Counting in Steps from Any Start

Counting in steps means adding the same number every time. Counting in 2s from 0 gives 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Counting in 5s gives 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25. In Grade 3 we mostly started at zero. In Grade 4 we start anywhere. Count in 2s from 347: 347, 349, 351, 353, 355. Count in 5s from 1 210: 1 210, 1 215, 1 220, 1 225, 1 230.

The taxi driver collects R25 from each passenger. One passenger: R25. Two: R50. Three: R75. Four: R100. Five: R125. Six: R150. Fifteen passengers: 15 times 25, which is 375, so R375. Counting in 25s follows the ending pattern 25, 50, 75, 00, 25, 50, 75, 00 and the hundreds digit grows by one every four steps.

Counting in 3s is the hardest of the set because the last digit does not repeat quickly: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30. After ten steps of 3 you have added 30, so the pattern of last digits repeats every ten numbers. From 2 004 in 3s: 2 004, 2 007, 2 010, 2 013, 2 016, 2 019.

Counting in 50s and 100s is the quickest. In 50s from 6 350: 6 350, 6 400, 6 450, 6 500, 6 550. In 100s from 9 400: 9 400, 9 500, 9 600, 9 700, 9 800, 9 900, 10 000. Only the hundreds digit changes until a thousand is filled, then the thousands digit moves up by one.

Counting in 10s from a number that does not end in zero keeps the units digit fixed: from 4 367 in 10s gives 4 367, 4 377, 4 387, 4 397, 4 407. Watch the tens digit: 6, 7, 8, 9 and then 0 with the hundreds digit climbing from 3 to 4.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting in 2s, 3s, 5s and 25s from a given start and reading the pattern of the last digits.

## Subtopic: Counting Backwards

Counting backwards means subtracting the same number each time. Count back in 100s from 3 250: 3 250, 3 150, 3 050, 2 950, 2 850. Notice the moment the hundreds digit reaches 0 and the next step drops the thousands digit from 3 to 2 and sets the hundreds digit to 9.

Count back in 50s from 1 000: 1 000, 950, 900, 850, 800, 750, 700. Count back in 25s from 500: 500, 475, 450, 425, 400, 375, 350. The endings run 00, 75, 50, 25, 00, 75, 50, 25 in reverse order.

The stadium has rows of 50 seats. If 2 000 people are seated and one full row empties at a time, the count goes 2 000, 1 950, 1 900, 1 850 and so on. After ten rows have emptied, 10 times 50 is 500 seats are empty, leaving 1 500 people.

Counting back in 2s from an odd number keeps you on odd numbers: 1 001, 999, 997, 995, 993. Counting back in 3s from 60: 60, 57, 54, 51, 48, 45. A useful check: counting forwards from the smallest number in your list should bring you back to where you started.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting back in 25s, 50s and 100s across a thousand boundary and checking a backwards count by counting forwards.

## Subtopic: Finding the Rule and the Missing Numbers

When a sequence is given with gaps, first find the step. Take any two neighbours and subtract the smaller from the larger. In 1 275, 1 300, 1 325, blank, 1 375 the step is 1 300 minus 1 275, which is 25, so the missing number is 1 325 plus 25, which is 1 350.

Check the step against another pair before trusting it. In 840, 843, 846, 849 each pair differs by 3, so the next is 852. If one pair gave 3 and another gave 5, the sequence is not a counting-in-steps sequence and you must look again.

The shop shelf holds packs of 3 stickers. Counting stickers across the shelf gives 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. If the shelf shows 3, 6, blank, 12, 15 the gap holds 9. If it shows blank, 6, 9, 12, the first number is 6 minus 3, which is 3.

The direction matters too. 7 400, 7 300, 7 200, blank, 7 000 goes down by 100 each time, so the gap holds 7 100. Always ask: is the sequence growing or shrinking, and by how much each time?

The error museum, four exhibits. One: counting in 25s as 25, 50, 75, 90; after 75 comes 100, because 75 plus 25 is 100. Two: writing 3 050 back 100 as 3 950; the thousands digit must drop to 2, giving 2 950. Three: finding the step from one pair of neighbours only; always check a second pair. Four: assuming every sequence counts up; read the direction before you fill a gap.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding the step from two neighbours, filling gaps, and deciding whether a sequence counts up or down.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the taxi, the stadium and the shop shelf — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Jumping in Steps

Counting in steps is like jumping along a number line. Every jump is the same size. Jump in 2s: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. Jump in 5s: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20. In Grade 4 you can start the jumps from any number, like 347, 349, 351.

The taxi fare is R25. Count the money: 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150. Say it like a song. The endings go 25, 50, 75, 00 and then start again.

Jumping in 100s is the easiest big jump. 9 400, 9 500, 9 600, 9 700, 9 800, 9 900, 10 000. Only one digit changes until you reach the next thousand.

Keep the number line in mind and try the questions for this part: jumping in 2s, 5s, 25s and 100s from any start.

## Subtopic: Going Backwards Too

Counting backwards is the same jump in the other direction. Back in 50s from 1 000: 1 000, 950, 900, 850, 800.

Watch the tricky spot where a thousand runs out. 3 050 back 100 is 2 950, not 3 950 and not 2 050. The thousands digit drops by one and the hundreds digit becomes 9.

Back in 2s from an odd number: 1 001, 999, 997, 995. All odd. Back in 3s from 60: 60, 57, 54, 51, 48. Count forwards again to check that you land back on 60.

Hold the backwards jumps in mind and try this part's questions: counting back in 2s, 3s, 50s and 100s.

## Subtopic: Fill the Gap

When a number is missing, find the jump first. Look at two neighbours and subtract. 1 275 and 1 300 differ by 25. So the gap after 1 325 holds 1 350.

Check your jump on another pair of neighbours. If both pairs give the same jump, you have found the rule.

Look at the direction. 7 400, 7 300, 7 200 is going down by 100, so the gap before 7 000 holds 7 100.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Counting in steps means adding or subtracting the same number each time. 2s, 3s, 5s, 10s, 25s, 50s and 100s each have their own ending pattern. You may start from any number up to 10 000. To fill a gap, find the step from two neighbours, check it on another pair, and read the direction.

Hold the jump-finding trick and take the final questions of the lesson: finding the step and filling gaps forwards and backwards.
