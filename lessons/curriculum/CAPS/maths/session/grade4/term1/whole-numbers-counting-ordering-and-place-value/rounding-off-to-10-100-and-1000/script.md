# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers rounding off whole numbers to the nearest 10, 100 and 1 000. The anchor is a match attendance of 3 468 at a stadium in Bloemfontein, a school trip costing R1 285 per learner and the 1 753 kilometre drive from Johannesburg to Plettenberg Bay. The governing idea is that to round to a given place you look at the digit immediately to its right: 0 to 4 rounds down and 5 to 9 rounds up. Rounding to the nearest 10, 100 and 1 000, choosing between two neighbouring multiples on a number line and using rounding to estimate are examinable.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 10

Rounding to the nearest 10 means choosing the multiple of 10 closest to the number. Every number sits between two multiples of 10. 3 468 sits between 3 460 and 3 470. Which is closer? 3 468 is 8 away from 3 460 and only 2 away from 3 470, so 3 468 rounds to 3 470.

You do not need to measure both gaps every time. Look at the units digit, the digit just to the right of the tens place. If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 the number is closer to the lower ten, so round down. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9 round up. 3 468 has units digit 8, so round up to 3 470. 1 285 has units digit 5, so round up to 1 290. 1 753 has units digit 3, so round down to 1 750.

Rounding down does not mean the number gets smaller by itself. It means the units digit is replaced with 0 and the tens digit stays the same. 1 753 to the nearest 10 is 1 750. Rounding up means the tens digit grows by one and the units become 0. 3 468 becomes 3 470.

When the tens digit is 9 and you round up, the hundreds digit changes too. 2 396 to the nearest 10 is 2 400, because 39 tens plus one more ten is 40 tens. The number line makes this clear: 2 396 sits between 2 390 and 2 400 and is closer to 2 400.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding the two neighbouring tens, using the units digit to decide, and rounding when a 9 rolls over.

## Subtopic: Rounding to the Nearest 100 and 1 000

The same rule works for hundreds, but now the deciding digit is the tens digit, because it is the digit just to the right of the hundreds place. 3 468 sits between 3 400 and 3 500. The tens digit is 6, so round up: 3 500. 1 285 has tens digit 8, so it rounds up to 1 300. 1 753 has tens digit 5, so it rounds up to 1 800.

To round to the nearest 1 000 the deciding digit is the hundreds digit. 3 468 sits between 3 000 and 4 000. The hundreds digit is 4, so round down: 3 000. 1 285 has hundreds digit 2, so it rounds down to 1 000. 1 753 has hundreds digit 7, so it rounds up to 2 000.

Notice that the same number rounds differently depending on the place. 3 468 rounds to 3 470, to 3 500 and to 3 000. None of these is wrong; each answers a different question. The question always says which place to round to, and you must read it.

A common slip is to round in stages. Rounding 3 468 to the nearest 10 gives 3 470, and then rounding 3 470 to the nearest 100 might tempt you to look at the 7 and say 3 500. Here it happens to agree, but with 1 249 it fails: to the nearest 100 it is 1 200, because the tens digit is 4, yet rounding first to 1 250 and then up would wrongly give 1 300. Always round the original number in one step.

The questions for this section are with you now: rounding to the nearest 100 using the tens digit, rounding to the nearest 1 000 using the hundreds digit, and rounding in one step.

## Subtopic: Rounding to Estimate

Rounding is most useful before a calculation. If 3 468 people attend one match and 2 795 attend the next, the exact total is 6 263, but a quick estimate rounds each to the nearest 100: 3 500 plus 2 800 is 6 300. The estimate is close enough to tell whether the exact answer is sensible.

The school trip costs R1 285 per learner and 4 learners from one family are going. Estimate: R1 300 times 4 is R5 200. The exact cost is R5 140. A parent can see straight away that about R5 000 is needed, and that an answer of R500 or R50 000 would be a mistake.

For a journey of 1 753 kilometres, a driver who rounds to the nearest 100 says about 1 800 kilometres and plans fuel and stops from that. Rounding to the nearest 1 000, about 2 000 kilometres, is good enough for a rough plan but too rough for fuel. The place you round to should match how exact you need to be.

Rounding also appears in everyday speech. A crowd of 3 468 is reported as about 3 500. A price of R1 285 is spoken of as about R1 300. Shops round the other way for effect: R1 285 is written as R1 285 rather than R1 300 because it looks cheaper. Knowing how rounding works lets you see through that.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: rounding 3 468 to the nearest 100 by looking at the units digit; the deciding digit is the tens digit, 6, so the answer is 3 500. Two: rounding in stages, 1 249 to 1 250 and then to 1 300; round the original number in one step to get 1 200. Three: thinking a 5 rounds down because it is in the middle; by agreement 5 rounds up, so 1 285 to the nearest 10 is 1 290. Four: giving the same answer whatever the question asks; 3 468 rounds to 3 470, 3 500 or 3 000 depending on the place named.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating a sum and a product by rounding first, and choosing a sensible place to round to.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the stadium crowd and the school trip — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Up or Down to the Nearest 10

Every number sits between two tens. 3 468 sits between 3 460 and 3 470. Pick the closer one. It is 3 470.

The shortcut: look at the units digit. 0, 1, 2, 3, 4: round down. 5, 6, 7, 8, 9: round up. 3 468 ends in 8. Up to 3 470. 1 753 ends in 3. Down to 1 750.

When a 9 rolls over, the next digit changes too. 2 396 rounds up to 2 400.

Keep the two neighbouring tens in mind and try the questions for this part: rounding to the nearest 10.

## Subtopic: Nearest 100 and Nearest 1 000

Same rule, different digit. For the nearest 100, look at the tens digit. 3 468 has tens digit 6, so round up to 3 500. 1 285 has tens digit 8, so round up to 1 300.

For the nearest 1 000, look at the hundreds digit. 3 468 has hundreds digit 4, so round down to 3 000. 1 753 has hundreds digit 7, so round up to 2 000.

One number, three answers: 3 468 is about 3 470, about 3 500 and about 3 000. Read the question to see which one it wants.

Hold the deciding digit in mind and try this part's questions: rounding to the nearest 100 and 1 000.

## Subtopic: Guess Before You Work It Out

Rounding lets you guess an answer before you work it out. 3 468 plus 2 795: round to 3 500 plus 2 800, about 6 300. If your exact answer is far from 6 300, check again.

The trip costs R1 285 each for 4 learners. About R1 300 times 4 is R5 200. The exact answer, R5 140, is close, so it makes sense.

Round to match what you need. For fuel on a 1 753 kilometre trip, about 1 800 is useful. About 2 000 is too rough.

Pocket summary of the lesson. To round to the nearest 10, 100 or 1 000, look at the digit just to the right of that place: 0 to 4 rounds down, 5 to 9 rounds up. Round the original number in one step. The same number gives different rounded values for different places. Rounding before calculating gives an estimate that checks whether the exact answer is sensible.

Hold the guess-first habit in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: estimating by rounding.
