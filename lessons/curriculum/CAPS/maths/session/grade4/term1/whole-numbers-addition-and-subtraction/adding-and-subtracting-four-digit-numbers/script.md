# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the addition and subtraction of whole numbers of at least four digits. The anchor is two classes at a school in Gqeberha raising R2 468 and R1 795 for a trip, a delivery van showing 5 324 kilometres at the start of the week and 7 162 at the end, and a concert hall with 4 000 seats of which 2 637 are sold. The governing idea is that digits of the same place value are added or subtracted together, and that ten of any place can be exchanged for one of the next place up, or one of the next place broken into ten of the place below. Column addition with carrying, column subtraction with exchanging and working across zeros are examinable.

## Subtopic: Column Addition with Carrying

Column addition lines up units under units, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds and thousands under thousands. Then each column is added on its own, starting from the units. The two classes raised R2 468 and R1 795. Units: 8 plus 5 is 13. That is 1 ten and 3 units, so write 3 and carry the 1 ten into the tens column.

Tens: 6 plus 9 is 15, plus the carried 1 makes 16 tens. Sixteen tens is 1 hundred and 6 tens, so write 6 and carry 1 into the hundreds. Hundreds: 4 plus 7 is 11, plus the carried 1 makes 12 hundreds. Write 2 and carry 1 into the thousands. Thousands: 2 plus 1 is 3, plus the carried 1 makes 4. The total is R4 263.

Carrying works because of place value. Ten units are worth exactly one ten, so moving a 1 from the units column to the tens column does not change the amount; it only writes it in a tidier way. Whenever a column total reaches 10 or more, the tens part of that total belongs in the next column.

Check with an estimate. R2 468 is about R2 500 and R1 795 is about R1 800, so the answer should be about R4 300. R4 263 is close, so the working is believable. Line-up mistakes show up quickly in the estimate: if the 1 795 is written one place to the left, the answer jumps to about R20 000 and the estimate rejects it.

The questions for this section are with you now: lining up the columns, adding from the units, carrying when a column passes 9, and checking with an estimate.

## Subtopic: Column Subtraction with Exchanging

The van showed 5 324 kilometres on Monday and 7 162 on Friday. How far did it travel? That is 7 162 minus 5 324. Line up the columns and start with the units: 2 minus 4 cannot be done with whole numbers, because 2 is smaller than 4. Exchange 1 ten from the tens column for 10 units. The 6 tens become 5 tens, and the units become 12. Now 12 minus 4 is 8.

Tens: 5 minus 2 is 3. Hundreds: 1 minus 3 cannot be done, so exchange 1 thousand for 10 hundreds. The 7 thousands become 6 and the hundreds become 11. Now 11 minus 3 is 8. Thousands: 6 minus 5 is 1. The van travelled 1 838 kilometres.

Exchanging is the opposite of carrying. Carrying bundles ten of a place into one of the next place up; exchanging breaks one of a place into ten of the place below. Neither changes the value of the number. 7 162 and 6 thousands, 11 hundreds, 5 tens and 12 units are the same amount written differently.

Check subtraction by adding back. The answer plus the number you took away should give the number you started with: 1 838 plus 5 324. Units 8 plus 4 is 12, write 2 carry 1. Tens 3 plus 2 plus 1 is 6. Hundreds 8 plus 3 is 11, write 1 carry 1. Thousands 1 plus 5 plus 1 is 7. That gives 7 162, so the subtraction is correct.

The questions for this section are with you now: exchanging when a digit is too small, working from the units to the thousands, and checking by adding back.

## Subtopic: Subtracting Across Zeros

The concert hall has 4 000 seats and 2 637 are sold. How many are still empty? 4 000 minus 2 637. The units column is 0 minus 7, which needs an exchange, but the tens column is also 0 and so is the hundreds column. There is nothing to exchange from until the thousands.

Do the exchange in one sweep. Break 1 thousand into 10 hundreds, then 1 of those hundreds into 10 tens, then 1 of those tens into 10 units. The number 4 000 becomes 3 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 units. Now subtract: 10 minus 7 is 3, 9 minus 3 is 6, 9 minus 6 is 3, 3 minus 2 is 1. There are 1 363 empty seats.

A faster route with round numbers is to subtract 1 first. 3 999 minus 2 637 needs no exchanging at all: 9 minus 7 is 2, 9 minus 3 is 6, 9 minus 6 is 3, 3 minus 2 is 1, giving 1 362. Then add the 1 back: 1 363. Same answer, no exchanges.

Check by adding back: 1 363 plus 2 637. Units 3 plus 7 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Tens 6 plus 3 plus 1 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Hundreds 3 plus 6 plus 1 is 10, write 0 carry 1. Thousands 1 plus 2 plus 1 is 4. That gives 4 000, so 1 363 is correct. Estimate as well: 4 000 minus about 2 600 is about 1 400, which agrees.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding from the thousands column; start from the units so that carries move in the right direction. Two: writing 13 in the units column instead of writing 3 and carrying 1; each column holds one digit. Three: swapping the digits when the top one is too small, doing 4 minus 2 instead of exchanging; exchange a ten and do 12 minus 4. Four: forgetting to reduce the column you exchanged from; 6 tens become 5 tens when one ten is broken into units.

The questions for this section are with you now: exchanging through several zeros, the subtract-one shortcut, and checking by adding back.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the fund-raising classes, the van and the concert seats — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Add in Columns

Line up the digits: units under units, tens under tens, and so on. Start on the right, with the units.

R2 468 plus R1 795. Units: 8 plus 5 is 13. Write 3, carry 1. Tens: 6 plus 9 plus 1 is 16. Write 6, carry 1. Hundreds: 4 plus 7 plus 1 is 12. Write 2, carry 1. Thousands: 2 plus 1 plus 1 is 4. Total R4 263.

Carrying is just moving ten of one column into one of the next. Ten units make a ten. Nothing is lost.

Keep the two classes in mind and try the questions for this part: adding in columns with carrying.

## Subtopic: Take Away in Columns

Subtracting works the same way, from the units. 7 162 minus 5 324. Units: 2 minus 4 does not work. Borrow a ten: the 6 tens become 5, the units become 12. 12 minus 4 is 8.

Tens: 5 minus 2 is 3. Hundreds: 1 minus 3 does not work. Exchange a thousand: 7 becomes 6, hundreds become 11. 11 minus 3 is 8. Thousands: 6 minus 5 is 1. Answer 1 838.

Check by adding back: 1 838 plus 5 324 should give 7 162. It does.

Hold the van in mind and try this part's questions: exchanging and checking.

## Subtopic: When There Are Zeros

4 000 minus 2 637. The units, tens and hundreds are all zero, so the exchange comes from the thousands. 4 000 becomes 3 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 units.

Now subtract: 10 minus 7 is 3. 9 minus 3 is 6. 9 minus 6 is 3. 3 minus 2 is 1. Answer 1 363.

Shortcut: take 1 off first. 3 999 minus 2 637 is 1 362 with no exchanging. Add the 1 back: 1 363.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Line up digits by place value and work from the units. In addition, a column total of 10 or more is split: write the units digit and carry the ten into the next column. In subtraction, a digit too small to subtract from needs an exchange from the column to its left, and zeros are exchanged through from the first non-zero column. Check addition with an estimate and subtraction by adding back.

Hold the three nines in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: subtracting across zeros.
