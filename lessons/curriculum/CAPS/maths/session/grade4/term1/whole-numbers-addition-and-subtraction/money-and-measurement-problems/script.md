# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems involving whole numbers in financial and measurement contexts using addition and subtraction. The anchor is a tuck shop in Kimberley that takes R2 375 on Monday and R1 860 on Tuesday, a marathon of 42 kilometres with a water point at kilometre 27, and a 5 000 litre tank holding 3 245 litres. The governing idea is the four-step routine: read and picture the problem, write the number sentence, calculate with two techniques, and check that the answer fits the context and its units. Reading for the operation, keeping units such as rands, kilometres and litres through the working, and interpreting the answer are examinable.

## Subtopic: Money Problems

Money problems in Grade 4 use whole rands. The tuck shop took R2 375 on Monday and R1 860 on Tuesday. How much altogether? The word altogether signals addition: 2 375 plus 1 860 equals box. Estimate first: about 2 400 plus 1 900 is 4 300. Calculate: 2 375 plus 1 860 is 4 235. The answer, R4 235, is close to the estimate.

How much more did the shop take on Monday than Tuesday? The words how much more signal a difference: 2 375 minus 1 860 equals box. Build up from 1 860: plus 40 is 1 900, plus 475 is 2 375. The jumps total 515. Monday took R515 more. Check: 1 860 plus 515 is 2 375.

Change problems are subtraction with the amount paid first. A learner buys a pie for R18 and a juice for R14 and pays with a R50 note. Total spent is 18 plus 14, which is 32. Change is 50 minus 32, which is 18. Written as one sentence: 50 minus 18 plus 14 in brackets equals 18. Count up to check: 32 plus 18 is 50.

Always write the rand sign in the final answer, and read the question again to see what it actually asked. A question asking for the change is not answered by the total spent, even if the total was correctly calculated. Many marks are lost by stopping one step early.

The questions for this section are with you now: totals and differences in rands, change from a note, and finishing the step the question actually asked for.

## Subtopic: Distance and Length Problems

Measurement problems use units such as kilometres, metres and centimetres. A marathon is 42 kilometres and a water point stands at kilometre 27. How far from the water point to the finish? That is 42 minus 27 equals box. Build up: 27 plus 3 is 30, plus 12 is 42, so 15. The water point is 15 kilometres from the finish.

Keep units consistent. A fence needs 1 250 metres of wire. The farmer has used 865 metres. How much more does she need? 1 250 minus 865 equals box. Round 865 to 900: 1 250 minus 900 is 350, then add back the 35 taken too much: 385. She needs 385 metres. Both numbers were in metres, so the answer is in metres.

Two-step distance problems chain additions and subtractions. A learner walks 1 450 metres to school, 600 metres to the shop after school and 1 875 metres home. How far altogether? 1 450 plus 600 plus 1 875. Pair 1 450 with 600 to get 2 050, then plus 1 875 is 3 925 metres. Estimate: 1 500 plus 600 plus 1 900 is 4 000, close.

If the units are different, convert before calculating, which you will practise fully in Term 4. For now, notice when one number is in metres and another in kilometres and do not add them as they stand. 2 kilometres plus 350 metres is not 352 of anything until you change the kilometres into 2 000 metres, when it becomes 2 350 metres.

The questions for this section are with you now: distances left to travel, lengths still needed, chaining three distances and keeping units consistent.

## Subtopic: Capacity, Mass and the Four-Step Routine

Capacity and mass problems follow the same routine. A tank holds 5 000 litres when full and contains 3 245 litres. How much more water fills it? 5 000 minus 3 245 equals box. Subtract 1 first: 4 999 minus 3 245 is 1 754, then add 1 back: 1 755 litres. Check: 3 245 plus 1 755 is 5 000.

A bakery uses 1 275 grams of flour for one batch and 2 850 grams for another. How much flour altogether? 1 275 plus 2 850. Break down: 1 000 plus 2 000 is 3 000, 275 plus 850 is 1 125, so 4 125 grams. Estimate: 1 300 plus 2 900 is 4 200, close.

The four-step routine is the same for every context. Step one, read and picture: what is happening and what is being asked? Step two, write the number sentence with a box for the unknown. Step three, calculate with one technique and check with another. Step four, write the answer with its unit and read the question once more to make sure that is what was asked.

Step four catches answers that cannot be right in the story. A tank cannot hold more water than its capacity, so if your answer to how much more fills it is bigger than 5 000 litres, the sentence was wrong. Change cannot be more than the amount paid. A distance left to travel cannot be more than the whole route. The story is the final check.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: answering the total spent when the question asked for the change; read the question again before writing the answer. Two: adding 2 kilometres and 350 metres as if they were the same unit; convert to 2 000 metres first. Three: leaving the unit off the answer; 385 on its own is not an answer, 385 metres is. Four: accepting an answer the story makes impossible, such as change bigger than the money paid; the story is the final check.

The questions for this section are with you now: litres and grams problems, the four-step routine, and using the story to check that an answer is possible.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the tuck shop, the marathon and the water tank — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Money Stories

Read the story and find the sign. Tuck shop: R2 375 on Monday and R1 860 on Tuesday. Altogether means add: R4 235. How much more on Monday means take away: R515.

Change is always the money paid minus the money spent. Pie R18 plus juice R14 is R32. R50 minus R32 is R18 change.

Read the question twice. If it asks for change, give the change, not the total.

Keep the tuck shop in mind and try the questions for this part: totals, differences and change.

## Subtopic: Distance Stories

The marathon is 42 kilometres and the water point is at 27. Left to run: 42 minus 27 is 15 kilometres.

The fence needs 1 250 metres and 865 are used. Still needed: 1 250 minus 865. Take away 900 to get 350, give back 35 to get 385 metres.

Keep the units the same. Metres with metres, kilometres with kilometres. Write the unit in your answer.

Hold the marathon in mind and try this part's questions: distance and length problems.

## Subtopic: Tanks, Flour and the Final Check

The tank holds 5 000 litres and has 3 245. To fill it: 5 000 minus 3 245. Do 4 999 minus 3 245, which is 1 754, then add 1: 1 755 litres.

Flour: 1 275 grams plus 2 850 grams is 4 125 grams.

Four steps every time: read and picture, write the sentence, calculate and check, write the answer with its unit. Then ask: could this answer really happen in the story?

Pocket summary of the lesson. Money and measurement problems use the four-step routine: read and picture, write the number sentence, calculate with two techniques, and write the answer with its unit. Altogether means add, how much more and left mean subtract, and change is the amount paid minus the amount spent. Keep units the same, and check that the answer is possible in the story.

Hold the four steps in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: capacity, mass and checking against the story.
