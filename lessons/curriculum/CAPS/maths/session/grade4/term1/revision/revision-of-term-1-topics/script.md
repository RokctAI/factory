# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 1 topics: whole numbers to 10 000 including counting, ordering, place value and rounding; number sentences and the properties of operations; addition and subtraction of four-digit numbers with techniques for checking; multiplication of two-digit numbers with techniques for checking; and multiples and factors. The anchor is a school fete in Mahikeng with 2 468 tickets sold on Saturday and 1 795 on Sunday, stalls arranged in rows, and a raffle with prizes shared out. The governing idea is that each topic supplies a tool, and that estimation and inverse operations check every tool. The whole of Term 1 is examinable in the Term 1 test.

## Subtopic: Numbers: Counting, Place Value and Rounding

Counting in steps: the fete sells tickets in books of 25. Twelve books is 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175, 200, 225, 250, 275, 300, so 300 tickets. Counting backwards from 3 050 in 100s crosses a thousand at 2 950. To fill a gap in a sequence, find the step from two neighbours and check it on a second pair.

Place value: 2 468 tickets is 2 thousands, 4 hundreds, 6 tens and 8 units, expanded as 2 000 plus 400 plus 60 plus 8. The 4 has a face value of 4 and a place value of 400. Comparing 2 468 and 1 795 starts from the left: 2 thousands beats 1 thousand, so Saturday sold more. Ascending order puts the smaller first.

Odd and even: look only at the last digit. 2 468 is even, 1 795 is odd. Rounding: look at the digit to the right of the place you want. 2 468 to the nearest 10 is 2 470, to the nearest 100 is 2 500, to the nearest 1 000 is 2 000. 1 795 to the nearest 100 is 1 800.

These number skills are the ones that make every calculation checkable. An estimate is a rounding; a column method is place value; a sequence is counting in steps. If any of these feels shaky, revise it first, because the later topics stand on it.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting in steps, place value and comparing, odd and even, and rounding.

## Subtopic: Sentences, Properties, Addition and Subtraction

Number sentences: tickets sold on both days altogether is 2 468 plus 1 795 equals box. The box goes where the story has its gap. Solve by inspection when you know the fact, by trial and improvement when you do not, or by the inverse operation, and prove the answer by substitution. Box plus 1 795 equals 4 263 is solved by 4 263 minus 1 795, which is 2 468.

Properties: swap the order for addition and multiplication, never for subtraction or division. Group to make tens: 17 plus 25 plus 3 plus 5 is 20 plus 30, which is 50. Distribute to multiply: 5 times 23 is 5 times 20 plus 5 times 3, which is 115. Adding zero changes nothing; multiplying by zero gives zero.

Addition: 2 468 plus 1 795 in columns from the units, carrying when a column passes 9: 8 plus 5 is 13, write 3 carry 1; 6 plus 9 plus 1 is 16, write 6 carry 1; 4 plus 7 plus 1 is 12, write 2 carry 1; 2 plus 1 plus 1 is 4. Total 4 263 tickets. Estimate 2 500 plus 1 800 is 4 300, close.

Subtraction: how many more on Saturday? 2 468 minus 1 795. Build up from 1 795: plus 5 is 1 800, plus 668 is 2 468, so 673. Or compensate: 2 468 minus 1 800 is 668, add back 5, which is 673. Check by adding: 1 795 plus 673 is 2 468. Across zeros, subtract 1 first: 4 000 minus 2 637 is 3 999 minus 2 637 plus 1, which is 1 363.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing and solving number sentences, using the properties, and adding and subtracting four-digit numbers with checks.

## Subtopic: Multiplication, Multiples and Factors

Tables to 10 are the foundation: 7 times 8 is 56, 6 times 7 is 42, 9 times 7 is 63. Two-digit by one-digit: 24 stalls each paying R6 is 24 times 6, which is 120 plus 24, which is 144, so R144. Two-digit by two-digit uses the grid: 23 times 14 has four products, 200, 30, 80 and 12, totalling 322.

Techniques: estimate first, break down, round and compensate, double and halve. 16 times 25 becomes 8 times 50, which is 400. 48 times 5 is 50 times 5 minus 10, which is 240. Check by division: 240 divided by 5 is 48. Use two techniques on every multiplication.

Multiples and factors: the multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and so on; the factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24, found in pairs. 24 raffle prizes can be set out in 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 24 equal rows. 24 and 36 have highest common factor 12, and 6 and 8 have lowest common multiple 24.

Ratio and rate: 2 cups of flour to 3 vetkoek scales to 10 to 15 by multiplying both parts by 5. A rate is an amount per one unit, like R35 per box, and 24 boxes cost 24 times 35, which is 840. Each, every and per all signal multiplication.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: rounding to the nearest 100 by looking at the units digit; look at the tens digit. Two: swapping the numbers in a subtraction; only addition and multiplication are commutative. Three: finding two products instead of four in a two-digit by two-digit grid; every part multiplies every part. Four: skipping the estimate and the inverse check; two techniques on every calculation.

The questions for this section are with you now: tables, two-digit multiplication and the grid, techniques and checks, multiples, factors, ratio and rate.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again, walking through the fete from the gate to the raffle — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: At the Gate: Numbers

Tickets come in books of 25. Twelve books: 25, 50, 75, 100 and on to 300. Counting in steps.

2 468 tickets on Saturday. The 4 is worth 400. Compare with Sunday's 1 795 from the left: 2 beats 1, so Saturday was bigger. 2 468 is even, 1 795 is odd. Look at the last digit.

Round 2 468: nearest 10 is 2 470, nearest 100 is 2 500, nearest 1 000 is 2 000. Look one digit to the right.

Keep the ticket gate in mind and try the questions for this part: counting, place value, odd and even, rounding.

## Subtopic: At the Till: Adding and Taking Away

Both days together: 2 468 plus 1 795. Columns from the units, carry when you pass 9. 4 263 tickets. Guess first: 2 500 plus 1 800 is 4 300. Close.

How many more on Saturday? 2 468 minus 1 795. Take away 1 800 to get 668, give back 5: 673. Check: 1 795 plus 673 is 2 468.

Write the sentence first, box where the gap is, then solve and put the answer back in.

Hold the till in mind and try this part's questions: adding, subtracting and checking.

## Subtopic: At the Stalls: Multiplying

24 stalls at R6 each: 24 times 6. Split 24: 120 plus 24 is 144. R144.

Two two-digit numbers? Grid with four boxes. 23 times 14: 200, 30, 80, 12. 322. Or make it friendly: 16 times 25 is 8 times 50, which is 400.

Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24. Ratio scales both parts; rate is per one, so multiply.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Term 1 gave you number skills, counting, place value, comparing and rounding; number sentences with a box and three ways to solve them; the commutative, associative and distributive properties and zero; column addition and subtraction with carrying, exchanging and checks; multiplication by breaking down, the grid, doubling and halving and division checks; and multiples, factors, ratio and rate. Estimate first and check with the inverse, every time.

Hold the stalls in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: multiplication, factors and multiples.
