### subtopic_1 — Money and Measurement Problems
**Assistant:** Why does a ratio need both numbers multiplied? Can't I just add more flour?

**Tutor:** You can add more flour, but then you will have a different recipe, and the vetkoek will not come out the same. A ratio describes a relationship, not two separate quantities. Two cups of flour for three vetkoek means that flour and vetkoek grow together: every time you add three more vetkoek to the plan, you need two more cups of flour. If you doubled only the flour, to 4 cups for 3 vetkoek, each vetkoek would have twice as much flour as before, and the dough would be stiff and dry. If you doubled only the vetkoek, to 2 cups for 6, each would have half the flour and would fall apart. Doubling both, to 4 cups for 6 vetkoek, keeps every single vetkoek exactly as it was and simply makes twice as many. That is what scaling means, and it is why both parts move together by the same multiplier. The same idea protects you in mixing squash, mixing cement, mixing paint colours and sharing out money fairly. A ratio is a recipe for how two things relate, and a recipe only stays the same recipe when every ingredient is scaled by the same amount. Adding a fixed amount to both parts, say one more cup and one more vetkoek, also breaks the ratio: 3 to 4 is not the same relationship as 2 to 3, as you can check by scaling 2 to 3 up to 6 to 9 and 3 to 4 up to 6 to 8.

### subtopic_2 — Ratio: Comparing the Same Kind
**Assistant:** Is R35 per box a rate or just a price? It doesn't feel like speed.

**Tutor:** It is a rate, and the reason it does not feel like one is that we meet prices so often that we stop noticing their structure. A rate is any amount of one kind given for each single unit of another kind. Speed is distance for each hour. A price is rands for each box, or for each kilogram, or for each litre of petrol. A wage is rands for each hour worked. A tap's flow is litres for each minute. In every case the word per, or the word each, marks the rate, and in every case you use it the same way: multiply by how many units you have. 60 kilometres per hour for 3 hours is 180 kilometres. R35 per box for 24 boxes is R840. R15 per car for 14 cars is R210. Seeing prices as rates is useful for comparing. If one shop sells chips at R35 per box and another at R410 for 12 boxes, you can find the second shop's rate by dividing, 410 divided by 12 is a little over 34, so the second shop is slightly cheaper per box. Supermarkets print the price per kilogram on the shelf label for exactly this reason. So a price is a rate, speed is a rate, and anything you can sensibly say per about is a rate. What makes it different from a ratio is that the two amounts are different kinds of thing: rands and boxes, not flour and flour.

### subtopic_3 — Rate: Comparing Different Kinds
**Assistant:** The chips problem had a two-digit times two-digit sum. What if I cannot do that yet?

**Tutor:** Then use a technique that gets you there in steps you can do, because every correct route earns the mark. 24 times 35 can be built up from pieces you already know. Start with 24 times 10, which is 240. Three of those make 24 times 30, which is 720. Then 24 times 5 is half of 24 times 10, which is 120. Add 720 and 120 to get 840. Or go the other way: 35 times 20 is 700, and 35 times 4 is 140, giving 840 again. Or use doubling and halving: 24 times 35 is 12 times 70, which is 840, because halving 24 and doubling 35 keeps the product the same, and 12 times 70 is 12 times 7 with a zero, which is 840. The grid method from the earlier lesson is the safe fallback when none of these occurs to you, and it is worth being able to draw one quickly. What you should not do is give up or guess, because the arithmetic is only one part of the problem; the mark for recognising that this is multiplication, for writing 24 times 35 equals box and for estimating about 850 is yours whether or not the final digits are perfect. If you run out of time, write the sentence and the estimate and move on. Then practise the two-digit multiplication at home, because by Term 2 you will meet it in division as well.
