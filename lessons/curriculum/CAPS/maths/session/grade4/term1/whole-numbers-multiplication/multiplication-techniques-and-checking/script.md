# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the techniques used to perform and check multiplication: estimation, building up and breaking down numbers, doubling and halving, and multiplication and division as inverse operations. The anchor is a bakery in Kroonstad making 16 trays of 25 rolls, a school that needs 48 chairs for each of 5 classrooms, and a bus company carrying 15 learners on each of 12 trips. The governing idea is that a multiplication can be reshaped into easier steps without changing the product, and that division undoes multiplication, so every product can be checked. Choosing and showing two techniques, and checking a product by division, are examinable.

## Subtopic: Estimate and Break Down

Estimate first. 48 times 5: 48 is about 50 and 50 times 5 is 250, so the answer should be a little under 250. Now break down: 40 times 5 is 200 and 8 times 5 is 40, so 240. The school needs 240 chairs. 240 is just under 250, as the estimate predicted.

Breaking down into tens and units is the method from the last lesson. 15 times 12: 15 is 10 plus 5, so 10 times 12 is 120 and 5 times 12 is 60, total 180. The bus carries 180 learners over the 12 trips. Alternatively break the 12: 15 times 10 is 150 and 15 times 2 is 30, total 180. Either number can be broken.

Building up uses a known product to reach a nearby one. If you know 25 times 10 is 250, then 25 times 16 is 250 plus 25 times 6, which is 250 plus 150, which is 400. The bakery makes 400 rolls. Or build from 25 times 4 is 100: 16 is four 4s, so 25 times 16 is four 100s, which is 400.

Breaking down can also use subtraction. 48 times 5 is 50 times 5 minus 2 times 5, which is 250 minus 10, which is 240. This is rounding and compensating applied to multiplication, and it is often faster than tens and units when the number is just below a round number.

The questions for this section are with you now: estimating a product, breaking down either number, building up from a known product, and compensating from a round number.

## Subtopic: Doubling and Halving

Doubling and halving is a technique that keeps a product the same while making the numbers friendlier. If you double one number and halve the other, the answer does not change. 16 times 25: halve the 16 to 8 and double the 25 to 50, giving 8 times 50, which is 400. Halve and double again: 4 times 100, which is 400. The rolls are counted three ways and the answer is always 400.

Why does it work? Picture 16 trays of 25 rolls. Push every two trays together to make 8 big trays of 50 rolls. The number of rolls has not changed; only the grouping has. Halving the number of groups while doubling the size of each group leaves the total untouched.

Doubling alone builds the 2, 4 and 8 times tables and beyond. 15 times 8: 15 doubled is 30, doubled is 60, doubled is 120. Three doublings is times 8. Halving alone gives times 5 from times 10: 48 times 10 is 480, so 48 times 5 is half of 480, which is 240. The chairs again, by a different road.

Doubling and halving works best when one number is even and the other is close to 25 or 50, because doubling 25 or 50 heads for 100. 12 times 15 becomes 6 times 30, which is 180. 14 times 5 becomes 7 times 10, which is 70. When neither number is even, use breaking down instead.

The questions for this section are with you now: halving one number and doubling the other, repeated doubling for times 8, and halving a times-10 answer for times 5.

## Subtopic: Checking with Division

Multiplication and division undo each other. If 48 times 5 is 240, then 240 divided by 5 must be 48, and 240 divided by 48 must be 5. Dividing the answer by one of the numbers should give the other number. 240 divided by 5: 200 divided by 5 is 40, and 40 divided by 5 is 8, so 48. The check passes.

Checking 15 times 12 equals 180: 180 divided by 12. Twelve times 10 is 120, leaving 60, and twelve times 5 is 60, so 180 divided by 12 is 15. The product is confirmed. Checking by division is strongest when the division is easy, so choose the easier of the two numbers to divide by.

Use two different techniques on every multiplication. If you found 16 times 25 by breaking down, check by doubling and halving, or by dividing 400 by 16 to get 25. If the two agree, the answer is trusted. If they disagree, estimate to see which is sensible, then redo the calculation slowly.

Estimation is itself a check, and it should come first and last. Before calculating, say about how big the answer will be. After calculating, compare. A product of 240 for 48 times 5 fits an estimate of about 250; a product of 2 400 or 24 does not, and the mistake is found before any division is needed.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: doubling both numbers in doubling and halving; one is doubled and the other halved, or the product changes. Two: halving an odd number and getting stuck; choose to halve the even number, or use breaking down instead. Three: checking a product by multiplying again the same way; use division or a different technique. Four: accepting 2 400 for 48 times 5 because the digits look right; the estimate of about 250 rejects it.

The questions for this section are with you now: dividing a product to check it, choosing which number to divide by, and using two techniques on every multiplication.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the bakery, the chairs and the bus — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Guess, Then Split

Guess first. 48 times 5: 48 is nearly 50, and 50 times 5 is 250. So a bit under 250.

Split it. 40 times 5 is 200. 8 times 5 is 40. Together 240 chairs.

Or round and fix. 50 times 5 is 250, take off the extra 2 times 5, which is 10. 240.

Keep the chairs in mind and try the questions for this part: estimating and breaking down.

## Subtopic: Halve One, Double the Other

16 trays of 25 rolls. Push pairs of trays together: 8 trays of 50. Same rolls. 8 times 50 is 400.

Halve one number, double the other, and the answer stays the same. 12 times 15 becomes 6 times 30, which is 180.

Times 8 is doubling three times. 15 doubled is 30, doubled is 60, doubled is 120.

Hold the trays in mind and try this part's questions: doubling and halving.

## Subtopic: Divide to Check

Times and divide undo each other. 48 times 5 is 240, so 240 divided by 5 must be 48. It is. Checked.

15 times 12 is 180. Check: 180 divided by 12. Twelve tens are 120, twelve fives are 60, so 15. Checked.

Two techniques on every sum: one to find, one to check. If they disagree, estimate and redo.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Estimate before multiplying. Break down either number into tens and units, build up from a known product, or round and compensate. Halving one number while doubling the other keeps the product the same and heads for friendly numbers like 50 and 100. Division undoes multiplication, so divide the product by one number to get the other. Use two different techniques on every calculation.

Hold the undo idea in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: checking multiplication with division.
