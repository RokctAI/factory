# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems involving whole numbers in financial and measurement contexts, ratio of amounts of the same kind, and rate between amounts of different kinds, all using multiplication. The anchor is a tuck shop in Thohoyandou ordering 24 boxes of chips at R35 a box, a vetkoek recipe that uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 vetkoek, and a taxi that travels 60 kilometres in one hour. The governing idea is that multiplication scales an amount up in equal steps, that a ratio states how two amounts of the same kind compare and stays fixed when both are scaled, and that a rate is an amount per one unit of something else. Writing a number sentence for a multiplication problem, scaling a ratio and using a rate to find an amount are examinable.

## Subtopic: Money and Measurement Problems

The tuck shop orders 24 boxes of chips at R35 a box. How much does the order cost? Equal groups of R35, 24 of them: 24 times 35 equals box. Estimate: 25 times 35 is about 875, or 24 times 40 is 960, so somewhere around 850 to 950. Calculate with the grid: 20 times 30 is 600, 20 times 5 is 100, 4 times 30 is 120, 4 times 5 is 20, total 840. The order costs R840.

Measurement problems work the same way. A roll of ribbon is 75 centimetres long and a teacher needs 8 rolls. 8 times 75: 8 times 70 is 560 and 8 times 5 is 40, so 600 centimetres, which is 6 metres. A bag of mealie meal is 12 kilograms and a shop stocks 45 bags: 45 times 12 is 450 plus 90, which is 540 kilograms.

Read for the multiplication signal words: each, every, per, rows of, boxes of, times as many. Then write the sentence before calculating, as in the number sentences topic. If a learner earns R15 for each car washed and washes 14 cars, the sentence is 14 times 15 equals box, and the answer is 210, so R210.

Finish with the unit and the story check. R840 for 24 boxes is about R35 a box, which is what we started with, so it fits. 600 centimetres of ribbon from 8 rolls means each roll is 75 centimetres, which fits. If the ribbon total came out as 60 centimetres, less than one roll, the story would reject it.

The questions for this section are with you now: spotting the multiplication in a money or measurement story, estimating, calculating, and checking against the story.

## Subtopic: Ratio: Comparing the Same Kind

A ratio compares two amounts of the same kind. The vetkoek recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 vetkoek. The ratio of cups of flour to vetkoek is 2 to 3, written 2 is to 3. Both amounts are counts of things in the recipe, and the ratio tells you how they go together, not how many of each there are in total.

A ratio stays the same when both numbers are multiplied by the same amount. For 6 vetkoek, double both: 4 cups for 6 vetkoek. For 15 vetkoek, multiply both by 5: 10 cups for 15 vetkoek. The ratio 2 to 3, 4 to 6 and 10 to 15 all describe the same recipe. Scaling a ratio is just multiplying each part by the same number.

Ratios appear in mixing and sharing. Orange squash is mixed 1 part concentrate to 4 parts water. For 2 cups of concentrate you need 8 cups of water. In a class the ratio of boys to girls is 3 to 2; if there are 12 boys, the 3 has been multiplied by 4, so the girls number 2 times 4, which is 8, and the class has 20 learners.

The order of a ratio matters. Flour to vetkoek is 2 to 3; vetkoek to flour is 3 to 2. Always say which amount comes first. And a ratio is not a fraction of the whole: 3 to 2 boys to girls means 3 boys for every 2 girls, so boys are 3 out of every 5 learners, not 3 out of every 2.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading a ratio of the same kind, scaling both parts by the same number, and keeping the order right.

## Subtopic: Rate: Comparing Different Kinds

A rate compares two different kinds of amount, and it is usually given per one unit of the second kind. The taxi travels 60 kilometres in one hour: its rate is 60 kilometres per hour. Chips cost R35 per box. A learner earns R15 per car washed. The word per means for each one.

To use a rate, multiply by how many units you have. The taxi at 60 kilometres per hour for 3 hours travels 3 times 60, which is 180 kilometres. For 5 hours, 300 kilometres. The chips at R35 per box for 24 boxes cost 24 times 35, which is R840, the problem we started with. Every price per item is a rate, and every total cost is a rate multiplied.

Rates can be scaled like ratios, but the two amounts are different kinds, so the answer always carries two units. If a tap fills 12 litres per minute, in 8 minutes it fills 96 litres. If a heart beats 70 times per minute, in 10 minutes it beats 700 times. Keep both units in view: litres and minutes, beats and minutes.

The difference between ratio and rate is the kind of amount. Flour to vetkoek compares two counts of ingredients and outputs in a recipe, both of the same kind, so it is a ratio. Kilometres to hours compares distance with time, two different kinds, so it is a rate. A quick test: if you can sensibly say per, as in rands per box or litres per minute, it is a rate.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: adding when the story says each; 24 boxes at R35 each is 24 times 35, not 24 plus 35. Two: scaling only one part of a ratio; 2 to 3 becomes 10 to 15, not 10 to 3. Three: reading 3 to 2 boys to girls as 3 out of 2; it means 3 boys for every 2 girls, so 3 out of every 5 learners. Four: leaving the second unit off a rate; 180 is not an answer, 180 kilometres in 3 hours is.

The questions for this section are with you now: reading a rate with per, multiplying a rate by a number of units, and telling ratio from rate.

## Subtopic: Equal Groups of Money and Measure

24 boxes of chips at R35 each. Equal groups, so multiply: 24 times 35. Grid: 600, 100, 120, 20. Total R840.

8 rolls of ribbon, 75 centimetres each: 8 times 75 is 600 centimetres, which is 6 metres.

Check against the story. R840 for 24 boxes is about R35 a box. Fits.

## Subtopic: Two of the Same Kind

A ratio compares two amounts of the same kind. 2 cups of flour for every 3 vetkoek: 2 to 3.

Want more vetkoek? Multiply both numbers by the same amount. Times 5: 10 cups for 15 vetkoek. Still the same recipe.

Order matters. Flour to vetkoek is 2 to 3. Vetkoek to flour is 3 to 2.

Hold the recipe in mind and try this part's questions: ratios and scaling them.

## Subtopic: Per Means For Each One

A rate compares two different kinds. 60 kilometres per hour. R35 per box. Per means for each one.

To use a rate, multiply. 60 kilometres per hour for 3 hours: 3 times 60 is 180 kilometres. 12 litres per minute for 8 minutes: 96 litres.

Ratio or rate? If you can say per, it is a rate. Rands per box, litres per minute. Flour to vetkoek has no per, so it is a ratio.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Multiplication solves problems with equal groups of money or measurement: write the sentence, estimate, calculate and check against the story. A ratio compares two amounts of the same kind and is scaled by multiplying both parts by the same number, with the order kept. A rate compares two different kinds, is stated per one unit, and is used by multiplying by the number of units.

Hold the taxi in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: rates and the word per.
