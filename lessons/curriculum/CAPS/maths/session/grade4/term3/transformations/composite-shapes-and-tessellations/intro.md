Today we put shapes together. When we join 2D shapes, we make new shapes called composite shapes, and some of them have lines of symmetry. When we repeat shapes to cover a whole surface with no gaps and no overlaps, we make a tessellation, like the tiles on a floor or the paving in a driveway. We use pattern blocks, a tiled kitchen floor and a paved driveway in East London to see which shapes fit together perfectly.
