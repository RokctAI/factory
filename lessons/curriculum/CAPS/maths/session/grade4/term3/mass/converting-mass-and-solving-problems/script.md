# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between grams and kilograms, using whole numbers and fractions, and solving problems in contexts involving mass. The anchor is a Saturday market in Paarl where a family buys potatoes, onions, flour and sweets, and a bakery that packs biscuits in 250 g packets. The governing idea is that 1 kilogram is 1 000 grams, so kilograms become grams by multiplying by 1 000 and grams become kilograms by grouping in thousands, and that fractions of a kilogram are fractions of 1 000 g. Converting whole and mixed masses, finding fractions of a kilogram in grams, and adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing masses in context are examinable.

## Subtopic: Changing Kilograms and Grams

1 kilogram is 1 000 grams. So 2 kg is 2 000 g, 3 kg is 3 000 g and 5 kg is 5 000 g. To change whole kilograms into grams, multiply by 1 000. On paper, that means writing three zeros after the number of kilograms.

Many masses are a mix of kilograms and grams, like 2 kg 300 g. To write this in grams, change the kilograms and add the grams: 2 000 g plus 300 g is 2 300 g. A bag of potatoes of 1 kg 750 g is 1 750 g.

Going the other way, group the grams into thousands. 4 250 g has 4 thousands and 250 left over, so it is 4 kg 250 g. 6 000 g is exactly 6 kg. 3 050 g is 3 kg 50 g. Look at the digits: the thousands digit gives the kilograms, and the last three digits give the grams.

Be careful with zeros. 2 kg 30 g is 2 030 g, not 2 300 g. The 30 g has to sit in the tens and units places of the grams. Saying the mass aloud helps: two thousand and thirty grams.

The questions for this section are with you now: changing whole kilograms to grams, writing kilograms and grams as grams, and going back from grams to kilograms and grams.

## Subtopic: Fractions of a Kilogram

Fractions of a kilogram are fractions of 1 000 g. Half a kilogram is half of 1 000 g, which is 500 g. A quarter of a kilogram is 1 000 divided by 4, which is 250 g. Three quarters of a kilogram is 3 times 250, which is 750 g.

Other fractions work the same way: divide 1 000 by the bottom number and multiply by the top. One fifth of a kilogram is 1 000 divided by 5, which is 200 g. Two fifths is 400 g. One eighth of a kilogram is 1 000 divided by 8, which is 125 g.

Mixed amounts with fractions are common in shops and recipes. One and a half kilograms is 1 000 g plus 500 g, which is 1 500 g. Two and a quarter kilograms is 2 000 g plus 250 g, which is 2 250 g. A recipe asking for half a kilogram of flour needs 500 g on the kitchen scale.

Going back, look for the fraction you know. 250 g is a quarter of a kilogram. 750 g is three quarters. 1 500 g is one and a half kilograms. Shops often sell mince in 500 g packs and call them half a kilo, which is short for half a kilogram.

The questions for this section are with you now: half, quarter, three quarters, fifths and eighths of a kilogram in grams, and mixed amounts like one and a half kilograms.

## Subtopic: Solving Mass Problems

Adding masses: at the market the family buys 2 kg 500 g of potatoes and 750 g of onions. In grams: 2 500 g plus 750 g is 3 250 g, which is 3 kg 250 g. Changing to grams first makes the adding the same as adding whole numbers.

Subtracting masses: gogo has a 5 kg bag of flour and uses 1 kg 200 g for bread. 5 000 g minus 1 200 g is 3 800 g, so 3 kg 800 g of flour is left. Check by adding: 3 800 plus 1 200 is 5 000.

Multiplying masses: the bakery packs biscuits in 250 g packets. 6 packets have a mass of 6 times 250 g, which is 1 500 g, or one and a half kilograms. Dividing masses: 2 kg of sweets shared equally into 8 party packs is 2 000 g divided by 8, which is 250 g in each pack.

Read the question for the unit it wants. If the question asks for the answer in kilograms, change back at the end. 3 250 g is 3 kg 250 g, or three and a quarter kilograms. Always write the unit with your answer.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 2 kg 30 g as 2 300 g; the 30 g goes in the tens and units places, so it is 2 030 g. Two: thinking a quarter of a kilogram is 25 g or 400 g; it is 1 000 divided by 4, which is 250 g. Three: adding 2 kg 500 g and 750 g as 2 kg 1 250 g and stopping there; change to grams, 3 250 g, which is 3 kg 250 g. Four: giving an answer without a unit; always say whether it is grams or kilograms.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing masses in market and bakery problems, and giving the answer in the unit asked for.

Times and share: 6 packets of 250 g is 1 500 g. 2 000 g into 8 packs is 250 g each.
