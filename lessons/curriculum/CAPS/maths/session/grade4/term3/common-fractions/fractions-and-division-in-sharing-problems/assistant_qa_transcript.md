### subtopic_1 — A Fraction Is a Division
**Assistant:** How can a fraction be a division? I thought fractions were pieces of pizza.

**Tutor:** They are pieces of pizza, and the pieces come from sharing, which is division. When you cut one pizza into 4 equal slices so 4 friends can share it, you are doing 1 divided by 4, and each friend's slice is one quarter. So the fraction one quarter is simply the answer to the division 1 divided by 4. The line in the middle of a fraction even behaves like a division sign: the number on top is being divided by the number underneath. That is why three quarters is the same as 3 divided by 4. Picture 3 pizzas and 4 friends. You cannot give everyone a whole pizza, so cut each pizza into quarters. Now there are 12 slices, and each friend gets 3 slices, three quarters of a pizza. In the same way, 2 divided by 5 is two fifths, and 7 divided by 8 is seven eighths. Seeing fractions as divisions is useful because it lets you write the answer to any sharing straight away, without drawing. When 5 people share 4 bars of chocolate equally, each gets four fifths of a bar: the amount shared goes on top, the number of people sharing goes underneath. You will use this idea for many years.

### subtopic_2 — Fractions of a Group
**Assistant:** Why do I divide by the bottom and multiply by the top to find three quarters of 20?

**Tutor:** Because that is exactly what three quarters means. The bottom number, 4, tells you to split the group into 4 equal parts. Splitting 20 sweets into 4 equal parts means dividing 20 by 4, and each part has 5 sweets. That is one quarter. The top number, 3, tells you how many of those parts to take. Three parts of 5 sweets is 3 times 5, which is 15 sweets. So three quarters of 20 is 15. If you did it the other way round and divided by 3 first, you would be making thirds, which is the wrong size of part, and 20 does not even share equally into 3. A picture always helps: draw 20 dots in 4 equal rows of 5. Circle 3 rows. You can see 15 dots inside the circles. A quick check is that your answer should be less than the whole group whenever the top is smaller than the bottom. Fifteen is less than 20, which makes sense, because three quarters is less than one whole. You can also check that the parts add up: three quarters is 15 and the other quarter is 5, and 15 plus 5 is 20.

### subtopic_3 — Sharing and Grouping Problems
**Assistant:** When is a remainder a fraction and when is it just a remainder?

**Tutor:** It depends on what is being shared, and the story always tells you. If the things being shared can be cut into equal pieces, like rotis, loaves, pizzas, chocolate bars, ribbon or money, then a remainder can be shared too, and it becomes a fraction. 7 rotis between 2 children gives 3 each with 1 left over; cut that roti in half and each child gets 3 and a half. If the things cannot be cut, like children, chairs, taxis, books or eggs you want to keep whole, then the remainder stays as a remainder, and you decide what it means. 7 children in teams of 2 gives 3 teams and 1 child left over; you cannot have half a child, so either one team has 3 children or the extra child waits. Sometimes the story wants you to round up: 7 children going home in cars that each take 2 children need 4 cars, not 3 and a half. So after every division with a remainder, ask yourself: can this thing be cut fairly? If yes, turn the remainder into a fraction. If no, think about what the story needs. Thinking about the real objects is what makes your answer sensible, not just correct on paper.
