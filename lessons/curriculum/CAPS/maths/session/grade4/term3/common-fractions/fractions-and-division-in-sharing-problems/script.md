# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers recognising, describing and using the equivalence of division and fractions, and solving problems in contexts involving fractions, including grouping and equal sharing. The anchor is a family gathering in Gqeberha with rotis to share among children, a bag of 20 sweets, and loaves of bread to be cut into equal portions. The governing idea is that a fraction is a division: three quarters is 3 divided by 4, the share each person gets when 3 things are shared equally among 4 people, and that finding a fraction of a group means dividing the group by the bottom number and multiplying by the top number. Writing a sharing as a fraction, finding fractions of whole numbers and solving grouping and sharing problems with fraction answers are examinable.

## Subtopic: A Fraction Is a Division

One roti is shared equally among 4 children. Each child gets one quarter of the roti. We can write this as a division: 1 divided by 4 equals one quarter. The fraction one quarter means 1 whole divided into 4 equal parts. Fractions and division are two ways of writing the same sharing.

Now share 3 rotis equally among 4 children. Cut each roti into quarters. There are 12 quarters altogether, and each child gets 3 of them. Each child gets three quarters of a roti. So 3 divided by 4 is three quarters. The top number of a fraction is the amount being shared, and the bottom number is the number of people sharing.

This works for every fraction. 2 divided by 5 is two fifths: 2 loaves shared among 5 people gives each person two fifths of a loaf. 5 divided by 8 is five eighths. If you see the fraction seven eighths, you can read it as 7 shared among 8.

It also works when the answer is a whole number. 8 divided by 4 is 2, and eight quarters is 2 wholes. 6 divided by 3 is 2, and six thirds is 2 wholes. The fraction line and the division sign do the same job.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing a sharing as a fraction and a fraction as a sharing.

## Subtopic: Fractions of a Group

A fraction can be a part of a group as well as a part of one thing. There are 20 sweets in a bag, and one quarter of them are red. To find one quarter of 20, share the 20 sweets into 4 equal groups. Each group has 5 sweets. So one quarter of 20 is 5.

To find three quarters of 20, find one quarter first, then take 3 of those groups. One quarter of 20 is 5, so three quarters of 20 is 3 times 5, which is 15. The rule is: divide by the bottom number, then multiply by the top number.

Try it with other groups. One third of 18 learners: 18 divided by 3 is 6. Two thirds of 18 is 2 times 6, which is 12. Two fifths of R30: 30 divided by 5 is 6, and 2 times 6 is 12, so R12. Three eighths of 40 cm: 40 divided by 8 is 5, and 3 times 5 is 15, so 15 cm.

Check with a picture. Draw 20 dots in 4 rows of 5. One row is one quarter: 5 dots. Three rows are three quarters: 15 dots. The dots that are not in those rows, 5 of them, are the other quarter, and 15 plus 5 is 20, the whole bag.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding a unit fraction of a group and then any fraction of a group.

## Subtopic: Sharing and Grouping Problems

When things are shared and there is a remainder, the remainder can often be cut into fractions. 7 rotis are shared equally between 2 children. Each child gets 3 whole rotis, and 1 roti is left over. Cut that roti in half and each child gets one half more. Each child gets 3 and a half rotis. 7 divided by 2 is 3 and a half.

Another example: 10 loaves of bread shared equally among 4 families. Each family gets 2 whole loaves, with 2 loaves left over. Share the 2 left-over loaves among 4 families: each gets two quarters of a loaf, which is one half. So each family gets 2 and a half loaves.

Grouping problems ask how many parts of a certain size fit into an amount. How many half cups of rice are in 3 cups? Each cup has 2 halves, so 3 cups have 6 halves. How many quarter-hour lessons fit into 2 hours? Each hour has 4 quarters, so 2 hours have 8 quarter-hours.

Read the story to decide whether a fraction answer makes sense. Rotis, loaves and pizzas can be cut, so the remainder becomes a fraction. Children, taxis and chairs cannot be cut, so there the remainder means rounding up or leaving some over, as you learned in division.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: writing 3 shared among 4 as 4/3; the amount shared goes on top, so it is 3/4. Two: finding 3/4 of 20 by dividing 20 by 3; divide by the bottom number, 4, then multiply by the top number, 3. Three: leaving the 1 roti over as a remainder of 1 when sharing food; food can be cut, so each child gets 3 and a half. Four: cutting a remainder of children into fractions; people cannot be cut, so the story decides how to deal with the remainder.

The questions for this section are with you now: sharing with remainders that become fractions, and grouping in halves and quarters.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the rotis, the sweets and the bread — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Share One, Share Many

Sharing makes a fraction. 1 roti among 4 children: one quarter each. 1 divided by 4 is one quarter.

3 rotis among 4 children: three quarters each. 3 divided by 4 is three quarters.

The top is what you share. The bottom is how many people share.

Keep the rotis in mind and try the questions for this part: fractions as sharing.

## Subtopic: A Fraction of a Group

One quarter of 20 sweets: make 4 equal groups. 5 in each. So 5.

Three quarters of 20: three of those groups. 3 times 5 is 15.

Divide by the bottom, times by the top. Two thirds of 18: 18 divided by 3 is 6, 2 times 6 is 12.

Hold the bag of sweets in mind and try this part's questions: fractions of a group.

## Subtopic: Leftovers Become Fractions

7 rotis between 2 children: 3 each and 1 over. Cut it in half. 3 and a half each.

10 loaves among 4 families: 2 each and 2 over. Each family gets half of a left-over loaf. 2 and a half each.

How many half cups in 3 cups? 2 halves in each cup, so 6. But do not cut children or taxis!

Pocket summary of the lesson. A fraction is a division: one quarter is 1 divided by 4, and three quarters is 3 divided by 4, the share each person gets when 3 things are shared among 4. To find a fraction of a group, divide by the bottom number and multiply by the top number: three quarters of 20 is 15. When food is shared and there is a remainder, cut the remainder into fractions, like 7 rotis between 2 being 3 and a half each, but never cut people or taxis.

Hold the family gathering in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: sharing and grouping with fractions.
