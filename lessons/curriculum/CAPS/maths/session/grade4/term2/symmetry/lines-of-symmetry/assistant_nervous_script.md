Hey — if symmetry feels slippery, here is the calm version: can you fold it so the halves match?

A line of symmetry is a fold line. Fold along it and one half sits exactly on the other. A butterfly folds down its body.

Equal halves are not enough. A rectangle's diagonal makes equal triangles but they do not land on each other.

Check every direction: up and down, across, and slanted. A square has 4 lines, a rectangle 2, an equilateral triangle 3.

Letters: A and T fold down the middle, B and C fold across, H and O do both, S does neither.

Open the practice set and imagine the fold every time. You've got this!
