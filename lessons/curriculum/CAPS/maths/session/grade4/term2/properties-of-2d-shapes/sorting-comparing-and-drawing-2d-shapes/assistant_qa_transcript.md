### subtopic_1 — Sorting by Straight and Curved Sides
**Assistant:** Why do we sort shapes? It feels like a game, not maths.

**Tutor:** Sorting is one of the oldest and most important things mathematicians do, because it is how we discover which properties matter. When you put all the four-sided shapes together, you notice that some have square corners and some do not, that some have equal sides and some do not, and you start to see the family of quadrilaterals and its special members. When you put a semicircle between the straight pile and the curved pile, you discover that your sorting rule needs a third category, which is exactly what scientists discover when a new animal does not fit the old groups. Sorting also forces you to say why, and saying why is the heart of mathematical thinking. A learner who puts a square in the right pile by luck has learned nothing; a learner who says because all four sides are straight has stated a property that will still be true in Grade 12. Later in school, sorting becomes classification in geometry, where you will prove that a shape belongs to a family by checking its properties, and in data handling, where you will sort numbers into categories to draw graphs. The two-way table you used today is the same tool that scientists use to organise results. It feels like a game because it is enjoyable, and that is a bonus, not a sign that it is not real maths.

### subtopic_2 — Sorting and Comparing by Number of Sides
**Assistant:** I keep drawing my squares one unit too big. What am I doing wrong?

**Tutor:** You are almost certainly counting lines instead of squares, which is the single most common grid mistake and nothing to be embarrassed about. When you start at a corner and count one, two, three along the grid, you may be counting the lines you touch, and your finger is on the fourth line by the time you say three. A 3 by 3 square should have three little squares along each side, which means your side runs from the first line to the fourth line, crossing three squares. The fix is to count the squares themselves, touching the middle of each one as you say its number, or to count the jumps rather than the landing points. Another way is to start by counting zero on the starting corner, then one, two, three as you move, so that the number you say is the number of squares you have crossed. Check your drawing when you finish by counting the small squares along one edge; if you see four, erase one unit. The same trouble appears when learners count fence posts and fence gaps, which you will meet in division problems, and when they count the days between two dates in Term 4. Posts, lines and dates are landing points; gaps, squares and days are the spaces between. Knowing which one a question wants saves many marks.

### subtopic_3 — Drawing Shapes on Grid Paper
**Assistant:** Can I sort shapes by colour or size? Those are easier to see.

**Tutor:** You can sort anything by anything, and colour and size are perfectly good properties for sorting buttons or beads. But they are not geometric properties, because they do not tell you anything about the shape itself. A red triangle and a blue triangle are the same shape; a tiny square and a huge square are the same shape. In this topic the sorting is meant to reveal what kind of shape each one is, and for that only shape properties count: straight or curved sides, the number of sides and corners, whether the sides are equal and whether the corners are square. The test of a good geometric property is that it stays the same if you make the shape bigger or smaller, turn it, flip it or paint it. Side count passes that test; colour and size do not. There is one place where size does matter in geometry, and that is when you compare two shapes of the same kind, saying for example that one rectangle is longer than another, or that one square has sides twice as long. That comparison is about measurement and will come in Term 4 under length and area. For sorting in this topic, if a question asks you to sort by a property, choose a shape property and name it; if you sort by colour, the marker will know that you have missed what the question was asking.
