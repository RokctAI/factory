### subtopic_1 — What Makes a Polygon
**Assistant:** Why does a tilted square sometimes get called a diamond? Is a diamond a shape?

**Tutor:** In everyday talk people say diamond for a square standing on its corner, but in mathematics there is no shape called a diamond, and the tilted square is simply a square. The name of a polygon depends on its sides and corners, and turning the shape does not change either. A square has four equal sides and four square corners whether it sits flat or balances on a point; nothing about the shape has moved, only your view of it. This matters because learners who think a tilted square is a different shape also tend to think a tilted triangle is not a triangle or a rectangle on its end is not a rectangle, and then they miss shapes in tests and in the world. The shape that people sometimes mean by diamond, with two sharp corners and two blunt ones, is a rhombus, which has four equal sides but corners that are not all equal, so it is an irregular quadrilateral and not a square at all. If a question shows a square on its corner, call it a square. If you want to be extra clear, say a square turned through half a right angle. The habit to build is to look at the properties, the count of sides, the lengths and the corners, and not at how the drawing happens to be placed on the page.

### subtopic_2 — Regular and Irregular Polygons
**Assistant:** Is a square a rectangle? My brother says yes and my teacher says yes but it feels wrong.

**Tutor:** Your brother and your teacher are right, and the feeling that it is wrong comes from the way we use words in everyday life, where we usually call something a rectangle only when it is clearly not a square. In mathematics the rule is different and more precise. A rectangle is any quadrilateral with four square corners. A square has four square corners, so it passes the test and is a rectangle. It happens to pass an extra test as well, having all four sides equal, which earns it the extra name square. So every square is a rectangle, but most rectangles are not squares. It is like saying every dog is an animal but not every animal is a dog; square is the more special word. The same thing happens all through the quadrilateral family, which you will explore more in Grade 5 and 6. A square is also a special rhombus, since it has four equal sides, and both squares and rectangles are special parallelograms. These nested names feel odd at first, but they are useful, because anything true about rectangles is automatically true about squares, and you do not have to prove it twice. For Grade 4, hold on to the simple version: a square is a regular quadrilateral, a rectangle that is not a square is an irregular quadrilateral, and a square counts as a rectangle because it has four square corners.

### subtopic_3 — Circles and Shapes in the World
**Assistant:** If a circle isn't a polygon, what is it? It must be something.

**Tutor:** It is a circle, and that is a complete answer, because not every flat shape has to be a polygon. Polygons are one family of 2D shapes, the ones built from straight sides; circles belong to a different family, shapes with curved edges, which also includes ovals and the semicircle you see in a protractor. A circle is defined by a rule rather than by sides: it is all the points that are exactly the same distance from one central point. That distance is the radius, and the distance across the whole circle through the centre is the diameter, which is twice the radius. You will measure these in later grades. Because every point on the edge is the same distance from the centre, a circle has no corners and no straight parts; it is perfectly round all the way. You can check whether something is a true circle by measuring from the centre to the edge in several directions and seeing that the measurement never changes. A wheel is a circle for exactly this reason: as it rolls, the centre stays the same height above the ground, so the ride is smooth. So when a test asks you to name a round shape, circle is the name, and if it asks whether it is a polygon, the answer is no, because a polygon must have straight sides and a circle has none.
