# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers solving problems involving whole numbers in financial and measurement contexts, ratio and rate, and grouping and equal sharing with remainders. The anchor is R936 shared equally by 8 learners who sold cakes at a school in Rustenburg, 125 learners taken on an outing in minibuses that seat 15, a 684 metre fence with posts every 6 metres, and a 480 kilometre drive at 80 kilometres per hour. The governing idea is that division appears whenever an amount is shared or grouped equally, that a rate or ratio is found by dividing, and that the remainder must be interpreted from the story: ignored, rounded up to another group, or shared further. Choosing division, interpreting the remainder and finding a rate by division are examinable.

## Subtopic: Sharing Money and Measurement

Eight learners sold cakes and made R936 together. They share it equally. How much each? Shared equally signals division: 936 divided by 8 equals box. Estimate: 800 divided by 8 is 100 and 960 divided by 8 is 120, so between 100 and 120. Break down: 936 is 800 plus 136; 800 divided by 8 is 100 and 136 divided by 8 is 17, because 8 times 17 is 136. Each learner gets R117. Check: 8 times 117 is 936.

A 684 metre fence has a post every 6 metres. How many gaps of 6 metres are there? 684 divided by 6. Estimate: 600 divided by 6 is 100 and 720 divided by 6 is 120. Break down: 684 is 600 plus 84; 600 divided by 6 is 100, 84 divided by 6 is 14. There are 114 gaps. Check: 6 times 114 is 684.

Measurement problems carry their units through. 684 metres divided into 6 metre lengths gives 114 lengths, a plain number of pieces. R936 divided among 8 people gives R117 per person, rands again. Ask what kind of thing the answer is before you write it: a count of groups, or an amount per share.

Division with money in whole rands sometimes leaves a remainder that cannot be split into whole rands. R940 shared among 8 is 117 remainder 4: each gets R117 and R4 is left over, which might be kept for the class fund. The story decides.

The questions for this section are with you now: sharing money equally, dividing a length into equal pieces, and keeping track of what kind of thing the answer is.

## Subtopic: What the Remainder Means

125 learners go on an outing in minibuses that seat 15. How many minibuses are needed? 125 divided by 15: 15 times 8 is 120, leaving 5, so 8 remainder 5. But 8 minibuses carry only 120 learners, and 5 learners would be left standing at the gate. Every learner must travel, so a ninth minibus is needed. The answer is 9, and the remainder forced us to round up.

The same numbers can give a different answer with a different question. 125 sweets are packed in bags of 15 for sale. How many full bags? 8 full bags, and the 5 leftover sweets are not a full bag, so they are not counted. Here the remainder is dropped and the answer is 8.

Sometimes the remainder is the answer. 125 sweets in bags of 15: how many sweets are left over? 5. And sometimes the remainder is shared further: 125 metres of ribbon cut into 15 equal pieces gives 8 metres each with 5 metres left, and those 5 metres can be cut further to make each piece a little longer, which you will do with fractions later in the year.

The routine is: divide, find the remainder, then read the question again. Round up when every item must be included, as with people in vehicles or boxes for all the loaves. Round down when only full groups count, as with bags for sale or complete teams. Report the remainder when the question asks what is left.

The questions for this section are with you now: deciding whether the remainder means rounding up, rounding down, or reporting what is left.

## Subtopic: Rate and Ratio by Division

In Term 1 a rate was used by multiplying. Now we find a rate by dividing. A car travels 480 kilometres in 6 hours. How far in one hour? 480 divided by 6 is 80, so the rate is 80 kilometres per hour. A rate is an amount per one unit, and dividing by the number of units gives the amount for one.

Prices per item work the same way. If 8 boxes of chips cost R280, one box costs 280 divided by 8, which is 35, so R35 per box. If a 5 kilogram bag of rice costs R95, the price per kilogram is 95 divided by 5, which is R19 per kilogram. Dividing to find the price per unit is how you compare two sizes of the same product.

Ratios are also simplified by division. A class has 24 boys and 16 girls. The ratio of boys to girls is 24 to 16. Dividing both numbers by 8, the highest common factor, gives 3 to 2. The same relationship, in its simplest form: for every 3 boys there are 2 girls. Both parts must be divided by the same number, just as both were multiplied by the same number in Term 1.

Sharing in a ratio combines division and multiplication. R100 is shared between two learners in the ratio 3 to 2. There are 3 plus 2, which is 5, parts altogether. 100 divided by 5 is 20, so each part is R20. The first learner gets 3 times 20, which is R60, and the second gets 2 times 20, which is R40. Check: 60 plus 40 is 100.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: answering 8 minibuses for 125 learners; 5 learners would be left behind, so the remainder means a ninth minibus. Two: counting the 5 leftover sweets as a bag for sale; only full bags count, so the answer is 8. Three: simplifying 24 to 16 by dividing only one side; both parts must be divided by the same number to get 3 to 2. Four: sharing R100 in the ratio 3 to 2 as R30 and R20; there are 5 parts of R20, giving R60 and R40.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding a rate by dividing, finding a price per unit, simplifying a ratio, and sharing in a ratio.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the cake money, the minibuses, the fence and the long drive — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Share It Out

Shared equally means divide. R936 among 8 learners: 936 divided by 8. Break into 800 and 136. 100 and 17. R117 each. Check: 8 times 117 is 936.

A 684 metre fence with a post every 6 metres: 684 divided by 6. 600 and 84. 100 and 14. 114 gaps.

Ask what the answer is. Rands per person? A number of gaps? Write the right kind of answer.

Keep the cake money in mind and try the questions for this part: sharing money and lengths.

## Subtopic: What Happens to the Leftover

125 learners, minibuses of 15. 125 divided by 15 is 8 remainder 5. Eight buses leave 5 learners behind. Everyone must go, so round up: 9 minibuses.

125 sweets in bags of 15 to sell. 8 full bags. The 5 leftover sweets do not make a bag, so round down: 8.

Divide, find the remainder, then read the question again. Everyone included: round up. Only full groups: round down. What is left: report the remainder.

Hold the minibuses in mind and try this part's questions: what to do with the remainder.

## Subtopic: Per One, and Fair Shares

480 kilometres in 6 hours: divide to find one hour. 480 divided by 6 is 80. So 80 kilometres per hour.

R280 for 8 boxes: 280 divided by 8 is 35. R35 per box.

24 boys to 16 girls: divide both by 8. 3 to 2. Share R100 in 3 to 2: 5 parts, R20 each part, so R60 and R40.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Division solves sharing and grouping problems in money and measurement. After dividing, read the story to decide what the remainder means: round up when everyone or everything must be included, round down when only full groups count, or report what is left. A rate is found by dividing the total by the number of units, a ratio is simplified by dividing both parts by the same number, and an amount is shared in a ratio by dividing it into the total number of parts.

Hold the long drive in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: rates and ratios by dividing.
