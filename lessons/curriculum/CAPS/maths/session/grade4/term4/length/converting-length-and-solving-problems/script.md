# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers converting between millimetres and centimetres, centimetres and metres, and metres and kilometres, using whole numbers and common fractions, and solving problems in contexts involving length. The anchor is a sewing shop in Upington selling ribbon and fabric by the metre, a builder cutting planks, and a 400 m school athletics track. The governing idea is that 10 mm make a centimetre, 100 cm make a metre and 1 000 m make a kilometre, so changing to a smaller unit multiplies by 10, 100 or 1 000 and changing to a bigger unit groups by them, and that fractions of a unit are fractions of those numbers. Converting whole and mixed lengths, finding fractions of units, and adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing lengths in context are examinable.

## Subtopic: Changing Units of Length

The units of length are linked by 10, 100 and 1 000. 1 cm is 10 mm. 1 m is 100 cm. 1 km is 1 000 m. To change to a smaller unit, multiply: 3 cm is 30 mm, 4 m is 400 cm, 2 km is 2 000 m. You need more of the smaller units to make the same length.

Mixed lengths are changed in two parts. 7 cm 4 mm is 70 mm plus 4 mm, which is 74 mm. 1 m 35 cm is 100 cm plus 35 cm, which is 135 cm. 3 km 250 m is 3 000 m plus 250 m, which is 3 250 m.

To change to a bigger unit, group. 90 mm is 9 cm, because each 10 mm make a centimetre. 450 cm is 4 m 50 cm, because 400 cm make 4 m. 2 450 m is 2 km 450 m. 6 000 m is exactly 6 km.

Watch the zeros. 1 m 5 cm is 105 cm, not 150 cm. 2 km 80 m is 2 080 m, not 2 800 m. The smaller unit's number must fit in the right places. Saying the length aloud, one hundred and five centimetres, helps you write it correctly.

The questions for this section are with you now: multiplying by 10, 100 or 1 000 to change to a smaller unit, grouping to change to a bigger unit, and mixed lengths.

## Subtopic: Fractions of Units

Fractions of a metre are fractions of 100 cm. Half a metre is 50 cm. A quarter of a metre is 100 divided by 4, which is 25 cm. Three quarters of a metre is 3 times 25, which is 75 cm. One fifth of a metre is 20 cm.

Fractions of a kilometre are fractions of 1 000 m. Half a kilometre is 500 m. A quarter of a kilometre is 250 m. Three quarters of a kilometre is 750 m. One and a half kilometres is 1 000 m plus 500 m, which is 1 500 m.

Fractions of a centimetre are fractions of 10 mm. Half a centimetre is 5 mm. So 3 and a half centimetres is 30 mm plus 5 mm, which is 35 mm.

At the sewing shop, ribbon is sold by the metre. Two and a half metres of ribbon is 200 cm plus 50 cm, which is 250 cm. A quarter of a metre of lace is 25 cm. Knowing these fractions lets you cut and measure without a calculator.

The questions for this section are with you now: halves, quarters and fifths of metres, kilometres and centimetres, and mixed lengths with fractions.

## Subtopic: Length Problems

Adding lengths: Nomvula walks 1 km 200 m to school and the same back. 1 200 m plus 1 200 m is 2 400 m, which is 2 km 400 m. Change to the smaller unit, calculate, then change back if needed.

Subtracting lengths: a builder has a plank 2 m long and cuts off 45 cm. 200 cm minus 45 cm is 155 cm, which is 1 m 55 cm left. Check by adding: 155 plus 45 is 200.

Multiplying lengths: the school track is 400 m around. 5 laps is 5 times 400, which is 2 000 m, which is 2 km. Dividing lengths: 3 m of ribbon is cut into 4 equal pieces. 300 cm divided by 4 is 75 cm each.

Read the question for the unit it wants. If it asks how many kilometres, change back at the end. If a piece of fabric is 150 cm and the question asks for metres, the answer is 1 and a half metres, or 1 m 50 cm. Always write the unit.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: dividing when changing to a smaller unit, 4 m as 4 cm; a smaller unit needs more of them, so multiply, 400 cm. Two: writing 1 m 5 cm as 150 cm; the 5 cm goes in the units place, so it is 105 cm. Three: thinking a quarter of a metre is 4 cm; it is 100 divided by 4, which is 25 cm. Four: subtracting 45 cm from 2 m as 2 minus 45; change to 200 cm first, then 200 minus 45 is 155 cm.

The questions for this section are with you now: adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing lengths in walking, building, running and sewing problems.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the ribbon, the planks and the running track — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Tens, Hundreds, Thousands

10 mm in a centimetre. 100 cm in a metre. 1 000 m in a kilometre.

Smaller unit: multiply. 4 m is 400 cm. 1 m 35 cm is 135 cm.

Bigger unit: group. 2 450 m is 2 km 450 m. Watch the zeros: 1 m 5 cm is 105 cm.

Keep the sewing shop in mind and try the questions for this part: changing units.

## Subtopic: Halves and Quarters of Lengths

Half a metre is 50 cm. A quarter is 25 cm. Three quarters is 75 cm.

Half a kilometre is 500 m. A quarter is 250 m.

Half a centimetre is 5 mm.

Hold the ribbon in mind and try this part's questions: fractions of units.

Change to the smaller unit, then work it out. To school and back: 1 200 m twice is 2 400 m.

Plank: 200 cm take away 45 cm is 155 cm.
