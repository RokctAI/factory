# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers calculating time intervals in seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years and decades, and solving problems in contexts involving time. The anchor is a family bus trip from Polokwane to Johannesburg, a school day from 07:45 to 13:30, and dates from South African history such as 1994. The governing idea is that the units of time are linked, 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day, 7 days in a week, 12 months in a year and 10 years in a decade, and that an interval is found by counting on from the start time to the end time in friendly steps. Converting between units of time, finding intervals between clock times and solving time problems in context are examinable.

## Subtopic: Units of Time

Time is measured in units that fit inside each other. 60 seconds make 1 minute. 60 minutes make 1 hour. 24 hours make 1 day. 7 days make 1 week. 12 months make 1 year, and a year has 365 days, or 366 in a leap year. 10 years make 1 decade, and 100 years make 1 century.

To change a bigger unit into a smaller one, multiply. 3 hours is 3 times 60, which is 180 minutes. 2 days is 2 times 24, which is 48 hours. 5 weeks is 5 times 7, which is 35 days. 4 decades is 4 times 10, which is 40 years.

To change a smaller unit into a bigger one, divide or group. 120 minutes is 120 divided by 60, which is 2 hours. 21 days is 3 weeks. 90 minutes is 60 minutes and 30 minutes, which is 1 hour 30 minutes, or one and a half hours.

Some changes give a mixed answer. 100 minutes is 60 minutes and 40 minutes, so 1 hour 40 minutes. 30 days is 4 weeks and 2 days, because 4 times 7 is 28. Do not write 100 minutes as 1 hour 0 minutes, or 1.40 hours; give the hours and the minutes.

The questions for this section are with you now: the links between units of time, multiplying to change to a smaller unit and dividing or grouping to change to a bigger one.

## Subtopic: Finding Intervals Between Clock Times

A time interval is how long it is from a start time to an end time. School starts at 07:45 and ends at 13:30. How long is the school day? Count on in friendly steps. From 07:45 to 08:00 is 15 minutes. From 08:00 to 13:00 is 5 hours. From 13:00 to 13:30 is 30 minutes. Total: 5 hours and 45 minutes.

A number line helps. Draw the start time on the left and the end time on the right, and jump to the next whole hour, then in whole hours, then to the end. Add the jumps. The family's bus leaves Polokwane at 06:50 and arrives in Johannesburg at 10:35. 06:50 to 07:00 is 10 minutes; 07:00 to 10:00 is 3 hours; 10:00 to 10:35 is 35 minutes. Total: 3 hours 45 minutes.

Do not subtract clock times like ordinary numbers. 10:35 minus 06:50 looks like 1035 minus 650, which is 385, but there are 60 minutes in an hour, not 100, so that answer is meaningless. Counting on in steps always works.

To find an end time, add the interval to the start. A movie starts at 14:20 and lasts 1 hour 50 minutes. 14:20 plus 1 hour is 15:20. 15:20 plus 40 minutes is 16:00, and 10 minutes more is 16:10. The movie ends at 16:10.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting on from a start time to an end time in friendly steps, and finding an end time by adding an interval.

## Subtopic: Longer Intervals and Time Problems

Some intervals are counted in days, weeks, months, years or decades. South Africa held its first democratic election in 1994. From 1994 to 2026 is 2026 minus 1994, which is 32 years, a bit more than 3 decades. A child born in 2016 is 10 years old in 2026, exactly one decade.

Weeks and days: a school term of 10 weeks is 10 times 7, which is 70 days. A holiday of 3 weeks and 4 days is 21 plus 4, which is 25 days. Months: from March to September of the same year is 6 months, counting the jumps from one month to the next.

Time problems often mix units. The family stops for 25 minutes for lunch on a trip that would otherwise take 3 hours 45 minutes. The total time is 3 hours 45 minutes plus 25 minutes. 45 plus 25 is 70 minutes, which is 1 hour 10 minutes. So the total is 4 hours 10 minutes. When the minutes pass 60, change 60 of them into an hour.

Read time problems carefully: is the question asking for a start time, an end time or the interval? Draw a number line, mark what you know, and put a box where the answer goes. Then count on, and check your answer by counting back.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: subtracting clock times like ordinary numbers, 1035 minus 650; count on in steps, because an hour has 60 minutes. Two: writing 100 minutes as 1 hour; it is 1 hour and 40 minutes. Three: leaving an answer as 3 hours 70 minutes; change 60 minutes into an hour, giving 4 hours 10 minutes. Four: answering with the interval when the question asks for the end time; read what the question wants and mark it on a number line.

The questions for this section are with you now: intervals in years and decades, weeks and days, mixed units in problems, and choosing what the question asks for.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the bus trip, the school day and the history dates — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Units That Fit Together

60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day, 7 days in a week, 10 years in a decade.

Big unit to small: multiply. 3 hours is 180 minutes.

Small to big: divide or group. 100 minutes is 1 hour 40 minutes.

Keep the bus trip in mind and try the questions for this part: units of time.

## Subtopic: Start to Finish

Count on from the start to the finish in easy jumps. Go to the next whole hour, then whole hours, then the last bit.

07:45 to 13:30: 15 minutes, 5 hours, 30 minutes. 5 hours 45 minutes.

To find an end time, add the time on: 14:20 plus 1 hour 50 minutes is 16:10.

Hold the school day in mind and try this part's questions: intervals between times.

## Subtopic: Years, Weeks and Mixed Problems

1994 to 2026 is 32 years. 10 years is a decade.

10 weeks is 70 days. 3 weeks and 4 days is 25 days.

When minutes pass 60, make an hour. 3 hours 45 minutes plus 25 minutes is 4 hours 10 minutes.

The units of time fit together: 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day, 7 days in a week, 12 months in a year, 10 years in a decade and 100 in a century. Multiply to change to a smaller unit and divide or group to change to a bigger one. Find an interval by counting on from the start to the end in friendly steps on a number line, never by subtracting clock times like ordinary numbers, and change every 60 minutes into an hour.
