# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers calculating the number of days between any two dates within the same year or in consecutive years. The anchor is a Grade 4 class in Durban counting the days until a school trip, the days between public holidays, and the days from the start of the December holidays to the first school day of the new year. The governing idea is that the days between two dates are found by counting on: from the first date to the end of its month, then whole months by their number of days, then the days into the last month, using the days-in-each-month facts and checking with a calendar. Counting days within one month, across several months and across the change of year are examinable.

## Subtopic: Days Within One Month

To find the number of days from one date to another in the same month, subtract the dates. From 3 March to 10 March is 10 minus 3, which is 7 days. You can check on a calendar: 3 March and 10 March are in the same column, one week apart.

Think of it as counting the jumps from the first date to the second. From the 3rd to the 4th is 1 jump, to the 5th is 2, and so on to the 10th, which is 7 jumps. We count the jumps, not the dates we land on. If you count the dates themselves, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, you get 8, which is one too many for days from one date to another.

The class trip is on 25 May and today is 12 May. How many days until the trip? 25 minus 12 is 13 days. If someone asks how many days including both today and the trip day, that is a different question, and the answer is one more, 14. Read the question carefully.

Use subtraction you already know. From 6 June to 30 June is 30 minus 6, which is 24 days. From 1 August to 31 August is 30 days of jumps, even though August has 31 dates.

The questions for this section are with you now: subtracting dates in the same month, counting jumps not dates, and reading whether both days are included.

## Subtopic: Days Across Months

When the two dates are in different months, count on in steps. First count from the first date to the end of its month. Then add any whole months in between. Then add the days into the last month.

From 20 April to 15 May: April has 30 days, so from 20 April to 30 April is 30 minus 20, which is 10 days. Then from 30 April to 15 May is 15 more days. Total: 10 plus 15, which is 25 days.

From 16 June, Youth Day, to 9 August, Women's Day: June has 30 days, so from 16 June to 30 June is 14 days. All of July is 31 days. From 31 July to 9 August is 9 days. Total: 14 plus 31 plus 9, which is 54 days.

The days in each month matter. If you treat every month as 30 days, the answer will often be wrong by a day or two. Write the number of days in each month above it before you start, using the rhyme: thirty days has September, April, June and November.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting to the end of the month, adding whole months and adding the days into the last month.

## Subtopic: Days Across the New Year

Dates in consecutive years work the same way, with the end of December as one of the steps. School closes on 9 December and opens on 14 January the next year. From 9 December to 31 December is 31 minus 9, which is 22 days. From 31 December to 14 January is 14 more days. Total: 22 plus 14, which is 36 days.

From the Day of Reconciliation, 16 December, to New Year's Day, 1 January: 16 December to 31 December is 15 days, then 1 more day to 1 January. Total: 16 days. Countdowns to New Year often start from dates like this.

For longer gaps, add whole months. From 15 November to 10 February the next year: 15 November to 30 November is 15 days, December is 31 days, January is 31 days, and 31 January to 10 February is 10 days. Total: 15 plus 31 plus 31 plus 10, which is 87 days. Remember February's length only matters if you go past the end of February.

Check your answer by estimating in months. 15 November to 10 February is a bit less than 3 months, and 3 months is about 90 days, so 87 days makes sense. A quick estimate catches a missed month or a month counted twice.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: counting the dates instead of the jumps, getting 8 days from 3 March to 10 March; subtract to count the jumps, 7 days. Two: treating every month as 30 days; check each month's length with the rhyme. Three: forgetting the days left in the first month when crossing months; count to the end of the first month first. Four: stopping at 31 December when the end date is in January; add the days into the new year as well.

The questions for this section are with you now: counting days across the end of December into the new year and estimating to check.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the school trip and the December countdown — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Same Month

Same month? Just subtract. 3 March to 10 March: 10 minus 3, 7 days.

Count the jumps, not the dates.

Today 12 May, trip 25 May: 13 days to go.

Keep the school trip in mind and try the questions for this part: days in one month.

## Subtopic: Across Months

Count to the end of the month, add the whole months, add the days into the last month.

20 April to 15 May: 10 days to the end of April, then 15 days. 25 days.

Know your months: 30 for September, April, June and November, 31 for most others.

Hold the public holidays in mind and try this part's questions: across months.

## Subtopic: Into the New Year

Count to 31 December, then into January. 9 December to 31 December is 22 days, plus 14 days to 14 January: 36 days.

16 December to 1 January: 15 days and 1 more, 16 days.

Estimate to check: about 30 days for each month.

Pocket summary of the lesson. To find the days between two dates in the same month, subtract the dates, counting jumps and not dates; add one only if both days are to be included. Across months, count to the end of the first month, add the whole months by their real lengths, and add the days into the last month. Across the new year, count to 31 December and then into January. Estimate with about 30 days a month to check.

Hold the December countdown in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: across the new year.
