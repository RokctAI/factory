### subtopic_1 — Days Within One Month
**Assistant:** Why is it 7 days from the 3rd to the 10th and not 8? I counted 8 numbers.

**Tutor:** You counted the dates, but the question is about the days in between, the time that passes. Think about one day. From Monday the 3rd to Tuesday the 4th, one day passes, even though two dates are involved. So the number of days from one date to another is the number of jumps between them, and that is found by subtracting: 10 minus 3 is 7. A calendar shows this clearly: the 3rd and the 10th are in the same column, one week apart, and a week is 7 days. Counting the numbers 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 gives 8 because it counts the starting day as well. There are times when that is what you want, for example if a holiday camp runs from the 3rd to the 10th and you want to know how many days you will be at camp, counting both the first and last days. Then 8 is right. So always read the question. How many days from, how many days until, or how many days between usually mean subtract. How many days including or how many days does it last from the first day to the last day often means subtract and add one.

### subtopic_2 — Days Across Months
**Assistant:** How do I count days when the dates are in different months?

**Tutor:** Break the journey into three parts, like a taxi trip with stops. First, ride from your starting date to the end of its month. To do that, subtract your date from the number of days in that month: from 20 April, April has 30 days, so 30 minus 20 is 10 days to reach 30 April. Second, ride through any whole months in between, adding each one's full number of days. Third, ride from the end of the last whole month into the final month, which is just the date you are going to: 15 May is 15 more days. Add the three parts: 10 plus 15 is 25 days. If there are no whole months in between, the middle part is zero. For 16 June to 9 August, the parts are 14 days to the end of June, 31 days for all of July, and 9 days into August, so 54 days. Writing the steps down neatly helps you see that nothing has been missed. And always use the real number of days in each month: one wrong month length makes the whole answer wrong by a day or more. If you are unsure, check with a calendar.

### subtopic_3 — Days Across the New Year
**Assistant:** What changes when the dates are in different years?

**Tutor:** Very little. The calendar simply carries on from December into January, so you count exactly as you would across any two months. The only thing to remember is that the end of December is one of your stops. From 9 December, count to 31 December: December has 31 days, so 31 minus 9 is 22 days. Then count into January: 14 January is 14 more days. The total is 36 days. You do not need to think about the whole year, because you are only crossing from one month into the next. If the gap is longer, add the whole months in between as usual. From 15 November to 10 February: 15 days to the end of November, 31 for December, 31 for January and 10 into February, giving 87 days. Leap years only matter if your count goes past the end of February, because only then does the 29th of February come into it. A good check for long gaps is to estimate in months, with about 30 days each. 15 November to 10 February is a bit less than 3 months, about 90 days, and 87 days fits that estimate nicely.
