### subtopic_1 — Seconds and Stopwatches
**Assistant:** Why is the winner the one with the smallest time? Bigger numbers usually win.

**Tutor:** In most games a bigger score wins, like more goals or more points. But a race time is not a score; it measures how long a runner took to cover the distance. Everyone runs the same 100 metres, so the runner who covers it in the least time is the fastest. If Lerato takes 15 seconds and Sipho takes 18, Lerato was already at the finish line while Sipho still had 3 seconds of running left. So in races, swimming, cycling and anything where everyone does the same task against the clock, the smallest time wins. It helps to picture the finish: the first person across the line stops the watch earliest, so their time is smallest. There are other events where bigger wins even in athletics, like the long jump, where a longer distance is better, or a shot put throw. The question to ask is: what is being measured, and is more of it good or bad? For time to finish a task, less is better. For distance jumped or thrown, more is better. Reading the event carefully before comparing numbers will stop you from crowning the wrong winner.

### subtopic_2 — Reading a Calendar
**Assistant:** Why does February have fewer days than the other months, and what is a leap year for?

**Tutor:** The months come from a very old calendar made by the Romans, and February ended up with the fewest days long ago because of how they arranged their year; it was simply the month that was left short. The leap year has a much more practical reason. The Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to go once round the Sun. Our calendar year has 365 days, so each year it falls about a quarter of a day behind the Earth's journey. After 4 years, those four quarter days add up to about one whole day. To catch up, every fourth year gets an extra day, 29 February, making 366 days. Without leap years, the calendar would slowly drift, and after a few hundred years the hottest months in South Africa would no longer fall in December and January. Leap years are years that divide exactly by 4, such as 2024 and 2028. There are a few special exceptions for years ending in 00, which you will learn about later. And people born on 29 February have a real birthday only once every 4 years, so they usually celebrate on 28 February or 1 March in other years.

### subtopic_3 — Using a Calendar to Count and Plan
**Assistant:** Why should I count in sevens on a calendar? Counting day by day works.

**Tutor:** Counting day by day does work, but it is slow, and the more days you count, the easier it is to lose track and be off by one. Counting in sevens uses the shape of the calendar. Every week has exactly 7 days, so any date and the date 7 days later always fall on the same day of the week. If 1 March is a Sunday, then 8, 15, 22 and 29 March are all Sundays, and you can jump straight to the Sunday nearest your date. Then you only count a few days forward or back. To find 24 March, jump to Sunday 22 March and count 2 more days: Monday, Tuesday. So 24 March is a Tuesday. This is much quicker and much safer than counting 23 days one by one. It also works across months if you know how many days each month has. If 29 March is a Sunday and March has 31 days, then 31 March is a Tuesday and 1 April is a Wednesday, and 5 April is a Sunday again. Calendars are built on sevens, so thinking in sevens makes them easy. You already know the seven times table, so put it to work.
