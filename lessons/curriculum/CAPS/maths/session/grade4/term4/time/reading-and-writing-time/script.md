# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers reading, telling and writing time in 12-hour and 24-hour formats on analogue and digital instruments, in hours, minutes and seconds. The anchor is one day in the life of Nomsa, a Grade 4 learner in Pietermaritzburg, from her alarm at 6:15 in the morning to bedtime at 8:30 at night, with a kitchen wall clock, a digital microwave clock and a bus timetable. The governing idea is that an analogue clock shows hours with the short hand and minutes with the long hand, that a day has 24 hours split into a.m. and p.m. in the 12-hour way, and that the 24-hour way numbers the hours from 00 to 23 so that a.m. and p.m. are not needed. Reading analogue clocks to the minute, writing times in both formats and converting between them are examinable.

## Subtopic: Reading Analogue and Digital Clocks

An analogue clock has a face with the numbers 1 to 12 and hands that move round. The short hand is the hour hand and the long hand is the minute hand. Some clocks also have a thin, fast hand, the second hand. A digital clock shows the time in numbers, like 6:15, with the hours before the colon and the minutes after it.

To read the minutes on an analogue clock, count in fives. Each number on the face is 5 minutes more than the one before for the minute hand: 1 is 5 minutes, 2 is 10 minutes, 3 is 15 minutes, and so on to 12, which is 0 or 60 minutes. Between the numbers are small marks, each worth 1 minute. When the minute hand points 2 marks past the 4, it is 20 plus 2, which is 22 minutes past the hour.

To read the hour, look at the short hand. It moves slowly from one number to the next during the hour. If it is between 6 and 7, the hour is 6, because it has passed 6 but not yet reached 7. So when Nomsa's alarm rings with the hour hand just past 6 and the minute hand on the 3, the time is 6:15, a quarter past six.

We say times in words too. 6:15 is a quarter past six. 6:30 is half past six. 6:45 is a quarter to seven, because there are 15 minutes left until seven o'clock. 6:50 is ten to seven. After the half hour we usually say how many minutes to the next hour.

The questions for this section are with you now: the hour and minute hands, counting minutes in fives, reading the hour, and saying times past and to the hour.

## Subtopic: The 12-Hour Way: a.m. and p.m.

A day has 24 hours, but an analogue clock shows only 12. The hour hand goes round twice each day: once from midnight to midday and once from midday to midnight. To show which half of the day we mean, the 12-hour way adds a.m. or p.m.

a.m. means the time from midnight to just before midday: the night and the morning. p.m. means the time from midday to just before midnight: the afternoon and the evening. Nomsa wakes at 6:15 a.m., school starts at 7:45 a.m., school ends at 1:30 p.m., supper is at 6:00 p.m. and she goes to bed at 8:30 p.m.

Midday is 12:00 in the middle of the day, also called noon. Midnight is 12:00 in the middle of the night, when one day ends and the next begins. These two times are where a.m. changes to p.m. and p.m. changes to a.m., so it is clearer to write 12 noon and 12 midnight than to add a.m. or p.m.

Without a.m. or p.m., a 12-hour time can be confusing. Meet me at 7:00 could mean seven in the morning or seven in the evening. Adding a.m. or p.m. removes the doubt.

The questions for this section are with you now: why the hour hand goes round twice a day, what a.m. and p.m. mean, and noon and midnight.

## Subtopic: The 24-Hour Way

The 24-hour way numbers every hour of the day from 00 to 23, so a.m. and p.m. are not needed. It is used on bus and train timetables, airline tickets, phones and many digital clocks. Times are written with four digits: two for the hour and two for the minutes, like 06:15 or 18:00.

Morning times look almost the same in both ways. 6:15 a.m. is 06:15, and 11:20 a.m. is 11:20. Midday is 12:00. For afternoon and evening times, add 12 to the hour. 1:30 p.m. is 13:30, because 1 plus 12 is 13. 6:00 p.m. is 18:00. 8:30 p.m. is 20:30.

To change a 24-hour time back, look at the hour. If it is 13 or more, take away 12 and write p.m. 15:45 is 15 minus 12, which is 3, so 3:45 p.m. 22:10 is 10:10 p.m. If the hour is less than 12, write a.m. 09:05 is 9:05 a.m. 12:something is p.m. and 00:something is a.m.

Midnight is 00:00 in the 24-hour way, the start of a new day. One minute after midnight is 00:01. The last minute of the day is 23:59. A bus timetable that says the last bus leaves at 21:40 means 9:40 p.m.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: reading the hour as the number the short hand is closest to, even when it has not reached it; the hour is the number it has just passed. Two: reading the minute hand on the 9 as 9 minutes; count in fives, so the 9 is 45 minutes. Three: writing 1:30 p.m. as 01:30 in the 24-hour way; add 12 to afternoon hours, so it is 13:30. Four: writing 15:45 as 5:45 p.m.; take away 12, not 10, so it is 3:45 p.m.

The questions for this section are with you now: writing times with four digits, adding 12 for afternoon and evening times, and changing 24-hour times back to a.m. and p.m.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with Nomsa's day, the kitchen clock and the bus timetable — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Hands and Numbers

Short hand: hours. Long hand: minutes. Count the minutes in fives: the 3 is 15 minutes, the 6 is 30.

The hour is the number the short hand has just passed. Just past 6, minute hand on the 3: 6:15.

After half past, say minutes to the next hour. 6:45 is a quarter to seven.

Keep Nomsa's alarm clock in mind and try the questions for this part: reading clocks.

## Subtopic: Morning or Evening

The day has 24 hours. The clock goes round twice.

a.m. is midnight to midday. p.m. is midday to midnight. Wake up 6:15 a.m., bed 8:30 p.m.

Noon is the middle of the day. Midnight is the middle of the night.

Hold Nomsa's day in mind and try this part's questions: a.m. and p.m.

## Subtopic: Twenty-Four Hours

Count the hours all day from 00 to 23. No a.m. or p.m. needed.

Afternoon and evening: add 12. 1:30 p.m. is 13:30. 8:30 p.m. is 20:30.

Going back: 13 or more, take away 12 and say p.m. 15:45 is 3:45 p.m.

Pocket summary of the lesson. On an analogue clock the short hand shows the hour it has just passed and the long hand shows the minutes, counted in fives with small marks for single minutes. A day has 24 hours; the 12-hour way uses a.m. from midnight to midday and p.m. from midday to midnight. The 24-hour way numbers the hours from 00 to 23: add 12 to afternoon and evening hours, and take 12 away to change back, writing p.m. Midnight is 00:00.

Hold the bus timetable in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: the 24-hour way.
