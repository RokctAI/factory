### subtopic_1 — Changing Litres and Millilitres
**Assistant:** Changing litres and millilitres looks exactly like changing kilograms and grams. Is it really the same?

**Tutor:** Yes, the pattern is exactly the same, and that is no accident. In the metric system, the big unit is always 1 000 of the small unit for these measures. 1 kilogram is 1 000 grams, 1 kilometre is 1 000 metres and 1 litre is 1 000 millilitres. The word milli means one thousandth, so a millilitre is one thousandth of a litre, just as a millimetre is one thousandth of a metre. Because the pattern is the same, the skills you learned for mass work here too. To change litres to millilitres, multiply by 1 000. To change millilitres to litres, group them in thousands. Half of the big unit is 500 of the small unit, and a quarter is 250. Watch the zeros in the same way: 1 l 50 ml is 1 050 ml, just like 2 kg 30 g is 2 030 g. The only thing that changes is what you are measuring. Mass tells you how heavy something is, length tells you how long it is, and capacity tells you how much it can hold. So you do not have to learn three different sets of rules, only one pattern and three sets of units. That makes revising for the test much easier.

### subtopic_2 — Fractions of a Litre
**Assistant:** How can I work out how many cups a big container fills without doing a hard division?

**Tutor:** Use what you know about one litre. A measuring cup holds 250 ml, and 4 of those cups make 1 000 ml, which is 1 litre. So every litre fills 4 cups. Now a big container is easy. A 2 litre bottle fills 2 times 4, which is 8 cups. A 5 litre pot fills 5 times 4, which is 20 cups. A 10 litre bucket fills 40 cups. You never had to divide 5 000 by 250, which would be hard. You turned the problem into a small multiplication. You can check your answer by going the other way: 20 cups of 250 ml is 20 times 250, which is 5 000 ml, and that is exactly 5 litres. If the cups are a different size, find how many fill one litre first. Cups of 200 ml fill a litre 5 times, because 5 times 200 is 1 000, so a 3 litre jug fills 15 of them. Cups of 500 ml fill a litre 2 times. This idea of finding how many fit into one unit first is a powerful tool, and it works in many problems, not only with juice and soup.

### subtopic_3 — Solving Capacity Problems
**Assistant:** What if a problem has litres and millilitres mixed together? Which one do I work in?

**Tutor:** Usually it is easiest to change everything to millilitres first, calculate, and then change back at the end if the question asks for litres. Millilitres are whole numbers, so you can use all the adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing you already know. For example, the kitchen has 2 l 500 ml of orange juice and adds 750 ml of guava juice. In millilitres that is 2 500 plus 750, which is 3 250 ml. If the question wants litres and millilitres, group the thousands: 3 l 250 ml. If you try to keep the litres and millilitres separate, you can end up with an answer like 2 l 1 250 ml, which is not wrong, but it is not finished, because 1 250 ml contains another whole litre. Subtraction is even safer in millilitres. 5 l minus 1 l 800 ml is hard to do in parts, but 5 000 minus 1 800 is 3 200 ml, easy. Before you start, estimate: about two and a half litres plus nearly one litre should be about three and a half litres. If your answer is close to your estimate, you can trust it. Then always finish with the unit.
