### subtopic_1 — What Volume Is
**Assistant:** Why do I have to count cubes I can't see? They might not be there.

**Tutor:** In a drawing of a solid box or a packed stack, the hidden cubes are there; you just cannot see them from where you are looking. A drawing shows only the front, the top and one side, because paper is flat. Think of a real box of building blocks packed 4 long, 3 wide and 2 high. If you look at it from the front corner, you see the cubes on the top and on two sides, maybe 15 or so faces of cubes, but the box really holds 24 cubes. The ones in the middle of the bottom layer and at the back are hidden behind the others. If you counted only the visible ones, you would get the volume wrong. That is why counting in layers is so useful. Work out how many cubes are in one layer by counting the rows and the cubes in each row, even the rows you cannot fully see, because a packed layer is a full rectangle. Then multiply by the number of layers. Sometimes a question shows a stack with gaps or missing cubes; then read carefully and count what is actually there. But for a full box, every layer is complete, hidden cubes and all.

### subtopic_2 — Packing Boxes in Layers
**Assistant:** What is the difference between volume and capacity?

**Tutor:** They are very closely related. Volume is the amount of space an object takes up. A solid brick has a volume, even though you cannot put anything inside it. Capacity is how much a container can hold, like a jug, a bottle, a box or a bucket. For a container, its capacity is the volume of the space inside it. So a lunch box has a capacity, the space for your sandwiches, and that space can be measured by packing cubes or by filling it with something that pours, like water, sand or beans. In this lesson we measured volume by packing cubes and capacity by filling with cups. In the next lesson you will meet the proper units for capacity, millilitres and litres, used for liquids like milk, juice and water. You will see that a 1 litre bottle of water is the same as a box that holds 1 000 small centimetre cubes, which shows how volume and capacity link together. For now, remember: volume is space taken up or held, measured in cubic units; capacity is how much a container holds, often measured with cups or in litres.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing Volumes and Filling Containers
**Assistant:** How can different shaped boxes have the same volume?

**Tutor:** Because volume only counts how many cubes fit inside, not how they are arranged. Take 24 cubes and build boxes with them. You can make a box 4 long, 3 wide and 2 high: 12 cubes in a layer, 2 layers, 24 cubes. You can make one 6 long, 2 wide and 2 high: 12 in a layer, 2 layers, again 24. You can make a tall one, 2 long, 3 wide and 4 high: 6 in a layer, 4 layers, still 24. You can even make a long thin one, 24 long, 1 wide and 1 high. They all look different, but each holds exactly 24 cubes, so they all have the same volume. This is just like area, where 12 squares can make rectangles of different shapes. It matters in real life too. Juice cartons and cans can look very different but hold the same amount, because the makers choose a shape that fits the shelf or the hand while keeping the same volume. When you compare volumes, do not judge by height alone. A tall thin glass can hold less than a short wide one. Count the cubes or measure by filling, and let the numbers decide.
