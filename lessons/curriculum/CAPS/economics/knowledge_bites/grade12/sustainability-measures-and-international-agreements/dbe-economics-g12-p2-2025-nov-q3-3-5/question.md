## Question (4 marks)

Why do markets fail to ensure environmental sustainability? (2 x 2)

(Refers to the INTERNATIONAL PROTOCOLS AND AGREEMENTS ON ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY cartoon in Q3.3.1.)

## Method

explanation

The failures all trace back to prices and ownership. The air and the seas are common resources nobody owns, and most environmental resources carry no price — so there is no economic incentive to economise on them. Producers do not bear the external costs of pollution, so they overproduce goods with negative externalities, and the profit motive pushes firms to cut costs at the environment's expense. Any two of these chains, fully explained, earn the 4 marks.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- The market views the environment as a common resource where many parts of the environment are not privately owned, such as the air and the seas.
- Most of the resources provided by the environment do not have a price, so there is no economic incentive to economise on their use.
- Markets may cause environmental damage due to lack of knowledge even if they would like to be more environmentally friendly in their activities.
- Consumers and businesses show carelessness as they continue with various harmful practices and leave future generations to worry about their environmental consequences.
- Businesses overproduce goods that cause negative externalities because they do not bear the external costs which result in environmental degradation.
- Markets are driven by the profit motive and they may reduce their cost of production at the expense of the environment.
