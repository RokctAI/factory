## Question (8 marks)

Briefly discuss specialisation and innovation as arguments in favour of free trade. (2 x 4)

## Method

discussion

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: two headings, four marks each. Specialisation: comparative advantage shows that world output and consumption rise when each country specialises where its opportunity cost is lowest; resources are used efficiently, waste is prevented and living standards rise as more goods and services are traded. Innovation: free trade intensifies competition, which is a powerful incentive to innovate — introducing new products for export markets, finding production methods that cut costs and lift quality, and continuously adopting the latest technology.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

**Specialisation**

- Comparative advantage shows that world output can be increased if countries specialise in what they are relatively best at producing.
- If countries specialise the goods for which they have a comparative advantage world trade and consumption will be maximised.
- The effect of increasing living standards results in the trading of more goods and services.
- Allows industries that have a comparative advantage with a lower opportunity cost.
- Specialisation increases economic efficiency and prevents the wasting of resources.
- Each country that is best suited for specific goods and services will engage in producing more of those goods or services.

**Innovation**

- Free trade leads to increased competition, which provides a powerful incentive for innovation.
- Free trade allows countries to introduce new products that can be exported to foreign markets
- Enterprises compete to find production methods that cut costs and improve quality and reliability of goods.
- Competition on international markets may force businesses to implement the latest technology and continuously improve their systems.
