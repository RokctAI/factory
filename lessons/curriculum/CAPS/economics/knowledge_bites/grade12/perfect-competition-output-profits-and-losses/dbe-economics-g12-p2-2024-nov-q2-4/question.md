## Question (8 marks)

With the aid of a correctly labelled graph, explain the relationship between total cost (TC), fixed cost (FC) and variable cost (VC). (2 x 4)

## Method

graph drawing explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: draw cost on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis. Fixed cost does not change with output, so FC is a horizontal line. Variable cost starts at zero — with no output there is no variable cost — and slopes upwards. Total cost is simply fixed cost plus variable cost, so the TC curve runs parallel to the VC curve but starts higher up, at the FC intercept on the vertical axis; the constant vertical gap between TC and VC is exactly the fixed cost. Marks: labelling of the axes = 1; correct drawing and labelling of the TC curve = 1; of the VC curve = 1; of the FC curve = 1 (max 4 for the graph, max 4 for the explanation).

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Graph: Cost on the vertical axis, Quantity on the horizontal axis; a horizontal FC line; a VC curve starting from the origin and sloping upwards; a TC curve parallel to VC starting from the FC intercept on the vertical axis.

Explanation (Max 4):

- Fixed cost (FC) is the difference between the total cost (TC) and the variable cost (VC) / Variable cost is the difference between the total cost and fixed cost / Total cost is the sum of fixed cost and variable cost
- The variable cost curve starts from zero (0) while the total cost curve starts from the fixed cost level on the vertical axis
- Total cost and variable cost curves slope upwards as quantity increases.
- The gap between the total cost curve and the variable cost curve represents the value of fixed cost
