## Question (8 marks)

With the aid of a correctly labelled graph, explain economic loss for a perfectly competitive firm.

## Method

graph drawing explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: draw price, cost and revenue on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis. Sketch a U-shaped average cost (AC) curve and a marginal cost (MC) curve rising through AC from below. Because the firm is a price-taker, its demand line is horizontal: D = AR = MR, drawn at the market price P (R10). The firm minimises loss where MC = MR — point e, at quantity Q (100 units). Now look straight up from e to the AC curve: point a shows the average cost P1 (R12), which sits above the price. Since average revenue (R10) is below average cost (R12), the firm makes an economic loss — the rectangle between the price line and the average cost level over the 100 units. Check the size of the loss: TR - TC = (P x Q) - (AC x Q) = (10 x 100) - (12 x 100) = R1 000 - R1 200 = -R200.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Graph: axes 'Price, Cost, Revenue' (vertical) and 'Quantity' (horizontal); a U-shaped AC curve and a rising MC curve cutting AC from below; a horizontal line at P/R10 labelled D = AR = MR; the loss-minimising point e where MC = MR at quantity Q/100; point a directly above e on the AC curve corresponds to P1/R12 on the price axis; the rectangle between P/R10 and P1/R12 over the quantity Q/100 is labelled 'Economic loss'.

Allocation of marks (Max 4): Correct labelling of the axes = 1 Mark; Correct drawing and labelling of cost curves = 1 Mark; Correct positioning and labelling of revenue curves = 1 Mark; Correct indication of economic loss = 1 Mark.

Explanation (Max 4):

- A firm minimises loss at point e, where MR = MC / At point e, the firm produces 100 units at the price of P/R10.
- The firm will make economic loss because the average revenue of (P/R10) is less than the average cost of (P1/R12).
- The firm makes total economic loss of TR (P x Q) - TC (AC x Q) = (10 x 100) - (12 x 100) = R1 000 - R1 200 = -R200.
