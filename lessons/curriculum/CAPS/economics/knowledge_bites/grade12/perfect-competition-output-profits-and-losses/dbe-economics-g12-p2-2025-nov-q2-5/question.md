## Question (8 marks)

Why would a firm in a perfect market always make normal profit in the long run? (4 x 2)

## Method

discussion

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: the driver is free entry and exit. Tell the story from both sides. If existing firms make economic profit in the short run, the absence of entry barriers lets new firms in; market supply rises, price falls, and profits shrink until average revenue equals average cost (AR = AC, or TR = TC) — normal profit. If existing firms make economic loss, some exit; market supply falls, price rises, and losses shrink until the survivors again make normal profit. In long-run equilibrium there is no incentive left to enter or leave, so normal profit persists. Four fully explained links earn the 8 marks. **Marking note: a maximum of 2 marks may be allocated for mere listing of facts/examples.**

**Note: the official memo's final bullet reads 'Normal profit in the long-run will remain as there is incentive to enter or leave the market' — contextually this appears to be a misprint for 'no incentive to enter or leave the market'. The memo wording is reproduced verbatim in the answer below.**

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- The absence of barriers to entry allows new businesses to enter the market when the existing firms make economic profit in the short-run.
- New entrants/businesses may compete for the economic profit until the existing firms earn normal profit in the long-run.
- An increase in the number of businesses in a perfect market may increase the market supply resulting in a decrease in prices.
- Profits will decline up to a point where average revenue is equal to average cost or total revenue is equal to total cost (AR = AC or TR = TC).
- Some existing businesses may be forced to exit/leave the market when they make economic loss in the short-run.
- A decrease in the number of businesses will reduce market supply which results in an increase in price.
- The economic loss will be reduced until all the firms make normal profit in the long-run.
- Normal profit in the long-run will remain as there is incentive to enter or leave the market.
