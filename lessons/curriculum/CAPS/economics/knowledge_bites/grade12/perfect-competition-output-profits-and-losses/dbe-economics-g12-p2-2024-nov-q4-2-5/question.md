## Question (4 marks)

Use the graphs to explain how economic loss in the short run leads to normal profit in the long run. (2 x 2)

(Two side-by-side graphs of a perfectly competitive market. The INDUSTRY/MARKET graph shows demand curve D1 with supply curves S1 and S2, where S2 lies to the left of S1; equilibrium E1 at price P1 and quantity Q1, and equilibrium E2 at higher price P2 and lower quantity Q2. The INDIVIDUAL FIRM graph shows U-shaped MC and AC curves with horizontal lines D1 = AR1 = MR1 at price P1, sitting below the AC minimum and showing an economic loss, and D2 = AR2 = MR2 at the higher price P2, tangent to AC at its minimum at point E2.)

## Method

graph interpretation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: read the story across both graphs. At price P1 the firm's price line lies below the bottom of its average cost curve, so every firm makes an economic loss. Losses drive some firms out; on the market graph that exit shifts supply left from S1 to S2. Less supply pushes the market price up from P1 to P2. At P2 the price line just touches the AC curve at its minimum — price equals average cost — so the surviving firms earn exactly normal profit, and the exit stops.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- Economic loss forces some businesses to exit the market and the market supply curve shifts to the left from S1 to S2
- The decrease in supply will result in an increase in market price from P1 to P2 which leads to a decrease in losses
- In the long-run the remaining businesses will make normal profit when the price, P2 will be equal to the average cost
