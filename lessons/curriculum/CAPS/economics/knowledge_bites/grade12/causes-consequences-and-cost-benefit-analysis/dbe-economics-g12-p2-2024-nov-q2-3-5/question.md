## Question (4 marks)

How can the government encourage the consumption of merit goods? (2 x 2)

(Refers to a graph titled POSITIVE EXTERNALITIES OF EDUCATION: price on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis; an upward-sloping supply curve S; demand curve D cuts S at price P and quantity Q; a second demand curve D1, further to the right, cuts S at the higher price P1 and larger quantity Q1.)

## Method

explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: merit goods, such as education and healthcare, are under-consumed because buyers ignore the external benefits — that is why the socially efficient demand curve D1 lies to the right of the private demand curve D. Any valid answer therefore names a government action that shifts consumption towards Q1, and then explains how it raises demand or affordability. Give TWO such measures, each with a brief explanation, for 2 marks each.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- The government can provide consumer subsidies to lower the prices of merit goods which will increase demand
- The government can provide tax exemptions which allow merit goods to be more affordable to consumers
- The government may provide merit goods, such as education and healthcare, free-of-charge or at a lower cost which will encourage people to buy more quantities
- Public positive campaigns may be used to inform the public about the benefits of merit goods to increase demand
