## Question (8 marks)

With the aid of a correctly labelled graph, explain the effect of maximum prices on the market. (2 x 4)

## Method

graph drawing explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: start with an ordinary demand-and-supply diagram in equilibrium at price P and quantity Q. A maximum price is a legal ceiling, so to have any effect it must sit BELOW equilibrium — draw a horizontal line at P1 under P. At that lower price, consumers want more (quantity demanded rises to Q2) while producers offer less (quantity supplied falls to Q1). The horizontal gap between Q1 and Q2 is the shortage, and shortages invite black markets where goods change hands above the legal price. Marks: correct labelling of the axes = 1; correct drawing and labelling of the demand and supply curves = 1; correct indication or labelling of the maximum price = 1; correct labelling on the axes = 1 (max 4 for the graph, max 4 for the explanation).

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Graph titled MAXIMUM PRICES: PRICE against QUANTITY; downward-sloping demand curve D and upward-sloping supply curve S intersecting at equilibrium price P and quantity Q; a horizontal maximum price line at P1 below P; at P1 quantity supplied is Q1 and quantity demanded is Q2, with the gap between Q1 and Q2 labelled 'Shortage'.

Explanation (Max 4):

- The government sets a maximum price of P1 below the equilibrium market price P to allow consumers to afford basic products
- The quantity demanded increases from Q to Q2 and the quantity supplied decreases from Q to Q1.
- A shortage of Q1 to Q2 is created in the market, as demand is more than supply.
- Product shortages can lead to the creation of black markets, where the goods may be sold at higher prices
