## Question (2 marks)

Why do markets undersupply goods that generate positive externalities?

(Refers to the IMPERFECT DISTRIBUTION OF INCOME AND WEALTH cartoon in Q4.2.1.)

## Method

explanation

A positive externality means part of the benefit goes to society, not the buyer or seller. Because businesses are not paid for that external benefit, goods like education look less profitable than they really are to society — so the market produces too little of them.

(see marking guidelines)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- Goods that generate positive externalities, such as education, are often not highly rated by the market which leads to undersupply.
- Businesses do not fully account for the benefits that accrue to the society, as a result they may not be incentivised to produce enough of the goods leading to undersupply.
- Goods with positive externalities are not profitable because businesses are not rewarded for the external benefits.
