## Question (8 marks)

Analyse the measures that can be used to combat cost-push inflation. (4 x 2)

## Method

analysis

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: cost-push inflation comes from rising production costs, so every valid measure must lower costs or restrain the cost-price spiral. Group them: raise productivity (education, training, technology), lower input and operating costs (subsidies, cheaper imported inputs, efficient energy and transport infrastructure, lower business taxes), restrain the spiral (wage policy, price controls) and sharpen competition (domestic and foreign competition, small business development). Give FOUR measures, each linked to how it reduces costs, for 2 marks each. **Marking note: a maximum of 2 marks may be allocated for mere listing of facts/examples.**

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- Improvement of education and training to increase productivity which helps to reduce the average cost of production and increase aggregate supply
- Investing in technology to improve productivity which will reduce the cost of production
- Promoting competition domestically and from abroad which helps to control high profit margins by businesses
- Imposing price controls, such as maximum prices, on certain goods and services to ensure that they remain affordable
- Implementing a wage policy to break the inflationary spiral of increased wages and prices
- Reducing import controls to make imported production inputs, such agricultural chemical, cheaper and reduce cost of production
- Improve the efficiency of infrastructural services, such energy and transport, to reduce the cost of doing business in the economy
- Providing subsidies to producers of certain goods and services to reduce their cost of production
- Promoting small business development to increase competition and increase the supply of goods and services
- Reducing taxes paid by business to reduce their cost of production which allows them to produce output
