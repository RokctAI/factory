## Question (2 marks)

How can the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) use open-market transactions to reduce money supply in the economy? (1 x 2)

## Method

explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: in an open-market transaction the SARB trades government securities with banks. To REDUCE the money supply it must pull money out of circulation, so it SELLS securities — the banks pay for them, and that payment drains money from the banking system. (Buying securities would do the opposite and inject money.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) can sell government securities or bonds to commercial banks which will reduce the amount of money in circulation
