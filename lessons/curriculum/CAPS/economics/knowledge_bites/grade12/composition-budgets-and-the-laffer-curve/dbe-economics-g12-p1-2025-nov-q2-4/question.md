## Question (8 marks)

Briefly discuss full employment and economic equity as macroeconomic objectives of the government. (2 x 4)

## Method

discussion

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: structure the discussion under two headings, four marks each. For full employment, define it first (everyone who is looking for work is employed), then explain how the government pursues it over the short term (direct employment schemes and youth employment subsidies), the medium term (supporting labour-intensive sectors such as agriculture and offering investment incentives in industries that employ many people) and the long term (supporting knowledge- and capital-intensive sectors and improving education and training). For economic equity, define it (the economy's resources equally distributed amongst the population), anchor it in the Gini coefficient (below 0,5 signals a more equal distribution; South Africa's is above 0,6), then explain the redistribution tools — progressive taxation, where higher earners pay higher rates, and social spending such as free basic education, health care, grants and subsidies.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

**Full employment**

- Full employment is when all people who are looking for jobs get employed.
- The aim of the government is to create decent and sustainable jobs.
- Employment rate is measured as the percentage of the economically active population (EAP) that is employed.
- In the short-term, employment can be increased by using direct employment schemes and subsidies such as youth employment subsidies.
- In the medium term, government can support labour intensive activities especially in agricultural sector and provide investment incentives to businesses in industries that employ many people such as the manufacturing industry.
- In the long term, the government can support knowledge intensive and capital-intensive sectors in order to remain competitive or improve the quality of education and training to increase the employability of the future labour force.

(Accept any other correct relevant response)

**Economic equity**

- Economic equity refers to a situation whereby the resources of the economy are equally distributed amongst the population.
- Income is more equally distributed when the Gini-coefficient is less than 0,5.
- The distribution of income in the economy is not fair, as some people earn a large amount of money while others earn less.
- In SA the income gap is still high as evidenced by the Gini-coefficient of above 0,6.
- Through the progressive tax system, the government can redistribute income.
- Progressive taxation means that higher income earners pay higher tax rates.
- The government can help the poor with free basic education, free basic health-care, social grants, free services and subsidies.
