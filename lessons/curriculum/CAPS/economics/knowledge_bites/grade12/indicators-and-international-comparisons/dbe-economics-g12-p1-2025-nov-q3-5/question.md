## Question (8 marks)

How can changes in the various economic indicators be used to show improvement in the performance of the economy? (4 x 2)

## Method

discussion

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines)

Teachable steps: choose any four indicators and, for two marks each, state the direction of change and explain why it signals improvement: a falling CPI (lower cost of living lifts demand); a falling PPI (lower production costs encourage output); improving terms of trade (more export income to finance imports, a healthier current account); a higher employment rate (more spending, tax revenue and growth); higher labour productivity (efficiency and competitiveness); interest-rate movements (lower rates stimulate spending and investment, higher rates curb inflation and attract foreign investment); exchange-rate movements (a weaker rand makes exports more competitive, a stronger rand cheapens imports and reduces inflation); or growing money supply (more liquidity and credit for short-term spending).

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- A decrease in the Consumer Price Index (CPI) shows lower cost of living in the economy which triggers an increase in demand for goods and services.
- A decline in the Producer Price Index (PPI) indicates lower cost of production and encourages the production of more goods and services.
- An improvement in the terms of trade means that South Africa gets more income for exports which is used to finance import expenditure, strengthening economic stability in the short term.
- An improved terms of trade may reflect a surplus in the current account which serves as a yardstick for a healthy economy.
- A higher employment rate reflects economic stability which boosts consumer spending, tax revenues, and economic growth.
- Higher labour productivity indicates enhanced economic efficiency, competitiveness, and GDP growth.
- Lower interest rates encourage spending and investment to boost economic activity.
- Higher interest rates curb inflation and attracts foreign investment which will lead to an increase in economic growth.
- A weaker exchange rate means results in exports becoming cheaper and more competitive in the global market which improves the trade balance of the current account.
- A stronger exchange rate shows that the country will pay less for imports which reduces inflation.
- An increase in money supply (M1, M2 and M3) reflects higher liquidity, expanding credit availability and boosting short-term spending.
