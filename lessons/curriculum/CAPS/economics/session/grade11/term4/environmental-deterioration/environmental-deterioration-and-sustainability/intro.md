Today we treat the environment the way economists must: as scarce, valuable and dangerously easy to destroy for free. We diagnose environmental deterioration as a precise market failure — externalities, the tragedy of the commons, and future generations who place no orders — then open the four-family policy toolbox governments use to fix it, from emission rules to the carbon tax. We define sustainability properly through the Brundtland definition and the three-legged tripod, and bring it all home to South Africa: a coal-dependent polluter, a climate victim of Day Zero and the Durban floods, and the site of one of the world's most watched energy transitions. Here, sustainability is not green sentiment but economic survival.
