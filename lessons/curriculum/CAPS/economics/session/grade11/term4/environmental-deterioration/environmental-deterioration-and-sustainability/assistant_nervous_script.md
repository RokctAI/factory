Hey, Bianca here — externalities, commons, carbon taxes, three-legged stools... if that felt heavy, good news: this whole topic runs on three small stories you already know.

Story one: the smoke nobody pays for. A factory's smoke lands on a village that was never part of the deal — so the product is priced too cheap and the market makes too much of it. That's the externality, and it's the exam's favourite word in this topic.

Story two: the river nobody owns. Everyone takes one more fish because the loss is shared — and the river empties. What belongs to everyone is cared for by no one.

Story three: the seed mealies. Eat the harvest, never the seed. Use nature slower than it renews, and turn once-off wealth like coal into things that last.

Every tool in the toolbox is just a repair for one of those stories: rules and taxes send the missing bill for the smoke; owners and quotas save the river; the sustainability tripod — nature, economy, fairness — guards the seed.

And the South African evidence? You lived it: Day Zero, the Durban floods, load shedding's coal problem. No memorising needed — just citing.

Three stories, four fixes, evidence from home. Take the practice questions now and match each one to its story — they'll fall over one by one.
