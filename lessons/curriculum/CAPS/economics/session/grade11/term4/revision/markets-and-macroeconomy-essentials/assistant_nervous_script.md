Hey, Bianca here — systems, factors, aggregates, elasticity, marginal cost AND two policies in one sitting? Deep breath. It folds down to four keys.

Key one: who reads the till. Free market — the till decides. Planned — the memo decides, and the memo doesn't know what the till knows. Mixed — both, which is everyone. South Africa adds the twist: dualistic, Sandton beside the paraffin stove. And the four pay packets: rent, wages, interest, profit — with profit the leftover, because someone has to carry the risk.

Key two: one slip, three adding machines. Value added, factor incomes, final spending — three methods, one GDP, and they must agree. Remember I is machines and roads, not shares; G is nurses' salaries, not grants. Then the map: ground, factory, counter — with the counter now two-thirds of our slip, and the whole slip running on wires and pipes. Load-shedding is your ready-made example for everything.

Key three: the stretch and the stop. Elasticity is just the bounce test — rubber bands versus planks — and it predicts the till before the price moves. Then the stopping rule: cook plates while the next one costs less than it earns, stop where marginal revenue meets marginal cost. Every table question is that rule wearing numbers.

Key four: two drivers. Reserve Bank, repo pedal, inflation target, fast. Treasury, budget ship, growth and jobs, slow. Never swap their tools in an answer.

Till, slip, stretch-and-stop, drivers. Four keys, and the practice questions are four locks — go open them.
