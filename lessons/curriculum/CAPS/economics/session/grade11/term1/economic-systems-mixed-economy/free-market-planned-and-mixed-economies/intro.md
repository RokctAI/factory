Today's topic is economic systems: the machinery a country uses to answer the three questions every society faces — what to produce, how, and for whom. We compare the free market, where the price mechanism co-ordinates without instruction, against the centrally planned economy, where a planning authority decides — judging each honestly by its strengths and its mechanisms of failure. Then we place South Africa precisely: a mixed economy, with markets, state-owned enterprises, regulators and substantial redistribution, and a dualistic one, where a modern formal economy and a survivalist informal economy live side by side. Examiners reward the candidate who explains dualism by its structural causes rather than merely describing it.
