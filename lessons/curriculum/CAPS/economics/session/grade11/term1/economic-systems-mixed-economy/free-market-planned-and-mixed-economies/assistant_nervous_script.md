Hey, it's Bianca — this topic has a reputation for being the "just write an essay" one, and that is exactly why people lose marks on it. It feels like opinion, so learners write opinions, and opinions do not score. Let me show you the skeleton hiding underneath.

There are only three questions in this entire topic: what, how, for whom. Three systems, three questions — that is a nine-block grid, and if you can fill in that grid you can answer almost anything they ask. Market: consumers decide what, producers decide how by choosing the cheapest method, income decides for whom. Plan: the planning authority decides all three. Mixed: markets decide, then government corrects and redistributes.

The second thing that saves you is the word "mechanism". Never write "the free market is unfair" — write "for whom is answered by ability to pay, so people without assets or skills receive little". Never write "planning does not work" — write "no central office can know what millions of prices know, and administered prices cannot adjust, so shortages persist". The mechanism is where the marks are. Sentiment is where the marks are not.

Two words also get mixed up constantly, so pin them now. MIXED means market plus government. DUALISTIC means formal economy plus informal economy. South Africa is both, and they are not the same claim. And when you explain dualism, give causes — concentrated ownership, distance from work, unequal schooling, no collateral — not a list of differences.

Concrete habit for the exam: before you write a single word of an economic systems question, draw the three-by-three grid in the margin. Nine boxes, a few words each. Then write your answer straight off the grid. It takes almost no time and it stops you drifting into an opinion piece.

Nine boxes, one word — mechanism — and two definitions kept apart. You've got this!
