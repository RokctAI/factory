Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic looks like two topics glued together. Half of it is four factors with four fancy incomes, and the other half is acronyms: IDP, LED, B-BBEE, PPPFA. If your brain filed that under "too much", that is a completely fair reaction. Let's shrink it.

The first half is honestly a matching exercise. Land earns rent. Labour earns wages. Capital earns interest. Enterprise earns profit. Four and four. Say it in the taxi order if that helps — rank, driver, kombi, owner — and the pairing sticks. Then only two ideas sit on top: money is not capital (the kombi is), and profit is a leftover, not a salary.

The calculation part is smaller than it looks too. Value added is just sales minus the stuff you bought in from other businesses. Then you hand out wages, rent, interest and depreciation, and whatever survives is profit. If your five pieces add back to value added, you are right. That add-back is your free marking check — use it every single time.

The second half is one sentence with examples hanging off it: South Africa built systems to widen who can reach the factors at all. The plan is the IDP. The barriers are collateral, information, transport and networks. The voice is community participation. The money is empowerment procurement — most tender points for price, a fixed handful for transformation. That is the shape; the acronyms just label the parts.

The habit that protects your marks here: never write a barrier or a policy without naming its mechanism. Not "people are poor" but "no registered title means no collateral, so no loan". Markers pay for the mechanism, not the sentiment.

Four factors, four rewards, one add-back check, and one chain from plan to tender. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
