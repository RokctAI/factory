Hey, Bianca here — sectors, percentages, infrastructure, access... if that felt like three lessons stacked into one, take a breath. It folds down small.

The whole topic is really two questions. Question one: who makes what? Answer with the chain — take it from nature, make it into goods, serve it to people. Primary, secondary, tertiary. Any business you ever meet is doing one of those three verbs, so just ask which verb the sentence describes.

Question two: what does everything ride on? Wires, roads, rails, pipes, schools, clinics — infrastructure. And the one idea to hold: when shared infrastructure breaks, everyone pays at once, and when it never reached you in the first place, you pay most of all. That's the whole access-and-opportunity argument in one line.

The numbers aren't a memory test either — you only need the shape: services about two thirds, factories about a fifth, mines and farms small but precious, and household electricity around ninety percent. Rough and confident beats precise and panicked.

Go do the practice questions now — verb first, chain second, and you'll be surprised how many answer themselves.
