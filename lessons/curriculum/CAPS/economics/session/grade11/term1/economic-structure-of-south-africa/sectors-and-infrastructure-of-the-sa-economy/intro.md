Today we map the economic structure of South Africa: the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors that produce everything the country makes, the rough GDP shares that reveal a services economy with famous mines, and the century-long shift from primary through secondary to tertiary — including the premature deindustrialisation examiners expect you to name. From there we turn to infrastructure, the economy's skeleton, separating economic from social infrastructure and weighing South Africa's strengths against the strain of load shedding and freight bottlenecks. We close with service access, because the argument that earns full marks runs: access converts infrastructure into opportunity, so unequal access means unequal opportunity.
