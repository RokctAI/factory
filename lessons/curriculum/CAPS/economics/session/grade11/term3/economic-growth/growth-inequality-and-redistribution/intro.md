Today we take on the two questions every economy must answer together: how do we make the total bigger, and how is the total shared? That is economic growth measured honestly in real, per-capita terms on one side, and the distribution of income and wealth on the other — with the Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient as the measuring instruments, and redistribution methods from progressive tax to social grants as the policy response. South Africa is the world's sharpest case study of all four, from a decade of growth below population growth to a Gini of about nought comma six three, and the exam knows it. By the end you will draw the sag, number it, and argue growth and redistribution as complements, not substitutes.
