Hey, Bianca here — Lorenz curves, Gini numbers, per capita this and real that... if your head is spinning, let me hand you the two pictures that carry the whole topic.

Picture one: the pot. Growth just asks — is there more food in the pot this year, really? Strip out price rises, then divide by the number of people eating. Real, then per person. Two moves, done.

Picture two: the queue. Line up a hundred people, poorest to richest, and count coins as you walk. Equal sharing counts one coin per person — the straight line. South Africa's count crawls at ten coins past person sixty, then explodes at the end — the sagging curve. The Gini is just how bad the sag is, from zero to one, and ours is about zero comma six three.

Everything else in the lesson is bolted onto those two pictures. Redistribution is moving coins along the queue. The growth debate is about keeping the pot growing while you move them.

The numbers you need fit on one hand: growth about one percent, population about one and a half, Gini zero comma six three, grants about twenty-eight million, top tax rate forty-five percent. Five numbers, two pictures.

Now go at the practice questions — start each one by asking: is this a pot question or a queue question? Half the battle ends right there.
