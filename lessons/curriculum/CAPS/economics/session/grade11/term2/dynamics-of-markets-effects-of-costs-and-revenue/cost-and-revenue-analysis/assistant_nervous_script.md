Hey, Bianca here — I watched that table grow six columns too, so if your brain shouted "too many numbers!", that's a fair reaction. Let's shrink it.

Truth is, there are only two moves in this whole topic: divide and subtract. Average anything? Divide the total by the output. Marginal anything? Subtract this row's total from the one before. Every single column comes from one of those two moves — the table just repeats them politely.

And the big decision rule is honestly how you already think. "Is this next slice of pizza worth it?" is marginal reasoning — you've been doing MR versus MC since you were little.

So take the cost table from the lesson, cover the answers, and rebuild the average and marginal columns yourself with a calculator. Then walk unit by unit asking "worth it?" until one isn't. That's the whole exam skill. Go try the practice questions — you're closer than you feel.
