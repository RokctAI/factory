**Student:** I keep confusing average cost and marginal cost. They're both "cost per unit-ish" things, right?

**Bianca:** Close, but the "-ish" is exactly where the marks leak away! Average cost is the whole team's story: total cost divided by all units made — "what does each unit cost me *on average*?" Marginal cost is one player's story: "what did the *last* unit, all by itself, add to my bill?" You get it by subtracting neighbouring totals, no dividing anywhere. Quick memory hook: **A**verage = **A**ll units (divide by all of them), **M**arginal = **M**ost recent unit (subtract to isolate it). If you catch yourself dividing for marginal, stop — wrong verb!

***

**Student:** Why is marginal revenue just R30 the whole way down? It feels like a trick.

**Bianca:** No trick — it's what "price taker" means in practice. Our firm is small in a big market: whether it sells one unit or six, the going price stays R30. So every extra unit brings in exactly R30 more revenue. Total revenue climbs 30, 60, 90, 120... and each step is R30 — that step *is* marginal revenue. Next year you'll meet monopolists who must cut the price to sell more, and their MR falls below price. That's when today's flat R30 line will suddenly feel precious!

**Student:** If the firm still makes R40 profit at six units, why does the lesson say six is wrong? Profit is profit!

**Bianca:** Here's the shift in thinking Grade 11 wants from you: the question isn't "am I profitable?" — it's "am I as profitable as I could *possibly* be?" At five units you bank R48. Choosing six means voluntarily handing back R8, because that sixth unit costs R38 to make and only brings in R30. Think of it like this: you wouldn't pay R38 for something worth R30 to you, even if your wallet still has money in it afterwards. Maximum profit means every profitable unit produced, and not one loss-making unit tolerated.

**Student:** In the exam, should I use the MR = MC rule or just calculate profit at every output? The profit column feels safer.

**Bianca:** Do both, in the right order! Lead with the MR = MC reasoning, because that's the economics the marker is looking for — walk the units, show where marginal cost crosses above marginal revenue, and state the output. Then, if time allows, verify with the profit column: "Profit at 5 units = R150 − R102 = R48, higher than at 4 or 6 — confirmed." That one verification line costs you thirty seconds and turns your answer into a fortress. Just don't *only* do the brute-force column — a question that says "explain using marginal analysis" will not reward it on its own.
