This whole lesson is three pocket rules. Rule one: rivals move together, partners move apart. Chicken up, beef demand up — rivals. Braai packs up, charcoal demand down — partners. Before anything else, just ask: same job, or job done together?

Rule two: every job hides inside something somebody buys. Houses first, bricklayers second. Find the product, and the factor market makes sense on its own.

Rule three: count the sellers and test the door. Many sellers and an open door — no price power. One seller and a locked door — full price power. Everything in between is just rungs on that ladder.

And the shift-versus-slide thing that trips everyone: check whose price changed. Own price — slide along the curve. Anyone else's price — the whole curve steps sideways.

That's it. Three rules and one check. Open the practice questions and run each one through the pockets — you'll be faster than you think.
