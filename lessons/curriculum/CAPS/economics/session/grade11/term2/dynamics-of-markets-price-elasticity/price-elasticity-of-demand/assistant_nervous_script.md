Hey, Bianca here — formulas, five categories, determinants, revenue rules... if elasticity feels like a lot of machinery for one idea, that's because it IS one idea wearing four outfits.

The one idea: when the price moves, how much do buyers move? That's it. Planks barely move — bread, taxi fare, airtime. Rubber bands move a lot — chocolate, one brand of cooldrink, anything with an easy escape route.

The number is just that idea measured: buying-change percent divided by price-change percent. Under one, plank. Over one, rubber band. And percentage change always divides by the original number — the number things started at. That single habit rescues most of the marks.

The revenue rule? You don't have to memorise it blind — rebuild it from Mama Dee's shop. Raise the airtime price: everyone still buys, till total up. Raise the chocolate price: buyers scatter, till total down. Plank, push price up. Rubber band, don't.

So grab the practice questions and do the taxi one and the chocolate one with your own calculator, slowly, once. After that the whole topic is just the same five lines of working again and again. You've got this.
