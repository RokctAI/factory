Hey, it's Thandi — scarcity, shortage, opportunity cost, three questions, three systems, externalities... that's a lot of vocabulary for one lesson, but here's the secret: it's all ONE story told in stages. Not enough → so choose → and every choice costs its runner-up → and whole societies face the same three choices → answered differently by different systems → with people and the planet feeling the results.

The two spots where marks leak, and their fixes. Leak one: scarcity versus shortage. Ask: can the delivery van fix it? Van fixes it — shortage. Nothing fixes it — scarcity. Leak two: opportunity cost in RANDS. The examiner wants the runner-up ITSELF — "one data bundle", "a day of revision" — never "R120". Real things, not money; the money was leaving either way.

The three questions — what, how, for whom — are just a family planning supper: what do we cook, do we cook it ourselves or buy it made, and who gets the biggest plate? Systems are who decides: the market (prices), the state (planners), or both (us — mixed).

And the rights-and-environment part isn't extra reading — it's the two-column habit: every scenario gets a promoting column and a violating column, both filled, fixes named. Balanced answers, full marks.

One story, two little tests, one two-column habit. That's the whole lesson. You've got this!
