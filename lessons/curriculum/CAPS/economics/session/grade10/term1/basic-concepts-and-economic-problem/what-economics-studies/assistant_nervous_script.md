Hey, it's Thandi — first Economics lesson of the year, and it came with a dictionary's worth of terms: elements, branches, methods, positive, normative, ceteris paribus... If your head is spinning, hold onto this: today had NO calculations and only one real idea — not enough for everybody — plus a set of name tags.

The name tags sort themselves with little tests you already passed. Micro or macro? Point at your street: if you can point at it — one shop, one price — micro; if you need a drone — inflation, unemployment — macro. Positive or normative? Is it a measurement or a wish? "Bread costs R18" measures; "bread costs too much" wishes. Factors and rewards? The four contributions each get their own pay: land rents, hands earn wages, machine-money earns interest, risk keeps profit. Say each pair aloud twice and it sticks.

Ceteris paribus sounds like a spell, but it just means "pretend only one thing changed" — the same move you make when you say "if the taxi fare hadn't gone up, I'd have money left." You already think this way.

The exam questions on this topic are definitions and sorting — no traps, just precision. Learn the definitions in the syllabus's words, run the little tests, and this is the friendliest marks-bank of the whole paper. You've got this!
