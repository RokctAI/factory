Today we confront the founding problem of economics: scarcity — limited resources set against unlimited wants — and the choices it forces on every household, business and government. We will separate scarcity precisely from a mere shortage, then meet opportunity cost, the true price of every decision, measured not in rands but in the next best alternative you give up. From there we take on the three questions every economy must answer and the syllabus's ethical thread: how economic activity can promote or violate human rights and the environment. Get these definitions exact and every later topic in the subject, from the production possibility curve to the budget debate, falls into place.
