Hey, it's Thandi — GDP, GNI, final goods, value added, per capita, four limitations... this lesson packed a statistics office into forty minutes. Shrink it back down with me.

Two measures, two questions. GDP asks WHERE: what did this place produce, inside the borders, whoever owns it. GNI asks WHO: what did our people earn, wherever they worked. Every exam distinction between them is those two words — where versus who — plus the bridge: subtract what foreign owners take out, add what our people bring home.

The definition traps are just two. Trap one: double counting — beaten by "final goods only," and the bread chain is your forever-example: wheat and flour live inside the loaf's R18; count once. Trap two: money-that-moves-without-making — second-hand sales, grants, share trades. Ask "was anything NEW produced?" If not, it's out.

The limitations aren't a list to cram — they're four blind spots you already know from your own street: averages hide the split; gogo's childcare has no till slip; more sold isn't better lived; the tree sold counts, the tree gone doesn't. Tell them as street stories and the marks follow.

Definitions word-perfect, two questions, two traps, four blind spots. That's the entire lesson in one pocket. You've got this!
