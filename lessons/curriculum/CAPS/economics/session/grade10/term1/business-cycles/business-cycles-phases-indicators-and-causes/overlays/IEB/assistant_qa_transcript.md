**Thandi:** Questions from the class before we close! First: if the Reserve Bank only dates a downswing long after it started, what's the point? Isn't that like a weather report for last month?

**Tutor:** It is exactly a weather report for last month — and those turn out to be surprisingly valuable, just not for the job you're imagining. Split the two tasks. For seeing the turn COMING, the dated record is the wrong tool; the composite leading indicator does that work, bending months before the economy does. The official dating serves a different master: the historical record itself. Policy analysis, wage negotiations, research, court disputes over contracts, comparisons across decades — all of these need an agreed, carefully verified answer to "when exactly did that downswing run?" Date the cycle hastily and you would have to keep revising it as late data trickled in, and every revision would reopen every argument built on the old dates. So the Bank deliberately trades speed for finality: the leading indicator whispers early and is sometimes wrong; the official dates speak late and are meant never to be corrected. An early guess and a final verdict are different instruments — the economy needs both, and confusing them is how news reports go wrong.

***

**Thandi:** Next: unemployment lagging feels backwards to me. Losing jobs IS the downswing, surely — how can the job numbers turn late?

**Tutor:** Distinguish the harm from the statistic. The downswing is falling PRODUCTION — orders shrinking, output sliding — and that starts well before the retrenchment letters go out, because employers respond to a slowdown in stages. First they trim overtime, freeze the vacant posts, run down stock; retrenchment is expensive and painful — packages, procedures, and the risk of losing trained people they'll need again — so it is the LAST resort, taken only once the bad months have clearly piled up. By then the downswing is old news in the production figures. The same caution runs the recovery in reverse: when orders return, employers first restore hours and work their existing teams harder, hiring new staff only once the upswing has proved it will last. So measured unemployment keeps worsening even after the trough — the economy has turned while the job numbers still look terrible. That is why the lesson called the mirror strangely comforting: when unemployment peaks, the recovery is usually already underway beneath it.

***

**Thandi:** Third: the causes felt tangled. A drought is exogenous, fine — but when banks then cut lending because of the drought, is that exogenous or endogenous? Which box does the whole story go in?

**Tutor:** Both boxes, in sequence — and being able to SAY that is the skill, not a dodge. Classify by origin, then follow the transmission. The drought itself is exogenous: weather is not produced by the economic system. But the credit tightening it triggers is the endogenous machinery responding — banks pulling back, caution spreading, spending contracting beyond the original farm losses. Economists would say an exogenous SHOCK was amplified by endogenous PROPAGATION: the stone came from outside; the size of the waves came from inside. This is why the lesson insisted real cycles mix both — a pure outside shock in a perfectly stable system would cause one splash and fade; it is the internal amplifiers, mood and credit feeding on themselves, that turn splashes into multi-year waves. So for a scenario answer, don't force one label onto the whole story: name the exogenous trigger, then trace the endogenous amplification. That two-step answer shows you understand the mechanism rather than just the vocabulary.

***

**Thandi:** Last one: you said settle debts near the peak and hold steady near the trough — but if the phases can't be predicted, how would an ordinary family ever know where in the cycle they are?

**Tutor:** You cannot know precisely — and you do not need precision for the advice to pay. Notice the asymmetry: the costly mistakes are borrowing heavily at the top and panic-selling at the bottom, and avoiding those needs only a ROUGH position, not a forecast. And a rough position is genuinely readable from public signals. Late-boom conditions announce themselves: everyone you know is confident, credit is offered unasked, prices and interest rates are climbing — historically the moment of maximum danger dressed up as maximum safety. Trough conditions announce themselves too: retrenchments everywhere, pessimism total, prices and rates low — historically closer to the turn than to more collapse, because replacement demand and cheap borrowing are quietly stacking up. The practical family rule is contrarian humility: when the mood says nothing can go wrong, borrow less than you're offered; when the mood says nothing will ever improve, remember that unemployment peaks after the turn and avoid decisions made in despair. You are not timing the cycle — you are refusing to let the cycle's mood time YOU. That much, any household can do.
