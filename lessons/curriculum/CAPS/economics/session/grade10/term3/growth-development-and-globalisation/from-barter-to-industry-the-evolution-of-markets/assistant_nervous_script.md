Hey, it's Thandi — a whole history of the world in one lesson can feel like too many dates and stages to hold. Good news: there are NO dates to memorise here. The exam wants the SEQUENCE and the WHY — and the sequence is a story where each stage fixes the previous stage's problem. Stories are the easiest things to remember.

Say the chain once, slowly: no extra → no trade. Farming makes EXTRA → specialists appear. Specialists must trade → swapping's four miseries. Money kills the four miseries → markets explode. Bigger markets need roads, rules and shared risk → transport, law, companies. Markets need what nobody builds alone → the state: regulate, tax, build. And hands hit their limit → machines and energy: industry. Seven links. If you can tell it as a story to a friend, you can answer any question in this topic.

The two lists worth having word-perfect: barter's four failures (double coincidence, indivisibility, no common measure, no store of value) and money's matching functions (medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, plus divisibility). They pair up one-to-one — learn them as a duet, not two lists.

And for South Africa: our machine-day came through the mines — say it with both columns, gains and costs, like every good answer this term.

One story, one duet, one balanced ending. That's the whole lesson. You've got this!
