Hey, it's Thandi — demographic cycles, nested definitions, two unemployment counts, derived demand... this lesson stacked vocabulary like firewood. Let's bundle it into three carryable armfuls.

Armful one: the cycle is just four seasons of one street — cradles-and-funerals balancing, then the clinic arrives (boom), then smaller families (slowing), then standing still. Tie each season to its CAUSE — clinics cut deaths, education cuts births — and the sequence tells itself.

Armful two: the team sheet. Population → old-enough → team (playing + benched). The sorting traps are exactly three people: the student (off the sheet), the searcher (benched — unemployed), and the one who stopped searching (off the STRICT sheet, on the EXPANDED one). Sort those three correctly and every definition question falls. And the golden rule: unemployment divides by the TEAM, not the household.

Armful three: the gate. Families sell hours, firms buy them, the wage is the price — and firms only buy when THEIR customers buy: no orders, no shifts. That's derived demand in five words. Then remember there are two gates — crowded unskilled, short skilled — and the certificate is the ticket between them.

Three armfuls: seasons, team sheet, gates. Carry those in and the whole topic unpacks itself. You've got this!
