Today we count the economy's people properly: how populations grow through births, deaths and migration, how the demographic cycle unfolds in four stages, and then the definitions that carry the marks — who exactly belongs to the labour force, and how the strict and expanded measures of unemployment differ. From there we treat the labour market as a true market, with households supplying labour, firms demanding it, and the wage as its price, before closing with the reading skills examiners reward when they hand you a labour statistics table. This topic anchors the rest of the term, because labour relations and unemployment both build directly on today's definitions.
