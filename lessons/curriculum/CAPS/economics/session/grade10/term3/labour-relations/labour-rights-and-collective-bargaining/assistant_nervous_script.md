Hey, it's Thandi — three Acts, five institutions, three CCMA processes, plus principles with long names... this topic looks like a law library fell on you. Here's the shelving system that puts every book back in seconds.

The Acts shelve by BAD DAY. Arguing day — strikes, bargaining, dismissal disputes? LRA. Ordinary day — hours, leave, overtime, notice? BCEA, the floor no job may sink below. Worst day — injury on duty? COIDA, the fund. Every scenario question describes a day; name the day, name the Act.

The institutions shelve by ROOM SIZE. One workplace, talking not wage-bargaining: workplace forum. Whole sector, unions and employers negotiating together: bargaining council. The workers' own organisation across all of it: the union. Three rooms, three sizes.

The CCMA rungs shelve by WHO DECIDES. Parties decide with help: conciliation. Parties decide from suggestions: mediation. Commissioner decides, binding: arbitration. That single question — who decides? — is the whole distinction, and it's the exam's favourite.

And the principles are just three house rules: sides govern themselves (self-government), the majority union speaks (majoritarianism), and big decisions need talking first (consultation).

Bad days, room sizes, who-decides, house rules. Four little shelvers, whole library tidy. You've got this!
