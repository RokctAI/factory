Today scarcity and opportunity cost get their picture: the production possibility curve, the boundary of everything an economy can produce with what it has. Working from a farming schedule of maize against vegetables, we plot the curve, read the three zones — efficient on it, wasteful inside it, unattainable beyond it — and measure rising opportunity cost in the outward bow. Then we ask what moves the boundary itself, and keep growth, recovery and reallocation rigorously apart, because confusing them is this topic's classic lost mark. One diagram carries scarcity, choice, unemployment and growth — and it earns marks in every paper that touches it.
