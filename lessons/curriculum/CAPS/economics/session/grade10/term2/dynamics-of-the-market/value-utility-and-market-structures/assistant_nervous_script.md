Hey, it's Thandi — utility, two kinds of value, four market structures, five functions, plus the whole internet... that's a museum's worth of exhibits in one lesson. Let's shrink each room to its one key.

Value room: worth lives in the PERSON (the stadium bottle), and trading-worth runs on scarcity (the diamond riddle). Two sentences, whole room.

Structures room: don't memorise four definitions — carry the three sorting questions: how many sellers? identical product or dressed-up-different? can newcomers walk in? Run any market through those and it classifies itself: fifty potato farmers (perfect), three networks (oligopoly), one patented pill (monopoly), a street of salons (monopolistic competition). Practise on your own street — it's honestly fun.

Electronics room: two lists, balanced. Gains: wider, faster, better-informed, easier to enter. Gates: platform owners, world competition, data costs. Exams love the balance — always give both.

Functions room: one sentence carries all five — markets make prices, and prices allocate, inform, ration and connect. Say it three times.

Nothing here calculates; everything classifies and explains. That makes this a vocabulary topic — and vocabulary is the most learnable thing in the world. Sort, list, balance, recite. You've got this!
