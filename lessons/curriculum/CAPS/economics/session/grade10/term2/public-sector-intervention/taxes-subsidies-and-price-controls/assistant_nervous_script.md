Hey, it's Thandi — taxes, subsidies, ceilings, floors, wages... five instruments and every one with side effects. If it felt like too many moving parts, here's the frame that holds them all: every intervention is the SAME exam question wearing different clothes — what was it for, what does the diagram say, what else follows?

The diagram work is just last week's skill reused. Tax: supply shifts LEFT (costs up) — price up, quantity down. Subsidy: supply shifts RIGHT — price down, quantity up. You already know how to shift curves; these just give the shifts government names.

The controls don't shift anything — they FORBID the price from moving, and that's why they misbehave. Keep the rhyme: queues under ceilings, piles on floors. Ceiling below equilibrium → shortage → queues and black markets. Floor above equilibrium → surplus → the state buys the mountain. If you remember which side of equilibrium each sits on, every consequence follows by itself.

And the minimum wage — the exam wants BOTH truths, always: protection and dignity for the employed, pricing-out risk for the young and unskilled, with the balance an evidence question. One-sided answers cap your marks; two-sided answers with a measured conclusion collect everything.

One frame, one rhyme, two truths. That's the whole topic folded small. You've got this!
