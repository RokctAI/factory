Hey, it's Thandi — half a year of Economics in one session can feel like drinking from a fire hose, so let me fold it down to the four structures the paper actually pays for.

Structure one: the gap. Scarcity is permanent, choice follows, and every choice has one price tag behind the price tag — the NEXT BEST thing given up, never the whole list. The curve that draws it is the oven: on the curve is efficient, inside is wasted capacity, beyond is a dream until the curve itself moves out — and that outward move is growth.

Structure two: the circle. Households and firms feeding each other, with three drains — savings, taxes, imports — and three taps — investment, government spending, exports. Then the two counting rules: GDP counts WHERE it was made, GNI counts WHO earns it, and only finished goods enter, or the flour gets counted twice.

Structure three: the argument. Demand slopes down, supply slopes up, and where they cross there's no pile and no queue. The mark-winning sentence: price moves you ALONG a curve; everything else moves the whole curve. Then run any shock in four steps — which curve, which direction, which excess, which new equilibrium.

Structure four: the breath. Recovery, prosperity, recession, depression — always in that order — read through headlights, speedometer and rearview mirror.

Four structures, one wallet, one street, one cooler box. The practice questions are waiting for exactly these — go show them you've got the structures cold.
