Hey, it's Thandi — this lesson lands close to home for a lot of us, and that can make it hard to study rather than just feel. So let me give you the structure that turns the feeling into marks, without losing the respect.

The whole topic is DIAGNOSE, then PRESCRIBE. Five types, five fixes, and every exam scenario is one of the five wearing a story. Between jobs? Frictional — fix the information. Skills or location don't match what's needed? Structural — training, trades, transport; that's our country's big one. Whole economy in a downswing? Cyclical — fix demand. Only works part of the year? Seasonal. Machine took the task? Technological — retrain. Read the scenario, name the type, name the fix. That's the mark scheme in one sentence.

The three solutions are just as sortable: the ENGINE (growth — but growth that needs people), the BRIDGE (public works — real income and experience, but temporary and must lead somewhere), the NET (UIF — cushions the fall, but only if you HAD a job). Strengths and limits for each; examiners want both halves, always.

And the consequences answer wants three levels: the person, the economy, society. Say them in that order and you'll never leave one out.

One more thing worth saying: knowing this properly is useful beyond the exam. Understanding why the queue is long — and which fixes actually reach it — is how you argue well about your own country. You've got this.
