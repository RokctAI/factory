Today we are studying business cycles — the waves of expansion and contraction that real economies ride around their long-term growth trend. This is a Paper 1 favourite because it asks four things in one topic: name the parts of the wave, explain why it exists, run the monetary and fiscal levers that smooth it, and read the indicators forecasters use to see the next turn coming. We build the labelled diagram first — trend line, trough, upswing, peak, downswing, with length and amplitude — then set monetarists against Keynesians, put South Africa's own repo-rate history to work, and finish with leading, coincident and lagging indicators. South Africa has lived every phase of this chapter in recent memory, so every mark here can be anchored to a real event.
