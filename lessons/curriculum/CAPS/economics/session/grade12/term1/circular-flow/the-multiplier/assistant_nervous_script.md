Hey, it's Mandy — the multiplier is one of the few places in Economics where you actually have to calculate something, and I know that makes it feel scarier than it is. Good news: it is one formula, one substitution, one sentence. That's the whole move.

The formula is k equals one over one minus the MPC. Same thing as one over the MPS, because those two always add to one. Write the formula down before you touch a single number — markers give a mark for the formula on its own, even if the arithmetic wobbles afterwards.

Then the three-step drill. State the formula. Substitute the figures. Write ONE interpretation sentence: "national income rises by R24 billion, four times the original injection, because the initial spending is re-spent round after round." That sentence is a mark on its own and almost everyone forgets it.

Two traps to watch. First, the backwards question: they give you the change in income and the injection and want the MPC. Divide income change by injection to get k, then work back — if k is 4, one minus MPC is 0,25, so the MPC is 0,75. Second, the open-economy version: when the question mentions tax and imports, the denominator holds all three leakages, MPS plus MPT plus MPM. Add them first, then divide one by the total. If the three add to 0,50, your multiplier is 2, not 5.

And for the discussion part, remember the multiplier is conditional, not magic. It creates real output only if there are idle workers and idle machines; at full capacity it just pushes prices up. It takes months to work through. And it runs backwards just as hard — that is what a recession is.

One formula, three steps, two traps, one honest caveat. You've got this!
