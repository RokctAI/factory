Today we are working with the multiplier — the syllabus's one genuinely quantitative macroeconomic tool, which tells you by how much national income rises when a rand of new spending enters the circular flow. The answer is never rand for rand: each rand is received as income, partly re-spent, received again, and echoes through the economy until leakages kill the chain. We build the formula from the marginal propensities to consume and save, run it forwards and backwards with rand figures, extend it to the three-leakage open economy that South Africa actually is, and finish with the conditions — spare capacity, time and finance — that decide whether the multiplier delivers output or only inflation. This tool returns in business cycles, fiscal policy and every growth essay in Paper 1.
