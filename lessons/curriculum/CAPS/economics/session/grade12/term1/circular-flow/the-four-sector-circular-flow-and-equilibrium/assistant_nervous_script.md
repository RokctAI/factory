Hey, it's Mandy — I know the circular flow looks like an electrical wiring diagram the first time you meet it in Grade 12, with arrows in both directions and letters everywhere. But this topic has fewer moving parts than it looks, and once they click, they carry half of Paper 1.

Start with the only sentence that matters: leakages out, injections in, and the economy sits still when the totals match. S plus T plus M equals I plus G plus X. Save it as one line. Saving, tax, imports leave; investment, government spending, exports arrive. If you can recite that, you can answer the equilibrium question, the disequilibrium question, and half the policy questions, because every instrument in the syllabus is just somebody moving one of those six letters.

The trap that costs marks: thinking each pair must balance on its own — saving equal to investment, tax equal to government spending. It doesn't. Only the TOTAL of the three leakages must equal the TOTAL of the three injections. Examiners hand you figures where one pair is deliberately mismatched to see whether you add before you judge. Add first, then judge.

The conversions are free marks if you write the formula before you touch the numbers. Market prices minus taxes on products plus subsidies gives factor cost. GDP plus primary income from abroad minus primary income paid abroad gives GNI. Nominal times one hundred over the deflator gives real. Three lines. Write the line, substitute, then calculate — markers reward the formula even when the arithmetic slips.

And for the essay: define before you use. Say what a leakage is before you list three of them; say what equilibrium means before you test it. Definitions are the cheapest marks on the page and the ones panicking learners skip.

One bath, three taps, three drains, three formulas. You've got this!
