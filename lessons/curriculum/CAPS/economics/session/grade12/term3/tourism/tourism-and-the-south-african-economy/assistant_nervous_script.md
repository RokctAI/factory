Hey, it's Mandy — tourism looks like the friendly topic until the exam asks for definitions with every clause, effects in four directions and policy with reasons, and suddenly the holiday is over. Let's pack light and smart.

The definition is three clauses on your fingers: outside the usual environment, less than a year, not paid at the destination. Miss a clause, lose the mark — say all three every time. Then the three directions in one supper-table: aunt to Durban is domestic, Germans to Kruger is inbound — that's the export, new money in — cousin to Dubai is outbound, money out.

For effects, drive one safari bus and count three rings. Direct: the lodge's own staff. Indirect: the farmer, the laundry, the builder who supply it. Induced: everyone's wages hitting the spaza and the taxi. Add the two big sentences: tourism hires hands, not machines, so a rand buys more jobs than mining; and it hires where no factory will ever come. If evaluation is asked, whisper the leak: foreign-owned lodge, imported food, package paid abroad — full bus, poor village.

IKS is two rules, and they rhyme in your head: own the telling, bank the earning. Culture with people, not about them.

Policy suggestions never go in bare — dress each in three links: obstacle, action, channel. Slow visas, e-visas for China and India, friction gone means arrivals grow. Crime fear, visible policing on tourist routes, perception converts to bookings. Load-shedding, keep the lights on, because one blackout video out-travels every advert.

Three clauses, three directions, three rings, two rules, three links. All threes and twos — count them on one hand and the topic is yours. You've got this!
