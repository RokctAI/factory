Today we close the loop on scarcity itself: environmental sustainability — why the market that allocates bread efficiently fails spectacularly at allocating clean air, and what governments and treaties do about it. We anchor on the Brundtland definition of sustainable development, diagnose the three market failures behind environmental damage, survey the state of the environment globally and in South Africa, and then work through the policy toolkit from command-and-control rules to the carbon tax, subsidies, tradable permits and property rights. We finish by climbing the treaty ladder from Montreal and Rio through Kyoto to Paris and the latest COPs, ending with South Africa's Just Energy Transition Partnership. This is a favourite essay topic, and the marks go to answers that price the problem, not just lament it.
