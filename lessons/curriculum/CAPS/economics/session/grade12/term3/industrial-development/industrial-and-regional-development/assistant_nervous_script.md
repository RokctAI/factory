Hey, it's Mandy — this topic looks like a phone book of programmes and acronyms, and nobody can love a phone book. So don't memorise the book; memorise the shelf it stands on.

The shelf has three questions. WHY industrialise? One picture answers it: the bag of ore against the bakkie. Making things at home keeps the wages, profits and taxes of every step — that's value added, and doing it before export is beneficiation. Add the web: one factory feeds suppliers behind it and dealers in front of it. Say ore, bakkie, web — the whole first section is yours.

WHAT does South Africa do? Pick five names and own them: the IPAPs as the master plan, the APDP that keeps seven carmakers assembling here, the IDC as the state's industrial lender, the SEZs — Coega with its port, East London, Dube TradePort — with their 15 percent tax rate, and the Maputo Corridor as the road that woke a route. Five names, one line each. Nobody needs the whole phone book.

And IS it working? Here's your safety rail for any evaluate question: two wins, two losses, one bottleneck, one verdict. Wins: cars export, Coega has real tenants. Losses: manufacturing's share of GDP has shrunk for twenty years, and some zones are empty sheds. Bottleneck: no incentive outweighs power cuts, broken rail and queuing ports. Verdict: right direction, crumbling foundations — fix the foundations first.

If the history point comes up, one sentence carries it: apartheid paid factories to arrive in the homelands, and they died when the paying stopped — factories stay only where business works.

Ore-bakkie-web, five names, two-two-one-one. That's the entire topic on one hand. You've got this!
