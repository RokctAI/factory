Hey, it's Mandy — I know this topic feels like being handed forty statistics and told to love them all. You don't need forty. You need one taxi and three honest rules.

The taxi sorts every timing question. Headlights see ahead: building plans, job adverts, factory orders — leading indicators. The speedometer reads now: retail sales, production, GDP — coincident. The mirror looks back: unemployment, insolvencies — lagging. If a question names an indicator, just ask where it sits in the taxi. And remember the trick with jobs: bosses fire late and hire late, so unemployment is always the mirror, never the headlights.

For the economic gauges, keep the four that carry marks: real GDP growth against population growth — if people grow faster than output, everyone gets poorer on average; M1, M2, M3 in size order, smallest to broadest; terms of trade as export prices over import prices — up is a national raise; and the two unemployment rates, strict for searching, expanded for the ones who gave up.

Social indicators are even friendlier — they are just questions to passengers. How long do people live? Do babies reach one? Are children learning? Do taps and lights work? Then the two composites: HDI blends health, education and income out of 1; Gini measures sharing from 0 to 1, and South Africa's 0,63 is world-top unfairness. Two numbers, two sentences, easy marks.

Comparisons: divide by the people, reprice by what money buys at home — that's PPP — and remember the meter misses the spaza and the garden. IMF paramedic, World Bank builder. Six words that settle a whole exam question.

One taxi, four gauges, five passenger questions, three honest rules, six words. That's the entire dashboard. You've got this!
