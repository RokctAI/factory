Hey, it's Mandy — inflation looks like a wall of definitions plus a calculation that punishes you for one wrong division, and I know that combination makes people freeze. Let's shrink it to what actually earns the marks.

The definition is three words doing three jobs: SUSTAINED, GENERAL, OVER TIME. A once-off jump fails the first test, bread alone fails the second, and no comparison year fails the third. Say all three and the definition mark is banked before you touch a number.

The calculation is one move: new index minus old index, divided by the OLD one, times 100. CPI goes 112 to 118,7 — that is 6,7 over 112, about 6 percent. Two traps live here, and both are cheap to dodge. Trap one: never divide by the new index — think of a taxi fare going from R10 to R12; the 20 percent is on the ten you used to pay. Trap two: index POINTS are not a percentage — 6,7 points is not 6,7 percent. Write the formula line first, every time, and the marker follows your working even if the arithmetic slips.

For the essay, one sorting rule carries you: ask where the pressure starts. At the till, with busy shops and cheap credit — demand-pull. At the factory's back door, with fuel, tariffs, wages and a weak rand — cost-push. Then the sentence that lifts you a level: the repo rate fights demand-pull well and cost-push badly, because interest rates cannot make petrol cheaper.

And if you blank on consequences, walk one street: the pensioner loses, the saver loses, the borrower quietly wins, and the state keeps it all inside 3 to 6 percent so the town can still plan a year ahead.

Three-word definition, one honest division, two fires, one street. That's the whole topic. You've got this!
