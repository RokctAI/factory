Hey, it's Mandy — Paper 2 practice can rattle anyone, because it is the paper where the graphs live, and graphs feel like they either come out right or collapse. Here is the secret the session just showed you: in this exam, a graph is not a drawing. It is a checklist.

Five items, every time: axes named. Curves named. Equilibrium found where marginal cost meets marginal revenue. Price and quantity traced to the axes. Shaded area named if profit or loss is asked. A wobbly curve with all five earns nearly everything; a gallery-perfect curve with none of them earns nearly nothing. You are being marked on labels, and labels are just words — words you can practise in the taxi, in the queue, anywhere.

The calculations are two moves you already own. Profit or loss: price minus average cost, times quantity — R150 minus R125, times 100, is R2 500. Inflation: new index minus old, over the OLD one, times 100 — and the old index is the one rule you never break.

The paragraphs tell you their own shape: four times two means four short developed facts; two times four means two bigger built ideas. Read the bracket, set the skeleton, fill it.

And the essay is four boxes — 2, 26, 10, 2 — where the ten-mark box just wants your honest, reasoned opinion. You have opinions about prices and monopolies; you live with both every day.

Checklist, two moves, brackets, boxes. Paper 2 is yours. You've got this!
