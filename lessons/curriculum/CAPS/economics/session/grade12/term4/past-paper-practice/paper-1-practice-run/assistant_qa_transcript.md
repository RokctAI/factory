**Mandy:** Questions from the class before we close! First: the paper has six questions but we only answer four. A few of us panicked in the practice run and started all six. How should we choose, fast?

**Tutor:** Choose before you write anything, with a quick survey done in your head. Question 1 is compulsory — no decision there. In Section B you face three forty-mark questions: one pure Macroeconomics, one pure Economic Pursuits, one combined, and you answer exactly two. Open each and look ONLY at the data-response stimuli — the graph, the table, the extract — because forty of each question's marks flow through data response and paragraphs. If the cycle diagram looks friendlier than the tax table, that is your signal. In Section C, read both essay instructions and pick the one whose BODY you can heading-map instantly: if you can silently name five headings for smoothing the cycle but only two for regional development, the choice is made. Then commit. Starting extra questions burns your writing capacity for zero extra marks, because markers only credit the required number in the order the rules prescribe. Answering four questions well beats six half-answered every single time — the paper is designed so that a focused candidate finishes and a scattered one does not.

**Mandy:** Next one: in the balance of payments calculation, someone got the right minus 250 but only three marks out of four. Where did the last mark go?

**Tutor:** Into the writing, not the arithmetic. A four-mark calculation in Economics is marked as a method chain: the setup line showing which items you are adding and subtracting, the receipts total, the payments deduction, and the final answer labelled with its sign, its units and its name. The learner who wrote a lone minus 250 skipped the setup line, so the marker could not award the method mark. Write it as the session modelled: receipts of 1 200 plus 100 plus 200 plus 150 give 1 650; payments of 1 400 plus 250 plus 200 give 1 850; 1 650 minus 1 850 gives minus 200; plus net transfers of minus 50 gives minus 250 million — a deficit on the current account. Notice the last three words: naming the result is part of the answer, because the question tests whether you know what the number MEANS. The same habit rescues you when arithmetic slips: with the method visible, an error in one addition still leaves the method marks standing. A naked wrong number earns nothing; a documented wrong number can still earn three.

***

**Mandy:** Third: essays. The forty-mark structure says the additional part is worth ten. What actually goes in there — more facts?

**Tutor:** Not more facts — a different KIND of thinking. The main body's twenty-six marks reward accurate, developed content: the tools of monetary and fiscal policy, each linked to smoothing. The additional part's ten marks reward what the guideline calls the higher-order response: your own substantiated position. For the smoothing essay, that means weighing the policies rather than describing them — stimulus arrives late because decisions and implementation take months, so it can land in the wrong phase; the Phillips relationship warns that pushing unemployment down stokes inflation; the repo rate cannot fix a drought or a power shortage, because those are supply problems; and borrowed stimulus becomes tomorrow's debt service. Then close the argument with a judgement: demand-side smoothing works, moderately, and works best when supply-side reform runs alongside. Notice the shape — claim, reason, judgement — repeated three or four times. If your additional part could be pasted into any essay on the topic, it is too generic; it must answer THIS question's evaluation instruction. And keep it separate in your mind from the conclusion, which earns its two marks with one fresh closing insight, never a summary.

***

**Mandy:** Last one: give-the-term keeps punishing us. Six little marks, but nobody gets all six. Any training method?

**Tutor:** Train it in reverse, because that is how the item works. Ordinary studying goes from term to meaning; give-the-term goes from meaning to term, and the recall pathway is different. Take your glossary and cover the terms, reading only the definitions: the compensation for the use of capital — interest. The system where the exchange rate is determined purely by supply and demand — free floating. The additional income created by an initial injection — the multiplier. Ten definitions a day, spoken aloud, answered in full words. Full words matter because the marking guideline explicitly refuses abbreviations, acronyms and examples: MTEF earns nothing where Medium Term Expenditure Framework earns the mark, and naming SACU as an example earns nothing where the question wants the term customs union. Build your list from this paper's high-frequency families: circular flow terms, cycle terms, budget terms, exchange rate terms, trade policy terms. Six marks sounds small, but Section A's thirty objective marks are the cushion that lets you write the essay without fear — and they are the cheapest marks in the entire paper to secure.
