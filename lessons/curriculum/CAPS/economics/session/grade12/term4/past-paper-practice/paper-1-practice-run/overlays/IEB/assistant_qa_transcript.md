**Mandy:** Questions from the class before we close! First one: the session kept saying the mark value is the instruction. A few of us still wrote half a page for two-mark items in the practice run. How do we actually break that habit?

**Tutor:** Break it with arithmetic before you write a word. A two-mark item pays for one line of cause and consequence; a four-mark item pays for two developed facts; a ten-mark paragraph pays for five. So before your pen moves, say the number silently: this question buys two facts from me, no more. The habit sticks when you see what over-writing costs — every extra sentence on a two-mark item is time stolen from a ten-mark paragraph later, and time is the real currency of a long exam session. There is also a marking reason: markers award the first correct facts they meet, so the third and fourth sentences on a two-mark item earn exactly nothing, no matter how good they are. Train it the way the session did: cover the answer, read only the mark value, and plan your fact count out loud — two marks, one line; four marks, two facts; ten marks, five. After a week of that, proportion becomes reflex, and the reflex is worth more than any single piece of content in the whole subject.

***

**Mandy:** Next: in the balance of payments calculation, someone reached minus 120 correctly but scored only half the marks. Where did the rest go?

**Tutor:** Into the writing around the number. A calculation in Economics is marked as a chain, not a destination: the setup line naming which items you are adding and subtracting, the receipts total, the payments total, the subtraction, and the final answer labelled with its sign, its units and its name. Write it exactly as the session modelled: receipts of 950 plus 150 plus 300 plus 100 give 1 500; payments of 1 250 plus 180 plus 220 give 1 650; 1 500 minus 1 650 gives minus 150; plus transfers of 30 gives minus 120 million — a deficit on the current account. Those last words matter, because the question is testing whether you know what the number MEANS, not just whether you can add. And the chain protects you: if one addition slips, the method marks still stand, because the marker can see your reasoning was right. A naked number is all-or-nothing; a documented chain pays out even on a bad day. In every calculation, the formula line and the units are marks in their own right — collect them.

***

**Mandy:** Third: essays. Our practice essay gave six marks to evaluation. What is the difference between more facts and actual evaluation?

**Tutor:** Facts describe; evaluation weighs. The main body's twenty marks pay for accurate, developed content — the repo rate cut, the spending injection, the multiplier, each tied to smoothing. The evaluation's six marks pay for a different mental act: your own substantiated position on how well those tools work. For the smoothing essay, that means arguing limits: stimulus decided today arrives in the economy months later, so it can land in the wrong phase; pushing unemployment down tends to push inflation up, so smoothing buys one goal with another; a repo rate cannot end a drought or switch a power station back on, because those are supply problems; and borrowed stimulus becomes tomorrow's interest bill. Then close the argument with a judgement — demand-side smoothing works moderately, and works best beside supply-side reform. Notice the shape: claim, reason, judgement, repeated. The test I give my own writing is this: could this paragraph be pasted into any essay on the topic? If yes, it is description wearing evaluation's clothes. Real evaluation answers THIS question's instruction, in your own voice, with reasons attached.

***

**Mandy:** Last one: give-the-term keeps catching people — the marks are small but nobody seems to get them all. Any training method?

**Tutor:** Train it backwards, because the item works backwards. Normal studying runs from term to meaning; give-the-term runs from meaning to term, and that recall direction needs its own practice. Take your glossary, cover the terms, and read only the definitions: the ratio of export prices to import prices, times one hundred — the terms of trade. An increase in a floating currency's value — appreciation. The fraction of each extra rand that households spend — the marginal propensity to consume. Ten definitions a day, spoken aloud, answered in full words — full words because careful marking refuses abbreviations and shortened forms, so the marginal propensity to consume earns the mark where a three-letter shorthand earns nothing. Build your list from the high-frequency families: circular flow terms, cycle terms, budget terms, exchange rate terms, trade terms. The objective marks are the cheapest in any Economics session to secure, and they are the cushion that lets you write the essay without fear. Small marks, banked early, buy calm — and calm writes better essays than brilliance under panic does.
