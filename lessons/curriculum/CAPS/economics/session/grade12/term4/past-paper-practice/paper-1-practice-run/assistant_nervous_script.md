Hey, it's Mandy — a full practice paper can feel like standing at the bottom of a mountain, and I heard the sighs when the essay section came up. Let me shrink the mountain.

You do not climb all of it. Six questions stand in the paper; you answer four — the compulsory objective question, TWO of the three forty-mark questions, and ONE of the two essays. Your first job in the exam room is choosing, and you now know how: pick the data you can read, pick the essay you can heading-map. Choice is not cheating; the paper is BUILT for it.

The marks come in small, honest pieces. Objective items: one word, two marks. Short items: one line. Data response: read the graph or table, then write little number sentences — formula, workings, labelled answer. Paragraphs: four facts, two marks each. Nothing in this paper asks for genius; it asks for pieces, delivered in order.

Even the essay is pieces. Two marks: define. Twenty-six: your headings, each fact developed with its link to the question. Ten: your own opinion with reasons — the part where you are ALLOWED to argue. Two: one fresh finishing thought. Forty marks, four boxes, and you fill boxes one at a time.

So tonight, do one thing: take the smoothing essay and write only its skeleton — the definition, five headings, three evaluation points, one closing line. Tomorrow the mountain is a staircase. You've got this!
