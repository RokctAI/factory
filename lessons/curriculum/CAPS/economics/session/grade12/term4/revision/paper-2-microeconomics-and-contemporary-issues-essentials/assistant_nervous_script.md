Hey, it's Mandy — Paper 2 is the graph paper, and I know those cost and revenue curves can blur into spaghetti the night before. Let's untangle them into one habit and three cards.

The habit: every market graph is built the same way. Axes first. Then the demand side — flat line for the perfect competitor, sloping line for everyone else, with marginal revenue tucked below it for the monopolist. Then the same two cost curves every time: the U of average cost, with marginal cost cutting its lowest point. Then find where marginal cost meets marginal revenue, and compare price with average cost right there. Above, profit. Below, loss. Touching, normal. One procedure, one switch. Draw it five times and your hand knows it without you.

The shutdown rule is one sentence: keep going while price covers average VARIABLE cost, stop the moment it does not.

The kinked demand curve is one social fact: raise your price alone and you lose everyone; cut it and everyone follows you down. So prices stick and the fight moves to adverts and brands.

And the three cards: inflation — define, two causes, who bleeds, the repo rate and its limit. Tourism — the export that never leaves home, jobs first. Environment — make the polluter pay, from bag levy to Paris.

One habit, one sentence, one social fact, three cards. That is Paper 2. You've got this!
