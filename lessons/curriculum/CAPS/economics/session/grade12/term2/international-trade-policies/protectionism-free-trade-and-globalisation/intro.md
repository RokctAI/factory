Today we take up the political question that follows from comparative advantage: how open should a country's borders be? We work through the full toolkit of protection — tariffs, quotas, subsidies, exchange control and standards — then set the case for protection against the case for free trade, argument and rebuttal in pairs, because that paired structure is exactly how the essay is marked. From there we compare import substitution with export promotion through South Africa's own history, and finish with globalisation's machinery: GATT to the WTO, its rules and critiques, and the ladder of integration from free trade area to economic union with the African examples the paper expects. No side wins outright here — the marks live in the weighing.
