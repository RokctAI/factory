Hey, it's Mandy — trade policy essays scare people because both sides sound right, and you sit there wondering which one the marker wants. Here's the release: the marker wants NEITHER side. The marks are in the weighing, and the weighing has a fixed shape you can learn tonight.

First, the instruments — learn them as one sentence each with the mechanism attached. Tariff: tax at the border, price up. Quota: quantity capped, licences become gold. Subsidy: home costs down, protection hidden in the budget. Exchange control: no currency, no imports. Standards and red tape: the polite barriers. And the two special words: dumping — selling below cost to kill rivals — and infant industry — young, promising, needs temporary shelter.

Then the argument structure, and this is the trick that writes the essay for you: every argument FOR protection comes with a built-in rebuttal, so learn them as pairs. Infants need shelter — but infants never admit to growing up. Protection saves jobs — visible ones, while killing invisible ones downstream and inviting retaliation. Self-sufficiency is safety — and also permanent expensiveness. Only anti-dumping stands mostly unrebutted. State the pair, both halves, every time — that IS the evaluation.

The two strategies are two directions: import substitution looks inward — walls up, safe, small, ceilinged by the home market. Export promotion looks outward — no ceiling, real competition, needs working ports and power. East Asia outward and it worked; old South Africa inward and it didn't; new South Africa chooses outward while wrestling its logistics.

Integration is a four-rung ladder — free trade area, customs union, common market, economic union — and you have an African example for the first two: AfCFTA and SACU. Climb in order, name the examples, done.

And every essay closes the same way: mostly open, protection rare, temporary and conditional — the scalpel, never the blanket.

Pairs, two directions, four rungs, one closing line. You've got this!
