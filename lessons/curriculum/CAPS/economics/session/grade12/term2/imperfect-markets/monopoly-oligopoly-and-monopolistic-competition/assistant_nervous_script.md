Hey, it's Mandy — three market structures in one topic feels like three topics, I know. Breathe. Here is the secret that shrinks it: you already know the whole method from perfect markets, and the three new structures only change two settings — how steep the firm's demand curve is, and how easy entry is. Slope and door. That's it.

Monopoly: steepest slope, locked door. The only new move is the MR curve below the demand curve — remember WHY with one number story: dropping the price to sell one more unit discounts every unit, so the extra unit earns less than its price tag. Draw D, then MR falling twice as fast, then the same tick and U. Decide quantity at MR equals MC, then — the mark everyone loses — read the PRICE up on the demand curve, never off MR. Locked door means the profit rectangle survives the long run.

Oligopoly: the kink, and the kink is just a story about neighbours. Raise your price — nobody follows, you lose alone: flat upper section. Cut it — everyone follows, nobody gains: steep lower section. Conclusion in one word: sticky. Then two sentences for the rest: rivalry moves to advertising and features, and the illegal shortcut — the cartel — pays like a monopoly but is unstable because every member wants to cheat, and the bread case shows what the Tribunal does about it.

Monopolistic competition: gentle slope, open door. Short run, draw a mini-monopoly and take the profit. Long run, the open door does its work: entrants shave your curve leftward until it just TOUCHES the AC curve. Tangency means price equals average cost — normal profit — and because the touch lands left of the U's bottom, excess capacity: the half-empty restaurant. Paid for in variety.

For the comparison table, recite the four in order — many, many, few, one — and run entry across them: free, free, difficult, blocked. Entry alone tells you who keeps profit in the long run.

Slope and door. Two settings, four structures, one method. You've got this!
