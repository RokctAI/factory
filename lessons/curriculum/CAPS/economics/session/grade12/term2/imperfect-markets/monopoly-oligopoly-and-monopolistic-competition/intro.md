Today we take the perfect market's assumptions down one by one and study what grows in their place: monopoly with one seller behind blocked entry, oligopoly where a few giants watch each other, and monopolistic competition where every high-street business is slightly special. These three imperfect structures fill most of the real economy and most of Paper 2's microeconomics section, and the examiner's method is always comparison against the perfect benchmark. We draw the price maker's diagram with marginal revenue running below demand, build the kinked curve that freezes oligopoly prices, follow free entry until demand just touches average cost, and finish with the four structures side by side — because mastering two dials, the slope of demand and the ease of entry, turns every diagram in this section into one diagram with different settings.
