Today we complete the microeconomic argument: even a market with many sellers and free entry can systematically produce the wrong quantities — too much of some things, too little of others, none at all of a few. That is market failure, and it supplies the intellectual foundation for the whole public-sector half of the syllabus. We work the six causes, build both externality diagrams — marginal social cost above private cost, marginal social benefit above private demand — then match the state's toolkit of taxes, subsidies, provision and regulation to the failures each one targets. We finish with cost-benefit analysis, the five-step tool government uses when no market price can judge a dam or a highway, together with the four limits that earn the evaluation marks. Paper 2 loves this topic in both essay and data-response form.
