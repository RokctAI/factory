Hey, it's Mandy — market failure is the topic where the lists multiply: six causes, two diagrams, seven instruments, five CBA steps. Deep breath. There is one idea underneath all of it, and if you hold the idea, every list rebuilds itself: prices sometimes lie, and wrong prices make wrong quantities.

The causes? Six ways a price can lie. It leaves out a cost that lands on bystanders — externalities. It cannot exist at all — public goods, the missing market. It is rigged by power — monopoly. It is built on ignorance — imperfect information. Resources cannot answer it — immobility. Or it only hears money, not need — inequality. Learn them as six lies, each one sentence.

The diagrams? Both are the SAME move: draw the ordinary demand and supply cross, then add one honest curve. Polluting mill: the honest cost curve sits ABOVE supply by the external cost, and the honest crossing is up-left of the market's — less paper, higher price. Market over-produces. Vaccination: the honest benefit curve sits ABOVE demand, honest crossing down-right — more jabs. Market under-produces. Costs lift supply, benefits lift demand. If you remember one line: external cost, too much; external benefit, too little.

The instruments? Each one un-lies a price. Tax the harm by its external cost — carbon tax, sin tax. Subsidise the spillover good — no-fee schools. Provide outright what markets cannot form — streetlights. Regulate when poison has no correct price. And always close with the caution sentence: the fixer can also fail, so choose the smaller failure.

CBA? Five verbs: list, list, value, shrink, compare. All costs, all benefits, money on everything, discount the future, then compare — and add the two cautions, gamed numbers and unasked whose-rands.

One idea, six lies, two lifted curves, five verbs. You've got this!
