Hey, it's Mandy — the growth and development essay is the big one, the twenty-sixer that decides distinctions, and I know it feels like it demands everything you know. Good news: it does — and you already know it. This topic is the year's material reassembled, so revision here is mostly connection, not memorisation.

Start with the two definitions, and keep them as two questions you ask of any country: is it producing more — real GDP, growth? And are its people living better — health, learning, standard of living, the HDI's three subjects — development? Then the discipline that impresses markers in the first paragraph: divide growth by population. If real growth trails population growth, the average person is getting poorer, whatever the headline says. That is one sentence and it is worth gold.

The two approaches: label them by what they push. Demand-side pushes SPENDING — grants, public works, low rates — and it is your term-one multiplier wearing policy clothes. Give it its four South African walls: inflation if capacity is the real problem, imports leaking the stimulus, debt eating the budget, and structure — spending cannot train an electrician. Supply-side pushes CAPACITY — electricity, ports, schools, competition, certainty — and give it its three costs: slow, hard on the poor first, and dependent on state capacity. Then the evaluation is one line you already know from business cycles: hold people through the present with demand, make the future bigger with supply — proportion, not either-or.

South Africa's plans: four names, four dates, one clause each. RDP 1994, basic needs. GEAR 1996, discipline and stability. ASGISA 2006, named the constraints. NDP 2012, the 2030 targets. Verdict: stability and the social wage achieved; growth, jobs and the named constraints not. Diagnosed well, delivered late.

And the north-south divide: four mechanisms — commodity terms of trade, foreign-currency debt, tariffs that climb with processing, and the brain drain — plus the counter-story: East Asia, BRICS, AfCFTA, remittances.

Two questions, four walls, three costs, four plans, four mechanisms. It is all material you own. You've got this!
