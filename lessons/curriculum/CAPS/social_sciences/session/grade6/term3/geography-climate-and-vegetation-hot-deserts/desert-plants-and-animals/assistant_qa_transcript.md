### subtopic_1 — How Desert Plants Survive
**Assistant:** Can stone plants really look like stones?

**Tutor:** Yes. Stone plants, sometimes called living stones, are small succulents that grow mainly in the dry parts of southern Africa, including Namaqualand and southern Namibia. Each plant usually has just a pair of thick, fleshy leaves that are joined together, and they grow low to the ground among pebbles. Their colours and patterns, grey, brown, cream or reddish, often match the stones and soil around them, so that thirsty animals looking for a juicy snack pass them by. This kind of disguise is called camouflage. Many stone plants also grow partly buried in the ground, which keeps them cooler and helps them save water. In autumn or winter, many produce a bright yellow or white flower that looks like a daisy, which suddenly shows where the plant is hiding.

### subtopic_2 — Special Desert Plants
**Assistant:** How do desert-adapted elephants survive in Namibia?

**Tutor:** In the dry north-west of Namibia, in the region of Kunene, there are elephants that live in desert and semi-desert country. They are the same kind of elephant as those in the Kruger National Park, but they have learned ways to survive in a very dry place. They can walk long distances between waterholes and food, sometimes going several days without drinking. They know where water can be found in dry riverbeds, and they sometimes dig in the sand of riverbeds with their trunks and feet to reach water below. They eat a wide range of plants, and they tend to live in smaller family groups than elephants in wetter areas. Mothers teach their young where to find water and food, which is why their knowledge is passed down.

### subtopic_3 — How Desert Animals Survive
**Assistant:** Why do desert flowers bloom so quickly after rain?

**Tutor:** In a desert, rain is rare and the water soon dries up or soaks away, so plants that grow from seed have only a short time to complete their lives. Their seeds lie in the soil, sometimes for years, with tough coverings that protect them from heat and drought. When enough rain falls, the seeds sprout within days. The young plants grow quickly, flower within a few weeks and attract insects, such as bees and beetles, to carry pollen from flower to flower. They then make new seeds before the soil dries out again, and the parent plants die. In Namaqualand, this is why the spring flowers are so spectacular in some years and much fewer in dry years: it depends on how much winter rain has fallen.
