### subtopic_1 — Where Hot Deserts Are
**Assistant:** How are sand dunes formed?

**Tutor:** Sand dunes are formed by the wind. In deserts, there are few plants to hold the ground together, so wind can pick up loose grains of sand and blow them along. When the wind slows down, or meets something in its path, such as a rock or a bush, it drops the sand, and a small heap begins to grow. Over time, the wind blows sand up the gentle side of the heap, and the sand tumbles down the steep side on the other side, so the dune slowly moves forward and grows. In the Namib, much of the sand is thought to have come from the Orange River, which carried it to the sea, where currents and wind moved it north along the coast and then inland. The red colour comes from a thin coating of iron oxide, which is like rust, on the grains.

### subtopic_2 — Why Hot Deserts Form
**Assistant:** What is a mirage?

**Tutor:** A mirage is an optical illusion that is common in hot deserts and even on hot roads in South Africa. On a very hot day, the air just above the hot ground is much hotter than the air higher up. Light from the sky bends as it passes through these layers of air at different temperatures. When it reaches your eyes, your brain thinks the light came from the ground, so you seem to see a shimmering pool of water on the ground ahead. In fact, you are seeing a picture of the sky. When you walk towards it, the pool seems to move away and never gets closer. In the past, thirsty travellers in deserts were sometimes fooled by mirages into thinking they had found water.

### subtopic_3 — The Climate of a Hot Desert
**Assistant:** Are deserts growing bigger?

**Tutor:** In some places, land on the edges of deserts is becoming more like desert. This is called desertification. It happens when dry land loses its plants and its soil becomes poor, for example through overgrazing by too many animals, cutting down trees and bushes for firewood, poor farming and long droughts. Climate change may make some dry areas drier still. A well-known area affected is the Sahel, the dry belt just south of the Sahara. In some countries of the Sahel, people have been working on projects to plant trees and protect plants across the land to hold the soil together and fight desertification. In South Africa, parts of the Karoo and other dry areas also need careful farming to prevent the land from becoming degraded.
