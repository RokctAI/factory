### subtopic_1 — Nomadic Herders
**Assistant:** Why is salt so important that people carried it across the Sahara?

**Tutor:** Salt is essential for life. People and animals lose salt when they sweat, especially in hot places, and they need to replace it to stay healthy. Salt was also used to preserve food, such as meat, before there were fridges. In the Sahara, there are places where thick layers of salt are found in the ground, left behind when ancient lakes dried up. In the lands south of the Sahara, salt was much harder to find. So for many centuries, camel caravans carried slabs of salt southwards across the desert, and traders in the south paid for it with goods such as gold, ivory and cloth. In some periods, salt was so valuable that it was traded weight for weight against gold, according to some old accounts. Some salt caravans still cross parts of the Sahara today.

### subtopic_2 — Hunter-Gatherers of the Kalahari
**Assistant:** What is the Kalahari like for people who live there today?

**Tutor:** Today, the Kalahari is home to a mix of people and ways of life. Most of it lies in Botswana, with parts in Namibia and the Northern Cape. Many people live in towns and villages and work in farming, especially cattle and game farming, in government jobs, in shops and in tourism. Boreholes, which are deep holes drilled into the ground, pump up water for people and livestock. The Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, shared by South Africa and Botswana, attracts visitors who come to see lions, gemsbok and meerkats. In Botswana, there are also diamond mines in the Kalahari, which have helped the country's economy grow. Some San communities run tourism projects where they share their knowledge of tracking and desert plants.

### subtopic_3 — Oases and Modern Desert Life
**Assistant:** How do people in Dubai get water in the desert?

**Tutor:** Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates, is a large modern city on the coast of the Arabian Desert, where it hardly ever rains. Most of its drinking water comes from desalination, which means removing the salt from sea water. Huge plants heat the sea water or push it through very fine filters to separate the fresh water from the salt. This uses a lot of energy, which is expensive. Some of the city's wastewater is cleaned and reused to water parks and gardens. The country also pumps some water from underground, but this water is slowly running out. Because water is so precious, people in desert cities are encouraged to use it carefully, a lesson that is just as important in dry parts of South Africa.
