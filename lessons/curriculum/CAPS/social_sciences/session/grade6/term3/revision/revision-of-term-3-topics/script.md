# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 3 topics. In Geography, the topic was climate and vegetation around the world: weather and climate, hot, mild and cold climates, wet and dry areas, tropical rainforests, hot deserts and coniferous forests. In History, the topic was democracy and citizenship: the meaning of democracy, the first democratic government, the 1994 election, Parliament and the Constitution, rules and laws, the justice system, rights and responsibilities, children's rights and national symbols. The anchor is a revision afternoon for a Grade 6 class in Welkom, a gold-mining town in the Free State. The school hall has three stations. Station 1 has a globe, a torch and world maps of temperature and rainfall. Station 2 has three climate graphs pinned side by side, labelled rainforest, hot desert and coniferous forest, with a pine cone, a succulent leaf and a picture of a toucan. Station 3 has a ballot box, a small copy of the Constitution and the national flag. Groups move from station to station, answering the questions on each card. The big idea is that climate shapes the natural regions of the world, and that democracy gives every citizen rights, responsibilities and a voice. The whole of Term 3 is examinable.

## Subtopic: Climates of the World

At Station 1, the class remembers the basics. Weather is the condition of the air at a particular place and time, described by temperature, rainfall, wind, cloud and humidity. Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over about thirty years or more. A climate graph shows monthly rainfall as bars and monthly temperature as a line.

Temperatures change mainly with distance from the equator. Near the equator, the sun's rays hit the Earth almost straight on, so it is hot. Near the poles, the rays arrive at a slant and spread out, so it is cold. This gives a hot zone between the tropics, two mild zones and two cold polar zones. Because the Earth is tilted, the seasons are opposite in the two hemispheres: in January, the south has summer; in July, the north has summer. Altitude, distance from the sea and ocean currents also affect temperature.

Rainfall is very uneven. The wettest areas lie near the equator, where warm, moist air rises, cools and forms rain clouds. The driest areas lie near the tropics, where dry air sinks. Mountains have a wet windward side and a dry rain shadow, and cold ocean currents make coasts dry and foggy. South Africa is a dry country, wetter in the east and drier in the west.

The questions for this section are with you now: weather and climate, temperature zones and rainfall patterns.

## Subtopic: Three Natural Regions

At Station 2, the class compares three natural regions. Tropical rainforests grow near the equator, in the Amazon, the Congo Basin and Southeast Asia. They are hot all year, about twenty-six degrees, with a very small temperature range, and get more than two thousand millimetres of rain, with rain in every month. Their climate graph has a flat line and tall bars. Plants grow in four layers: emergent, canopy, understorey and forest floor, and have drip tips, buttress roots, lianas and epiphytes. Rainforests have enormous biodiversity, but deforestation for cattle ranching, soya, logging and mining is destroying habitats, harming indigenous people, washing away soil and adding to climate change.

Hot deserts lie near the tropics, such as the Sahara, the Namib and the Atacama. They get less than two hundred and fifty millimetres of rain, with very hot days and cold nights. Plants store, find and save water, like succulents, the quiver tree and the welwitschia. Animals hide from the heat and many are nocturnal, like the fennec fox. People live as nomadic herders such as the Tuareg, as hunter-gatherers such as the San, at oases and in modern cities.

Coniferous forests form a belt across the far north, in Canada, Scandinavia and Russia. Winters are long and freezing and summers short and cool. Conifers have thin, waxy needles and cone shapes that shed snow. Most conifers are evergreen, so they can start making food as soon as the short summer begins, and animals such as bears survive the winter by sleeping through much of it. South Africa's pine plantations are farmed trees from the north, not natural forests.

The questions for this section are with you now: rainforests, hot deserts and coniferous forests.

## Subtopic: Democracy and Citizenship

At Station 3, the class remembers History. Democracy means rule by the people. On the twenty-seventh of April 1994, all adult South Africans could vote for the first time, using a secret ballot. The ANC won about sixty-two per cent, and on the tenth of May 1994, Nelson Mandela became President of a Government of National Unity. The RDP built houses and the TRC worked for reconciliation.

Parliament, with the National Assembly and the NCOP, makes laws, elects the President and watches over the government. The Constitution, signed in 1996, is the highest law. Laws apply to everyone, and under the rule of law nobody is above the law. Police investigate, prosecutors charge and courts decide, and everyone is equal before the law and innocent until proven guilty. The Constitutional Court protects the Constitution.

The Bill of Rights protects everyone's rights, and every right comes with responsibilities. Pius Langa rose from factory work to become Chief Justice. Children have special rights, and in 1992 children wrote the Children's Charter. The flag, coat of arms and anthem stand for unity in diversity.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: mixing up weather and climate; climate is the pattern over many years. Two: thinking deserts are hot day and night; desert nights can be cold. Three: thinking the President is above the law; the Constitution is the highest law. Four: thinking rights come without responsibilities; every right has a matching duty.

The questions for this section are with you now: democracy, the Constitution, rights and national symbols.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the three revision stations in Welkom — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Hot, Cold, Wet and Dry

Climate is the pattern over years. Hot at the equator, cold at the poles.

Keep the globe and the torch in mind and try the questions for this part: climates of the world.

## Subtopic: Forest, Desert, Forest

Rainforests are hot and wet, deserts dry, and coniferous forests freezing in winter.

Hold the three climate graphs side by side in mind and try this part's questions: three natural regions.

## Subtopic: Power to the People

Votes, Parliament, the Constitution, courts, rights and symbols.

Pocket summary of the term. Climate is the long-term pattern of weather, shaped by distance from the equator, altitude, the sea and air that rises or sinks. Rainforests near the equator are hot and wet with four layers of plants, but are threatened by deforestation. Hot deserts near the tropics are dry, with hot days and cold nights. Coniferous forests in the far north have freezing winters. In 1994, South Africa became a democracy, with a Constitution as the highest law, independent courts, rights and responsibilities, and symbols of unity.

Hold all three revision stations in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: democracy and citizenship.
