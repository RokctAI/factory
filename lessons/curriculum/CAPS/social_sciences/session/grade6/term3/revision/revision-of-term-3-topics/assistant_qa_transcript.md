### subtopic_1 — Climates of the World
**Assistant:** How should I study for the Term 3 Geography test?

**Tutor:** Start by practising climate graphs, because they appear often. Make sure you can read the bars against the rainfall scale and the line against the temperature scale, find the hottest, coldest, wettest and driest months, and work out the temperature range. Then make a comparison table with three columns, rainforest, hot desert and coniferous forest, and four rows: location, climate, plants and animals. Fill it in from memory and then check your notes. Learn the key words, such as convectional rain, rain shadow, biodiversity, deforestation, adaptation and nocturnal. Practise writing a short paragraph explaining one idea, such as the causes and consequences of deforestation, because the test includes paragraph writing.

### subtopic_2 — Three Natural Regions
**Assistant:** How are the Geography and History topics of Term 3 linked?

**Tutor:** At first they seem very different, but both are about how people live together and share the world. In Geography, we saw that people must make decisions about natural regions, such as whether to cut down rainforests or how to share scarce water in deserts. Those decisions affect animals, indigenous people and the climate for everyone. In History, we learned how a democracy makes decisions fairly, through elections, Parliament and courts, and how rights such as the right to a healthy environment are protected by the Constitution. So a democracy gives citizens a voice in decisions about the environment, and responsible citizens look after the natural world as well as each other.

### subtopic_3 — Democracy and Citizenship
**Assistant:** What should I remember about the History project on democracy?

**Tutor:** Your teacher will give you the exact instructions for the project, so read them carefully and ask if anything is unclear. In general, a good project uses information from more than one source, such as your textbook, library books, newspapers, trustworthy websites and interviews with family members about the 1994 election. Write in your own words instead of copying, and say where your information comes from. Include pictures, maps or timelines where they help. Plan your time, so that you work on it a little each week rather than all at once at the end. Before you hand it in, check that you have answered every part of the task and checked your spelling.
