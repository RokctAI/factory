### subtopic_1 — Reading an Annual Rainfall Map
**Assistant:** How do plants and animals in the Namib get water from fog?

**Tutor:** Along the coast of the Namib Desert, fog forms over the cold sea on many mornings and drifts inland. Some living things have special ways of using it. A small beetle, often called the fog-basking beetle, climbs to the top of a sand dune, faces the foggy wind and tilts its body forward. Tiny drops of fog collect on its back and run down into its mouth. Some lizards lick fog drops from their own bodies and eyes, and some plants and lichens soak up fog through their leaves and surfaces. People have learned from this too. In some dry, foggy places, communities hang large fine-mesh nets, called fog nets or fog catchers, which collect drops of water from the fog that then run into containers.

### subtopic_2 — Why Some Places Are Wet and Others Dry
**Assistant:** What is a monsoon?

**Tutor:** A monsoon is a seasonal change in the direction of the main winds, which brings a wet season and a dry season. The best-known example is in South Asia, especially India. In summer, the land heats up much more than the Indian Ocean, so moist winds blow in from the ocean over the land, bringing months of heavy rain. In winter, the winds change direction and blow from the land, so the weather is dry. The summer monsoon rains are very important for farmers, because they water the crops, but very heavy rain can also cause floods. Some of the wettest places on Earth, in north-east India, get thousands of millimetres of rain a year when monsoon winds meet steep hills. Remember that traders on the Indian Ocean used the monsoon winds to sail to and from Africa.

### subtopic_3 — Rainfall in South Africa
**Assistant:** Why are dams so important in South Africa?

**Tutor:** Because South Africa is a dry country and its rain is uneven, falling mostly in certain seasons and in the east, dams are built across rivers to store water for the dry months and dry years, and to send it to places that need it. The Gariep Dam on the Orange River is South Africa's largest dam by the amount of water it can hold. The Lesotho Highlands Water Project collects water in the high, wet mountains of Lesotho and sends it through tunnels to Gauteng, where millions of people live but there is not enough local water. Because water is so precious, droughts can be very serious, and everyone needs to use water carefully, for example by fixing leaking taps and not wasting water at home or at school.
