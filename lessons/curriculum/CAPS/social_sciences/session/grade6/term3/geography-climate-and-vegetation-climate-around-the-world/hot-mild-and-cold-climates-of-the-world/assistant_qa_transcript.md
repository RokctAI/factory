### subtopic_1 — Why Some Places Are Hot and Others Cold
**Assistant:** Why does Norway get so little daylight in winter?

**Tutor:** Because the Earth is tilted, in December and January the Northern Hemisphere leans away from the sun. Places far to the north, such as Norway, get only a few hours of daylight, and the sun stays low in the sky. North of the Arctic Circle, there are some weeks in winter when the sun does not rise above the horizon at all, which is sometimes called the polar night. In summer, the opposite happens. The Northern Hemisphere leans towards the sun, and places north of the Arctic Circle have days when the sun never sets, even at midnight. This is why parts of Norway are sometimes called the land of the midnight sun. In South Africa, which is much closer to the equator, the length of the day changes much less between summer and winter.

### subtopic_2 — Reading January and July Temperature Maps
**Assistant:** What is the hottest and coldest temperature ever measured?

**Tutor:** The hottest air temperature generally accepted by scientists is about fifty-seven degrees Celsius, recorded in Death Valley in the United States in 1913, although some scientists have questioned that old reading. Temperatures of over fifty degrees have also been measured in deserts in North Africa and the Middle East. The coldest temperature measured at a weather station is about minus eighty-nine degrees Celsius, at the Russian Vostok Station in Antarctica in 1983. Satellites have detected even colder spots on the high ice of Antarctica. In South Africa, very hot days of around forty-five degrees and more have been recorded in the Northern Cape, and the coldest temperatures are measured in high places such as the mountains of the Eastern Cape and the Drakensberg.

### subtopic_3 — Other Things That Affect Temperature
**Assistant:** Does South Africa ever get snow?

**Tutor:** Yes, though not in most towns. Snow falls most winters on the high mountains, such as the Drakensberg, the mountains of the Eastern Cape near Barkly East and Rhodes, and some mountains in the Western Cape near Ceres and Matroosberg. Sometimes cold fronts bring snow to high towns on the plateau, and very occasionally even to places such as Johannesburg, which had a well-known snowfall in August 2012. The snow usually melts within a few days. There is even a ski resort in the mountains of Lesotho, near the border with South Africa. Snow falls in these places because they are high above sea level, which shows how altitude affects temperature.
