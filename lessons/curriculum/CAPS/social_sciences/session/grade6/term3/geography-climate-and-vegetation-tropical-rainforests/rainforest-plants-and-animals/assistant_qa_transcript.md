### subtopic_1 — The Layers of the Rainforest
**Assistant:** How can a rainforest plant help to make medicine?

**Tutor:** Many medicines were first discovered in plants, and rainforests contain a huge number of plant species, so scientists study them carefully. A famous example is the rosy periwinkle, a small flowering plant from Madagascar. Scientists found substances in it that are used to make medicines that treat some kinds of cancer, including a type of leukaemia that affects children. Quinine, a medicine used for a long time to treat malaria, comes from the bark of the cinchona tree from the forests of South America. Many of the people who live in rainforests have deep knowledge of which plants can heal, and scientists sometimes learn from them. Because many rainforest plants have never been studied, there may be useful medicines that are lost when forests are cut down.

### subtopic_2 — How Rainforest Plants Are Adapted
**Assistant:** Why are rainforest frogs so brightly coloured if animals want to hide?

**Tutor:** Not all animals survive by hiding. Some, like the tiny poison dart frogs of Central and South America, have poison in their skin. Their bright colours, such as red, yellow, blue and green, act as a warning sign to birds, snakes and other predators that the frog is dangerous to eat. A predator that tries one and gets sick learns to leave brightly coloured frogs alone. This is called warning colouration. Many other rainforest animals use the opposite trick, called camouflage. Stick insects look like twigs, some moths look like dead leaves, and the spotted coat of a jaguar helps it disappear into the patchy light of the forest floor.

### subtopic_3 — Rainforest Wildlife
**Assistant:** How do sloths survive moving so slowly?

**Tutor:** Sloths live in the canopy of the forests of Central and South America and eat mostly leaves, which do not give them much energy. So they save energy by moving very slowly, sleeping for many hours a day and having a slow body. Moving slowly also helps them to hide, because predators such as eagles and jaguars notice movement. Their long, curved claws let them hang from branches without effort, even when asleep. Their fur is so damp that tiny green plants called algae grow in it, which may help them blend in with the leaves. Sloths come down to the ground only about once a week, to go to the toilet, which is when they are most in danger.
