### subtopic_1 — What Deforestation Is and Why It Happens
**Assistant:** Does South Africa have deforestation too?

**Tutor:** South Africa has only a small area of natural forest, less than one per cent of the country, so it is very precious. In the past, much of it was cut down. In the eighteen hundreds, woodcutters in the Knysna forest cut huge numbers of yellowwood and stinkwood trees for building, wagons and furniture, until the government began to control the cutting. Today, most of South Africa's indigenous forests are protected, and cutting the big trees is strictly limited. In some parts of the country, people still cut trees from forests and woodlands for firewood and building, which can damage them. South Africa also has large plantations of pine and gum trees for timber and paper, but these are farmed trees from other countries, not natural forest.

### subtopic_2 — The Consequences of Deforestation
**Assistant:** Why do people burn the rainforest?

**Tutor:** Burning is a quick and cheap way to clear land. Farmers and ranchers often cut down the trees in the dry part of the year, leave them to dry out and then set them on fire. The ash adds some nutrients to the soil for a short time, which helps grass or crops to grow at first. But the fires release large amounts of carbon dioxide and smoke into the air, and they can spread into forest that was not meant to be cleared, because a damaged forest dries out more easily. Smoke from big fire seasons in the Amazon has sometimes made the air unhealthy in cities far away. In many places, setting such fires without permission is against the law.

### subtopic_3 — Case Study: Saving the Amazon
**Assistant:** What is a protected area?

**Tutor:** A protected area is a place where the law limits what people may do, to protect nature. National parks are one kind. In South Africa, examples are the Kruger National Park and the Garden Route National Park, which includes much of the Knysna forest. In the Amazon, there are national parks and other reserves, and there are also large indigenous territories where the land belongs to the indigenous people who live there. Studies of satellite photographs have often found that these indigenous territories lose much less forest than land around them, because the people who live there protect it. Protected areas only work if the laws are enforced, and rangers and officials need support to stop illegal logging and mining.
