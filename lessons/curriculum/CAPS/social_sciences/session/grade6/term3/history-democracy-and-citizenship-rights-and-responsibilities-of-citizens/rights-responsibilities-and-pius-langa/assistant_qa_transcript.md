### subtopic_1 — Our Rights
**Assistant:** Why do we celebrate Human Rights Day on 21 March?

**Tutor:** Human Rights Day remembers the events of the twenty-first of March 1960 at Sharpeville, near Vereeniging. On that day, thousands of people gathered peacefully outside the police station to protest against the pass laws, which forced black South Africans to carry passes. The police opened fire on the crowd, and sixty-nine people were killed and many more were injured, many of them shot in the back as they ran away. The massacre shocked the world. After 1994, the twenty-first of March was declared Human Rights Day, so that South Africans would remember the people who suffered and died for their rights, and think about protecting human rights today. Remember that Mandela signed the Constitution at Sharpeville in 1996.

### subtopic_2 — Rights and Responsibilities
**Assistant:** What can I do if my rights are being abused?

**Tutor:** First, tell a trusted adult, such as a parent, teacher, principal or school counsellor, who can help you. If the problem is serious, such as abuse or violence, the police and social workers can step in. Children can also call Childline, a free helpline for children, on the number one one six. South Africa also has a body called the South African Human Rights Commission, which was set up by the Constitution to protect human rights. People can make complaints to it, and it investigates cases where rights may have been abused, including in schools. In some cases, people can go to court to protect their rights. You do not have to deal with it alone.

### subtopic_3 — Case Study: Pius Langa
**Assistant:** Who was Fatima Meer?

**Tutor:** Fatima Meer was another South African who fought for human rights, and some schools study her instead of Pius Langa. She was a writer, a university lecturer in Durban and an activist against apartheid. She was active in the Federation of South African Women, which organised the famous Women's March to the Union Buildings in Pretoria in 1956, where around twenty thousand women protested against pass laws for women. She was banned by the apartheid government, which means her movement and activities were restricted. She wrote many books, including one of the first biographies of Nelson Mandela, who was a close friend. She also started projects to build schools and help people in poor communities. She died in 2010.
