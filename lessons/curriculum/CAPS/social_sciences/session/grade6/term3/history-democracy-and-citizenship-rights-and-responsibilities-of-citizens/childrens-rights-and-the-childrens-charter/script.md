# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers children's rights and responsibilities and the Children's Charter of South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 6 class at a school in Bloemfontein, the capital of the Free State, during Child Protection Week, which is held in South Africa every year to raise awareness about keeping children safe. The class is designing posters to put up around the school. One group draws a baby with a birth certificate and writes every child has the right to a name. Another draws a family around a table: every child has the right to family care. A third draws a child with a schoolbag: every child has the right to basic education. A fourth group writes, in big red letters, no child should be abused or neglected, and adds the free Childline number, one one six. While they work, the teacher tells them something surprising: in 1992, before South Africa was a democracy, a group of children from all over the country met and wrote their own list of rights, called the Children's Charter of South Africa. The big idea is that children have special rights to protect them because they are young and vulnerable, that children helped to shape those rights, and that children also have responsibilities. Children's rights, responsibilities and the Children's Charter are examinable.

## Subtopic: Children's Rights in the Constitution

A child is anyone under the age of eighteen. Children have all the rights in the Bill of Rights, but because they are young and depend on adults, the Constitution also gives them special rights in section 28.

Every child has the right to a name and a nationality from birth. That is why a baby's birth must be registered, so that the child receives a birth certificate. Every child has the right to family care or parental care, or to suitable alternative care, such as foster care, if they are removed from their family. Every child has the right to basic nutrition, shelter, basic health care services and social services.

Every child has the right to be protected from maltreatment, neglect, abuse or degradation, which means being treated in a way that harms their dignity. Children must be protected from exploitative child labour, and may not be made to do work that is inappropriate for their age or that puts their health, education or well-being at risk. Children may not be used in armed conflict.

A child may be detained only as a last resort, for the shortest possible time, and must be kept separate from adults. Section 29 gives everyone the right to a basic education.

Most importantly, section 28 says that a child's best interests are of paramount importance in every matter concerning the child. Paramount means most important. This means that when adults, courts or the government make decisions about a child, they must put what is best for that child first.

South Africa has also agreed to follow international agreements on children's rights, such as the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child.

The questions for this section are with you now: the special rights of children in the Constitution.

## Subtopic: The Children's Charter of South Africa

In 1992, while South Africa was negotiating the end of apartheid, an international summit on the rights of children was held in Cape Town. Children from many different communities and regions across South Africa took part, together with adults who supported them. Many of these children had experienced violence, poverty and unequal education under apartheid.

The children discussed the problems in their lives and what they wanted to change, and together they drew up the Children's Charter of South Africa. What made the charter special was that it was written mainly by children themselves, to express their own views.

The charter called for all children to be treated equally, whatever their race, colour, gender, language, religion or disability. It called for children to be protected from violence, abuse and harassment, including violence by the police and security forces, which many children had experienced. It asked for the right to family life, free and equal education, health care, housing, food and clean water, and the right to play and to rest. It called for children to be protected from child labour, and it said that children have the right to express their own opinions and to be listened to on matters affecting them.

The charter was not a law, but it helped to show adults what children needed, and many of its ideas appear in the children's rights in the Constitution that was written a few years later.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Children's Charter of South Africa.

## Subtopic: Children's Responsibilities

Just like adults, children have responsibilities that go with their rights.

With the right to education comes the responsibility to go to school regularly, to do your best, to do your homework and to respect your teachers and classmates, so that everyone can learn. With the right to be protected from abuse comes the responsibility not to bully, hurt or insult other children. With the right to family care comes the responsibility to respect and help your family, for example by doing your share of chores. With the right to a healthy environment comes the responsibility to look after it, by not littering and by saving water and electricity. With the right to express your opinion comes the responsibility to listen to others and disagree respectfully.

Children who are being harmed should tell a trusted adult, such as a parent, teacher or school counsellor, or call Childline on one one six. Children also have a responsibility to help friends who are in danger by telling a trusted adult.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking children have no rights until they turn eighteen; children have rights from birth. Two: thinking the Children's Charter was written only by adults; it was written mainly by children. Three: thinking a child's best interests are just one thing to consider; they are of paramount importance. Four: thinking children's rights mean children have no responsibilities; rights and responsibilities go together.
