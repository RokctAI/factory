### subtopic_1 — Children's Rights in the Constitution
**Assistant:** What is the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child?

**Tutor:** The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child is an international agreement about the rights of children, adopted by the United Nations in 1989. It lists the rights that all children everywhere should have, such as the right to life, to a name and nationality, to education, to health care, to play, to be protected from violence and exploitation, and to have their views taken seriously. Almost every country in the world has agreed to it, which makes it one of the most widely accepted agreements in history. South Africa agreed to it in 1995, after becoming a democracy. In Africa, there is also the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child, which South Africa has also agreed to follow.

### subtopic_2 — The Children's Charter of South Africa
**Assistant:** What is the Day of the African Child?

**Tutor:** The Day of the African Child is held every year on the sixteenth of June, the same day as Youth Day in South Africa. It remembers the learners of Soweto who, on the sixteenth of June 1976, marched in protest against being forced to learn some of their school subjects in Afrikaans and against the poor education they received under apartheid. The police opened fire, and many young people were killed, including a schoolboy named Hector Pieterson, whose photograph became famous around the world. The African Union, the organisation of African countries, chose this date to honour these learners and to draw attention to the rights of children across Africa, especially the right to a good education.

### subtopic_3 — Children's Responsibilities
**Assistant:** Are children allowed to work in South Africa?

**Tutor:** The law protects children from harmful work. In general, children under the age of fifteen may not be employed, and older children under eighteen may not do work that is dangerous or harmful to their health, education or development, such as work in mines or with dangerous machines. There are some exceptions, for example child actors and performers, who may work only with special permission and under strict conditions. Helping at home with age-appropriate chores, such as washing dishes or tidying your room, is not child labour; it is part of being a responsible family member. Child labour means work that keeps children out of school, harms their health or exploits them, and it is against the law.
