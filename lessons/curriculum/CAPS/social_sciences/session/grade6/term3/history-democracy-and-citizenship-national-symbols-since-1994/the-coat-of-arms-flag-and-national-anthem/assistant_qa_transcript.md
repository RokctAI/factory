### subtopic_1 — The National Flag
**Assistant:** How should we behave when the flag is raised or lowered?

**Tutor:** When the national flag is raised or lowered, everyone present should stop what they are doing, stand still and face the flag. It is respectful to stay quiet until it has reached the top of the pole or has been fully lowered. The flag should be raised briskly and lowered slowly and with dignity. It should not touch the ground or be allowed to become dirty or torn. A damaged flag should be replaced and disposed of respectfully. On days of national mourning, such as after the death of an important leader, the flag may be flown at half-mast, which means halfway down the pole, as a sign of sadness and respect. Similar respect applies when the national anthem is sung.

### subtopic_2 — The Coat of Arms
**Assistant:** Why is the motto in a language that nobody speaks today?

**Tutor:** The /Xam language was once spoken by San people who lived in what is now the Northern Cape. By the late eighteen hundreds, the /Xam had suffered terribly from colonial violence and loss of their land, and very few speakers were left. Some of their stories and words were written down at that time by researchers who worked with /Xam people. When the coat of arms was designed, the motto was put into /Xam to honour the San as some of the first people of southern Africa, and to remember that their culture was nearly destroyed. Choosing a language that is not linked to any single group living today was also a way to include everyone equally. The motto reminds us that our unity is built on respect for all cultures.

### subtopic_3 — The National Anthem
**Assistant:** What are the other national symbols of South Africa?

**Tutor:** Besides the flag, coat of arms and anthem, South Africa has several national symbols from nature. The national animal is the springbok, a graceful antelope of the dry interior, famous for leaping high into the air, which is also the name of the national rugby team. The national bird is the blue crane, a tall, elegant grey-blue bird found mainly in South Africa. The national flower is the king protea, the largest of the protea flowers. The national tree is the real yellowwood, a tall tree of our indigenous forests, such as the Knysna forest. The national fish is the galjoen, found along the coast. Together, these symbols celebrate the country's rich and special natural heritage.
