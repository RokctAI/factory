### subtopic_1 — Mandela Becomes President
**Assistant:** What is an inauguration?

**Tutor:** An inauguration is a formal ceremony at which a new leader officially begins their time in office. At the inauguration of a South African president, the new president takes an oath, which is a solemn promise, in front of a judge, usually the Chief Justice. The president promises to be faithful to the country, to obey and uphold the Constitution and to do the job to the best of their ability. Since 1994, presidential inaugurations have usually been held at the Union Buildings in Pretoria, with guests from South Africa and other countries, music, a military parade and often a fly-past by the air force. Mandela's inauguration in 1994 was one of the largest gatherings of world leaders that South Africa had ever seen.

### subtopic_2 — The Government of National Unity
**Assistant:** Why did F. W. de Klerk release Nelson Mandela?

**Tutor:** By the late nineteen-eighties, apartheid was in deep trouble. Inside South Africa, there were strikes, protests and violence, and many people were demanding freedom. Outside South Africa, many countries had placed sanctions on South Africa, refusing to trade or play sport with it, which hurt the economy. When F. W. de Klerk became President in 1989, he decided that apartheid could not continue and that the government had to negotiate with the liberation movements. On the second of February 1990, he announced that banned organisations such as the ANC would be allowed again, and on the eleventh of February 1990, Nelson Mandela walked out of prison. In 1993, Mandela and de Klerk were jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for their work in ending apartheid peacefully.

### subtopic_3 — Healing and Rebuilding
**Assistant:** Did the Government of National Unity make the new Constitution?

**Tutor:** The new Constitution was written by the Constitutional Assembly, which was made up of all the members of the two houses of the new parliament elected in 1994. It worked from 1994 to 1996, and it asked the public for their ideas. Millions of submissions, many of them signatures on petitions, were sent in by ordinary people and organisations. The Constitution had to follow a set of principles agreed during the negotiations, and the new Constitutional Court checked that it did. On the tenth of December 1996, President Mandela signed the Constitution into law at Sharpeville, a place where police had killed many protesters in 1960. It came into effect in February 1997. We will study the Constitution in more detail in a later lesson.
