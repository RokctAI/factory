### subtopic_1 — Major Cities of the World
**Assistant:** Why are the pyramids so close to a modern city?

**Tutor:** The pyramids of Giza were built about four and a half thousand years ago as tombs for kings, called pharaohs, on a rocky plateau on the edge of the desert, just west of the Nile valley. They were placed there partly because the desert was dry and not used for farming, and because the west, where the sun sets, was linked with death in ancient Egyptian beliefs. For thousands of years, the pyramids stood near farmland and villages. But in the last century, Cairo and the city of Giza grew so fast that buildings and roads spread right up to the edge of the plateau. Today, the area is protected as a World Heritage Site, and Egypt has built a large new museum nearby to display ancient treasures.

### subtopic_2 — Case Study: Where and What Cairo Is
**Assistant:** Which is bigger, Cairo or Lagos?

**Tutor:** It depends on which estimate you use and how you draw the city's borders. Both are enormous and growing fast. Many estimates have placed Greater Cairo as the largest urban area in Africa, with more than twenty million people. Lagos, in Nigeria, is often said to be one of the fastest-growing cities in the world, and some estimates put its wider area at around fifteen to twenty million people or more, while others give lower figures. Kinshasa, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, is also growing very quickly. Because counting is so difficult, geographers usually say these are among Africa's largest cities rather than giving one exact ranking.

### subtopic_3 — Why Cairo Grew There
**Assistant:** Why is Egypt building new cities in the desert?

**Tutor:** Cairo is very crowded, with traffic jams, air pollution and pressure on housing, water and services. The fertile land of the Nile valley and delta is also precious for farming, and every time the city spreads onto it, farmland is lost. To reduce crowding and protect farmland, Egypt has built a number of new towns and cities in the desert around Cairo, such as New Cairo, and is building a large new administrative capital east of Cairo, where many government offices are moving. These new cities need water piped from the Nile, roads, and public transport. Some people welcome the extra space, while others worry about the cost and whether ordinary people can afford to live there.
