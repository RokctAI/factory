## Subtopic: Major Cities of the World

A city is a large urban settlement. Some cities have grown so big that they are called megacities, which means cities with more than ten million people in their wider urban area.

On a world map of major cities, each city is often shown with a circle or dot, and the size of the circle shows the size of the population. The key explains what each size means. Such a map shows that many of the largest cities are in Asia, such as Tokyo in Japan, Delhi and Mumbai in India, Shanghai and Beijing in China, and Dhaka in Bangladesh. Others include São Paulo in Brazil, Mexico City in Mexico, New York in the United States, and, in Africa, Cairo, Lagos in Nigeria and Kinshasa in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Estimates of city populations vary a great deal, because cities draw their borders in different ways, and counting people in fast-growing cities is difficult. Tokyo is often named as the world's largest urban area, with well over thirty million people in its wider area.

In South Africa, the largest cities are Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban in the eThekwini municipality, Ekurhuleni and Tshwane, which includes Pretoria. According to the 2022 census, each of these metropolitan municipalities had roughly four to five million people. Johannesburg, Ekurhuleni and Tshwane are so close together in Gauteng that they form one huge urban region.

## Subtopic: Case Study: Where and What Cairo Is

Cairo is the capital city of Egypt, in the north-east of Africa. It lies on the banks of the River Nile, the longest river in Africa, about one hundred and sixty kilometres south of the Mediterranean Sea.

Egypt is almost entirely desert. The country gets very little rain, and most of its land is part of the Sahara. But the Nile flows through Egypt from south to north, and along its banks there is a narrow strip of green, fertile land. Near the coast, the river splits into many branches and spreads out into a wide, fan-shaped area of fertile land called the delta. Almost all of Egypt's more than one hundred million people live in the Nile valley and delta, which make up only a small part of the country's land.

Cairo stands at a special point: just south of where the narrow Nile valley opens out into the delta. Greater Cairo, which includes the city of Giza on the west bank, has more than twenty million people by many estimates, making it one of the largest urban areas in Africa and the Middle East.

The city has a very long history. The ancient Egyptian capital Memphis stood nearby, and the pyramids of Giza, on the edge of today's city, were built about four and a half thousand years ago. The city called Cairo was founded in 969, on the site of earlier settlements.

## Subtopic: Why Cairo Grew There

Cairo's location can be explained by several reasons.

First, water. In a desert country, the Nile provides water for drinking, washing, farming and industry. Without the river, a huge city could not exist there.

Second, fertile land. For thousands of years, the Nile flooded each year and left behind rich, dark mud, which made the soil very fertile. Farmers could grow wheat and other crops to feed many people. Today, the Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, controls the floods.

Third, transport and trade. The Nile was a natural highway for boats, carrying goods and people up and down the country. Cairo's position, where the valley meets the delta, made it a meeting point for routes between the south and north of Egypt. It also lies on trade routes between Africa, Asia and Europe, and the Suez Canal, opened in 1869, is not far away.

Fourth, government and history. As the capital for more than a thousand years, Cairo attracted rulers, soldiers, scholars and traders, and today it is the centre of Egypt's government, universities, business and tourism.

Rapid growth brings challenges, including traffic jams, air pollution, overcrowded housing and pressure on water and services. Egypt has built new cities in the desert nearby to share the load.
