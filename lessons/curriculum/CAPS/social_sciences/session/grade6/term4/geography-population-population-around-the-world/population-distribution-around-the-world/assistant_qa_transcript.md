### subtopic_1 — Crowded Regions of the World
**Assistant:** Why is Africa's population growing so fast?

**Tutor:** Africa's population is growing faster than any other continent's for several reasons. In many African countries, families have more children on average than in Europe or East Asia, and the population is very young, so many people are of an age to have children. At the same time, improvements in health care, vaccines and clean water mean that more babies survive and people live longer than in the past. The United Nations expects Africa's population to keep growing for many decades, so that a growing share of the world's people will be African. A young population can be a great strength if young people get good education and jobs, but it also means countries must build many more schools, clinics and homes.

### subtopic_2 — Empty Regions of the World
**Assistant:** Will the world's population keep growing for ever?

**Tutor:** Most experts do not think so. The world's population grew very fast during the last century, from under two billion in 1900 to more than eight billion today, mainly because fewer people died young thanks to better food, medicine and clean water. But in many countries, families are now choosing to have fewer children. In some countries, such as Japan, Italy and China, the population has already started to shrink. The United Nations projects that the world's population will probably keep growing for several more decades, perhaps to over ten billion, and then level off or slowly decline later this century. Projections like these can change.

### subtopic_3 — Reasons for the Pattern
**Assistant:** What would a map of the world's people look like if it were drawn by population?

**Tutor:** Some mapmakers draw special maps, called cartograms, where the size of each country is changed to match its population instead of its land area. On a population cartogram, India and China become enormous, much bigger than all of the Americas together, and countries such as Bangladesh, Indonesia, Nigeria and Japan swell up too. Russia, Canada and Australia, which are huge on a normal map, shrink to small shapes, because few people live there. Greenland almost disappears. Africa looks larger than on some maps, because of its big population. South Africa would look a little smaller than on a normal map. These maps help us picture where the world's people really live.
