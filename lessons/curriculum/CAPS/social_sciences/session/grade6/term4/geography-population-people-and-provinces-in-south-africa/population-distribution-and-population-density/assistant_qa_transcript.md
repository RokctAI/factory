### subtopic_1 — Population Distribution
**Assistant:** What is the most densely populated place in the world?

**Tutor:** Some of the most densely populated places in the world are small city territories, where many people live on a very small area of land. Examples are Monaco, a tiny country on the coast of France, Singapore, an island city-state in Southeast Asia, and Hong Kong and Macau, which are special regions of China. In these places, there are many thousands of people per square kilometre, and many live in tall apartment buildings. Among large countries, Bangladesh is one of the most densely populated, with well over one thousand people per square kilometre on average, most of them living on the flat, fertile land of great river deltas. Densities in some parts of big cities, such as Dhaka, Mumbai and Cairo, can be even higher.

### subtopic_2 — Population Density
**Assistant:** Which province is the most and least densely populated?

**Tutor:** Gauteng is South Africa's most densely populated province by far. It is the smallest province by area, but it has the most people, so its density is many hundreds of people per square kilometre. The Northern Cape is the least densely populated. It is the largest province, covering nearly a third of South Africa's land, but it has the fewest people, so its density is only a few people per square kilometre. The difference is huge. We will look at the population and density of all nine provinces using graphs in the next lesson, so you can compare them for yourself.

### subtopic_3 — Why South Africa's Population Is Spread Unevenly
**Assistant:** Why is population density useful for planning?

**Tutor:** Population density helps governments and businesses decide where services are needed and what kind. In densely populated areas, many people can share services that are close together, such as schools, clinics, buses, trains, shops and water pipes, but these services can easily become overcrowded, so planners must build enough of them. In sparsely populated areas, people live far apart, so it is expensive to bring electricity lines, water pipes and roads to every home, and people may have to travel long distances to reach a clinic or school. Planners may use mobile clinics, solar power and boreholes in such areas. Knowing the density helps to share resources more fairly.
