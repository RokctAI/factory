# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers population distribution and population density, and how to read a population distribution map of South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 6 class at a school in Ermelo, a farming and coal-mining town in Mpumalanga. Their teacher starts with an experiment. The forty learners measure their classroom with a long tape measure and find that it is about eight metres long and seven metres wide, so its area is about fifty-six square metres. Then they all go outside to the school field, which is about one hundred metres long and sixty metres wide, an area of six thousand square metres, and spread themselves out across it. Standing far apart in the open, they laugh at how empty the field looks. Back in class, the teacher asks what changed. The number of learners was the same, but they were spread over a much bigger area, so the field was much less crowded. Then she hands out a map of South Africa covered in dots, crowded in some places and almost absent in others. The big idea is that population density tells us how crowded a place is, and population distribution tells us the pattern of where people live. Population distribution, density and distribution maps are examinable.

## Subtopic: Population Distribution

Population distribution means the way people are spread out over an area. It describes the pattern of where people live.

Population is never spread evenly. Some areas are densely populated, which means many people live close together. Other areas are sparsely populated, which means few people live there, far apart. Some areas may have no people at all.

A population distribution map shows this pattern. One common kind is a dot map. Each dot stands for a certain number of people, for example ten thousand people, and the key tells you how many. Where the dots are crowded together, the area is densely populated. Where they are spread out, it is sparsely populated. Another kind of map uses shading, with darker colours for areas where more people live and lighter colours where fewer live.

On a population distribution map of South Africa, the dots crowd together in some areas. The biggest cluster is in Gauteng, around Johannesburg, Pretoria and Ekurhuleni. There are also clusters around Durban and along the KwaZulu-Natal coast, around Cape Town, around Gqeberha and East London, and in parts of Limpopo, Mpumalanga and the Eastern Cape. The dots are very spread out across the Northern Cape, the Karoo and the western Free State, which are sparsely populated.

## Subtopic: Population Density

Population density measures how crowded a place is. It is the average number of people living in each square kilometre of land.

To calculate population density, divide the number of people by the area of the place in square kilometres.

Population density equals population divided by area.

For example, if a town has twenty thousand people and covers an area of ten square kilometres, its population density is twenty thousand divided by ten, which equals two thousand people per square kilometre.

Here is another example. Imagine two villages that each have three thousand people. Village A covers thirty square kilometres, so its density is three thousand divided by thirty, which is one hundred people per square kilometre. Village B covers only three square kilometres, so its density is three thousand divided by three, which is one thousand people per square kilometre. The villages have the same number of people, but Village B is ten times more crowded. This shows why we always need both the population and the area to compare how crowded places are.

For the Ermelo classroom, forty learners divided by fifty-six square metres gives about zero point seven learners per square metre, while forty learners on six thousand square metres gives less than one learner for every hundred square metres. The field has a much lower density.

South Africa had about sixty-two million people in the 2022 census and has an area of about one point two million square kilometres. So the average population density of South Africa is about fifty people per square kilometre. But remember that this is an average. In parts of Gauteng and in townships such as Khayelitsha and Soweto, thousands of people live in each square kilometre, while in parts of the Northern Cape, there may be fewer than one person per square kilometre.

The questions for this section are with you now: calculating population density.

## Subtopic: Why South Africa's Population Is Spread Unevenly

South Africa's population distribution is shaped by the natural and human reasons we have studied.

Rainfall and climate matter. The east of the country is wetter and better for farming, so more people live there, while the dry west, the Karoo and the Kalahari have few people.

Jobs matter. Gauteng is the economic heart of the country, with mining, factories, offices and shops, so millions of people live there. Port cities such as Durban, Cape Town and Gqeberha also have many jobs.

History matters too. Under apartheid, many black South Africans were forced to live in homelands, many in the Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal and Limpopo, and in townships on the edges of cities. Many of these areas are still densely populated today, even where there are few jobs.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: mixing up distribution and density; distribution is the pattern, density is people per square kilometre. Two: thinking population density means the total population; it is population divided by area. Three: thinking an average density means people are evenly spread; some parts are crowded and others nearly empty. Four: thinking one dot on a dot map is one person; each dot stands for many people, as the key shows.

The questions for this section are with you now: why South Africa's population is spread unevenly.
