### subtopic_1 — Reading Graphs
**Assistant:** How do I draw a good bar graph?

**Tutor:** Start with a clear title that says what the graph shows, including the date and the source of the numbers, such as the 2022 census. Draw two axes with a ruler: a horizontal axis along the bottom and a vertical axis up the left side. Label the horizontal axis with the categories, such as the names of the provinces, spaced evenly. Choose a scale for the vertical axis that fits your largest value, with equal steps, for example one million people for each centimetre, and label it with the units. Draw each bar with a ruler, all the same width, with equal gaps between them, up to the correct value. You can colour the bars, but keep it neat, and check every bar against your numbers.

### subtopic_2 — Population of the Provinces
**Assistant:** Why do population numbers differ between sources?

**Tutor:** Population figures can differ because they come from different sources, dates and methods. A census counts people at one time, but between censuses, Stats SA publishes yearly estimates, called mid-year population estimates, which use information on births, deaths and migration. International organisations, such as the United Nations, make their own estimates too. Some figures are for a city's municipality, and others for a larger metropolitan area. Rounding also makes small differences. And every census misses some people, so numbers are adjusted. That is why good Geography answers say where a figure comes from and use words such as about or roughly, and why you should always use the figures given in a question.

### subtopic_3 — Population Density of the Provinces
**Assistant:** Which province will have the most people in the future?

**Tutor:** Nobody can know for certain, but planners make projections, which are careful predictions based on current trends. If people keep moving to Gauteng for jobs and services, and its population keeps growing faster than most provinces, it is likely to remain the province with the most people for many years. The Western Cape has also been growing quickly. Projections can change if things such as the economy, job opportunities, birth rates or government policies change. For example, if more jobs were created in other provinces, fewer people might move to Gauteng. Projections help governments plan how many schools, clinics, houses and water supplies each province will need in the future.
