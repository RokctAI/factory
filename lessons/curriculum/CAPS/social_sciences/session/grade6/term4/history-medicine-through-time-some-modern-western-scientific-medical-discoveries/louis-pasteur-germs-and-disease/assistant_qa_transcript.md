### subtopic_1 — Old Ideas About Disease
**Assistant:** Is all milk in South Africa pasteurised?

**Tutor:** Most milk sold in South African shops is pasteurised, or treated at even higher temperatures to make long-life milk, which can be kept unopened without a fridge for months. There are rules about selling milk safely, because raw milk, which comes straight from the cow without heating, can sometimes carry harmful germs that cause serious illnesses, such as tuberculosis in cattle and other infections. Some people who live on farms drink raw milk, and they are usually advised to boil it first. Pasteurisation does not make milk last for ever, so pasteurised milk must still be kept in the fridge and used by the date on the carton. Pasteur's discovery helps keep millions of people safe every day.

### subtopic_2 — Pasteur Proves Germ Theory
**Assistant:** What is the difference between bacteria and viruses?

**Tutor:** Both bacteria and viruses are types of germs that can cause disease, but they are very different. Bacteria are tiny single-celled living things that can be seen with an ordinary microscope. Many bacteria are harmless or even helpful, such as those in our stomachs that help digest food, but some cause diseases such as tuberculosis and cholera. Bacteria can often be killed with medicines called antibiotics. Viruses are much smaller and can only be seen with very powerful electron microscopes. They can only multiply inside living cells. Viruses cause diseases such as flu, measles, HIV and rabies. Antibiotics do not work against viruses, but vaccines can prevent many viral diseases. Pasteur's rabies vaccine worked even though he could not see the virus.

### subtopic_3 — Vaccines and Pasteur's Legacy
**Assistant:** Why is washing your hands so important?

**Tutor:** Our hands touch many surfaces, such as door handles, desks, phones, money and other people, and they can pick up germs. When we touch our eyes, nose or mouth, or handle food, these germs can get into our bodies and make us ill. Washing hands with soap and clean water for about twenty seconds, especially before eating, after using the toilet and after coughing or sneezing, removes most germs. This simple habit helps prevent diarrhoea, colds, flu and many other infections. It is one of the cheapest and most effective ways to protect health, and it is a direct result of the discoveries of Semmelweis and Pasteur that germs cause disease.
