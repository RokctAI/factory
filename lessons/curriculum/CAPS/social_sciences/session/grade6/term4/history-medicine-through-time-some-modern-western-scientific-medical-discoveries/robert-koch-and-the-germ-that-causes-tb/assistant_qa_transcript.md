### subtopic_1 — Robert Koch and His Methods
**Assistant:** Why does TB need such a long treatment?

**Tutor:** TB germs grow and multiply very slowly, and some of them can hide in the body in a resting state, where medicines have difficulty reaching them. Medicines work best on germs that are actively growing. So the treatment has to continue for months, to make sure that every last germ is killed, including the slow and hidden ones. Usually, several medicines are taken together, because using more than one makes it much harder for the germs to become resistant. After a few weeks, most people feel much better, but many germs are still alive. That is why patients are encouraged to take every dose until the end, and clinics often support them with reminders and treatment buddies.

### subtopic_2 — Discovering the Germ That Causes TB
**Assistant:** What is drug-resistant TB?

**Tutor:** Drug-resistant TB is TB caused by germs that are no longer killed by the usual medicines. It can develop when people do not take their medicine properly, for example if they stop too early or miss many doses, because the strongest germs survive and multiply. Drug-resistant TB can also spread from person to person, just like ordinary TB. It is much harder to treat, needs different medicines, and in the past needed up to two years of treatment with many side effects. In recent years, newer medicines have made treatment shorter and easier, and South Africa has been one of the countries using them. But preventing resistance, by always finishing treatment, is still very important.

### subtopic_3 — TB in South Africa Today
**Assistant:** How did people treat TB before medicines were discovered?

**Tutor:** Before the first effective medicines were discovered in the nineteen-forties, there was no reliable cure for TB. Doctors believed that fresh air, rest and good food helped the body fight the disease, so many patients were sent to special hospitals called sanatoriums, often in the mountains or by the sea, where they rested outdoors for months, even in cold weather. Some patients recovered, but many did not. In some cases, doctors used surgery to collapse part of an infected lung so that it could rest. The discovery of antibiotics such as streptomycin in the nineteen-forties, followed by other medicines, finally made it possible to cure TB.
