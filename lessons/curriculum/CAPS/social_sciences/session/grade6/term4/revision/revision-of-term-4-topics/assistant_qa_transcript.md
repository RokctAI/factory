### subtopic_1 — People in South Africa
**Assistant:** How should I study for the Term 4 test?

**Tutor:** Start with Geography and practise the skills: reading a dot map, reading bar graphs of the provinces, and calculating population density by dividing the population by the area. Make a list of the reasons for the location of settlements, with a South African example for each, and a table of push and pull factors. Learn the case study of Cairo with at least four reasons for its location. For History, make a timeline of the medical discoveries with dates and names: Jenner 1796, Pasteur's germ theory in the eighteen-sixties, Koch 1882, anaesthetics in the eighteen-forties, Lister 1865, Röntgen 1895, Landsteiner 1901 and Barnard 1967. Remember that the test also includes some Term 3 content, so revise that too.

### subtopic_2 — People Around the World
**Assistant:** How are the Geography and History topics of Term 4 linked?

**Tutor:** Both topics are about people's lives and wellbeing. In Geography, we saw that people settle where they can find water, food and work, and that crowded cities can have problems such as overcrowding, poor sanitation and the spread of disease. In History, we learned that germs spread more easily in crowded places, which is why TB spread so fast in crowded nineteenth-century cities and still spreads in crowded, poorly ventilated rooms today. Clean water, good housing and health care, which are linked to where and how people live, are just as important for health as famous medical discoveries. So planning settlements well and making medicine available to everyone go hand in hand.

### subtopic_3 — Medicine Through Time
**Assistant:** Which discovery in medicine do you think was most important?

**Tutor:** There is no single right answer, and historians often debate questions like this. You could argue for Pasteur's germ theory, because it explained why people get infections and led to vaccines, pasteurisation, antiseptic surgery and better hygiene, so it affected almost everything else. You could argue for Jenner's vaccine, because it began the fight against infectious diseases and led to the eradication of smallpox. You could argue for anaesthetics, because without them most modern surgery would be impossible. In a test, choose one, state your view clearly, and support it with at least two pieces of evidence, such as dates, people and results. That is how historians build an argument.
