### subtopic_1 — Rural and Urban Areas
**Assistant:** Why do so many people travel at Christmas and Easter in South Africa?

**Tutor:** Because so many South Africans have moved to cities for work while keeping strong links with their rural homes, holidays are a time to go home. In December especially, millions of people travel from cities such as Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban to towns and villages in provinces such as the Eastern Cape, Limpopo and KwaZulu-Natal, and to neighbouring countries. They visit family, attend weddings, initiation ceremonies and other events, and look after their rural homes. Taxis, buses and roads become very busy, and road safety campaigns are run to try to reduce accidents. In January, the movement reverses as people return to work and school in the cities. This shows how many families live between two places.

### subtopic_2 — Why People Move to Towns and Cities
**Assistant:** Are there people who move from cities to rural areas?

**Tutor:** Yes, although fewer people move that way. Some people move from cities to small towns or rural areas when they retire, because life is quieter, cheaper and safer, and some return to the villages where they grew up. Others move to rural areas for work, for example in farming, mining, tourism or teaching. With better internet connections, some people can now work from home in smaller towns. Towns along the coast and in beautiful areas, such as parts of the Western Cape and the Garden Route, have attracted people from big cities. Moving from cities to smaller places is sometimes called counter-urbanisation. In South Africa, however, the main movement is still from rural to urban areas.

### subtopic_3 — Results of Urbanisation
**Assistant:** What is being done about informal settlements?

**Tutor:** The government and municipalities use several approaches. They build subsidised houses, such as RDP houses, and provide serviced sites with water, toilets and electricity on which people can build. Some informal settlements are upgraded in place, which means that instead of moving people, the settlement is given roads, taps, toilets, electricity and proper streets, and people are given ownership of their plots. Where settlements are on dangerous land, such as flood plains, people may be moved to safer areas. Community organisations also work to improve living conditions. Because many people keep arriving in cities, the need for housing is very large, and it remains one of South Africa's biggest challenges.
