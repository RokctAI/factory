### subtopic_1 — South Africa's Healing Plants
**Assistant:** Is rooibos tea a medicine?

**Tutor:** Rooibos is a plant that grows naturally only in the Cederberg area of the Western Cape, as we learned in the fair trade lesson in Term 2. Khoikhoi and San people are said to have used it long ago, and it has been used as a traditional remedy, for example by some families to calm babies with stomach cramps, and on the skin. Today, rooibos is mainly enjoyed as a drink all over the world. It has no caffeine, and scientists have found that it contains natural substances called antioxidants, which are being studied for possible health benefits. But a healthy drink is not the same as a medicine that has been proven to cure an illness, so it should not replace medical treatment.

### subtopic_2 — From Traditional Remedy to Modern Medicine
**Assistant:** How do scientists test whether a plant medicine really works?

**Tutor:** First, scientists talk to healers and communities about how they use the plant, with their permission. Then, in a laboratory, they separate the different substances in the plant and test each one on cells or germs, to see whether it has an effect, for example killing bacteria. If a substance looks promising, they test whether it is safe, and how much is needed. Only after many careful steps is it tested on people, in trials where some people get the medicine and others get a dummy treatment, so that the results can be compared fairly. This process can take many years, and most substances tested do not become medicines, but the ones that pass are known to be safe and effective.

### subtopic_3 — Protecting Plants and Knowledge
**Assistant:** Why do some medicinal plants grow only in South Africa?

**Tutor:** South Africa has an unusually rich variety of plants, partly because it has so many different climates, soils and landscapes in one country, from winter rainfall fynbos to summer rainfall grasslands, deserts and forests. The Cape Floral Kingdom, in the south-western Cape, is one of the richest areas for plants in the world for its size, and thousands of its plant species grow nowhere else. Over millions of years, plants adapted to these special conditions and developed in their own ways. Many plants produce substances to protect themselves from insects, diseases and grazing animals, and some of these substances can affect the human body, which is why some plants are useful as medicines and others are poisonous.
