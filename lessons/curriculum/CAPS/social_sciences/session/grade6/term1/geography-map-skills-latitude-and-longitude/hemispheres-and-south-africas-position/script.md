# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how the Earth is divided into hemispheres by the equator, the Greenwich Meridian and the one hundred and eighty degree line, and where South Africa lies. The anchor is a Grade 6 class from Bredasdorp, in the Western Cape, on an outing to Cape Agulhas, the most southern point of the continent of Africa. The wind is strong, and waves crash against the dark rocks. A plaque on the ground marks the southern tip of Africa, and a sign explains that this is where the Atlantic Ocean and the Indian Ocean are officially said to meet. The teacher asks each learner to stand next to the plaque and say which half of the world they are standing in. Some say the south, some say the east, and one learner says both. The teacher smiles, because that learner is right. The big idea is that the Earth can be cut into halves in two different ways, and that every place is in one northern or southern half and one eastern or western half. South Africa's position in the hemispheres is examinable.

## Subtopic: North and South

The word hemisphere means half a sphere, or half a ball. Because the Earth is round, any line that cuts it exactly in half makes two hemispheres.

The first cut is along the equator, at zero degrees latitude. The equator divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere. Everything north of the equator is in the Northern Hemisphere, and everything south of it is in the Southern Hemisphere.

The Northern Hemisphere has much more land than the Southern Hemisphere. Europe, North America and most of Asia are in the Northern Hemisphere, and so is the northern part of Africa. The Southern Hemisphere has Australia, Antarctica, most of South America and the southern part of Africa. It has a lot more ocean than land.

The seasons are opposite in the two hemispheres. When it is winter in South Africa, in June and July, it is summer in Europe. When we celebrate New Year in the middle of summer, people in Canada are in the middle of winter. This happens because the Earth's axis is tilted, so each hemisphere takes turns to lean towards the sun.

The questions for this section are with you now: the equator and the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

## Subtopic: East and West

The second cut runs from pole to pole. It follows the Greenwich Meridian, at zero degrees longitude, on one side of the Earth, and the one hundred and eighty degree line of longitude on the other side. Together these two lines make a full circle around the Earth, through both poles. This circle divides the Earth into the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere.

Everything east of the Greenwich Meridian, up to one hundred and eighty degrees, is in the Eastern Hemisphere. Everything west of it, up to one hundred and eighty degrees, is in the Western Hemisphere. Most of Africa, Europe, Asia and Australia are in the Eastern Hemisphere. North America and South America are in the Western Hemisphere.

The Greenwich Meridian passes through London, then through France and Spain, and then through West Africa, through countries such as Algeria, Mali, Burkina Faso, Togo and Ghana, before it crosses the Atlantic Ocean towards Antarctica. Because both the equator and the Greenwich Meridian cross Africa, Africa has land in all four hemispheres. The equator and the Greenwich Meridian meet at a point in the Atlantic Ocean, in the Gulf of Guinea, south of Ghana.

The one hundred and eighty degree line runs mostly through the Pacific Ocean. The International Date Line, where the calendar date changes, follows it closely, but bends in places so that small island countries are not split into two different days.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Greenwich Meridian, the one hundred and eighty degree line and the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

## Subtopic: Where South Africa Lies

Now we can describe South Africa's position. South Africa is south of the equator, so it is in the Southern Hemisphere. It is east of the Greenwich Meridian, so it is in the Eastern Hemisphere. South Africa is in both the Southern Hemisphere and the Eastern Hemisphere.

We can be even more exact with degrees. South Africa lies roughly between twenty-two degrees south, at the Limpopo River in the north, and thirty-five degrees south, at Cape Agulhas in the south. It lies roughly between sixteen degrees east, near the mouth of the Gariep River on the west coast, and thirty-three degrees east, near Kosi Bay on the north-east coast. Cape Agulhas itself is at about thirty-five degrees south and twenty degrees east.

South Africa also owns two small islands far out in the Southern Ocean, Marion Island and Prince Edward Island, at about forty-seven degrees south and thirty-eight degrees east. They are cold, windy and have no permanent residents, but South African scientists live there for months at a time to study the weather, the seabirds and the seals. They are also in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres, much further south than Cape Agulhas.

Other countries can be described in the same way. Australia is in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres, like South Africa. India is in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres. The United States is in the Northern and Western Hemispheres. Most of Brazil is in the Southern and Western Hemispheres.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a place can only be in one hemisphere; every place is in a northern or southern half and in an eastern or western half. Two: thinking the Greenwich Meridian alone divides east and west; it works together with the one hundred and eighty degree line. Three: thinking South Africa is in the Western Hemisphere because it is on the left of many world maps; it is east of Greenwich. Four: thinking the seasons are the same everywhere; they are opposite in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

The questions for this section are with you now: South Africa's hemispheres and its position in degrees.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the windy plaque at Cape Agulhas — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Top Half, Bottom Half

The equator cuts the Earth into north and south halves.

Keep the line around the middle in mind and try the questions for this part: north and south.

## Subtopic: Left Half, Right Half

Greenwich and the one hundred and eighty degree line cut the Earth into east and west halves.

Hold the pole-to-pole circle in mind and try this part's questions: east and west.

## Subtopic: South and East

South Africa is south of the equator and east of Greenwich.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A hemisphere is half of the Earth. The equator divides the Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The Greenwich Meridian and the one hundred and eighty degree line divide it into the Eastern and Western Hemispheres. Africa has land in all four. South Africa is in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres, roughly between twenty-two and thirty-five degrees south and sixteen and thirty-three degrees east.

Hold the class standing beside the plaque at the tip of Africa in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: where South Africa lies.
