### subtopic_1 — Atlases and Positions
**Assistant:** How do I remember which is latitude and which is longitude?

**Tutor:** Here are a few tricks that many learners find helpful. Latitude sounds a bit like ladder, and lines of latitude look like the rungs of a ladder going across the globe. Another trick is to think of lat as flat: lines of latitude lie flat, across the map. Longitude lines are long, because they stretch all the way from the North Pole to the South Pole. When you write a position, remember that latitude comes first, just as l-a comes before l-o in the alphabet. Then practise. Take an atlas, choose five cities you know, and write their latitude and longitude, always with north or south and east or west. Then do the opposite: ask a friend to give you some degrees and find the place. A few minutes of practice every day is far better than a long session the night before the test.

### subtopic_2 — Scale and Distance
**Assistant:** How do I answer a source-based question about Mapungubwe?

**Tutor:** First, study the source carefully before you look at the questions. If it is a picture, such as the golden rhino or a drawing of the hill, look at every detail, including the caption. If it is a piece of writing, read it twice. Ask yourself: what is this source, who made it, and when? Next, read each question and underline the key words. Name means just give the name. Describe means say what something is like. Explain means say why or how. Then find the evidence in the source for your answer. Many marks come from using clues from the source itself, so quote or point to them. Use your own knowledge from the lessons when the question asks for it. Look at the marks: a two-mark question usually needs two clear points. Write in full sentences.

### subtopic_3 — Mapungubwe
**Assistant:** How should I write a History paragraph about Mapungubwe?

**Tutor:** A good paragraph has three parts. Start with a topic sentence that answers the question directly. For example: Mapungubwe was the first state in southern Africa. Next, write three or four sentences that give evidence and details. For example: From about 1220 AD, a king ruled over many people and a large area. He was a sacred leader who lived apart from ordinary people on top of Mapungubwe Hill. He controlled the trade in gold and ivory, which made him rich and powerful. Society was divided into classes, and the royal family was buried with gold objects such as the golden rhino. Finally, write a closing sentence that sums up your answer. For example: In these ways, Mapungubwe was much more organised than earlier farming villages. Keep to the topic, link your sentences, and check your spelling at the end.
