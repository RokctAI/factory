### subtopic_1 — Grade 6 History
**Assistant:** Why is the golden rhino so important to South Africans?

**Tutor:** The golden rhino is important for several reasons. First, it is beautiful and shows the great skill of metal workers in southern Africa about eight hundred years ago. Second, it was found in a royal grave, which tells us that Mapungubwe had powerful leaders who were buried with precious objects. Third, it helps to prove that southern Africa had complex, wealthy kingdoms long before Europeans arrived. In the past, under colonial rule and apartheid, some people wrongly claimed that Africans had not built anything important, and the story of Mapungubwe was not taught widely in schools. Today, the golden rhino has become a symbol of African achievement and heritage. That is one reason why South Africa named a national honour, the Order of Mapungubwe, after the kingdom. It reminds us that our history goes back a very long way.

### subtopic_2 — Remembering the Golden Rhino
**Assistant:** How do archaeologists know how old something is?

**Tutor:** Archaeologists use several clues together. One clue is the layers of soil. Over time, new layers of soil and rubbish build up on top of older ones, so objects deeper down are usually older. Another clue is the style of objects. Pottery styles changed over time, so a certain kind of pot with certain decorations can show roughly when people lived there. A very important method is called radiocarbon dating. All living things, such as trees, animals and people, contain a tiny amount of a special kind of carbon that slowly disappears after they die, at a known speed. By measuring how much is left in a piece of charcoal, seed or bone, scientists can work out roughly how long ago it died. Imported objects help too: Chinese pottery of a known style can show that a layer is not older than when that style was made.

### subtopic_3 — How We Know About the Past
**Assistant:** Can we still visit Mapungubwe today?

**Tutor:** Yes. Mapungubwe is part of the Mapungubwe National Park, in the far north of Limpopo, west of Musina. The park protects both the wildlife and the archaeological sites, and it is part of a World Heritage Site. Visitors can go to an interpretation centre, a building with displays that tell the story of the kingdom. On guided tours, visitors can climb up to the top of Mapungubwe Hill, using wooden stairs, and see where the royal family lived and where the graves were found. Visitors can also see where the Limpopo and Shashe Rivers meet, at a viewpoint looking into Botswana and Zimbabwe. The original gold objects are kept safely in the museum in Pretoria, though copies and pictures help visitors imagine them. Remember that at heritage sites, we must never move or take anything.
