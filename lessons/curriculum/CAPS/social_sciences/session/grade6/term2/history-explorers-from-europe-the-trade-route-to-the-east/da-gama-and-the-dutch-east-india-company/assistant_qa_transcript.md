### subtopic_1 — Vasco da Gama Sails to India
**Assistant:** Why did da Gama sail so far out into the Atlantic instead of following the coast?

**Tutor:** Dias had found that sailing close to the west coast of Africa was slow, because the winds and currents near the coast often blow against ships going south. Sailors had learned that out in the middle of the South Atlantic, the winds move in a huge circle. By sailing far out to the south-west, a ship could catch westerly winds further south that would carry it east towards the tip of Africa. This made the journey faster overall, even though the ships were out of sight of land for about three months. It was a bold choice, and it needed good navigation with the compass and by measuring the height of the sun and stars. Later Portuguese fleets followed a similar wide curve, and one of them, in 1500, swung so far west that it reached Brazil.

### subtopic_2 — Along the East Coast to Calicut
**Assistant:** Who was the pilot from Malindi?

**Tutor:** The ruler of Malindi gave da Gama a pilot who knew the Indian Ocean and its monsoon winds very well, and who guided the ships across to Calicut in less than a month. For a long time, many books said that this pilot was Ahmad ibn Majid, a famous Arab navigator who wrote books about sailing. Today, many historians think this is unlikely, and that the pilot may have been an experienced sailor from Gujarat, in India, whose name we do not know for certain. What matters most is that da Gama could only cross the ocean so quickly because of the knowledge of local sailors, who had been using these sea routes for centuries before the Portuguese arrived.

### subtopic_3 — The Dutch at the Cape
**Assistant:** Who were Autshumao and Krotoa?

**Tutor:** Autshumao was a Khoikhoi leader who lived near Table Bay before the Dutch station was set up. He had learned some English and Dutch from sailors, and he worked as an interpreter and helper for passing ships, so Europeans called him Harry. Krotoa was his niece. As a young girl, she went to live and work in the household of Jan van Riebeeck, where she learned Dutch and later Portuguese. She became an important interpreter between the Dutch and the Khoikhoi, which was a hard role, because neither side fully trusted her. She later married a Danish surgeon in the VOC's service, but her later life was difficult. Today both are remembered as important people in the early history of the Cape.
