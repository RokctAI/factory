### subtopic_1 — The Khoikhoi at Mossel Bay
**Assistant:** What is the Post Office Tree in Mossel Bay?

**Tutor:** The Post Office Tree is an old milkwood tree near the spring at the Dias Museum Complex. According to tradition, in 1500, a Portuguese captain whose fleet had been hit by a storm left a letter at Mossel Bay, possibly inside an old shoe hung in a tree, describing what had happened. The next year, another Portuguese fleet found the letter. After this, passing sailors sometimes left letters there for other ships to collect and take home, so it became an early kind of post office. Historians are not certain that today's tree is the very same tree, but it is very old and is a protected national monument. Today there is a post box shaped like a boot next to the tree, and if you post a letter there, it receives a special postmark. It is a reminder of how important Mossel Bay became as a stopping place on the sea route.

### subtopic_2 — What Happened in 1488
**Assistant:** Could the meeting at Mossel Bay have ended peacefully?

**Tutor:** Perhaps, if both sides had found a way to understand each other. Some later meetings between Portuguese sailors and the Khoikhoi did begin peacefully, with exchanges of gifts, music and dancing, and trade of cattle and sheep for metal, beads and cloth. For a peaceful meeting, the sailors would have needed to ask permission before taking water, show respect for the herders and their animals, and offer something in return. The Khoikhoi would have needed to feel that the strangers were not a threat to their water and cattle. Without a shared language, this was very difficult, and fear on both sides made violence more likely. Historians cannot change what happened, but asking how things might have gone differently helps us understand the causes of conflict and how people can resolve disagreements today.

### subtopic_3 — Two Points of View
**Assistant:** What happened to Bartolomeu Dias after 1488?

**Tutor:** After returning to Portugal in 1488, Dias helped to prepare the ships for the next great voyage, led by Vasco da Gama, using his experience to suggest stronger ships with square sails for the open ocean. In 1500, he sailed again as one of the captains in a large Portuguese fleet heading for India, led by Pedro Álvares Cabral. That fleet swung so far west into the Atlantic that it reached the coast of Brazil, which Portugal then claimed. As the fleet crossed the South Atlantic towards the Cape, a violent storm struck, and several ships were lost, including the ship commanded by Dias. He died at sea near the Cape of Good Hope, the stormy cape he had been the first European to sail past.
