### subtopic_1 — Adding Value
**Assistant:** Why does the farmer get so little of the price of tomato sauce?

**Tutor:** The farmer is at the start of a long chain, and many other people and businesses add work and costs after the tomatoes leave the farm. The factory pays for machines, electricity, workers, bottles, sugar and spices. Transport companies pay for trucks and fuel. Shops pay for buildings, staff and refrigerators. Each business also wants a profit. So the money a consumer pays for a bottle of sauce is shared among many people, and the farmer's share may be quite small. Farmers also often sell to a few big buyers, who can push prices down, especially when there are lots of tomatoes on the market at the same time. Some farmers try to earn more by adding value themselves, for example by making their own jams, sauces or dried fruit, or by selling directly to customers at farm stalls and markets.

### subtopic_2 — Comparing Values
**Assistant:** Why are diamonds so expensive if they are just carbon?

**Tutor:** Diamonds are made of carbon, the same element as the graphite in your pencil, but the carbon atoms are packed together in a very strong pattern, formed deep underground under great heat and pressure. Good-quality diamonds are rare, so they are hard to find, and mining them is expensive. Rough diamonds look like dull pebbles, and expert cutters must cut and polish them carefully to bring out their sparkle, which adds a lot of value. Diamonds are also the hardest natural substance, so lower-quality diamonds are useful in industry, for cutting and drilling. A big part of their high price also comes from the fact that many people want them for jewellery, especially engagement rings, and companies have advertised them as special for many years. So a diamond's value comes from being rare, beautiful, useful and wanted.

### subtopic_3 — Why It Matters
**Assistant:** Can I add value to something myself?

**Tutor:** Absolutely, and many young people do. If you buy a bag of oranges and squeeze them into fresh juice to sell at a school event, you have added value with your work and skill. If you collect old jeans and sew them into bags, you have added value. If you grow herbs and dry and pack them in neat little bags with labels, you have added value. Even a car wash adds value, because a clean car is worth more to its owner. To add value successfully, you need to think about your costs, such as ingredients, packaging and time, and charge a price that covers them and gives you a fair profit. You also need customers who want what you make. This is how many small businesses start, and it is a good way to learn about trade in real life.
