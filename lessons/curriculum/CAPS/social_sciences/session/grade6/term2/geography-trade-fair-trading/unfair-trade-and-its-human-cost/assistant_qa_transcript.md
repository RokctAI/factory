### subtopic_1 — Fair and Unfair Trade
**Assistant:** If I buy cheap clothes, am I doing something wrong?

**Tutor:** Not at all. Most families need to stretch their money, and buying affordable clothes is sensible. The point of this lesson is not to make anyone feel guilty, but to help us understand that prices do not always tell the whole story. You can still make good choices within your budget. Look after your clothes so they last longer, pass them on to others when they no longer fit, and buy second-hand clothes, which saves money and waste. When you can, find out a little about where things come from, and support shops and brands that treat workers fairly. As you grow older, you might also support laws and organisations that protect workers. Big changes in fairness usually happen because many people, governments and companies work together, not because one learner stops buying a T-shirt.

### subtopic_2 — The Human Cost
**Assistant:** Why don't factory workers just find better jobs?

**Tutor:** For many workers, especially in poorer countries, there are very few other jobs available. Some have moved from rural areas where farming no longer earns enough, and a factory job, even with low pay, may be better than no job at all. Many have families depending on them, so they cannot risk losing their income. Some do not have the education or skills for better-paid work, often because they had to leave school early. In some places, workers who complain or try to join a trade union may lose their jobs. That is why laws, inspections, unions and pressure from companies and customers are so important: they help to raise wages and improve safety for everyone, so that workers do not have to choose between a dangerous job and no job.

### subtopic_3 — What Can Be Done
**Assistant:** Does unfair trade happen in South Africa too?

**Tutor:** Yes, unfair practices can happen anywhere, including in South Africa. Some farm workers, domestic workers and workers in small businesses have been paid less than the minimum wage or have worked in poor conditions. Some small-scale farmers struggle to get fair prices from big buyers, and some people from neighbouring countries are exploited because they are afraid to complain. South Africa has strong laws to protect workers, including the Constitution, which says everyone has the right to fair labour practices, and laws on minimum wages, working hours and child labour. There are trade unions and government departments to enforce these laws. But laws only work when people know their rights and the laws are enforced. South Africa also has positive projects, including fair trade farms, which we will look at in the next lesson.
