### subtopic_1 — Spreading Christianity
**Assistant:** What happened to the cross that Dias placed at Kwaaihoek?

**Tutor:** Over the centuries, the original padrão that Dias placed near Kwaaihoek was broken, and its pieces were scattered and buried in the sand and rocks. In 1938, a South African historian, Professor Eric Axelson, searched the area using old Portuguese records and found broken pieces of the pillar and cross. The pieces were carefully put back together, and the restored padrão is kept at the University of the Witwatersrand, in Johannesburg. A copy now stands on the headland at Kwaaihoek, so that visitors can see where it once stood. The discovery helped historians confirm how far east Dias sailed before he turned back. It is a good example of how historians use written sources, such as old ship records and maps, together with objects found on the ground, to check the details of the past.

### subtopic_2 — Crossing the Sea to Find the East
**Assistant:** Why did Columbus think the world was smaller than it is?

**Tutor:** Columbus, like most educated Europeans of his time, knew that the Earth was round. The idea that people in his time thought the Earth was flat is a myth. His mistake was about its size. Scholars had different estimates of the Earth's distance around. Columbus chose one of the smallest estimates, and he also believed that Asia stretched much further east than it really does. Putting these two mistakes together, he calculated that Japan was only a few thousand kilometres west of Europe. Many experts told him he was wrong, and they were right: the real distance to Asia going west is far greater. If the Americas had not been in the way, his ships would probably have run out of food and water long before reaching Asia. He was lucky that there were lands where he expected to find none.

### subtopic_3 — God, Gold and Glory
**Assistant:** Did the Portuguese force people to become Christians?

**Tutor:** In some places, yes, and in other places they tried to persuade people. In parts of Africa, Asia and South America, Portuguese and Spanish rulers and priests put pressure on local people to give up their own beliefs and become Christians, sometimes using threats, force or rewards. In some places, such as the kingdom of Kongo, in Central Africa, a local king chose to become Christian and worked with the Portuguese for a time. Many people who became Christians mixed the new religion with their own traditions. In southern Africa, there was little missionary work by the early Portuguese explorers, who were mainly passing by on their way to India. Later, from the 1700s and 1800s, missionaries from many European countries came to southern Africa. Today, South Africa's Constitution protects freedom of religion for everyone.
