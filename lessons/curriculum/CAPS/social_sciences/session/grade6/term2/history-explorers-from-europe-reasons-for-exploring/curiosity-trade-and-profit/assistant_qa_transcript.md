### subtopic_1 — Curiosity
**Assistant:** Did people really think the sea boiled at the equator?

**Tutor:** Some Europeans did believe this, or something like it. Ancient Greek thinkers had suggested that the Earth had a very hot zone around its middle, where it was too hot for people to live or travel. In the 1400s, some sailors feared that if they sailed too far south, the sun would be so fierce that the sea would boil, ships would burn or people would turn black. Sea charts sometimes showed monsters in unknown waters. These ideas came from a lack of information, not from evidence. Of course, Arab, Indian, Swahili and African sailors and travellers already lived and sailed in the tropics without any boiling seas. As Portuguese ships sailed further south and came back safely, these myths faded, which is a good example of how observation and evidence can replace fear.

### subtopic_2 — Spices and Silk
**Assistant:** Is pepper still valuable today?

**Tutor:** Pepper is still one of the most traded spices in the world, but it is no longer rare or very expensive, and almost every family can afford it. Today, pepper is grown in many tropical countries, such as Vietnam, Brazil, Indonesia, India and Sri Lanka, and it is shipped cheaply in large amounts. This shows how trade changes over time. Five hundred years ago, pepper passed through many middlemen and travelled slowly, so it was expensive. Once European ships sailed directly to Asia, and later as pepper was grown in more places, its price fell. Some spices remain expensive today, such as saffron, which comes from the tiny threads inside a crocus flower and must be picked by hand, and vanilla, which also needs a lot of careful handwork. Price depends on supply, demand and the cost of producing and transporting goods.

### subtopic_3 — Gold and Profit
**Assistant:** Why do we learn about the slave trade in this topic?

**Tutor:** Historians try to tell the whole story of the past, including the painful parts. The voyages of exploration brought new knowledge, trade and contact between peoples, but they also led to conquest, the taking of land and the slave trade. Millions of Africans were captured and forced across the Atlantic Ocean to work on plantations and in mines in the Americas, where they and their children were treated as property. Many died on the terrible voyages. This had a huge impact on Africa, the Americas and the world, and its effects are still felt today. Learning about it helps us understand why human rights are so important, and why our Constitution protects the dignity and equality of every person. It also teaches us to ask whose story is being told, and who paid the price for other people's profits.
