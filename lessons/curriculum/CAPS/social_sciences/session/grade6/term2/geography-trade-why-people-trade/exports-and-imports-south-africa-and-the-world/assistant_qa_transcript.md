### subtopic_1 — Exports
**Assistant:** Why do we import things that we could make ourselves?

**Tutor:** Sometimes another country can make a product more cheaply or better, so it makes sense to buy it. For example, some countries have huge factories that make millions of cellphones, so each phone costs less to make than it would in a small factory here. Sometimes we cannot make enough of something, such as wheat in a dry year. And sometimes consumers simply want more choice. But importing goods we could make ourselves can cost jobs in South Africa, if local factories close because they cannot compete. That is why governments sometimes protect local industries, for example by adding taxes on some imports, or by encouraging people to buy local products. Many people try to support local businesses by looking for labels that say proudly South African or made in South Africa. Getting the balance right is a big challenge for every country.

### subtopic_2 — Imports
**Assistant:** How long does it take a ship to reach China from Durban?

**Tutor:** It depends on the ship, its speed, its route and how many ports it stops at on the way. A large container ship travelling from Durban across the Indian Ocean to a port in China might take roughly two to four weeks, including stops. Ships carrying coal or iron ore usually sail directly and fairly slowly, to save fuel. An aeroplane can make a similar journey in less than a day, but flying goods is far more expensive, so it is only used for light, valuable or urgent goods. Ships are slow, but one large ship can carry thousands of containers at once, which makes the cost of moving each item very low. That is why most of the world's trade, by weight, still travels by sea, just as it did in the time of the dhows, though much faster and on a much bigger scale.

### subtopic_3 — How Goods Cross Borders
**Assistant:** Can South Africa run out of things to export?

**Tutor:** Some of our exports could run out one day. Minerals such as gold, coal and platinum are non-renewable resources. Once they are dug out of the ground and used, they cannot be replaced. South Africa's gold mines, for example, are now very deep and produce much less gold than they did fifty years ago. That is why it is important for the country to use mineral wealth wisely and to build other kinds of exports. Farm products, such as fruit and wine, are renewable, because new crops can be grown every year, as long as we look after the soil and water. Manufactured goods and services, such as cars, machines, tourism and computer services, depend mainly on people's skills, which can keep growing. Many people believe that South Africa's future depends on making and selling more finished goods, not only raw materials.
