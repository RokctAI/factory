### subtopic_1 — Trade and Why People Trade
**Assistant:** How should I study for the Term 2 test in Social Sciences?

**Tutor:** Start by making two short lists, one for Geography and one for History. For Geography, write the key words with their meanings: trade, goods, services, barter, surplus, export, import, raw material, manufactured good, value, exploitation and fair trade. For History, make a timeline with the most important dates: Mapungubwe around 1220 to 1300, the Renaissance from the 1400s, Dias in 1488, da Gama in 1497 to 1498, the VOC formed in 1602 and van Riebeeck in 1652. Then practise explaining one chain, such as cocoa to chocolate, in your own words, and practise describing the Mossel Bay meeting from both points of view. Short sessions every day work better than one long session the night before.

### subtopic_2 — Value and Fair Trade
**Assistant:** How are the Geography and History topics of Term 2 linked?

**Tutor:** They are both about trade. In Geography, we learned that people trade because places have different resources, and that goods often travel long distances, with value added at each stage. In History, we saw the same ideas long ago. Europeans wanted spices and silk from the East, but the goods passed through many middlemen and were very expensive, so the Portuguese searched for their own sea route around Africa. That search brought Dias and da Gama to southern Africa, and later the Dutch set up a station at the Cape to serve their trade ships. The question of who gets a fair share of trade also links both topics, from cocoa farmers today to the Khoikhoi who lost their land.

### subtopic_3 — Explorers from Europe
**Assistant:** What is the difference between the Portuguese and the Dutch at the Cape?

**Tutor:** The Portuguese, such as Dias and da Gama, sailed past the Cape and stopped at places such as Mossel Bay for water and food, but they did not build a settlement at the Cape. Their main interest was reaching India and controlling the spice trade. The Dutch came later. The Dutch East India Company, formed in 1602, was a trading company, and in 1652 it sent Jan van Riebeeck to set up a refreshment station at Table Bay, with a fort and a vegetable garden. Over time, this small station grew, VOC farms spread onto Khoikhoi land, and it became a settlement that changed the history of South Africa.
