### subtopic_1 — What the Renaissance Was
**Assistant:** Did the Renaissance happen in Africa too?

**Tutor:** The word Renaissance is used for a particular period in European history, so we do not usually use it for Africa at that time. But Africa had its own centres of learning, art and trade, long before and during the European Renaissance. In West Africa, the city of Timbuktu, in what is now Mali, was a famous centre of Islamic learning in the 1400s and 1500s, with scholars, libraries and thousands of handwritten books on religion, law, mathematics and astronomy, many of which still survive. In what is now Nigeria, artists in the kingdom of Benin made remarkable bronze and brass sculptures. On the East African coast, Swahili towns built stone mosques and traded across the Indian Ocean. And in southern Africa, kingdoms such as Great Zimbabwe built impressive stone walls. So creativity and learning were flourishing in many parts of the world.

### subtopic_2 — New Ideas in Art and Learning
**Assistant:** How did the printing press change people's lives?

**Tutor:** Before the printing press reached Europe, most books were copied by hand, letter by letter, often by monks. One book could take months to copy, so books were very expensive, and only rich people, churches and universities owned them. After Gutenberg's press, a printing workshop could make hundreds of copies of a book in the time it once took to copy one by hand. Books became cheaper, so more people could buy them, and more people learnt to read. Ideas, news, maps, science and religious arguments spread quickly across Europe, and people in different countries could read the same books. Some historians compare its impact to the impact of the internet today, because it changed how quickly information could reach large numbers of people.

### subtopic_3 — A Turning Point
**Assistant:** Why were rich families willing to pay artists?

**Tutor:** Rich families, such as the Medici in Florence, had made their fortunes from trade and banking. Paying for beautiful paintings, sculptures, churches and palaces showed everyone how wealthy, powerful and generous they were, a bit like how some rich people and companies today sponsor sports teams, buildings or festivals. Many patrons also genuinely loved art and learning, and wanted their cities to be admired. Some paid for religious art, to honour God and to be remembered for their good works. Artists needed patrons, because art took a long time to make and materials such as paint, marble and gold were expensive. In return, patrons sometimes asked to be painted into the artworks themselves. This system helped to produce some of the most famous art in history.
