# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers new ideas and inventions that made European voyages of exploration possible, especially gunpowder, the magnetic compass and the caravel, and the influences on Europe from other parts of the world. The anchor is a Grade 6 class in Saldanha, a fishing and port town on the West Coast of the Western Cape. The teacher has made two small model boats. One has a square sail across its middle. The other has a triangular sail fixed along a long pole at an angle. She puts both boats in a tub of water and switches on a fan. When the fan blows from behind, both boats move well. But when the fan blows from the side and slightly in front, the boat with the square sail is pushed backwards, while the boat with the triangular sail can be angled to keep moving forward. Then she places a compass, a picture of a cannon and a model ship on the table. The big idea is that new inventions and ideas, many of them borrowed from other parts of the world, gave European sailors the tools they needed for long, dangerous ocean voyages. These inventions and their origins are examinable.

## Subtopic: Gunpowder

Gunpowder is an explosive mixture of three ingredients: saltpetre, charcoal and sulphur. When it is lit, it burns extremely quickly and produces hot gases that can push a cannonball or a bullet out at great speed.

Gunpowder was invented in China, probably more than a thousand years ago, around the ninth century AD. According to historical records, Chinese scholars who were experimenting with mixtures discovered it. At first, it was used for fireworks and signals, and later for weapons such as fire arrows, bombs and early guns.

Knowledge of gunpowder spread westwards along trade routes, through Central Asia and the Middle East, and it reached Europe by about the 1200s. Over the next two hundred years, Europeans developed cannons and handheld guns.

By the 1400s, Portuguese and other European ships carried cannons. This gave European sailors a powerful advantage in battles at sea and in attacks on coastal towns. It helped them to force their way into trade networks, such as the Indian Ocean trade, that had been controlled by others. So gunpowder was not only an invention; it also changed the balance of power between peoples, often in ways that caused great harm.

The questions for this section are with you now: what gunpowder is, where it was invented and how it helped European explorers.

## Subtopic: The Compass and the Stars

On the open sea, with no land in sight, sailors need to know which way they are going. On cloudy days and nights, they cannot see the sun or the stars.

The magnetic compass solved this problem. A compass has a magnetised needle that always points roughly north, whatever the weather. People in China knew about magnetic stones, called lodestones, for a very long time, and they were using magnetic needles to find direction at sea by around the eleventh century AD. Knowledge of the compass reached European sailors by about the late 1100s, possibly through Arab traders or travellers. With a compass, sailors could keep a steady direction for days.

Sailors also needed to know how far north or south they were, which is latitude. Instruments such as the astrolabe and the quadrant helped. The astrolabe was developed by ancient Greek scholars and greatly improved by scholars in the Arab and Islamic world. Portuguese sailors used simple versions to measure the height of the sun at midday, or of certain stars above the horizon. Using tables of numbers worked out by astronomers, they could then calculate their latitude.

Mapmakers also drew better sea charts, showing coastlines, harbours and directions, which were improved with every voyage.

Portuguese sailors also learnt the patterns of the winds and ocean currents of the Atlantic. They discovered that, to sail south past Africa, it was often better to swing far out to the west, into the open ocean, to catch helpful winds, rather than to hug the coast. This knowledge was as valuable as any instrument.

The questions for this section are with you now: the magnetic compass, the astrolabe and how sailors found their way.

## Subtopic: The Caravel

The caravel was a small, light and fast sailing ship developed by the Portuguese in the 1400s. It was the main ship used to explore the west coast of Africa, and Bartolomeu Dias sailed around the southern tip of Africa with caravels in 1488.

The caravel had several important features.

It used triangular sails, called lateen sails, fixed to long poles set at an angle. Lateen sails had long been used on Arab dhows in the Indian Ocean and on ships in the Mediterranean Sea. They allowed the caravel to sail closer into the wind, just like the model boat in Saldanha, so it could make progress even when the wind was not behind it. Some caravels also had square sails, which were good for speed when the wind was behind.

It was small and sat fairly high in the water, so it could sail close to the coast, into bays and up rivers, which was important for exploring unknown coastlines.

It had a rudder at the back, which made it easier to steer. The stern rudder had also been used earlier in China.

It was strong enough for long voyages across the open ocean, but small enough to need only a few dozen sailors.

So the caravel combined ideas from Europe, the Arab world and elsewhere into a new ship that was well suited to exploration.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Europeans invented gunpowder; it was invented in China. Two: thinking the compass was invented in Portugal; it was first used for navigation in China. Three: thinking the caravel's triangular sail was a Portuguese idea; it came from Arab and Mediterranean ships. Four: thinking inventions only brought good things; cannons were used to attack people and take control of trade.

The questions for this section are with you now: the caravel and how it combined ideas from different places.
