### subtopic_3 — The San
**Assistant:** Why do people disagree about the names San and Bushmen?

**Tutor:** Because names carry history. Bushmen was the name used by Dutch and later English colonists, and it was often used with contempt, so many people find it offensive. San was originally a Khoikhoi word for people who did not own livestock, and some say it meant something like foragers or even had a negative sense, but it became the preferred term in academic writing. Some groups, however, have chosen to use the word Bushmen themselves, with pride, for example in parts of Namibia and Botswana, and in 1997 a meeting of southern African representatives accepted both terms. Many people prefer to be known by the name of their own group, such as the !Xam, who lived in the Northern Cape, the ju/'hoansi of Namibia and Botswana, or the ‡Khomani of the Kalahari. The names Khoikhoi and Khoe are also used, and the combined term Khoisan or Khoesan is sometimes used for both groups together. The best practice for a historian is to explain what term you are using, to be respectful, and where possible to use the names people use for themselves.

### subtopic_2 — How to Write a History Essay
**Assistant:** Can an essay ever have more than three body paragraphs?

**Tutor:** Yes, definitely. The number of body paragraphs depends on the question, the marks available and the time you have. A short Grade 7 essay might have three body paragraphs, but a longer one could have four, five or more. What matters is that each paragraph deals with one main point, and that all the paragraphs together answer the question. A useful habit is to plan with a simple table or mind map: write the question in the middle, then branch out the main points, and under each point list the evidence you will use. Then decide on the order of paragraphs, often chronological in history, or from the most important point to the least. Teachers usually mark essays using a rubric that rewards a clear line of argument, relevant evidence, good structure and clear language. Simply listing every fact you remember, without linking it to the question, will earn fewer marks than a shorter essay with a strong argument. Practise turning facts into arguments by asking: why does this fact matter for the question?

### subtopic_4 — The Khoikhoi
**Assistant:** How do we know about Khoikhoi life before 1652 if they did not write?

**Tutor:** Historians and archaeologists use several kinds of evidence. Archaeology is very important: excavations at sites such as caves and shell middens, heaps of shells and bones left by people who ate shellfish, reveal bones of sheep and cattle, pottery, tools and beads, and can be dated, showing when herding arrived at the Cape, about two thousand years ago. Rock art shows sheep, cattle and people in different dress. Accounts by Portuguese, Dutch and English sailors who visited the Cape before 1652 describe Khoikhoi people, trade and conflicts, although these writers often misunderstood or looked down on what they saw. Language studies trace how Khoe languages are related to each other. Oral traditions and the knowledge of descendants, including Nama people in Namibia and the Northern Cape whose language is a Khoe language, also give insights. Each type of evidence has strengths and weaknesses, so historians compare them. Together they show that the Khoikhoi had complex societies, with leaders, laws, trade networks, beliefs and a deep knowledge of the land and animals.
