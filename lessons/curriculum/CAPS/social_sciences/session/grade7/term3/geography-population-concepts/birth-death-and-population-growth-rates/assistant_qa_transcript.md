### subtopic_1 — Population and the Census
**Assistant:** How can a census count sixty-two million people in one night?

**Tutor:** It cannot literally visit everyone in one night, but it counts everyone as they were on one chosen night, called census night. Before the census, Stats SA divides the whole country into small areas called enumeration areas, each about a hundred to two hundred households, using maps and satellite images so that no area is missed or counted twice. Over a few weeks, around a hundred thousand fieldworkers, called enumerators, visit every household in their area and record everyone who was there on census night. In 2022, for the first time in South Africa, people could also complete the census online or by telephone, and enumerators used tablets rather than paper. Special teams count people in hostels, hospitals, prisons and those who are homeless. Afterwards, a smaller follow-up survey checks a sample of areas to estimate how many people were missed, and the final figures are adjusted. That is why the census results are released many months later and are described as estimates.

### subtopic_2 — Birth Rate and Death Rate
**Assistant:** Why do poorer families often have more children?

**Tutor:** Mainly for practical and social reasons that make sense in their circumstances, and these change as conditions change. In rural farming communities, children help with work such as herding, fetching water and caring for younger children, so they contribute to the family economy. Where there are no pensions or social grants, children are the main support for parents in old age. Where many children die young, parents may have more children to ensure that some survive. Cultural and religious traditions may value large families. And where girls leave school early and marry young, and where family planning services are hard to reach, women tend to have more children over their lifetimes. When these conditions change, birth rates fall. Education, especially for girls, is the strongest single factor: women who stay longer in school tend to marry later and have fewer children. Better health care that keeps children alive, access to contraception, pensions, and moving to cities, where children are more expensive and less needed for farm work, all lower birth rates. South Africa's birth rate has fallen a lot since the 1960s for these reasons.

### subtopic_3 — Natural Increase and Growth Rate
**Assistant:** What is the rule of seventy and why does it work?

**Tutor:** It is a quick way to estimate how long it takes something that grows at a steady percentage rate to double in size. Divide seventy by the yearly growth rate, written as a number of percent, and you get the approximate doubling time in years. At one percent, about seventy years; at two percent, about thirty-five; at three point five percent, about twenty years. It works because of the way compound growth behaves: each year's growth is added to the total, and the next year's growth is calculated on the larger total, so growth speeds up in absolute numbers even when the percentage stays the same. The mathematics of compound growth, which you will meet in more detail in later grades, shows that the doubling time is close to seventy divided by the percentage for small growth rates. The same rule is used for money in a savings account earning interest, and for prices rising with inflation. For population, it shows why even growth rates that sound small, such as two or three percent, lead to very large increases within a lifetime.
