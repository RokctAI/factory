The ground under your feet feels solid and still, but it is moving, a few centimetres every year. Today we travel to the centre of the Earth. We learn about its three main layers, the crust, the mantle and the core, how hot and how thick each one is, and how we know, since nobody has ever drilled through the crust. Then we discover that the crust is broken into giant pieces called tectonic plates, which slowly move apart, collide and slide past each other, and we see how that movement explains volcanoes and earthquakes.
