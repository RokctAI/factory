Most resistance to slavery was quiet and everyday, but sometimes enslaved people rose up openly against their captors. Today we study two famous rebellions. In 1831 Nat Turner, an enslaved preacher in Virginia, led a violent revolt that terrified the slaveholding South. In 1839 a group of captive Africans led by Sengbe Pieh, known as Joseph Cinque, seized the Spanish ship Amistad, and their case ended up before the Supreme Court of the United States. We examine what happened, why, how people reacted, and how historians judge these events today.
