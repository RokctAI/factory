### subtopic_2 — The World Map of Volcanoes
**Assistant:** Why do people live near dangerous volcanoes?

**Tutor:** For several good reasons, which is why hundreds of millions of people do. The most important is fertile soil. Volcanic ash and lava break down into soils rich in minerals such as potassium and phosphorus, which grow excellent crops: the slopes of Kilimanjaro grow coffee and bananas, and the volcanic soils of Java in Indonesia support some of the densest farming populations on Earth. Volcanic areas also offer geothermal energy, used to generate electricity in Kenya, Iceland and New Zealand, valuable minerals, building stone, hot springs, and tourism. Many volcanoes erupt only rarely, so people may live for generations without seeing an eruption and come to feel safe. And for many people there is no real choice: their families, land and livelihoods are there, and moving would mean losing everything. Modern monitoring helps. Scientists watch for warning signs such as small earthquakes, swelling of the ground and changes in gases, which allows warnings and evacuations, though predicting the exact timing of an eruption remains very difficult.

### subtopic_3 — Why Volcanoes Occur
**Assistant:** If South Africa has no active volcanoes, why are there volcanic rocks here?

**Tutor:** Because the African Plate has not always been as quiet as it is today, and South Africa contains rocks from some of the most ancient volcanic activity on Earth. About one hundred and eighty million years ago, as the supercontinent Gondwana began to break apart, enormous volumes of basalt lava poured out across southern Africa. The layers of dark basalt that cap the Drakensberg and form much of Lesotho are the remains of these lava flows, which once covered a vast area and were more than a kilometre thick in places. Even older is the Pilanesberg, a ring-shaped complex in North West province, the remains of a volcano that erupted about one thousand three hundred million years ago; its ring of hills is now a game reserve. The Bushveld Complex, which contains the world's largest reserves of platinum, formed from magma about two thousand million years ago. And the diamonds of Kimberley were brought up from deep in the mantle by explosive eruptions in narrow pipes called kimberlites. So South Africa's volcanic past is the reason for some of its greatest mineral wealth.

### subtopic_4 — Kinds of Eruption and Volcano
**Assistant:** Can a volcanic eruption really cool the whole world?

**Tutor:** Yes, a large explosive eruption can, for a year or two. When a big volcano explodes, it blasts sulphur dioxide gas high into the stratosphere, above the weather, where there is no rain to wash it out. There the gas forms tiny droplets of sulphuric acid, a haze that spreads around the globe and reflects some of the sun's energy back into space before it reaches the ground. After Mount Pinatubo erupted in the Philippines in 1991, global average temperatures fell by about half a degree Celsius for over a year. A much bigger example was Tambora in Indonesia in 1815, the largest eruption in recorded history. The following year, 1816, was called the year without a summer in Europe and North America, with snow in June, failed harvests and food shortages. Volcanic ash alone falls out of the air within days or weeks, so it is the sulphur gas that matters for climate. Scientists study these events to understand how the atmosphere works, which helps them understand climate change too.
