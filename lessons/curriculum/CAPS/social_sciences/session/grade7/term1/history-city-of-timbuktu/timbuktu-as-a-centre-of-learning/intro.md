Five hundred years ago, students travelled hundreds of kilometres across the desert to study in Timbuktu. Today we discover how a trading town became one of the great centres of learning in the world. We look at the scholars and the mosques where they taught, how students learned, and what they studied: mathematics, astronomy, medicine, history, geography, Islamic religion, government and law. We meet Ahmad Baba, Timbuktu's most famous scholar, and see why the city's learning is a powerful answer to the false idea that Africa had no history of written scholarship.
