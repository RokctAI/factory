For centuries, Europeans used the name Timbuktu to mean the most distant, mysterious place on Earth. But for the people of West Africa and North Africa it was a busy, real city at the meeting point of the desert and the river. Today we read the city through the eyes of an eyewitness, Leo Africanus, whose book Description of Africa was published in 1550. We test his account as a historical source, and we find out why Timbuktu's position made it one of the great trading cities of Africa, where salt and Mediterranean goods were exchanged for gold.
