### subtopic_1 — The World Map: Continents and Oceans
**Assistant:** Why does Greenland look so huge on most world maps?

**Tutor:** Because of the way a round surface is flattened. The most familiar world map, the Mercator projection, was designed in 1569 for sailors. Its great advantage is that a straight line drawn on it keeps the same compass direction all the way, which made it easy to plot a course. To achieve that, the map stretches the lines of latitude further and further apart towards the poles, and keeps the lines of longitude parallel instead of letting them meet. The result is that places near the poles are blown up enormously. Greenland, which is about two million square kilometres, appears about the size of Africa, which is about thirty million square kilometres. Other projections, such as equal-area maps, keep sizes correct but bend shapes instead. No flat map can get everything right. So when a news report compares countries, the safest source of true size is a globe or an equal-area map, and it is worth remembering that the map we choose can make some parts of the world seem more or less important than they are.

### subtopic_2 — Places in the News
**Assistant:** Why do so many earthquakes and volcanoes happen around the Pacific?

**Tutor:** Because the edge of the Pacific Ocean is where several of the great plates of the Earth's crust meet. The outer layer of the Earth is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates, which move a few centimetres a year. Around most of the Pacific, the ocean floor plate is sliding underneath the continents and island chains around it. That movement builds up stress that is released suddenly as earthquakes, and the sinking plate melts deep down, producing magma that rises to form volcanoes. The ring of volcanoes and earthquakes stretches from New Zealand through Indonesia, the Philippines and Japan, across to Alaska, and down the west coasts of North and South America to Chile, and is often called the Ring of Fire. This is exactly the kind of pattern a class news map reveals over a year, and it is the subject of the second term, when we study the structure of the Earth, volcanoes and earthquakes in detail.

### subtopic_3 — Locating News Places in Degrees
**Assistant:** How can latitude tell me what the weather might be like?

**Tutor:** Because latitude controls how much of the sun's energy a place receives. Near the equator, the midday sun is almost overhead all year, so its rays are concentrated on a small area and the ground heats strongly. Places at low latitudes, such as Nairobi or Kinshasa, are warm throughout the year, although Nairobi is cooled by its height above sea level. Further from the equator, the sun's rays strike the ground at a lower angle and are spread over a larger area, so they heat it less, and the difference between summer and winter grows. At sixty degrees north or south, winters are long, dark and cold. Latitude is not the only factor. Height above sea level, ocean currents, distance from the sea and mountains all change the climate too, which is why Johannesburg is cooler than Durban although they lie at a similar latitude. But latitude is the first clue, and a geographer reading a news story about a heat wave or snowfall checks it first.

### subtopic_4 — Compass Directions on a Local Sketch Map
**Assistant:** What if it's cloudy and I can't see the sun?

**Tutor:** Then use other clues, starting with the most reliable. A phone compass works anywhere, but needs to be held flat and away from metal objects, which can disturb it. Without any instrument, look at the local landscape and ask people. Satellite television dishes in South Africa all point towards the northern sky, because the satellites they receive hang above the equator, which lies to our north. So a row of dishes on a street is a very good north indicator. In many towns the main roads and streets are laid out roughly north-south and east-west, which a printed street map or a map app can confirm. Some churches are built facing east. If you know where the sun rose that morning, that memory helps too. Combine two or three clues rather than relying on one. Finally, once north has been found, write it on the sketch map at once with a north arrow, so that the rest of the map is drawn the right way round.
