In the markets of medieval West Africa, a slab of salt could be worth almost as much as gold. Today we look at what the trans-Saharan caravans carried and why. From the north came salt, cloth, copper, horses, glass beads and books. From West Africa went gold, ivory, ostrich feathers, kola nuts and enslaved people. We find out why salt was so precious, where Mali's gold came from, how the famous silent trade may have worked, and who gained and who suffered from this exchange.
