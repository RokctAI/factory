### subtopic_4 — Large-Scale and Small-Scale Maps
**Assistant:** Why is a map of a small area called large-scale? It seems backwards.

**Tutor:** It does seem backwards, and many adults mix it up too. The name comes from thinking about scale as a fraction, which you will meet in later grades as a ratio scale such as one to fifty thousand. A scale of one to five hundred means that one unit on the map stands for five hundred of the same unit on the ground; as a fraction, one five-hundredth. A scale of one to ten million is one ten-millionth. One five-hundredth is a much larger fraction than one ten-millionth, so the map of the small area has the larger scale. In practical terms, the larger the scale, the less the world has been shrunk, so things look larger, and the map can only fit a small area on the page. So the trick works because it follows the maths: on a large-scale map, the reduction is small and the features are large. On a small-scale map, the reduction is huge and features are tiny.

### subtopic_2 — Word Scales
**Assistant:** How do I convert between metres and kilometres without getting confused?

**Tutor:** Remember one fact and one check. The fact is that one kilometre is one thousand metres; the word kilo means a thousand, as in kilogram, a thousand grams. To change kilometres into metres, multiply by a thousand, so three point five kilometres is three thousand five hundred metres. To change metres into kilometres, divide by a thousand, so seven hundred metres is zero point seven kilometres. The check is common sense: after converting, ask whether the number should have become bigger or smaller. Metres are smaller units than kilometres, so the same distance needs more of them, and the number of metres must be larger. If you convert two kilometres and get two thousandths of a metre, you have divided when you should have multiplied. For centimetres, use a hundred centimetres to a metre, so a hundred thousand centimetres to a kilometre. In map questions, write the units at every step of the working, which makes mistakes much easier to spot.

### subtopic_3 — Line Scales
**Assistant:** Why does the scale bar on my phone keep changing?

**Tutor:** Because every time you zoom, you change the scale of the map. Zooming in makes features appear larger: the app is now showing a smaller area on the same screen, so it has switched to a larger scale. Zooming out shows a bigger area, at a smaller scale. A word scale could not keep up with this, but a line scale can, so map apps draw a small bar, usually in a corner, with a label such as two hundred metres or five kilometres, and redraw it at each zoom level. Often the bar changes length as well as label, because the app prefers round numbers. That means a phone map is a whole set of maps at different scales in one. Zoomed right in, it is a large-scale street plan showing individual buildings; zoomed right out, it is a small-scale map of the world. The geography is the same as in a printed atlas, but the app moves smoothly from one scale to the next.
