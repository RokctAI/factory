At the end of the eighteenth century, the Cape changed hands. The Dutch East India Company was bankrupt, Europe was at war, and in 1795 British warships sailed into False Bay. After a short return to Dutch rule, the British took the Cape again in 1806 and kept it. In this lesson we learn why the British came, how they took control, what changed under British rule, and how the frontiers of European settlement continued to expand east and north, setting the scene for co-operation and conflict on the frontiers.
