## Subtopic: The Landscape of South Africa

At Table 1, the relief map reminds the class that physical features are natural features of the land, such as mountains, rivers, plains and valleys, while human features are made by people, such as roads, towns and dams.

Height is measured above sea level. On a physical map, low land is shown in green and high land in yellow, orange and brown. Most of the interior of South Africa is a high plateau. Around the coast is a narrow, low coastal plain. Between them is the Great Escarpment, a steep edge, which is highest in the Drakensberg.

The plateau includes the Highveld, which is high and grassy, and covers much of the Free State and Gauteng. The Lowveld lies below the escarpment in Mpumalanga and Limpopo, and it is lower and hot. The Karoo is a dry, semi-desert area, and the Kalahari, in the north-west, is a dry, sandy area with red sand. Namaqualand, on the west coast, is famous for its spring flowers.

Other features include mountains, valleys, capes, which are points of land sticking out into the sea, and bays, which are curves where the sea goes into the land. Examples are the Cape of Good Hope, Cape Agulhas and False Bay.

The questions for this section are with you now: physical features, heights and regions of South Africa.

## Subtopic: Rivers, Dams and People

At Table 2, the drawing of a river shows its source, where it begins, often in mountains, and its mouth, where it ends, usually in the sea. A tributary is a smaller river that joins a bigger one. A river with all its tributaries is a river system, and the land that drains into it is its catchment area.

South Africa's main rivers include the Gariep, or Orange, the longest river in South Africa, which flows west into the Atlantic Ocean; the Vaal, its biggest tributary; the Limpopo, which forms part of the northern border and flows into the Indian Ocean; and the Thukela in KwaZulu-Natal.

Physical features affect where people live and what they do. People settle where there is water, flat land and good soil. Farms are found in fertile valleys and on plains, ports on bays and harbours, and few people live in dry areas or on steep mountains.

A dam is a wall built across a river to store water. Dams supply water to towns, farms and factories, can make electricity, and help to control floods. But they also flood land, homes and habitats, and change the river below them. The Gariep Dam, on the border of the Free State and the Eastern Cape, is the biggest dam in South Africa.

The questions for this section are with you now: rivers, river systems, dams and how people use the land.

## Subtopic: The First Farmers

At Table 3, the model homestead reminds the class about History. The first farmers were the ancestors of many South Africans today. Their ancestors spread slowly from West and Central Africa over many generations, and reached southern Africa about one thousand eight hundred years ago. They grew crops, kept cattle, made iron tools and lived in settled villages, so their time is called the Iron Age.

They settled in the summer-rainfall east, near rivers, good soil, grazing and wood. Land belonged to the community, and the chief gave families land to use. When they met the San and Khoikhoi, they often welcomed them, traded and married, and many Khoisan people became part of farming communities, which is why isiXhosa and isiZulu have clicks.

A homestead was a family's home, with round houses of wattle and daub around a cattle kraal. They grew sorghum and millet with iron hoes, and kept cattle, sheep and goats. Families with a shared ancestor formed clans. The chief governed with a council and headmen, and people met at the kgotla or imbizo. Men mostly cared for cattle, women mostly grew crops, and children herded and helped. Initiation marked the step into adulthood. Cattle were wealth, joined families through lobola, and helped the chief win support through mafisa.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the Gariep flows into the Indian Ocean; it flows west into the Atlantic. Two: thinking a dam is a natural feature; it is built by people. Three: thinking the first farmers grew maize; they grew sorghum and millet. Four: thinking lobola was buying a wife; it joined two families.

The questions for this section are with you now: the first farmers and their way of life.
