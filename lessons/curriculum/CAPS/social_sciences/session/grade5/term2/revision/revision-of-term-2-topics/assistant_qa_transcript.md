### subtopic_1 — The Landscape of South Africa
**Assistant:** How can I remember the plateau, escarpment and coastal plain?

**Tutor:** Think of an upside-down plate, or a table. The flat top of the table is the plateau: high, wide and fairly flat, covering most of the middle of South Africa, including the Highveld, where Bloemfontein and Johannesburg are. The edge of the table, where it drops down steeply, is the Great Escarpment. In some places it is a wall of mountains, like the Drakensberg, the highest part. The floor around the table is the coastal plain: low, narrow land along the coast, where cities like Durban and Gqeberha are. So if you drive from Johannesburg to Durban, you start on the high table top, drive down the steep edge, and arrive on the low floor near the sea. Draw a quick side view of a table on the back of your page in a test, and label the three parts. It helps a lot.

### subtopic_2 — Rivers, Dams and People
**Assistant:** How did the land shape where the first farmers lived?

**Tutor:** This is a great link between Geography and History. The first farmers needed summer rain, because their crops, sorghum and millet, grow in summer. Most of the east of South Africa gets summer rain, while the west is drier. So the farmers settled mostly in the east: in the valleys of Limpopo, Mpumalanga, KwaZulu-Natal and the Eastern Cape. They chose places near rivers for water, with fertile soil in river valleys for crops, grassland for their cattle, and wood for building and for iron furnaces. They avoided the dry Karoo and Kalahari, and the very cold, high mountains. This is the same thing we learnt in Geography this term: people settle where there is water, good soil and flat land. So the physical features of South Africa, its rivers, valleys and rainfall, shaped where the first farmers lived, and they still shape where many people live today.

### subtopic_3 — The First Farmers
**Assistant:** What are the most important history words for this term?

**Tutor:** Here is a short word bank to revise. Iron Age: the time of the first farmers, who made iron tools. Homestead: the home of one family, with houses around a cattle kraal. Wattle and daub: walls of woven sticks plastered with mud and dung. Kraal: an enclosure for cattle. Sorghum and millet: the main crops. Integration: when people from one group become part of another, like Khoisan people joining farming communities. Clan: families who share an ancestor. Chiefdom: a chief, his people and their land. Kgotla or imbizo: a public meeting. Tribute: gifts given to the chief. Barter: swapping goods without money. Initiation: ceremonies marking the step into adulthood. Lobola or bohadi: cattle given by the groom's family to the bride's family. Mafisa or ukusisa: lending cattle. Write each word on a card, with its meaning on the back, and test yourself with a friend.
