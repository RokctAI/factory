# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the location of selected famous physical features of South Africa: Table Mountain, the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg, the Waterberg, Lake St Lucia, Augrabies Falls, Cape Point and Algoa Bay, and how to find the main physical features in your own province. The anchor is a Grade 5 classroom in Gqeberha, in the Eastern Cape, on a hill with a view over the wide blue curve of Algoa Bay, where container ships wait to enter the harbour. On the floor, the class has laid out a giant map of South Africa, drawn on sheets of paper taped together, with the nine provinces outlined. Each group has a sheet of stickers, one for each famous feature, and a fact card. The challenge is to put each sticker in exactly the right place. The big idea is that South Africa has world-famous physical features spread across its provinces, and that every province, including your own, has physical features worth knowing. The locations of these features are examinable.

## Subtopic: Mountains and Ranges

Table Mountain is in the Western Cape, rising above the city of Cape Town. It is famous for its flat top, about three kilometres long, and its steep cliffs. Its highest point is about one thousand and eighty-five metres above sea level. When a south-easterly wind blows, a white cloud often pours over the top, which people call the tablecloth. Table Mountain is part of Table Mountain National Park, and in 2011 it was named one of the New Seven Wonders of Nature. People reach the top by cable car or by climbing one of many paths.

The uKhahlamba-Drakensberg is the highest mountain range in South Africa. It forms the border between KwaZulu-Natal and Lesotho. Its peaks rise to more than three thousand metres, and it has huge basalt cliffs, deep valleys, waterfalls and rivers. It is also home to thousands of San rock paintings. In 2000, the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park was named a World Heritage Site, both for its natural beauty and for its rock art.

The Waterberg is a mountain range in Limpopo, north-west of Mokopane. Its name means water mountain, because many rivers and streams begin there. It has red sandstone cliffs, bushveld and wildlife, and it is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, an area where people and nature are protected together.

On the floor map in Gqeberha, the groups place the Table Mountain sticker at the south-western tip, the Drakensberg sticker along the eastern border with Lesotho, and the Waterberg sticker in the far north.

The questions for this section are with you now: Table Mountain, the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg and the Waterberg.

## Subtopic: Water Features

Lake St Lucia is in KwaZulu-Natal, on the coastal plain in the north-east. It is a large estuary lake, a long, shallow lake connected to the sea, where fresh water from rivers mixes with salt water from the Indian Ocean. It is part of the iSimangaliso Wetland Park, which in 1999 became one of South Africa's first World Heritage Sites. Hippos, crocodiles, pelicans and flamingos live in and around the lake, and leatherback and loggerhead turtles nest on the nearby beaches.

Augrabies Falls is in the Northern Cape, on the Gariep River, west of Upington. Here, the river plunges into a deep, narrow gorge carved into granite. The name is believed to come from a Khoikhoi word meaning place of great noise, because of the roar of the water. The falls are in the Augrabies Falls National Park.

Cape Point is in the Western Cape, at the southern end of the Cape Peninsula. It is a high, rocky cape with steep cliffs and a lighthouse, inside Table Mountain National Park.

Algoa Bay is in the Eastern Cape. It is a large, wide bay of the Indian Ocean. The city of Gqeberha lies on its western shore, and the deep-water port of Ngqura is on its northern side. Islands in the bay are home to African penguins and gannets, and dolphins and whales are often seen in its waters.

The questions for this section are with you now: Lake St Lucia, Augrabies Falls, Cape Point and Algoa Bay.

## Subtopic: Features in Your Own Province

Every province has its own physical features. To find the main features of your province, follow these steps. First, find your province on a physical map of South Africa. Second, look at the colours to see whether your province is mostly high or low, and find the highest areas. Third, trace the blue lines to find the main rivers, and follow them to see which way they flow. Fourth, look for lakes, dams, waterfalls, and, if your province is on the coast, capes and bays. Finally, find where your own town is, and which features are close to it.

Here are some examples. Gauteng is on the Highveld, with the Witwatersrand ridge and the Magaliesberg, and the Vaal River on its southern edge. The Free State is on the plateau, with the Gariep River on its southern border and the Maloti and Drakensberg mountains along Lesotho. Mpumalanga has the escarpment, the Blyde River Canyon, and the Lowveld. Limpopo has the Waterberg, the Soutpansberg and the Limpopo River. North West has the Magaliesberg and flat plains. The Northern Cape has the Gariep River, Augrabies Falls, the Kalahari and Namaqualand. The Western Cape has Table Mountain, Cape Point, the Cederberg and the Little Karoo. The Eastern Cape has Algoa Bay, the Amathole mountains and a long coastline with many river mouths. KwaZulu-Natal has the Drakensberg, Lake St Lucia and the Thukela River.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: putting Table Mountain in the Eastern Cape; it is in the Western Cape, above Cape Town. Two: thinking Lake St Lucia is a freshwater lake far from the sea; it is an estuary lake on the coast of KwaZulu-Natal. Three: thinking Augrabies Falls is in the Drakensberg; it is on the Gariep River in the Northern Cape. Four: thinking only coastal provinces have interesting features; every province has its own physical features.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding the physical features of your own province.
