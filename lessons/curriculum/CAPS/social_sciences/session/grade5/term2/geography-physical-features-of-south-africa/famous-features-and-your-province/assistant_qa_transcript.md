### subtopic_1 — Mountains and Ranges
**Assistant:** What makes the tablecloth on Table Mountain?

**Tutor:** The tablecloth forms when a strong south-easterly wind blows in summer. The wind picks up a lot of moisture as it crosses the warm water of False Bay. When it reaches Table Mountain, it is forced to rise up the slopes. As air rises, it cools down, and cool air cannot hold as much water vapour as warm air. So the water vapour condenses into tiny droplets, forming a thick white cloud on top of the mountain. The wind then pushes the cloud over the edge, and it pours down the cliffs towards the city. But as it sinks, the air warms up again, and the droplets turn back into invisible vapour. That is why the cloud seems to vanish halfway down, like a cloth hanging over the edge of a table. A Cape Town legend says the cloud is smoke from a pipe-smoking contest between a pirate and the devil.

### subtopic_2 — Water Features
**Assistant:** Why is iSimangaliso a World Heritage Site?

**Tutor:** iSimangaliso Wetland Park, which includes Lake St Lucia, was one of the first three places in South Africa to be named World Heritage Sites, in 1999. It is special because so many different kinds of environment are found close together: coral reefs, sandy beaches, some of the highest forested dunes in the world, lakes, swamps, grasslands and wetlands. This variety means that a huge number of animals and plants live there, including hippos, crocodiles, leopards, rhinos, more than five hundred kinds of birds, and turtles that come ashore to lay their eggs. The word iSimangaliso is isiZulu and means miracle or something wondrous. The park also shows how a lake connected to the sea changes over time, as the mouth opens and closes and the water becomes saltier or fresher, which makes it very interesting for scientists.

### subtopic_3 — Features in Your Own Province
**Assistant:** Why is Gqeberha's bay called Algoa Bay?

**Tutor:** The name Algoa is believed to come from Portuguese. In 1488, Bartolomeu Dias sailed into the bay on his voyage around Africa and landed on a small island there. Portuguese sailors later called the bay Baía da Lagoa, which means bay of the lagoon, and over time this was shortened to Algoa. The city on its shore was called Port Elizabeth for a long time, after the wife of a British official. In 2021, its name was officially changed to Gqeberha, the isiXhosa name for the Baakens River, which flows into the bay in the city. The bay itself kept the name Algoa Bay. Today, the bay is busy with ships, and its islands, including St Croix Island and Bird Island, protect large colonies of seabirds, such as African penguins and Cape gannets.
