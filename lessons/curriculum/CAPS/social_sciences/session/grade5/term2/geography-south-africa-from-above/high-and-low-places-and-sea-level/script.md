# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers high and low places in South Africa, sea level, and how height above sea level is measured and shown on maps. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Underberg, in KwaZulu-Natal, on a trip up Sani Pass, a steep gravel road that zigzags up the Drakensberg to the border with Lesotho. They travel in 4x4 vehicles, because ordinary cars cannot manage the steep bends. One of the guides holds up a phone showing the height above sea level. At the bottom, in Underberg, it reads about one thousand five hundred and fifty metres. As the vehicles climb, the number rises, and the air gets colder. At the top, near the Lesotho border post, it reads about two thousand eight hundred and seventy metres, and there is snow beside the road. The big idea is that the height of every place is measured from the level of the sea, and that South Africa has places of many different heights, from the low coast to the high mountains. Sea level and height above sea level are examinable.

## Subtopic: Sea Level

To measure how high a place is, geographers need one starting point that everyone agrees on. They use the level of the sea, called sea level.

The surface of the sea moves up and down. Twice a day, the tide comes in and goes out. Waves rise and fall. So sea level is worked out as the average, or middle, height of the sea's surface, measured over a long time. Sea level is given the height of zero metres.

The height of any place on land is measured from sea level, straight up, in metres. This is called its height above sea level. A beach at the edge of the sea is at about zero metres. A town that is one thousand metres above sea level is one kilometre higher than the sea, even if it is hundreds of kilometres away from the coast.

Height above sea level is sometimes called altitude or elevation. It is not the same as how tall something is. A building in Durban that is one hundred metres tall is still standing on land near sea level. A small hill on the Highveld might be only fifty metres taller than the land around it, but its top could be one thousand eight hundred metres above sea level.

Some places in the world are even below sea level, and their height is given as a negative number. There are no places below sea level on land in South Africa.

The deepest parts of the oceans are far below sea level, but we usually measure those as depths, not heights. The deepest point in the oceans is more than ten thousand metres deep, deeper than the highest mountain on land is high.

The questions for this section are with you now: sea level and height above sea level.

## Subtopic: High and Low in South Africa

South Africa has places of very different heights.

The lowest places are along the coast, at or near sea level. Cape Town, Durban, Gqeberha, East London and Richards Bay are all coastal cities near sea level.

The highest places are in the mountains along the edge of the plateau. The highest point in South Africa is Mafadi, a peak in the Drakensberg on the border with Lesotho, about three thousand four hundred and fifty metres above sea level. The highest point in all of southern Africa, Thabana Ntlenyana, is just across the border, in Lesotho.

In between, the high plateau covers most of the country. Johannesburg is about one thousand seven hundred and fifty metres above sea level, and Bloemfontein about one thousand four hundred. Kimberley is about one thousand two hundred metres high. Polokwane is about one thousand three hundred metres. Even towns in the dry Karoo are often more than one thousand metres high.

Height affects temperature. In general, the higher a place is, the colder it is. The temperature drops by roughly six degrees Celsius for every one thousand metres you climb. That is why there is snow at the top of Sani Pass in winter, while it is mild in Durban on the same day. It is also why Johannesburg is cooler than Durban, even though Johannesburg is further north, and why frost is common on the Highveld in winter.

The questions for this section are with you now: the heights of places in South Africa and how height affects temperature.

## Subtopic: Showing Height on a Map

Maps show height in several ways.

A spot height is a dot or small triangle with a number next to it, showing the exact height of that place in metres. For example, a map of the Drakensberg might show a small triangle with 3450 next to it, for the peak of Mafadi.

Colour layering, also called layer colouring, uses a different colour for each band of height. The key tells you which colour stands for which heights. Usually green shows low land near sea level, yellow and light brown show higher land, and dark brown, red or purple show the highest land. On a physical map of South Africa, the coast is mostly green, the plateau is yellow and brown, and the Drakensberg and Lesotho are dark brown.

A side view, also called a cross-section, shows the shape of the land as if you had cut through it and looked at it from the side. A side view of the road from Durban to Sani Pass would show a low start at the sea, a gentle climb inland, then a very steep rise up the escarpment to the top of the pass.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking height above sea level is the same as how tall a building is; it is measured from the sea, not from the ground. Two: thinking places far from the sea must be low; most of the interior is high. Three: thinking it gets warmer as you climb towards the sun; it gets colder. Four: thinking green on a physical map means grassland; it means low land.

The questions for this section are with you now: spot heights, colour layering and side views.
