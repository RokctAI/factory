# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the coastal plain, the escarpment and the plateau: what each one is, and where each is found in South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Durban on a bus trip to Johannesburg along the N3 highway. They leave Durban early in the morning, with the warm, damp air of the coast and the blue Indian Ocean behind them. The road climbs through green, rolling hills past Pietermaritzburg. After a few hours, a great wall of mountains appears on the horizon, and the bus slowly grinds up a long, winding road called Van Reenen's Pass. At the top, the land suddenly flattens out into wide, open grassland stretching as far as the eye can see, and the air feels colder and drier. The big idea is that South Africa has three main parts: a low coastal plain around the edge, a steep escarpment, and a high plateau in the middle. Knowing what these are and where they are is examinable.

## Subtopic: The Coastal Plain

A plain is a large area of flat or gently rolling land. A coastal plain is a plain next to the sea.

South Africa's coastal plain is a strip of low land that runs along most of the coast. It is low, mostly less than about five hundred metres above sea level, and much of it is close to sea level.

The coastal plain is not the same width everywhere. In the north-east of KwaZulu-Natal, in the area called Maputaland, near Lake St Lucia and Kosi Bay, the coastal plain is wide and very flat, with sandy soil, swamps, lakes and forests. Further south, along most of the coast of KwaZulu-Natal and the Eastern Cape, the coastal belt is narrower and hillier, because rivers have cut deep valleys into it. Along the west coast, the coastal plain is dry and sandy.

The coastal plain has a mild climate, because it is low and close to the sea. On the east coast, it is warm and humid, and sugar cane, bananas and other crops that like warmth are grown there. Many of South Africa's big cities, ports and holiday towns are on the coastal plain, such as Durban, Richards Bay, East London, Gqeberha and Cape Town.

On the bus trip, the first part of the journey, from the beach in Durban into the hills, is across the coastal belt.

The coastal plain is also where the land meets the sea, so it has beaches, sand dunes, lagoons, estuaries where rivers meet the sea, and in places mangrove swamps. Along the coast of KwaZulu-Natal, there are tall, forested sand dunes, some of the highest vegetated dunes in the world. These coastal environments are home to many plants, birds and fish, and some are protected in nature reserves.

The questions for this section are with you now: the coastal plain.

## Subtopic: The Great Escarpment

An escarpment is a long, steep slope or cliff that separates high land from lower land.

The Great Escarpment is the long line of steep mountains and cliffs that separates South Africa's coastal plain and lower areas from the high plateau. It runs in a long curve, almost like a horseshoe, around the country, roughly parallel to the coast. It begins in Limpopo, runs south through Mpumalanga, along the border between KwaZulu-Natal and Lesotho, through the Eastern Cape, and then turns north-west through the Western and Northern Cape, and on into Namibia.

The escarpment has different names in different places. The highest and most famous part is the Drakensberg, in KwaZulu-Natal, Mpumalanga and the Eastern Cape. In the Eastern Cape, there are the Stormberg and the Sneeuberg. In the Western and Northern Cape, there are the Nuweveld, Roggeveld and Hantam mountains.

Because it is so steep, the escarpment is hard to cross. Roads and railways climb it using passes, such as Van Reenen's Pass on the N3, which the bus climbs between KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State. Rivers tumble down the escarpment in waterfalls and rapids. The Drakensberg escarpment also catches a lot of rain and snow, which feeds many rivers.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Great Escarpment, where it runs and its names.

## Subtopic: The Plateau

A plateau is a large area of high, fairly flat land. It is like a table: high up, but flat on top.

The South African plateau covers most of the interior of the country, inside the curve of the Great Escarpment. Most of it is more than one thousand metres above sea level, and some parts are much higher. The plateau is highest in the east, near the escarpment, and slopes gently down towards the west.

The plateau has several regions. The Highveld, in Gauteng, the Free State and parts of Mpumalanga and North West, is high grassland. The Great Karoo, in the south of the plateau, is dry. The Kalahari, in the north-west, has red sand. Lesotho sits on the highest part of the plateau.

Between the escarpment and the plateau, there is no sharp line in every place. In some areas, the land climbs up the escarpment in one great step. In others, it rises in several smaller steps, with hills and valleys in between.

The plateau has hot summers with thunderstorms on the Highveld, and cold winters with frost. Much of it is used for farming maize, wheat, cattle and sheep, and it is rich in minerals such as gold, coal and diamonds. Johannesburg, Pretoria, Bloemfontein and Kimberley are all on the plateau.

When the bus reaches the top of Van Reenen's Pass, it is on the plateau. The rest of the trip to Johannesburg crosses the flat, open grassland of the Highveld.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the coastal plain is the same width everywhere; it is wide in Maputaland and narrow in other places. Two: thinking the escarpment is a single mountain; it is a long line of mountains with many names. Three: thinking a plateau is a mountain peak; it is high land that is fairly flat on top. Four: thinking Johannesburg is low because it is a big city; it is on the plateau, high above sea level.

The questions for this section are with you now: the plateau and its regions.
