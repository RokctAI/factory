# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the meanings of these physical features: mountains, mountain ranges, valleys, hills, rivers, waterfalls, capes and bays, with South African examples of each. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Simon's Town, on the Cape Peninsula, at the old lighthouse at Cape Point, in Table Mountain National Park. They have climbed up the steep path to the lookout, high on the cliffs. Below them, waves crash against the rocks. To the east, a huge curve of calm blue water stretches away towards distant mountains. To the west is the open Atlantic Ocean. Behind them, a line of rocky mountains runs north along the peninsula towards Table Mountain. The teacher hands out a worksheet with eight words on it, and asks the class to find an example of each one from where they are standing. The big idea is that each kind of physical feature has its own shape and meaning, and that South Africa has famous examples of all of them. The meanings of these features and examples of each are examinable.

## Subtopic: Mountains, Ranges and Hills

A mountain is a large, high landform that rises steeply above the land around it, usually to a peak or a ridge at the top. There is no exact rule for how high a mountain must be, but mountains are much higher and steeper than hills. The top of a mountain is called its peak or summit, and the sides are its slopes.

A mountain range is a line or group of mountains that are joined together. South Africa has many ranges. The Drakensberg is the highest and longest. The Cederberg, in the Western Cape, is famous for its orange rocks. The Swartberg separates the Great Karoo from the Little Karoo. The Magaliesberg runs between Pretoria and Rustenburg. The mountains of the Cape Peninsula, which run from Table Mountain to Cape Point, form a smaller range.

A hill is a raised area of land, smaller and lower than a mountain, with gentler slopes. Hills often have rounded tops. In South Africa, small, rocky hills that rise out of flat land are often called koppies, from the Afrikaans word for little heads. The Valley of a Thousand Hills, between Pietermaritzburg and Durban, is named for its many rolling hills.

At Cape Point, the learners stand on the last mountain at the southern end of the peninsula range. Looking north, they can see the line of peaks of the range stretching towards Table Mountain.

The questions for this section are with you now: mountains, ranges and hills.

## Subtopic: Valleys, Rivers and Waterfalls

A valley is a long, low area of land between hills or mountains. Many valleys have a river flowing along the bottom. Rivers often carve their own valleys, slowly wearing away the rock over a very long time. Valleys with steep sides are often V-shaped. A very deep, narrow valley with steep rock walls is called a gorge or a canyon.

South Africa has many valleys. The Hex River Valley, in the Western Cape, is famous for grapes. The Blyde River Canyon, in Mpumalanga, is one of the largest canyons in the world, with red sandstone cliffs and a river far below.

A river is a large stream of fresh water that flows across the land, from high ground to lower ground, usually ending in the sea, a lake or another river. The Gariep, or Orange, is South Africa's longest river.

A waterfall is a place where a river flows over a steep drop, such as a cliff, and falls down. Waterfalls often form where a river flows from hard rock onto softer rock, or over the edge of the escarpment. The Tugela Falls, in the Drakensberg, is one of the highest waterfalls in the world, dropping more than nine hundred metres in a series of falls. The Howick Falls, near Pietermaritzburg, drops about ninety-five metres. The Augrabies Falls, on the Gariep River in the Northern Cape, roars into a deep granite gorge.

The questions for this section are with you now: valleys, rivers and waterfalls.

## Subtopic: Capes and Bays

A cape is a piece of land that sticks out into the sea, usually with high ground or cliffs. Ships must sail around a cape. Cape Point, where the learners are standing, is a cape. So is the Cape of Good Hope, just next to it. Cape Agulhas, further east, is the southernmost tip of Africa. Capes are often dangerous for ships, because of strong winds, big waves and rocks, which is why lighthouses are built on them.

A bay is an area of sea that curves into the land, with land on three sides. A bay is the opposite of a cape. Bays often have calmer water than the open sea, so they are good places for harbours, fishing and beaches.

From Cape Point, the learners look east across False Bay, the large bay between the Cape Peninsula and the mountains near Gordon's Bay. Sailors long ago gave it its name, because they sometimes mistook it for Table Bay, which is the bay at Cape Town, around the other side of the peninsula. Other famous bays in South Africa are Algoa Bay at Gqeberha, Mossel Bay, Walker Bay at Hermanus, famous for whale watching, and St Helena Bay on the west coast.

Capes and bays often come together. A coastline that has hard rock and soft rock wears away unevenly: the soft rock is worn away to form bays, and the hard rock is left sticking out as capes.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a hill and a mountain are the same; a mountain is much higher and steeper. Two: thinking a range is one mountain; it is a line or group of mountains joined together. Three: mixing up capes and bays; a cape sticks out into the sea, and a bay curves into the land. Four: thinking Cape Point is the southernmost tip of Africa; it is Cape Agulhas.

The questions for this section are with you now: capes and bays.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the lookout at Cape Point — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Up High

Mountains are high and steep. Hills are lower. A range is a line of mountains.

Keep the line of peaks running north in mind and try the questions for this part: mountains and hills.

## Subtopic: Down Low

A valley is low land between hills. A waterfall is where a river drops.

Hold the river far below the canyon cliffs in mind and try this part's questions: valleys and waterfalls.

## Subtopic: Land and Sea

A cape sticks out. A bay curves in.

Pocket summary of the lesson. A mountain is a high, steep landform, and a mountain range is a line of mountains, like the Drakensberg. A hill is lower, with gentle slopes. A valley is low land between hills or mountains, often with a river. A river flows from high to low ground, and a waterfall forms where it drops over a cliff, like the Tugela Falls. A cape, like Cape Point, sticks out into the sea. A bay, like False Bay, curves into the land.

Hold the view of False Bay from the lighthouse in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: capes and bays.
