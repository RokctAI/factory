### subtopic_1 — The Coastal Plain
**Assistant:** Why is it called the Drakensberg?

**Tutor:** Drakensberg is an Afrikaans name that means mountains of the dragon. It was given by Dutch-speaking settlers, and there are different stories about why. Some say that the jagged peaks looked like the spikes on a dragon's back. Others say that settlers told stories of dragons living in the mountains. The isiZulu name is uKhahlamba, which means barrier of spears, describing the long wall of sharp peaks that rise up like a line of spears. In Sesotho, the mountains are called Maloti, especially on the Lesotho side. The World Heritage Site in KwaZulu-Natal is called the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park, which uses both names. This is a good example of how the same physical feature can have several names, each telling us something about the people who named it and how they saw it.

### subtopic_2 — The Great Escarpment
**Assistant:** How do trains get up the escarpment?

**Tutor:** Trains find it much harder than cars to climb steep slopes, because their steel wheels can slip on steel rails. So railway engineers have to plan very carefully. They build the line so that it climbs slowly, with gentle slopes, curving backwards and forwards across the mountain, much longer than a road would be. In some places, they cut tunnels through the mountains, or build bridges across deep valleys. In the Hex River Mountains in the Western Cape, for example, a long railway tunnel was opened in 1989 to replace a steep, winding route. Long ago, some lines used special engines that pushed from behind. Today, powerful electric and diesel locomotives pull long goods trains up the escarpment, carrying coal, maize, cars and containers between the ports on the coast and the cities and mines on the plateau.

### subtopic_3 — The Plateau
**Assistant:** Is the plateau completely flat?

**Tutor:** No, not completely. A plateau is high land that is fairly flat compared with the steep escarpment around it, but it still has hills, ridges, valleys and rivers. On the Highveld, there are long, low ridges, such as the Witwatersrand ridge in Johannesburg, where gold was found, and the Magaliesberg, a ridge of mountains near Pretoria and Rustenburg. In the Karoo, there are flat-topped hills, which are called mesas when they are wide and buttes when they are narrow, and small hills called koppies. Rivers such as the Vaal and the Gariep have cut valleys into the plateau. But if you drive across the Free State, you can travel for hours across land that looks almost perfectly flat, with big skies and a horizon far away in every direction. So the plateau is flat on the whole, with interesting bumps.
