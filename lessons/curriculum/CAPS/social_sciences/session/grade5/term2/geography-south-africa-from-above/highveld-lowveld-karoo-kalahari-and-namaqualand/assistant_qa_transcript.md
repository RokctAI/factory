### subtopic_1 — Highveld and Lowveld
**Assistant:** Why are there so many flowers in Namaqualand if it is so dry?

**Tutor:** It seems surprising, but the dryness is part of the reason. Many Namaqualand plants are annuals, which means they live for only a few weeks or months. Their seeds lie in the dry soil, sometimes for years, waiting. When enough winter rain falls, the seeds sprout all at once, grow quickly, flower, and make new seeds before the hot, dry summer arrives. Because so many plants flower at the same time, the land turns into a carpet of colour. Bright colours attract bees, beetles and other insects, which carry pollen from flower to flower. Namaqualand is part of the Succulent Karoo, one of the richest dry areas for plants in the world, with thousands of kinds of plants, many found nowhere else. In years with little rain, there are fewer flowers, so each season is different.

### subtopic_2 — The Great and Little Karoo
**Assistant:** Why are ostriches farmed in the Little Karoo?

**Tutor:** Ostriches are wild birds of dry, open country, so the hot, dry climate of the Little Karoo suits them well. Farmers began to farm ostriches there in the late eighteen hundreds, because fashionable people in Europe and America wanted ostrich feathers for hats and clothes. Ostrich feathers became so valuable that some farmers in Oudtshoorn became very rich and built grand houses, which people called feather palaces. Then, around the time of the First World War, fashions changed and cars became popular, and nobody wanted big feathered hats any more, so the price of feathers crashed. Today, ostriches are farmed for their meat, which is low in fat, their leather, which is used for shoes and bags, and their feathers, which are used for dusters and decorations. Tourists also visit ostrich farms to see the birds.

### subtopic_3 — Kalahari and Namaqualand
**Assistant:** Do lions really live in the Kalahari?

**Tutor:** Yes. The Kalahari lions are famous for their size and the dark manes of the males. They live in the Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park and other parts of the Kalahari in Botswana and Namibia. Life is hard for them, because there is very little water. They get much of the moisture they need from the animals they eat, such as gemsbok, springbok and wildebeest, and they drink at waterholes when they can. Their territories are very big, because prey is spread out over a large area, so they walk long distances. They often rest in the shade of camel thorn trees during the heat of the day, and hunt at night when it is cooler. Visitors to the park drive along dry riverbeds, where the animals gather, and may see lions lying on the red sand or drinking at a waterhole.
