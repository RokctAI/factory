### subtopic_1 — From Hunting to Farming
**Assistant:** What were the Lydenburg Heads used for?

**Tutor:** Nobody knows for certain, because the people who made them did not leave any writing, but archaeologists have some ideas. The heads are carefully made and decorated, and they were found buried together in a pit, which suggests they were special objects, not everyday things. The two largest heads are big enough to fit over a person's head, like a helmet or a mask, so they may have been worn in important ceremonies. Many researchers think they were used in initiation ceremonies, when young people were taught the knowledge they needed as adults, or in other religious rituals. Afterwards, they may have been carefully buried to keep their power safe. The heads are some of the oldest known sculptures from southern Africa, and they show that the first farmers were skilled artists as well as farmers and metalworkers.

### subtopic_2 — When and How They Came
**Assistant:** Did the first farmers grow maize?

**Tutor:** No. This surprises many people, because maize is so important in South Africa today, as mealie meal for pap. But maize did not grow anywhere in Africa when the first farmers arrived. Maize was first grown thousands of years ago by farmers in the Americas, in what is now Mexico. It was brought to Africa by Portuguese traders only about five hundred years ago, after European ships began sailing to the Americas, and it spread slowly inland. Before that, the first farmers grew African crops, especially sorghum and millet, which they ground into flour for porridge and used to make beer. They also grew beans, cowpeas, pumpkins and gourds. Sorghum is still grown in South Africa, and is used to make traditional beer and the porridge called mabele in Setswana and Sesotho.

### subtopic_3 — Iron and the Iron Age
**Assistant:** Why is it called the Iron Age if they also used stone and clay?

**Tutor:** Historians and archaeologists often name long periods of time after the most important new material people used for tools. The Stone Age is named after stone tools, which people used for hundreds of thousands of years. When people learnt to make iron, it was such a big change that the time after that is called the Iron Age. People did not stop using stone, wood, bone and clay. They still used grinding stones for flour, wooden bowls and clay pots. But iron tools made farming, building and hunting much easier, and changed how people lived. In southern Africa, the Iron Age is often divided into the Early Iron Age, when the first farmers arrived, and the Later Iron Age, from about a thousand years ago, when bigger chiefdoms and kingdoms, such as Mapungubwe, grew up.
