# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the main rivers of South Africa: their sources, major tributaries and directions of flow, shown on a map. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Pietermaritzburg, in KwaZulu-Natal, watching the start of the Dusi Canoe Marathon, a famous race that has been held for more than seventy years. Hundreds of paddlers in long, narrow canoes set off down the uMsunduzi River. Over three days, they paddle and sometimes run, carrying their canoes, until the uMsunduzi joins the uMngeni River, and then they follow the uMngeni all the way down to the sea at Durban. The teacher shows the class a map of all of South Africa's main rivers, and says the race is like a three-day lesson in how rivers flow. The big idea is that South Africa's main rivers begin on high ground and flow in different directions, shaped by the escarpment, to the Indian Ocean or the Atlantic Ocean. The main rivers, their sources, tributaries and directions of flow are examinable.

## Subtopic: Rivers Flowing West

The longest river in South Africa is the Gariep, or Orange. It is about two thousand two hundred kilometres long. Its source is high in the mountains of Lesotho, where it is called the Senqu. It flows west across the plateau, through the Gariep Dam and the Vanderkloof Dam, past Upington and over the Augrabies Falls, and then forms part of the border with Namibia. Its mouth is on the Atlantic Ocean at Alexander Bay. Its major tributaries are the Caledon and the Vaal.

The Vaal is the Gariep's biggest tributary. Its source is on the Highveld in Mpumalanga, near the town of Breyten. It flows west and south-west through the Vaal Dam, past the south of Gauteng, along the border between the Free State and North West, and joins the Gariep near Douglas in the Northern Cape. Its tributaries include the Wilge, the Klip and the Harts. The Vaal supplies water to millions of people and many factories and mines in Gauteng.

In the Western Cape, the Berg River begins in the mountains near Franschhoek and flows north and then north-west, past Paarl, to the Atlantic Ocean at Velddrif, on St Helena Bay.

The west-flowing rivers cross the dry interior and the west, so many of their smaller tributaries flow only after rain.

Because the plateau slopes gently, these rivers flow slowly for most of their journey, and they wind across wide, flat land. Their water is very precious in the dry west. Towns such as Upington, Kakamas and Augrabies grew up along the Gariep, and farmers there use its water to grow grapes, raisins, dates, cotton and lucerne, in a long green ribbon across the dry land. Without the river, there would be almost no farming in this part of the Northern Cape.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Gariep, the Vaal and the Berg.

## Subtopic: Rivers Flowing East and South

Many rivers begin on the escarpment and flow east or south-east, on a short, steep journey to the Indian Ocean.

The Thukela is the largest river in KwaZulu-Natal. Its source is at Mont-aux-Sources in the Drakensberg, where it plunges over the Tugela Falls. It flows east across KwaZulu-Natal, joined by tributaries such as the Buffalo, and reaches the Indian Ocean north of Durban.

The uMngeni is a smaller river that begins in the Midlands of KwaZulu-Natal and flows east, through several dams, to Durban. Its tributary, the uMsunduzi, flows through Pietermaritzburg. These are the rivers of the canoe race.

In the Eastern Cape, the Mzimvubu begins in the Drakensberg near Lesotho and flows south-east to the Indian Ocean at Port St Johns. It is one of the biggest rivers in South Africa without a large dam. The Great Fish River and the Sundays River also flow south-east to the Indian Ocean.

In the Western Cape, the Breede River begins in the mountains near Ceres and flows south-east through a wide, fertile valley, past Worcester and Swellendam, to the Indian Ocean at Witsand.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Thukela, the uMngeni, the Mzimvubu, the Fish and the Breede.

## Subtopic: The Limpopo and Reading the Map

The Limpopo forms South Africa's northern border. It begins where the Crocodile and the Marico rivers meet, in North West, near the border with Botswana. It flows north-east and then east, along the borders with Botswana and Zimbabwe, into Mozambique, and reaches the Indian Ocean. Its tributaries include the Mogalakwena and, in Mozambique, the Olifants, which flows from the Highveld of Mpumalanga through the Kruger National Park.

On a map of South Africa's rivers, a pattern stands out. The escarpment and the Drakensberg form the main watershed. On the eastern and southern side, many rivers are short and steep, and flow quickly to the Indian Ocean. They get more rain, so most of them flow all year round. Their fast-flowing water carves deep valleys into the land between the escarpment and the coast, which is why the coast of KwaZulu-Natal and the Eastern Cape is so hilly. On the western side, the land slopes gently down to the west, and the rivers are long and slow. The Gariep collects water from most of the plateau and carries it all the way to the Atlantic.

To read a river map, find the river's source, usually in high land, then follow the blue line downhill to its mouth. Look at where tributaries join, and remember that the water always flows from the source to the mouth.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the Vaal flows into the sea on its own; it is a tributary of the Gariep. Two: thinking the Gariep begins in South Africa; its source is in Lesotho. Three: thinking the Limpopo flows into the Atlantic; it flows east into the Indian Ocean through Mozambique. Four: thinking the east-flowing rivers are long; most are short and steep, because the escarpment is close to the coast.
