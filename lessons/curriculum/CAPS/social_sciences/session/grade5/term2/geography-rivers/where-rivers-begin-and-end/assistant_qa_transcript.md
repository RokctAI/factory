### subtopic_1 — The Source
**Assistant:** Is the Tugela Falls the highest waterfall in the world?

**Tutor:** It is one of the highest, and some measurements say it is the highest of all, but it depends on how you measure. The Tugela Falls drops a total of about nine hundred and fifty metres in five separate falls, one after the other, with the highest single drop being about four hundred metres. Angel Falls in Venezuela, in South America, has the highest single, unbroken drop in the world, of about eight hundred metres, and a total height of about nine hundred and eighty metres. Different measurements give slightly different answers, so it is safest to say that the Tugela Falls is one of the two highest waterfalls in the world. In the dry winter, the flow can become very thin, and in very cold weather the top can even freeze.

### subtopic_2 — Flowing Downhill
**Assistant:** Why are wetlands so important for rivers?

**Tutor:** Wetlands act like giant sponges. When it rains, wetlands soak up the water and hold it, instead of letting it rush straight down the slopes. Then, over the following weeks and months, they slowly let the water seep out into streams. This means rivers keep flowing during the dry season, and floods are smaller during the wet season. Wetlands also clean the water, because plants and soil trap mud and some pollution, so the water that leaves them is clearer. They are home to many birds, frogs, insects and plants. In South Africa, many wetlands have been drained for farming or building, or damaged by mining and overgrazing. When that happens, rivers can dry up in winter and flood more in summer. That is why the high wetlands of the Drakensberg and Lesotho, where so many of our rivers begin, are protected.

### subtopic_3 — The Mouth
**Assistant:** Why do some rivers dry up before they reach the sea?

**Tutor:** In very dry places, a river may lose more water than it gets. The hot sun makes water evaporate, which means it turns into invisible water vapour and rises into the air. Water also soaks down into dry, sandy ground. Plants along the banks drink water too, and so do people and animals. If not much rain falls, the river can shrink and disappear before it reaches the sea. Many rivers in the dry west of South Africa, in the Karoo, the Kalahari and Namaqualand, flow only after rain, sometimes only for a few days a year. The rest of the time, they are dry, sandy riverbeds. These are called seasonal or non-perennial rivers. Even so, there may still be water under the sand, which plants with long roots, animals and people digging wells can reach. A river that flows all year round is called perennial.
