# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the topic an ancient African society: Egypt. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Polokwane, in Limpopo, on the first day of the new History topic. The teacher has pinned a large map of Africa on the classroom wall. One learner holds the end of a red ribbon on Polokwane, near the top of South Africa, and another stretches it north, across Zimbabwe, Zambia, Tanzania, Uganda, South Sudan and Sudan, all the way to Cairo, the capital city of Egypt, in the north-east corner of the continent. The ribbon is more than a metre long on the map, and in real life, Cairo is more than five thousand kilometres away. Around the map, the teacher has put up pictures: the pyramids of Giza standing in the desert, a stone wall carved with rows of little pictures, a golden mask, and a long, blue river winding through yellow sand. The big idea is that ancient Egypt was one of the greatest early societies in the world, that it was an African society, built along the Nile River in north-eastern Africa, and that we can learn about it from the many things its people left behind. The introduction to ancient Egypt is examinable.

## Subtopic: Where and When

Egypt is a country in the north-east of Africa. To the north is the Mediterranean Sea, and to the east is the Red Sea. Most of the country is desert, part of the great Sahara. Through the desert flows the Nile River, from the south to the north, into the Mediterranean.

Ancient means very old, from long, long ago. Ancient Egypt was the society that grew up along the Nile thousands of years ago.

About five thousand one hundred years ago, the land along the Nile was united into one kingdom, ruled by one king. Before that, there were two kingdoms: Upper Egypt in the south, upstream, and Lower Egypt in the north, around the Nile Delta, where the river splits into many branches before reaching the sea. The kings of ancient Egypt came to be called pharaohs.

Ancient Egyptian society lasted for about three thousand years. That is a very long time. The famous pyramids were already ancient when Tutankhamun, a later pharaoh, was born, and Tutankhamun had already been dead for more than a thousand years when Cleopatra, one of the last rulers of ancient Egypt, was born.

The questions for this section are with you now: where ancient Egypt was and when it existed.

## Subtopic: An African Society

Ancient Egypt was an African society. It grew up in Africa, along an African river, and its people were Africans. It was linked by trade, travel and sometimes war with other African peoples, especially the kingdom of Kush, to the south, in what is now Sudan, which at one time even ruled Egypt.

Ancient Egypt was one of the world's earliest great civilisations. A civilisation is a large, organised society with cities, rulers, laws, writing, religion, art and skilled workers.

The ancient Egyptians are famous for many achievements. They built huge pyramids and temples, which still stand today. They developed one of the world's earliest systems of writing, called hieroglyphics. They used mathematics to measure land and plan buildings, studied the stars to make a calendar, and had skilled doctors. They made paper from papyrus reeds, and created beautiful art, jewellery and furniture.

Ancient Egypt was also a society of farmers, builders and craftspeople. Most people grew crops along the river, while others worked as scribes, priests, soldiers, potters, weavers and builders. In this topic, we will study how they lived, what they built, how they wrote and counted, how they cared for the sick, and what one famous tomb has taught us.

Many of their ideas spread to other peoples around the Mediterranean and beyond. Studying ancient Egypt shows us that Africa was home to one of the greatest early societies in the world.

The questions for this section are with you now: why ancient Egypt is called an African society, and its achievements.

## Subtopic: How We Know

We know a great deal about ancient Egypt, because its people left so much behind, and the dry desert climate helped to preserve it.

Buildings and monuments, such as pyramids, temples and tombs, still stand, many with paintings and carvings on their walls showing gods, pharaohs, farming, feasts and daily life.

Writing is a very important source. The Egyptians wrote on stone walls, on papyrus scrolls and on pieces of pottery. After the knowledge of reading hieroglyphics was lost for many centuries, scholars learnt to read them again about two hundred years ago, so we can now read their own words: stories, prayers, letters, lists, medical books and records of the pharaohs.

Objects found by archaeologists, such as tools, pots, jewellery, clothes, furniture and toys, tell us how people lived. Mummies, which are bodies that were carefully preserved, tell scientists about people's health, food and age.

Writers from other lands, such as the Greek traveller Herodotus, who visited Egypt about two and a half thousand years ago, also described Egypt.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking ancient Egypt was not in Africa; it was in north-eastern Africa. Two: thinking ancient Egypt lasted a few hundred years; it lasted about three thousand years. Three: thinking the Nile flows south; it flows north into the Mediterranean. Four: thinking we only know about Egypt from stories; we have buildings, writing, objects and mummies.

The questions for this section are with you now: the sources that tell us about ancient Egypt.

Ancient Egypt was an African society, built by Africans along an African river.

It was one of the world's earliest great civilisations, with pyramids, temples, writing, mathematics, a calendar and doctors.

It traded with Kush, its neighbour to the south.

Hold the picture of the pyramids in the desert in mind and try this part's questions: an African society.

## Subtopic: How We Know

We learn from buildings, wall paintings, writing on stone and papyrus, objects, mummies, and visitors' writings. The dry desert preserved them for thousands of years. Today we can read their own words again.
