### subtopic_1 — Beliefs About the Afterlife
**Assistant:** How was a mummy made?

**Tutor:** Making a mummy took about seventy days and was done by specialists called embalmers, who were also priests. First, the body was washed. Then the embalmers removed the brain, usually through the nose with a hook, and made a cut in the left side to remove the lungs, liver, stomach and intestines. These organs were dried and kept in special containers. The heart was usually left inside, because the Egyptians believed it would be weighed in the afterlife. Next, the body was covered and packed with natron, a natural salt that dries things out, for about forty days, until it was completely dry. Then it was rubbed with oils and resin, and wrapped in many layers of linen bandages, with amulets placed between the layers and prayers said. Finally, a mask was placed over the head, and the mummy was put in its coffin. Mummification was expensive, so the best methods were for the rich.

### subtopic_2 — Wealth, Power and Skill
**Assistant:** Where are Tutankhamun's treasures today?

**Tutor:** The treasures have stayed in Egypt. For about a hundred years, most of them were displayed at the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, where millions of visitors saw them. In recent years, Egypt built a huge new museum near the pyramids of Giza, called the Grand Egyptian Museum, and the Tutankhamun collection has been moved there, so that, for the first time, almost all the objects from the tomb can be seen together. Tutankhamun's mummy is still in his tomb in the Valley of the Kings, in a glass case, where visitors can see him. Over the years, some objects have travelled to other countries in special exhibitions, which attracted huge crowds, but they always returned to Egypt. Egypt is proud of this heritage, and believes, like many countries, that important objects from its history should stay in the country they come from.

### subtopic_3 — Daily Life and Family
**Assistant:** Why is a meteorite dagger so special?

**Tutor:** In Tutankhamun's time, about three thousand three hundred years ago, Egyptians used mostly copper, bronze and gold, and iron-making was not yet common in Egypt. Iron was rare and very valuable, more valuable than gold. In 2016, scientists studied the iron blade of a dagger found wrapped with the king's mummy, using a special machine that can tell what metals are in an object without damaging it. They found that the iron contained a lot of nickel and other elements in amounts typical of iron meteorites, which are rocks from space that fall to Earth. So the blade was most likely made from a meteorite. The Egyptians may have seen iron from the sky as a gift from the gods. This shows how modern science keeps revealing new facts about objects found a hundred years ago.
