### subtopic_1 — Weather and Measuring
**Assistant:** What are the most important Geography words for this term?

**Tutor:** Here is a short word bank to revise. Weather: the state of the air at a particular time and place. Climate: the usual weather of a place over about thirty years. Temperature: how hot or cold the air is, in degrees Celsius. Precipitation: rain, hail or snow. Cloud cover: how much of the sky is covered, from clear to overcast. Thermometer: measures temperature. Rain gauge: measures rain in millimetres. Wind vane: shows wind direction. Anemometer: measures wind speed. Stevenson screen: a white box that keeps a thermometer in the shade. Cold front: the edge of a mass of cold air that brings winter rain to Cape Town. Summer, winter and all-year rainfall: the three rainfall patterns. Natural vegetation: plants that grow on their own. Adaptation: a feature that helps a plant survive its climate. Fynbos, grassland, savanna and succulents. Make flashcards and test yourself.

### subtopic_2 — Rainfall, Climate and Vegetation
**Assistant:** What are the most important History words for this term?

**Tutor:** Here is a short word bank for ancient Egypt. Pharaoh: the king of Egypt, believed to be a god on Earth. Inundation: the yearly flood of the Nile. Silt: the rich black mud left by the flood. Kemet: the black land, the fertile strip along the Nile. Shaduf: a pole and bucket for lifting water. Vizier: the chief minister. Scribe: a person trained to read and write. Social structure: how society is arranged into groups. Pyramid: a tomb for a pharaoh. Sphinx: a statue with a lion's body and a human head. Temple: the house of a god. Hieroglyphics: Egyptian picture-writing. Cartouche: an oval around a royal name. Papyrus: a reed used to make paper. Physician: a doctor. Mummy: a preserved body. Afterlife: life after death. Shabti: a small figure meant to work in the afterlife. Archaeologist: a person who studies the past by digging up objects.

### subtopic_3 — Ancient Egypt
**Assistant:** How can I link Geography and History this term?

**Tutor:** There is a great link between the two subjects this term: climate and water. In Geography, you learnt that South Africa is a dry country, that rainfall decreases from east to west, and that people settle where there is water and good soil. In History, you learnt that Egypt is even drier, with almost no rain, and that people could only live there because of the Nile, a river that brought water from rain that fell far away, in the highlands of Ethiopia. The Nile's summer flood, caused by a rainfall pattern in another part of Africa, decided the Egyptian calendar, the three seasons and the life of farmers. And the dry desert climate preserved the pyramids, papyrus scrolls, mummies and Tutankhamun's treasures for thousands of years. So weather and climate shaped both the past and the present. In a paragraph answer, this kind of link can earn you good marks.
