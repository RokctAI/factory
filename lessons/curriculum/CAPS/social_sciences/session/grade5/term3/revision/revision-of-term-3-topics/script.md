# Part 1 — Expert

This session revises the Term 3 topics. In Geography, the topic was weather, climate and vegetation of South Africa: the elements of weather, rain, hail and snow, measuring temperature and rain, wind direction and weather maps, how weather affects daily life, the weather project, rainfall maps and rainfall patterns, climate, and natural vegetation. In History, the topic was an ancient African society, Egypt: the Nile and settlement, social structure, the Sphinx, pyramids and temples, hieroglyphics, mathematics and the stars, medicine, and the tomb of Tutankhamun. The anchor is a revision morning in a Grade 5 class in Durban, in KwaZulu-Natal. The classroom has three tables. Table 1 has a thermometer, a rain gauge, a wind vane and a weather map from a newspaper. Table 2 has a rainfall map of South Africa, three bar graphs, and pictures of a protea, a camel thorn tree and an aloe. Table 3 has a model pyramid, a sheet of hieroglyphs, a picture of the Nile from space and a replica of Tutankhamun's golden mask. Groups move from table to table, answering questions on cards. The big idea is that weather and climate shape where plants grow and how people live, and that ancient Egypt was a great African civilisation shaped by the Nile. The whole of Term 3 is examinable.

## Subtopic: Weather and Measuring

At Table 1, the instruments remind the class that weather is what the air is like at a particular time and place. Its elements are temperature, wind, cloud cover and precipitation, which can fall as rain, hail or snow. Hail forms in tall thunderclouds, mostly in summer, and snow falls mostly on high mountains in winter.

Temperature is measured with a thermometer, in degrees Celsius, in the shade, inside a white Stevenson screen. Rain is measured with a rain gauge, in millimetres, in an open place, read at the same time every day and then emptied.

Wind is named after the direction it comes from. A wind vane's arrow points into the wind, and a windsock shows direction and strength. Weather maps in the media use symbols, explained in a key, and give the minimum and maximum temperatures for each city.

Weather affects what we wear, our homes, food, travel, work and health. In extreme weather, listen to warnings, stay indoors in thunderstorms and never cross floodwater.

In the weather project, learners observe at the same times each day, record in a table, draw line graphs for temperature and bar graphs for rain, and look for patterns.

The questions for this section are with you now: weather, instruments, wind and weather maps.

## Subtopic: Rainfall, Climate and Vegetation

At Table 2, the rainfall map reminds the class that rainfall in South Africa decreases from east to west. The east is wetter because of the warm Indian Ocean, and the west is dry because of the cold Benguela Current. South Africa is a dry country, with an average of about 450 millimetres a year.

Most of South Africa has summer rainfall, from thunderstorms. The south-western Cape, including Cape Town, has winter rainfall from cold fronts. The southern coast, around George, has all-year rainfall. On a bar graph, tall bars in summer, tall bars in winter or even bars show the pattern.

Climate is the usual weather of a place over about thirty years. South Africa has several climates: warm and wet on the east coast; summer rain and cold, dry winters on the Highveld; cold in the high mountains; Mediterranean in the south-western Cape; mild on the southern coast; and dry in the Karoo and the west.

Natural vegetation grows on its own and is adapted to the climate: forest where it is wet all year, grassland on the Highveld, savanna with thorn trees in the warm north and east, fynbos in the winter rainfall south-west, and succulents in the dry west.

The questions for this section are with you now: rainfall, climate and natural vegetation.

## Subtopic: Ancient Egypt

At Table 3, the model pyramid reminds the class about History. Ancient Egypt was an African civilisation in north-eastern Africa, which lasted about three thousand years. The Nile flows north through the desert, and its yearly flood left black silt on the land, so the Egyptians lived along the river and farmed. Herodotus called Egypt the gift of the Nile.

Society was shaped like a pyramid: the pharaoh at the top, then officials and priests, scribes and soldiers, craftspeople, farmers, and servants and enslaved people. Women could own property, and Hatshepsut ruled as pharaoh.

Pyramids were tombs for pharaohs, built by paid workers. The Great Sphinx was carved from rock, and temples were houses of the gods. Hieroglyphics had more than seven hundred signs, and the Rosetta Stone helped to read them again. The Egyptians counted in tens, made a 365-day calendar, and had physicians who examined patients carefully.

In 1922, Howard Carter found the tomb of Tutankhamun, a young pharaoh, in the Valley of the Kings. It revealed beliefs in the afterlife, great wealth, skilled crafts, trade and daily life.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a wind vane points where the wind goes; it points where the wind comes from. Two: thinking Cape Town gets summer rain; it gets winter rain. Three: thinking the Nile flows south; it flows north. Four: thinking slaves built the pyramids; evidence shows mostly paid workers.

The questions for this section are with you now: ancient Egypt.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the three revision tables in Durban — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Weather

Weather is the air today: temperature, wind, clouds and rain, hail or snow.

Thermometer: degrees Celsius. Rain gauge: millimetres. Wind is named after where it comes from.

Weather maps use symbols and a key. In storms, stay indoors. Never cross floodwater.

Keep the thermometer, rain gauge and wind vane on Table 1 in mind and try the questions for this part: weather and measuring.

## Subtopic: Rain, Climate, Plants

The east is wet and the west is dry. Most places get summer rain, Cape Town gets winter rain, and George gets rain all year.

Climate is the usual weather over many years. Plants are adapted to the climate: fynbos in the south-west, grassland on the Highveld, and succulents in the dry west.

Hold the protea, the camel thorn and the aloe in mind and try this part's questions: rainfall, climate and vegetation.

## Subtopic: Egypt

Ancient Egypt was an African civilisation. The Nile flooded every year and left rich black soil. The pharaoh was at the top of society, and most people were farmers. Pyramids were tombs, temples were houses of the gods, and hieroglyphics was their writing. Carter found Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922.

Pocket summary of the term. Weather is described by temperature, wind, cloud cover and precipitation, measured with thermometers and rain gauges. Rainfall decreases from east to west, with summer, winter and all-year patterns. Climate is the usual weather over many years, and natural vegetation is adapted to it. Ancient Egypt was an African civilisation along the Nile, with pharaohs, pyramids, hieroglyphics and Tutankhamun's tomb.

Hold the replica golden mask on Table 3 in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: ancient Egypt.
