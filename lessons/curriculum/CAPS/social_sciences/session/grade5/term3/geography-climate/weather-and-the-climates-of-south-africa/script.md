# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the difference between climate and weather, and the different kinds of climate in South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Johannesburg, in Gauteng, working on a project called the Big Trip. Each group must plan a pretend holiday that visits five places: Johannesburg itself, Durban, Cape Town, the Karoo town of Beaufort West, and a mountain hut in the Drakensberg. For each place, they must decide what to pack, but there is a catch: the trip is in July, in the middle of winter, and they must plan it today, without knowing what the weather will be. So they cannot use a weather forecast. Instead, they must use climate: what each place is usually like in July. One group packs shorts and sandals for Durban, a raincoat and umbrella for Cape Town, a thick jacket and gloves for the Drakensberg, warm pyjamas for the Karoo nights, and a jersey and lip balm for dry, sunny Johannesburg. The big idea is that weather is what happens on a particular day, while climate is the usual pattern of weather over many years, and that South Africa has several different climates. Climate is examinable.

## Subtopic: Weather and Climate

Weather is the state of the air at a particular time and place: the temperature, wind, cloud cover and precipitation today, this morning, or this week. Weather changes often, sometimes within hours.

Climate is the average or usual weather of a place, worked out from records over many years, usually about thirty years. Climate tells us what to expect in each season.

We describe a climate mainly by two things: temperature and rainfall. For temperature, we ask: how hot are the summers, and how cold are the winters? For rainfall, we ask: how much rain falls in a year, and in which season does it fall?

For example, Johannesburg's climate can be described like this: warm summers with afternoon thunderstorms, and cold, dry, sunny winters, with frost on some nights. That is true almost every year, even though the weather on any one day can be different.

A useful test is to ask whether a statement could change by tomorrow. It is snowing in the Drakensberg today could change by tomorrow, so it is weather. The Drakensberg usually has snow on its peaks in winter will still be true next year, so it is climate.

Climate helps people to plan: which crops to grow, how to build houses, what clothes to buy, when to have holidays and sports seasons. Weather forecasts help people to plan for the next few days.

## Subtopic: Climates of the East and the Interior

South Africa has several kinds of climate, because it is a large country with mountains, a high plateau, two oceans and different rainfall patterns.

The east coast, including Durban and the KwaZulu-Natal coast, has a warm, wet climate, sometimes called subtropical. Summers are hot and humid, which means the air feels damp and sticky, with plenty of rain. Winters are mild and fairly dry. It almost never freezes. The Lowveld in Mpumalanga and Limpopo is similar, though further from the sea.

The Highveld, including Johannesburg, Pretoria and much of the Free State, has a climate with warm summers and summer thunderstorms, and cold, dry winters. Because the Highveld is high above sea level, nights are cool, and in winter, there is often frost.

The high mountains, such as the Drakensberg, have a cold mountain climate. Temperatures fall as you go higher, so the mountains are much colder than the land below. Winters are very cold, with snow on the peaks, and summers are cool and rainy, with many thunderstorms and mist.

The questions for this section are with you now: the climates of the east coast, the Highveld and the mountains.

## Subtopic: Climates of the South and the West

The south-western Cape, including Cape Town, has what is called a Mediterranean climate, because it is similar to the climate around the Mediterranean Sea in southern Europe. Summers are warm to hot and dry, and winters are cool and wet, with rain from cold fronts.

The southern coast, along the Garden Route, has a mild climate, with rain in all seasons. Because it is next to the sea, it is not very hot in summer or very cold in winter.

The interior of the west, including the Karoo, Namaqualand and the Kalahari, has a dry climate, called semi-desert in most places. It gets little rain, and the rain is unreliable. Summers are hot, often very hot, and winter nights are cold, sometimes freezing, because dry, clear air lets heat escape quickly at night. So the difference between day and night temperatures is large. Places such as Beaufort West, in the Karoo, and Upington have this kind of climate.

The west coast is dry too, but it is cooler than the interior, because of the cold Benguela Current, and it often has fog.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: describing one rainy day as Cape Town's climate; climate is the usual pattern over many years. Two: thinking the Highveld is warm in winter because it is inland; it is high, so winter nights are cold and frosty. Three: thinking desert places are hot day and night; dry places often have cold nights. Four: thinking the Drakensberg has the same climate as Durban; temperature drops as you go higher.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Mediterranean, southern coast and dry climates.

## Subtopic: Weather or Climate?

Weather is today. Climate is the usual pattern over about thirty years.

We describe climate by temperature and rainfall: how hot or cold, how much rain, and when.

Keep the packing lists for a trip in July in mind and try the questions for this part: weather and climate.

## Subtopic: East and Highveld

Durban: hot, humid summers and mild winters.

The Highveld: summer thunderstorms, and cold, dry winters with frost.

The Drakensberg: cold, with snow in winter.

Hold the thick jacket and gloves for the mountain hut in mind and try this part's questions: east, Highveld and mountains.

## Subtopic: South and West

Cape Town: dry summers and wet winters. The Garden Route: mild, with rain all year. The Karoo: dry, with hot days and cold nights. The west coast is dry and cool, often with fog.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Weather is the air today, and climate is the usual weather over many years, described by temperature and rainfall. South Africa has several climates: warm and wet on the east coast; summer rain and cold, dry winters on the Highveld; cold in the high mountains; dry summers and wet winters in the south-western Cape; mild with all-year rain on the southern coast; and dry, with hot days and cold nights, in the Karoo and the west.
