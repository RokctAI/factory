# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers how weather affects people's daily lives. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Mbombela, in the Lowveld of Mpumalanga, during a week of very changeable weather. On Monday and Tuesday, there is a heatwave. It is over thirty-five degrees, the classroom is stuffy, the learners carry water bottles, the netball practice is moved to the early morning, and the fruit sellers at the taxi rank keep their mangoes and litchis in the shade. On Wednesday afternoon, a violent thunderstorm hits. Rain pours down, a low bridge on the way to school floods, the electricity goes off for two hours, and the farmer who supplies the school kitchen says the rain was good for his crops but the hail damaged some fruit. On Friday, the morning is cool and misty, and parents drive slowly with their lights on. By the end of the week, the class has filled a chart showing how each day's weather changed people's lives. The big idea is that weather affects almost everything people do, from what we wear and eat to how we travel, work, stay healthy and stay safe. How weather affects daily life is examinable.

## Subtopic: Clothes, Homes and Food

Weather affects what we wear. On hot days, people wear light, loose, light-coloured clothes and hats, because light colours reflect the sun's heat. On cold days, people wear warm, thick clothes in layers, and on rainy days, raincoats and umbrellas.

Weather affects our homes. In hot places, houses often have wide verandas, shady trees, high ceilings and windows that let a breeze through. In cold places, people need heaters, blankets and thick walls, and roofs need to be strong enough for heavy rain or hail. Many families collect rainwater in tanks.

Weather affects food. Farmers plan when to plant and harvest according to the weather. Rain at the right time brings good harvests, but a drought, which is a long time with too little rain, can make crops fail and food prices rise. Hail and frost can destroy crops in a single night. Fruit grown in the warm, wet Lowveld, such as mangoes, litchis and bananas, would not grow well in a cold, dry place.

Weather also affects the food we choose. On hot days, people want cold drinks and fruit, and on cold days, hot soup and porridge.

The questions for this section are with you now: how weather affects clothing, homes and food.

## Subtopic: Work, Travel and Fun

Weather affects how people travel. Heavy rain makes roads slippery and can flood low bridges, like the one in Mbombela. Mist and fog make it hard to see, so drivers must slow down and switch on their lights. Snow can close mountain passes. Strong winds and storms can delay aeroplanes and ships, and fishing boats may not go out to sea.

Weather affects work. Builders, farmers, road workers and people who sell goods outdoors depend on good weather. Some people earn more in certain weather: umbrella sellers on rainy days, ice cream sellers on hot days. Storms can damage power lines and cause power cuts, which affect businesses and homes.

Weather affects sport and leisure. Matches may be cancelled because of lightning or heavy rain. Beach outings, picnics and hikes depend on good weather. Tourists choose when to visit places partly because of the weather.

Weather even affects school: on very hot days, schools may move outdoor activities to the cooler early morning, and in floods, schools may close for safety.

Weather affects electricity use too. In cold winters, many families switch on heaters, so more electricity is needed in the evenings. On very hot days, fans and air conditioners are used more. Solar panels make more electricity on clear, sunny days than on cloudy ones.

The questions for this section are with you now: how weather affects travel, work and leisure.

## Subtopic: Health, Safety and Extreme Weather

Weather affects our health. In very hot weather, people can suffer from heatstroke and dehydration, which means the body has lost too much water. To stay safe, drink plenty of water, stay in the shade, and wear a hat and sunscreen. In very cold weather, people can get too cold, especially babies and older people, and some families use fires or heaters indoors, which must be used carefully, with fresh air, to avoid fires and dangerous fumes.

Extreme weather is weather that is much more severe than usual, such as heatwaves, droughts, floods, severe thunderstorms, hailstorms, strong winds and heavy snow. Extreme weather can damage homes, roads, crops and power lines, and can put lives in danger.

People can prepare for extreme weather. Weather warnings from the South African Weather Service, shared on radio, television and phones, help people to get ready. In a thunderstorm, stay indoors and away from water and tall trees, because of lightning. Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater, because even shallow, fast-moving water can sweep a person or a car away. In a drought, save water.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking dark clothes are coolest in summer; light colours reflect heat. Two: thinking it is safe to cross a flooded low bridge if the water looks shallow; fast water can sweep people and cars away. Three: thinking rain is always good for farmers; hail, floods and rain at harvest time can damage crops. Four: thinking it is safe to shelter under a tall tree in a thunderstorm; lightning often strikes tall objects.

The questions for this section are with you now: weather, health and safety, and extreme weather.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the changeable week in Mbombela — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Clothes, Homes, Food

Hot days: light, loose clothes and hats. Cold days: warm layers. Rainy days: raincoats.

Homes in hot places have shade and breezes. Homes in cold places need heaters and blankets.

Farmers need rain at the right time. Drought, hail and frost can ruin crops.

Keep the mangoes in the shade at the taxi rank in mind and try the questions for this part: clothes, homes and food.

## Subtopic: Travel, Work, Fun

Rain floods roads and bridges. Mist means drive slowly with lights on.

Storms can cut the power. Matches can be cancelled for lightning.

Hold the flooded low bridge in mind and try this part's questions: travel, work and fun.

## Subtopic: Staying Safe

Too much heat can make you sick. In heat, drink water and stay in the shade. In storms, stay indoors, away from tall trees. Never cross floodwater. Listen to weather warnings on the radio, television or phone.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Weather affects what we wear, how we build homes, what we eat and how farmers grow food. It affects travel, work, sport and school, and can cause power cuts and floods. It affects health, through heatstroke, dehydration and cold. Extreme weather, such as heatwaves, droughts, floods and storms, is dangerous, so people listen to warnings, stay indoors in storms and never cross floodwater.

Hold the class chart of the whole week's weather in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: health, safety and extreme weather.
