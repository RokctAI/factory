# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers determining and describing wind direction, and reading weather maps in the media. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Cape Town on a bright, windy summer day. From the school field, the learners can see Table Mountain, with a thick white cloud pouring over its flat top and down its sides, like a tablecloth spread over a table. A strong wind is blowing, bending the trees and sending chip packets flying across the field. Locals call this south-easter the Cape Doctor, because people say it blows away smoke and dust. On the school roof, a metal wind vane in the shape of an arrow swings and points towards the south-east. In the evening, the learners watch the weather report on the television news, where a presenter stands in front of a map of South Africa covered in suns, clouds, raindrops and numbers. The big idea is that wind direction is described by the compass direction the wind comes from, that we can find it with simple instruments, and that weather maps use symbols to show the weather across the country. Wind direction and weather maps are examinable.

## Subtopic: Wind Direction

Wind is always named after the direction it blows from, not the direction it blows to. The south-easter in Cape Town blows from the south-east, towards the north-west. A north wind blows from the north.

We describe wind direction using the eight points of the compass: north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west and north-west.

There are simple ways to find wind direction. You can wet a finger and hold it up: the side that feels cold is the side the wind is coming from. You can drop a few bits of dry grass and watch which way they blow. You can look at a flag, smoke from a chimney, or trees bending: they point away from where the wind comes from.

Remember the trick: winds come from somewhere. Think of a visitor who comes from Durban. We name the visitor by where they come from, not where they are going. In the same way, a wind that comes from the sea is called a sea breeze, even though it blows onto the land.

Some winds have special names in South Africa. The Cape Doctor is the south-easter in Cape Town in summer. Berg winds are hot, dry winds that blow from the interior plateau down towards the coast, mostly in winter, and can raise temperatures suddenly.

## Subtopic: Wind Vanes and Windsocks

A wind vane is an instrument that shows wind direction. It has an arrow that can turn freely on a pole, with a broad tail at one end. The wind pushes on the broad tail and turns it away, so the arrow points into the wind, towards the direction the wind is coming from. Below the arrow, there are fixed arms marked N, E, S and W, which must be lined up with the real compass directions. Then you can read the direction the arrow points to. If the arrow points south-east, it is a south-easterly wind.

A windsock is a tube of cloth, often orange and white, open at both ends and fixed to a pole. The wind fills the sock and it blows away from the wind, so the open, wide end points towards where the wind comes from. Windsocks are used at airports, on farms and near highways, because they also show wind strength: in a strong wind, the sock sticks straight out, and in a light wind, it droops.

Wind speed can be measured with an anemometer, which has little cups that spin faster in a stronger wind. Wind speed is usually given in kilometres per hour.

## Subtopic: Weather Maps in the Media

The media, such as newspapers, television, radio, websites and phone apps, share weather forecasts every day. In South Africa, forecasts are prepared by the South African Weather Service.

A weather map shows what the weather will be like across an area. It uses symbols, which are explained in a key. Common symbols are a sun for clear, sunny weather; a sun partly behind a cloud for partly cloudy weather; a cloud for overcast weather; a cloud with raindrops for rain; a cloud with a lightning bolt for thunderstorms; and a cloud with snowflakes for snow. Arrows can show wind direction, and sometimes wind strength.

Next to each city, there are usually two numbers: the expected minimum and maximum temperatures in degrees Celsius. For example, Cape Town, 16 and 27, means a low of sixteen degrees and a high of twenty-seven.

Some weather maps also show cold fronts as a line with triangles. A cold front is the edge of a mass of cold air, which often brings rain, wind and cooler weather to the Western Cape in winter.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a south-easter blows towards the south-east; it blows from the south-east. Two: thinking the wind vane arrow points where the wind is going; it points into the wind, where it comes from. Three: reading the two numbers on a weather map as rainfall; they are the minimum and maximum temperatures. Four: thinking a weather map shows the climate; it shows the weather for one day.

The questions for this section are with you now: weather symbols, temperatures and fronts on weather maps.
