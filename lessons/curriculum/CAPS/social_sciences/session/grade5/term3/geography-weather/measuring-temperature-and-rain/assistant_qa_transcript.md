### subtopic_1 — The Thermometer
**Assistant:** What does ten millimetres of rain actually mean?

**Tutor:** It means that if all the rain that fell had stayed exactly where it landed, on flat ground, without soaking into the soil, running away or drying up, it would make a layer of water ten millimetres deep. That is about the thickness of your little finger. It does not matter how wide the rain gauge is. A wide container catches more water, but the water spreads over a bigger area, so the depth stays the same. That is why we can compare rainfall measured in different rain gauges all over the country. Ten millimetres sounds small, but it is a lot of water over a big area. On one square metre of ground, ten millimetres of rain is ten litres of water, about the same as a bucket. A farmer with a big field would be very happy with a few good falls of ten millimetres in summer.

### subtopic_2 — Measuring Temperature Properly
**Assistant:** Can we make our own rain gauge?

**Tutor:** Yes, and it is a great project. Take a clear plastic bottle with straight sides, about two litres. Ask an adult to cut off the top part, just where the bottle starts to curve. Turn the top part upside down and push it into the bottom part, like a funnel, which helps to stop water from evaporating. Put a few stones in the bottom so that it does not blow over, and add water up to a level line, which you mark as zero. Then stick a ruler, or draw a scale in millimetres, on the side, starting from the zero line. Place your gauge in an open spot, away from trees and walls, and dig it a little into the ground so that it stands firmly. Read it at the same time every day, then pour out the water down to the zero line. Record your readings in a table.

### subtopic_3 — The Rain Gauge
**Assistant:** Why do weather stations record the maximum and minimum temperature?

**Tutor:** The temperature changes all day long. If you measure it only once, at eight in the morning, you know what it was at eight, but not how hot it got in the afternoon or how cold it got at night. The maximum, the highest temperature of the day, and the minimum, the lowest, give a fuller picture. They are very useful. Farmers want to know the minimum, because if it drops below zero, frost can damage crops. People planning outdoor work or sport want to know the maximum, to avoid dangerous heat. Weather forecasts on television and phones usually give the expected maximum and minimum for each city. Over many years, these records also help scientists to describe a place's climate, for example, the average maximum in January, and to notice whether temperatures are slowly changing over time.
