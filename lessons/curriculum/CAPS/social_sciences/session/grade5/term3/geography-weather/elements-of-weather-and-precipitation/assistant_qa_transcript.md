### subtopic_1 — Temperature and Wind
**Assistant:** Why does the wind get strong and cool just before a storm?

**Tutor:** Inside a big thunderstorm cloud, there is a lot going on. Warm air rises fast into the cloud, and rain and hail start to fall. As the rain falls, it pulls cold air down with it, and some of the rain evaporates, which cools the air even more. This cold, heavy air rushes down to the ground and spreads out in all directions, like water poured onto a floor. When it reaches you, you feel a sudden, strong, cool wind, often just a few minutes before the rain arrives. That is why, on a hot summer afternoon, the temperature can drop quickly as a storm comes near. People who live on the Highveld know this feeling well: when the wind suddenly turns cool and the trees start to bend, it is time to bring in the washing and go indoors. Storms can also bring lightning, so stay inside until it has passed.

### subtopic_2 — Clouds and Cloud Cover
**Assistant:** Why does hail fall in summer and not winter?

**Tutor:** It sounds strange, because hail is ice, but hail needs heat to form. On hot summer days, the sun heats the ground, and warm, moist air rises very fast, building huge thunderclouds that can reach many kilometres high, where it is freezing cold even in summer. Inside these clouds, strong upward winds carry raindrops up into the freezing air, where they turn to ice. They fall, are carried up again, and add new layers of ice each time, like the layers of an onion. When the hailstones are too heavy for the wind to hold up, they fall. In winter, the ground is not hot enough to make these tall storm clouds in most of South Africa, so there is little hail. If you cut a big hailstone in half, you can sometimes see the rings of its layers.

### subtopic_3 — Rain, Hail and Snow
**Assistant:** Where can you see snow in South Africa?

**Tutor:** Snow falls mostly in winter, from about June to August, on the highest mountains and high ground. The Drakensberg, along the border of KwaZulu-Natal and Lesotho, often has snow on its peaks, and the eastern Free State, near Harrismith, sometimes gets snow too. Snow also falls in the mountains of the Eastern Cape, near towns such as Barkly East and Rhodes, and on high peaks in the Western Cape, such as the Hex River Mountains and the Cederberg. Sutherland, in the Northern Cape, is one of the coldest towns in South Africa, and it sometimes has snow. Occasionally, after a very cold front, snow even falls on parts of the Highveld, but this is rare. Snow can close mountain passes and cause danger for farm animals, so the weather service warns people when heavy snow is expected.
