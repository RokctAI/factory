### subtopic_1 — Clothes, Homes and Food
**Assistant:** Why is floodwater so dangerous, even if it looks shallow?

**Tutor:** Floodwater is much more dangerous than it looks. First, it is often moving fast, and moving water is very powerful. Water only about knee-deep, flowing quickly, can knock an adult off their feet, and water a little deeper can lift and float a car, because cars are hollow and light for their size. Second, you cannot see what is under the water. The road or bridge may have been washed away, there may be holes, sharp objects or open drains, and the water may be much deeper than you think. Third, floodwater is often dirty and can carry germs. In South Africa, people have lost their lives trying to cross flooded low-water bridges. The rule is simple: if it is flooded, turn around. Wait for the water to go down, or find another way. No trip is more important than your life.

### subtopic_2 — Work, Travel and Fun
**Assistant:** How does a drought affect people who do not live on farms?

**Tutor:** A drought affects everyone, not just farmers. When there is too little rain for a long time, dams and rivers get low, so towns and cities may have to limit water use. People might only be allowed a certain amount of water each day, and gardens, car washes and swimming pools may be banned. Cape Town had a serious drought from about 2015 to 2018, when people saved water in many creative ways. Food prices also rise in a drought, because farmers grow less, and some may have to sell or lose their animals. This affects every family that buys food. Jobs can be lost on farms and in businesses linked to farming. Even electricity can be affected, because some power stations need water. That is why it is important to save water all the time, not only during a drought.

### subtopic_3 — Health, Safety and Extreme Weather
**Assistant:** Why does lightning strike tall things?

**Tutor:** Lightning is a giant spark of electricity between a thundercloud and the ground. It looks for the easiest path, and tall objects, such as trees, towers, poles and even a person standing in an open field, give it a shorter path to the ground. That is why it is dangerous to shelter under a tall tree or stand on a hilltop in a storm. Water also carries electricity, so swimming pools, rivers and dams are dangerous. South Africa, especially the Highveld and the eastern parts, has many thunderstorms in summer, and lightning injures and kills people every year. The safest places are inside a solid building or a car with a metal roof, with the windows closed. If you hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck, so go indoors. Wait about thirty minutes after the last thunder before going out again.
