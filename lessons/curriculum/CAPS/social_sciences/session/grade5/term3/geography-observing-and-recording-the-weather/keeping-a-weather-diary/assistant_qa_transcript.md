### subtopic_1 — Observing and Recording
**Assistant:** How do I make a wind vane at home?

**Tutor:** You need a plastic straw, a pin, a pencil with an eraser on the end, a piece of stiff card, some tape or glue, and a plastic cup or a lump of clay to stand it in. Cut a small triangle and a bigger square from the card. Make a slit at each end of the straw, and slide the triangle into one end as the arrowhead and the square into the other end as the tail. Push the pin through the middle of the straw, where it balances, and then into the eraser of the pencil, so that the straw can spin freely. Stand the pencil upright in the clay or in the cup. Outside, use a compass, or the sun, to mark north, east, south and west around it. The wind pushes the big tail away, so the arrow points to where the wind comes from.

### subtopic_2 — Graphs and Patterns
**Assistant:** What if nothing changes for days?

**Tutor:** That is still important information. If the weather is clear, dry and calm for several days in a row, you have found a pattern: a spell of settled weather. In winter on the Highveld and in much of the interior, it is normal to have many clear, dry days, with cold mornings and mild afternoons. Keep recording carefully, because small changes still matter. Look closely at the temperature: is each morning a little colder or warmer than the last? Is the wind coming from the same direction each day? Does a little cloud appear in the afternoons? When the weather finally does change, for example, when a cold front brings wind and cloud, your records will show exactly what came before and after. In your comments, you can explain how a long dry spell affects daily life: dusty roads, dry gardens and the need to save water.

### subtopic_3 — Weather and Daily Life
**Assistant:** How do I write a good conclusion for my project?

**Tutor:** A good conclusion is short, clear and based on your own records. Start by saying what you did: for example, I observed the weather in Kimberley for two weeks in August. Then describe two or three main patterns you found, using numbers from your diary: the afternoon temperature was always higher than the morning temperature, by about ten degrees; it rained on only one day, when eight millimetres fell during a thunderstorm; the wind usually came from the north-west. Then say how the weather affected daily life: the cold mornings meant we wore jerseys, and the dusty wind kept us inside. Finally, say what you learnt or what surprised you, or what you would do differently next time. Do not add facts that are not in your records, and do not copy from the internet. Your teacher wants to see your own careful observations.
