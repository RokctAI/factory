### subtopic_1 — What Is Weather?
**Assistant:** How can I quickly tell weather and climate apart in a question?

**Tutor:** Look for clue words about time. If the question talks about today, tomorrow, this morning, last night or this week, it is about weather. For example: it is raining in Durban today. That is weather. If the question talks about usually, normally, every year, most years, or an average, it is about climate. For example: Durban usually has hot, wet summers. That is climate. Another trick is to ask yourself: could this change by tomorrow? If yes, it is weather. A thunderstorm can come and go in an hour. But Durban's climate will not suddenly change into a desert climate tomorrow. Climate changes only slowly, over many years. You can also remember the saying: weather is your mood today, climate is your personality. Your mood can change quickly, but your personality stays mostly the same. In a test, underline the time words first, then decide.

### subtopic_2 — What Is Climate?
**Assistant:** Why is the camel thorn tree so special?

**Tutor:** The camel thorn is one of the best-known trees of the dry Northern Cape, Namibia and Botswana. It is very well adapted to a hot, dry climate. Its main root can grow extremely deep, much deeper than the tree is tall, to reach water underground, so it can stay green even in very dry times. Its leaves are made of many tiny leaflets, which lose less water in the sun. Its long, white thorns protect it from being eaten by some animals, though giraffes, with their tough tongues, still eat its leaves. Its pods are eaten by cattle, antelope and other animals. Its shade is precious: animals and people rest under it in the heat, and sociable weaver birds sometimes build huge shared nests in it. It is a protected tree in South Africa, which means it may not be cut down without a permit.

### subtopic_3 — What Is Natural Vegetation?
**Assistant:** Is precipitation only rain?

**Tutor:** No. Precipitation means any water that falls from clouds to the ground. Rain is the most common kind: drops of liquid water. Hail is balls or lumps of ice, which form inside tall thunderstorm clouds, where strong winds carry raindrops high up into freezing air, again and again, so that layers of ice build up. Hailstorms are common on the Highveld in summer, and big hailstones can damage crops, cars and roofs. Snow is made of ice crystals, which form when it is very cold high up in the clouds and stay frozen all the way to the ground. In South Africa, snow falls mainly in winter, on high mountains such as the Drakensberg, and sometimes in high places in the Eastern Cape, the Northern Cape and the Western Cape. Dew and frost are not precipitation, because they form on the ground rather than falling from clouds.
