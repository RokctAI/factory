# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the topic weather, climate and vegetation of South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Upington, a town on the banks of the Gariep River in the Northern Cape. It is ten o'clock on a summer morning, and it is already hot. The sky is clear and blue, with not a single cloud, and a light, dry wind blows across the schoolyard. The learners sit in the shade of a big camel thorn tree, with thin, feathery leaves and long white thorns. The teacher asks three questions. What is the weather like today? Hot, dry and sunny. What is it usually like in Upington? Hot in summer, cold at night in winter, and dry for most of the year. And why does this tree grow here, while there is no thick forest around the town? Its long roots reach deep for water, and its small leaves lose little water. The big idea is that weather is the state of the air on a particular day, climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years, and natural vegetation is the plant life that grows on its own in a place, shaped by its climate. The introduction to this topic is examinable.

## Subtopic: What Is Weather?

Weather is what the air around us is like at a particular time and place. It can change from hour to hour and from day to day.

When we describe the weather, we talk about its elements. The main elements of weather are temperature, which is how hot or cold the air is; wind, which is moving air; cloud cover, which is how much of the sky is covered by clouds; and precipitation, which is water falling from the sky as rain, hail or snow.

In Upington this morning, the temperature is high, there is a light wind, there are no clouds, and there is no rain. But by the afternoon, the weather could change. In summer, clouds sometimes build up, and a thunderstorm brings a short, heavy shower.

Weather matters to everyone. It helps us to decide what to wear, whether to carry an umbrella, when farmers should plant or harvest, and whether it is safe to fly a plane or go out to sea. That is why weather forecasts are shown on television, on the radio, in newspapers and on phones.

We can observe the weather with our senses: we see clouds and rain, feel the heat and the wind, and hear thunder. We can also measure it with instruments, such as a thermometer for temperature and a rain gauge for rain. Measuring gives us numbers that we can write down and compare from day to day.

The questions for this section are with you now: what weather is, and its main elements.

## Subtopic: What Is Climate?

Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place, worked out over many years. Weather experts usually use records of about thirty years to describe a climate.

One hot day does not tell you about a climate. But if you know that Upington has hot summers, cold winter nights and very little rain almost every year, then you know about its climate. We say Upington has a hot, dry climate.

A simple way to remember it is: weather is what you get today, and climate is what you expect. You choose your clothes for the day by looking at the weather, but you choose what clothes to own by thinking about the climate.

South Africa has several kinds of climate. Cape Town gets most of its rain in winter. Durban is warm and wet, with most rain in summer. Johannesburg has warm, rainy summers and cold, dry winters. The west of the country is very dry, and the high mountains of the Drakensberg can have snow in winter.

The questions for this section are with you now: what climate is and how it differs from weather.

## Subtopic: What Is Natural Vegetation?

Natural vegetation is the plant life that grows on its own in a place, without being planted by people. It includes trees, bushes, grasses and flowers.

The natural vegetation of a place depends mostly on its climate, especially on how much rain falls and how hot or cold it gets. Plants are adapted to their climate. An adaptation is a special feature that helps a plant or animal to survive where it lives.

The camel thorn tree in Upington is adapted to a hot, dry climate. Its roots grow very deep to reach water far underground. Its small leaves lose less water in the hot sun, and its thorns protect it from animals that want to eat it.

Different climates have different natural vegetation. Wet, warm places can have forests. Places with summer rain and cold winters often have grassland. Dry places have low bushes and succulents, which store water in thick leaves or stems. The south-western Cape has fynbos, a special vegetation of small bushes that grows where the rain falls in winter.

The plants in a garden, a farm field or a park are not natural vegetation, because people planted them.

So weather, climate and vegetation are linked. The weather, day after day and year after year, makes up the climate, and the climate decides which plants can grow naturally in a place. In this topic, we will study each of them in turn, and see how they affect the lives of people in South Africa.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking weather and climate are the same; weather is today, climate is the usual pattern over many years. Two: thinking precipitation means only rain; it also includes hail and snow. Three: thinking crops in a field are natural vegetation; they were planted by people. Four: thinking all of South Africa has the same climate; it has several different climates.

The questions for this section are with you now: natural vegetation and how plants are adapted to climate.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the schoolyard in Upington — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Weather

Weather is what the air is like today. It can change quickly.

The elements of weather are temperature, wind, cloud cover and precipitation. Precipitation is rain, hail or snow.

Weather helps us to choose our clothes and plan our day. We can see it, feel it and measure it.

Keep the clear blue sky over the schoolyard in mind and try the questions for this part: what is weather.

## Subtopic: Climate

Climate is the usual weather of a place over many years.

Weather is what you get today. Climate is what you expect.

Upington has a hot, dry climate. Cape Town gets winter rain. Durban is warm and wet.

Hold the hot, dry wind of Upington in mind and try this part's questions: what is climate.

## Subtopic: Natural Vegetation

Natural vegetation is plants that grow on their own. They are adapted to the climate. The camel thorn has deep roots and small leaves.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Weather is the state of the air today, described by temperature, wind, cloud cover and precipitation, which is rain, hail or snow. Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over many years, and South Africa has several climates. Natural vegetation is the plant life that grows on its own, and it is adapted to the climate, like the camel thorn tree with its deep roots and small leaves.

Hold the shade of the camel thorn tree in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: natural vegetation.
