# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers rainfall patterns in South Africa: summer, winter and all-year rainfall, shown on maps and bar graphs. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in George, a town on the Garden Route in the Western Cape, between the Outeniqua Mountains and the sea. The teacher has put three bar graphs on the board, each with twelve bars, one for each month from January to December. The first graph, for Pretoria, has tall bars at both ends, in the summer months, and almost no bars in the middle, in winter. The second graph, for Cape Town, is the opposite: low bars at both ends, and tall bars in the middle, in winter. The third graph, for George, has bars of fairly similar heights all through the year. Next to the graphs is a map of South Africa, coloured in three colours, with George marked on the narrow strip along the southern coast. A learner says that it seems to rain in George whenever you forget your umbrella. The big idea is that different parts of South Africa get their rain at different times of the year, and that these patterns can be shown on maps and read from bar graphs. Rainfall patterns are examinable.

## Subtopic: Summer Rainfall

Most of South Africa is a summer rainfall area. This means that most of the rain falls in the summer months, from about October to March, and the winters are dry.

The summer rainfall area covers most of the country, including Gauteng, Limpopo, Mpumalanga, North West, the Free State, KwaZulu-Natal and most of the Eastern Cape, as well as parts of the Northern Cape.

In summer, the land heats up, warm, moist air rises, and thunderclouds build up in the afternoons. So most summer rain falls in thunderstorms, often short and heavy, sometimes with lightning and hail. Along the east coast, warm, moist air from the Indian Ocean also brings longer periods of rain.

In winter, the air over the interior is usually cold, dry and sinking, so the sky is clear, the days are sunny, the nights are cold, and there is very little rain. Grass turns brown, and the risk of veld fires rises.

On a bar graph for a summer rainfall place like Pretoria, the tallest bars are in the summer months: January, February, March, October, November and December. The bars for June and July are very short or missing.

The questions for this section are with you now: the summer rainfall area and its pattern.

## Subtopic: Winter Rainfall

The south-western part of South Africa is a winter rainfall area. Most of the rain falls in the winter months, from about April or May to August or September, and the summers are hot and dry.

The winter rainfall area includes Cape Town and the south-western Cape, and stretches up the west coast into Namaqualand, although the west coast gets much less rain.

In winter, cold fronts move in from the Atlantic Ocean, one after another. A cold front is the edge of a mass of cold air. When it arrives, the wind becomes strong, the temperature drops, and rain falls, sometimes for a day or two. After the front passes, the weather clears, until the next one comes. On the highest mountains, cold fronts can bring snow.

In summer, the south-western Cape is mostly dry, hot and windy, with the south-easter blowing.

On a bar graph for a winter rainfall place like Cape Town, the tallest bars are in the middle of the graph: May, June, July and August. The bars for December, January and February are short.

The questions for this section are with you now: the winter rainfall area, cold fronts and the bar graph pattern.

## Subtopic: All-Year Rainfall and Bar Graphs

A narrow strip along the southern coast, between about Mossel Bay and the area around Gqeberha, including George, Knysna and Plettenberg Bay, is an all-year rainfall area. Here rain can fall in any month, and it is spread fairly evenly through the year. This area gets rain from cold fronts in winter, and rain from moist winds off the ocean in summer.

Because rain falls in all seasons, the Garden Route is green all year, with indigenous forests, such as the Knysna forest, as well as plantations and dairy farms.

On a bar graph for an all-year rainfall place like George, the bars are fairly similar in height in every month, with no long dry season.

To read a rainfall bar graph, look at the title to see which place it is for. The months go along the bottom, and the rainfall in millimetres goes up the side. Find the tallest bars: if they are in the summer months, it is summer rainfall; if they are in the winter months, it is winter rainfall; and if the bars are all fairly even, it is all-year rainfall. Remember that in South Africa, summer is from about December to February, and winter is from about June to August.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking summer in South Africa is in June and July; that is our winter, because we are in the Southern Hemisphere. Two: thinking Cape Town gets summer rain; it gets winter rain from cold fronts. Three: thinking all-year rainfall means it rains every day; it means rain is spread through all the months. Four: reading a bar graph without checking its title; you must know which place it shows.

The questions for this section are with you now: all-year rainfall and reading rainfall bar graphs.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the three bar graphs in George — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Summer Rain

Most of South Africa gets summer rain, from about October to March.

It falls mostly in afternoon thunderstorms. Winters are dry and sunny, with cold nights.

Pretoria's graph has tall bars in summer and short bars in winter.

Keep the Pretoria graph with tall bars at both ends in mind and try the questions for this part: summer rainfall.

## Subtopic: Winter Rain

The south-western Cape, including Cape Town, gets winter rain.

Cold fronts from the Atlantic bring wind, cold and rain. Summers are hot and dry.

Cape Town's graph has tall bars in the middle, in winter.

Hold the Cape Town graph with tall bars in the middle in mind and try this part's questions: winter rainfall.

## Subtopic: Rain All Year

The southern coast, including George and Knysna, gets rain in every month, from cold fronts in winter and sea winds in summer. Its graph has even bars. That is why the Garden Route is green all year.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Most of South Africa gets summer rainfall, mostly from afternoon thunderstorms, with dry winters. The south-western Cape gets winter rainfall from cold fronts, with dry summers. The southern coast around George gets all-year rainfall. On a bar graph, tall bars in summer months mean summer rainfall, tall bars in winter months mean winter rainfall, and even bars mean all-year rainfall.

Hold the learner who always forgets an umbrella in George in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: all-year rainfall and bar graphs.
