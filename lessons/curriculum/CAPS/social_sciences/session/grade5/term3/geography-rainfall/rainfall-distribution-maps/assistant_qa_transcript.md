### subtopic_1 — Reading a Rainfall Map
**Assistant:** Why does the map show average rainfall and not one year?

**Tutor:** Because rainfall changes a lot from year to year, especially in South Africa. If we made a map using only one year, it might be a very wet year, with floods, or a very dry year, with drought, and the map would give the wrong idea about what a place is usually like. So weather experts add up the rainfall for each year over a long time, often about thirty years, and work out the average, which is the total divided by the number of years. This gives the amount a place can expect in a normal year. Farmers, engineers and town planners use these averages to decide which crops to grow, how big to build dams, and how much water a town will need. But they also know that real years can be very different from the average, so they plan for droughts and floods too.

### subtopic_2 — Wet East, Dry West
**Assistant:** How can Springbok's people survive with so little rain?

**Tutor:** People in dry places like Springbok have learnt many ways to live with little water. Towns get water from boreholes, which are deep holes drilled into the ground to reach water stored in rocks, and from pipelines that bring water from the Gariep River, many kilometres away. Families often collect rainwater from their roofs in tanks when it does rain, and they are very careful not to waste water. Farmers keep animals such as sheep and goats, which can live on the low, tough bushes of the dry land, and they need large farms because each animal needs a lot of land to graze. The natural plants of Namaqualand are adapted to the dry climate, and after good winter rains, the area bursts into colour with spring flowers. People there know that water is precious, and many city people could learn from them.

### subtopic_3 — A Dry Country
**Assistant:** Why is South Africa so dry when it has oceans on three sides?

**Tutor:** It is a good question, because you might expect a country with so much coastline to be wet. But it depends on the type of ocean water and the winds. On the west coast, the cold Benguela Current brings cold water from the south. Cold water does not evaporate much, so the air above it is dry, which is why the west coast is one of the driest areas. On the east coast, the warm Agulhas Current does give moist air, and that is why the east is wetter. Another reason is that South Africa lies in a belt of the world where dry, sinking air is common, which makes it hard for clouds to form for much of the year. Also, most of the interior is a high plateau, far from the sea, so moist air must travel a long way and climb high to get there.
