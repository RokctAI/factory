### subtopic_1 — Summer Rainfall
**Assistant:** Why are our seasons opposite to Europe's?

**Tutor:** It is because of how the Earth is tilted. The Earth is tilted a little on its axis, and it keeps this tilt as it travels around the sun during the year. For part of the year, the Southern Hemisphere, where South Africa is, leans towards the sun. The sun is higher in the sky, its rays are more direct, and the days are longer, so it is summer here, from about December to February. At the same time, the Northern Hemisphere, where Europe is, leans away from the sun, so it is winter there. Six months later, it is the other way around: the Northern Hemisphere leans towards the sun and has summer, while we have winter, from about June to August. That is why South Africans celebrate Christmas in summer, often at the beach, while people in Europe may have snow at Christmas.

### subtopic_2 — Winter Rainfall
**Assistant:** What is a cold front, and how do I know it is coming?

**Tutor:** A cold front is the leading edge of a large mass of cold air, moving in from the cold Southern Ocean and the Atlantic. When it pushes into warmer air, the warm air is forced upwards, cools, and forms clouds and rain. In the Western Cape in winter, you can often tell a cold front is coming. The day before, the wind may blow from the north-west, the sky may fill with high, thin clouds and then thicker grey clouds, and it may feel mild. Then the front arrives: the wind gets strong, the rain starts, and the temperature drops. After it has passed, the wind turns to the south-west, the sky clears and the air feels cold and fresh. On weather maps, a cold front is drawn as a line with little triangles. The weather service warns people about strong cold fronts that may bring storms, big waves at sea, or snow.

### subtopic_3 — All-Year Rainfall and Bar Graphs
**Assistant:** Why does the summer rainfall area have so many veld fires in winter?

**Tutor:** In the summer rainfall area, the grass grows well during the wet summer. When winter comes, there is almost no rain for several months. The grass dries out and turns brown, and dry grass burns very easily. Winter also brings cold nights, so people make more fires for warmth and cooking, and strong, dry winds, especially in late winter, can spread a fire quickly. That is why late winter and early spring, around July to September, is the main fire season on the Highveld and in other summer rainfall areas. Veld fires can destroy grazing, crops, homes and power lines, and they can be dangerous to people and animals. Farmers make fire breaks, which are strips of burnt or cleared ground, to stop fires from spreading. Some grasslands are adapted to fire, and new green grass grows quickly from the roots after the first spring rains.
