# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the Sphinx, the pyramids and the temples of ancient Egypt. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Pietermaritzburg, in KwaZulu-Natal, on a building afternoon. On one table, a group is building a model of the Great Pyramid of Giza from sugar cubes, layer by layer, each layer a little smaller than the one below. On another, a learner is shaping a model of the Great Sphinx from clay: a lying lion's body with its front paws stretched out, and a human head wearing a striped royal headcloth. A third group is making a model temple from cardboard boxes, with a tall gateway, an open courtyard, a hall full of thick columns made from toilet-roll tubes, and a small, dark room at the very back. The teacher asks a question: the real Great Pyramid was built from more than two million blocks of stone, without cranes, trucks or iron tools, so how did they do it? The big idea is that the pyramids, the Sphinx and the temples were built to honour the pharaohs and the gods and to prepare for the afterlife, and that they show the organisation, skill and beliefs of ancient Egyptian society. The Sphinx, pyramids and temples are examinable.

## Subtopic: The Pyramids

The Egyptians believed in an afterlife: that after death, a person's spirit lived on in another world. To live well in the afterlife, the body needed to be preserved, and the person needed a safe tomb, filled with the things they would need. Pharaohs, as god-kings, needed the grandest tombs of all.

The pyramids were tombs for pharaohs. The first was the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, built about four thousand seven hundred years ago for the pharaoh Djoser, and designed by his official Imhotep. It looks like six huge steps, one on top of the other.

Later pyramids had smooth, sloping sides. The most famous are the three pyramids at Giza, near Cairo. The biggest, the Great Pyramid, was built about four and a half thousand years ago for the pharaoh Khufu. It was about 146 metres tall when it was finished, and for thousands of years, it was the tallest building in the world. It is the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World that still stands.

Inside, narrow passages lead to burial chambers. The outside was once covered with smooth, white limestone, which shone in the sun, though most of it was later taken away to use in other buildings.

Pyramids were hard to protect from robbers, so later pharaohs were buried in tombs cut into the rock in the Valley of the Kings, near the city of Thebes.

The questions for this section are with you now: why and when the pyramids were built.

## Subtopic: Building the Pyramids

Building a pyramid took many years and thousands of workers. Archaeologists believe the Great Pyramid took about twenty years to build.

The stone was cut from quarries using copper chisels, stone hammers and wooden wedges. Most limestone came from nearby, but some fine limestone and huge granite blocks came from quarries far away and were carried on boats along the Nile.

The blocks were dragged on wooden sledges. A painting from an ancient tomb shows a worker pouring water onto the sand in front of a sledge carrying a huge statue, and scientists have shown that wet sand makes dragging much easier. To raise the blocks, the workers probably built ramps of mud brick and rubble, although historians still discuss exactly how the ramps were arranged.

Who built the pyramids? Archaeologists have found the remains of a town near the Giza pyramids where workers lived, with bakeries, breweries and sleeping places, and the workers' own graves near the pyramids. This shows that the builders were mostly paid workers, given food, drink and medical care, and many may have been farmers who worked there during the flood season. The old idea that the pyramids were built by enslaved crowds is not supported by this evidence.

The questions for this section are with you now: how the pyramids were built, and by whom.

## Subtopic: The Sphinx and the Temples

The Great Sphinx of Giza is a huge statue with the body of a lying lion and the head of a man wearing a royal headcloth. It was carved out of the limestone rock of the Giza plateau about four and a half thousand years ago, and many experts think its face shows the pharaoh Khafre. It is about 73 metres long and 20 metres high. A sphinx combined the strength of a lion with the wisdom of a king, and was seen as a guardian.

Temples were the houses of the gods. The Egyptians believed in many gods, such as Ra, the sun god, Osiris, god of the afterlife, Isis, a mother goddess, and Anubis, the jackal-headed god of mummification. Each temple had a statue of its god in a small, dark room at the back, called the sanctuary. Only priests could enter it. Every day, they washed and dressed the statue, and offered it food. Ordinary people prayed in the outer courtyards, and took part in great festivals.

The temple of Karnak, at Luxor, is one of the largest religious sites in the world, with a hall of more than a hundred huge columns. At Abu Simbel, two temples built by the pharaoh Ramesses the Second were cut into a cliff. In the 1960s, they were cut into blocks and moved higher up, to save them from the lake behind the Aswan High Dam.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the pyramids were palaces; they were tombs for pharaohs. Two: thinking enslaved crowds built the pyramids; evidence shows mostly paid workers. Three: thinking the Sphinx was built from blocks; it was carved from the rock. Four: thinking everyone could enter the temple's inner room; only priests could.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Great Sphinx and the temples.

## Subtopic: Tombs for Pharaohs

The Egyptians believed in life after death.

Pyramids were tombs for pharaohs. The first was the Step Pyramid of Djoser. The biggest is the Great Pyramid of Giza, built for Khufu about four and a half thousand years ago.

Later pharaohs were buried in the Valley of the Kings.

Keep the sugar-cube pyramid rising layer by layer in mind and try the questions for this part: the pyramids.

## Subtopic: Building

Workers cut stone with copper chisels. They dragged blocks on sledges over wet sand, and used ramps. Boats carried stone along the Nile.

Most builders were paid workers, not slaves. Their town and graves have been found.

## Subtopic: Sphinx and Temples

The Great Sphinx has a lion's body and a king's head, and it was carved from the rock. Temples were houses of the gods, and only priests entered the inner room.

Pocket summary of the lesson. The Egyptians believed in an afterlife, and built pyramids as tombs for pharaohs, from Djoser's Step Pyramid to Khufu's Great Pyramid at Giza. Paid workers built them over many years, with copper tools, sledges, wet sand, ramps and boats. The Great Sphinx, with a lion's body and a king's head, was carved from rock. Temples, such as Karnak and Abu Simbel, were houses of the gods.
