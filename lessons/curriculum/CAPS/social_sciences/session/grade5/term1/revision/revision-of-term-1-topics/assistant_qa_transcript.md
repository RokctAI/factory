### subtopic_1 — The World and the Compass
**Assistant:** What is the best way to learn the countries of Africa?

**Tutor:** Start small and close to home, then work outwards, a few at a time. First, learn South Africa's six neighbours and their capitals until you know them well. Then add countries in one region at a time, such as East Africa, with Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda, or North Africa, with Egypt, Libya, Algeria and Morocco. Use a blank outline map and colour in each country as you learn it, saying the name out loud. Make a game: one person points to a country and the other names it, or one names a capital and the other finds the country. Link each country to something interesting, like the pyramids in Egypt, Kilimanjaro in Tanzania or lemurs in Madagascar, because a fact helps your memory hold on to the name. A few minutes every day works much better than trying to learn everything the night before a test.

### subtopic_2 — The Map of Africa
**Assistant:** How do I answer a source-based question in History?

**Tutor:** First, look at the source carefully before you read the questions. If it is a picture, look at every part of it, including the background and any caption. If it is a piece of writing, read it twice. Ask yourself: what kind of source is this, who made it, when and why? Next, read each question carefully and underline the key words, such as name, describe or explain. Name means give just the name. Describe means say what something is like. Explain means say why or how. Then go back to the source and find the evidence for your answer. Many marks come from using clues from the source itself. Use your own knowledge from the lessons too, when the question asks for it. Finally, look at the marks: a two-mark question usually needs two clear points. Write in full sentences.

### subtopic_3 — Hunter-Gatherers and Herders
**Assistant:** How should I write a History paragraph?

**Tutor:** A good paragraph has three parts. Start with a topic sentence that answers the question directly, in one sentence. For example: The San lived off the environment in clever ways. Next, write three or four sentences that give evidence and details to support your topic sentence. For example: Women gathered roots, bulbs and berries with digging sticks. Men hunted antelope with bows and poisoned arrows. They stored water in ostrich eggshells and moved with the seasons. Use facts from the lessons, and link your sentences with words such as also, because and so. Finally, write a closing sentence that sums up your answer. For example: In this way, the San found everything they needed in nature. Keep to the topic, do not write lists with no sentences, and read your paragraph again at the end to check spelling and that it makes sense.
