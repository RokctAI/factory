### subtopic_1 — Geography and the Grade 5 Year
**Assistant:** Why are some countries stretched on a world map?

**Tutor:** The Earth is round, like a ball, but a map is flat, like a sheet of paper. Try to flatten the peel of an orange and you will see the problem: the peel tears or stretches. Mapmakers have the same problem when they draw the round Earth on a flat page. They choose a way of drawing, called a projection, and every projection stretches something. On a very common kind of world map, the places near the North Pole and the South Pole are stretched a lot. Greenland, a big island in the far north, looks almost as big as Africa on those maps, but Africa is really about fourteen times bigger. That is one reason why it is good to look at a globe too. On a globe, Africa looks as huge as it really is.

### subtopic_2 — Revising Compass Directions
**Assistant:** What does N, E, S, W stand for on a compass?

**Tutor:** The letters stand for the four main compass directions: N for north, E for east, S for south and W for west. They are called the cardinal points. On a compass rose, the drawing that shows directions, N is at the top, E is on the right, S at the bottom and W on the left. If you go round clockwise from north, you meet east, then south, then west, and then you are back at north. Some compass roses also show the directions in between, such as north-east, written NE, and south-west, written SW. You will learn all eight points this term. In Afrikaans, north is noord and south is suid, so the letters N and S work in Afrikaans too.

### subtopic_3 — Maps, Globes and Africa
**Assistant:** Why do we study Africa first and not the whole world?

**Tutor:** Geographers often start with the places closest to us and then move outwards. In Grade 4, you studied the places where people live and the map of South Africa. In Grade 5, the next step is our continent, Africa. South Africa is part of Africa, and we share rivers, mountains, deserts, weather, trade and history with our neighbours. For example, the Limpopo River forms our border with Zimbabwe and Botswana, and the water from the Lesotho Highlands helps supply Gauteng. People travel, work and trade between African countries every day. So knowing the map of Africa helps you to understand news reports, sport and travel, and your own place in the world. Later, in other grades, you will look at other continents in more detail.
