### subtopic_1 — Four Points Become Eight
**Assistant:** Are there more than eight compass points?

**Tutor:** Yes, there are. Between each of the eight points, you can add another in-between direction. For example, halfway between north and north-east is north-north-east, written NNE. Doing this all the way round gives sixteen points. Sailors long ago used thirty-two points, and they had to learn to say them all in order, which was called boxing the compass. Today, pilots, sailors and hikers often use numbers instead, called bearings. They measure directions in degrees, going clockwise from north: north is zero degrees, east is ninety, south is one hundred and eighty, and west is two hundred and seventy. A full circle is three hundred and sixty degrees. For Grade 5, the eight points are all you need, and they are already enough to describe directions quite exactly.

### subtopic_2 — Directions from a Fixed Point
**Assistant:** Is Cape Agulhas really where two oceans meet?

**Tutor:** Geographers have agreed to use Cape Agulhas as the official dividing point between the Atlantic Ocean and the Indian Ocean, and there is a marker there to show it. But in the sea itself there is no wall or line where one ocean stops and the other starts. The water mixes. What you can find around the south of Africa are two big currents. The warm Agulhas Current flows down the east coast from the Indian Ocean, and the cold Benguela Current flows up the west coast in the Atlantic. Where they come together, the water temperature can change quite quickly, and so can the fish and plants that live there. Many people think the oceans meet at Cape Point, near Cape Town, but the official meeting point is Cape Agulhas.

### subtopic_3 — Directions on a World Map
**Assistant:** Why does north or south always come first in a direction like north-east?

**Tutor:** It is a rule that mapmakers, sailors and pilots all agreed on long ago, so that everyone in the world says directions in the same way and nobody gets confused. North and south are given first because they are measured from the poles, which are the two fixed ends of the Earth's axis. East and west are then added to show which side you are leaning towards. Imagine if some people said north-east and others said east-north. A ship's captain or a pilot could easily make a dangerous mistake. Having one rule keeps everyone safe. So remember: north or south first, then east or west, joined with a short line called a hyphen. North-east, south-east, south-west, north-west.
