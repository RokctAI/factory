### subtopic_1 — The Poles and the Equator
**Assistant:** Why is it hot at the equator and cold at the poles?

**Tutor:** It is all about how the sun's rays reach the Earth. At the equator, the sun shines from high in the sky, almost straight down, for most of the year. The sunlight is concentrated on a small area, like a torch shining straight down onto a table, making a small, bright circle. That heats the ground strongly. Near the poles, the sun is always low in the sky, so its rays arrive at a slant. The same amount of sunlight is spread over a much bigger area, like a torch shining at an angle, making a long, weak patch of light. So each part of the ground gets less heat. The ice and snow at the poles also reflect a lot of sunlight back into the sky. That is why the poles stay cold even in their summer.

### subtopic_2 — The Seven Continents
**Assistant:** Is Antarctica really a continent if nobody lives there?

**Tutor:** Yes. A continent is a very large area of land, and Antarctica is a huge piece of land, bigger than Europe or Australia. Most of it is covered by a thick sheet of ice, in some places more than three kilometres thick, but there is solid rock underneath. No people live there permanently because it is the coldest, windiest and driest continent. But thousands of scientists visit, especially in summer, to study the ice, the weather and animals such as penguins and seals. South Africa has its own research base there, called SANAE IV, and the research ship SA Agulhas II sails from Cape Town to take scientists and supplies. Countries have agreed to use Antarctica only for peace and science.

### subtopic_3 — The Oceans and the Hemispheres
**Assistant:** Is the Southern Ocean a real ocean?

**Tutor:** Yes, today most geographers count the Southern Ocean as the fifth ocean. It is the cold water that flows all the way around Antarctica. For a long time, many maps did not name it, and simply showed the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans reaching down to Antarctica. But scientists noticed that the water around Antarctica is different. It is much colder, and a strong current flows right around the continent from west to east, separating it from the warmer oceans further north. Because of this, many organisations now recognise the Southern Ocean as its own ocean. Some older maps and books still show only four oceans, so do not be surprised if you see that. In this lesson we use five.
