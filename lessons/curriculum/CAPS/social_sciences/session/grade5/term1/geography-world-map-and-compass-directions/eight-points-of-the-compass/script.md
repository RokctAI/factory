# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the eight points of the compass, how to give the direction of one place from a fixed point, and how to use the eight directions on a world map. The anchor is a school trip to Cape Agulhas, in the Western Cape, the southernmost tip of the whole continent of Africa. Near the old red and white lighthouse, a stone marker shows the place where, according to geographers, the Atlantic Ocean meets the Indian Ocean. The learners stand beside the marker, looking out at the waves, and the guide points out a big map of Africa on a sign, with a compass rose drawn on it. The big idea is that adding four in-between directions to north, east, south and west lets us describe directions much more exactly, on the ground and on a world map. The eight compass directions and describing directions from a fixed point are examinable.

## Subtopic: Four Points Become Eight

You already know the four main compass directions: north, east, south and west. These are called the cardinal points.

Between each pair of cardinal points is an in-between direction. Halfway between north and east is north-east, written NE. Halfway between south and east is south-east, written SE. Halfway between south and west is south-west, written SW. Halfway between north and west is north-west, written NW. These four are sometimes called the intermediate points.

So the eight points of the compass, going clockwise from north, are: north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west and north-west.

When you name an in-between direction, north or south always comes first. We say north-east, never east-north. We say south-west, never west-south.

Opposite directions are always straight across the compass rose from each other. North is opposite south, and east is opposite west. North-east is opposite south-west, and north-west is opposite south-east.

On a compass rose drawing, the four cardinal points are usually drawn longer, and the four in-between points are drawn shorter. At Cape Agulhas, the compass rose on the sign has long points for N, E, S and W, and short points for NE, SE, SW and NW.

A quick way to work out an in-between direction is to look at the two cardinal points on either side of it and join their names. The direction between south and west joins south and west, so it is south-west. The direction between north and west joins north and west, so it is north-west.

You can practise with your own body. Stand and face north. Stretch your right arm out to the side: it points east. Now move your right arm halfway back towards the front: it points north-east. Swing it round behind you, halfway between east and south: it points south-east. Do the same with your left arm for north-west and south-west.

## Subtopic: Directions from a Fixed Point

When we describe a direction, we always describe it from a fixed point. A fixed point is the place we start from. It does not move.

At Cape Agulhas, the stone marker is the fixed point. From the marker, the sea is to the south. The lighthouse is to the north-west. The car park is to the north. The town of Struisbaai is to the north-east, along the coast. All of these directions are measured from the marker.

If we change the fixed point, the directions change too. From the lighthouse, the marker is to the south-east. Notice that this is the opposite of north-west. If the lighthouse is north-west of the marker, then the marker must be south-east of the lighthouse. This is a good way to check your answer.

To give a direction correctly, use this pattern: the place you are describing, the direction, the word of, and then the fixed point. For example: The lighthouse is north-west of the marker. Struisbaai is north-east of the marker.

Be careful with the word of. It always comes before the fixed point, the place we start from.

Here is a step-by-step way to work out a direction on a map. First, find the fixed point and put your finger on it. Second, imagine a small compass rose drawn on top of the fixed point, with north pointing to the top of the map. Third, find the place you are describing and see which point of the imagined compass rose points towards it. That point is your answer.

Eight directions are much more exact than four. With only four directions, we would have to say the lighthouse is north or west of the marker, which is not quite true either way. With eight directions, we can say it is north-west, which is exactly right.

## Subtopic: Directions on a World Map

On a world map with north at the top, we can use the eight compass directions to describe where continents, countries and oceans are.

Use Africa as the fixed point. Europe is north of Africa. Asia is north-east of Africa. Australia is south-east of Africa, across the Indian Ocean. Antarctica is south of Africa. South America is south-west of Africa, across the Atlantic Ocean, and North America is north-west of Africa.

Now use South Africa as the fixed point. Namibia is north-west of South Africa. Botswana is north of South Africa. Zimbabwe is north-east. Mozambique is north-east too, along the east coast. The Atlantic Ocean is west of South Africa, and the Indian Ocean is east and south-east.

Directions on a world map help people in real life. A ship's captain sailing from Cape Town to Australia steers south-east across the Indian Ocean. A pilot flying from Johannesburg to London flies mostly north, and a little to the west, over the whole length of Africa and then over Europe.

Use Cape Agulhas as the fixed point. Cape Town is north-west of Cape Agulhas. The Atlantic Ocean is to the west, and the Indian Ocean is to the east. From here, Antarctica lies far to the south, across the cold Southern Ocean.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: saying east-north instead of north-east; north or south always comes first. Two: forgetting the fixed point; always say which place you are measuring from. Three: thinking north-east is opposite north-west; north-east is opposite south-west. Four: thinking directions stay the same when the fixed point changes; if the lighthouse is north-west of the marker, the marker is south-east of the lighthouse.
