# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the position of the equator and the North and South Poles on a globe, the two hemispheres, and the seven continents and five oceans on a world map. The anchor is a Grade 5 classroom in Polokwane, in Limpopo, where the teacher has placed a large globe on a stand in the middle of the room, and a world map on the wall. One learner spins the globe slowly, and the class watches the blue oceans and the brown and green land go round. A strip of red tape has been stuck around the middle of the globe. The big idea is that the equator and the poles help us to find our way around the whole Earth, and that all the land on Earth is grouped into seven continents surrounded by oceans. The positions of the equator, the poles and the continents are examinable.

## Subtopic: The Poles and the Equator

The Earth is shaped like a ball, and it spins. It turns all the way round once every day, and that is why we have day and night. The Earth spins around an imaginary line called its axis, which runs through the middle of the Earth from top to bottom.

The two ends of the axis are called the poles. The North Pole is the most northern point on Earth, at the very top of the globe. The South Pole is the most southern point on Earth, at the very bottom of the globe. Both poles are very cold, and they are covered in ice. The North Pole is in the middle of the Arctic Ocean, which is frozen for much of the year. The South Pole is on the frozen continent of Antarctica.

The equator is an imaginary line that runs all the way around the middle of the Earth, exactly halfway between the North Pole and the South Pole. You cannot see it on the ground, but mapmakers draw it on globes and maps. The red tape around the middle of the globe in Polokwane shows the equator. Places on or near the equator are hot all year round, because the sun shines high overhead.

The equator divides the Earth into two halves. Each half is called a hemisphere, which means half a sphere, or half a ball. The half north of the equator is the Northern Hemisphere. The half south of the equator is the Southern Hemisphere. South Africa is in the Southern Hemisphere. Countries such as Egypt and the United Kingdom are in the Northern Hemisphere.

When it is summer in the Southern Hemisphere, it is winter in the Northern Hemisphere. That is why Christmas in South Africa is in summer, while in Europe it is in winter.

Mapmakers also draw other imaginary lines on globes. Lines that run around the globe, parallel to the equator, are called lines of latitude. Lines that run from the North Pole to the South Pole are called lines of longitude. Together they make a grid that helps people find any place on Earth, a bit like the grid references you used on maps in Grade 4. In this lesson, the equator is the most important line to know, because it is the starting line for all the lines of latitude.

The questions for this section are with you now: the poles, the equator and the two hemispheres.

## Subtopic: The Seven Continents

A continent is a very large area of land. There are seven continents on Earth.

Asia is the biggest continent, and it has the most people. It includes China and India. Africa is the second biggest continent, and it is our home. North America includes the United States, Canada and Mexico. South America includes Brazil and Argentina. Antarctica is the frozen continent at the South Pole, where no people live permanently, only scientists who visit to do research. Europe includes countries such as France, Germany and the United Kingdom. Australia is the smallest continent. It is sometimes grouped with nearby islands such as New Zealand and called Oceania.

A way to remember all seven is four A's, two Americas and one E. The four A's are Africa, Asia, Antarctica and Australia. The two Americas are North America and South America. The one E is Europe. Four, plus two, plus one, makes seven.

On the world map in Polokwane, the learners find Africa in the middle of the map. Europe is north of Africa, across the Mediterranean Sea. Asia is to the north-east. South America is to the west, across the Atlantic Ocean. Australia is to the east, across the Indian Ocean. Antarctica is far to the south.

The questions for this section are with you now: the seven continents, from biggest to smallest, and where they are.

## Subtopic: The Oceans and the Hemispheres

An ocean is a huge body of salt water. Oceans cover about seventy percent of the Earth, more than two-thirds of its surface. This is why the Earth looks blue from space, and why the globe in Polokwane looks mostly blue when it spins.

There are five oceans. The Pacific Ocean is the biggest and deepest. It lies between Asia and Australia on one side, and North and South America on the other. The Atlantic Ocean is the second biggest. It lies between the Americas on one side and Europe and Africa on the other. The Indian Ocean lies between Africa, Asia and Australia. The Southern Ocean surrounds Antarctica. The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and lies around the North Pole.

South Africa has two oceans on its coast: the Atlantic Ocean on the west and the Indian Ocean on the east.

The equator passes through Africa, South America and parts of Asia. It does not pass through Europe, North America, Australia or Antarctica. Africa is special because the equator runs almost through its middle, so Africa has land in both the Northern and the Southern Hemispheres.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the equator is a real line you can see on the ground; it is an imaginary line. Two: thinking the North Pole is on a continent; it is in the frozen Arctic Ocean, while the South Pole is on Antarctica. Three: thinking Africa is the biggest continent; Asia is bigger. Four: thinking South Africa is in the Northern Hemisphere; it is south of the equator, in the Southern Hemisphere.

The questions for this section are with you now: the five oceans and which continents the equator crosses.
