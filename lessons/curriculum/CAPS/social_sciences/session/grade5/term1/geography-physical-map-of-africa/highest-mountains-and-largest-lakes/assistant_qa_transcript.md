### subtopic_1 — Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya
**Assistant:** Why do climbers get sick high up on Kilimanjaro?

**Tutor:** High up on a mountain, the air is thinner. There is still air, but each breath has less oxygen in it than at sea level. At the top of Kilimanjaro, each breath has only about half as much oxygen as a breath at the beach. Our bodies need oxygen to work, so if a person climbs too high too quickly, they can get altitude sickness. They may get a headache, feel dizzy and tired, lose their appetite or struggle to sleep. In serious cases, it can be dangerous, and the person must go down quickly. That is why guides on Kilimanjaro say pole pole, which is Swahili for slowly, slowly. Climbers walk slowly, drink lots of water, and spend extra days on the way up, so their bodies can get used to the thin air. Going down to a lower height usually makes people feel better quickly.

### subtopic_2 — Africa's Largest Lakes
**Assistant:** Is the snow on Kilimanjaro disappearing?

**Tutor:** Yes, scientists have measured that the ice on top of Kilimanjaro has shrunk a great deal over the last hundred years or so. Photographs from long ago show much bigger areas of ice than there are today. Most of the ice that was there a century ago has already gone. Scientists think there are several reasons. The air around the mountain has become a little warmer, and it may also be drier, with fewer clouds and less snow falling to replace the ice. The bright sun near the equator also makes the ice slowly turn straight into water vapour. Some scientists think the ice could disappear completely within the next few decades. The glaciers on Mount Kenya are shrinking too. This is one of the ways people can see that the world's climate is changing.

### subtopic_3 — Finding Them on a Map
**Assistant:** How can a lake have more kinds of fish than any other lake?

**Tutor:** Lake Malawi is very old, very big and very deep, and it has many different kinds of places for fish to live: rocky shores, sandy beaches, plants and deep, open water. Over a very long time, the cichlid fish in the lake changed to suit all these different places. Some learnt to scrape tiny plants off rocks, some to eat snails, some to catch other fish, and some even to steal scales from other fish. Each kind became a little different, until they became separate species, which means separate kinds of fish that do not breed with each other. Scientists think there are many hundreds of kinds of cichlids in Lake Malawi, and most of them live nowhere else on Earth. That is why part of the lake is protected as a national park and a World Heritage Site.
