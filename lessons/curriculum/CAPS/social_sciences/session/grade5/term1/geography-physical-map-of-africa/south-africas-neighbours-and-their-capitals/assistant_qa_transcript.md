### subtopic_1 — Six Neighbours
**Assistant:** Why is Lesotho called the Kingdom in the Sky?

**Tutor:** Lesotho is called the Kingdom in the Sky for two reasons. First, it is a kingdom, ruled by a king, although the government is chosen by the people in elections. Second, it is very high. The whole country lies more than one thousand four hundred metres above sea level, and most of it is much higher, in the Maloti and Drakensberg mountains. The highest point in southern Africa, Thabana Ntlenyana, is in Lesotho, at about three thousand four hundred and eighty metres. In winter, snow often falls on the mountains, and there is even a ski resort. People in the mountains often travel on horseback, wrapped in colourful woollen blankets, which are part of Basotho tradition. Lesotho's high mountains also catch a lot of rain and snow, which is why it has so much water to share with South Africa.

### subtopic_2 — Capitals of the Neighbours
**Assistant:** Why does Eswatini have two capital cities?

**Tutor:** Eswatini is a kingdom, ruled by a king. Mbabane, in the hills in the north-west, is the administrative capital, where most of the government offices are found. Lobamba, a short drive away in a valley called the Ezulwini Valley, is the royal and legislative capital. The Parliament building is there, and the royal homes of the king and the queen mother are nearby. Important national ceremonies, such as the reed dance, take place at Lobamba. So the two cities have different jobs, a little like South Africa's three capitals. Eswatini used to be called Swaziland. In 2018, the king announced that the country would be called by its siSwati name, Eswatini, which means land of the Swati people. Many old maps and books still use the name Swaziland.

### subtopic_3 — Links Between Neighbours
**Assistant:** What is the Okavango Delta?

**Tutor:** Most rivers end in the sea, but the Okavango River does something unusual. It starts in the highlands of Angola and flows south-east into Botswana. There, instead of reaching the sea, it spreads out over the flat land of the Kalahari, into a huge, fan-shaped area of channels, lagoons, islands and swamps. This is the Okavango Delta. Most of the water slowly sinks into the sand or dries up in the hot sun. Each year, rain that fell in Angola months before arrives in Botswana during the dry season, so the delta floods when the land around it is driest. This attracts huge numbers of animals, such as elephants, lions, hippos and many birds. People travel through the channels in narrow wooden boats called mokoro. The Okavango Delta is a World Heritage Site, protected because it is so special.
