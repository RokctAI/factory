# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers Africa's highest mountains, Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya, and its largest lakes, Lake Victoria, Lake Tanganyika and Lake Malawi. The anchor is a Grade 5 classroom in Rustenburg, in North West province, below the rocky ridges of the Magaliesberg. The Geography teacher has just come back from a holiday in East Africa, and she shows the class her photographs on the screen. The first shows a huge, lonely mountain with a flat, snowy top, rising above green farmland and clouds. Another shows her at the top, wrapped in a thick jacket and gloves, standing next to a wooden sign. The last shows a long, clear lake with sandy beaches and colourful fish. The big idea is that East Africa has Africa's highest mountains and largest lakes, and that we can find them on a physical map by their colours and symbols. The names, countries and positions of these mountains and lakes are examinable.

## Subtopic: Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya

Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa. It is in Tanzania, in East Africa, close to the border with Kenya. Its highest point, called Uhuru Peak, is five thousand eight hundred and ninety-five metres above sea level. Uhuru is a Swahili word that means freedom. The peak was given this name when the country became independent from British rule.

Kilimanjaro is a volcano. It formed long ago when hot, melted rock pushed up from deep inside the Earth and piled up, layer upon layer. It has not erupted for a very long time, so it is called a dormant volcano, which means it is sleeping. Kilimanjaro is not part of a mountain range. It stands on its own, rising high above the flat plains around it. It is one of the highest free-standing mountains in the world.

Even though Kilimanjaro is only a few hundred kilometres south of the equator, there is snow and ice on its top, because it is so high. The higher you go, the colder it gets. Climbers pass through farmland, then rainforest, then heath and moorland, then a cold, rocky desert, and finally reach the snow. Most climbers take about five to eight days to go up and down, walking slowly to let their bodies get used to the thin air. The teacher in Rustenburg tells the class that the last part of the climb started at midnight, in the freezing cold.

Mount Kenya is the second highest mountain in Africa. It is in Kenya, just south of the equator, about one hundred and fifty kilometres north of Nairobi. Its highest peak is five thousand one hundred and ninety-nine metres above sea level. Mount Kenya is also an old volcano, and it has small glaciers, which are slow-moving rivers of ice, near its jagged peaks. The country of Kenya is named after this mountain.

The questions for this section are with you now: Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya.

## Subtopic: Africa's Largest Lakes

A lake is a large body of water surrounded by land. Most lakes are filled with fresh water from rivers and rain. Africa's three largest lakes are all in East Africa, and together with some others they are called the Great Lakes of Africa.

Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa. It is so big that, standing on its shore, you cannot see the other side, and it looks like a sea. It is shared by three countries: Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. The equator crosses its northern part. Lake Victoria is one of the sources of the River Nile, which flows out of the lake in Uganda. Millions of people live around the lake, and many of them catch fish, such as tilapia and Nile perch, to eat and to sell.

Lake Tanganyika is the second largest lake in Africa. It is very long and narrow, and very deep. It is the longest freshwater lake in the world, about six hundred and seventy kilometres long, and the second deepest lake in the world. It is shared by four countries: Tanzania, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi and Zambia.

Lake Malawi is the third largest lake in Africa. It is also long and narrow, and lies between Malawi, Mozambique and Tanzania. In Tanzania it is called Lake Nyasa. Lake Malawi is famous for its many kinds of small, brightly coloured fish, called cichlids. Scientists believe it has more different kinds of fish than any other lake in the world. These are the fish in the teacher's last photograph.

The questions for this section are with you now: Lake Victoria, Lake Tanganyika and Lake Malawi.

## Subtopic: Finding Them on a Map

On a physical map of Africa, high mountains are coloured dark brown, red or purple, and the very highest points may be white. A mountain peak is often shown with a small triangle and its height in metres, such as 5895 for Kilimanjaro. On the map, the learners in Rustenburg find a small dark patch with a triangle in the north-east of Tanzania. That is Kilimanjaro. Not far to the north, in the middle of Kenya, is another triangle: Mount Kenya.

Lakes are shown in blue. Lake Victoria is easy to find: it is the biggest blue shape in Africa, roughly round, where Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania meet. Lake Tanganyika is a long, thin blue shape running north to south, west of Lake Victoria. Lake Malawi is another long, thin blue shape, further south and east, running north to south as well.

Lake Tanganyika and Lake Malawi are long and thin because they lie in the Great Rift Valley, a long, deep valley where the Earth's crust is slowly pulling apart. Rain and rivers have filled these deep cracks with water.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Kilimanjaro is in Kenya; it is in Tanzania, near the Kenyan border. Two: thinking there is no snow in Africa; there is snow on Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya, because they are so high. Three: thinking Lake Victoria belongs to one country; it is shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. Four: thinking Lake Tanganyika is the largest lake in Africa; it is the longest, but Lake Victoria is the largest.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding mountains and lakes on a physical map.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the teacher's photographs in Rustenburg — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Two Giants

Kilimanjaro, in Tanzania, is the highest.

Mount Kenya, in Kenya, is second.

Keep the snowy top of Kilimanjaro in mind and try the questions for this part: the highest mountains.

## Subtopic: Three Great Lakes

Victoria is the largest. Tanganyika is the longest. Malawi has the most fish.

Hold the colourful fish in the clear lake in mind and try this part's questions: the largest lakes.

## Subtopic: On the Map

Triangles for peaks. Blue shapes for lakes.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Kilimanjaro, in Tanzania, is Africa's highest mountain at five thousand eight hundred and ninety-five metres. Mount Kenya is the second highest. Both are old volcanoes with snow on top. Lake Victoria, shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania, is Africa's largest lake. Lake Tanganyika is the longest freshwater lake in the world, and Lake Malawi has many colourful fish. On a physical map, peaks have triangles and lakes are blue.

Hold the map of East Africa with its brown peaks and blue lakes in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: finding them on a map.
