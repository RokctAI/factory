# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers South Africa's neighbouring countries and their capital cities. The anchor is a Grade 5 classroom in Kimberley, in the Northern Cape, a city famous for its Big Hole, where diamonds were once dug out by hand. The class has spread a big map of southern Africa across four desks pushed together. The teacher gives each group a toy car and a challenge: plan a road trip that visits every country that shares a border with South Africa, and stops at each capital city. The groups move their cars across the map, arguing about the best route. The big idea is that South Africa shares borders with six countries, and that each one has its own capital city, people and links with South Africa. The names, positions and capital cities of South Africa's neighbours are examinable.

## Subtopic: Six Neighbours

A neighbouring country is a country that shares a border with your country. South Africa has six neighbours: Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Eswatini and Lesotho.

Using the compass, we can say where each neighbour is, with South Africa as the fixed point. Namibia is to the north-west. Botswana is to the north. Zimbabwe is to the north-east. Mozambique is to the north-east and east, along the coast of the Indian Ocean. Eswatini is a small country to the east, tucked in between South Africa and Mozambique. Lesotho is completely surrounded by South Africa, so it lies inside the country, near its east.

Some borders follow rivers. The Gariep, or Orange, River forms part of the border with Namibia. The Limpopo River forms the border with Zimbabwe and part of the border with Botswana. Part of the border with Lesotho follows the Mohokare, or Caledon, River, and part follows the high peaks of the Drakensberg mountains.

Four of the neighbours are landlocked: Botswana, Zimbabwe, Eswatini and Lesotho. Two are coastal: Namibia, on the Atlantic Ocean, and Mozambique, on the Indian Ocean.

The groups in Kimberley start their road trip by heading north-west to Namibia, because that is the neighbour closest to the Northern Cape.

To cross into a neighbouring country, travellers must go through a border post and show their passports. Some of the busiest are Beitbridge, on the Limpopo River into Zimbabwe, Lebombo, on the road to Maputo in Mozambique, and Maseru Bridge, into Lesotho. Some border posts in the mountains of Lesotho are open only during the day, and the roads to them are steep and rough.

Notice that the neighbours are very different in size. Namibia is a big country, with lots of land but not many people. Eswatini and Lesotho are small. On the map in Kimberley, Eswatini is so small that the groups can cover it with a thumb.

The questions for this section are with you now: the six neighbours and their directions from South Africa.

## Subtopic: Capitals of the Neighbours

Each neighbour has its own capital city, where its government works.

Namibia's capital is Windhoek. It lies in the middle of the country, on high ground, surrounded by hills. Most of Namibia is dry, with the Namib Desert along the coast and the Kalahari in the east.

Botswana's capital is Gaborone. It lies in the south-east of Botswana, close to the border with South Africa. Botswana is known for its diamonds and its wildlife, including the Okavango Delta, a huge inland delta where a river spreads out into the Kalahari.

Zimbabwe's capital is Harare. It lies in the north-east of Zimbabwe, on high ground. Zimbabwe is home to the stone ruins of Great Zimbabwe, which gave the country its name.

Mozambique's capital is Maputo. It lies in the far south of Mozambique, on a bay of the Indian Ocean, very close to the border with South Africa. Maputo is a busy port, and many goods from South Africa, Zimbabwe and Eswatini pass through it.

Eswatini's capital is Mbabane. It lies in the hills in the north-west of Eswatini. Eswatini is ruled by a king, and Lobamba, nearby, is the royal and parliamentary capital.

Lesotho's capital is Maseru. It lies on the western border of Lesotho, on the Mohokare River, right next to South Africa.

On the map in Kimberley, the groups mark each capital with a star and draw the road between them. The route from Gaborone to Harare, for example, heads north-east.

The questions for this section are with you now: the six capital cities and where they are.

## Subtopic: Links Between Neighbours

South Africa and its neighbours are linked in many ways.

Many people speak the same or related languages on both sides of a border. Setswana is spoken in Botswana and in North West province. Sesotho is spoken in Lesotho and in the Free State. SiSwati is spoken in Eswatini and in Mpumalanga. Families often have relatives in more than one country. Mozambique's official language is Portuguese, because it was once a Portuguese colony.

Neighbours trade with each other. Trucks carry food, fuel, machines and goods across the borders every day. Water from the Lesotho Highlands is sent through tunnels into South Africa, to help supply Gauteng, and Lesotho is paid for it. Electricity is shared too.

The money of Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini is linked to the rand, and in these countries the rand can usually be used as well.

The neighbours also share history. During apartheid, many South Africans who opposed the government had to leave the country and lived in neighbouring countries, and those countries sometimes suffered because they gave them shelter. After South Africa became a democracy in 1994, the countries of the region worked more closely together.

All six neighbours, together with South Africa and other countries in the region, are members of the Southern African Development Community, called SADC. Its headquarters is in Gaborone. SADC helps the countries to work together on trade, transport, health, water and peace.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Lesotho is a province of South Africa; it is a separate country with its own king and government. Two: thinking Mozambique is landlocked; it has a long coastline on the Indian Ocean. Three: mixing up capitals; Gaborone is in Botswana and Harare is in Zimbabwe. Four: thinking Zambia is a neighbour of South Africa; it shares no border with South Africa.

The questions for this section are with you now: languages, trade and SADC.
