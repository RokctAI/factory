### subtopic_1 — Africa's Great Rivers
**Assistant:** Is the Nile or the Amazon the longest river in the world?

**Tutor:** Scientists are still discussing this. For a long time, most books said the Nile was the longest river in the world, at about six thousand six hundred and fifty kilometres. The Amazon, in South America, was said to be a little shorter, but it carries far more water than any other river. Then some scientists measured the Amazon again, starting from a different source high in the mountains of Peru, and they found that it might be slightly longer than the Nile. The difficulty is that it is hard to decide exactly where a river begins, because many small streams join together, and it is hard to decide exactly where it ends, in a wide delta or mouth. So it is safest to say that the Nile and the Amazon are the two longest rivers in the world, and that the Nile is the longest river in Africa.

### subtopic_2 — Famous Waterfalls
**Assistant:** Why is it called Victoria Falls?

**Tutor:** The local name, used for hundreds of years by the people living nearby, is Mosi-oa-Tunya, which means the smoke that thunders in the Lozi language. In 1855, a Scottish missionary and explorer called David Livingstone was taken to the falls by local guides. He was the first European known to have seen them, and he named them after Queen Victoria, who was the queen of Britain at the time. Both names are used today. Zambia and Zimbabwe share the falls, and the town on the Zimbabwe side is called Victoria Falls, while the national park on the Zambian side is called Mosi-oa-Tunya. Many people today like to use the older African name as well, as a way of showing respect for the people who knew and named the falls long before any European arrived.

### subtopic_3 — Sahara and Namib Deserts
**Assistant:** Can anything live in the Sahara Desert?

**Tutor:** Yes, more than you might think. Plants and animals in the Sahara have special ways to survive the heat and the lack of water. Camels can go for days without drinking, and store fat in their humps for energy. The fennec fox is a tiny fox with huge ears, which help it to lose heat and hear its prey under the sand. Many animals sleep in burrows during the day and come out at night, when it is cooler. Some plants have very long roots to reach water deep underground, and some seeds wait in the sand for years until rain falls, then grow, flower and make new seeds very quickly. People live in the Sahara too. Some live in towns at oases, and some groups have travelled across the desert with camels and goats for hundreds of years.
