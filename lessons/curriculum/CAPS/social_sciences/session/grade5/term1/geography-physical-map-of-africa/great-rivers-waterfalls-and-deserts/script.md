# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers Africa's great rivers, the Nile, Niger, Congo, Zambezi, Limpopo and Gariep-Orange, the famous waterfalls Victoria Falls and Maletsunyane Falls, and the Sahara and Namib deserts. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Springbok, in Namaqualand, in the dry north-west of the Northern Cape. The learners have built a large model of Africa in the school sandpit, with heaps of sand for mountains. Now each group lays blue string to show a river, from where it begins to where it reaches the sea. Smooth patches of dry sand show the deserts, and a little crumpled foil shows a waterfall. Outside the school fence, the hills are dry and rocky, but the teacher reminds the class that after good winter rains, the whole area bursts into flowers. The big idea is that Africa's rivers, waterfalls and deserts are some of the most famous natural features in the world, and that we can find them all on a physical map. Their names and positions are examinable.

## Subtopic: Africa's Great Rivers

A river is a large stream of fresh water that flows across the land, from high ground towards the sea or a lake. The place where a river begins is its source. The place where it ends, usually at the sea, is its mouth.

The Nile is the longest river in Africa, and one of the two longest rivers in the world. It is about six thousand six hundred and fifty kilometres long. Its main sources are Lake Victoria and Lake Tana, in Ethiopia. It flows north through Uganda, South Sudan, Sudan and Egypt, and ends in a wide delta on the Mediterranean Sea. The Nile is unusual, because it flows north for almost all of its long journey.

The Niger is the great river of West Africa. It begins in the hills of Guinea, not far from the Atlantic Ocean, but instead of flowing straight to the sea, it flows inland, north-east towards the Sahara, then curves south-east through Niger and Nigeria. It reaches the Atlantic in a big delta in the Gulf of Guinea.

The Congo is the second longest river in Africa, and it carries more water than any other river in Africa. It flows through the rainforests of central Africa, crossing the equator twice, and reaches the Atlantic Ocean. In places, it is the deepest river in the world.

The Zambezi rises in Zambia and flows south and then east, along the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe, and through Mozambique to the Indian Ocean. The Limpopo begins in South Africa and flows in a big curve along our borders with Botswana and Zimbabwe, then through Mozambique to the Indian Ocean.

The Gariep, or Orange, River is the longest river in South Africa. Its source is high in the mountains of Lesotho, where it is called the Senqu. It flows west across South Africa, forms part of the border with Namibia, and reaches the Atlantic Ocean at Alexander Bay, in the far north-west of the Northern Cape.

The questions for this section are with you now: the six great rivers, their sources and their mouths.

## Subtopic: Famous Waterfalls

A waterfall forms where a river flows over a steep drop, such as a cliff, and the water falls down to a lower level.

Victoria Falls is one of the largest waterfalls in the world. It is on the Zambezi River, on the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. Here, the whole wide river, more than one and a half kilometres across, suddenly drops into a deep, narrow gorge, about one hundred metres deep. The roar of the water can be heard far away, and a huge cloud of spray rises high into the air. That is why the local name of the falls is Mosi-oa-Tunya, which means the smoke that thunders. Victoria Falls is a World Heritage Site, and thousands of tourists visit it every year.

Maletsunyane Falls is in Lesotho, near the small town of Semonkong, which means the place of smoke. Here, the Maletsunyane River drops about one hundred and ninety metres in a single, straight fall into a deep gorge. It is one of the highest single-drop waterfalls in southern Africa. In very cold winters, the falls can partly freeze.

The questions for this section are with you now: Victoria Falls and Maletsunyane Falls.

## Subtopic: Sahara and Namib Deserts

A desert is a place that gets very little rain, so very few plants can grow there. Some deserts are hot and sandy, but many are rocky and stony. Deserts can be very hot by day and cold at night.

The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world. It covers most of North Africa, stretching across about eleven countries, from the Atlantic Ocean in the west to the Red Sea in the east. It is almost as big as the whole of the United States. Parts of the Sahara are covered with huge sand dunes, but much of it is rocky plateau and gravel plain. In a few places, called oases, there is water under the ground, and people grow date palms and live in small towns. The Nile flows through the eastern Sahara, bringing a strip of green across the desert.

The Namib Desert lies along the Atlantic coast of Namibia, in south-western Africa. It is one of the oldest deserts in the world. Some of its red sand dunes are among the highest in the world. Very little rain falls in the Namib, but cold sea air often brings fog from the ocean. Some beetles stand on dune tops and let fog collect on their backs, then drink the drops. The Namib stretches as far south as the coastal strip of the Northern Cape, west of Springbok.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the Nile flows south; it flows north into the Mediterranean Sea. Two: thinking the Gariep River begins in South Africa; its source is in the mountains of Lesotho. Three: thinking Victoria Falls is in South Africa; it is on the Zambezi, between Zambia and Zimbabwe. Four: thinking all deserts are hot sand dunes; many are rocky, and nights can be cold.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Sahara and the Namib.
