This session opens Grade 5 History. It explains what History is, what is studied this year and how work is assessed, and introduces the first topic: hunter-gatherers and herders in southern Africa. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Graaff-Reinet, an old town in the Eastern Cape Karoo, surrounded by flat-topped hills. The teacher has brought a long rope into the school hall and stretched it from one wall to the other. At one end is a card that says Today. The big idea is that History is the study of the past using evidence, and that the history of southern Africa goes back a very long time, to the hunter-gatherers and herders who lived here long before towns, roads or writing. ## Subtopic: History and the Grade 5 Year

History is the study of the past. Historians ask questions about what happened, when it happened, why it happened, and how it changed people's lives. They are a bit like detectives. They cannot travel back in time, so they look for clues, called sources or evidence, such as objects, pictures, stories and old writing, and use them to work out what happened.

In Grade 5 History there are four topics, one for each term. In Term 1, we learn about hunter-gatherers and herders in southern Africa. In Term 2, we learn about the first farmers in southern Africa. In Term 3, we travel north to learn about an ancient African society, Egypt. In Term 4, we follow a heritage trail through the provinces of South Africa, finding out about special places, objects and people.

Your History work is assessed in two ways. Informal assessment happens all the time, through class work, homework, discussions and short activities. It helps you and your teacher see what you understand. Formal assessment counts for your report. In Grade 5 History, the formal tasks include tests in which you answer questions about sources, such as a picture, a map or a short piece of writing, and write a short paragraph of your own. Learning to read a source carefully and to explain your ideas in a paragraph are two of the most important History skills.

On the timeline rope in Graaff-Reinet, the learners notice that their own lives take up only a tiny piece at the Today end. History stretches much, much further back.

The questions for this section are with you now: what History is, the four Grade 5 topics, and assessment.

## Subtopic: Hunter-Gatherers and Herders

This term's topic is about two groups of people who lived in southern Africa long ago: the San hunter-gatherers and the Khoikhoi herders.

Hunter-gatherers are people who get all their food from the wild. They hunt wild animals for meat, and gather wild plants, such as roots, bulbs, berries, nuts and seeds. They do not plant crops or keep farm animals. Because the plants and animals change with the seasons, hunter-gatherers move from place to place during the year.

In southern Africa, the hunter-gatherers were the San. The San and their ancestors have lived in southern Africa for a very, very long time, many tens of thousands of years. They are among the first people of this land.

Herders are people who keep herds of animals, such as sheep, goats and cattle, and live mainly from their milk and meat. They move with their animals to find fresh grass and water.

In southern Africa, the herders were the Khoikhoi. About two thousand years ago, sheep and, later, cattle were brought into southern Africa, and some people began to herd them. These herders became the Khoikhoi, which in their own language means something like people of people, or real people.

Together, the San and the Khoikhoi are often called the Khoisan. Their languages are famous for click sounds, made with the tongue. Some of these clicks are now part of other South African languages, such as isiXhosa and isiZulu.

The questions for this section are with you now: hunter-gatherers, herders, the San and the Khoikhoi.

## Subtopic: A Very Long Time Ago

On the rope in Graaff-Reinet, the card with the hunter is far, far from Today. To show the whole history of the San, the rope would have to be much longer than the hall.

Historians and archaeologists talk about the Stone Age. This was the very long period when people made their tools mainly from stone, as well as wood, bone and shell, before they learnt to make tools from metals such as iron. The last part of the Stone Age is called the Later Stone Age. In southern Africa, it began tens of thousands of years ago and lasted until quite recent times, and the San of the Later Stone Age are the people we study this term.

The Khoikhoi herders arrived much later, about two thousand years ago. For a long time, San hunter-gatherers and Khoikhoi herders lived in the same landscape. Then, some time later, the first farmers, who grew crops and made iron tools, settled in the eastern parts of southern Africa.

The San and Khoikhoi are not only people of the past. Their descendants live in South Africa, Namibia and Botswana today. Some still speak Khoisan languages, such as the Nama language, and many people of all backgrounds in South Africa have Khoisan ancestors.

In this topic, we ask: how do we know about these people? How did they live, hunt, share and believe? What did their rock art mean? And how did hunters and herders share the land?

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking History is just remembering dates; it is about asking questions and using evidence. Two: thinking hunter-gatherers were lazy because they did not farm; they had great skill and knowledge of the land. Three: thinking the Khoikhoi were the first people in southern Africa; the San were here much earlier. Four: thinking the San and Khoikhoi have disappeared; their descendants live in southern Africa today.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Later Stone Age, the order of events, and the descendants of the San and Khoikhoi.
