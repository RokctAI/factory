# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the position of Africa on a world map and on a globe, its size and shape, its neighbouring continents, and the oceans and seas around it. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Durban, in KwaZulu-Natal, on an outing to the beachfront promenade. In front of them, the warm Indian Ocean rolls onto the sand. Surfers wait for waves, and big cargo ships wait out at sea to enter Durban harbour. The teacher holds up a small globe and turns it until Africa faces the class. She asks: if we could sail straight out from this beach, where would we end up? The big idea is that Africa is a huge continent that lies across the middle of the Earth, with oceans on both sides, and that knowing its position helps us to understand our place in the world. The position of Africa and the names of the oceans and seas around it are examinable.

## Subtopic: Where Africa Is

On a globe, Africa is easy to find. It is the large continent that lies right across the middle of the Earth. The equator passes almost through the middle of Africa. The northern part of Africa, with countries such as Egypt, Morocco and Nigeria, is in the Northern Hemisphere. The southern part, with countries such as South Africa, Namibia and Mozambique, is in the Southern Hemisphere. Only a few continents are crossed by the equator, and Africa is crossed close to its middle, with large areas of land on both sides.

On most world maps, Africa is drawn near the centre of the map. This is partly because of the equator, and partly because of another important imaginary line, which runs from the North Pole to the South Pole through Greenwich, in London. This line passes through West Africa, through countries such as Algeria, Mali and Ghana.

Africa's shape is easy to recognise. The north is wide, and the south becomes narrower and narrower until it ends in a point, at Cape Agulhas in South Africa. On the east, a part of Africa sticks out into the Indian Ocean like a horn. This area, which includes Somalia and Ethiopia, is called the Horn of Africa. On the west, the coast curves inwards at the Gulf of Guinea.

Durban is on the east coast of South Africa, in the far south-east of the continent. If the class could sail straight east from the beach, they would cross the Indian Ocean and, after a very long journey, reach Australia.

The questions for this section are with you now: where Africa is on a globe and a world map, and its shape.

## Subtopic: Africa's Size and Neighbours

Africa is the second biggest continent on Earth. Only Asia is bigger. Africa covers about thirty million square kilometres, which is about one-fifth of all the land on Earth. It is so big that you could fit the United States, China, India and most of Europe inside it, all at the same time.

From the north of Africa to the south is about eight thousand kilometres. A car driving day and night, without stopping, at the speed of a highway, would take more than three days to cover that distance, if there were a straight road.

More than a billion people live in Africa, in more than fifty countries. Africa has more countries than any other continent.

Africa has two neighbouring continents. Europe lies to the north, across the Mediterranean Sea. At one place, between Morocco in Africa and Spain in Europe, the two continents are only about fourteen kilometres apart, across a narrow strip of sea called the Strait of Gibraltar. On a clear day you can see Europe from Africa there.

Asia lies to the north-east. Africa and Asia are joined by a narrow strip of land in Egypt. Long ago, people dug a canal through it, called the Suez Canal, so that ships can sail from the Mediterranean Sea into the Red Sea, without having to sail all the way around Africa.

The other continents are much further away. South America is to the west, across the Atlantic Ocean. Australia is to the east, across the Indian Ocean, and Antarctica is far to the south.

The questions for this section are with you now: Africa's size, its many countries and its neighbouring continents.

## Subtopic: Oceans and Seas Around Africa

Africa is surrounded by water on almost every side. Two great oceans and two seas lie around it.

The Atlantic Ocean lies to the west of Africa. Its waters wash the coasts of countries such as Morocco, Senegal, Nigeria, Angola and Namibia, and the west coast of South Africa. Off the west coast of South Africa, the water is cold, because a cold current, the Benguela Current, flows north, bringing cold water from the far south.

The Indian Ocean lies to the east of Africa. It washes the coasts of countries such as Somalia, Kenya, Tanzania and Mozambique, and the east coast of South Africa, including Durban. Here the water is warm, because the warm Agulhas Current flows south from near the equator. That is why the class in Durban can swim in warm water, while people in Cape Town often find the sea icy.

A sea is a smaller area of salt water, often partly surrounded by land. The Mediterranean Sea lies to the north of Africa, between Africa and Europe. The Red Sea lies to the north-east, between Africa and Asia.

At the southern tip of Africa, the Atlantic and the Indian Oceans meet.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Africa is a country; it is a continent with more than fifty countries. Two: thinking Africa is only in the Southern Hemisphere; the equator crosses it, so it is in both. Three: mixing up the oceans; the Atlantic is on the west and the Indian Ocean is on the east. Four: thinking Africa is the biggest continent; it is the second biggest, after Asia.

The questions for this section are with you now: the oceans and seas around Africa.
