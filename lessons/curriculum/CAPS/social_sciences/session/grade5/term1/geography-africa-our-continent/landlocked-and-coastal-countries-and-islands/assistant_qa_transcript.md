### subtopic_1 — Landlocked and Coastal Countries
**Assistant:** What are the other countries completely surrounded by one country?

**Tutor:** There are only three in the whole world. One is Lesotho, surrounded by South Africa. The other two are both in Europe, and both are completely surrounded by Italy. The first is Vatican City, inside the city of Rome. It is the smallest country in the world, smaller than many South African farms, and it is the home of the Pope, the leader of the Roman Catholic Church. The second is San Marino, a small country on a mountain in the north of Italy, which is one of the oldest republics in the world. Lesotho is much, much bigger than both of them put together. It covers about thirty thousand square kilometres, and more than two million people live there. It is also very high: the lowest point in Lesotho is higher than the lowest point of any other country.

### subtopic_2 — North, South or on the Equator
**Assistant:** Is it very hot in countries on the equator?

**Tutor:** Many places on or near the equator are hot all year round, because the sun shines from high in the sky every day of the year. Many of them also get a lot of rain, which is why the great rainforests of the Congo grow there. But height above sea level makes a big difference. Nairobi, in Kenya, is just south of the equator, but it is nearly one thousand eight hundred metres above sea level, so it has pleasant, mild weather, and can be chilly at night. Mount Kenya and Mount Kilimanjaro, both close to the equator, are so high that they have ice and snow near their tops. So close to the equator, it is usually hot at sea level, but it gets cooler as you go higher. That is a good example of why geographers look at many things together.

### subtopic_3 — Madagascar and Zanzibar
**Assistant:** Why do Madagascar's animals only live there?

**Tutor:** Long, long ago, all the land on Earth was joined together. Over many millions of years, the land slowly split apart and drifted, and Madagascar separated first from Africa and later from India. Since then, it has been an island, with deep sea all around it. The animals and plants that lived on Madagascar, or that floated across the sea on rafts of plants, were cut off from the rest of the world. Over a very long time, they changed in their own ways, to suit life on the island. That is why lemurs, many chameleons and many kinds of plants are found in the wild only on Madagascar. Scientists say that most of Madagascar's animals and plants are found nowhere else. Sadly, many are now in danger because forests are being cut down, so people are working hard to protect them.
