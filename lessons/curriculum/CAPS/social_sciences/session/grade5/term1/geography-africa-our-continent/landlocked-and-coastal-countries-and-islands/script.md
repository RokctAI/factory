# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the countries of Africa: whether they are landlocked or coastal, whether they lie north of, south of or on the equator, and two important islands, Madagascar and Zanzibar. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Ladybrand, a small town in the eastern Free State, on an outing to a hill just outside town. From the top, the learners can see the Mohokare River, which English speakers call the Caledon River, winding through the valley. On the other side of the river are the buildings of Maseru, the capital city of Lesotho, with the blue Maloti mountains rising behind it. The teacher asks a question: if you started walking from Maseru, in which direction would you find the sea? The answer is that you would have to cross South Africa in any direction you chose. The big idea is that countries can be grouped by where they lie: inland or on the coast, north or south of the equator, or out at sea as islands. Describing countries in these ways is examinable.

## Subtopic: Landlocked and Coastal Countries

A coastal country has a coastline. This means that at least one side of the country touches the sea or an ocean. South Africa is a coastal country, with a long coastline on the Atlantic Ocean and the Indian Ocean. Namibia, Mozambique, Kenya, Egypt and Nigeria are also coastal countries.

A landlocked country has no coastline at all. It is completely surrounded by land, with other countries on every side. Africa has sixteen landlocked countries. They include Lesotho, Eswatini, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Malawi, Uganda, Ethiopia, Mali, Niger and Chad.

Lesotho is very unusual. It is completely surrounded by just one country, South Africa. There are only three countries in the whole world that are completely surrounded by a single other country, and Lesotho is by far the biggest of them. That is why, from the hill near Ladybrand, every road to the sea from Maseru must go through South Africa.

Being landlocked makes some things harder. A landlocked country has no harbour of its own, so ships cannot bring goods straight to it. Goods must be sent through a neighbouring coastal country, by road or rail, which takes longer and costs more. Botswana, Zimbabwe and Zambia send and receive many goods through ports such as Durban in South Africa, Maputo and Beira in Mozambique, and Walvis Bay in Namibia. Ethiopia uses the port of Djibouti, its small coastal neighbour. So landlocked countries depend on good relationships with their neighbours.

On a map, you can tell whether a country is landlocked by tracing its border with your finger. If your finger touches the blue of the sea anywhere, the country is coastal. If it touches only other countries, it is landlocked.

The questions for this section are with you now: landlocked and coastal countries, and why it matters.

## Subtopic: North, South or on the Equator

The equator crosses Africa near its middle, so we can also describe African countries by where they are compared with the equator.

Countries north of the equator lie entirely in the Northern Hemisphere. They include Egypt, Morocco, Algeria, Nigeria, Ghana, Ethiopia and Sudan.

Countries south of the equator lie entirely in the Southern Hemisphere. They include South Africa, Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Mozambique, Lesotho, Eswatini, Angola and Madagascar.

Some countries are on the equator, which means the equator passes right through them. Part of the country is in the Northern Hemisphere and part is in the Southern Hemisphere. On the mainland of Africa, these countries are Gabon, the Republic of the Congo, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Kenya and Somalia. The equator also crosses Lake Victoria, which is shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania, and it passes through a tiny island of the island country of São Tomé and Príncipe, in the Atlantic Ocean.

To work out whether a country is north or south of the equator, or on it, first find the equator on the map. It is usually drawn as a line across the map and labelled. Then find the country. If the whole country is above the line, it is north of the equator. If it is all below the line, it is south. If the line passes through the country, the country is on the equator.

The questions for this section are with you now: countries north of, south of and on the equator.

## Subtopic: Madagascar and Zanzibar

An island is a piece of land completely surrounded by water. Africa has many islands, and some of them are countries.

Madagascar is a large island country in the Indian Ocean, off the south-east coast of Africa. It is separated from Mozambique by a stretch of sea called the Mozambique Channel. Madagascar is one of the biggest islands in the world. Its capital city is Antananarivo. Madagascar broke away from the rest of Africa many millions of years ago, so many of its plants and animals developed on their own and are found nowhere else on Earth. The best-known are the lemurs, furry animals with long tails and large eyes. Madagascar is also famous for its tall baobab trees.

Zanzibar is a group of islands in the Indian Ocean, off the coast of Tanzania, in East Africa. Zanzibar is not a separate country. It is part of Tanzania, but it has its own local government. Zanzibar is famous for its spices, such as cloves, which is why it is sometimes called the Spice Island. Its old town, called Stone Town, has narrow streets, carved wooden doors and buildings that show a mixture of African, Arab, Indian and European influences, because traders sailed there across the Indian Ocean for hundreds of years.

Other island countries of Africa include Mauritius, the Seychelles and the Comoros in the Indian Ocean, and Cabo Verde in the Atlantic Ocean.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking landlocked means a country with no water; Lesotho has rivers and dams, but no sea. Two: thinking Lesotho is part of South Africa; it is a separate country, surrounded by South Africa. Three: thinking South Africa is on the equator; it is far south of it. Four: thinking Zanzibar is a separate country; it is part of Tanzania.

The questions for this section are with you now: islands, Madagascar and Zanzibar.
