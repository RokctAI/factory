### subtopic_1 — Where Africa Is
**Assistant:** Why does Africa look smaller on some world maps than it really is?

**Tutor:** It is because of the way the round Earth is flattened onto a page. On one very common kind of world map, the places far from the equator, near the poles, are stretched to make them bigger. Places near the equator, like most of Africa, are not stretched much. So countries in the far north, like Greenland, Canada and Russia, look huge, and Africa looks smaller than it should, even though it is much bigger than all of them, even Russia. In real life, Greenland is about fourteen times smaller than Africa. Some mapmakers now use other kinds of world maps that show the true sizes of the continents more fairly. If you compare a globe with a map, you will see the difference for yourself.

### subtopic_2 — Africa's Size and Neighbours
**Assistant:** How did people sail from Europe to Asia before the Suez Canal?

**Tutor:** Before the Suez Canal opened in 1869, ships sailing between Europe and Asia had to go all the way around Africa. They sailed south along the west coast, past the Cape of Good Hope near Cape Town, and then north again along the east coast and across the Indian Ocean. The journey could take months, and the seas around the Cape were stormy and dangerous. This is one reason why a supply station was set up at the Cape in 1652, so that ships could get fresh water, meat and vegetables on the long voyage. When the Suez Canal opened, ships could sail through Egypt instead, saving thousands of kilometres. Today, when the Suez Canal is blocked or unsafe, many ships still sail around South Africa, and our ports become busier.

### subtopic_3 — Oceans and Seas Around Africa
**Assistant:** Why is the sea so cold in Cape Town but warm in Durban?

**Tutor:** It is because of ocean currents, which are like huge rivers flowing inside the ocean. Along the east coast of South Africa, the Agulhas Current flows south from the warm waters near the equator, carrying warm water past Durban. That is why the sea in Durban is pleasant for swimming for most of the year. Along the west coast, the Benguela Current flows north, carrying cold water that comes from the far south, near Antarctica. Cold water also rises up from deep in the ocean along that coast. So the sea at Cape Town and further up the west coast is cold, even in summer. The cold water is full of food for fish, which is why the west coast has big fishing industries.
