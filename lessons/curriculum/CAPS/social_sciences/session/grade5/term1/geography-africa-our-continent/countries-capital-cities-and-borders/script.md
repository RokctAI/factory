# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the concepts of a country, a capital city and a border, including borders that follow rivers and lakes. The anchor is a Grade 5 class from Musina, in Limpopo, at a viewpoint above the Beitbridge border post, one of the busiest border posts in southern Africa. Below them, a long line of trucks waits to cross the bridge over the wide, sandy Limpopo River. On the far bank is Zimbabwe. People with bags and suitcases walk towards the border buildings to show their passports. The teacher holds up a map of southern Africa and points to a thin dashed line that runs along the river. The big idea is that Africa is divided into countries, each with its own government and capital city, and that the borders between them are shown on maps, sometimes following natural features such as rivers and lakes. Understanding countries, capital cities and borders is examinable.

## Subtopic: What a Country Is

A country is an area of land that has its own government, its own laws and its own people, who are called its citizens. A country usually also has its own flag, its own national anthem and its own money, called its currency.

South Africa is a country. Its government makes laws for all South Africans, its flag has six colours, its anthem is sung in five languages, and its currency is the rand. Zimbabwe, just across the river from Musina, is a different country, with its own government, flag and laws.

Africa has fifty-four countries. Some are very big. Algeria, in the north, is the biggest country in Africa by area. Others are tiny. The Seychelles, a group of islands in the Indian Ocean, is the smallest. Nigeria has the most people of any country in Africa.

A country is divided into smaller parts so that it is easier to govern. In South Africa, these parts are the nine provinces, such as Limpopo, Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal. A province is part of a country, not a country on its own. A continent, such as Africa, is made up of many countries.

So, from smallest to biggest: a town is in a province, a province is in a country, and a country is in a continent. Musina is a town, in the province of Limpopo, in the country of South Africa, on the continent of Africa.

The questions for this section are with you now: what makes a country, and how towns, provinces, countries and continents fit together.

## Subtopic: Capital Cities

A capital city is the city where a country's government does its main work. The leaders of the country, such as the president and the ministers, have their offices there, and the most important government buildings are found there.

Most countries have one capital city. The capital city of Zimbabwe is Harare. The capital city of Kenya is Nairobi. The capital city of Egypt is Cairo. The capital city of Nigeria is Abuja.

South Africa is unusual, because it has three capital cities, each with a different job. Pretoria, in Gauteng, is where the President and the government departments work, at the Union Buildings. Cape Town, in the Western Cape, is where Parliament meets to make the country's laws. Bloemfontein, in the Free State, is where the Supreme Court of Appeal sits, one of the country's highest courts. Sharing the capital between three cities in three provinces goes back to 1910, when South Africa was formed by joining four colonies, and the leaders agreed to share the government between them.

A capital city is not always the biggest city in a country. Johannesburg is the biggest city in South Africa, but it is not a capital. Lagos is the biggest city in Nigeria, but the capital is Abuja.

On maps, capital cities are often shown with a special symbol, such as a star or a dot inside a circle, so that they stand out from other cities. Always check the key.

The questions for this section are with you now: capital cities, and South Africa's three capitals.

## Subtopic: Borders on a Map

A border is the line where one country ends and another country begins. On a map, borders are usually drawn as thin lines, often dashed or dotted. Each country may be coloured differently, so that you can see where it starts and ends.

On the ground, there is usually no painted line. Sometimes there is a fence, a sign or a border post, where people must show their passports and where goods are checked. At Beitbridge, the trucks must stop so that officials can check what they carry.

Some borders follow natural features. The Limpopo River forms the border between South Africa and Zimbabwe, and part of the border between South Africa and Botswana. The Gariep, or Orange, River forms part of the border between South Africa and Namibia. The Zambezi River forms the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. On the map, the border line runs along the river.

Borders can also run through lakes. Lake Victoria is shared by three countries: Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania. The border lines run across the water.

Other borders are straight lines. If you look at a map of Africa, you will see many straight borders, especially in the Sahara Desert. Most of Africa's borders were drawn by European countries in the late eighteen hundreds, when they took control of most of Africa as colonies. They often drew lines on maps without asking the African people who lived there, and some borders cut through the lands of a single community. When African countries became independent, most of them kept these borders.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Africa is one country; it has fifty-four countries. Two: thinking the biggest city is always the capital; Johannesburg is South Africa's biggest city, but not a capital. Three: thinking South Africa has one capital; it has three, Pretoria, Cape Town and Bloemfontein. Four: thinking a border is a painted line on the ground; it is shown on maps, and sometimes follows a river or a lake.

The questions for this section are with you now: borders, and borders that follow rivers and lakes.
