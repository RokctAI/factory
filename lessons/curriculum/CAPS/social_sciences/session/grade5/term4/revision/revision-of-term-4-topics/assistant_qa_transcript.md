### subtopic_1 — Minerals and Mines
**Assistant:** How can I link mining and heritage this term?

**Tutor:** There are several strong links between the two topics this term. First, Kimberley and Johannesburg are mining towns, and their mining history is now heritage: the Big Hole in Kimberley is a museum, and Gold Reef City and old mine headgear in Johannesburg remind people of the gold rush. Second, Mapungubwe's golden objects were made from gold mined and worked in southern Africa about eight hundred years ago, long before the gold rush of 1886, which shows that Africans were skilled miners and goldsmiths. Third, mining can threaten heritage: acid water from old mines near the Cradle of Humankind has been a concern, and there were debates about coal mining near Mapungubwe. Fourth, the lives of miners, including migrant workers far from home, are part of South Africa's history and heritage. In a paragraph answer, linking the two topics like this shows deep understanding.

### subtopic_2 — Mining, the Environment and People
**Assistant:** What are the most important Geography words for this term?

**Tutor:** Here is a short word bank to revise. Mineral: a natural, solid substance in the Earth's crust. Ore: rock rich in a valuable mineral. Non-renewable: cannot be replaced in a human lifetime. Renewable: can be replaced or will not run out, like sunlight and wind. Fossil fuel: a fuel formed from ancient living things, such as coal. Peat: the first stage in the formation of coal. Seam: a layer of coal. Surface or strip mining, open pit mining, shaft mining and deep-level mining: the types of mining. Overburden: rock above the mineral. Stope: the working area underground. Ventilation: bringing in fresh air and removing used air. Acid mine drainage: acidic water from old mines. Tailings: fine waste after processing. Rehabilitation: repairing the land after mining. Silicosis: a lung disease from silica dust. Self-rescuer and refuge bay: emergency safety equipment. Make flashcards and test yourself.

### subtopic_3 — The Heritage Trail
**Assistant:** What are the most important History words for this term?

**Tutor:** Here is a short word bank for the heritage trail. Heritage: what we inherit from the past, value and pass on. Natural and cultural heritage. Tangible heritage: can be touched. Intangible heritage: cannot be touched, such as languages and songs. Heritage trail: a route to heritage places. World Heritage Site: a place recognised as important to all humanity. Fossil: remains of an ancient living thing preserved in rock. Cradle of Humankind: a fossil site in Gauteng. Australopithecus: an early human relative, such as Mrs Ples. Mapungubwe: an African kingdom in Limpopo, about eight hundred years ago. Golden rhinoceros. Bastion: a pointed corner of a fort. Identity: who you are and where you belong. Indigenous knowledge: knowledge built up by communities over generations. Sustainable harvesting: taking only part of a plant so it survives. Rock art. Ochre: red or brown earth used for paint.
