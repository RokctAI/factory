# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the topic a heritage trail through the provinces of South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Mahikeng, in North West, on the first day of the topic. Each learner receives a small booklet made of folded paper, with a cover that says heritage passport and a page for each of the nine provinces. On the classroom wall, a big map of South Africa shows the provinces in different colours, and a dotted line winds from one to the next, like a road trip. At each stop on the line, there is a small picture: a skull of an early human ancestor in Gauteng, a little golden rhinoceros in Limpopo, a five-pointed fort in the Western Cape, a spiky aloe plant in the Eastern Cape, a painted eland on a rock in KwaZulu-Natal, and a stone-walled town here in North West. The teacher explains that during this term, they will travel along this trail in their imaginations, and get a stamp in their passport at each stop. The big idea is that South Africa has a rich and varied heritage, found in places, objects, people's achievements, names, buildings, plants, knowledge, art and nature, in every province, and that a heritage trail is a way of exploring and learning about it. ## Subtopic: What Is a Heritage Trail?

A trail is a path or route that you follow, often with interesting places to stop along the way. A heritage trail is a route that takes you to places and things that are part of a country's or community's heritage, so that you can learn about them.

Heritage is what we inherit from the past, value today, and want to pass on to future generations. It can be a place, an object, a building, a natural feature, a story, a song, a language, a custom or a skill.

Heritage trails exist in many towns and cities in South Africa. Some are walking trails, with signs and plaques that explain the history of buildings and places. Others are driving routes that link several heritage sites in a region. There are trails that follow the story of a famous person, such as Nelson Mandela, or of an event, such as the struggle for freedom, or of a theme, such as rock art or the gold rush.

A heritage trail helps people to understand where they come from, to respect other people's heritage, to remember important events, both happy and painful, and to protect heritage for the future. It also brings visitors and tourists, which creates jobs.

## Subtopic: Nine Provinces

South Africa has nine provinces: Gauteng, Limpopo, North West, Mpumalanga, KwaZulu-Natal, the Eastern Cape, the Western Cape, the Northern Cape and the Free State. Each province has its own heritage, shaped by its land, its people and its history.

On this heritage trail, the class will visit examples from different provinces. In Gauteng, they will visit the Cradle of Humankind, a site where fossils of very early human ancestors have been found. In Limpopo, they will learn about the golden objects of Mapungubwe, an ancient African kingdom. In the Western Cape, they will visit the Castle of Good Hope, and learn how heritage and identities change over time. In the Eastern Cape, they will learn about the aloe plant and indigenous knowledge of medicine. In KwaZulu-Natal, they will see San rock art in the Drakensberg.

Other provinces have heritage too. In the Northern Cape, people remember Frances Baard, a brave leader in the struggle for workers' and women's rights. In the Free State, many rivers, dams and towns have names with a history. In North West, there are the ruins of Kaditshwene, a large stone-walled Tswana town. In Mpumalanga, the Makhonjwa Mountains near Barberton contain some of the oldest rocks on Earth.

## Subtopic: Kinds of Heritage

As they travel along the trail, the class will meet different kinds of heritage.

Heritage can be found in sites of significance, which are important places, such as the Cradle of Humankind. It can be found in objects, such as the golden rhinoceros of Mapungubwe. It can be found in people's achievements, such as the work of Frances Baard. It can be found in names, of rivers, dams, towns and streets, which tell stories about the past. It can be found in buildings and architecture, such as the Castle or the stone walls of Kaditshwene. It can be found in indigenous knowledge, such as how to use the aloe as medicine. It can be found in art, such as San rock paintings. And it can be found in nature, such as mountains, rivers and plants.

Some of these sites are so important that they are protected as World Heritage Sites, which means the whole world recognises their value. South Africa has several, including the Cradle of Humankind, Mapungubwe, the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park and the Barberton Makhonjwa Mountains.

In South Africa, a law protects important heritage, and the South African Heritage Resources Agency helps to look after it.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking heritage is only old buildings; it includes places, objects, names, knowledge, art, nature and customs. Two: thinking only some provinces have heritage; every province has heritage. Three: thinking a heritage trail is only for tourists; it helps everyone to learn and remember. Four: thinking heritage only remembers happy events; it also remembers painful ones.
