# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers what heritage is. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Soweto, in Gauteng, on the day before Heritage Day. The classroom is full of colour. Learners have come in traditional clothing: beaded necklaces and headbands, colourful shweshwe dresses, blankets, a kufi cap, a sari and a pair of veldskoene. On a long table, there is food from their families: dumplings, chakalaka, samoosas, koeksisters, morogo and pap. A group sings a song in isiZulu, another recites a praise poem in Setswana, and a learner shows a photograph of his great-grandmother and tells her story. In the afternoon, the class walks to Vilakazi Street, where the houses of Nelson Mandela and Archbishop Desmond Tutu stand, and past the memorial to Hector Pieterson, a schoolboy killed in the Soweto uprising of 1976. The teacher asks: what do all these things, the clothes, the food, the songs, the photographs, the houses and the memorial, have in common? The big idea is that heritage is what we inherit from the past and value enough to keep and pass on, and that it includes nature and culture, things we can touch and things we cannot, and belongs to individuals, communities and the whole nation. What heritage is, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Natural and Cultural Heritage

Heritage is anything we inherit from the past, value today and want to pass on to future generations. Heritage is different from history. History is the study of what happened in the past. Heritage is the part of the past that people choose to treasure and keep.

There are two main kinds of heritage.

Natural heritage is heritage that comes from nature. It includes landscapes, mountains, rivers, wetlands, forests, coastlines, plants and animals, and even rocks and fossils. Examples are Table Mountain, the Drakensberg, the Blyde River Canyon, the Kruger National Park and the fynbos of the Cape. Natural heritage was not made by people, but people value it and must protect it.

Cultural heritage is heritage that has been made or developed by people. It includes buildings, monuments, objects, artworks, languages, music, dance, stories, customs, beliefs, food and skills. Examples are the houses on Vilakazi Street, San rock paintings, the songs of the class, and family recipes.

Some places are both natural and cultural heritage. The uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park is valued both for its beautiful mountains and wildlife, and for the rock art painted by the San.

The questions for this section are with you now: what heritage is, and natural and cultural heritage.

## Subtopic: Tangible and Intangible Heritage

Cultural heritage can be divided into two kinds.

Tangible heritage is heritage that you can touch and see. It is physical. It includes buildings, monuments, graves, statues, objects, tools, clothing, artworks, photographs and documents. The beaded necklaces, the photograph of the great-grandmother, and the houses on Vilakazi Street are tangible heritage.

Intangible heritage is heritage that you cannot touch. It is carried in people's minds, voices and actions. It includes languages, songs, dances, praise poems, stories, proverbs, games, customs, ceremonies, beliefs, cooking methods and skills, such as beadwork or building. The song in isiZulu, the praise poem in Setswana and the way of cooking a family recipe are intangible heritage.

Intangible heritage is passed on by people, from one generation to the next, by telling, showing, teaching and doing together. If it is not passed on, it can be lost. For example, some of South Africa's indigenous languages, such as some San languages, are spoken by very few people today, and some have already been lost.

The questions for this section are with you now: tangible and intangible heritage.

## Subtopic: Whose Heritage, and Why Protect It?

Heritage can belong to different groups. Personal or family heritage is what you inherit from your own family, such as a family name, a photograph, a recipe or a story. Community heritage belongs to a community, such as a local language, a church, a market or a memorial. National heritage belongs to the whole country, such as national symbols, important sites and public holidays. World heritage is valued by all humanity.

South Africa's national symbols are part of our national heritage. They include the flag, the coat of arms, whose motto, written in a San language, means diverse people unite, and the national anthem, sung in five languages. There are also the national animal, the springbok, the national bird, the blue crane, the national flower, the king protea, and the national tree, the real yellowwood.

Heritage is important because it gives people a sense of identity and belonging, helps us to understand and respect each other, teaches lessons from the past, and attracts visitors. Some heritage remembers painful events, such as the Hector Pieterson Memorial, so that we never forget the struggle for freedom.

Heritage can be damaged or lost through neglect, vandalism, development, pollution, theft, or simply by being forgotten. We protect heritage by looking after sites, keeping laws, recording stories, and practising and teaching our languages and customs.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking heritage and history are the same; heritage is the part of the past we choose to treasure. Two: thinking heritage must be old buildings; songs, languages and recipes are heritage too. Three: thinking intangible heritage cannot be lost; it is lost if it is not passed on. Four: thinking heritage belongs to only one group; every person, community and the nation has heritage.

The questions for this section are with you now: personal, community and national heritage, and protecting heritage.

## Subtopic: Nature and Culture

Heritage is what we inherit, value and pass on.

Natural heritage comes from nature, like mountains, rivers, plants and animals.

Cultural heritage is made by people, like buildings, art, songs and food.

Keep the long table of family food in mind and try the questions for this part: natural and cultural heritage.

## Subtopic: Touch or Not

Tangible heritage can be touched, like buildings, clothes and photographs.

Intangible heritage cannot be touched, like languages, songs, stories and customs.

Intangible heritage is lost if it is not passed on, so elders teach it to children.

Hold the song in isiZulu and the beaded necklace in mind and try this part's questions: tangible and intangible heritage.

Heritage can belong to a family, a community, the nation or the world. National symbols, like the flag, the springbok and the king protea, are heritage. Heritage gives us identity, and some of it remembers painful events. We must protect it, so that it is not lost.
