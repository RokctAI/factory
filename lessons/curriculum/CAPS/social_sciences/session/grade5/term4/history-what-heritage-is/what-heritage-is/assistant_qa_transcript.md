### subtopic_1 — Natural and Cultural Heritage
**Assistant:** Why is the coat of arms motto in a San language?

**Tutor:** South Africa's coat of arms was introduced in the year 2000. Its motto, written in the language of the |Xam San people, who once lived in what is now the Northern Cape, means diverse people unite. The designers chose this language on purpose. The San are among the oldest peoples of southern Africa, and their heritage, including their rock art, belongs to all South Africans. By choosing a San language, rather than one of the languages spoken by larger groups, the motto does not favour any one group today, and it honours the first peoples of the land, whose languages and way of life suffered great losses. The figures in the centre of the coat of arms are also based on a San rock painting, the Linton Stone. Sadly, the |Xam language is no longer spoken, which reminds us how important it is to protect languages while they are still alive.

### subtopic_2 — Tangible and Intangible Heritage
**Assistant:** What happened in Soweto in 1976?

**Tutor:** On the 16th of June 1976, thousands of school learners in Soweto marched in protest against a government rule that forced black schools to teach some subjects in Afrikaans, a language that many learners and teachers did not speak well. The learners also protested against the unfair system of education under apartheid. The police opened fire on the peaceful march, and many young people were killed and injured. One of the first to be killed was a young schoolboy, Hector Pieterson. A photograph of him being carried by another learner, with his sister running alongside, was published around the world and showed the cruelty of apartheid. The protests spread across the country. Today, the 16th of June is Youth Day, a public holiday, and the Hector Pieterson Memorial and Museum in Soweto, close to where he was shot, remembers him and all the young people who stood up for their rights.

### subtopic_3 — Whose Heritage, and Why Protect It?
**Assistant:** How can I find out about my own family heritage?

**Tutor:** The best place to start is with the older people in your family, such as your parents, grandparents, aunts and uncles. Ask them questions, and write down or record their answers, with their permission. You could ask: where did our family come from? What is the meaning of our surname or clan name? What languages did our grandparents speak? What games, songs or stories do you remember from your childhood? What foods were cooked for special occasions? Are there any special objects, photographs or documents in our family, and what are their stories? You could also ask about important events in the family's past. Then you can make a family tree, a scrapbook or a short book about your family heritage. Remember that some family stories may be sad or painful, so be respectful. What you collect is precious, and one day you can pass it on to your own children.
