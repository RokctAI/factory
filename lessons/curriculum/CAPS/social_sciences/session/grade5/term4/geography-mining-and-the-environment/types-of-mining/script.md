# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the concept of mining, and the types of mining: open pit, surface, shaft and deep-level mining. The anchor is a Grade 5 class on a visit to the Big Hole in Kimberley, in the Northern Cape. They walk out onto a steel viewing platform that sticks out over the edge, and look down into an enormous hole in the ground, so wide that the people on the far side look tiny. Far below, the bottom is filled with still, green water. A guide explains that from 1871, thousands of miners dug this hole, much of it by hand with picks and shovels, searching for diamonds, until the mine closed in 1914. Back in the museum, the class watches a video of two modern mines. In the first, giant trucks, each as big as a house, drive down a spiral road into a huge stepped pit. In the second, miners in hard hats step into a steel cage that drops down a shaft, deep into the Earth, to tunnels where they work by the light of their headlamps. The big idea is that mining means taking minerals out of the ground, and that the type of mining used depends mostly on how deep the mineral is and how it lies in the rock. ## Subtopic: What Is Mining?

Mining is the process of removing valuable minerals, coal or other materials from the Earth. It usually has several steps.

First, geologists explore to find where minerals are, by studying rocks, using maps and special instruments, and drilling to take samples from deep underground. If there is enough of a valuable mineral, a mine can be planned.

Second, the ore is mined, which means it is broken and removed from the ground, using explosives, drills, diggers and trucks.

Third, the ore is processed. It is crushed and treated to separate the valuable mineral from the waste rock. The waste rock is dumped, and the mineral is sent to be sold or made into products.

Finally, when the mine closes, the land should be rehabilitated, which means repaired, so that it is safe and plants can grow again.

The type of mining depends mostly on how deep the mineral is. Minerals near the surface are mined from the top, in surface mines and open pits. Minerals deep underground are reached through shafts and tunnels. Mining companies choose the cheapest and safest method for each mineral.

## Subtopic: Surface and Open Pit Mining

Surface mining is used when minerals or coal lie in layers close to the surface. First, the plants and the topsoil are removed and kept aside. Then the rock above the mineral, called overburden, is removed with huge machines, and the layer of coal or mineral is dug out. This type of surface mining is often called strip mining, because it is done in long strips. Much of the coal in Mpumalanga is mined this way. After a strip is mined, it can be filled in with the overburden, and the topsoil can be put back, so that plants can grow again.

Open pit mining is used when a large body of ore lies near the surface but goes down deep. Miners dig a huge hole, which gets wider and deeper over time. The sides of the pit are cut into steps, called benches, so that the walls do not collapse and so that trucks can drive down a road that spirals around the pit. Explosives break the rock, and giant diggers load it onto trucks. Examples include the iron ore mine at Sishen in the Northern Cape, and the copper mine at Phalaborwa in Limpopo, one of the largest open pits in Africa.

The Big Hole in Kimberley began as an open pit dug by hand. Later, as it got deeper, the miners dug shafts and tunnels to reach the diamonds below.

Surface and open pit mining are cheaper and safer than underground mining, but they disturb a large area of land.

## Subtopic: Shaft and Deep-Level Mining

Shaft mining is used when the mineral lies too deep to dig from the surface. A shaft is a deep, vertical hole, like a well, dug down to the level of the ore. Lifts called cages carry miners and equipment up and down the shaft, and skips carry the ore up. From the shaft, horizontal tunnels are dug out to the ore. Miners drill holes in the rock, fill them with explosives, and blast the ore loose. Machines and small trains carry the ore to the shaft to be lifted to the surface. Platinum, coal and gold are often mined this way.

Deep-level mining is shaft mining that goes very deep. South Africa's gold mines on the Witwatersrand are among the deepest mines in the world. Some reach about four kilometres below the surface, which is deeper than the height of the Drakensberg's highest peak above sea level. It can take an hour or more for a miner to travel from the surface to the place where they work.

Shaft and deep-level mining disturb less land on the surface than open pits, but they are more expensive and more dangerous, because of heat, rock falls, dust and the danger of fire or flooding underground.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking all mines are tunnels underground; many are surface mines and open pits. Two: thinking the steps in an open pit are for decoration; they stop the walls from collapsing and let trucks drive down. Three: thinking deep gold mines are a few hundred metres deep; some reach about four kilometres. Four: thinking a mine is finished when the mineral is removed; the land must be rehabilitated.
