# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the main minerals mined in South Africa, such as gold, diamonds and coal, and their uses. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Bloemfontein, in the Free State, during a show-and-tell lesson. Each learner has brought something from home connected to a mineral. One has her grandmother's gold wedding ring. Another has an old cellphone, opened up to show its tiny circuit board. A learner whose father works in building brings a drill bit with a tip coated in tiny diamonds, used for cutting through concrete and stone. Someone has brought a bag of coal and a photograph of a power station. Another has a stainless steel spoon, a copper wire and a platinum-coloured part from an old car exhaust. The teacher writes three headings on the board, gold, diamonds and coal, and the class sorts the objects under them, with an extra column for other minerals. The big idea is that South Africa mines many valuable minerals, especially gold, diamonds, coal and platinum, and that each is used in many ways because of its special properties. ## Subtopic: Gold

Gold is a soft, heavy, bright yellow metal. It is special because it does not rust or tarnish, so it stays shiny for thousands of years. It can be hammered very thin and pulled into fine wire, and it carries electricity very well.

In South Africa, most gold is found in a huge underground area called the Witwatersrand Basin, which stretches in an arc through Gauteng, North West and the Free State. Gold mining towns include Johannesburg, Carletonville, Klerksdorp and Welkom, near Bloemfontein. The gold is spread through hard rock in tiny grains, often too small to see, so huge amounts of rock must be mined and crushed to get a small amount of gold.

For about a hundred years, South Africa produced more gold than any other country. Today, other countries produce more, but South Africa still has large amounts of gold deep underground.

Gold is used for jewellery, such as rings, chains and earrings. Banks and governments keep gold as a store of wealth. Because it carries electricity well and does not rust, small amounts are used in cellphones, computers and other electronics. It is used in dentistry, for medals and awards, and even in space, where thin layers of gold protect astronauts' visors and satellites from the sun's rays.

The questions for this section are with you now: the properties, location and uses of gold.

## Subtopic: Diamonds

A diamond is a crystal made of pure carbon, the same element that is in coal and pencil lead, but arranged in a very strong pattern. Diamonds formed very deep underground, more than a hundred kilometres down, under enormous heat and pressure. Volcanic eruptions long ago carried them up towards the surface in pipes of rock called kimberlite, named after the city of Kimberley.

Diamonds are the hardest natural substance on Earth. Nothing natural can scratch a diamond except another diamond.

In South Africa, the first diamond was found in 1867 near Hopetown, in the Northern Cape, and soon afterwards, huge finds were made at Kimberley. Diamonds have also been mined in Gauteng, Limpopo and the Free State, and along the west coast, in gravels and in the sea near Namaqualand, where rivers carried them long ago.

Diamonds are used in two main ways. The best stones are cut and polished into sparkling gems for jewellery. Because they are so hard, many diamonds, including tiny or poor-quality ones, are used in industry, to make tools for cutting, drilling, grinding and polishing hard materials, such as the drill bit in the Bloemfontein classroom, saws for cutting stone, and drills for mining and for finding oil.

The questions for this section are with you now: how diamonds form, where they are found and how they are used.

## Subtopic: Coal and Other Minerals

Coal is a black or dark brown rock that burns. It is a fossil fuel, formed from plants that lived millions of years ago. Most of South Africa's coal is mined in Mpumalanga, especially around eMalahleni, and also in Limpopo, KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State.

Coal is used mainly to make electricity. Most of South Africa's electricity is made in power stations that burn coal. Coal is also used to make steel, to make fuel and chemicals, and for heating and cooking in some homes. South Africa also sells a lot of coal to other countries.

South Africa has many other important minerals. Platinum, found mainly around Rustenburg and in Limpopo, is used in car exhaust systems to clean harmful gases, in jewellery, and in electronics and medicine. Iron ore, from the Northern Cape, is used to make steel. Chrome makes steel shiny and stops it from rusting, as in stainless steel spoons. Manganese is also used to make steel. Copper is used in electrical wires. Titanium, from sand along the KwaZulu-Natal coast, is used to make white paint.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the show-and-tell in Bloemfontein — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Gold

Gold is soft, heavy and shiny, and it never rusts. It carries electricity well.

Most gold is in the Witwatersrand Basin in Gauteng, North West and the Free State.

Gold is used for jewellery, money, cellphones, medals and even space. Lots of rock must be crushed to get a little gold.

Keep the grandmother's gold wedding ring in mind and try the questions for this part: gold.

## Subtopic: Diamonds

Diamonds are pure carbon, formed very deep underground. They are the hardest natural substance.

The first was found near Hopetown in 1867, then big finds at Kimberley.

The best are cut into gems. Most are used in tools for cutting and drilling.

Hold the diamond-tipped drill bit in mind and try this part's questions: diamonds.

## Subtopic: Coal and More

Coal is black rock that burns. It is mined mostly in Mpumalanga and is used mainly to make electricity. Platinum cleans car exhausts. Iron ore makes steel. Chrome makes stainless steel. Copper makes wires.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Gold is shiny, never rusts and carries electricity, and is used for jewellery, wealth and electronics; it is mined in the Witwatersrand Basin. Diamonds are pure carbon, the hardest natural substance, used as gems and in cutting and drilling tools; they are found at Kimberley and elsewhere. Coal, mined mainly in Mpumalanga, makes most of our electricity. Platinum, iron ore, chrome and copper are also important.
