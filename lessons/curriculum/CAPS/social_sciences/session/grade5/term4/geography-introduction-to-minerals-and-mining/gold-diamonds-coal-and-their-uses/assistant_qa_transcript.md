### subtopic_1 — Gold
**Assistant:** Why does it take so much rock to get a little gold?

**Tutor:** On the Witwatersrand, gold is not found in big nuggets or thick seams. It is spread through hard rock, called reef, in tiny grains, most of them too small to see. On average, a tonne of rock, which is about the weight of a small car, may contain only a few grams of gold, about the weight of a coin or less. So, to get enough gold to make one wedding ring, miners may have to dig out, lift to the surface and crush several tonnes of rock. The crushed rock is then treated with chemicals that dissolve the gold, and the gold is collected, melted and poured into bars. That is why gold mining needs so much work, energy, water and machinery, and why it leaves behind such huge mine dumps of crushed rock. It is also one reason why gold is so valuable.

### subtopic_2 — Diamonds
**Assistant:** How can a diamond and coal both be carbon?

**Tutor:** It is amazing, but true. Carbon is an element, one of the basic building blocks of everything. The difference between diamond and the carbon in coal or pencil lead is how the tiny particles, called atoms, are arranged. In a diamond, every carbon atom is tightly linked to four others in a strong, three-dimensional pattern, like a perfect climbing frame. This makes diamond extremely hard and clear. In graphite, the soft grey material in pencils, the carbon atoms are arranged in flat layers that slide over each other easily, which is why a pencil leaves a mark. Coal is mostly carbon too, from ancient plants, mixed with other substances, and its atoms are not neatly arranged at all. Diamonds only form under the huge heat and pressure found very deep inside the Earth, which squeezes the carbon atoms into their special pattern.

### subtopic_3 — Coal and Other Minerals
**Assistant:** What was the Cullinan diamond?

**Tutor:** The Cullinan diamond is the largest gem-quality rough diamond ever found. It was discovered in 1905 at the Premier Mine at Cullinan, near Pretoria, in Gauteng. It weighed about 3,106 carats, which is about 620 grams, roughly the weight of a large mango. It was named after the owner of the mine, Thomas Cullinan. The diamond was given to King Edward the Seventh of Britain, and was cut into several large stones and many smaller ones. The two biggest, called the Great Star of Africa and the Second Star of Africa, are now part of the British Crown Jewels in London. Some South Africans feel that this diamond, which came from South African soil and was mined by South African workers, should be returned, while others see it as part of history. The mine at Cullinan is still producing diamonds, and the town has become a tourist destination.
