# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the location of mineral and coal mines, and the links between mining and settlement patterns. The anchor is a Grade 5 class in Johannesburg, in Gauteng, a city often called eGoli, which means place of gold. On the classroom floor, the learners have spread out a large outline map of South Africa. Each group has a set of sticky symbols, explained in a key in the corner of the map: a yellow triangle for gold, a white diamond shape for diamonds, a black square for coal, a grey circle for platinum, and a red circle for iron ore. With an atlas to help them, they stick the symbols onto the map. Yellow triangles form an arc through Gauteng, North West and the Free State. Black squares crowd into Mpumalanga. Grey circles gather around Rustenburg and in Limpopo. Then the teacher asks them to mark the big towns, and they notice that many of the towns sit right on top of the mines. The big idea is that South Africa's minerals are found in particular areas, which we can show on a map with symbols and a key, and that many towns and cities grew up because of mining. Mines and settlement are examinable.

## Subtopic: Where the Mines Are

Minerals are not spread evenly across South Africa. They are found where the rocks are right for them, so mines are found in particular areas.

Gold is mined mainly in the Witwatersrand Basin, an arc that runs from the East Rand, through Johannesburg and the West Rand, to Klerksdorp in North West and Welkom in the Free State.

Coal is mined mainly in Mpumalanga, around eMalahleni, Middelburg and Secunda, and also in Limpopo, KwaZulu-Natal and the northern Free State.

Platinum is mined mainly in the Bushveld area, around Rustenburg in North West, and in Limpopo, near Mokopane and Polokwane. This area holds most of the world's known platinum.

Diamonds have been mined around Kimberley in the Northern Cape, at Cullinan in Gauteng, in Limpopo, and along the west coast.

Iron ore and manganese are mined in the Northern Cape, around Kathu, Sishen and Hotazel. Copper and other minerals are mined at Phalaborwa in Limpopo. Heavy minerals, such as titanium, are mined from coastal sands near Richards Bay in KwaZulu-Natal.

The questions for this section are with you now: where the main minerals are mined in South Africa.

## Subtopic: Reading a Mining Map

A mining map shows where minerals are found and mined. Like other maps, it uses symbols, and a key, also called a legend, explains what each symbol means.

Symbols on mining maps can be shapes, colours or small pictures. For example, a pick and shovel can show a mine, a yellow triangle can show gold, and a black square can show coal. Always read the key first.

To describe where minerals are found, use the names of provinces and towns, and compass directions. For example: coal is mined mainly in the east, in Mpumalanga; iron ore is mined in the north-west, in the Northern Cape; gold is mined in an arc in the north-east, through Gauteng, North West and the Free State.

A mining map also helps to show patterns. Most mining is in the north and north-east of the country, where old, mineral-rich rocks lie near the surface. Few minerals are mined in the south, in the Eastern Cape and the southern Western Cape.

Railway lines on a mining map show how minerals are moved to factories, power stations and ports. For example, coal goes by rail from Mpumalanga to Richards Bay, and iron ore goes by a very long railway from the Northern Cape to the port of Saldanha Bay, on the west coast, to be sent to other countries.

The questions for this section are with you now: symbols, keys, directions and patterns on mining maps.

## Subtopic: Mining and Settlement

A settlement is a place where people live, from a small village to a big city. A settlement pattern is the way settlements are spread across an area.

Many South African towns and cities began as mining settlements. When gold was found on the Witwatersrand in 1886, thousands of people rushed there, and a camp of tents and huts quickly grew into the city of Johannesburg. Other towns along the gold reef grew too, joined by roads and railways, so that today Gauteng is a long, almost continuous stretch of cities, the most crowded part of the country. Kimberley grew because of diamonds, eMalahleni because of coal, Rustenburg because of platinum, and Welkom because of gold found in the Free State in the 1940s.

Mining towns attract other activities: factories, shops, schools, hospitals, roads, railways and power stations. People come from far away, looking for work, and new suburbs and informal settlements grow.

But when a mine closes, the town can shrink. Pilgrim's Rest, in Mpumalanga, was a busy gold-rush town in the 1870s. When the gold ran out, the mines closed, and today it is a small museum town for tourists.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking minerals are found everywhere; they are found in particular areas. Two: reading a mining map without the key; the key explains the symbols. Three: thinking Johannesburg was built first and gold was found later; the city grew because of gold. Four: thinking mining towns last forever; when the minerals run out, towns can shrink.

The questions for this section are with you now: how mining has shaped settlement patterns.

## Subtopic: Mining Towns

Many towns grew because of mines: Johannesburg for gold, Kimberley for diamonds, eMalahleni for coal and Rustenburg for platinum. Mines bring shops, schools, roads and people looking for work. When a mine closes, a town can shrink, like Pilgrim's Rest.
