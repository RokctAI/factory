### subtopic_1 — Collecting and Carrying Water
**Assistant:** What is cholera?

**Tutor:** Cholera is a serious illness caused by tiny germs called bacteria, which are far too small to see. People get cholera by drinking water or eating food that has been made dirty with the germs, usually because human waste has got into the water. A person with cholera gets very bad diarrhoea and may also vomit. Their body loses a huge amount of water and salts very quickly, so they become dehydrated, and without treatment, they can die within a day. The good news is that cholera can be prevented and treated. It is prevented by drinking safe water, by boiling or treating water from rivers and streams, by using toilets and keeping them away from water sources, and by washing hands with soap. People who are ill can be treated with a special drink of clean water with sugar and salt, called oral rehydration solution, and very sick people go to a clinic or hospital. That is why clean water and toilets save so many lives.

### subtopic_2 — Problems and Dangers
**Assistant:** How do I make a simple water filter?

**Tutor:** You can make a simple filter to see how filtering works, but remember: it cleans out dirt, but it does not kill all germs, so the water is still not safe to drink until it has been boiled. Ask an adult to help you cut the bottom off a clear plastic bottle. Turn the bottle upside down, with the lid off, and hold it over a jar. Put a small piece of clean cloth or cotton wool into the neck. Then add layers on top: first fine sand, then coarse sand, then small gravel or pebbles. Slowly pour muddy water in at the top. As it trickles down through the layers, the gravel catches big bits, the sand catches smaller bits, and the cloth catches the finest dirt. The water that comes out at the bottom should look much clearer than the water you poured in. Water treatment works use the same idea, with huge sand filters, and then they kill the germs with chemicals.

### subtopic_3 — Making Water Safer
**Assistant:** Why do girls often fetch the water?

**Tutor:** In many communities, in South Africa and around the world, women and girls have done most of the work of fetching water for a very long time. This comes from old ideas about which jobs belong to women and which to men. Women were usually responsible for the home, cooking and caring for children, and water was needed for all of these jobs. As a result, many girls have spent hours each day fetching water instead of being at school, studying or playing. This is one reason why bringing taps close to every home is so important: it gives girls and women more time for school, work and rest. Today, many people believe that fetching water, like other household work, should be shared fairly by everyone in the family, boys and men too. In Asanda's home, her older brother fetches water from the spring on weekends, and when the new taps arrive, the whole family will have more time.
