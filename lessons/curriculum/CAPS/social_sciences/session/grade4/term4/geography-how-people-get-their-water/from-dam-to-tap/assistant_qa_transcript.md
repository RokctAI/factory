### subtopic_1 — From the Dam to the Works
**Assistant:** Why does tap water smell like a swimming pool?

**Tutor:** Swimming pools and tap water both have chlorine in them, which is the chemical that kills germs. At the treatment works, a carefully measured, small amount of chlorine is added to the drinking water. Most of it kills the germs straight away, but a very small amount is left in the water on purpose. This leftover chlorine keeps on protecting the water as it travels for many kilometres through pipes and sits in reservoirs, so that any germs that get in along the way are killed before the water reaches your tap. The amount is tiny and safe to drink, but some people can smell or taste it, especially when the water is first poured. Swimming pools have much more chlorine, which is why they smell much stronger and why you should not drink pool water. If you do not like the smell, you can fill a jug with tap water and leave it in the fridge for a while, and much of the smell will go away.

### subtopic_2 — Cleaning the Water
**Assistant:** How does a water meter work?

**Tutor:** A water meter is a small machine fitted to the pipe that brings water into a home or building, usually near the front boundary, often in a small box in the ground. Inside it, there is a little wheel or fan. When water flows through the pipe, it turns the wheel, and the more water that flows, the more the wheel turns. The wheel is connected to a set of numbers, like a car's distance counter, which counts how much water has passed through, usually in kilolitres. One kilolitre is a thousand litres. Every month, someone from the municipality reads the numbers, or a smart meter sends them electronically. The difference between this month's reading and last month's reading shows how much water was used, and the bill is worked out from that. If all the taps in the house are closed but the meter is still turning, it means there is a leak somewhere, which should be fixed quickly.

### subtopic_3 — To the Tap and Beyond
**Assistant:** What happens at a wastewater treatment works?

**Tutor:** A wastewater treatment works cleans the dirty water that flows from our toilets, showers, kitchens and factories, so that it can be returned safely to rivers. First, screens remove rubbish, such as rags and plastic, which is why we should never flush anything except toilet paper. Then the water flows slowly through tanks where sand and grit sink. Next, it flows into large tanks where tiny living things called bacteria eat up most of the waste in the water, while air is bubbled through to keep them alive. After that, the water settles again, and the solid waste, called sludge, is removed. Finally, the cleaned water is often disinfected and tested before it is let out into a river. Some cities clean wastewater so well that it can be used again, for example to water parks and golf courses or in factories. When wastewater works break down or are overloaded, dirty water can flow into rivers, which harms people, fish and plants downstream.
