### subtopic_1 — Who Needs Water Trucks?
**Assistant:** How much water does a water truck carry?

**Tutor:** Water trucks come in different sizes. A small water tanker might carry about 5,000 litres, and a large one can carry 18,000 litres or even more. To picture this, think of a normal 20-litre container, like the ones Lutho carries. A large tanker with 18,000 litres would fill about 900 of those containers. If each person needs at least 25 litres a day, one large truckload could supply about 700 people for one day. Water is very heavy: every litre weighs about one kilogram, so 18,000 litres weighs about 18 tonnes, as heavy as several elephants. That is why water trucks need strong engines, strong brakes and good roads, and why they can get stuck on muddy dirt roads after rain. It is also why a community may need several truckloads every week, and why delivering water by truck is so expensive compared with sending it through pipes.

### subtopic_2 — How Water Is Delivered
**Assistant:** Why did the taps run dry in Makhanda?

**Tutor:** Makhanda had water problems for several reasons, which came together. First, the area suffered a long and severe drought, and the dams that supply the town became almost empty. Second, much of the town's water system was old. Some pipes were leaking, pumps broke down, and one of the water treatment works could not clean as much water as the town needed. Third, during power cuts, the pumps could not pump water up to the reservoirs that feed the taps. When there was too little water, it could not reach every part of town, and some areas, especially on the eastern side, often went without. Water trucks were used to bring water to the areas that went without. Since then, the government and the municipality have worked to upgrade the treatment works and fix the pipes. Makhanda's story shows that a town needs both enough rain and a well-looked-after water system to keep its taps running.

### subtopic_3 — Problems With Water Trucks
**Assistant:** What does a municipality do about water?

**Tutor:** A municipality is the local government of a town, a city or a district. In South Africa, municipalities are responsible for many basic services that people need every day, such as water, electricity, rubbish collection, roads and street lights. For water, the municipality must make sure that people have safe water to drink. It must build and look after the dams, pumps, treatment works, reservoirs and pipes that bring water to homes, and fix leaks and broken pipes. Where there are no taps yet, it must find other ways to supply water, such as boreholes, community taps or water trucks, and it must test the water to make sure it is safe. People pay the municipality for the water they use, but poor households get a certain amount of free basic water each month. If your water stops, an adult can phone the municipality's number to report the problem, so that it can be fixed.
