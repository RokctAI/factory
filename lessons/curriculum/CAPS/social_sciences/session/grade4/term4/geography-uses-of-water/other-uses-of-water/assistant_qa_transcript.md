### subtopic_1 — Farming
**Assistant:** How do cooling towers work?

**Tutor:** In a coal power station, coal is burned to boil water into very hot steam. The steam rushes through the turbines and makes them spin, and the spinning turbines make electricity. After that, the steam must be cooled and turned back into water, so that it can be boiled again. To do this, the power station uses cooling water, which flows in pipes past the hot steam and becomes warm. The warm cooling water is then sprayed inside the huge, curved cooling towers. Cool air flows in at the bottom of the tower and rises up through it, cooling the water as it falls. Some of the warm water evaporates and rises out of the top of the tower as a white cloud of water vapour. That white cloud is not smoke. The smoke from burning coal comes out of the tall, thin chimneys instead. Because some water evaporates, a power station needs a steady supply of new water from a dam or river.

### subtopic_2 — Factories, Mines and Electricity
**Assistant:** Why does farming use so much water?

**Tutor:** Farming uses so much water because plants need a lot of water to grow, and South Africa has large areas of crops. A plant takes up water through its roots, and much of it evaporates from its leaves, especially on hot, sunny days. A field of maize or wheat can use millions of litres of water in one season. In wetter areas, the rain provides most of this water. But in drier areas, such as the Northern Cape and parts of the Western Cape, farmers must irrigate their crops, often for months. Fruit trees, vineyards, sugar cane and vegetables are often irrigated. Water is also needed for animals and for washing and processing products. Because farming uses so much water, saving even a little water on each farm makes a big difference. That is why farmers use drip irrigation, water at night when less evaporates, and measure how wet their soil is before they water.

### subtopic_3 — Gardens and Recreation
**Assistant:** What is the Dusi Canoe Marathon?

**Tutor:** The Dusi Canoe Marathon is one of the most famous canoe races in South Africa, and one of the toughest in the world. It is held every year, usually in February, when the rivers are full after the summer rains. Paddlers race over three days, from Pietermaritzburg to Durban, a distance of more than one hundred kilometres. They paddle down the Msunduzi River and then the Umgeni River, past the Valley of a Thousand Hills, to the sea at Durban. The race is famous because the paddlers often have to jump out of their canoes and run with them on their shoulders, called portaging, to get around rapids, weirs and dams, or to take short cuts over the hills. The first race was held in 1951. Today, many paddlers from the communities along the rivers take part, and several local paddlers have become champions. The race also reminds people to keep these rivers clean.
