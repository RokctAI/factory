### subtopic_1 — Using Water and the Water Cycle
**Assistant:** How can I remember the water cycle?

**Tutor:** Think of one drop of water and tell its story, in four steps, like a little journey. First, the drop is lying in the warm sea, and the sun heats it until it rises up into the sky as invisible vapour, leaving its salt behind. That is evaporation: think of the drop escaping upwards. Second, high up where it is cold, the drop cools down and joins millions of other tiny drops to make a cloud. That is condensation: think of the drops crowding together. Third, the drops in the cloud join and grow heavy, until they fall as rain, hail or snow. That is precipitation: think of the drop falling. Fourth, the drop lands, runs into a stream, a river, a lake or soaks into the ground, and finally flows back to the sea. That is collection: think of the drop going home. Draw a circle with four arrows and a small picture for each step, and practise telling the story to someone at home.

### subtopic_2 — Storing and Getting Water
**Assistant:** How do water and communication fit together in one term?

**Tutor:** At first, they seem like very different topics, but they are linked in interesting ways. People have always needed to communicate about water. Long ago, communities passed on knowledge through stories about where to find springs and how to know when the rains would come. Today, communication helps people to manage water. Weather forecasts on radio, television and phones warn people about droughts and floods. Municipalities send messages to tell people when the water will be off or when a water truck will come. People can phone or use an app to report a leaking pipe. During the Cape Town drought, the city used websites, radio and social media to show how full the dams were and to ask people to save water, which helped Day Zero never come. So, good communication helps everyone share and look after water. When you revise, try to notice how ideas from different topics connect.

### subtopic_3 — Communication Through Time
**Assistant:** How should I prepare for the end-of-year test?

**Tutor:** Start early, and do a little revision every day, rather than everything the night before. Make a list of the topics: from Term 3, food and farming and transport through time, and from Term 4, water in South Africa and communication through time. For each topic, write a short summary in your own words, or make a mind map with pictures. Learn the important words and their meanings, such as irrigation, evaporation and telegraph. Make a timeline for transport and communication with the main dates. Practise answering questions, especially source-based questions, where you look at a picture, a map or a short text and answer questions about it. Read each question carefully and look at the mark allocation, which tells you how much to write. For the paragraph question, plan your main idea first, then write a few clear sentences with facts. Get enough sleep, eat breakfast, and stay calm. You have already learnt it all.
