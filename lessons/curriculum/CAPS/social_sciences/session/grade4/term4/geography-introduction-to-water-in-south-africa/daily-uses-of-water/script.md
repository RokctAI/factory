# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the Geography topic Water in South Africa. It explains the daily uses of water in our personal lives, why people need clean water, how much water a person uses, and how we can save water. The anchor is a Grade 4 class at a primary school in Cape Town. Their teacher, Ms Daniels, gives each learner a water diary, a page with the hours of the day down one side. For one whole day, they must write down every time they or their family use water, and roughly how much they use. The next morning, the learners compare their diaries. Yusuf is surprised at how often his family flushes the toilet. Lindiwe's family filled two buckets from the tap in the yard. Ms Daniels then tells them a true story. In 2018, after three very dry years, the dams that supply Cape Town were nearly empty, and the city warned that one day, called Day Zero, the taps might be turned off. People were asked to use only 50 litres of water each a day. The big idea is that we use water all day long, that we cannot live without it, and that every person can help to save it. Explaining the daily uses of water in our personal lives is examinable.

## Subtopic: Why We Need Water

All living things need water: people, animals and plants. Without food, a person can live for a few weeks, but without water, a person can live for only a few days.

About two thirds of the human body is water. It is in our blood, our muscles, our brains and even our bones. Our bodies lose water all day, when we breathe out, sweat and go to the toilet, so we must drink water every day to replace it. Children should drink several glasses of water a day, and more when it is hot or when they play sport.

Water helps our bodies in many ways. It carries food and oxygen around the body in our blood. It helps us to digest our food. It keeps our bodies cool when we sweat. It helps us to think and concentrate. When we do not drink enough, we feel tired, get headaches and become thirsty. This is called dehydration.

The water we drink must be clean and safe. Dirty water can carry germs that cause illnesses, such as diarrhoea and cholera. Clean water, together with washing our hands with soap, protects us from many diseases. In South Africa, the Constitution says that everyone has the right to have access to enough water.

The questions for this section are with you now: why people need water and why it must be clean.

## Subtopic: How We Use Water Every Day

The water diaries show many daily uses of water.

We use water for drinking, either straight from the tap or in tea, coffee and other drinks.

We use water for cooking. We boil water to cook pap, rice and vegetables, and we wash our fruit and vegetables before we eat them.

We use water for personal hygiene, which means keeping our bodies clean. We wash our hands, bath or shower, brush our teeth and wash our hair.

We use water to wash our clothes, either in a washing machine or by hand in a basin, and to wash our dishes after meals.

We use water to clean our homes, to wipe tables and to wash floors.

Many homes use water to flush the toilet. Each flush of an older toilet uses about nine to twelve litres of clean water.

We use water to water our gardens and to give our pets and farm animals water to drink. Some families also use water to wash the car, to fill a paddling pool, or to make ice and cool drinks on hot days.

Ms Daniels shows how much water some activities use. A shower uses about ten litres every minute, so a five-minute shower uses about fifty litres. A full bath can use eighty litres or more. Leaving the tap running while you brush your teeth can waste several litres a minute.

The questions for this section are with you now: the daily uses of water and how much water they use.

## Subtopic: Saving Water at Home

South Africa is a dry country. Many parts get little rain, and droughts happen often. So water is precious, and everyone should use it wisely.

During the Cape Town drought, people found many ways to save water. They took short showers, standing in a bucket to catch the water. They used grey water, which is water that has already been used for washing, to flush toilets and water gardens. They flushed the toilet less often. They put a bottle filled with water in the toilet cistern, so each flush used less. They fixed leaking taps and pipes. A tap that drips all day can waste many litres. Together, the people of Cape Town cut their water use by about half, and Day Zero never came.

Here are other ways to save water. Turn off the tap while you brush your teeth or soap your hands. Wash dishes in a basin, not under running water. Only run a washing machine when it is full. Catch rain water from the roof in a tank, and water gardens in the early morning or evening, when less water evaporates.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking water is only for drinking; we use it for cooking, washing, cleaning and flushing. Two: thinking any water is safe to drink; dirty water can carry germs. Three: thinking a dripping tap wastes almost nothing; it can waste many litres a day. Four: thinking one person cannot save water; Cape Town saved water together.

The questions for this section are with you now: grey water, Day Zero and ways to save water.
