### subtopic_1 — Rain, Streams and Rivers
**Assistant:** Why are the Drakensberg called a water tower?

**Tutor:** The Drakensberg and the mountains of Lesotho next to them are called a water tower because so much of South Africa's fresh water starts there. These mountains are very high, so when moist air from the Indian Ocean rises over them, it cools, and a lot of rain falls, and in winter there is often snow on the peaks. The high slopes are covered with grasslands and wetlands that soak up the rain and release it slowly into many streams. Some of South Africa's most important rivers begin in or near these mountains, including the Orange River, the Tugela River and tributaries of the Vaal River. These rivers carry water to farms, towns, factories and cities far away, even to Gauteng. A small area of high mountains provides a large share of the country's river water. That is why it is so important to protect these mountains, their grasslands and their wetlands from damage, fires that burn out of control and pollution.

### subtopic_2 — Wetlands and Lakes
**Assistant:** Is spring water always safe to drink?

**Tutor:** Spring water is often clean, because as it trickles slowly down through layers of sand and rock, much of the dirt is filtered out. That is why many people enjoy drinking from mountain springs, like the class in the Drakensberg did, and why some bottled water comes from springs. But spring water is not always safe. If there are toilets, rubbish dumps, farm animals, fertilisers or mines nearby, harmful germs or chemicals can seep into the groundwater and come out at the spring. Some springs also have a lot of natural minerals, which can make the water taste odd or be unhealthy to drink every day. You cannot always see, smell or taste germs. So, if you are not sure whether a spring is safe, it is better to boil the water for at least a minute, or to treat it, before drinking it. In many communities, springs are protected with concrete boxes and pipes to keep them clean.

### subtopic_3 — Water Under the Ground
**Assistant:** What is a World Heritage Site?

**Tutor:** A World Heritage Site is a place that is so special that it is important to all the people of the world, not only to the country where it is found. These places are chosen by an organisation of the United Nations called UNESCO. Some are natural places, such as mountains, forests, wetlands and coral reefs, and some are cultural places, made or used by people, such as old cities, buildings and rock art. When a place becomes a World Heritage Site, the country agrees to protect it for the future. South Africa has several World Heritage Sites. The iSimangaliso Wetland Park in KwaZulu-Natal was one of the first three places in South Africa to be chosen, in 1999. It has lakes, swamps, beaches, coral reefs and forests, and it is home to hippos, crocodiles, flamingos and many other animals. Other World Heritage Sites in South Africa include Robben Island, the Cradle of Humankind, Mapungubwe and the Maloti-Drakensberg Park.
