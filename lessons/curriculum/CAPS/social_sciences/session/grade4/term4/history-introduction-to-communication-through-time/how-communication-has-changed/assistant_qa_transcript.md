### subtopic_1 — What Is Communication?
**Assistant:** What is a praise poem?

**Tutor:** A praise poem is a poem that is spoken or chanted aloud to honour a person, a family, a clan, an animal or an event. In isiZulu, praise poems are called izibongo, and in Sesotho, they are called dithoko. A praise poet, called an imbongi in isiZulu and isiXhosa, performs the poem in a strong, loud voice, often moving and wearing traditional dress. Long ago, praise poets lived close to kings and chiefs. Their poems told the story of the leader's family, his brave deeds and his mistakes, so they were a way of remembering history before it was written down. Praise poets were also allowed to say things that others could not, so they sometimes gently criticised leaders. Today, praise poets still perform at weddings, funerals, graduations and big national events. A famous example is the imbongi Zolani Mkiva, who performed at President Nelson Mandela's inauguration in 1994.

### subtopic_2 — Communication Long Ago
**Assistant:** How old are the San rock paintings?

**Tutor:** San rock art in southern Africa is very old. Some rock paintings are a few hundred years old, and others are thousands of years old. In the Drakensberg, many paintings are thought to be up to about three thousand years old, and in other parts of southern Africa, some rock art is much older than that. Scientists can work out the age of some paintings by testing tiny samples of the paint, which can contain charcoal, or the layers of rock and dirt around them. The San painted with paints made from natural materials, such as red and yellow ochre, which are coloured soils, charcoal for black, and white clay, mixed with things like animal fat, blood or plant juices. Their paintings show animals, especially the eland, people hunting and dancing, and scenes that were important in their beliefs. The uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park has one of the largest collections of rock art in the world, and it is protected as a World Heritage Site.

### subtopic_3 — Change and Continuity
**Assistant:** Why is face-to-face talking still important?

**Tutor:** Even with cell phones, video calls and the internet, talking face to face is still one of the most important ways to communicate. When you talk to someone in person, you do not only hear their words. You also see their face, their eyes, their smile and their body language, and you hear the tone of their voice, which show how they really feel. This helps people to understand each other better and to avoid misunderstandings, which can easily happen with short text messages. Face-to-face conversations help to build friendships and trust. In many South African cultures, greeting people properly in person, asking about their family and showing respect to elders are very important. Some important things, such as comforting a sad friend, solving an argument or learning from a teacher, often work best in person. That is why, even in the time of smartphones, families still gather, like Palesa's family on a Sunday afternoon, to talk and share stories.
