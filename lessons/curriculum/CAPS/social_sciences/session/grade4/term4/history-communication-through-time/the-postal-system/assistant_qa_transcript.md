### subtopic_1 — How the Post Began
**Assistant:** Why were the Cape triangle stamps triangular?

**Tutor:** The Cape of Good Hope's first stamps, issued in 1853, were shaped like triangles, which was very unusual at the time. There are a few stories about why. One explanation often given is that the triangle shape helped post office workers to tell Cape stamps apart from stamps of other places at a glance. Whatever the reason, the shape made them famous. They show a seated woman called Hope, leaning on an anchor, which was a symbol of the Cape of Good Hope. The stamps were printed in different colours for different prices. Today, Cape triangle stamps are known to stamp collectors all over the world, and some rare ones are very valuable. Collecting stamps is called philately. Stamps are like small history lessons, because they show the animals, plants, leaders and events that a country wanted to celebrate at the time.

### subtopic_2 — Stamps, Addresses and Sorting
**Assistant:** What is a pen pal?

**Tutor:** A pen pal, or pen friend, is a person you write letters to regularly, usually someone who lives in another town or country and whom you have not met in person. Pen pals tell each other about their lives, their families, their schools, their hobbies, their food and their culture, and they sometimes send photographs, drawings or postcards. Having a pen pal is a fun way to learn about other places and other ways of life, and to practise writing. Many schools organise pen pals between classes in different provinces or countries, like the class in Mossel Bay and the class in Polokwane. In the past, pen pals had to wait weeks for each letter, which made every letter exciting. Today, some pen pals write by email, but many people still enjoy writing and receiving real letters on paper. If you have a pen pal, an adult should always help you, so that you only share safe information.

### subtopic_3 — The Post Today
**Assistant:** How do I address an envelope properly?

**Tutor:** On the front of the envelope, in the middle, write the receiver's details, each on a new line. First, write the person's name, for example, Lerato Nkosi. On the next line, write the house number and street, or the post office box number, for example, 12 Jacaranda Street, or PO Box 345. On the next line, write the suburb or township, if there is one. On the next line, write the town or city. On the last line, write the postal code, which has four numbers in South Africa. Write clearly, so that the sorting machines and postal workers can read it. Then stick the stamp in the top right corner. On the back of the envelope, or in the top left corner of the front, write your own name and address as the sender. This is called the return address, so that the letter can be sent back to you if it cannot be delivered. Seal the envelope well before posting it.
