# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the History topic for Term 2, learning from leaders. It explains what a leader is, the many kinds of leaders in families, schools, communities and countries, and the different ways people become leaders. The anchor is a Grade 4 classroom in Pietermaritzburg, the capital of KwaZulu-Natal, at the start of the term. The class must choose a class captain. Before the vote, the teacher writes a question on the board: what makes a good leader? The learners call out ideas: someone strong, someone clever, someone kind, someone who listens, someone popular. The teacher writes them all down and says: by the end of this topic, we will know much more. She also tells the class a story about a young lawyer who was thrown off a train at the Pietermaritzburg railway station in 1893, and who later became one of the most famous leaders in the world. The big idea is that leaders are people who guide others towards a goal, that there are leaders all around us, and that we can learn from the lives of good leaders. Understanding what a leader is, and the kinds of leaders, is examinable.

## Subtopic: What Is a Leader?

A leader is a person who guides or directs a group of people towards a goal. The people who follow a leader are called followers. A leader helps a group to decide what to do, and then helps them to do it.

Every group needs some kind of leadership. A soccer team needs a captain to encourage the players and make decisions on the field. A class needs a captain to help the teacher and speak for the class. A country needs a government and a president to make laws and look after the people.

Leadership is not the same as being the boss and giving orders. A leader who only shouts orders, and does not care about the people, is not a good leader. A good leader thinks about what is good for the whole group, not only for themselves.

The class in Pietermaritzburg talks about their ideas. Is the strongest person always the best leader? No. Is the most popular person always the best leader? Not always. A good leader may be quiet, but fair, honest and hard-working. A good leader listens to others, works well with a team, is brave, and is willing to give up things for the good of others. We will learn about each of these qualities in the next lessons.

The young lawyer thrown off the train in 1893 was Mohandas Gandhi, later called Mahatma Gandhi. He was told to leave a first-class carriage because he was not white, even though he had a ticket. He refused to move and was put off the train. This unfair treatment helped him decide to lead peaceful protests against unjust laws, first in South Africa and later in India.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a leader and followers are, and leadership.

## Subtopic: Leaders All Around Us

There are leaders in every part of our lives.

At home, parents, grandparents and guardians lead the family. They make decisions, set rules and look after the children.

At school, the principal leads the school. Teachers lead their classes. Learners can be leaders too: class captains, sports captains, prefects, and leaders of clubs.

In the community, there are many leaders. Religious leaders, such as ministers, priests, imams and rabbis, lead their congregations. Traditional leaders, such as kings, queens and chiefs, called amakhosi in isiZulu, lead their communities and look after their customs. Councillors are elected to lead wards in towns and cities. Leaders of organisations, such as a clinic committee or a youth club, lead groups of people who work together.

In the country, the President leads the national government. The premier leads each province. Members of Parliament make laws.

Some leaders are famous all over the world, such as Nelson Mandela, Mahatma Gandhi and Archbishop Desmond Tutu. Others are famous only in their own village or street. Both kinds of leaders matter.

Leaders can be men or women, young or old. Albertina Sisulu, a nurse, was a great leader for many South Africans. In 1956, women like Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph, Rahima Moosa and Sophia Williams-De Bruyn led twenty thousand women in a march to the Union Buildings, protesting against unfair laws.

The questions for this section are with you now: leaders at home, at school, in the community and in the country.

## Subtopic: How People Become Leaders

People become leaders in different ways.

Some people are born into leadership. In many African communities, a king or chief inherits leadership from a parent. A king's son or daughter may become the next king or queen. These are called hereditary leaders.

Some people are elected, which means chosen by a vote. The class captain in Pietermaritzburg will be elected by the learners. In a democracy, such as South Africa, adults vote in elections to choose leaders, such as councillors, members of Parliament and, through Parliament, the President. If people are unhappy with an elected leader, they can choose someone else at the next election.

Some people are appointed, which means chosen by someone in charge. A principal is appointed by the education department. A team coach chooses a captain.

And some people become leaders because of what they do. When they see something unfair or a problem that needs fixing, they stand up and act, and other people follow them. Many of the leaders who fought for freedom in South Africa became leaders in this way.

Some leaders are good, and some are bad. A bad leader might be selfish, unfair, dishonest or cruel. We study good leaders so that we can learn from them, and so that we can choose good leaders ourselves.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a leader is just a boss who gives orders; a good leader works for the good of the group. Two: thinking only adults can be leaders; learners can be leaders too. Three: thinking the most popular person is always the best leader; a good leader is fair, honest and caring. Four: thinking all leaders are elected; some are born into leadership, some are appointed, and some step up.

The questions for this section are with you now: hereditary, elected and appointed leaders, and good and bad leaders.
