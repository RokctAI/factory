### subtopic_1 — What Is a Leader?
**Assistant:** Can a quiet person be a good leader?

**Tutor:** Yes, absolutely. Many people think a leader must be loud, confident and always talking, but that is not true. Some of the best leaders are quiet people. A quiet leader often listens very carefully, so they understand what everyone in the group needs. They think before they speak, so when they do speak, people pay attention. They lead by example, which means they show others what to do by doing it themselves: arriving on time, working hard, being kind and being fair. People trust them because they are calm and honest. Mahatma Gandhi was a small, gentle man with a soft voice, yet millions of people followed him. Being a leader is not about being the loudest. It is about caring for the group, making good choices, and helping everyone do their best. So if you are a quiet person, do not think that leadership is not for you. Your way of leading may be just what a group needs.

### subtopic_2 — Leaders All Around Us
**Assistant:** What is a traditional leader?

**Tutor:** A traditional leader is a leader of a community according to its customs and history, rather than by being elected in a modern election. In South Africa, traditional leaders include kings and queens, such as the Zulu king, and chiefs, called amakhosi in isiZulu, dikgosi in Setswana, and by other names in other languages. Many traditional leaders inherit their position from their family. They help to settle disputes between people, look after customs and ceremonies, and in some areas help decide how land is used. They work alongside elected leaders, such as councillors. South Africa's Constitution recognises traditional leaders and their role, and there are houses of traditional leaders in several provinces and at national level, where they meet and give advice to government. Traditional leaders have played important roles in South African history, both in leading their people and, for some, in the struggle for freedom. Chief Albert Luthuli, for example, was a traditional leader who won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1960.

### subtopic_3 — How People Become Leaders
**Assistant:** Why did twenty thousand women march to the Union Buildings in 1956?

**Tutor:** They marched to protest against the pass laws. Under these unfair laws, Black men already had to carry a document called a pass at all times, showing where they were allowed to live and work. If they did not have it, they could be arrested. In 1956, the government said that Black women would also have to carry passes. Women across the country were angry, because passes made it hard to move freely, find work and keep families together. On the 9th of August 1956, about twenty thousand women of all races marched to the Union Buildings in Pretoria. Their leaders, Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph, Rahima Moosa and Sophia Williams-De Bruyn, delivered thousands of signed petitions to the office of the Prime Minister. The women stood in silence, then sang a song with the words: you strike a woman, you strike a rock. Today, the 9th of August is National Women's Day, a public holiday that honours their courage.
