### subtopic_1 — Views, Symbols, Grids and Directions
**Assistant:** How can I learn the nine provinces and their capitals?

**Tutor:** Here are some tricks that many learners find helpful. First, learn them in groups by where they are on the map, instead of as one long list. The three Capes in the south and west: Northern Cape, Kimberley; Western Cape, Cape Town; Eastern Cape, Bhisho. The east coast: KwaZulu-Natal, Pietermaritzburg. The middle: Free State, Bloemfontein. The north: North West, Mahikeng; Gauteng, Johannesburg; Limpopo, Polokwane; Mpumalanga, Mbombela. Second, draw a blank map of South Africa and fill in the provinces and capitals from memory, then check, and do it again a few days later. Third, make flash cards, with the province on one side and the capital on the other, and quiz a friend or family member. Fourth, link each one to something you know, such as Mpumalanga, place where the sun rises, in the east. Little and often works better than one long study session the night before the test.

### subtopic_2 — The Map of South Africa
**Assistant:** How do I write a good paragraph in a Social Sciences test?

**Tutor:** A paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea. In the test, read the question carefully and underline the key words, so you know exactly what to write about. Start with a topic sentence that says what the paragraph is about, for example: Nelson Mandela was a good leader for several reasons. Then write three or four sentences that give facts and examples to support your topic sentence, such as: he listened to people, he was brave, and he chose peace instead of revenge. Use the key words you learnt, such as courage, sacrifice and commitment. Try to explain, not only list. End with a concluding sentence that sums up your idea, such as: this is why many people see him as an example of a good leader. Write in full sentences, in your own words, and check your spelling at the end. A short, clear paragraph with good facts is better than a long, messy one.

### subtopic_3 — Learning from Leaders
**Assistant:** How do map skills and leaders fit together in one term?

**Tutor:** They seem very different, but they can connect. Leaders often need maps. Mapmakers have helped leaders plan roads, schools and clinics, and decide where to send help after floods or droughts. The provinces we learnt about on the map of South Africa were created in 1994, when leaders like Nelson Mandela helped to build a democratic country, and each province has its own leader, the premier. The places in Mandela's life story can all be found on a map: Mvezo and Qunu in the Eastern Cape, Johannesburg in Gauteng, Robben Island off Cape Town in the Western Cape, and the Union Buildings in Pretoria. You could even draw a map of Mandela's life, with symbols, a key and a north arrow, and mark each place with a grid reference. Linking the two topics like this is a great way to remember both. History happens in places, and geography helps us understand where history happened.
