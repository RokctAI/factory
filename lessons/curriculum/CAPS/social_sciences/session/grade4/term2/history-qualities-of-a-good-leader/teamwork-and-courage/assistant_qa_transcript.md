### subtopic_1 — A Good Leader Works with a Team
**Assistant:** What is exile?

**Tutor:** Exile means having to live outside your own country, because it is not safe for you to stay, or because you are not allowed to come back. During apartheid, many South Africans who fought for freedom went into exile. If they had stayed, they would probably have been arrested, put in prison for many years, or even killed. Oliver Tambo left South Africa in 1960, after the ANC was banned, and he lived in exile for about thirty years, mostly in Zambia, Tanzania and the United Kingdom. From there, he travelled to many countries, asking other governments to put pressure on the apartheid government. Living in exile was hard. People were far from their families, their homes and their languages, and many never saw their parents again. Tambo returned to South Africa in 1990, after the ANC was unbanned, but he was already ill, and he died in 1993, a year before the first democratic election. Johannesburg's main airport is named after him.

### subtopic_2 — A Good Leader Has Courage
**Assistant:** Why did Helen Suzman keep speaking when everyone else disagreed?

**Tutor:** Helen Suzman believed that apartheid was wrong, and that it was her duty as a member of Parliament to say so, even if she was the only one. She said that she did not need to win every vote to make a difference. By speaking, she made sure that the truth about unfair laws was written down in the records of Parliament and reported in the newspapers, so people in South Africa and the rest of the world knew about it. She also asked many questions in Parliament about how people were being treated in prisons and in townships, which forced the government to give answers. She was white and lived a comfortable life, so she could have stayed quiet and been safe. Instead, she chose to use her position to speak for people who had no vote and no voice in Parliament. Nelson Mandela later thanked her for her courage. She was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize twice.

### subtopic_3 — Teamwork and Courage Together
**Assistant:** How can I show courage at school?

**Tutor:** You do not have to do something huge to show courage. Everyday courage matters just as much. You can show courage by putting up your hand to answer a question, even when you are not sure if your answer is right. You can show courage by telling a teacher when someone is being bullied, even if you are worried that the bully might be angry with you. You can sit with a new learner or a learner that others ignore. You can say sorry when you have made a mistake, which can be very hard. You can try out for a team or a play, even if you are nervous. You can say no when your friends want you to do something wrong. Each time you do something right while feeling a little afraid, you are practising courage, and it gets easier with practice. Remember that courage is not about having no fear. It is about not letting fear stop you from doing what is right.
