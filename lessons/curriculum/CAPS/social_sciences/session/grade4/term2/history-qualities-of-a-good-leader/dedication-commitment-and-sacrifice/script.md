# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers three more qualities of a good leader: a good leader is dedicated, a good leader is committed to others, and a good leader is prepared to sacrifice for others. The anchor is a visit by a Grade 4 class from KwaDukuza to the Luthuli Museum in Groutville, on the north coast of KwaZulu-Natal. The museum is in the house where Chief Albert Luthuli lived. In the rooms are his books, his desk, his chief's walking stick and a copy of the Nobel Peace Prize medal. Their own teacher, Mrs Dlamini, has taught at the village school for thirty years, and on Saturdays she gives extra lessons to learners who are struggling, without being paid. On the bus home, a learner says: Mrs Dlamini is a bit like Chief Luthuli. The big idea is that good leaders are dedicated to their work, committed to the people they serve, and willing to give up things they value for the good of others. Explaining these qualities, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: Dedication

Dedication means giving a lot of time and effort to something, and not giving up, even when it is hard or when it takes a very long time.

A dedicated leader keeps working towards the goal, year after year. They do not stop when things go wrong, or when they get tired, or when nobody is watching.

Mrs Dlamini is dedicated. For thirty years, she has arrived at school early, prepared her lessons, marked books late into the night, and helped learners who find school hard. She has taught the parents of some of the children in her class today.

Albert Luthuli was dedicated too. He was born around 1898 and grew up in Groutville. He trained as a teacher and taught for many years at Adams College, near Durban. In 1936, the people of Groutville asked him to become their chief, and he left teaching to serve them. He worked hard to help the sugar cane farmers of the area and to settle problems in the community.

Later, Luthuli became the leader of the ANC, the African National Congress, which was fighting peacefully for equal rights for all South Africans. For many years, he travelled, spoke and wrote to persuade people that apartheid was wrong and must end. He never gave up, even when the government made life very hard for him.

The questions for this section are with you now: what dedication means, Mrs Dlamini, and Albert Luthuli's dedication.

## Subtopic: Commitment to Others

Commitment means making a promise to do something, and then keeping that promise, even when it becomes difficult. A leader who is committed to others keeps their promises to the people they lead, and stays loyal to them.

A committed leader does not change sides when things get tough. People can trust a committed leader, because they know the leader will not let them down.

Albert Luthuli was deeply committed to the people of South Africa and to freedom for everyone. He believed in peaceful ways of protest, and he believed that all South Africans, Black and white, could one day live together as equals. He stayed true to these beliefs his whole life.

Mahatma Gandhi was also committed to his people. Gandhi lived in South Africa for twenty-one years, from 1893 to 1914. He was committed to helping Indian South Africans who were treated unfairly by unjust laws. He developed a peaceful way of protesting, called satyagraha, which means holding on to truth. People who followed him broke unfair laws peacefully and accepted going to prison, without fighting back. Later, in India, he led millions of people in peaceful protests until India became independent from Britain in 1947.

At school, commitment means things like turning up to every practice when you have joined a team, or finishing the job you promised to do in a group project.

The questions for this section are with you now: commitment, keeping promises, and Gandhi's peaceful protest.

## Subtopic: Sacrifice

Sacrifice means giving up something that is important to you, such as your time, your money, your comfort, your safety or even your freedom, to help others.

Good leaders are prepared to sacrifice for others. They put the needs of the people ahead of their own comfort.

In 1952, the government told Albert Luthuli that he must choose: either leave the ANC, or lose his position as chief. Being chief was a great honour and gave him a home and an income. But he refused to leave the ANC, because he was committed to the struggle for freedom. So the government removed him as chief. He said that the road to freedom was hard, but he would walk it. After that, he was banned many times and forced to stay in the Groutville area for years, away from the people he wanted to lead.

In 1961, Albert Luthuli received the Nobel Peace Prize for 1960, for his peaceful struggle against apartheid. He was the first person from Africa to receive this prize. The government only allowed him to leave the country for a few days to travel to Norway and collect it. He also wrote a book about his life, called Let My People Go. He died in 1967, still banned, but his ideas lived on.

Gandhi also made sacrifices. He was sent to prison several times in South Africa, and later in India. He gave up a comfortable life as a lawyer to live very simply.

Mrs Dlamini sacrifices her Saturdays. That is a smaller sacrifice, but it is still a sacrifice for others.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking dedication means doing something only when it is easy; it means not giving up when it is hard. Two: thinking commitment means making promises; it means keeping them. Three: thinking sacrifice is only for famous people; ordinary people sacrifice time and comfort for others every day. Four: thinking Gandhi only lived in India; he lived in South Africa for twenty-one years.

The questions for this section are with you now: sacrifice, and the sacrifices of Luthuli, Gandhi and ordinary people.
