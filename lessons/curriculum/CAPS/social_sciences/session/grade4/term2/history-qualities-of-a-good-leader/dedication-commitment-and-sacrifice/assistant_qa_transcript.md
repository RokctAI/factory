### subtopic_1 — Dedication
**Assistant:** What is the Nobel Peace Prize?

**Tutor:** The Nobel Peace Prize is one of the most famous prizes in the world. It is given each year to a person or an organisation that has done outstanding work for peace. The prize was started by Alfred Nobel, a Swedish scientist who invented dynamite and became very rich. Before he died in 1896, he left most of his money to create five prizes, for physics, chemistry, medicine, literature and peace. The Peace Prize is awarded in Oslo, in Norway. Winners receive a gold medal, a certificate and a large sum of money. South Africa has had several winners of the Nobel Peace Prize: Albert Luthuli for 1960, Desmond Tutu in 1984, and Nelson Mandela and F.W. de Klerk together in 1993. That is remarkable for one country. Each of them was honoured for working to end apartheid, or to bring peace between people, without more violence.

### subtopic_2 — Commitment to Others
**Assistant:** How did the Satyagraha protests work?

**Tutor:** Satyagraha was Gandhi's way of fighting unfair laws without using violence. The word means holding on to truth, or truth-force. Here is how it worked. First, the people would explain clearly and politely why a law was unfair, and ask the government to change it. If the government refused, the people would break the unfair law on purpose, openly and peacefully. For example, in South Africa, Indian people had to carry registration certificates, which they felt were insulting. In 1908, Gandhi and his followers burnt their certificates in a big fire at a mosque in Johannesburg. When the police came to arrest them, they did not fight back or run away. They accepted going to prison. The idea was that when thousands of peaceful people are willing to go to prison, the unfairness of the law becomes clear to everyone, and the government is shamed into changing it. This idea later inspired leaders all over the world.

### subtopic_3 — Sacrifice
**Assistant:** Is it ever wrong to sacrifice too much?

**Tutor:** That is a very wise question. Sacrifice for others is a good quality, but a leader also needs to look after their own health and their family, so that they can keep helping. A leader who never sleeps, never eats properly and never rests may become ill and unable to lead at all. Many leaders who sacrificed a lot also said that their families paid a heavy price. Their children grew up without them, and their husbands or wives faced hard times alone. Albert Luthuli, Albertina Sisulu and Nelson Mandela all spoke about the pain this caused their families. So good leaders try to find a balance. They give generously, but they also accept help, share the work with a team, and take care of themselves. For you, sacrifice might mean giving up some playtime to help a friend with homework. That is wonderful. But it does not mean you should never play again.
