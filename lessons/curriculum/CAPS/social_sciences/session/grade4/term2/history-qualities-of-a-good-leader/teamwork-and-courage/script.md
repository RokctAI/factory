# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers two more qualities of a good leader: a good leader works with a team, and a good leader has courage and is brave. The anchor is a primary school netball match in Rustenburg, in North West. At half-time, the Grade 4 team is losing badly. Some players are blaming each other, and one girl is almost in tears. The captain, Naledi, does not shout at anyone. She calls the team into a circle, asks the defenders what is going wrong, gives every player a clear job, and reminds them that they win or lose together. In the second half, the team plays as one, and they win by two goals. Later, Naledi admits she was scared when she spoke up to the older girls on her team. Her teacher tells her that this is what courage is. The big idea is that good leaders work together with others as a team, and that they show courage by doing what is right, even when it is hard or frightening. Explaining these qualities, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: A Good Leader Works with a Team

A team is a group of people working together towards the same goal. Teamwork means each person does their part, and everyone helps each other.

A good leader knows that no one can do everything alone. A leader brings people together, listens to their ideas, gives each person a job that suits them, and helps the team to trust each other. When the team succeeds, a good leader shares the praise. When something goes wrong, a good leader does not blame others, but helps the team to fix it.

Naledi did this at half-time. She asked her teammates for ideas, gave each player a clear job, and stopped the blaming. The team won because everyone worked together.

Siya Kolisi is a good example of a leader who builds a team. He grew up in a poor township called Zwide, near Gqeberha, and sometimes went to bed hungry. In 2018 he became the first Black captain of the Springboks, South Africa's national rugby team. He led the Springboks to win the Rugby World Cup in 2019, and again in 2023. He always said that the team won because the players, coming from very different backgrounds, worked together for one goal.

Oliver Tambo was a leader who believed in teamwork. He worked closely with his friends Nelson Mandela and Walter Sisulu. When the ANC was banned in 1960, Tambo left South Africa to lead the organisation from other countries, in exile, for about thirty years. He kept many different people and groups working together towards the goal of freedom.

The questions for this section are with you now: teams, teamwork, and Siya Kolisi and Oliver Tambo.

## Subtopic: A Good Leader Has Courage

Courage means being brave. It means doing what is right, even when you are afraid, or when it is hard, dangerous or unpopular.

Courage does not mean having no fear. Naledi was scared to speak up to the older girls, but she did it anyway, because she knew it would help the team. That is courage.

Leaders often need courage, because leading sometimes means standing up against unfairness, making a hard decision, or saying something that people do not want to hear.

Helen Suzman showed great courage. She was a member of Parliament during apartheid. For thirteen years, from 1961 to 1974, she was the only member of her party in Parliament. Almost every other member supported apartheid. Again and again, she stood up alone to speak against unfair laws, such as the pass laws and detention without trial, even though other members shouted at her and tried to make fun of her. She also visited prisons, including Robben Island, where she met Nelson Mandela, and she spoke out about the bad conditions there.

Courage can also be quiet. A learner who tells a teacher that a classmate is being bullied, or who sits with a child that everyone is ignoring, is showing courage.

The questions for this section are with you now: what courage is, Helen Suzman, and everyday courage.

## Subtopic: Teamwork and Courage Together

Teamwork and courage often go together. It is easier to be brave when you have a team behind you. And a team needs a brave leader to keep going when things are hard.

In 1956, twenty thousand women marched together to the Union Buildings in Pretoria to protest against the pass laws. It took great courage, because protesting against the government was dangerous. But they were not alone. They marched as a team, and their leaders, Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph, Rahima Moosa and Sophia Williams-De Bruyn, were brave enough to walk at the front.

Lilian Ngoyi was a factory worker who became one of the most powerful speakers in the struggle against apartheid. She was arrested and banned for many years, but she never gave up. She became the first woman elected to the national executive committee of the ANC, and she was known as Ma Ngoyi. Her courage gave courage to thousands of other women.

A brave leader also needs to know when to listen to the team. Courage is not the same as being reckless, which means taking silly risks. A good leader thinks carefully, listens to others and then acts bravely.

At school, you can practise teamwork and courage every day: by doing your part in a group project, by encouraging a teammate who is struggling, and by standing up for what is right.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a good leader does everything alone; a good leader works with a team. Two: thinking a good leader blames the team when things go wrong; a good leader helps fix the problem. Three: thinking courage means having no fear; courage means acting rightly even when you are afraid. Four: thinking courage means taking silly risks; courage is thinking carefully, then doing what is right.

The questions for this section are with you now: teamwork and courage together, and the women's march of 1956.
