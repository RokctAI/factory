# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers three qualities of a good leader: a good leader listens to people, a good leader is a servant of the people, and a good leader works for the good of others. The anchor is a visit by a Grade 4 class in Gqeberha, in the Eastern Cape, to a soup kitchen in their neighbourhood. It is run by a grandmother everyone calls Mama Nomsa, from her garage. Every weekday afternoon, she and a team of volunteers cook a huge pot of soup and hand out bowls to children and older people who do not have enough to eat. Mama Nomsa is not famous, and nobody elected her, but the whole street respects her as a leader. Back in class, the teacher tells the learners about two famous South African leaders who had the same qualities, Albertina Sisulu and Archbishop Desmond Tutu. The big idea is that good leaders listen to the people they lead, see themselves as servants of those people, and work for the good of others rather than for themselves. Explaining these qualities, with examples, is examinable.

## Subtopic: A Good Leader Listens

A good leader listens to people. Listening means paying careful attention to what someone is saying, trying to understand their feelings and needs, and not interrupting.

Why is listening so important for a leader? Because a leader cannot help people if they do not know what the people need. When Mama Nomsa started the soup kitchen, she did not just decide on her own what to do. She walked down the street, knocked on doors and asked families what they needed most. Many said their children were going to school hungry. So she started with soup in the afternoons, when children come home.

A leader who does not listen may make decisions that do not help anyone, or that even hurt people. A leader who listens makes people feel respected and heard, and that makes them trust the leader.

Archbishop Desmond Tutu was a great listener. After apartheid ended, he led a group called the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. For more than two years, from 1996, he and the other members listened to thousands of people tell their stories of what had happened to them and their families during apartheid. Some stories were so painful that Tutu wept. By listening, he helped the country face the truth about the past, so that people could begin to heal and live together. Listening did not fix everything, but it showed people that their pain mattered.

The questions for this section are with you now: what listening is, why leaders must listen, and Desmond Tutu.

## Subtopic: A Servant of the People

A good leader is a servant of the people. This might sound strange, because we often think that people serve the leader. But a good leader believes it is the other way around: the leader's job is to serve the people.

A servant leader puts the needs of the people first. They do not use their position to become rich, to get special treatment or to boss others around. They see leadership as a responsibility, not a reward.

Mama Nomsa does not take any soup until everyone in the queue has been served. She does not get paid. She uses her own garage and much of her own time.

Albertina Sisulu was a servant leader. She trained as a nurse and worked for many years as a nurse and midwife in Soweto, helping mothers give birth and caring for the sick, often at night and far from home. Her house in Soweto was always open to people who needed help. At the same time, she was a leader in the struggle against apartheid. She was arrested many times and banned for years, which meant she was not allowed to leave her home area or meet with groups of people. Her husband, Walter Sisulu, was in prison for more than twenty-five years, and she raised their children mostly on her own. People called her Ma Sisulu, the mother of the nation.

The questions for this section are with you now: servant leadership and Albertina Sisulu.

## Subtopic: Working for the Good of Others

A good leader works for the good of others. This means the leader's goal is to make life better for the whole group, especially for the people who need help the most, and not just for themselves, their family or their friends.

Working for the good of others can be big or small. Mama Nomsa's soup kitchen helps about fifty children every day. That is working for the good of others. A class captain who makes sure that new learners have someone to sit with is working for the good of others. A leader of a country who makes sure that every child can go to school is working for the good of others.

A leader who only works for their own good is called selfish. A selfish leader might take money meant for the people, give jobs only to friends, or make rules that help only themselves. Selfish leadership harms a community, and people stop trusting their leaders.

Desmond Tutu worked for the good of others all his life. Long before he led the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, he spoke out against apartheid, even though it was dangerous. In 1984, he received the Nobel Peace Prize for his peaceful struggle against apartheid. Later, he spoke up for people with HIV, for the poor and for the environment, all over the world.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a leader should do all the talking; a good leader listens first. Two: thinking people exist to serve the leader; a good leader serves the people. Three: thinking only famous people can be servant leaders; ordinary people like Mama Nomsa are leaders too. Four: thinking a leader should help only their own friends; a good leader works for the good of everyone.

The questions for this section are with you now: working for the good of others, selfish leaders, and examples.
