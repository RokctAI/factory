# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the life story of Nelson Mandela and why he is an example of a good leader. The CAPS curriculum allows the study of either Nelson Mandela or Mahatma Gandhi, and in these lessons we study Nelson Mandela. The anchor is a visit by a Grade 4 class from Mthatha to the Nelson Mandela Museum at Qunu, in the Eastern Cape. From the museum, the learners look out over green, rolling hills dotted with homesteads, where Mandela herded cattle as a boy. Inside, there are photographs of his life, a timeline on the wall, and gifts he received from people all over the world. The guide begins: let me tell you the story of a boy called Rolihlahla. The big idea is that Nelson Mandela's life shows the qualities of a good leader: he listened, served his people, worked with a team, was brave, was dedicated and committed, and made great sacrifices for the freedom of all South Africans. Knowing the main events of his life and why he was a good leader is examinable.

## Subtopic: Growing Up in the Eastern Cape

Nelson Mandela was born on the 18th of July 1918, in the small village of Mvezo, on the banks of the Mbashe River, in the Eastern Cape. His father was a chief and adviser to the Thembu royal family. His parents named him Rolihlahla, which in isiXhosa can mean pulling the branch of a tree, or troublemaker. His clan name was Madiba, and many people still call him Madiba as a sign of respect.

As a young child, he moved with his mother to Qunu. There he herded cattle and sheep, played with other boys in the veld, and listened to stories told by the elders around the fire. On his first day at school, his teacher gave him the English name Nelson, because at that time children were given English names at school.

When his father died, Rolihlahla, then about nine years old, was taken to live with the regent of the Thembu people, Chief Jongintaba, at the Great Place in Mqhekezweni. There he watched the regent lead meetings, where everyone was allowed to speak and the chief listened to all of them before making a decision. Mandela later said that this taught him how a leader should listen.

He went to school and then to the University College of Fort Hare, one of the few universities for Black students at that time. In 1941, he moved to Johannesburg.

The questions for this section are with you now: Mandela's birth, childhood, names, and what he learnt about listening.

## Subtopic: The Long Walk to Freedom

In Johannesburg, Mandela saw how unfairly Black South Africans were treated. He studied law, and in 1944 he joined the ANC, the African National Congress, and helped to start its Youth League. In 1952, he and Oliver Tambo opened the first law firm run by Black lawyers in South Africa, helping people who were in trouble because of unfair laws.

From 1948, the government made the system of apartheid even stricter. Apartheid kept people apart by race. Black, Coloured and Indian South Africans could not vote, and were forced to live in separate areas, go to separate schools and use separate facilities. Mandela and the ANC organised peaceful protests, such as the Defiance Campaign of 1952.

In 1960, at Sharpeville, police shot and killed sixty-nine people at a peaceful protest against the pass laws, and the government banned the ANC. Mandela and other leaders decided that peaceful protest alone was not working. They started an armed group to attack government buildings and power lines, while trying to avoid harming people. This was a hard and serious choice, and people still discuss it today.

In 1962, Mandela was arrested. At the Rivonia Trial, in 1964, he and his comrades were sentenced to life in prison. In court he said that he had fought against white domination and against black domination, and that he cherished the ideal of a free society where all live together in harmony.

Mandela spent twenty-seven years in prison, eighteen of them on Robben Island, where prisoners broke rocks in a lime quarry.

The questions for this section are with you now: apartheid, the ANC, the Rivonia Trial and Robben Island.

## Subtopic: President and Peacemaker

On the 11th of February 1990, Nelson Mandela walked out of prison, a free man, at the age of seventy-one. Millions of people around the world watched on television.

Many people feared that there would be a war in South Africa. Instead, Mandela chose to talk and to forgive. He worked with President F.W. de Klerk and many other leaders to agree on a new, fair way of governing the country. In 1993, Mandela and De Klerk were together awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

On the 27th of April 1994, South Africans of all races voted together for the first time. Mandela became the first democratically elected President of South Africa, and the first Black President, on the 10th of May 1994. He served one term of five years and then stepped down in 1999, letting others lead.

As President, he worked to bring South Africans together. In 1995, he wore a Springbok rugby jersey at the Rugby World Cup final, which many white South Africans loved and many Black South Africans had seen as a symbol of apartheid. He started the Nelson Mandela Children's Fund. He died on the 5th of December 2013, aged ninety-five.

Mandela is an example of a good leader because he listened, served the people, worked as part of a team, was brave, was dedicated to freedom for over fifty years, and sacrificed his freedom and family life.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking Mandela was born in Johannesburg; he was born in Mvezo, in the Eastern Cape. Two: thinking Mandela was in prison for two years; it was twenty-seven years. Three: thinking Mandela worked alone; he worked with a team, including Oliver Tambo and Walter Sisulu. Four: thinking Mandela sought revenge after prison; he chose peace and reconciliation.

The questions for this section are with you now: Mandela's release, the 1994 election, and why he was a good leader.

## Subtopic: Free and President

Free in 1990. President in 1994. A peacemaker.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Nelson Mandela was born in Mvezo in 1918 and grew up herding cattle in Qunu. He became a lawyer and an ANC leader who fought against apartheid, and he spent twenty-seven years in prison. He was released in 1990, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1993, and became the first democratically elected President in 1994. He chose peace instead of revenge.

Hold the timeline on the museum wall in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: president and peacemaker.
