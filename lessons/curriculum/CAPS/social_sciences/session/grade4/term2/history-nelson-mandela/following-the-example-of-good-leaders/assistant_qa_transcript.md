### subtopic_1 — Mandela Day
**Assistant:** What is spekboom, and why do people plant it?

**Tutor:** Spekboom is a hardy South African plant with small, round, juicy green leaves. Its scientific name is Portulacaria afra, and in English it is sometimes called elephant's food, because elephants love to eat it. It grows naturally in dry parts of the Eastern Cape and other areas. Spekboom is very good at surviving in hot, dry places, because it stores water in its thick leaves. People like to plant it on Mandela Day and other community days for several reasons. It is easy to grow from a cutting, so you just push a piece of stem into the soil. It needs very little water once it is growing. It helps to stop soil from washing away. And scientists say that, like all green plants, it takes in carbon dioxide from the air, which helps to fight climate change, and spekboom thickets can store a lot of carbon. Some people even eat the slightly sour leaves in salads.

### subtopic_2 — Leading in Everyday Life
**Assistant:** Why can people only vote when they turn eighteen?

**Tutor:** In South Africa, the Constitution says that every adult citizen has the right to vote, and an adult is a person who is eighteen years or older. The idea is that by eighteen, most people have finished school, understand how the country is run, and are ready to make important decisions on their own. Many countries use the same age, though a few use sixteen or other ages. Before 1994, most South Africans could not vote at all, no matter how old they were, because the apartheid laws allowed only white people to vote for the national Parliament. Many people struggled and sacrificed so that all adults could vote. On the 27th of April 1994, millions of people stood in long queues, some for many hours, to vote for the first time. Even if you cannot vote yet, you can learn about how the country is run, talk about the news with your family, and practise choosing leaders wisely at school.

### subtopic_3 — Growing into Good Leaders
**Assistant:** How did students in 1976 show leadership?

**Tutor:** In 1976, the government said that many subjects in Black schools must be taught in Afrikaans, a language that many students and even teachers did not speak well. Students felt this was unfair, and that it would make it even harder to learn. School students in Soweto organised themselves. They chose leaders, made plans and painted posters. On the 16th of June 1976, thousands of students marched peacefully through Soweto to protest. Police opened fire, and many young people were killed, including a twelve-year-old boy, Hector Pieterson. The protests spread across the country. The students showed courage, teamwork and commitment, even though they were young and the danger was great. Their protest helped to change the country, and many of them later became leaders. Today, the 16th of June is Youth Day, a public holiday, which reminds us that young people can be leaders and can help to change the world.
