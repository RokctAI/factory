### subtopic_1 — Growing Up in the Eastern Cape
**Assistant:** What was life like for prisoners on Robben Island?

**Tutor:** Life on Robben Island was very hard. Political prisoners, like Mandela, lived in small cells, about two metres wide, with a mat on the floor to sleep on and a bucket for a toilet. They were given less food than other prisoners, and at first Black prisoners had to wear short trousers, even in the cold winters. Every day they had to break rocks, and later dig in a lime quarry, where the bright white rock hurt their eyes in the sun. They were allowed only one visitor and one letter every six months at first, and letters were often cut by the guards. But the prisoners did not give up. They taught each other, discussed ideas and studied, so that people called the prison the University of Robben Island. Mandela studied law by letter. Today Robben Island is a museum and a World Heritage Site, and former prisoners often guide visitors and tell their stories.

### subtopic_2 — The Long Walk to Freedom
**Assistant:** Why did Mandela wear a Springbok jersey in 1995?

**Tutor:** For many years, rugby and the Springbok emblem were seen by many Black South Africans as symbols of apartheid, because during apartheid the national team was only for white players. In 1995, South Africa hosted the Rugby World Cup, its first big world sporting event after apartheid. The Springbok team still had mostly white players and supporters. Mandela knew that many white South Africans were afraid of the new democracy and worried about losing things they loved, like their rugby team. When the Springboks reached the final in Johannesburg, Mandela walked onto the field wearing a Springbok jersey with the captain's number six on it. The mostly white crowd began to chant his name. South Africa won the match. It was a moment that showed Mandela wanted all South Africans to feel at home in the new country. Some people disagreed with him, but many say it helped to bring people closer together.

### subtopic_3 — President and Peacemaker
**Assistant:** Why did Mandela only serve one term as President?

**Tutor:** Mandela could have stayed as President for a second term of five years, as the Constitution allowed, and he was very popular. But he chose to step down in 1999, when he was eighty years old. He said that a leader must know when to hand over to a new generation. In many countries, leaders have refused to give up power, and stayed in office for decades, sometimes causing great harm. Mandela wanted to show that in a democracy, leaders serve for a time and then let others lead, and that no single person is bigger than the country. This was another lesson in good leadership. After he left office, he kept working for good causes. He raised money for children's hospitals and schools, spoke out about HIV and AIDS at a time when many people were afraid to talk about it, and helped to make peace in other countries in Africa.
