### subtopic_1 — The Side View
**Assistant:** How do satellites take pictures of the Earth from above?

**Tutor:** Satellites are machines that travel around the Earth far up in space, hundreds of kilometres above our heads. Some of them carry very powerful cameras that point down at the Earth. As the satellite moves around the Earth, the camera takes picture after picture, and these pictures are sent back to stations on the ground by radio signals. Because the satellite is so high, one picture can show a whole city, or even a whole country. The best satellite cameras can show things as small as a car. Satellite pictures are plan views, because they look straight down. Mapmakers use them to draw and update maps, farmers use them to check their crops, and weather forecasters use them to watch clouds and storms moving across the country. South Africa has its own small satellites too. If you use a map on a cell phone and switch to the satellite view, you are looking at pictures taken from space.

### subtopic_2 — The View from Above
**Assistant:** Why do maps use the view from above and not the side view?

**Tutor:** A map has to show where things are, compared with each other. From the side, things block each other. If you stand at the school gate, the hall might hide the classrooms behind it, and you cannot tell how far back they go. From above, nothing is hidden. You can see every building, path and field, and exactly where each one is, next to the others. You can also measure distances on a plan view, because everything is laid out flat. That is why a map of a town, a farm or a whole country is always drawn as if you were looking down from very high up. Map makers sometimes add little side-view pictures as symbols, such as a tree or a church, because they are easy to recognise, but the layout of the map is always from above. Next lesson, we will learn about symbols and keys on maps.

### subtopic_3 — Drawing Plans
**Assistant:** What does a roof look like from above?

**Tutor:** It depends on the shape of the roof. A flat roof, which many shops and offices have, looks like a simple rectangle from above. A roof that slopes down on two sides, like an upside-down V, looks like a rectangle with a line along the middle, where the two sides meet at the top. That top line is called the ridge. A roof that slopes down on four sides looks like a rectangle with lines running from the corners towards the middle. A round rondavel with a thatched cone roof looks like a circle from above, with a dot in the middle where the point of the cone is. When you look at aerial photographs of your area, you can often recognise buildings just from the shapes of their roofs. Long rectangles may be factories or schools, circles may be rondavels, and lots of small rectangles close together may be houses.
