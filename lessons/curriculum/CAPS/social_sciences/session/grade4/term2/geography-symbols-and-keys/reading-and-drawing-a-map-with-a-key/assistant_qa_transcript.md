### subtopic_1 — Reading a Map of a Farm
**Assistant:** How is sugar made from sugar cane?

**Tutor:** Sugar cane is a tall grass with thick stems, full of sweet juice. It grows well in warm, wet places, which is why the coast of KwaZulu-Natal is covered in cane fields. When the cane is ready, after twelve to eighteen months or even longer, workers or machines cut the stems. The cut cane is loaded onto trucks and taken quickly to a sugar mill, because the sugar in the stems starts to spoil after cutting. At the mill, the cane is shredded and crushed between heavy rollers to squeeze out the juice. The juice is cleaned and then boiled to remove most of the water, until it becomes a thick syrup. As the syrup cools, sugar crystals form. A spinning machine separates the crystals from the leftover syrup, called molasses. This gives brown sugar. Further cleaning makes white sugar. The leftover crushed stems, called bagasse, are often burnt at the mill to make energy.

### subtopic_2 — Reading a Map of a Village or Town
**Assistant:** Why do some maps have symbols that look like pictures and others just use letters?

**Tutor:** Both kinds of symbols have good points. Picture symbols, such as a tree, a tent or a shopping trolley, are easy to understand quickly, even for small children or people who speak another language. That is why tourist maps and maps in shopping centres use lots of pictures. But pictures take up more space, and some things are hard to draw as a small picture, such as a police station or a post office. Letter symbols, such as H, P or PO, are tiny and quick to draw, so they fit easily on a crowded map. But you need to know the language, or check the key, to understand them. Official maps often use a mix of both, plus lines and colours. When you design your own map, choose whatever is clearest for the person who will use it. The most important thing is that every symbol is explained in your key.

### subtopic_3 — Drawing Your Own Map
**Assistant:** What is the best way to get the places in the right position on my own map?

**Tutor:** Start with the biggest and most important things, because everything else fits around them. On Sanele's map, he draws the main road and the stream first, because they cross the whole area. Then he puts in the big buildings, such as the farmhouse and the shed, in the right places compared with the road and the stream. Ask yourself questions like: is the shed on the same side of the road as the house? Is the dam closer to the stream or to the road? It helps to walk around the area first, or to remember it carefully, and even to make a quick rough sketch before drawing the neat map. If you can, look at your area from a high place, such as a hill, because it gives you a view that is close to a plan view. You can also ask an adult to show you your area on a satellite picture on a phone, to check your positions.
