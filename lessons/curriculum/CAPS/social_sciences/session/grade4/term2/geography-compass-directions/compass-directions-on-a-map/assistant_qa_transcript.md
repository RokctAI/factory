### subtopic_1 — The Four Compass Directions
**Assistant:** Why does a compass needle always point north?

**Tutor:** The Earth is like a giant magnet. Deep inside the Earth, there is a core of hot, melted iron that moves around slowly, and this makes the Earth magnetic. Every magnet has two ends, called poles, a north pole and a south pole. A compass needle is a tiny, light magnet balanced on a point so that it can turn easily. The Earth's magnetism pulls on the needle and turns it until it lines up with the Earth's magnetism, pointing towards the north. This is why the needle always swings back to point north, no matter how you turn the compass. Interestingly, the place the needle points to, the magnetic north pole, is not exactly the same as the true North Pole at the top of the globe, and it slowly moves over the years. But for finding your way on a hike or reading a map in class, the compass needle is close enough.

### subtopic_2 — Using a Compass
**Assistant:** Does the sun really rise exactly in the east every day?

**Tutor:** Not exactly, which is a clever thing to notice. The sun rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west every day, so it is a good guide to directions. But the exact spot changes a little through the year. In South Africa, in summer, the sun rises a bit towards the south of east and sets a bit towards the south of west, and it climbs high in the sky at midday. In winter, it rises a bit towards the north of east, sets a bit towards the north of west, and stays lower in the northern sky. Only on two days a year, in March and September, does it rise almost exactly in the east and set almost exactly in the west. You can test this yourself by marking where the sun rises from your home at different times of the year. So the sun is a helpful clue, but a compass is more exact.

### subtopic_3 — Directions on a Map
**Assistant:** Is north always at the top of every map?

**Tutor:** On almost all maps, yes, and that is the normal rule. But not on every single one. Some maps are turned around so that they fit better on a page, or so that they match the way a person is walking. For example, a map in a shopping centre might be turned so that the direction you are facing is at the top. Some old maps, made hundreds of years ago, put east or south at the top. And some people have made maps with south at the top, to show that there is no real top or bottom to the Earth in space. That is exactly why maps have a north arrow. Before you use any map, look for the north arrow and see which way it points. If it points up, north is at the top. If it points another way, turn the map or turn your thinking to match. Never just assume.
