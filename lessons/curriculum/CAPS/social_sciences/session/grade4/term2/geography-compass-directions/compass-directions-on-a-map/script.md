# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the four main compass directions, north, east, south and west, how a compass works, and how to use compass directions to describe where places are on a map. The anchor is a Grade 4 school camp at Golden Gate Highlands National Park, in the eastern Free State, where tall golden sandstone cliffs glow in the sun. On the first morning, the learners wake up early to watch the sun come up over the mountains. Their teacher hands out small compasses and a map of the camp. On the map there is an arrow with the letter N at its tip. The map shows the camp, the dining hall, a dam, a stream, a hiking trail and a lookout point on a hill. The big idea is that compass directions do not change when we turn around, unlike left and right, so they are the best way to describe where places are on a map. Using north, east, south and west on a map is examinable.

## Subtopic: The Four Compass Directions

There are four main compass directions: north, east, south and west. They are also called the cardinal points. We write them with capital letters: N, E, S and W.

North and south are opposites. If you face north, south is directly behind you. East and west are opposites too. If you face east, west is directly behind you.

The four directions are always in the same order going clockwise, which is the way the hands of a clock move. Starting at north: north, east, south, west. A fun way to remember the order is the sentence: Never Eat Soggy Weet-Bix. The first letters, N, E, S, W, give the four directions in clockwise order.

A drawing that shows the directions is called a compass rose. It looks like a star or a cross, with N at the top, E on the right, S at the bottom and W on the left.

The sun helps us find directions. In the morning, the sun rises in the east. In the evening, it sets in the west. At the camp, the learners watched the sun rise over the mountains, so they knew those mountains were in the east. In the evening, they watched it set behind the hills in the west. In South Africa, for most of the year, the midday sun is in the northern part of the sky.

Compass directions are different from left and right. Left and right change when you turn around. North stays north, no matter which way you face. That is why sailors, pilots, hikers and mapmakers use compass directions.

## Subtopic: Using a Compass

A compass is an instrument that shows directions. Inside a compass is a thin needle that is a tiny magnet. The Earth acts like a giant magnet, and the compass needle always swings round to point towards the north. One end of the needle is usually coloured red, and that end points north.

To use a compass, hold it flat in your hand, so that the needle can swing freely. Keep it away from metal objects, such as keys, cell phones and fences, because they can pull the needle the wrong way. Wait for the needle to stop moving. The red end now points north. Turn your body so that you face the same way the red end points. Now you are facing north. East is on your right, west is on your left, and south is behind you.

At the camp, the learners stand in a circle and each hold a compass. All the red needles point the same way, towards the lookout point on the hill. So the lookout point is north of the camp.

People have used compasses for hundreds of years to find their way across seas and deserts. Today, many cell phones have a compass app, but a simple compass does not need batteries or signal, so hikers always carry one.

## Subtopic: Directions on a Map

On most maps, north is at the top. The mapmaker shows this with a north arrow, an arrow with the letter N at its tip, usually in a corner of the map. If the map has a north arrow pointing up, then east is on the right of the map, south is at the bottom and west is on the left.

We can use compass directions to describe where places are compared with each other. On the camp map, the lookout point is at the top of the map, so it is north of the camp. The dam is at the bottom, so it is south of the camp. The dining hall is on the right of the camp, so it is east of the camp. The stream is on the left, so it is west of the camp.

When we describe a direction, we always say it from one place to another. The dam is south of the camp. That also means the camp is north of the dam. Be careful to say which place you are starting from.

We can also use compass directions to give directions for a journey on a map: walk north along the trail to the lookout point, then walk east to the waterfall.

In later grades, you will learn the directions in between, such as north-east and south-west.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking north always means up into the sky; north is a direction across the ground, shown at the top of most maps. Two: mixing up east and west; going clockwise, Never Eat Soggy Weet-Bix. Three: thinking the compass needle points wherever you face; it always swings to point north. Four: forgetting the starting place; the dam is south of the camp, but the camp is north of the dam.
