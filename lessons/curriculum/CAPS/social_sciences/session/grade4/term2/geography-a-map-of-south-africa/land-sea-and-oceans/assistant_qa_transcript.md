### subtopic_1 — Land and Sea on a Map
**Assistant:** Why is the sea water salty but river water is fresh?

**Tutor:** Rain water is fresh, which means it has almost no salt. When rain falls on the land, it runs over rocks and soil into streams and rivers. On the way, it dissolves tiny amounts of salts and minerals from the rocks. The amount is so small that river water still tastes fresh. But all rivers flow to the sea, and they have been carrying these tiny amounts of salt into the sea for millions and millions of years. At the sea, the sun heats the water, and some of it evaporates, which means it turns into water vapour and rises into the air. The salt cannot evaporate, so it stays behind in the sea. Over a very long time, the sea has become salty. The water vapour forms clouds and falls again as fresh rain. You will learn more about this journey of water, called the water cycle, in Term 4.

### subtopic_2 — Two Oceans
**Assistant:** Why is Lesotho inside South Africa?

**Tutor:** Lesotho is a separate country, with its own king, government, flag and money, even though it is completely surrounded by South Africa. Its history explains this. In the 1800s, King Moshoeshoe the First united the Basotho people in the high mountains, where they could defend themselves. His mountain fortress, Thaba Bosiu, was very hard to attack. During wars over land with the Boer republic of the Orange Free State, Moshoeshoe lost much of the fertile land to the west of the Caledon River. To protect his people from losing all their land, he asked Britain for protection, and in 1868 Basutoland became a British protectorate. Because it was ruled separately, it did not become part of the Union of South Africa in 1910. In 1966 it became the independent country of Lesotho. Today, many people from Lesotho and South Africa visit and work in each other's countries, and Lesotho supplies water to Gauteng through big dams and tunnels.

### subtopic_3 — South Africa's Coast and Neighbours
**Assistant:** Is Cape Agulhas or Cape Point the tip of Africa?

**Tutor:** Many people think Cape Point, near Cape Town, is the southern tip of Africa, because it is so famous and its cliffs look like the end of the land. But Cape Agulhas is further south, by about one hundred and fifty kilometres to the south-east. So Cape Agulhas is the true southernmost tip of Africa. Cape Point is at the end of the Cape Peninsula, a long finger of land that points south from Cape Town. Cape Point is also not where the two oceans meet, even though some people say so. The waters around Cape Point are cold Atlantic water. The official meeting point is at Cape Agulhas. The name Agulhas comes from the Portuguese word for needles. Portuguese sailors gave it this name more than five hundred years ago, possibly because their compass needles pointed almost exactly north there. Both capes are dangerous for ships, and there have been many shipwrecks along this stormy coast.
