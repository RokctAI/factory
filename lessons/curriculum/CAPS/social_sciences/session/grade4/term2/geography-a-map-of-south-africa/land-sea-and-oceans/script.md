# Part 1 — Expert

This session begins our study of the map of South Africa. It explains how land and sea are shown on a map, names the two oceans along South Africa's coastline, and shows where South Africa's land borders and neighbours are. The anchor is a class trip by Grade 4 learners from Bredasdorp, a small town in the Western Cape, to Cape Agulhas, about forty kilometres away. Cape Agulhas is the southernmost point of the whole continent of Africa. A strong wind blows off the sea, and waves crash on the rocks. Next to the rocks is a stone monument with a large metal map of Africa on the ground. Arrows on the map point to the west and to the east, with the names of two oceans. A sign says that this is where the two oceans officially meet. The big idea is that on a map, the sea and the land are shown in different colours, that South Africa has a long coastline with two oceans, and that it shares land borders with other countries. Naming the oceans and reading land and sea on a map are examinable.

## Subtopic: Land and Sea on a Map

On a map, land and sea are shown in different colours, so that you can tell them apart at a glance.

The sea is shown in blue. All water, such as oceans, seas, lakes, dams and rivers, is coloured blue on maps, even though real water can look grey, green or brown. Deeper parts of the sea are sometimes shown in a darker blue.

Land is shown in other colours. On many maps of South Africa, low land near the coast is shown in green, higher land in yellow and light brown, and high mountains in dark brown. Some maps show all the land in one colour, such as cream or light green, and use other colours to show provinces or countries.

The line where the land meets the sea is called the coastline, or coast. On a map it is the edge between the blue and the land colours. A coastline is not straight. It has bays, where the sea curves into the land, and capes or points, where the land sticks out into the sea. Cape Agulhas is a cape. False Bay and Algoa Bay are bays. An island is a piece of land with sea all around it. Robben Island, in Table Bay near Cape Town, is a small island off the coast of South Africa.

Land that is not next to the sea is called inland, or the interior. Gauteng, for example, is inland, far from the coast.

The questions for this section are with you now: how land, sea and coastlines are shown on a map, bays, capes and islands.

## Subtopic: Two Oceans

An ocean is a very large area of salt water. There are five oceans on Earth. Two of them wash against the coast of South Africa.

The Atlantic Ocean lies along the west coast of South Africa. It stretches from Cape Agulhas up the west coast past Cape Town, Saldanha Bay and Port Nolloth, to the Orange River mouth, where South Africa meets Namibia. The water of the Atlantic along our west coast is very cold, because a cold current of water flows up from near Antarctica. This cold water is full of tiny living things, so there are many fish, and fishing is very important on the west coast.

The Indian Ocean lies along the south and east coasts of South Africa. It stretches from Cape Agulhas past Mossel Bay, Gqeberha, East London and Durban, all the way to the border with Mozambique. The water of the Indian Ocean along our east coast is warm, because a warm current flows down from the tropics, near the equator. Durban's beaches are warm enough for swimming for most of the year.

The two oceans officially meet at Cape Agulhas. In real life, there is no line in the water, and the waters mix, but mapmakers have agreed that the boundary is at Cape Agulhas.

To remember which is which: the Atlantic is on the west, and the Indian is on the east. A trick is that the Indian Ocean lies between Africa and India, which is to the east.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Atlantic and Indian Oceans, warm and cold water, and Cape Agulhas.

## Subtopic: South Africa's Coast and Neighbours

South Africa has a very long coastline, almost three thousand kilometres from the border with Namibia in the west to the border with Mozambique in the east. Four of South Africa's provinces have a coastline: the Northern Cape, the Western Cape, the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal. The other five provinces are inland.

South Africa's busiest harbours are on the coast, such as the ports of Durban, Cape Town, Gqeberha, Ngqura, East London, Richards Bay, Mossel Bay and Saldanha Bay. Ships bring goods to South Africa and carry goods to other countries.

In the north, South Africa has land borders with six countries. From west to east, they are Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique and Eswatini. The sixth, Lesotho, is completely surrounded by South Africa. It is a separate country, inside our borders, high in the mountains.

On a map, borders between countries are shown by lines, often dashed lines. Rivers sometimes form part of a border. The Orange River forms part of the border with Namibia, and the Limpopo River forms part of the border with Botswana and Zimbabwe.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the sea is brown or grey on a map; it is always blue. Two: putting the Atlantic Ocean on the east coast; the Atlantic is on the west, and the Indian on the south and east. Three: thinking Lesotho is a province of South Africa; it is a separate country. Four: thinking there is a line in the sea where the oceans meet; the waters mix, but Cape Agulhas is the agreed meeting point.

The questions for this section are with you now: South Africa's coastline, harbours and neighbouring countries.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the trip to Cape Agulhas — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Blue for Sea

The sea is blue on a map. Land is green, yellow or brown.

The coastline is where land meets sea.

Keep the waves on the rocks at Cape Agulhas in mind and try the questions for this part: land and sea.

## Subtopic: Atlantic and Indian

The Atlantic Ocean is on the west coast. It is cold.

The Indian Ocean is on the south and east coast. It is warm.

Hold the metal map with its two arrows in mind and try this part's questions: the two oceans.

A long coast, four coastal provinces, six neighbours.

Pocket summary of the lesson. On a map, the sea is blue and the land is shown in other colours, and the coastline is where they meet. The Atlantic Ocean is along South Africa's west coast, and the Indian Ocean along the south and east coasts. They meet at Cape Agulhas, the southernmost tip of Africa. South Africa shares land borders with Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Eswatini and Lesotho.
