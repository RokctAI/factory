### subtopic_1 — Main Cities and Towns
**Assistant:** Why is the capital of KwaZulu-Natal not Durban, if Durban is bigger?

**Tutor:** Capital cities are often chosen for reasons of history, not size. Pietermaritzburg was founded in 1838, and it became the seat of government for the colony of Natal under British rule. Over the years, government buildings, a legislature and offices were built there, so it stayed the capital. Durban grew faster, because it has a harbour and so many businesses and factories, and today it is much bigger. After 1994, there was a debate about whether the capital of KwaZulu-Natal should be Pietermaritzburg or Ulundi, which had been the capital of the KwaZulu homeland and was once the royal capital of the Zulu king Cetshwayo. In the end, Pietermaritzburg was chosen as the single capital in 2004. Many countries have capitals that are not their biggest city. Australia's biggest city is Sydney, but its capital is Canberra. The capital is where the government works, not necessarily where the most people live.

### subtopic_2 — Finding Your Own Settlement on a Map
**Assistant:** What is an atlas?

**Tutor:** An atlas is a book of maps. A school atlas usually has maps of your own country, its provinces and cities, and then maps of Africa and the other continents, and a map of the whole world. Many atlases also have maps that show special information, such as rainfall, temperature, farming, population and where minerals are found. At the back, there is an index, which lists thousands of places in alphabetical order. Next to each place, it gives the page number of the map and a grid reference, so that you can find it quickly. The name atlas comes from an ancient Greek story about a giant called Atlas, who was said to hold up the sky on his shoulders. An old mapmaker put a picture of Atlas on the cover of his book of maps, and the name stuck. Ask your teacher or librarian to show you a school atlas, and try to find your province and your town.

### subtopic_3 — Describing Where You Live
**Assistant:** My village is not on any map. Does that mean it is not important?

**Tutor:** Not at all. Your village is just as important as any city. A map of the whole country is a small-scale map. It covers a huge area on one page, so there is only space to show the bigger cities and towns. Thousands of villages, farms and small settlements are left out, simply because there is no room, not because they do not matter. If you look at a large-scale map of your area, such as a map of your district or a topographic map, which shows a smaller area in more detail, your village will very likely be there, with its roads, streams, school and fields. You can also find it on a satellite map on a phone. In Term 1, you made your own map of your area, so in a way, you are already putting your village on the map. Every settlement has its own people, its own landmarks and its own history.
