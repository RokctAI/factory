# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the nine provinces of South Africa: their names, where each one is on a map of South Africa, and their capital cities. The anchor is a Grade 4 classroom in Pretoria, in Gauteng. The teacher has brought a giant floor puzzle of South Africa. Each of the nine pieces is a province, painted in a different colour, with its name and a star for its capital city. The learners sit in a circle and take turns fitting a piece into place, describing where it goes using compass directions, until the whole country is complete. The big idea is that South Africa is divided into nine provinces, each with its own name, location and capital city, and that we can describe where each province is on a map. Naming the provinces and locating them on a map of South Africa are examinable.

## Subtopic: What a Province Is

A country can be divided into smaller parts so that it is easier to govern. In South Africa, these parts are called provinces. South Africa has nine provinces. The provinces we have today were created in 1994, when South Africa became a democracy.

Each province has its own government, led by a premier, and its own legislature, where laws for the province are made. The provincial government looks after things such as schools, hospitals, clinics and roads in the province. The national government, in Pretoria and Cape Town, looks after the whole country.

Each province has a capital city, where the provincial government works. On a map, a capital city is often shown with a star or a special dot.

On a map, the borders between provinces are shown with lines, often dashed lines, and each province may be coloured differently so you can see where one ends and the next begins.

The provinces are very different in size. The biggest province by area is the Northern Cape. It covers almost one third of the whole country, but it is mostly dry and has the fewest people. The smallest province is Gauteng, but it has the most people, because it has the big cities of Johannesburg and Pretoria. Gauteng is so small that it would fit into the Northern Cape more than twenty times. When you look at a map of the provinces, use the compass directions you have learnt to describe where each province is, such as: Limpopo is in the north.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a province is, provincial governments and capitals, and the biggest and smallest provinces.

## Subtopic: The Provinces in the South and Centre

The learners start with the big pieces in the south and west.

The Northern Cape is the largest piece. It lies in the west of South Africa. Its west coast is on the Atlantic Ocean, and it borders Namibia and Botswana. Its capital is Kimberley. The Orange River flows across it, and in spring, after good rains, parts of it called Namaqualand are covered in wild flowers.

The Western Cape is in the south-west corner of the country. It has the Atlantic Ocean on its west coast and the Indian Ocean on its south coast. Its capital is Cape Town, which is also where South Africa's Parliament meets. The Western Cape is known for Table Mountain, fruit farms and vineyards.

The Eastern Cape is in the south-east, along the Indian Ocean coast, east of the Western Cape. Its capital is Bhisho. Cities such as Gqeberha and East London are in the Eastern Cape. Nelson Mandela was born in a small village in this province, as we will learn this term.

KwaZulu-Natal lies further north-east, along the Indian Ocean coast. It borders Lesotho, Eswatini and Mozambique. Its capital is Pietermaritzburg, and its biggest city is Durban.

The Free State is in the centre of the country. It has no coastline. It borders Lesotho to the east. Its capital is Bloemfontein. Wide, flat farmlands in the Free State grow much of the country's maize and wheat, and there are gold mines around Welkom.

So, in the south we have three provinces with Cape in their names, all with coastlines: the Northern Cape, the Western Cape and the Eastern Cape. Together with KwaZulu-Natal, these are the four coastal provinces.

The questions for this section are with you now: the Northern Cape, Western Cape, Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal and Free State.

## Subtopic: The Provinces in the North

Now the learners fit the four pieces in the north. None of them has a coastline. They are all inland provinces.

North West is in the north, on the border with Botswana. Its capital is Mahikeng. It is known for platinum mines and for farming maize and cattle.

Gauteng is the small piece in the north-east of the interior, between North West, Limpopo, Mpumalanga and the Free State. Its capital is Johannesburg, and the city of Pretoria, the country's administrative capital, is also in Gauteng. The name Gauteng means place of gold, because of the gold found there.

Limpopo is the province furthest north. It borders Botswana, Zimbabwe and Mozambique, and the Limpopo River forms its northern border. Its capital is Polokwane.

Mpumalanga is in the east, on the borders of Mozambique and Eswatini. Its capital is Mbombela. The name Mpumalanga means place where the sun rises, which makes sense because it is in the east. Part of the Kruger National Park is in Mpumalanga, and part is in Limpopo.

With the last piece in place, the class looks at the whole puzzle. Nine provinces, nine capitals, one country.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the biggest province has the most people; the Northern Cape is biggest, but Gauteng has the most people. Two: thinking Gauteng has a coastline; it is inland. Three: thinking Lesotho and Eswatini are provinces; they are separate countries. Four: thinking Pretoria is the capital of Gauteng; the capital of Gauteng is Johannesburg, and Pretoria is South Africa's administrative capital.

The questions for this section are with you now: North West, Gauteng, Limpopo and Mpumalanga.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the giant floor puzzle — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Nine Parts

South Africa has nine provinces.

Each one has a capital city.

The Northern Cape is biggest. Gauteng is smallest.

Keep the nine coloured puzzle pieces in mind and try the questions for this part: what a province is.

## Subtopic: South and Centre

Northern Cape, Western Cape, Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State.

Four of them have a coastline.

Hold the three Cape pieces in mind and try this part's questions: the provinces in the south and centre.

## Subtopic: The North

North West, Gauteng, Limpopo and Mpumalanga are all inland.

Pocket summary of the lesson. South Africa has nine provinces. The Northern Cape, Western Cape, Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal have coastlines. The Free State, North West, Gauteng, Limpopo and Mpumalanga are inland. The capitals are Kimberley, Cape Town, Bhisho, Pietermaritzburg, Bloemfontein, Mahikeng, Johannesburg, Polokwane and Mbombela.

Hold the finished puzzle on the classroom floor in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: the provinces in the north.
