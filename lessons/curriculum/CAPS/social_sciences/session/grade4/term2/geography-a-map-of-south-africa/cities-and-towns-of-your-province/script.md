# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the main cities and towns in each province, how to find your own settlement on a map of South Africa, and how to describe where you live. The anchor is a pen-pal project in a Grade 4 class in Mahikeng, the capital of North West. Months ago, the class wrote to Grade 4 classes in all nine provinces. Today the replies have arrived in a big box. Each pen pal begins their letter the same way, by saying exactly where they live: the name of their settlement, their province, and where it is compared with a city or landmark. The teacher pins a large map of South Africa on the wall, and as each letter is read aloud, a learner finds the place on the map and sticks a small flag on it. The big idea is that every province has a capital city and other important cities and towns, and that every learner can find their own settlement on a map and describe where it is. Naming the main cities and towns of your province and locating your settlement are examinable.

## Subtopic: Main Cities and Towns

A map of South Africa shows cities and towns as dots or small squares. Bigger dots or bolder writing usually show bigger cities, and a capital city is often shown with a star.

Here are the main cities and towns of each province, as the pen-pal letters show.

Gauteng: Johannesburg, the capital and the biggest city in the country; Pretoria, also called Tshwane; Soweto; and towns such as Vereeniging. North West: Mahikeng, the capital; Rustenburg, near the platinum mines; Potchefstroom; and Klerksdorp. Limpopo: Polokwane, the capital; Thohoyandou; Tzaneen; Giyani; and Musina, near the Zimbabwe border. Mpumalanga: Mbombela, the capital; eMalahleni, a coal mining town; Middelburg; and Secunda.

KwaZulu-Natal: Pietermaritzburg, the capital; Durban, the biggest city and busiest port; Richards Bay; Newcastle; and KwaDukuza. Free State: Bloemfontein, the capital; Welkom, a gold mining town; Bethlehem; and Phuthaditjhaba. Eastern Cape: Bhisho, the capital; Gqeberha; East London; Mthatha; and Makhanda. Western Cape: Cape Town, the capital; George; Stellenbosch; Paarl; and Worcester. Northern Cape: Kimberley, the capital; Upington; and Springbok.

Notice that many big cities are on the coast, near harbours, such as Cape Town, Durban and Gqeberha, or near mines, such as Johannesburg, Rustenburg and Welkom. Settlements grow where there is work, water and transport.

You do not need to remember all of these. But you must know the capital and the main cities and towns of your own province.

The questions for this section are with you now: capitals and the main cities and towns of the provinces.

## Subtopic: Finding Your Own Settlement on a Map

To find your own settlement on a map of South Africa, follow these steps.

First, find your province. Use the province borders and colours. If you live in Mahikeng, find North West, in the north, on the border with Botswana.

Second, find the capital of your province, marked with a star. This helps you find your way around the province.

Third, look for your settlement's name. If it is a big city or town, its name will be on the map. If it is a small village or a farm, it may be too small to show on a map of the whole country. Then find the nearest town that is on the map.

Fourth, use the index and grid references. Many atlases and maps have an index at the back, which lists places in alphabetical order with a page number and a grid reference, such as C3. Look up your town, go to the page, and find the block. You learnt how to do this in the lesson on grid references.

Fifth, use landmarks on the map, such as rivers, mountains, dams and main roads. If you live near the Vaal River, follow the blue line until you find your area.

When Lebo's letter arrives from a village near Giyani, the learners cannot find her village on the wall map, because it is too small. So they find Giyani, in Limpopo, and put the flag next to it.

The questions for this section are with you now: finding your province, capital and settlement on a map, and using an index.

## Subtopic: Describing Where You Live

Once you have found your settlement, you can describe where it is in a clear sentence, like the pen pals did.

A good description has four parts. The name of your settlement. The name of your province. A big city, town or landmark nearby. And a compass direction, and if you can, a distance.

Here are some examples from the letters. I live in Thohoyandou, in Limpopo, north-east of Polokwane. I live in Upington, in the Northern Cape, on the Orange River, west of Kimberley. I live in Phuthaditjhaba, in the Free State, close to the border with Lesotho. I live in a village near Mthatha, in the Eastern Cape, about thirty kilometres from the town.

The Mahikeng class writes their own description: we live in Mahikeng, the capital of North West, close to the border with Botswana, west of Johannesburg.

You can also describe where your province is in South Africa: North West is in the north of South Africa, west of Gauteng. And where South Africa is in Africa: South Africa is at the southern tip of Africa.

So your full address in the world grows bigger and bigger: your home, your street, your settlement, your province, your country, your continent.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking every village appears on a map of the whole country; small places may only appear on large-scale maps. Two: forgetting to say which province you live in; it helps people find the right part of the country. Three: mixing up the capital of the province with the biggest city; in KwaZulu-Natal, the capital is Pietermaritzburg, but the biggest city is Durban. Four: giving a direction without a starting place; say north-east of Polokwane, not just north-east.

The questions for this section are with you now: describing the location of your settlement.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the pen-pal letters in Mahikeng — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Big Places

Every province has a capital and other big towns.

Know the capital and the main towns of your province.

Keep the box of letters from nine provinces in mind and try the questions for this part: main cities and towns.

## Subtopic: Find Your Place

Find your province, then the capital, then your town.

Use the index and grid references.

Hold the little flags on the wall map in mind and try this part's questions: finding your settlement.

## Subtopic: Say Where You Live

Settlement, province, a big place nearby, and a direction.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Each province has a capital city and other main cities and towns, such as Durban in KwaZulu-Natal and Rustenburg in North West. To find your settlement, find your province, its capital and then your town, using the index and grid references. To describe where you live, give your settlement, your province, a big place nearby and a compass direction.

Hold the Mahikeng class's own description in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: describing where you live.
