### subtopic_1 — What a Province Is
**Assistant:** Did South Africa always have nine provinces?

**Tutor:** No. Before 1994, South Africa had only four provinces: the Cape Province, Natal, the Orange Free State and the Transvaal. They were created in 1910, when the Union of South Africa was formed. During apartheid, the government also created so-called homelands, such as the Transkei and Bophuthatswana, where Black South Africans were forced to belong, even if they lived and worked elsewhere. This was unfair, and it divided people. When South Africa became a democracy in 1994, the homelands were brought back into South Africa, and the country was divided into nine new provinces, so that every part of the country would be governed fairly and closer to the people. The old Cape Province became three provinces: the Northern Cape, the Western Cape and the Eastern Cape. The Transvaal became four: Gauteng, North West, Limpopo and Mpumalanga. Natal became KwaZulu-Natal, and the Orange Free State became the Free State.

### subtopic_2 — The Provinces in the South and Centre
**Assistant:** Why does Gauteng have so many people if it is so small?

**Tutor:** It is all about gold and jobs. In 1886, a huge amount of gold was found under the ground in the area that is now Johannesburg. People came from all over South Africa, from neighbouring countries and from other continents to work on the mines and in the businesses that grew up around them. Johannesburg grew very quickly into a big city. Pretoria, nearby, became an important centre of government. Over the years, factories, banks, offices, shops and universities were built in Gauteng, creating even more jobs. People keep moving to Gauteng from other provinces and countries to find work and study. Today about a quarter of all South Africans live in Gauteng, even though it covers only a very small part of the country. The Northern Cape is the opposite: it is huge, but much of it is dry semi-desert, with few jobs and few towns, so very few people live there.

### subtopic_3 — The Provinces in the North
**Assistant:** What does the premier of a province do?

**Tutor:** The premier is the leader of the government of a province, a little like a president, but for one province only. After each election, the members of the provincial legislature choose the premier. The premier chooses a team of people, called members of the executive council, each of whom looks after one area, such as education, health, roads or agriculture. Together, they run the provincial government. They decide how to spend the money the province gets, for example on building schools and clinics, paying teachers and nurses, and fixing provincial roads. The premier also works with the national government and the municipalities, which run towns and cities. The provincial legislature makes laws that apply only to that province and checks that the premier and the team are doing their jobs properly. So each of the nine provinces has its own premier, its own legislature and its own capital city.
