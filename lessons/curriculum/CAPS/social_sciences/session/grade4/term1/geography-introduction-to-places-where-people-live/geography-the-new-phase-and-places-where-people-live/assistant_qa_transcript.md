### subtopic_1 — Geography and History in Social Sciences
**Assistant:** Is a map the same as a picture taken from an aeroplane?

**Tutor:** They are close cousins, but they are not the same. A photograph taken from an aeroplane or a satellite shows everything that is there: every tree, every car, every shadow and every roof, all in their real colours. It can be hard to read, because there is so much in it. A map is a drawing that a person makes on purpose. The mapmaker chooses what to show and leaves out what is not needed. Roads may be drawn as lines, a school as a small square, and a river as a blue line, even if the real river looks brown. A map also has names written on it, which a photograph does not have. Mapmakers often start from aerial photographs and then draw the map from them. So a map is like a tidy, simplified picture from above, made to help you find your way and understand a place.

### subtopic_2 — From Foundation Phase to Intermediate Phase
**Assistant:** What should I do if I get a source-based question and do not understand the source?

**Tutor:** First, do not panic. Read the heading or title of the source, because it tells you what the source is about. If it is a picture, look at it slowly, from one side to the other, and say to yourself what you can see: people, buildings, roads, animals. If it is a piece of writing, read it twice, and underline words that look important. If it is a map, look for the key, which explains the symbols. Then read the question carefully. Most questions ask about something you can find in the source, so look back at the source to find the answer, instead of guessing. If a word in the question is new to you, look at the words around it for a clue. Answer in a full sentence when you can. With practice, you will find that sources are like clues in a puzzle. The answer is often right there in front of you.

### subtopic_3 — Places Where People Live
**Assistant:** Why did people build cities in places with no river nearby, like Johannesburg?

**Tutor:** That is a sharp question, because most old settlements did start near rivers. Johannesburg is different. It grew very fast after gold was found there in 1886. People came from all over South Africa and the world to work on the gold mines, and a city grew around the mines. Gold, and the jobs it brought, was the reason for the settlement, not water. But a city still needs water. Johannesburg gets most of its water from the Vaal River, and also from dams far away in the mountains of Lesotho. Huge pipes and tunnels carry the water to the city. So people can build a settlement where there is no river, but then they must work hard to bring water to it. This shows that settlements grow where there is something people need, such as water, good soil, work, or a safe harbour.
