# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the four kinds of places where people live: farms, villages, towns and cities. It shows how they differ in size, in the number of people, in the buildings and services they have, and in how close together the homes are. The anchor is a Saturday trip by Lerato, a Grade 4 learner who lives on a farm near Bethlehem in the eastern Free State. Her family leaves the farm early in the morning, drives past a village with a small church and a school, stops in the town of Bethlehem to buy supplies, and then drives on to the city of Bloemfontein to visit her aunt. Out of the car window, Lerato watches the places get bigger and busier. The big idea is that settlements come in different sizes, from the small farm to the large city, and that bigger settlements have more people, more buildings and more services. Describing and comparing the four kinds of settlement is examinable.

## Subtopic: Life on a Farm

A farm is a large piece of land where people grow crops, keep animals, or both. Lerato's farm grows wheat and maize and keeps a herd of cattle.

A farm is the smallest kind of settlement. Only a few families live there: the farmer's family and the families of the farmworkers. The houses are far apart, with big fields and open land between them. On a farm, the land is the most important thing, because it is where the food grows.

Most of the buildings on a farm are for the work of farming. There is the farmhouse, a few homes for the workers, a big shed for tractors and machines, a store for grain, and shelters for the animals, such as a cattle shed, a milking shed or a chicken house. There may be a dam to store water, and a windmill to pump water from under the ground.

A farm has few services. There is usually no shop, no school, no clinic and no bank. To go to school or buy what they need, people on farms must travel to a village or a town. Lerato travels by bus to school every day. The roads on farms are often gravel roads, made of stones and sand, not tar.

Almost all the people on a farm do one kind of work: farming. They plough, plant, water, harvest, feed the animals, fix fences and drive tractors.

Farms are found in the countryside, which is also called a rural area. Rural means the countryside, where there is lots of open land and few people.

The questions for this section are with you now: what a farm is, the buildings on a farm, and rural areas.

## Subtopic: Villages and Towns

A village is a small settlement in a rural area. More people live in a village than on a farm, perhaps a few hundred or a few thousand. The homes are closer together, but there is still open land around them for gardens, fields and grazing animals.

A village usually has a few services. Along the road, Lerato's family drives through a village with a primary school, a church, a small shop called a spaza shop, and a water tap that people share. Some villages have a clinic. Many people in villages grow vegetables, keep chickens or goats, or travel to a town to work.

In many parts of South Africa, especially in the Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal and Limpopo, villages are spread out across the hills, with homesteads of round and square houses.

A town is bigger than a village. Thousands of people live there, and the buildings are close together along streets with names. Bethlehem is a town. When Lerato's family stops there, they see shops, a supermarket, banks, a post office, a hospital, a police station, a high school, a petrol station, churches and offices. People in a town do many different jobs.

A town is a service centre for the farms and villages around it. People come from the countryside to buy, to bank, to see a doctor, or to sell their crops. Lerato's father buys seed and diesel in Bethlehem.

A town is not countryside any more. It is the beginning of an urban area. Urban means a built-up place with many buildings, roads and people.

The questions for this section are with you now: villages, towns, services, and rural and urban areas.

## Subtopic: Cities

A city is the biggest kind of settlement. Many thousands, even millions, of people live in a city. Bloemfontein is a city, and it is the capital of the Free State province. It is also the judicial capital of South Africa, where the highest appeal court, called the Supreme Court of Appeal, is found.

As the family drives into Bloemfontein, Lerato sees tall office buildings, many traffic lights, busy roads, shopping centres, factories, a university, big hospitals, sports stadiums, and thousands of houses and flats packed close together. There is a lot of traffic: cars, taxis, buses and trucks.

A city has the most services of all, and people do the greatest number of different jobs. Some work in offices, some in factories, some in shops, hospitals and schools, and some drive taxis or build new buildings.

Other big cities in South Africa include Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, Pretoria and Gqeberha. Johannesburg is the biggest city in the country.

So here is the size ladder, from smallest to biggest: farm, village, town, city. As we climb the ladder, there are more people, the buildings are closer together, there are more services, and there are more kinds of jobs. Farms and villages are rural. Towns and cities are urban.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a farm has shops and a school; most farms do not, so people travel to a village or town. Two: thinking town and city mean exactly the same thing; a city is much bigger, with many more people. Three: thinking rural means a city; rural means the countryside. Four: thinking people in a city all do the same job; cities have the most kinds of jobs.

The questions for this section are with you now: cities, and comparing the four kinds of settlement.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with Lerato's road trip — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: The Farm

A farm has lots of land and only a few families.

People farm crops and animals.

There are no shops or schools nearby.

Keep the wheat fields near Bethlehem in mind and try the questions for this part: farms.

## Subtopic: Village and Town

A village is small, with a school and a small shop.

A town is bigger, with banks, a hospital and streets.

Farms and villages are rural. Towns are urban.

Hold the stop in Bethlehem in mind and try this part's questions: villages and towns.

## Subtopic: The City

A city is the biggest settlement.

Tall buildings, busy roads, many jobs.

Pocket summary of the lesson. There are four kinds of settlement. A farm has a lot of land and few people. A village is small, with a few services. A town is bigger, with many shops and services. A city is the biggest, with the most people, buildings and jobs. Farms and villages are rural, and towns and cities are urban.

Hold the busy streets of Bloemfontein in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: cities.
