# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the buildings found in places where people live, and what each kind of building is used for: houses, animal shelters, shops, schools, clinics, banks, places of worship, factories and stations. The anchor is a walk by a Grade 4 class in Kimberley, the capital city of the Northern Cape. Their teacher gives each learner a clipboard with a table. In the first column they write the name of a building they see, and in the second column they write what it is used for. The class walks from the school through the streets of houses, past the shops and the clinic, down to the city centre and the railway station. Later, they compare their list with a list of buildings in a village. The big idea is that every building has a use, and that the buildings in a place tell us how people live and work there. Naming buildings and their uses is examinable.

## Subtopic: Houses and Animal Shelters

The most common building in any settlement is a house. A house is a home, a shelter where people sleep, cook, eat, wash and keep their belongings safe. It protects people from rain, wind, heat and cold.

Houses come in many shapes and are made of many materials. On their walk in Kimberley, the learners see brick houses with tin or tiled roofs, a block of flats where many families live in one tall building, and a small house built from corrugated iron sheets. In villages in many parts of South Africa, people build round houses called rondavels, with walls of mud bricks and roofs of thatch, which is dried grass. Thatch keeps a house cool in summer and warm in winter.

People who keep animals also build shelters for them. A kraal is a fenced enclosure, often round, where cattle, goats or sheep are kept at night to keep them safe from thieves and wild animals. Kraals can be built from thorny branches, stones or wooden poles. A chicken coop is a small shelter where chickens sleep and lay eggs. A stable is a building for horses, and a pigsty is a pen for pigs. On a big farm there may be a milking shed for cows.

Near the edge of Kimberley, the class sees a family's chicken coop in the back yard and, further on, a horse in a stable. Animal shelters are most common on farms and in villages, where many people keep animals.

The questions for this section are with you now: houses, building materials, and shelters for animals.

## Subtopic: Shops, Schools, Clinics and Banks

Many buildings in a settlement provide a service. A service is something useful that is done for people.

A shop is a building where people buy and sell goods, such as food, clothes and medicine. In a village there may be one small spaza shop. In Kimberley the learners pass a supermarket, a hardware store, a pharmacy and a big shopping centre with many shops under one roof.

A school is a building where children go to learn. It has classrooms, often a library and a sports field. Most villages have a primary school. Towns and cities have many primary and high schools, and some have colleges and universities.

A clinic is a building where nurses help people who are ill or hurt, check that babies are growing well, and give injections to protect children from disease. A hospital is bigger than a clinic. It has doctors, nurses and beds for people who must stay overnight. Kimberley has a large hospital that serves people from all over the Northern Cape.

A bank is a building where people keep their money safe, take out money, and borrow money. Banks are found mostly in towns and cities. Today many people also use bank machines, called ATMs, or bank on a cell phone.

Other service buildings include the post office, the police station, the fire station, the library and the municipal offices, where the local government works.

The questions for this section are with you now: service buildings and what they are used for.

## Subtopic: Places of Worship, Factories and Stations

A place of worship is a building where people gather to pray and practise their religion. South Africa has people of many faiths, and all are free to worship. Christians worship in a church, Muslims in a mosque, Jewish people in a synagogue and Hindus in a temple. In Kimberley the learners see a church with a tall steeple and a mosque with a dome and a tall tower called a minaret. Some people also worship in homes, in tents, or outside under a tree.

A factory is a building where people and machines make goods. Raw materials go into a factory, and finished goods come out. A factory might make bread, clothes, bricks, furniture or cars. Factories are usually built in towns and cities, near roads or railways, where there are workers and electricity.

A station is a building where journeys start and end. At a railway station, people get on and off trains, and goods are loaded. At a bus station or a taxi rank, people catch buses and taxis. Kimberley has a railway station on the main line between Johannesburg and Cape Town, and the city grew partly because of the railway. An airport is a station for aeroplanes.

Different settlements have different buildings. A farm has a farmhouse, workers' houses and animal shelters. A village has houses, a school, a small shop, a place of worship and perhaps a clinic. A city has all of these, and also banks, factories, big hospitals, stations and shopping centres.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a kraal is a house for people; it is a shelter for animals. Two: thinking a clinic and a hospital are exactly the same; a hospital is bigger, with doctors and beds. Three: thinking every religion uses a church; Muslims use a mosque, Jewish people a synagogue, and Hindus a temple. Four: thinking factories sell goods; factories make goods, and shops sell them.

The questions for this section are with you now: places of worship, factories, stations, and buildings in different settlements.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with the walk through Kimberley — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Homes for People and Animals

A house is a home where people live.

A kraal keeps cattle safe. A coop is for chickens.

Keep the chicken coop in the back yard in mind and try the questions for this part: houses and animal shelters.

## Subtopic: Service Buildings

A shop is for buying. A school is for learning.

A clinic helps sick people. A bank keeps money safe.

Hold the clipboard list in mind and try this part's questions: service buildings.

## Subtopic: Worship, Making and Travelling

Places of worship are for praying.

A factory makes goods. A station is where journeys start.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Every building has a use. Houses are homes, and animal shelters such as kraals and coops keep animals safe. Shops, schools, clinics and banks give services. Churches, mosques, synagogues and temples are places of worship. Factories make goods, and stations are where journeys start and end. Bigger settlements have more kinds of buildings.

Hold the railway station in Kimberley in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: worship, factories and stations.
