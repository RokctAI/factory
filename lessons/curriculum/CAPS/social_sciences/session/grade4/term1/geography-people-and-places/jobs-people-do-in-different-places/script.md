# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the jobs people do in different places to live, and why different settlements have different kinds of work. The anchor is career day at a primary school in Polokwane, the capital city of Limpopo. Five parents visit the Grade 4 class. Mr Mokoena farms tomatoes on a farm outside the city. Sister Ngobeni is a nurse at a village clinic. Mrs Baloyi drives a minibus taxi. Mr Maluleke works at a platinum mine. Ms Sithole works in a factory that makes fruit juice. Each parent brings something from work: a basket of tomatoes, a stethoscope, a taxi sign, a hard hat and a juice carton. The big idea is that the jobs people do depend on the place where they live: what the land offers, what buildings there are, and how many people live there. Naming jobs and matching them to farms, villages, towns and cities is examinable.

## Subtopic: Jobs on Farms and in Villages

On a farm, most jobs are about growing food or keeping animals. Mr Mokoena explains that tomatoes grow very well around Polokwane, because the area is warm. On his farm, people prepare the soil, plant seedlings, water the plants, pull out weeds, pick the ripe tomatoes and pack them into boxes. Other farms in Limpopo grow mangoes, avocados, bananas and potatoes, or keep cattle, goats and chickens.

Farm jobs include the farmer, who plans and runs the farm, and farmworkers, who do the planting, harvesting and caring for animals. There are also tractor drivers, people who fix machines and fences, and people who milk cows. Much farm work changes with the seasons. Planting happens in one season and harvesting in another.

In a village there are a few more kinds of jobs, because a village has a few services. There are teachers at the village school and nurses at the clinic. Sister Ngobeni works at a clinic in a village near Polokwane. She checks babies, gives injections to keep children healthy, and helps people who are ill. In a village there may also be a shopkeeper at the spaza shop, a person who fixes shoes or bicycles, a builder, someone who sells vegetables at the roadside, and a religious leader at the church.

Many people in villages also grow food in their own gardens and fields, and keep chickens and goats. And many travel each day to work in a nearby town or city, because there are not enough jobs in the village.

The questions for this section are with you now: jobs on farms, jobs in villages, and seasonal farm work.

## Subtopic: Jobs in Towns and Cities

Towns and cities have many more kinds of jobs, because they have many more people and many more buildings.

Mrs Baloyi drives a minibus taxi between the townships and the centre of Polokwane. Every day she carries people to their work, to school and to the shops. In a city, many people work in transport: taxi drivers, bus drivers, truck drivers and people who fix cars.

Ms Sithole works in a factory. Fruit from the farms of Limpopo comes to the factory by truck. Machines wash and squeeze the fruit, and workers check the juice, fill the cartons and pack them into boxes to send to shops all over the country. Factories are usually in towns and cities, where there are workers, roads and electricity.

Mr Maluleke is a miner. Limpopo has rich mines, especially for platinum, a precious metal. A mine is built wherever the minerals are found, and a town often grows up near it.

In towns and cities, people also work in shops and supermarkets, banks, post offices, offices, hospitals, schools, restaurants, hotels and police stations. Some people build houses and roads. Some clean streets and collect rubbish. Some work for the government. Some sell goods at markets or on the pavement.

Each building in a town or city is a workplace. A hospital needs doctors, nurses, cleaners and cooks. A school needs teachers, a principal, an office worker and a caretaker.

The questions for this section are with you now: jobs in towns and cities, factories and mines.

## Subtopic: Why Jobs Differ from Place to Place

Why do jobs differ from place to place? There are three main reasons.

First, what the land offers. Where there is good soil and enough rain, people farm. Where there are minerals under the ground, people mine. Where there is a sea, people fish and work in harbours. So you find fishers in a coastal town such as Hout Bay, and miners near the platinum mines of Limpopo and North West.

Second, how many people live there. A farm has only a few people, so it needs only a few jobs. A city has many people, who need food, transport, schools, health care and shops, so a city has the most kinds of jobs.

Third, what buildings and services there are. A village with only a school and a clinic has teachers and nurses, but no bankers. A city with banks, offices and factories has bankers, office workers and factory workers.

Jobs in different places also depend on each other. Mr Mokoena's tomatoes are carried by truck drivers to a market in the city, where shopkeepers sell them to people who work in offices and factories. Ms Sithole's factory needs fruit from farmers. The farm, the village, the town and the city all need one another.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking all jobs are in cities; farms and villages have important jobs too. Two: thinking farm work is the same all year; it changes with the seasons. Three: thinking a mine can be built anywhere; it must be where the minerals are. Four: thinking farms and cities do not need each other; cities need food from farms, and farms need buyers and goods from cities.

The questions for this section are with you now: why jobs differ, and how jobs in different places depend on each other.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again with career day in Polokwane — plain words, same facts.

## Subtopic: Farm and Village Jobs

On a farm, people grow crops and keep animals.

In a village, there are teachers, nurses and shopkeepers.

Many village people travel to town to work.

Keep the basket of tomatoes in mind and try the questions for this part: farm and village jobs.

## Subtopic: Town and City Jobs

Towns and cities have the most kinds of jobs.

Drivers, factory workers, bankers, shop workers.

Every building is a workplace.

Hold the taxi sign and the juice carton in mind and try this part's questions: town and city jobs.

## Subtopic: The Place Decides

Land, people and buildings decide the jobs.

Farms and cities need each other.

Pocket summary of the lesson. People do different jobs in different places. On farms, people grow crops and keep animals. In villages there are a few jobs, such as teachers, nurses and shopkeepers. Towns and cities have the most kinds of jobs, in shops, banks, offices, factories, hospitals and transport. The land, the number of people and the buildings decide the jobs in a place.

Hold the five parents on career day in mind and take the final questions of the lesson: why jobs differ.
