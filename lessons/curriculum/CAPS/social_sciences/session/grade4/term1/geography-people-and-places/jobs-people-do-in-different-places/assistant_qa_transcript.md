### subtopic_1 — Jobs on Farms and in Villages
**Assistant:** Why do so many people in villages travel to town for work?

**Tutor:** Most villages have only a few buildings that offer jobs: a school, a clinic, a few shops and maybe a church. So there are only a few jobs for teachers, nurses and shopkeepers. But many people in the village need to earn money to buy food, clothes, school uniforms and electricity. Towns and cities have many more workplaces, such as factories, offices, shops, mines, building sites and other people's homes. So every morning, many people leave the village early by taxi or bus to work in a town or city, and come home in the evening. Some people work so far away that they can only come home at weekends, or even only at the end of the year. This is called migrant work, and it has a long history in South Africa. It can be hard for families, because a parent may be away from home for a long time. That is one reason why creating more jobs in rural areas is so important.

### subtopic_2 — Jobs in Towns and Cities
**Assistant:** What is platinum used for?

**Tutor:** Platinum is a shiny, silvery precious metal. It is very rare and very valuable, even more rare than gold. South Africa has the biggest platinum deposits in the world, mostly in a great band of rock called the Bushveld Complex, which stretches across parts of Limpopo, North West and Mpumalanga. One of the most important uses of platinum is in cars. A part of the exhaust, called a catalytic converter, uses platinum to make the exhaust fumes less harmful. Platinum is also used to make jewellery, such as rings, and in some medical equipment, in computers and in factories that make chemicals. Because platinum is so useful, it is sold to many other countries, and the money helps the economy of South Africa. Mining platinum gives jobs to many thousands of people, like Mr Maluleke. But mining is hard and sometimes dangerous work, deep underground, so miners must wear hard hats, boots and other safety gear.

### subtopic_3 — Why Jobs Differ from Place to Place
**Assistant:** Can a job move from one place to another?

**Tutor:** Yes, some jobs can move, and some cannot. A miner's job cannot move, because the minerals are in one place under the ground. A fisher's job cannot move away from the sea. A farmer needs land with the right soil and rain. These jobs are tied to the land. But many other jobs can move. A factory might move to a new town where there are good roads, electricity and workers. A shop might open a new branch in a growing village. A teacher can teach in a school in any settlement. Today, because of computers and cell phones, some people can even do office work from home, far away from a city. When new jobs come to a place, more people often move there, and the settlement grows. When jobs leave, for example when a mine closes, people may have to move away to find work, and the settlement can shrink. So jobs and settlements change together over time.
