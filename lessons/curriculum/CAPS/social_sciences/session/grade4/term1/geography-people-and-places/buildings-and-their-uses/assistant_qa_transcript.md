### subtopic_1 — Houses and Animal Shelters
**Assistant:** Why are some houses round and others square?

**Tutor:** Long ago, many African communities built round houses, and many still do today. A round house uses the materials around it: mud or stone for the walls and grass for the thatch roof. A round shape is strong, because there are no corners where the walls can crack. It is also easy to put a cone-shaped thatch roof on round walls, and the wind blows smoothly around it. Square and rectangular houses became more common when people started using bricks, cement, wooden beams and sheets of iron for roofs. It is easier to build straight walls with these materials, to divide the inside into rooms, and to put furniture against flat walls. Today, many homesteads in the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal have both round and square buildings side by side. So the shape of a house depends on the materials, the skills of the builder, the weather and the way of life of the people who live in it.

### subtopic_2 — Shops, Schools, Clinics and Banks
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a clinic and a hospital?

**Tutor:** Both help people to stay healthy and get better, but they are different sizes and do different jobs. A clinic is small. It is usually run by nurses, and sometimes a doctor visits on certain days. At a clinic you can get help for an ordinary illness, injections for babies, medicine for long illnesses, and advice about staying healthy. Most clinics are open during the day, and people go home after their visit. A hospital is much bigger. It has many doctors and nurses, and special rooms called wards with beds, where people stay overnight or for many days. A hospital has operating theatres where surgeons do operations, and machines such as X-ray machines to see inside the body. If a person is very ill, the nurse at the clinic may send them to the hospital. Villages often have a clinic, but hospitals are usually in towns and cities, so people from rural areas may travel a long way to reach one.

### subtopic_3 — Places of Worship, Factories and Stations
**Assistant:** Why do factories need to be near roads and railways?

**Tutor:** A factory needs a lot of things to come in, and a lot of things to go out. Raw materials, such as wheat for bread, cotton for clothes or clay for bricks, must be brought to the factory, often from far away. After the factory has made its goods, they must be sent to shops all over the country, and sometimes to other countries. Trucks carry goods on roads, and trains carry heavy goods on railways. If a factory were built far from any road or railway, it would be very hard and expensive to move all these things. Factories also need many workers, who must be able to get there each day by taxi, bus or train. And they need electricity and water to run the machines. That is why factories are usually built in towns and cities, often in a special area called an industrial area, close to main roads and railway lines.
