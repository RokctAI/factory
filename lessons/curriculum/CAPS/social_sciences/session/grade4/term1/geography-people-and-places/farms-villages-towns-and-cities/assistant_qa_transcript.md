### subtopic_1 — Life on a Farm
**Assistant:** Why do farmworkers live on the farm and not in town?

**Tutor:** There are a few reasons. Farm work starts very early. Cows must be milked at dawn, and animals must be fed and watered every day, even on Sundays and holidays. If the workers lived in a town far away, they would have to travel for a long time every morning, and many do not have cars. So it is easier for them to live close to their work. Also, on a farm there is often something that needs attention at night, such as a cow giving birth or a fire that must be put out. Today, some farmworkers do live in nearby towns and villages and travel to the farm every day by bus or bakkie, especially where farms are close to town. And many farm families send their children to school in a nearby village or town, on a school bus. So where farmworkers live depends on how far the farm is from town and what kind of farming happens there.

### subtopic_2 — Villages and Towns
**Assistant:** How does a village become a town?

**Tutor:** A village can slowly grow into a town over many years. It usually starts with a reason for more people to come. Perhaps a road or a railway line is built through the village, so it becomes easy to reach. Perhaps a mine opens nearby, or a factory, or the soil around it is good for farming. People come to find work. They need homes, so more houses are built. They need to buy food, so more shops open. Then a bank, a post office, a clinic and a police station may follow. Streets are laid out and given names. When there are many people and many services, people start to call the place a town. Many South African towns started like this, as a church, a trading store or a stop on a railway line, and grew bigger and bigger. Some towns have grown so much that they became cities.

### subtopic_3 — Cities
**Assistant:** Is Bloemfontein the capital of South Africa?

**Tutor:** South Africa is unusual, because it has three capital cities, each with a different job. Pretoria, in Gauteng, is the administrative capital, where the President has offices and where government departments do their work. Cape Town, in the Western Cape, is the legislative capital, where Parliament meets to make the country's laws. Bloemfontein, in the Free State, is the judicial capital, where the Supreme Court of Appeal is found. Courts are about justice and the law, which is what judicial means. The Constitutional Court, the highest court on questions about the Constitution, is in Johannesburg. So Bloemfontein is one of South Africa's three capital cities. It is also the capital of the Free State province, which means the provincial government works there. Every province has its own capital city.
