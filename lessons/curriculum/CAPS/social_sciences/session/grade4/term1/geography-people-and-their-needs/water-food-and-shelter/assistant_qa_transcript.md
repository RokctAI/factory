### subtopic_1 — Water
**Assistant:** Why can people live longer without food than without water?

**Tutor:** Your body is more than half water. Water is in your blood, in your muscles and even in your bones. Every day, you lose water when you breathe out, when you sweat and when you go to the toilet. That water must be replaced, or your body cannot work properly. Without water, your blood gets thick, your body overheats, and important organs such as the kidneys and brain begin to fail within a few days, even sooner in hot weather. Food is different. Your body stores energy as fat, and it can use that store for a while when there is no food. So a person can survive for some weeks without eating, although they become very weak. This is why, in emergencies such as floods or droughts, getting clean water to people is always one of the very first things rescue workers do.

### subtopic_2 — Food
**Assistant:** What are mopane worms, and do people really eat them?

**Tutor:** Yes, many people in Limpopo, and in neighbouring countries such as Botswana and Zimbabwe, eat mopane worms, and they are a traditional, much-loved food. A mopane worm is not really a worm. It is the large, colourful caterpillar of the mopane emperor moth. The caterpillars feed on the leaves of the mopane tree, which grows in hot, dry areas. People pick them from the trees, squeeze out the insides, and dry them in the sun or smoke them. Dried mopane worms can be stored for months without a fridge. They are cooked in a stew with tomato and onion, or eaten crunchy as a snack. They are very good food, because they contain lots of protein, which helps the body grow. Collecting and selling mopane worms also gives many families in rural areas a way to earn money. So they are a good example of people using what nature provides to meet their need for food.

### subtopic_3 — Shelter
**Assistant:** Why do some people live in shacks?

**Tutor:** Many people move from rural areas to towns and cities to look for work. When they arrive, they often have very little money. Building or renting a brick house costs a lot, so some people build a home from whatever materials they can afford, such as sheets of corrugated iron, wood and plastic. These homes are called informal homes, or shacks, and areas with many of them are called informal settlements. A shack does give shelter, but it can be cold in winter, hot in summer, and leaky in heavy rain. Shacks are built close together, often without enough taps, toilets or electricity, and fires can spread quickly between them. The government builds houses for people who cannot afford them, and many families have moved into new homes, but there are still many people waiting. It is important to remember that people living in shacks are working hard to make a better life, and they deserve respect.
