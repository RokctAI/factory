# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers three things that all people need: water, food and shelter. It explains why each one is a need, and how people in different places, on farms, in villages, in towns and in cities, get them. The anchor is one day in the life of Tsakani, a Grade 4 learner who lives with her grandmother in a village near Giyani, in Limpopo. Before sunrise, Tsakani carries a bucket to the communal tap and back. After school, she helps her grandmother water the vegetable garden, where spinach, tomatoes and pumpkins grow, and they cook maize meal porridge on the stove. At night, the family sleeps safe and dry in their house of cement blocks with a corrugated iron roof. The big idea is that all people, everywhere, have the same basic needs, but the ways they meet those needs depend on the place where they live. Explaining people's needs for water, food and shelter is examinable.

## Subtopic: Water

A need is something we must have to stay alive and healthy. A want is something we would like to have but can live without, such as a new toy or a cell phone. Water is the most important need of all. A person can live for some weeks without food, but only a few days without water.

People need water for drinking, for cooking, for washing their bodies, their clothes and their dishes, and for keeping homes and toilets clean. Farmers need water for their crops and animals. The water we drink must be clean and safe. Dirty water can carry germs that cause illnesses such as diarrhoea, which can be very dangerous, especially for babies and small children.

People get their water in different ways. In Tsakani's village, many families share a communal tap in the street, and people carry the water home in buckets or containers. Some people collect water from a river or spring, or pump it from a borehole. On farms, a windmill or an electric pump often brings water up from under the ground into a tank. In towns and cities, most homes have taps inside, connected by pipes to dams and water treatment works.

Water is precious. South Africa is a dry country, and many places do not get much rain. Everyone can help to save water by closing taps, fixing leaks and using only what they need. Tsakani uses the water from washing vegetables to water the garden, so that not a drop is wasted. When water must be carried a long way, every bucket matters, and families learn to use it very carefully.

The questions for this section are with you now: needs and wants, why people need water, and how people get their water.

## Subtopic: Food

Food is a need. It gives our bodies energy to work and play, helps us grow, and keeps us healthy and strong. We need different kinds of food. Starchy foods, such as maize meal, bread, rice and potatoes, give energy. Foods such as beans, eggs, milk, meat and fish help the body grow and repair itself. Fruit and vegetables, such as spinach, pumpkin, oranges and mangoes, help keep us healthy. A healthy diet has a mix of these foods.

People get their food in different ways. Some people grow their own food. Tsakani's grandmother grows vegetables in her garden and keeps chickens for eggs. Many families in villages grow maize in a field. On farms, people grow crops and keep animals, and some of it is for the family. Most people in towns and cities buy their food from shops, supermarkets, markets and street sellers.

To buy food, people need money. Some people struggle to get enough food every day. Schools in South Africa help by giving many learners a meal at school, through the school feeding programme. Communities sometimes run soup kitchens and food gardens to help people who are hungry.

Food is also part of culture. Pap, morogo, which is wild spinach, mopane worms, chakalaka and bunny chow are some South African foods, and each family and community has its own favourite dishes.

The questions for this section are with you now: why we need food, kinds of food, and how people get food.

## Subtopic: Shelter

Shelter is a place that protects people from the weather and keeps them safe. A home protects us from rain, wind, heat and cold, and from danger at night. It is a place to sleep, rest, cook, eat and keep our belongings.

Shelters differ from place to place. Tsakani's house is built of cement blocks with a corrugated iron roof. In many villages there are also round houses, called rondavels, built of mud bricks with thatched roofs. In towns and cities there are brick houses, townhouses and tall blocks of flats where many families live. Some families live in informal homes, often called shacks, made of corrugated iron sheets, wood and plastic, because they cannot afford a brick house.

People build with the materials they can find and afford. Mud and thatch are found nearby and cost little. Bricks, cement and iron must be bought. The weather matters too. In hot places, thick walls and thatch keep homes cool. In cold places, homes need warm walls and a way to heat them.

A good shelter keeps out the rain, has a strong roof that will not blow away, lets in light and fresh air, and is safe from fire. Some people have no shelter at all. They are homeless and must sleep outside, which is cold and dangerous. Helping people get safe homes is an important job for the government and for communities.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a cell phone is a need; it is useful, but it is a want, because we can live without it. Two: thinking any water is safe to drink; dirty water can carry germs. Three: thinking all people grow their own food; most people in towns and cities buy it. Four: thinking only brick houses are shelters; any home that protects people from the weather is a shelter.

The questions for this section are with you now: what shelter is, different kinds of homes, and what makes a good shelter.
