### subtopic_1 — Places Where People Live
**Assistant:** How should I study for a source-based test?

**Tutor:** Practise reading sources, because that is what the test will ask you to do. Ask someone at home to show you a picture from a newspaper or a magazine, and describe what you see: the people, buildings, transport and landmarks. Look at a simple map of your area and find the landmarks. Read a short paragraph and pick out the names, places and dates. Then practise answering questions in full sentences. In the test, always read the heading or caption of the source first, then the question, and then look back at the source to find your answer. Many answers are right there in the source. Learn the key words of the term, such as settlement, rural, urban, landmark, need, want, source and oral, and be able to explain each one in a sentence. For the paragraph question, practise writing four or five sentences on one topic, such as the four kinds of settlements, with a clear first sentence that says what the paragraph is about.

### subtopic_2 — Finding the Way and People's Needs
**Assistant:** Are the Geography and History topics in Term 1 connected?

**Tutor:** Yes, they are connected in interesting ways. In Geography we learnt about the places where people live: farms, villages, towns and cities, their buildings, roads and landmarks. In History we learnt how to find out about the past of our own area. If you put them together, you can ask questions such as: how did my settlement start? Why was it built near this river? When was the main road tarred? How has the town grown from a village into a town? What did the buildings look like fifty years ago? Old photographs, maps and interviews can answer these geography questions about the past. And landmarks are often historical too. A very old church, a station or a big old tree has a story to tell. Many local history projects are really about how a place has changed over time, which is both history and geography. Thinking about both subjects together helps you understand your area much better.

### subtopic_3 — Local History and Sources
**Assistant:** What is the best way to remember the four kinds of sources?

**Tutor:** Here are a few tricks. Use your hands: picture a camera for pictures, a pen for writing, a mouth for stories, and an open hand holding something for objects. Or remember the first letters P, W, S, O, and make a silly sentence, such as: Please Write Short Openings. Another trick is to think of your own home. In your home there are probably old photographs, which are pictures. There may be a birth certificate or a letter, which is writing. There are older people who remember things, which are stories. And there are old things in a cupboard, such as a pot, a coin or a tool, which are objects. Every home is a small museum. Finally, practise by sorting. When you see something from the past, ask yourself which kind of source it is. With practice, you will sort them quickly.
