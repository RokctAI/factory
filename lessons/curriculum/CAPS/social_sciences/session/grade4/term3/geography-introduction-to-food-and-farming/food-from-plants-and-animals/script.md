# Part 1 — Expert

This session introduces the Geography topic for Term 3, food and farming in South Africa. It explains that all the food people eat comes from plants or animals, and shows how to classify foods into plant foods and animal foods, and into smaller groups. The anchor is a food market set up by a Grade 4 class in Bloemfontein, in the Free State. The learners have brought food from home and from the shops, and laid it out on long tables in the school hall: a bag of maize meal, a loaf of bread, apples, oranges, a pumpkin, bags of dried beans, a bunch of spinach, peanut butter, sunflower oil, a carton of milk, a tray of eggs, a block of cheese, a tin of pilchards, a packet of boerewors and a jar of honey. Two big cards stand at the ends of the tables: one says plants, and the other says animals. The big idea is that all our food comes from plants or animals, which are grown or raised by farmers or collected from nature, and that sorting, or classifying, food helps us understand where it comes from. Classifying food from plants and animals is examinable.

## Subtopic: Food from Plants

Many of the foods we eat come from plants. People eat different parts of plants.

Some foods are seeds. Grains, also called cereals, are the seeds of grass plants, such as maize, wheat, rice and sorghum. Maize meal, used to make pap, is ground maize seeds. Bread is made from flour, which is ground wheat seeds. Beans, lentils and peas are seeds that grow in pods. Peanuts and sunflower seeds are seeds too, and sunflower oil is squeezed from sunflower seeds.

Some foods are fruits, the part of the plant that holds the seeds, such as apples, oranges, mangoes, bananas, grapes, tomatoes and pumpkins.

Some foods are leaves, such as spinach, cabbage and lettuce. In many South African homes, people eat morogo, which is a mix of wild or garden green leaves.

Some foods are roots or underground stems, such as carrots, beetroot, sweet potatoes and potatoes.

Even sugar comes from a plant. Most of South Africa's sugar comes from sugar cane, a tall grass that grows in KwaZulu-Natal and Mpumalanga.

At the market, the learners carry the maize meal, bread, apples, oranges, pumpkin, beans, spinach, peanut butter and sunflower oil to the plants card.

Foods from plants give us energy, and fruit and vegetables help keep us healthy. Plant foods are also usually cheaper than animal foods, so in many homes, plant foods such as pap and beans make up most of each meal.

The questions for this section are with you now: the parts of plants we eat and foods made from plants.

## Subtopic: Food from Animals

Many other foods come from animals.

Some foods are the meat of animals. Beef comes from cattle, mutton and lamb from sheep, pork from pigs, and chicken from chickens. Boerewors is a sausage made mostly from beef and pork. People in some areas also eat goat meat and game, such as springbok and kudu.

Some foods are products that animals make without being killed. Milk comes mainly from cows, and also from goats. Milk can be made into cheese, yoghurt, butter and maas, which is sour milk that many South Africans love. Eggs come from hens. Honey is made by bees from the nectar of flowers.

Fish and other seafood come from the sea, rivers and dams. Pilchards, hake and snoek are fish caught in the sea off South Africa's coast.

At the market, the learners carry the milk, eggs, cheese, pilchards, boerewors and honey to the animals card.

Foods from animals, such as meat, fish, eggs and milk, help the body to grow and repair itself.

Some people choose not to eat meat. They are called vegetarians. They get the food their bodies need from beans, nuts, grains and vegetables. Some people do not eat certain animal foods because of their religion. Muslims and Jewish people do not eat pork, and many Hindus do not eat beef.

The questions for this section are with you now: meat, animal products and fish.

## Subtopic: Classifying Food

To classify means to sort things into groups that belong together. Classifying food helps us understand where it comes from and what kind of food it is.

The first, big way to classify food is into two groups: food from plants and food from animals. Some foods can be tricky. Cheese does not look like a cow, but it is made from milk, so it is an animal food. Peanut butter is made from peanuts, so it is a plant food. To classify a food that is made from other foods, think about its main ingredient.

Some foods are made from both. A pizza has a base made from wheat flour and tomatoes, which are plant foods, and cheese, which is an animal food. A meat and vegetable stew has both too.

We can also classify food into smaller groups. Plant foods can be sorted into grains, fruit, vegetables, and beans and nuts. Animal foods can be sorted into meat, fish, eggs and dairy, which means milk and foods made from milk.

Finally, we can classify food by how it is produced. Food that is grown or raised by people, such as maize, apples, cattle and chickens, comes from farming. Food that is collected from nature, such as wild fruit, honey from wild bees and fish from the sea, is gathered or caught. Most of the food South Africans eat comes from farms, which is why this topic is called food and farming.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking cheese is a plant food; it is made from milk, so it is an animal food. Two: thinking bread is an animal food; it is made from wheat, a plant. Three: thinking honey comes from plants only; it is made by bees, so it is an animal product. Four: thinking all food comes from shops; shops sell it, but it comes from plants and animals on farms or in nature.

The questions for this section are with you now: classifying food into groups, tricky foods, and farming.
