### subtopic_1 — Food from Plants
**Assistant:** Is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable?

**Tutor:** That is a famous question, and the answer depends on who you ask. To a scientist who studies plants, a tomato is a fruit, because it grows from the flower of the plant and holds the seeds inside. By that rule, pumpkins, cucumbers, green peppers and even mealies, the cobs of maize, are fruits too. But in the kitchen, people call a tomato a vegetable, because it is not sweet and is usually cooked in savoury dishes, like stews and chakalaka, or eaten in salads, instead of being eaten as a sweet snack or dessert. So both answers can be right, depending on whether you are thinking like a scientist or like a cook. For Social Sciences, the most important thing is that a tomato is a plant food, grown by farmers. South Africa grows a lot of tomatoes, especially in Limpopo, where the warm weather suits them well.

### subtopic_2 — Food from Animals
**Assistant:** How do bees make honey?

**Tutor:** Bees fly from flower to flower, sucking up a sweet liquid called nectar with their long tongues. They store the nectar in a special honey stomach, separate from the stomach they use for their own food. Back at the hive, they pass the nectar from bee to bee, mouth to mouth. As they do this, substances from their bodies change the nectar, making it thicker. Then they put it into the little six-sided wax cells of the honeycomb, and fan it with their wings to dry out more of the water. When it is thick enough, it is honey, and the bees seal the cell with a cap of wax. Bees make honey to feed the colony during times when there are few flowers. Beekeepers keep bees in wooden boxes and collect some of the extra honey. People have also collected wild honey in Africa for thousands of years, and San rock paintings even show people collecting honey.

### subtopic_3 — Classifying Food
**Assistant:** What is maas, and how is it made?

**Tutor:** Maas is thick, sour milk. In isiZulu it is called amasi. It is one of the oldest and best-loved foods in many South African communities. Long ago, families kept fresh cow's milk in a special container, such as a calabash, which is a dried, hollow gourd, or a clay pot or skin bag. They left it to stand for a few days. Tiny living things called bacteria, which are naturally present, slowly turn the milk sour and thick. Each time milk was poured out, a little of the old maas was left behind to help the next batch start. The whey, a watery liquid, was poured off, leaving the thick curds. People eat maas on its own, or with crumbly pap called umphokoqo. Maas is good for us, because it contains protein and calcium for strong bones, and the helpful bacteria are good for digestion. Today, most people buy maas in cartons at the shop, made in dairy factories.
