### subtopic_1 — Why Grow Food in Towns and Cities
**Assistant:** How long does it take to grow spinach?

**Tutor:** Spinach, also called Swiss chard in many South African gardens, is one of the easiest and quickest vegetables to grow, which is why it is so popular in community and school gardens. You can plant the seeds straight into the soil, about two centimetres deep, or plant small seedlings bought from a nursery. With enough water and sunshine, the first leaves are ready to pick in about eight weeks, and sometimes sooner. The best thing about Swiss chard is that you do not pull up the whole plant. You pick the big outer leaves and leave the small inner leaves to keep growing. That way, one plant can keep giving leaves for many months. It grows in most parts of South Africa and in most seasons, though it does best in cooler weather. Spinach is very good for you, because it is full of vitamins and iron, which helps your blood carry oxygen around your body.

### subtopic_2 — Where and How
**Assistant:** How does a keyhole garden work?

**Tutor:** A keyhole garden is a clever design that was made popular in Africa by organisations working in dry countries like Lesotho. It is a round garden bed, about two metres across, with walls of stones or bricks about knee-high, and a narrow path cut into one side, so the shape from above looks like a keyhole. In the very middle stands a tall basket made of sticks and grass. The gardener fills the bed with layers of soil, compost, manure, dry leaves and old cardboard, sloping down from the middle. Every day, kitchen scraps and grey water, the used water from washing dishes or vegetables, go into the middle basket. As the scraps rot, they feed the plants growing all around, and the water soaks slowly outwards into the soil. The raised walls make it easy to work without bending too much, which helps older gardeners. The thick layers hold water well, so a keyhole garden can grow vegetables using much less water than an ordinary bed.

### subtopic_3 — Benefits and Challenges
**Assistant:** Can you really grow vegetables on a roof?

**Tutor:** Yes, and it is done in many cities around the world, including Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban. A flat roof gets lots of sunshine, which plants need, and it is space that would otherwise be empty. But there are some things to be careful about. Soil and water are heavy, so engineers must check that the roof is strong enough. Gardeners usually use light soil mixes and grow plants in tubs, boxes, or special beds with thin layers of soil. They also make sure that water drains away properly, so the roof does not leak into the building below. Rooftops can be windy and hot, so gardeners may put up shade cloth and choose tough plants, such as lettuce, herbs, spinach, tomatoes and peppers. Some rooftop farms grow vegetables to sell to restaurants nearby. A rooftop garden also keeps the building below a little cooler in summer, and it gives birds and bees a green place to visit in the middle of the city.
