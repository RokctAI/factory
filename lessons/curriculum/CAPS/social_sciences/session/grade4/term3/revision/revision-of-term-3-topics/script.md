## Subtopic: Food and Ways of Farming

The first stop is the orange tree. An orange comes from a plant. Food comes from plants, such as fruit, vegetables, grains like maize and wheat, and nuts and seeds. Food also comes from animals, such as meat, milk, eggs, fish and honey. We can classify, or sort, our food into these two groups.

People get their food in different ways. Most people buy food in shops, at markets and from street vendors. Some grow their own food. Some collect food from nature, such as wild fruit, mopane worms and honey. Others catch fish or hunt.

There are different ways of farming. Subsistence farming is farming mainly to feed your own family, on small plots, with simple tools such as hoes, and with family members doing the work. Any small surplus may be sold.

Commercial farming is farming mainly to sell for a profit. Commercial farms are usually large, they use machines such as tractors and combine harvesters, they employ many paid workers and they often specialise in a few crops or animals. Some of their products, like these oranges, are exported to other countries.

Urban farming is growing food in towns and cities, in backyards, community gardens, school gardens, on rooftops and in containers. It saves money, gives fresh food and brings people together, but space and water are often scarce.

The questions for this section are with you now: food from plants and animals, ways of getting food, and subsistence, commercial and urban farming.

## Subtopic: Crop and Stock Farming

The next stop is the packhouse, where the oranges are washed, sorted, packed and cooled. This is like the apple case study in Ceres. Fruit trees grow in orchards, they are pruned in winter, they blossom in spring, and the fruit is picked by hand and kept cold so that it stays fresh.

Crop farming means growing plants. Crops need good soil, water, the right temperature and sunlight. Maize is South Africa's most important crop and grows mainly in the Free State, North West and Mpumalanga. Wheat grows in the Western Cape and the Free State, and sugar cane in KwaZulu-Natal and the Mpumalanga Lowveld. Citrus grows in Limpopo, Mpumalanga, the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape.

Stock farming means keeping animals. Large stock are cattle, for beef, milk and leather. Small stock are sheep, goats and pigs. Poultry are farm birds, such as chickens, for meat and eggs. Animals need food, water, shelter and care, and too many animals cause overgrazing.

In the Karoo case study, sheep do well in a dry semi-desert with small bushes. Farms are very large, windmills pump water from boreholes, and wool is shorn, baled and sold in Gqeberha.

On a farming map, symbols and a key show the pattern: more crops in the wetter east, and more sheep and goats in the drier west.

The questions for this section are with you now: crops, the fruit case study, stock, the Karoo case study and farming maps.

## Subtopic: Transport Through Time

The next stops are all about transport. Transport means moving people and goods, on land, on water and in the air.

On land, people first walked and used pack oxen and sledges. After 1652, wheeled carts, wagons and coaches came to the Cape. Ox-wagons travelled about twenty kilometres a day. The bicycle began in 1817 with no pedals, and by 1885 the safety bicycle had a chain. South Africa's first railway opened in Durban in 1860, and Karl Benz made a petrol car in 1886. Today, the truck carrying the oranges joins minibus taxis, buses and cars on the road, and exhaust fumes cause air pollution in big cities.

On water, people used rafts, dugout canoes like the mokoro and reed boats, then sailing ships such as dhows and caravels. Dias sailed around the Cape in 1488. Steamships did not need the wind. Today, container ships carry most trade goods, like the oranges.

In the air, the Montgolfier brothers flew the first balloons in 1783. Airships could be steered. The Wright brothers made the first powered aeroplane flight on 17 December 1903, and today jets and helicopters carry people and goods.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking subsistence farmers farm mainly to sell; they farm mainly for their families. Two: thinking the Karoo is good for crops; it is too dry without irrigation. Three: thinking the first railway in South Africa was in Johannesburg; it was in Durban. Four: thinking the first flight lasted hours; it lasted 12 seconds.

The questions for this section are with you now: transport on land, on water and in the air.

Land: oxen, wagons, bicycles, trains and cars. Water: canoes, sailing ships and steamships. Air: balloons, airships and aeroplanes.

Pocket summary of the term. Food comes from plants and animals, and people buy, grow, collect, fish or hunt for it.
