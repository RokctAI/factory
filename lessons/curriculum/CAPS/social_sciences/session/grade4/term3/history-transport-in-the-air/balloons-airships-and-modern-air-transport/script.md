# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers balloons, airships and modern forms of air transport. It explains how the first balloons flew, how airships could be steered, and how aeroplanes, jets and helicopters became the air transport we use today. The anchor is Kagiso, a Grade 4 learner who lives in Hartbeespoort, in North West, near the Magaliesberg mountains. Early on a Saturday morning, he and his father go outside to watch something special: colourful hot-air balloons rising slowly into the pale sky above the mountains. Each one has a big basket underneath, and every few seconds, a roaring flame shoots up into the balloon. A few weeks later, Kagiso and his mother fly from OR Tambo International Airport, near Johannesburg, to Cape Town to visit his aunt. The jet races down the runway, lifts into the air and, two hours later, lands in Cape Town, about 1,300 kilometres away. By ox-wagon, the same journey would have taken many weeks. The big idea is that people first flew in balloons, then in airships, and that aeroplanes, jets and helicopters now carry people and goods quickly across the world. ## Subtopic: Balloons

A hot-air balloon flies because hot air is lighter than the cooler air around it, so it rises. You can see this when smoke rises from a fire.

The first hot-air balloons were made in France by two brothers, Joseph and Etienne Montgolfier, whose family made paper. In 1783, they tested a large balloon of cloth and paper in front of the king of France. The first passengers were a sheep, a duck and a rooster, sent up to see if living things could survive in the air. They flew for about eight minutes and landed safely. A few weeks later, in November 1783, two men flew over Paris in a Montgolfier balloon for about twenty-five minutes. They were the first people ever to fly.

Soon after, other inventors filled balloons with a gas called hydrogen, which is much lighter than air.

But balloons have one big problem: they cannot be steered. They go wherever the wind blows them. So balloons were not very useful for transport. They were used for science, for sport, and in wars, to watch the enemy from high up. During the South African War, from 1899 to 1902, the British army used balloons to watch the movements of Boer forces.

Today, hot-air balloons use gas burners to heat the air, and people take balloon rides for fun. Weather balloons carry instruments high into the sky to measure the weather.

The questions for this section are with you now: how balloons fly, the Montgolfier brothers and the problems with balloons.

## Subtopic: Airships

An airship is like a long balloon with engines and propellers, so it can be steered. It is filled with a gas that is lighter than air, such as hydrogen or helium.

In 1900, a German, Count Ferdinand von Zeppelin, flew his first large airship, which had a long, cigar-shaped frame covered with cloth. His airships became so famous that many large airships were called zeppelins.

In the 1920s and 1930s, airships carried passengers across oceans in comfort. Passengers had cabins, a dining room and big windows. In 1929, the airship Graf Zeppelin flew all the way around the world. Airships were slow compared with aeroplanes, but they could fly much further without stopping, at a time when aeroplanes were still small.

But hydrogen burns very easily. In 1937, the Hindenburg, one of the largest airships ever built, caught fire as it came in to land in the United States, and thirty-six people died. After this disaster, people stopped trusting passenger airships, and aeroplanes took over.

Today, a few small airships, called blimps, are used for advertising and for filming sports events from the air. They are filled with helium, which does not burn.

The questions for this section are with you now: airships, zeppelins and the Hindenburg.

## Subtopic: Modern Air Transport

After the Wright brothers' first flight in 1903, aeroplanes quickly became bigger, faster and safer. In 1934, South African Airways was started.

The jet engine changed air travel. A jet engine sucks in air, squeezes it, mixes it with fuel, burns it and blasts hot gas out of the back, which pushes the plane forward very fast. In 1952, the world's first jet airliner passenger service flew from London to Johannesburg. In 1970, the Boeing 747, called the jumbo jet, began carrying hundreds of passengers on each flight.

Helicopters have spinning blades on top called rotors. They can take off straight up, hover in one place and land in small spaces. Igor Sikorsky flew a successful helicopter in 1939. Helicopters are used to rescue people, fight fires and carry sick or injured people to hospital as air ambulances.

Aeroplanes also carry goods, such as post, medicines, flowers and fresh fruit, that must arrive quickly. Today, small aircraft without pilots, called drones, are also used to film, to check on crops and even to deliver small parcels. OR Tambo International Airport is one of the busiest airports in Africa.

But aeroplanes burn a lot of fuel, which adds to air pollution and climate change, and airports are noisy.

Three: thinking airships replaced aeroplanes; aeroplanes took over after the Hindenburg disaster. Four: thinking aeroplanes cause no pollution; they burn a lot of fuel.
