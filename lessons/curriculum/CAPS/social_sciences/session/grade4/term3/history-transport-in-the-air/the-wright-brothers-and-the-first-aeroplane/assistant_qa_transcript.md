### subtopic_1 — Two Brothers With a Dream
**Assistant:** Who went first, Orville or Wilbur?

**Tutor:** The brothers decided who would fly first by tossing a coin. On 14 December 1903, Wilbur won the toss and tried first. But he pulled the front of the Flyer up too steeply, it stalled after about three seconds, and it bumped down into the sand. The brothers did not count this as a real flight, because the machine had not flown under control. They spent the next two days fixing the damage. On 17 December, it was Orville's turn. This time the Flyer took off, flew under control for 12 seconds and landed safely, so Orville is known as the first person to fly an aeroplane. But the brothers always shared the credit, because they had planned, built and tested everything together. That same day, Wilbur flew the longest flight, 59 seconds. Neither brother ever married, and they worked together as a team for the rest of Wilbur's life.

### subtopic_2 — Solving the Problems of Flight
**Assistant:** Where is the first aeroplane today?

**Tutor:** The damaged 1903 Flyer was taken back to Dayton and packed away. Later, after a long disagreement with a big museum in the United States about who should be called the inventor of the aeroplane, Orville sent it to a museum in London, England, in 1928. It stayed there for twenty years. When the disagreement was settled, the Flyer was returned to the United States. Since 1948, it has been on show at the Smithsonian Institution in Washington, D.C., where millions of people have seen it. It now hangs in the National Air and Space Museum, close to spacecraft that travelled to the Moon. There is another amazing link: when the astronaut Neil Armstrong flew to the Moon in 1969, he took a small piece of wood and fabric from the Wright Flyer with him. So, only sixty-six years after its first short flight, a piece of the first aeroplane travelled all the way to the Moon.

### subtopic_3 — The First Flight
**Assistant:** How do wings lift an aeroplane?

**Tutor:** An aeroplane wing is shaped and tilted so that, as it moves forward quickly through the air, it pushes air downwards. When the wing pushes air down, the air pushes the wing up, and this upward push is called lift. The faster the wing moves through the air, the more lift it makes. That is why an aeroplane must race along the runway before it can take off. The engine and propellers, or jet engines, give the forward push, called thrust. Gravity pulls the aeroplane down, and the air rubbing against it slows it down, which is called drag. To fly, the lift must be at least as strong as the pull of gravity. You can feel lift yourself: when you are a passenger in a car, if an adult says it is safe, hold your flat hand out of the window and tilt the front edge up a little. You will feel the air push your hand upwards.
