### subtopic_1 — Balloons
**Assistant:** How does a hot-air balloon go up and down?

**Tutor:** A hot-air balloon has three main parts: the big fabric bag, called the envelope, a basket underneath for the pilot and passengers, and a gas burner between them. To go up, the pilot turns on the burner, which shoots a roaring flame up into the envelope. This heats the air inside. Hot air is lighter than the cooler air outside, so the balloon rises. To stay at the same height, the pilot gives short bursts of flame every now and then. To go down, the pilot lets the air inside cool slowly, or opens a flap at the top of the envelope to let some hot air out, and the balloon sinks. The pilot cannot steer left or right. Instead, the pilot goes higher or lower to find winds blowing in different directions. A team on the ground follows the balloon in a car and helps pack it up after it lands.

### subtopic_2 — Airships
**Assistant:** Why is helium safer than hydrogen?

**Tutor:** Both hydrogen and helium are gases that are much lighter than air, so both can lift a balloon or an airship. Hydrogen is the lightest gas of all, so it lifts very well, and it is cheap to make. But hydrogen catches fire and burns very easily if it meets a spark or a flame when it is mixed with air. That is what happened to the Hindenburg in 1937: its hydrogen caught fire, and the whole airship burned within a minute or so. Helium is a little heavier than hydrogen, so it lifts slightly less, but it does not burn at all. That makes it much safer. Today, airships, blimps and party balloons that float are filled with helium, never with hydrogen. Weather balloons sometimes still use hydrogen, but nobody rides in them. Helium is more expensive than hydrogen, because it is harder to find.

### subtopic_3 — Modern Air Transport
**Assistant:** What is the Red Cross Air Mercy Service?

**Tutor:** The Red Cross Air Mercy Service is a South African organisation that uses aeroplanes and helicopters to help sick and injured people, especially in rural areas far from big hospitals. It began in 1966. Its aircraft act as flying ambulances. They have medical equipment on board, and they carry doctors, nurses and paramedics who care for patients during the flight. Helicopters can land close to an accident or a small clinic and fly a badly injured person quickly to a big hospital. Aeroplanes also fly doctors and specialists out to rural hospitals, so that patients can be treated close to home without travelling far. The service also helps with rescues in the mountains and at sea. This shows one of the most important ways air transport has changed people's lives: in an emergency, it can bring help across long distances in a short time, which can save lives.
