# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the Wright brothers and the invention of the first aeroplane. It explains who the Wright brothers were, how they solved the problems of flight step by step, what happened on the day of the first flight, and how flying came to South Africa. The anchor is a Grade 4 classroom in East London, in the Eastern Cape. It is a windy afternoon, and the learners are folding paper planes. They go outside to test them. Some planes nosedive straight into the ground, some loop up and crash, and a few glide smoothly across the field. Their teacher, Mr Pillay, asks why some fly better than others. Back in class, he tells them about two brothers who asked the same kind of question more than a hundred years ago, and who tested their ideas again and again until they solved the puzzle. He also tells them that the first aeroplane flight in South Africa took place right here, in East London. The big idea is that the Wright brothers invented the first successful aeroplane through careful study, testing and teamwork, and that this changed transport forever. ## Subtopic: Two Brothers With a Dream

Wilbur Wright was born in 1867, and his younger brother Orville in 1871. They grew up in the United States of America, and later lived in the city of Dayton, in the state of Ohio. When they were boys, their father brought home a small toy helicopter, powered by a twisted rubber band. The boys played with it until it broke, and then they built their own copies.

As young men, the brothers opened a bicycle shop in Dayton, where they sold, repaired and later built bicycles. This work taught them a lot about light, strong machines, chains and balance, which helped them later.

At that time, many people around the world were trying to fly. Some had flown in balloons, and some had flown gliders. A glider is an aircraft without an engine that floats on the air. A German inventor, Otto Lilienthal, made more than two thousand glider flights, but he died after a crash in 1896. The Wright brothers read about his work and decided to try to solve the problem of flight themselves.

They watched birds carefully and noticed that birds twist the tips of their wings to turn and keep their balance. They read everything they could find about flight and wrote letters to experts. They were patient and careful, and they wrote down the results of every test in notebooks.

The questions for this section are with you now: who the Wright brothers were and how they started.

## Subtopic: Solving the Problems of Flight

The brothers knew that an aeroplane needed three things: wings to lift it, power to move it forward, and a way to control it in the air.

They started with control. They built a large kite, and then gliders that a person could lie on. They found a way to twist the wings, just as birds do, to turn and balance. This was called wing warping. Wing warping was one of their most important ideas.

From 1900, they tested their gliders at Kitty Hawk, on the coast of North Carolina. This place had strong, steady winds to lift the gliders, soft sand for safer landings, and few people to watch.

At first, their gliders did not lift as well as they expected. So, in 1901, they built a wind tunnel in their shop. It was a long wooden box with a fan that blew air through it. Inside, they tested about two hundred small wing shapes to find which ones lifted best.

Next, they needed an engine. No engine maker could give them one that was light and powerful enough, so they built their own, with the help of their mechanic, Charlie Taylor. They also designed their own propellers.

The questions for this section are with you now: wings, power, control, gliders and the wind tunnel.

## Subtopic: The First Flight

On 17 December 1903, at Kill Devil Hills, near Kitty Hawk, the brothers were ready with their aeroplane, the Flyer. It was made of wood, wire and cloth, with two wings, one above the other, and two propellers.

It was cold and windy. Orville lay flat on the lower wing to fly it. The engine started, and the Flyer moved along a wooden rail and lifted into the air. It flew for 12 seconds and covered 37 metres. It was the first time a person had flown in a powered machine that took off by itself, flew under control and landed safely.

That day, the brothers took turns and made four flights. The longest, by Wilbur, lasted 59 seconds and covered 260 metres. Then a gust of wind tipped the Flyer over and damaged it, and it never flew again. Only five people watched, and one of them took a famous photograph.

The brothers kept improving their aeroplanes, and by 1905, they could fly for more than half an hour. Soon, people across the world were building aeroplanes. On 28 December 1909, a French pilot, Albert Kimmerling, made the first powered aeroplane flight in South Africa, in East London.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking the first flight lasted hours; it lasted 12 seconds. Two: thinking the Wright brothers were rich scientists; they ran a bicycle shop. Three: thinking they got it right the first time; they tested again and again. Four: thinking nobody had flown before 1903; people had flown in balloons and gliders.
