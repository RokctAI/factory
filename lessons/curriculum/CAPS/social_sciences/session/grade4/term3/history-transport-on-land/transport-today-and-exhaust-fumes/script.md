# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers transport on land today. It explains the most common ways people travel, how goods are moved on land, and a case study of the damage that exhaust fumes cause in big cities. The anchor is Naledi, a Grade 4 learner who lives in Soweto, the biggest township in Johannesburg. Every weekday, her mother wakes up before five o'clock, walks to the taxi rank and takes a minibus taxi to her job in the city centre. Naledi's older brother takes a Rea Vaya bus to college, and her uncle, who works in Pretoria, sometimes takes the Gautrain. Naledi herself walks to school with her friends. One cold winter morning, her class goes on an outing to a hilltop in Johannesburg. From up there, they can see a brown haze hanging over the city, like a dirty blanket. Their teacher, Mr Sithole, tells them that much of it comes from vehicles. The big idea is that people and goods depend on many kinds of transport today, but that exhaust fumes from vehicles pollute the air in big cities, and that people can help to reduce this damage. Explaining transport today and the damage caused by exhaust fumes is examinable.

## Subtopic: How People Travel Today

Many South Africans still walk to school, to work and to the shops. Walking is free, healthy and causes no pollution.

Minibus taxis are the most common kind of public transport in South Africa. Public transport means vehicles that anyone can use by paying a fare, such as taxis, buses and trains. Millions of people take minibus taxis every day. Taxis go almost everywhere, from big cities to small villages, and they leave from busy taxi ranks.

Buses carry many people at once. Some cities have bus services with special lanes, so the buses do not get stuck in traffic, such as Rea Vaya in Johannesburg, which started in 2009, A Re Yeng in Tshwane and MyCiTi in Cape Town.

Trains carry many people to and from work in big cities, such as the Metrorail trains in Gauteng, Cape Town and Durban. The Gautrain, which opened in 2010, is a fast train that links Johannesburg, Pretoria and OR Tambo International Airport.

Many families own cars, and people also use motorbikes and bicycles. Delivery riders on scooters bring food and parcels to people's homes. E-hailing means booking a car and driver with an app on a cell phone.

The questions for this section are with you now: walking, taxis, buses, trains and cars today.

## Subtopic: Moving Goods on Land

Goods are things that people make, buy and sell, such as food, clothes, fuel, building materials and machines. Every day, huge amounts of goods are moved across South Africa.

Most goods in South Africa are carried by road, in trucks. Some trucks are very long, with two trailers. They carry goods from factories and farms to shops, and from the harbours to towns and cities. For example, the N3 highway between Durban and Johannesburg is one of the busiest routes for trucks in the country, because many goods arrive by ship at Durban harbour and are taken by truck to Gauteng.

Freight trains carry heavy goods over long distances. Freight means goods that are carried by train, truck, ship or plane. Some freight trains in South Africa are very long. They carry coal to Richards Bay harbour in KwaZulu-Natal and iron ore to Saldanha Bay in the Western Cape, to be loaded onto ships for other countries. Trains can carry heavy loads using less fuel than many trucks, and they take trucks off the roads.

Smaller goods are carried in delivery vans and bakkies. Fuel, such as petrol and diesel, is also pumped through long underground pipes called pipelines.

The questions for this section are with you now: trucks, freight trains and pipelines.

## Subtopic: Case Study: Exhaust Fumes

Most cars, taxis, buses and trucks have engines that burn petrol or diesel. When fuel burns, it makes gases and tiny bits of soot, called exhaust fumes, which come out of the exhaust pipe.

In big cities, there are millions of vehicles, and many of them sit in traffic jams with their engines running. Their exhaust fumes mix with smoke from factories, power stations and fires. Together, these make the air dirty. This is called air pollution. When the dirty air forms a haze over a city, it is called smog.

On cold winter mornings on the Highveld, cold air near the ground is trapped under warmer air above, like a lid on a pot. The fumes and smoke cannot rise and blow away, so the brown haze that Naledi's class saw lies over the city.

Air pollution harms people's health. It can make eyes sting and cause coughing, and it makes illnesses such as asthma worse. Children and older people suffer most. Pollution also harms plants and animals, and some gases from vehicles, such as carbon dioxide, trap heat around the earth and add to climate change.

People can help. They can walk or cycle for short trips, use buses and trains, and share lifts. Vehicles that are serviced burn fuel more cleanly. Electric vehicles make no exhaust fumes, and planting trees helps clean the air. The government also sets rules about how much pollution vehicles and factories may make.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking most goods travel by train; most travel by truck. Two: thinking exhaust fumes disappear; they pollute the air. Three: thinking smog is just fog; it is fog or haze mixed with pollution. Four: thinking one person cannot help; walking and lift clubs reduce fumes.

The questions for this section are with you now: exhaust fumes, smog and how to reduce air pollution.

## Subtopic: Getting Around

People walk, take taxis, buses and trains, and drive cars. Minibus taxis are the most common public transport.

The Gautrain is a fast train in Gauteng. Rea Vaya buses have their own lanes.

Keep Naledi's mother at the taxi rank in mind and try the questions for this part: how people travel.

## Subtopic: Trucks and Trains

Most goods travel by truck. Freight trains carry heavy goods, such as coal and iron ore, to harbours.

Vans and bakkies carry smaller goods.

Hold the busy N3 highway in mind and try this part's questions: moving goods.

## Subtopic: Dirty Air

Vehicles burn fuel and make exhaust fumes. Many fumes make smog, which harms people's health.

Walking, cycling, buses, trains and lift clubs mean fewer fumes.
