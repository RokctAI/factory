### subtopic_1 — How People Travel Today
**Assistant:** What is a lift club?

**Tutor:** A lift club is a group of people who share one car to travel to the same place, such as work or school, instead of each person driving their own car. For example, four parents who live in the same street and work in the same area might take turns to drive. On Monday, one parent drives everyone, and on Tuesday, another parent drives. Lift clubs have many good sides. Each person saves money on petrol and parking. There are fewer cars on the road, which means less traffic and fewer traffic jams. And fewer cars means less exhaust fumes, so the air stays cleaner. Lift clubs for children going to school are also common in South Africa, and some parents pay a driver with a minibus to take a group of children to school every day. Using public transport, such as buses, trains and taxis, works in the same way, because many people share one vehicle.

### subtopic_2 — Moving Goods on Land
**Assistant:** Why are freight trains so long?

**Tutor:** Freight trains are long because they carry huge amounts of heavy goods at once, such as coal, iron ore, maize or containers. One locomotive, or a few working together, can pull many wagons, so a long train moves a lot of goods with only a few drivers and less fuel than many trucks would use. In South Africa, the iron ore trains that run from the mines near Sishen in the Northern Cape to Saldanha Bay harbour in the Western Cape are among the longest in the world, with more than three hundred wagons. Coal trains to Richards Bay are also very long. One long train can carry as much as hundreds of trucks. This saves fuel, causes less pollution for each tonne of goods, and keeps many heavy trucks off the roads, which makes roads safer and stops them from breaking up so quickly. But trains can only go where there are railway lines.

### subtopic_3 — Case Study: Exhaust Fumes
**Assistant:** Do electric cars make any pollution?

**Tutor:** Electric cars have no exhaust pipe, because they do not burn petrol or diesel. Instead, they have a large battery that powers an electric motor. So electric cars do not puff fumes into the air of busy city streets, which is very good for the air people breathe. But the electricity to charge the battery has to come from somewhere. In South Africa, most electricity is still made in power stations that burn coal, and these power stations make air pollution of their own. So an electric car charged with electricity from coal still causes some pollution, just not in the city street. When the electricity comes from the sun or the wind, through solar panels or wind turbines, electric cars cause much less pollution. Making the batteries also uses energy and materials from mines. Today, electric cars are still expensive in South Africa, so only a few people have them, but there are more each year.
