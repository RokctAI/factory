### subtopic_1 — The Karoo and Its Sheep
**Assistant:** How does a windmill pump water?

**Tutor:** A farm windmill, also called a windpump, is a tall metal tower with a wheel of metal blades at the top and a tail that turns the wheel to face the wind. When the wind blows, it pushes against the blades and the wheel spins. The spinning wheel moves a long metal rod up and down, all the way down into the borehole. At the bottom of the rod, deep under the ground, is a pump. Each time the rod moves up and down, the pump lifts a little water up the pipe. Stroke by stroke, the water climbs up and pours out into the reservoir. A windmill needs no electricity or petrol, only wind, which is why windmills became so common on farms across the dry parts of South Africa. If the wind stops for a few days, the reservoir still has water stored. Farm workers check the windmills often and fix them when they break.

### subtopic_2 — Farming in a Dry Place
**Assistant:** Why are jackals a problem for sheep farmers?

**Tutor:** Black-backed jackals are wild animals, a bit like small, thin dogs, with a dark, silvery patch of fur on their backs. They live naturally in the Karoo and in many other parts of South Africa. Jackals hunt small animals such as mice, birds and hares, and they also eat insects and fruit. But they can easily catch a young lamb, and sometimes they kill adult sheep too. For a farmer, losing many lambs means losing a lot of money. Caracals, which are wild cats with long black tufts on their ears, also attack sheep. Farmers try to protect their sheep in ways that do not harm other wildlife. They use jackal-proof fences, keep lambs in camps close to the house, and use special guard dogs, such as Anatolian shepherd dogs, which live with the sheep and chase predators away. Some farmers also use donkeys or alpacas as guards.

### subtopic_3 — From Farm to Market
**Assistant:** What happens to the wool after it is sold?

**Tutor:** After the wool is sold in Gqeberha, it is sent to factories, mostly in other countries. First, the wool is washed very well to remove dirt, grease and bits of plants. This is called scouring. The grease that comes out of wool, called lanolin, is used to make creams and soaps. Then the wool is combed so that all the fibres lie in the same direction. Next, it is spun, which means it is twisted into long, strong threads called yarn. The yarn can be dyed in many colours. Finally, the yarn is knitted into jerseys, scarves and socks, or woven into cloth for suits, coats and blankets. Some of these clothes come back to South Africa and are sold in our shops. Merino wool is valued because it is soft, warm, and keeps people comfortable in both cold and warm weather. So a jersey you buy may have started on a sheep in the Karoo.
