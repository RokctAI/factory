# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers the location of the main crop and stock farming areas in South Africa. It explains how symbols and a key show farming on a map, where the main crops are grown, where the main farm animals are kept, and why. The anchor is a Grade 4 classroom at a school in Bloemfontein, in the Free State. Their teacher, Mrs Mokoena, has pinned a huge outline map of South Africa, with the nine provinces, onto the back wall. The learners have drawn and cut out small symbols: yellow maize cobs, golden ears of wheat, tall green sugar cane, oranges, bunches of grapes, apples, cows, sheep, goats and chickens. Group by group, they stick the symbols onto the map in the right places, and a learner called Lerato makes the key. The big idea is that each kind of farming happens where the rain, temperature and soil suit it, so a farming map shows a clear pattern: more crops in the wetter east, and more sheep and goats in the drier west. Locating the main farming areas on a map and using symbols and a key is examinable.

## Subtopic: Symbols and a Key

You learnt last term that a map uses symbols, which are small pictures, shapes, lines or colours that stand for real things. A farming map uses symbols to show where crops are grown and where animals are kept.

Good farming symbols are simple and easy to recognise. A maize cob can stand for maize, a bunch of grapes for grapes, a small cow for cattle and a sheep for sheep. Some maps use colours as well: for example, yellow for maize areas and light brown for sheep areas.

Every map with symbols needs a key, also called a legend. The key is a box on the map that shows each symbol and explains what it means. Without a key, a reader cannot be sure what the symbols stand for. Lerato draws each symbol in the key with its meaning next to it, such as a sheep and the words sheep farming.

A map also needs a title, which tells the reader what the map is about, such as Main farming areas of South Africa. It should have a compass, or at least an arrow showing north, so that the reader can talk about directions, such as the east or the west of the country.

To read a farming map, first read the title, then study the key, then look for the symbols on the map. When you draw your own farming map, keep the symbols small and neat, and use the same symbol every time for the same thing.

## Subtopic: Where Crops Are Grown

Maize is grown mainly in the Free State, North West and Mpumalanga, and also in parts of Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal and Limpopo. This large maize-growing area is sometimes called the maize triangle. These areas get summer rain and have good, deep soils.

Wheat is grown mainly in the Western Cape, especially in the Swartland, where it rains in winter, and in the Free State.

Sugar cane is grown along the warm, wet coast of KwaZulu-Natal and in the Lowveld of Mpumalanga.

Citrus fruit grows in Limpopo, Mpumalanga, the Eastern Cape, especially in the Sundays River Valley near Gqeberha, and in the Western Cape, around Citrusdal.

Apples and pears grow in the cool valleys of the Western Cape, such as Ceres and Elgin. Grapes grow in the Western Cape and along the Orange River in the Northern Cape, where farmers irrigate with river water.

Bananas, mangoes and avocados grow in warm areas of Limpopo, Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal.

Vegetables, such as potatoes, cabbages and tomatoes, are grown in many provinces, often close to big towns and cities, where they can be sold fresh.

## Subtopic: Where Animals Are Kept

Beef cattle are kept in many parts of the country with good grass, such as the Free State, North West, Limpopo, KwaZulu-Natal and the Eastern Cape, and in the dry Kalahari area of the Northern Cape.

Dairy cattle need good green grass and plenty of water, so dairy farms are found in wetter areas, such as the KwaZulu-Natal Midlands, the coast of the Eastern Cape and the southern coast of the Western Cape.

Sheep are kept mainly in the dry Karoo, in the Eastern Cape, Northern Cape and Western Cape, and also in the Free State. Angora goats for mohair are kept mainly in the Eastern Cape Karoo. Ostriches are farmed near Oudtshoorn.

Large chicken and pig farms are often close to big cities, especially in Gauteng, North West, the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal, because many people there buy chicken, eggs and pork.

Now look at the whole map. A pattern appears. The east of South Africa gets more rain than the west. So most crops are grown in the wetter east and in places with irrigation, while sheep and goats are kept in the drier centre and west, where crops cannot grow without irrigation.

The error museum, four exhibits. One: thinking a map needs no key; without a key, symbols can be misunderstood. Two: thinking maize is mainly grown in the Western Cape; it is grown mainly in the Free State, North West and Mpumalanga. Three: thinking the west is wetter; the east gets more rain. Four: thinking sheep farms need lots of rain; sheep do well in the dry Karoo.
