### subtopic_1 — What Crops Need
**Assistant:** Why do farmers add fertiliser to the soil?

**Tutor:** Plants take food, called nutrients, out of the soil as they grow. When a farmer grows crops on the same field year after year, the plants slowly use up those nutrients, and the soil becomes poor. Then the crops grow small and weak. To put the nutrients back, farmers add things to the soil. Manure is animal dung, such as cow dung, which rots and feeds the soil. Compost is made from rotted plant material, such as leaves and vegetable peels. Fertiliser is plant food made in factories, often sold in bags as small grains. Many commercial farmers use fertiliser because it works quickly, but it costs money, and too much of it can wash into rivers and harm the plants and fish there. So good farmers test their soil and add only what their crops need. Some farmers also rotate their crops, which means growing a different crop in a field each year, to help keep the soil healthy.

### subtopic_2 — Important Crops of South Africa
**Assistant:** Why does rooibos only grow in the Cederberg?

**Tutor:** Rooibos is a small bush with needle-like leaves. It grows naturally only in a small area of the Western Cape, mainly in the Cederberg mountains around Clanwilliam and nearby. It has grown there for a very long time and is suited to the special conditions of that area: sandy, poor soil, hot, dry summers and winter rain. Farmers have tried to grow rooibos in other parts of the world, but it does not grow well there. The Khoikhoi and San people of the area were the first to use rooibos leaves to make a drink. Today, farmers cut the bushes, chop the leaves and stems, wet them and leave them in the sun. As they dry, they turn a red-brown colour, which is why it is called rooibos, meaning red bush. Rooibos tea is drunk all over South Africa and is exported to many countries, so it brings jobs and money to the small towns of the area.

### subtopic_3 — A Year on a Fruit Farm
**Assistant:** Why do the apple boxes go into cold stores?

**Tutor:** After apples are picked, they are still alive and they keep slowly ripening. If they are left in a warm place, they become soft and start to rot within a few weeks. Cold slows the ripening down a lot. So after the fruit is packed, the boxes are moved into huge cold stores, where the temperature is kept just above freezing. Some cold stores also change the air inside, with less of the gas oxygen, so the apples ripen even more slowly. In this way, apples picked in March can be kept fresh and crunchy for many months and sold later in the year. Fruit for other countries also travels in cold containers on ships, so that it arrives fresh after a journey of a few weeks. Keeping fruit cold all the way from the farm to the shop is called the cold chain. If the cold chain is broken, the fruit can spoil.
