### subtopic_1 — Symbols and a Key
**Assistant:** Why is it called the maize triangle?

**Tutor:** If you look at a map of South Africa and mark the places where the most maize is grown, they make a rough triangle shape. The corners of this triangle are roughly in the Free State, in North West and in Mpumalanga. The name is just a simple way to remember the main maize area, so it is not an exact shape with straight lines. Maize grows well there because the area gets most of its rain in summer, which is the growing season for maize, the summers are warm, and there are large areas of flat land with deep, good soil. The flat land is easy to plough and harvest with big machines. Many small farming towns in this area, such as Bothaville in the Free State, have tall grain silos next to the railway line. Bothaville even holds a big farming show every year where farmers see new machines and learn new ways of farming.

### subtopic_2 — Where Crops Are Grown
**Assistant:** Why do dairy farms need wetter areas?

**Tutor:** Dairy cows give the most milk when they eat plenty of fresh, green, juicy grass, and they also drink a lot of water every day, much more than a person. On a hot day, a dairy cow that gives a lot of milk can drink 100 litres of water or more. Green grass grows best where it rains often, or where farmers can irrigate their pastures. That is why many dairy farms are found in wetter, greener areas, such as the KwaZulu-Natal Midlands, the coast of the Eastern Cape and the southern coast of the Western Cape near George. Milk also spoils quickly, so it must be cooled and taken to a dairy factory soon after milking. That means dairy farms do better when they are not too far from factories and good roads. In dry areas, farmers can still keep dairy cows, but they must buy a lot of feed, which costs more money.

### subtopic_3 — Where Animals Are Kept
**Assistant:** Why are chicken farms close to big cities?

**Tutor:** Big cities such as Johannesburg, Pretoria, Cape Town and Durban have millions of people, and many of them buy chicken and eggs every week. Chicken farms that are close to the cities can get their meat and eggs to the shops quickly and cheaply, with short truck journeys. This matters because eggs can break and meat must be kept cold and fresh. Chickens do not need large areas of grass to graze on. They are kept in sheds and fed on feed made mainly from maize and soya beans, so a chicken farm does not need to be in an area with good rain or good grass. It can be built on a fairly small piece of land, as long as there is water, electricity and good roads. Many chicken farms are in Gauteng, North West, the Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal. The maize for their feed often comes from the maize triangle nearby.
