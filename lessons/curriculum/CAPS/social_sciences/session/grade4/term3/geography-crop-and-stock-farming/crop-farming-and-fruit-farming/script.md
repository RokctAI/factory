# Part 1 — Expert

This session covers crop farming in South Africa. It explains what crop farming is and what crops need to grow, names the most important crops and where they grow, and follows a case study of fruit farming. The anchor is the Ceres valley in the Western Cape, about two hours' drive from Cape Town. The valley is surrounded by high mountains, and in winter, snow often covers the peaks. On the valley floor, there are long, neat rows of apple and pear trees as far as you can see. These rows of fruit trees are called orchards. A Grade 4 class from Cape Town visits Mr Adams, who manages an apple and pear farm here, and Mrs Booysen, who runs the packhouse where the fruit is sorted and packed. The big idea is that crop farmers grow plants in fields and orchards, that each crop needs the right soil, water and weather, and that fruit farming takes careful work all year round. Explaining crop farming and the case study of fruit farming is examinable.

## Subtopic: What Crops Need

A crop is a plant that farmers grow for food or to sell, such as maize, wheat, potatoes or apples. Crop farming means growing crops. Farming animals is called stock farming, which is the next lesson.

Crops need four main things to grow well.

The first is good soil. Soil gives plants the food they need to grow. Farmers add compost, manure or fertiliser to keep their soil rich.

The second is water. In some areas, enough rain falls for crops. In drier areas, farmers bring water to the crops from rivers or dams, which is called irrigation.

The third is the right temperature. Some crops, such as bananas and mangoes, need warm weather all year. Others, such as apples, need cold winters.

The fourth is sunlight, because plants use sunlight to make their food.

Different parts of South Africa get rain at different times of the year. Most of the country gets its rain in summer, often in thunderstorms. The south-western part of the Western Cape gets its rain in winter. So farmers in different areas grow different crops.

## Subtopic: Important Crops of South Africa

Maize is the most important crop in South Africa. More land is used for maize than for any other crop. Maize meal, used to make pap, is the main food of millions of South Africans. Maize grows best in the summer rainfall areas, especially in the Free State, North West and Mpumalanga. Maize is also fed to animals.

Wheat is used to make bread, flour and pasta. It grows in the winter rainfall area of the Western Cape, especially in the Swartland, and in the Free State.

Sugar cane is a tall grass that is used to make sugar. It needs a hot, wet climate, so it grows mainly in KwaZulu-Natal and in the Lowveld of Mpumalanga.

Sunflowers are grown for their seeds, which are pressed to make cooking oil.

Citrus fruit, such as oranges, lemons and naartjies, grows in warm areas of Limpopo, Mpumalanga, the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape.

Grapes grow well in the Western Cape, where they are used to make juice, raisins and wine, and along the Orange River in the Northern Cape. Table grapes for eating come from the Hex River Valley.

Bananas, mangoes and avocados need warm weather, so they grow in Limpopo, Mpumalanga and parts of KwaZulu-Natal.

Rooibos is a special plant used to make tea. It grows naturally only in the Cederberg area of the Western Cape, and nowhere else in the world.

## Subtopic: A Year on a Fruit Farm

In Ceres, apples and pears grow well because the winters are cold. The trees need cold weather to rest, so that they flower well in spring. The summers are warm and dry, so the fruit can ripen, and water from dams is used to irrigate the orchards.

In winter, the trees lose their leaves. Workers prune the trees, which means cutting off some of the branches so that the trees grow strong and give good fruit.

In spring, around September, the trees are covered with white and pink blossoms. Bees carry pollen from flower to flower, and the flowers become small fruits. Many farmers rent beehives and place them in the orchards.

In summer, the fruit grows. Some farmers cover their trees with nets to protect them from hail, birds and strong sun.

From about January to May, the fruit is picked by hand. Pickers stand on ladders and put the fruit gently into bags, then into large bins. Many seasonal workers are hired at this time.

The bins go to the packhouse. Here, Mrs Booysen's team washes the fruit, sorts it by size and colour, removes damaged fruit and packs the best fruit into boxes. The boxes are kept in cold stores, which keep the fruit fresh for months. Trucks take some fruit to shops in South Africa, and some to Cape Town harbour, where it is loaded onto ships for other countries. Fruit that is not good enough to sell whole is made into juice.
