### subtopic_1 — Large Stock
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a layer and a broiler?

**Tutor:** Both are chickens, but they are kept for different reasons. A layer is a hen that is kept to lay eggs. A good layer can lay an egg almost every day for many months. On big egg farms, thousands of layers live in large sheds, and their eggs are collected, cleaned, checked and packed into boxes for the shops. A broiler is a chicken that is kept for its meat. Broilers grow very fast. In just five to seven weeks, a small fluffy chick becomes a chicken that is big enough to sell. Most of the chicken meat sold in South African shops comes from broilers. Chicken is the meat South Africans eat the most, because it is usually cheaper than beef, mutton or pork. Many families in rural areas and townships also keep a few chickens in their yards, both for eggs and for meat, and some sell eggs to their neighbours.

### subtopic_2 — Small Stock and Poultry
**Assistant:** Why are Nguni cattle so special?

**Tutor:** Nguni cattle are a breed that has lived in southern Africa for many hundreds of years. They came south with the ancestors of Nguni-speaking people, such as the amaZulu, amaXhosa and amaSwati, which is how they got their name. Over all those years, they became very well suited to African conditions. Nguni cattle can live on poor grass, they cope well with heat and dry periods, and they resist ticks and many diseases better than some breeds from Europe. So they are cheaper for farmers to keep. Nguni cattle are also famous for their beautiful hides, in many patterns of brown, black, red and white. In Zulu culture, there are many words to describe the different patterns. Today, Nguni cattle are farmed both by small rural farmers and by large commercial farmers, and their hides are used to make rugs, cushions and drums.

### subtopic_3 — Caring for Farm Animals
**Assistant:** What does a vet do on a farm?

**Tutor:** A vet, short for veterinarian, is an animal doctor. A vet studies for many years at a university, learning about the bodies and illnesses of many kinds of animals. On a farm, a vet checks that the animals are healthy. The vet gives vaccinations, which are injections that protect animals from serious diseases. When an animal is sick or hurt, the vet finds out what is wrong and treats it with medicine. A vet may also help a cow that is having trouble giving birth to her calf. Vets also help to stop dangerous diseases from spreading from farm to farm, such as foot-and-mouth disease in cattle and bird flu in chickens. When an outbreak happens, vets test animals and may say that animals may not be moved until the disease is under control. State vets, who work for the government, also help small farmers in rural areas to keep their animals healthy.
