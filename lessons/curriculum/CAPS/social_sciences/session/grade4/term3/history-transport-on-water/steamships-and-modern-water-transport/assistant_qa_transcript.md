### subtopic_1 — The First Steamships
**Assistant:** What does a marine pilot do?

**Tutor:** A marine pilot is an experienced ship's officer who knows a particular harbour extremely well: where the water is deep and where it is shallow, how the currents move, and how the wind and waves change. Big ships come from all over the world, and their captains may never have been to that harbour before. Entering a busy harbour through a narrow entrance is difficult and dangerous for a ship that can be hundreds of metres long and very slow to turn or stop. So, before a big ship enters, a pilot comes aboard. In Durban, pilots reach the ships by a small pilot boat, or are lowered onto the deck from a helicopter. The pilot then gives orders to steer the ship and tells the tugboats where to push and pull, until the ship is safely tied up at the quay. When the ship leaves, a pilot guides it out to sea again.

### subtopic_2 — Steamships and South Africa
**Assistant:** Why are all containers the same size?

**Tutor:** Before containers, goods were packed in sacks, barrels and boxes of many different sizes. Workers had to carry them on and off ships one by one, which took many days and many workers, and goods were often damaged or stolen. In the 1950s, an American businessman, Malcom McLean, came up with a simple idea: pack goods into large steel boxes of standard sizes. Because every container is the same shape and has the same corners, cranes can lift any container quickly, and ships, trucks and trains can be built to carry them exactly. A container can be loaded at a factory far away, locked, put on a truck, then a ship, then a train, and only opened when it reaches the shop's warehouse. Today, a ship can be loaded or unloaded in a day or two instead of a week or more. This made shipping much cheaper and helped trade between countries grow.

### subtopic_3 — Water Transport Today
**Assistant:** How do you clean oil off a penguin?

**Tutor:** When oil from a spill covers a penguin's feathers, the feathers stick together and stop keeping out the cold water, so the penguin can get very cold. Penguins also swallow oil when they try to clean themselves, which makes them sick. After the Treasure spill in 2000, rescuers caught the oiled African penguins and took them to cleaning centres. First, the birds were warmed, fed and given medicine to help them get stronger. Then trained volunteers washed each penguin very gently in warm water with a special mild soap, carefully cleaning every feather, while keeping the soap out of the bird's eyes. Then they rinsed the bird with clean water until no soap was left. After washing, the penguins needed time to rest and get their waterproof feathers back before they could be released into the sea. Thousands of volunteers helped, and most of the penguins that were cleaned survived.
