### subtopic_1 — What Is Transport?
**Assistant:** Why did Gogo Nomsa's father come home only once a year?

**Tutor:** For many years in South Africa, large numbers of men from rural areas, such as the Eastern Cape, travelled far away to work on the gold mines around Johannesburg and in other cities. This is called migrant labour. The men usually lived in hostels or compounds near the mines, and the laws of the time did not let most of them bring their wives and children to live with them. Their contracts often lasted for many months, and the train journey was long and cost money. So many men, like Gogo Nomsa's father, came home only once a year, often at Christmas. Their wives, children and parents stayed in the village, looked after the fields and animals, and waited for the money the men sent home. The railway made this kind of work possible, because it could carry thousands of workers. Many families today still remember how hard it was to live apart for so long.

### subtopic_2 — Transport Long Ago
**Assistant:** How do we know when the wheel was invented?

**Tutor:** Nobody wrote down the day the wheel was invented, because it happened long before writing was common. So historians and archaeologists use other kinds of sources. Archaeologists dig carefully in very old places and find objects, such as pieces of wooden wheels and small clay models of carts. They also find pictures of wheeled carts drawn on pots and on clay tablets. Scientists can test very old wood to find out roughly how old it is. Using all this evidence, they think that wheels for carts were first made about five and a half thousand years ago, in an area called Mesopotamia, which is now mostly in Iraq. Similar wheels appeared in parts of Europe at about the same time. Interestingly, the potter's wheel, used to make clay pots, may be even older than the cart wheel. Historians keep studying new finds, so the dates may change a little as they learn more.

### subtopic_3 — How Transport Changed Lives
**Assistant:** Is walking still a kind of transport today?

**Tutor:** Yes, walking is still one of the most important kinds of transport, and it is free. In South Africa, millions of people walk every day, to school, to work, to the shops, to taxi ranks and to fetch water. Many children walk long distances to school, especially in rural areas, just as Gogo Nomsa did. Walking is healthy for the body, it does not cause air pollution, and it needs no petrol. This is a good example of continuity in history, because people have walked for as long as there have been people. But walking also has dangers, especially on busy roads without pavements. That is why it is important to use pedestrian crossings, to walk facing the oncoming traffic when there is no pavement, and to wear bright clothes at night. Some organisations even give bicycles to learners who live far from school, so that their journey is quicker and safer.
