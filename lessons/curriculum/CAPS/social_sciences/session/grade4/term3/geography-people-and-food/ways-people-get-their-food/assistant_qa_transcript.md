### subtopic_1 — Buying and Growing Food
**Assistant:** Why do people in cities buy most of their food instead of growing it?

**Tutor:** There are a few reasons. First, space. Many people in cities live in flats, townhouses or small houses with tiny yards, so there is little or no land to grow food on. Second, time. Many city people work long hours, often far from home, and spend a lot of time travelling, so they have little time to care for a garden or animals. Third, cities have many shops and markets close by, so it is easy to buy food. Fourth, city people earn money from their jobs, which they use to buy food. But buying food means that families depend on having money. When prices go up, or when someone loses their job, it can be hard to buy enough food. That is why some people in cities still grow vegetables in small spaces, in containers, on rooftops or in community gardens, which we will learn about in a later lesson.

### subtopic_2 — Collecting Food from Nature
**Assistant:** Why did the San people not farm?

**Tutor:** For thousands of years, the San people found everything they needed in nature, so they did not need to farm. They lived in small groups and moved from place to place with the seasons, following water, game animals and the plants that were ripe at different times of the year. They knew the veld extremely well. They knew which roots held water in the dry season, which plants could be used as medicine, and how to track animals by their footprints. Moving around meant they did not use up the food in any one place. Farming, on the other hand, means staying in one place, clearing land and waiting months for crops to grow. Farming came to southern Africa much later, brought by herders of sheep and cattle, and then by farmers who grew crops such as sorghum and millet. The San way of life was a clever and successful way of living with nature, not a lesser one.

### subtopic_3 — Fishing and Hunting
**Assistant:** Why do fishers need permits?

**Tutor:** Fish in the sea and rivers belong to everyone, including the people of the future. If every fisher caught as many fish as they wanted, there would soon be too few fish left to breed, and the fish could disappear. This has happened in many parts of the world. Some fish in South Africa, such as certain kinds of kob and abalone, have become very rare because too many were caught. So the government gives out permits, which are official letters that say who may fish, where, for which fish, and how many. There are also rules about the size of fish you may keep, so that young fish can grow up and breed first, and about seasons when some fish may not be caught. Some areas of the sea are marine protected areas, where no fishing is allowed at all, so that fish can breed safely. These rules help make sure there will still be fish for Jacob's children and grandchildren.
