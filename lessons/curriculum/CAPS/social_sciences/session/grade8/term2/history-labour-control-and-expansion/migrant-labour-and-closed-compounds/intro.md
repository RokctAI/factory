Thousands of young men walked hundreds of kilometres to the diamond fields of Kimberley in the 1870s. They came from Pedi country in the north-east, from Lesotho, from the land of the Tsonga and from as far as Mozambique. They worked for a few months, bought guns, ploughs or cattle, and walked home. Within fifteen years, that system had changed into something very different: workers on long contracts, living behind high fences in closed compounds, searched every day and unable to leave until their contract ended. Today we study how and why the mine owners increased their control over black workers, through pass laws, the migrant labour system and the closed compounds, and what this meant for the workers and for the future of South Africa.
