In the space of about three years, from 1877 to 1879, three powerful African kingdoms in southern Africa were defeated by British forces: the Xhosa under Sarhili in 1878, the Zulu under Cetshwayo in 1879, and the Pedi under Sekhukhune at the end of 1879. Before the diamond fields, Britain had often been content to leave these kingdoms independent. After the diamond fields, it was not. Today we study why Britain turned to conquest in the late 1870s, what happened in each of the three wars, including the famous Zulu victory at Isandlwana, and how the defeats led to further loss of land and pushed more people into labour on the mines and farms.
