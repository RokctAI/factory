On 11 July 1888, a fire broke out deep inside the De Beers Mine at Kimberley. Smoke filled the tunnels, and more than two hundred miners died, most of them black workers who could not get out in time. The disaster showed what mining had become. The open pits of the early diggings had turned into deep underground mines, with shafts, tunnels, machines and explosives, and the work had become far more dangerous. Today we study the conditions underground in the diamond mines and the early gold mines: the dangers of rockfalls, fires, explosives, heat and dust, the long hours and the diseases, and the way the work was divided between black and white workers. We also look at how historians use sources to reconstruct the experience of the miners themselves.
