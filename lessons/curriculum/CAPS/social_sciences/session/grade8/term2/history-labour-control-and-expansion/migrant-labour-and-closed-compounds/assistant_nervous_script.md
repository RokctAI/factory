Hey — this lesson is about how mine owners controlled black workers at Kimberley.

Workers came as migrants: they worked for a few months and went home. At first they chose to come, for money to buy guns, cattle or ploughs. Later, taxes and lost land pushed them.

From 1872, black workers had to carry passes and sign contracts. Leaving early was a crime.

From 1885, the companies built closed compounds. Workers lived behind fences for their whole contract and were searched every day.

The companies said this stopped diamond theft. It also kept wages low and workers under control.
