At Newlands, on the eastern side of Table Mountain, the yearly rainfall is well over one thousand millimetres. At Worcester, about ninety kilometres away on the other side of the mountain ranges, it is only about two hundred and fifty. Mountains make the difference. Today we learn how mountains and hills, called relief, affect climate. We see how moist air forced up a mountain cools and drops its rain on the windward side, and how the air that comes down the far side is dry, creating a rain shadow. We compare Mthatha, formerly called Umtata, which lies inland behind hills and higher ground, with Durban on the coast, and we look at how slopes facing north or south receive different amounts of sun.
