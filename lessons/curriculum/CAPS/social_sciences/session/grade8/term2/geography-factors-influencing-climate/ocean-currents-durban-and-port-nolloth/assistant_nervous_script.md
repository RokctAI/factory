Ocean currents are like rivers in the sea.

On the east coast, the warm Agulhas Current flows south from the tropics. It warms the air and adds moisture. So Durban is warm, humid and gets about one thousand millimetres of rain a year.

On the west coast, the cold Benguela Current flows north from the Southern Ocean, and cold water rises from the deep. It cools the air, so the air stays still and dry. So Port Nolloth is cool, foggy and gets only about sixty millimetres.

Both towns are at the same latitude and at sea level, so the currents make the difference.

Bonus: the cold water is full of nutrients, so the west coast has lots of fish.
