Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania stands only about three degrees south of the equator, yet its summit is capped with ice. Closer to home, Johannesburg is more than eight degrees of latitude nearer the equator than Cape Town, yet its average yearly temperature is slightly lower. The reason is altitude, the height of a place above sea level. Today we learn why air gets colder as you go higher, at about six and a half degrees for every thousand metres, and how this shapes the climate of South Africa's high plateau, the highveld, and its mountains. We compare Johannesburg, at about one thousand seven hundred and fifty metres, with Cape Town at sea level, and we practise calculating the effect of height on temperature.
