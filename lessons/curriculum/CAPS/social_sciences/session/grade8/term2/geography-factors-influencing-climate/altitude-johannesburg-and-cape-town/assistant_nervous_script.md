Hey — this one has a simple rule, so hold on to it.

The higher you go, the colder it gets: about six and a half degrees colder for every thousand metres.

Why? The sun heats the ground, and the ground heats the air above it. Higher up, you are further from that warm ground, the air is thinner, and rising air cools as it spreads out.

Johannesburg is about one thousand seven hundred and fifty metres high. Cape Town is at sea level. Even though Johannesburg is closer to the equator, it is a little cooler on average, and its winter nights are frosty.

To calculate: divide the height by one thousand, multiply by six and a half, and take that off the lower temperature.
