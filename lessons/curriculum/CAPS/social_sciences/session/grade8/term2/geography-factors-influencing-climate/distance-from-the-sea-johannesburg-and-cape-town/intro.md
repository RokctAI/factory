On a winter morning in July, a Johannesburg lawn can be white with frost while in Cape Town the grass is merely damp. By the afternoon both cities may reach similar temperatures. Why are Johannesburg's nights so much colder and its days and nights so different? Part of the answer is distance from the sea. Today we learn why water heats up and cools down more slowly than land, how the sea keeps coastal places mild, and why places far inland have bigger differences between day and night and between summer and winter. We use figures for Johannesburg, about five hundred kilometres from the nearest coast, and Cape Town, right beside the ocean, and we learn to separate the effect of the sea from altitude and latitude.
