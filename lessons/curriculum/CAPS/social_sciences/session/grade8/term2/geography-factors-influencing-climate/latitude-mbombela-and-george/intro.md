Mbombela in Mpumalanga and George in the southern Cape are both South African towns, both get a fair amount of rain, and both are surrounded by green hills. Yet in January Mbombela is several degrees warmer, and its rain falls almost entirely in summer, while George gets rain in every month. One big reason is latitude, the distance from the equator. Today we learn why places closer to the equator are warmer: the angle of the sun's rays, the length of daylight, and how much atmosphere the sunlight passes through. Then we use real climate figures for Mbombela and George to see latitude at work, and we learn to separate the effect of latitude from the other factors that you will meet in the next lessons.
