Hey — this one is all about one picture, so let's draw it in your head.

Wet air blows from the sea towards a mountain. It has to climb. As it climbs, it cools, clouds form, and it rains on that side. That side is the windward side.

The air goes over the top with less water and sinks down the other side. Sinking air warms up, so the clouds disappear. That dry side is the leeward side, in a rain shadow.

Example: Newlands gets about six times more rain than Worcester, on the other side of the mountains.

Durban, on the coast, gets about one thousand millimetres. Mthatha, inland behind higher ground, gets about six hundred and fifty.

In South Africa, north-facing slopes get more sun.
