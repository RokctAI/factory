Durban and Port Nolloth lie at almost exactly the same latitude, about thirty degrees south, and both are at sea level on the coast. Yet Durban is warm, humid and green, with about one thousand millimetres of rain a year, while Port Nolloth is cool, foggy and a near desert, with only about sixty millimetres. The difference comes from the sea itself. A warm current, the Agulhas, flows past Durban, and a cold current, the Benguela, flows past Port Nolloth. Today we learn what ocean currents are, why they are warm or cold, and how they change the temperature, humidity and rainfall of the land beside them. We also see how the currents shape fishing, farming and life on South Africa's two very different coasts.
