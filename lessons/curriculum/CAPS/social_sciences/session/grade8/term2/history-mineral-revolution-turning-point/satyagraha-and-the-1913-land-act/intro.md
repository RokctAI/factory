In June 1913, the journalist Sol Plaatje cycled across the Orange Free State and met families on the roads with their cattle, their wagons and their children, evicted from farms where they had lived for years. He wrote that the African, waking on the morning of 20 June 1913, found himself not actually a slave, but a pariah in the land of his birth. A few months later, thousands of Indian workers walked out of coal mines and sugar fields in Natal and marched across the border into the Transvaal, deliberately breaking the law. Today we study two great events of 1913: the Natives Land Act and the satyagraha campaign of 1913 to 1914 led by Mohandas Gandhi. We also practise using figures and sources to explain their impact.
