Lay two maps of southern Africa side by side. On the map of 1860, the land is a patchwork: two British colonies on the coast, two Boer republics inland, and between and around them independent African kingdoms, the Zulu, the Pedi, the Basotho, the Swazi, the Xhosa chiefdoms beyond the Kei, the Tswana and many more. On the map of 1913, most of that patchwork has gone. One state, the Union of South Africa, covers almost the whole region, with a few British protectorates and the colonies of other European powers around it. Today we compare the two maps, explain how and why the change happened in just over fifty years, and practise the map skills that history examinations ask for.
