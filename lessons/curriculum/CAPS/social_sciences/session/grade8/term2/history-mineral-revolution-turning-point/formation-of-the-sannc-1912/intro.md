On 8 January 1912, in a hall in Bloemfontein, chiefs, lawyers, teachers, ministers and journalists from every part of South Africa and from neighbouring territories sat down together. The lawyer who had called them, Pixley ka Isaka Seme, told them that they were one people, and that the divisions between them had to end. That day they founded the South African Native National Congress, which in 1923 became the African National Congress. Today we study why the Congress was formed, who its founders were, what it aimed to do and how it worked in its early years. We also practise reading a speech as a historical source.
