On 31 May 1910, exactly eight years after the peace that ended the South African War, a new country came into being: the Union of South Africa. Four British colonies became one state with one parliament. But almost everyone who could vote for that parliament was white, although white people were only about one in five of the population. How did the wealth of diamonds and gold lead here? Today we follow the shifting balance of power from 1902 to 1910: the defeat of the Boer republics, the new political organisations formed by coloured and Indian South Africans, the Bambatha Rebellion of 1906, and the making of the Union in 1910. We also practise how historians weigh sources that see the same events very differently.
