Hey — this lesson is about families split by the mines.

Most black mineworkers were migrants. They lived in compounds for months and their families stayed at home in the rural areas.

The mines liked this because they could pay low wages for one man, while the family at home grew its own food.

At home, women did almost all the work. Children often grew up without their fathers nearby.

Money came home, but so did disease, like tuberculosis.

This system shaped South African families for generations.
