Johannesburg was not planned as a city. It grew out of a gold rush, from a camp of tents in 1886 to a town of about one hundred thousand people ten years later and nearly a quarter of a million by 1911. People came from all over southern Africa and the world, and the town quickly divided into rich suburbs on the ridges, crowded working-class areas near the mines, and locations where black, Indian and coloured residents were made to live. In this city, workers did not simply accept the conditions imposed on them. They deserted, refused work, went slow, and in 1913 tens of thousands went on strike. Today we study the forms of labour resistance on the Witwatersrand and the growth of the city of Johannesburg, and how the two were linked.
