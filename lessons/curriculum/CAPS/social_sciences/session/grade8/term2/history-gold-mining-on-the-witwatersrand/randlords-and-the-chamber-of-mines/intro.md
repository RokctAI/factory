In 1886, gold was found on a farm called Langlaagte, on a long ridge of rock in the South African Republic called the Witwatersrand. Within ten years, a mining camp had become Johannesburg, the largest town in southern Africa, and the Witwatersrand was producing more gold than any other place on Earth. The men who controlled this wealth were called the Randlords: Cecil John Rhodes, Alfred Beit, Julius Wernher, Barney Barnato, Joseph Robinson, Lionel Phillips and others, many of whom had made their first fortunes in Kimberley. Today we study who the Randlords were, why deep-level gold mining needed huge amounts of money, and how the mine owners joined together in the Chamber of Mines to recruit labour, cut costs and influence government.
