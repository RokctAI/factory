On the Witwatersrand around 1900, a Cornish miner with years of experience in the tin mines of Cornwall could earn many times what a black worker earned for the same shift underground. Nearby, a young Afrikaner who had lost his family's farm after drought, cattle disease and war struggled to find any work at all. And in the towns, Indian traders and workers faced laws that told them where they could live, what land they could own and whether they could enter the colony at all. Today we study how the mineral revolution created divisions among workers and communities: between skilled and unskilled white workers, between white and black workers, and through laws aimed at Indian South Africans. We look at why these divisions were created and how they shaped the country.
