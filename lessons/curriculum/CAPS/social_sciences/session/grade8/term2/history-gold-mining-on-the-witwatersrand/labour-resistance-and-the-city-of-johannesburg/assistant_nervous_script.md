Hey — this lesson is about how workers fought back and how Johannesburg grew.

Black miners could not vote or join unions, so they resisted quietly: running away, warning friends about bad mines, and working slowly.

White workers had unions and went on strike in 1907, 1913 and 1914. Black miners struck in 1913 and 1920.

Johannesburg grew from tents in 1886 to about two hundred and thirty-seven thousand people in 1911.

The rich lived on the ridges, the poor near the mines, and the council began moving black residents to places like Klipspruit.
