Hey — if starting History this year feels big, here is the calm version.

Historians are detectives. Their clues are sources. A primary source comes from the time itself, like a letter or a photo. A secondary source was written later, like your textbook. Always ask: who made this, when, why, and could they know?

You will answer source questions, write paragraphs, and from next term write essays. A good paragraph starts with your answer, backs it up, and finishes strongly.

Now the story. Last year you learned about the slave trade: British ships took goods to West Africa, carried captured Africans across the Atlantic, and brought back sugar and cotton. That made a lot of money in Britain, and some of it helped pay for new industries. Historians argue about how much, but most agree it was one important part.

The Industrial Revolution was the change from making things by hand to making them by machines in factories, using steam and coal. It started in Britain around 1750.

Factories needed raw materials and minerals, and that reached southern Africa: Indian workers in Natal in 1860, diamonds in 1867, gold in 1886.
