This term's History begins with a question that links three continents: how did the wealth of Britain grow so fast between 1750 and 1900, and what did that growth mean for southern Africa? Today we set up the tools first: how historians use primary and secondary sources, how to judge whether a source is reliable, and how History is assessed this year through source-based questions, paragraphs and essays. Then we revise the transatlantic slave trade from Grade 7, the triangular trade that carried more than twelve million enslaved Africans across the Atlantic and helped build fortunes in British ports. Finally we meet the Industrial Revolution itself, machines, steam, coal and factories, and see how its demand for raw materials and markets reached southern Africa, from Natal's sugar fields to the diamond diggings of Kimberley.
