Hey — if maps and scales feel like a lot to start the year with, here is the calm version.

Social Sciences is two subjects. Geography asks where things are and why they are there. History asks what happened, why, and what it changed. Each term has one theme in each, and each term ends with a check.

Maps first. A street map is cut into squares. Letters along the top, numbers down the side, and the letter always comes first, so C4 means column C, row 4. Don't know where a street is? Look it up in the index, like a phone's contacts list.

Read the key before anything else. It tells you what every little symbol means.

Scale is how much the world was shrunk. If one centimetre represents two kilometres, then 6,5 centimetres on the map is 13 kilometres on the ground. Measure, multiply, say the unit. For a bendy road, use a piece of string.

A line scale is a little ruler on the map, and it stays correct even if the map is photocopied bigger or smaller.

Large scale means a small area with lots of detail, like your suburb. Small scale means a big area, like the whole of Africa.

Open the practice set and try a few distances. You've got this!
