Hey — if ratio scales look like a wall of zeros, here is the calm version.

A scale tells you how much the real world was shrunk to fit on the page. You already know two kinds: the word scale, like one centimetre represents five kilometres, and the line scale, a little ruler printed on the map.

The third kind looks like 1:50 000. It means one of anything on the map is fifty thousand of the same thing on the ground. Same unit on both sides, so no units are written.

To turn it into kilometres, remember one fact: one kilometre is one hundred thousand centimetres, a one with five zeros. Knock five zeros off. 1:50 000 becomes 0,5, so one centimetre is half a kilometre. 1:250 000 becomes 2,5, so one centimetre is two and a half kilometres.

Going the other way, add five zeros. One centimetre represents five kilometres becomes 1:500 000.

The trap: a bigger number after the one means a smaller scale. 1:50 000 is zoomed in, with lots of detail. 1:10 000 000 is zoomed out, with very little.

World maps are small scale, local maps are large scale, regional maps sit between.
