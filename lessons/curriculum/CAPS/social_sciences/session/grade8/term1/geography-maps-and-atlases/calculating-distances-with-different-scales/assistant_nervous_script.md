Hey — if distance calculations feel like a new kind of maths, here is the calm version.

Every distance question is three moves. Ruler from the middle of one town to the middle of the other. Multiply by what one centimetre stands for. Write the unit.

The scale tells you what one centimetre stands for. On a ratio scale, knock off five zeros. 1:50 000 gives half a kilometre. 1:250 000 gives two and a half. 1:10 000 000 gives a hundred kilometres.

So 12,6 centimetres on a map of South Africa at 1:10 000 000 is 1 260 kilometres. That is Johannesburg to Cape Town in a straight line.

Roads bend, so the road distance is longer. If the question says along the road, follow it with string.

Long trips across the world are tricky on flat maps, because flattening a round Earth stretches it. A globe and a piece of string give the better answer.

Working backwards? Divide instead of multiply.

And always ask: does this answer make sense? Two villages are not thirty-seven kilometres apart on one small map, and no South African trip is longer than the flight to London.

Open the practice set and measure a few trips. You've got this!
