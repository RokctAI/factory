Every place on Earth has an address made of two numbers. Today we learn to read and write that address. Latitude measures how far north or south of the equator a place lies, and longitude how far east or west of the prime meridian at Greenwich. We split each degree into sixty minutes so that Johannesburg becomes 26 degrees 12 minutes south, 28 degrees 3 minutes east, and we learn the rule that latitude always comes first. Then we use the atlas index to find any place by name, page, grid square and coordinates, and we estimate minutes between the printed lines. Finally we turn the news into geography: when a cyclone, a flood or an election makes headlines, we find the place, give its coordinates and mark it on our own news map of the world.
