Hey — if degrees and minutes look scary, here is the calm version.

Imagine the Earth wrapped in belts and orange segments. The belts are latitude. They tell you how far north or south of the equator a place is, from zero up to ninety at the poles. The segments are longitude. They tell you how far east or west of Greenwich in London, from zero up to one hundred and eighty.

One degree is about a hundred and eleven kilometres, so we chop it into sixty minutes, just like an hour on a clock. Halfway is thirty minutes.

To write an address, latitude comes first, with its letter, then longitude, with its letter. Johannesburg is 26 degrees 12 minutes south, 28 degrees 3 minutes east. In South Africa it is always S and E.

Minutes never reach sixty, the same as a clock.

To find a place, use the index at the back of the atlas like a search engine. It gives the page, the square and the address.

And when something happens in the news, look the place up and mark it on your own news map. Bit by bit, the world fills in.
