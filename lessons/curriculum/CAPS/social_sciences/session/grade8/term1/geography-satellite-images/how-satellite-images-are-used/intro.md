Every weather forecast on television, every map app on a phone and every report of a flood seen from space begins with a satellite image. Today we find out what satellite images are and how they are made. We meet two kinds of satellite: weather satellites that hang over the same spot nearly thirty-six thousand kilometres up and photograph Africa every fifteen minutes, and Earth observation satellites that circle from pole to pole about seven hundred kilometres up and record fine detail. We learn that an image is a grid of pixels, that resolution decides what can be seen, and that sensors record light our eyes cannot see, which is why vegetation can appear red in a false-colour image. Then we look at how South Africa uses satellite images for weather, farming, water, fire and disaster response.
