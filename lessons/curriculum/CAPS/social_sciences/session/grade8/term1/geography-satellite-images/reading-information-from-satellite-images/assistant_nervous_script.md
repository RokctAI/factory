Hey — if reading satellite images feels like guessing, here is the calm version.

You read them like a detective, using clues. Colour: water is dark, clouds are white, sand is pale. Roughness: dams are smooth, forests are bumpy. Shape: people make straight lines, squares and circles; nature makes wiggly shapes. Size, pattern, and what is nearby help too.

Check four things before you start: where north is, how much detail there is, the date, and whether it is normal colour or false colour.

Water is dark. Plants are green, or bright red in false colour. Neat blocks of dark green are plantations. Bright green circles in dry land are irrigated fields.

People show up as grey and busy: suburbs make grids, and the pale yellow line of mine dumps near Johannesburg follows the gold reef.

In the sky, a cold front is a long curved band of cloud, thunderstorms are bright blobs, a cyclone is a spiral with an eye, and fog is a flat grey sheet along the coast.

Always use at least two clues before you decide what something is.
