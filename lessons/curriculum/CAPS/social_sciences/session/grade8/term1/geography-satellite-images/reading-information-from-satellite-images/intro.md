A satellite image has no labels, no key and no names. Everything we learn from it, we must work out ourselves, like a detective reading clues. Today we learn the clues: colour and tone, texture, shape, size, pattern and location. We use them to find water, from deep dams to muddy rivers; vegetation, from dark plantations to green circles of irrigated crops; and land use, from the grids of city suburbs to the pale mine dumps of the Witwatersrand. Then we turn to the sky and read cloud patterns: the long band of a cold front sweeping towards the Cape, the bright clusters of Highveld thunderstorms, the spiral of a tropical cyclone and the smooth sheet of coastal fog. Finally we compare images from two dates to measure change, as when Cape Town's dams shrank during the drought.
