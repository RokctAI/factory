India was a British colony. Many villagers there were poor, in debt or hit by famine.

Natal, another British colony, grew sugar on its warm coast. Sugar needs lots of workers. Most Zulu men had land of their own and refused long, low-paid contracts.

So, copying Mauritius, Natal brought workers from India. The first ship, the Truro, arrived in Durban in November 1860.

Workers signed an indenture: a contract to work for one employer for five years. Pay started at ten shillings a month and went up by one shilling a year. After ten years in Natal they could get a free ticket home, but most stayed.

About one hundred and fifty-two thousand came between 1860 and 1911.

Indenture was not slavery, but it was harsh.
