In 1866 the government of India suspended emigration to Natal, and it did not allow it again until 1874. The reason was complaints about how indentured labourers were being treated. Today we look inside the system. We study daily life on the sugar estates: the barracks, the long days in the cane, the rations, the passes, the punishments, and the illnesses. We look at how labourers resisted and how commissions of inquiry investigated. Then we meet a second group of Indian immigrants, the passenger Indians, who paid their own way to Natal as traders, and see how settlers reacted to both groups with taxes and restrictions, and how the community organised to fight back.
