On the sixteenth of November 1860 a ship called the Truro sailed into Durban harbour carrying more than three hundred men, women and children from Madras in India. They had signed contracts to work on Natal's sugar plantations. Over the next fifty years about one hundred and fifty thousand more would follow. Today we ask why. We look at how India became a British colony, why Natal's sugar planters wanted workers from so far away, what indenture meant, how labourers were recruited, and what the long voyage across the Indian Ocean was like. Their arrival began the story of South Africa's Indian community, one of the largest in the world outside India.
