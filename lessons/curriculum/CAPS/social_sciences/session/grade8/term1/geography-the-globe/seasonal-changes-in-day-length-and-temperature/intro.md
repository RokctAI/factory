In Cape Town in December the sun is up for about fourteen and a half hours; in June, for under ten. In Johannesburg the swing is smaller, and on the equator there is hardly any swing at all. Today we put the tilt of the Earth to work. We explain why days are long in summer and short in winter, why the difference grows the further a place is from the equator, and why the poles have days without night and nights without day. Then we connect day length and the angle of the midday sun to temperature, using real averages for Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban, and we find out why the hottest month is January rather than the longest day in December. We finish by reading the seasons in farming, school life and nature around us.
