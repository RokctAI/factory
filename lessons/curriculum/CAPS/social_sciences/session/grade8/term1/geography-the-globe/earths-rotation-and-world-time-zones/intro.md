Why is it already dark in Tokyo when the school day ends in Johannesburg, and why do the sun's highest moments arrive in Durban before they arrive in Cape Town? Today we follow the Earth's rotation. The Earth spins from west to east once every twenty-four hours, which gives us day and night and makes the sun appear to rise in the east. Because it turns through three hundred and sixty degrees in twenty-four hours, it turns fifteen degrees every hour, and that one fact builds the whole system of world time. We meet Greenwich time, time zones, South African Standard Time at two hours ahead of Greenwich, and the international date line, where the calendar jumps a day. Then we calculate the time anywhere in the world and the arrival time of a long-haul flight.
