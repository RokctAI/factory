Hey — if hemispheres are a bit fuzzy since Grade 6, here is the calm version.

A hemisphere is just half the Earth. There are two ways to cut it.

Cut around the middle along the equator, and you get the Northern Hemisphere on top and the Southern Hemisphere below.

Cut from pole to pole along the prime meridian through Greenwich, and back up the other side along the hundred and eightieth line, and you get the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

So every place is in two halves at once. Look at its address. S means south, N means north, E means east, W means west. South Africa is S and E, so it is in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres.

Africa is special: both cuts go through it, so it is in all four.

The north has most of the land and about nine out of ten people. The south is mostly ocean.

And because of the tilt of the Earth, the seasons are opposite. Our Christmas is in summer; in London it is winter.

Open the practice set and sort a few places into their halves. You've got this!
