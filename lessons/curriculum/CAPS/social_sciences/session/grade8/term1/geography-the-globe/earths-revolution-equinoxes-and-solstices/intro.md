The Earth does two things at once: it spins every day and it travels around the sun every year. Today we follow the yearly journey, called revolution. We see that the Earth's axis is tilted at about twenty-three and a half degrees and always points the same way in space, so that for half the year the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun and for half the year it leans away. We mark the four turning points of that journey: the solstices in June and December, when the sun is overhead at one of the tropics, and the equinoxes in March and September, when it is overhead at the equator. Then we calculate the angle of the midday sun in Johannesburg and Cape Town on each of those days, and we bust the common myth that the seasons come from the Earth's distance from the sun.
