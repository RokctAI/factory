Hey — if day length and seasons feel like a lot, here is the calm version.

Because the Earth leans, our half of the world spends more of each spin in sunlight in December and less in June. So December days are long and June days are short. In Cape Town that is about fourteen and a half hours against under ten.

The further you are from the equator, the bigger that swing. On the equator, days are about twelve hours all year. At the poles you get about six months of day and six months of night.

Summer is hot for two reasons that work together: the sun is high, so its heat is concentrated, and the days are long, so the ground heats for longer and cools for less time.

The hottest month is usually January, not December, because land and sea keep storing heat for a few weeks after the longest day. Think of a pot that keeps warming after you turn the stove down. That is the seasonal lag.

In South Africa, summer is December to February, autumn March to May, winter June to August, spring September to November.
