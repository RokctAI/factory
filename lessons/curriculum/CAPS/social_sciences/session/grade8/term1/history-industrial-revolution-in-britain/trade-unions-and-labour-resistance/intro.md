In 1834 six farm labourers from a Dorset village called Tolpuddle were sentenced to be transported to Australia for seven years. Their crime was swearing an oath to join a trade union. Today we study how working people in industrial Britain resisted low wages, long hours and dangerous conditions. We follow the Luddites who smashed machines, the crowd attacked at Peterloo, the long fight to make unions legal, the Chartists who demanded the vote for working men, and the great strikes of the matchgirls and dockers. Then we trace the right to organise all the way to South Africa, where workers' unions helped shape the country and where the Constitution now protects the right to join a union and to strike.
