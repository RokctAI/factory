Revolution is a big word. It can mean a king overthrown, enslaved people winning their freedom, or a slow change in farming that alters how a whole country lives. Today we sort out what historians mean by revolution and meet several kinds: political revolutions in America, France and Haiti, the agricultural revolution in Britain's fields, and the industrial revolution that followed. Then we step back to Britain around 1750, before the factories, and see how people lived and worked. Most worked on the land; cloth was spun and woven in cottages under the domestic system; goods moved slowly by horse and river; and energy came from muscles, water, wind and wood. Knowing this world is the only way to measure how much the Industrial Revolution changed.
