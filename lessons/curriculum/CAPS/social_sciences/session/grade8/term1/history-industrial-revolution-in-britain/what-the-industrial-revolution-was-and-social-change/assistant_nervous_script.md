Hey — if the Industrial Revolution feels like a pile of inventions and dates, here is the calm version.

It was four big changes working together. Machines did the work hands used to do. Power came from water wheels and then steam engines burning coal. Factories brought workers, machines and power into one building with a boss. And transport sped up, first with canals and then with railways from 1825 and 1830.

Coal fed the engines and iron built the machines, bridges and rails.

Then society changed. Families moved from villages to towns for work. Manchester grew from about twenty-five thousand people to over three hundred thousand. By 1851, most people in Britain lived in towns, the first country in the world where that happened.

Two new groups formed: the middle class, who owned factories and banks and got rich, and the working class, who worked for wages.

Work changed too. A bell rang at six, the machines set the pace, and days were twelve to fourteen hours long.

Life got better eventually, but mostly after 1850.
