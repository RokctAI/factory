In 1750 a cotton spinner worked a wheel by hand in a cottage. By 1850 one worker could mind machines turning hundreds of spindles in a mill powered by steam. Today we define the Industrial Revolution precisely through its four engines of change: new machines, new sources of power, the factory system and new transport. We follow coal and iron from the mine to the furnace, and canals and railways from the first lines to a national network. Then we turn to people. Britain's population tripled, the majority moved from countryside to town, and two new social classes appeared, the industrial middle class and the working class. We see how the factory clock changed time, work and family life, and why the vote became the next great struggle.
