In the 1830s a child of eight in a Lancashire cotton mill could work from six in the morning until eight at night, crawling under moving machines to sweep up fluff. In a Yorkshire coal mine, a child of six could sit alone in the dark for twelve hours opening and closing a ventilation door. Today we study work in Britain's mills and mines and the children who did so much of it. We look at the jobs children did, the hours, the dangers and the punishments, using the evidence that parliamentary inquiries collected from the children themselves. Then we follow the campaign that changed the law, from the Factory Act of 1833 and the Mines Act of 1842 to the Ten Hours Act of 1847 and compulsory schooling, and we connect it to children's rights in South Africa today.
