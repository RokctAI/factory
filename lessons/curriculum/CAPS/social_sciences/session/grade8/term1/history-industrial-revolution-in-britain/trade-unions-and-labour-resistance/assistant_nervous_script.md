Hey — lots of names and dates here, so let's keep it calm.

Workers in industrial Britain had low pay, long hours and no vote. They resisted in different ways.

Luddites, 1811 to 1816: skilled workers smashed machines that were cutting their wages. The government sent soldiers and hanged some.

Peterloo, 1819: about sixty thousand people met peacefully in Manchester to ask for the vote. Horsemen charged, and about eighteen died.

Unions: banned by the Combination Acts from 1799, made legal in 1824. In 1834 the six Tolpuddle Martyrs were sent to Australia for swearing a union oath, then pardoned in 1836 after huge protests.

Chartists, from 1838: six demands, including votes for all men over twenty-one and a secret ballot. They failed then, but five points became law later.

Great strikes: the matchgirls in 1888 and the dockers in 1889 won.

South Africa: the ICU in 1919, COSATU in 1985, and the right to join a union in the Constitution.
