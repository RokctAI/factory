Hey — if the start of the Industrial Revolution feels like a lot of names, here is the calm version.

A revolution is a huge change that makes life very different afterwards. Some revolutions change who is in charge, like America in 1775, France in 1789 and Haiti, where enslaved people won their freedom and made their own country in 1804. Others change how people live and work, like the agricultural and industrial revolutions.

Before factories, Britain was a farming country. Villages had big fields cut into strips and common land for everyone's animals. Then farming got smarter, with seed drills, turnips and clover, and better breeding. Landowners fenced the land into private farms. That is enclosure. It grew more food, but many poor families lost their land and moved to the towns.

Cloth was made at home under the domestic system. A merchant brought wool, the family spun and wove it, and the merchant paid by the piece. Families worked together at their own pace, but pay was low and the work was slow.

Spinning was the slow part, so the first great inventions were spinning machines.
