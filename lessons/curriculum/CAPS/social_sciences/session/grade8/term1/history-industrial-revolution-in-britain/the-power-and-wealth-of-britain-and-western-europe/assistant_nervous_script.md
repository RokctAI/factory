Hey — this one is a big-picture lesson, so let's keep it simple.

By about 1860 Britain was called the workshop of the world. It made about half the world's iron and coal.

Why? Coal and iron close together, inventions, banks, railways and steamships, and lots of trade.

The 1851 Great Exhibition in the Crystal Palace showed off Britain's machines.

Trade: raw cotton came from America, grown by enslaved people; wool came from Australia and South Africa. Britain sold cloth and machines everywhere. London's banks lent money worldwide, and the Royal Navy guarded the sea routes, including the Cape.

Other countries followed: Belgium, France, Germany and the United States. Germany made more steel than Britain by 1900.

The cost: Indian weavers lost work, and industrial power helped Europe conquer Africa. In South Africa, British money opened the diamond and gold mines.
