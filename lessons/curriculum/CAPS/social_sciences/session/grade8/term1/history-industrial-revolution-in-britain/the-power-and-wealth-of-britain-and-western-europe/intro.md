In 1851 more than six million visits were made to a giant building of iron and glass in London called the Crystal Palace. Inside, Britain showed the world its machines, locomotives and goods, and boasted that it was the workshop of the world. Today we ask how the Industrial Revolution made Britain, and then other countries of Western Europe, the richest and most powerful states on earth. We look at factories, coal and iron, railways and steamships, banks and free trade, and the navy that guarded it all. Then we look at the other side of the story: where the raw materials came from, who paid the price in India, the Americas and Africa, and why industrial power led to the colonisation of Africa, including South Africa.
