In 1889 a cheque for five million three hundred and thirty-eight thousand six hundred and fifty pounds was signed in Kimberley. It was one of the largest cheques ever written up to that time, and it completed the takeover of one diamond company by another. Behind it were two very different men: Cecil John Rhodes, a vicar's son who came to South Africa for his health, and Barney Barnato, a former music hall performer from the poor East End of London. Today we follow how each built a diamond company, how they fought for control of Kimberley's mines, and how their rivalry ended in De Beers Consolidated Mines, a company that controlled almost all the world's diamonds. Then we ask what their power meant for South Africa.
