When the diggings began, the diggers made a rule: one man, one claim. The idea was to give every digger a fair chance and stop rich men from buying up the whole mine. Twenty years later, one company owned nearly every claim and decided how many diamonds the world would see. Today we study how the supply and price of diamonds were regulated, first by the diggers themselves and then by the monopoly. We look at why diamond prices depend on scarcity, how overproduction caused prices to crash, how De Beers and a syndicate of London merchants controlled sales, and how controls on theft and labour were part of controlling supply. Then we follow the story to diamonds in South Africa and the world today.
