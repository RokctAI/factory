In 1872 the Kimberley Mine was divided into hundreds of small claims, each about nine and a half metres square, worked by individual diggers, and some of those claim owners were black. By 1888 a single company owned almost the whole mine, and black South Africans worked there only as low-paid labourers locked in compounds. Today we ask how that happened. We go down into the mine to see how diamond digging worked, why it became harder and more expensive the deeper the diggers went, and how small diggers were replaced by companies. We also follow the black and coloured claim owners and see how laws, licences and violence pushed them out, and how the labour system of passes and compounds was born.
