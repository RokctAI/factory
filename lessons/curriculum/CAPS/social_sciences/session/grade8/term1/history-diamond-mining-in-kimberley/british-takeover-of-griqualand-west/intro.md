In 1867 a boy called Erasmus Jacobs picked up a shiny pebble on his family's farm near Hopetown, on the banks of the Orange River. It turned out to be a diamond of more than twenty-one carats. Within four years tens of thousands of people had rushed to the dry diggings north of the river, and the land they dug on was claimed by four different rulers at once. Today we follow the discovery of diamonds and the dispute over who owned the diamond fields: the Orange Free State, the South African Republic, the Griqua leader Nicolaas Waterboer and Tswana chiefs. We see how Britain used the dispute to annex the area as Griqualand West in 1871, and what that meant for the people who lived there.
