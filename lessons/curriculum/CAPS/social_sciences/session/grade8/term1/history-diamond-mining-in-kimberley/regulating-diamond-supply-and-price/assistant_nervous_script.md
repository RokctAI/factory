Hey — this lesson has a bit of economics in it, so let's make it simple.

At first, diggers made a rule: one man, one claim. It gave each digger a fair chance, though mainly white diggers. As the mine got deep and costly, the limit rose to ten claims in 1876 and then ended. Companies took over.

Diamonds are valuable because they are rare. When Kimberley produced loads of them, prices fell. Digging more to make up for it made prices fall even more.

Once De Beers owned nearly all the mines in 1888, it dug fewer diamonds and stored extras. From 1890 a group of London merchants, the Diamond Syndicate, sold them slowly. Prices stayed high.

Stopping theft with compounds and searches was part of controlling supply.

Today the Kimberley Process tries to stop conflict diamonds.
