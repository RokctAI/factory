### subtopic_1 — Militarism and the Arms Race
**Assistant:** Why did Germany plan to go through Belgium?

**Tutor:** Because the German army wanted to defeat France quickly and avoid the strong French forts along the border between the two countries. The Schlieffen Plan aimed to send a large force through flat, neutral Belgium and sweep around behind the French armies, capturing Paris within weeks. Then German troops could be moved by railway to fight Russia in the east. The problem was that Belgium was neutral, and Britain had promised in 1839 to protect its neutrality. When Germany invaded Belgium in August 1914, Britain declared war on Germany. So a military plan designed to win quickly helped to bring Britain into the war.

### subtopic_2 — The Alliance System
**Assistant:** What is the difference between an alliance and an entente?

**Tutor:** An alliance is a formal agreement in which countries promise to support each other, often including a promise to fight if one is attacked. An entente is a friendly understanding, which settles disagreements and encourages cooperation, but it does not necessarily contain a firm promise to go to war. The Triple Alliance was a formal alliance. Britain's links with France and Russia were ententes, which is why some British leaders felt they had a choice in 1914. In practice, close military talks with France and the invasion of Belgium led Britain to join the war.

### subtopic_3 — Imperialism and Nationalism
**Assistant:** Was Germany to blame for the war?

**Tutor:** This is one of the most debated questions in history. The Treaty of Versailles of 1919 said that Germany and its allies were responsible. In the 1960s the German historian Fritz Fischer argued that German leaders aimed to expand German power and were willing to risk a major war. Other historians argue that Austria-Hungary, Russia, France and Britain also made decisions that led to war, and that the alliance system, war plans and nationalism created a situation that no single country controlled. A good answer explains the evidence for these views and reaches a reasoned judgement rather than simply blaming one country.

### subtopic_4 — Weighing the Causes, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I remember the long-term causes?

**Tutor:** Use the letters M, A, I and N: militarism, alliances, imperialism and nationalism. For each, learn a definition and one or two examples with dates: conscription, the Schlieffen Plan and HMS Dreadnought in 1906 for militarism; the Triple Alliance of 1882 and the Triple Entente of 1907 for alliances; the Moroccan crises of 1905 and 1911 for imperialism; and French revenge for Alsace and Lorraine and Balkan Slav nationalism for nationalism. Then practise explaining how the causes linked together, because examiners reward connections.
