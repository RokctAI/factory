In August 1914, crowds gathered in Berlin, Paris, London and St Petersburg to cheer as their countries went to war. Many believed it would be over by Christmas. Instead, the war lasted more than four years and killed about nine million soldiers, among them thousands of South Africans. How could a quarrel in the Balkans drag the great powers of Europe, and their empires around the world, into such a catastrophe? Historians look beyond the events of 1914 to long-term causes that built up over decades. Today we study those causes, often remembered as militarism, alliances, imperialism and nationalism.
