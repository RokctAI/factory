Look at the labels on things in your home. The phone may have been assembled in China, the rice grown in Thailand, the petrol refined from oil pumped in the Middle East, the bananas grown in Mozambique. Now think of what South Africa sends the other way: platinum and gold from the North West and Gauteng, coal from Mpumalanga, iron ore from the Northern Cape, cars made in Gqeberha and East London, oranges from Limpopo and the Eastern Cape. Every one of these goods crossed the world by ship, aircraft, train, truck or pipeline. Today we study why countries trade, the basic language of trade, and why trade and transport are so closely linked that one cannot grow without the other.
