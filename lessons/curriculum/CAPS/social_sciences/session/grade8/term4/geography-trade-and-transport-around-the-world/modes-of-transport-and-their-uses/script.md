# Part 1 — Expert

This session examines the five main modes of transport used to move goods and people: sea, air, road, rail and pipelines. The anchor is a set of South African and African examples, from iron ore trains and container ships to air freight and fuel pipelines. The governing idea is that each mode has advantages and disadvantages in cost, speed, capacity, flexibility, reliability and environmental impact, so the choice of mode depends on the type of goods, the distance, the urgency and the cost, and modern trade often combines several modes in one journey. Describing each mode, its uses, and its advantages and disadvantages is examinable this term.

## Subtopic: Sea Transport

Sea transport carries goods across oceans and along coasts in ships. It carries most of the world's international trade by volume.

There are different kinds of ships for different cargoes. Container ships carry manufactured goods, packed in standard containers stacked on deck and in the hold; the largest can carry more than twenty thousand twenty-foot containers. Bulk carriers carry loose dry cargo in large holds, such as iron ore, coal, grain and manganese; South Africa's iron ore leaves from Saldanha Bay and its coal from Richards Bay on bulk carriers. Tankers carry liquids, such as crude oil, refined fuel, chemicals and liquefied gas. Refrigerated ships, called reefers, and refrigerated containers carry fruit, meat and fish; South African citrus and grapes are shipped to Europe, Asia and the Middle East in refrigerated containers. Roll-on roll-off ships, called ro-ro ships, carry vehicles that drive on and off; cars made in South Africa are exported this way from ports such as Durban, Gqeberha and East London. Passenger ships include ferries and cruise ships.

Advantages of sea transport. It is the cheapest way to move large quantities of heavy and bulky goods over long distances, because one ship carries an enormous load with a small crew and relatively little fuel per tonne. It can cross oceans, which land transport cannot.

Disadvantages. It is slow; a voyage from South Africa to Europe or China takes weeks. It needs ports with deep water, cranes, storage and transport links, which are expensive to build and maintain. Goods usually need another mode of transport to reach the port and to travel inland from the port. Ships can be delayed by storms, port congestion and blocked routes. Ships cause pollution through emissions, oil spills and waste, and can damage marine ecosystems.

Uses. Sea transport is best for heavy, bulky, low-value goods that are not urgent, such as minerals, grain and fuel, and for large volumes of manufactured goods in containers.

Inland waterways, such as rivers, lakes and canals, are also used in some parts of the world, such as the Rhine in Europe, the Mississippi in the United States and the Congo River in Africa, though many African rivers are difficult to navigate because of waterfalls, rapids and seasonal changes in water level.

South Africa's commercial ports are run by the state-owned Transnet National Ports Authority. The main ports are Richards Bay, Durban, East London, Port of Ngqura and Port Elizabeth in Nelson Mandela Bay, Mossel Bay, Cape Town and Saldanha Bay, and each specialises in particular cargoes. Their efficiency affects the cost of almost everything South Africans buy and sell abroad, which is why delays at the ports are reported in the news.

## Subtopic: Air and Road Transport

Air transport carries passengers and cargo in aircraft. Cargo travels in the holds of passenger aircraft or in dedicated freight aircraft.

Advantages. Air transport is the fastest mode over long distances; goods can reach another continent in hours. It is ideal for perishable goods that spoil quickly, such as cut flowers, fresh fish, berries and some vegetables; for valuable goods that are light and small, such as diamonds, gold, jewellery, electronics and medicines; and for urgent goods, such as spare parts, documents, vaccines and emergency aid. It can reach places that are hard to reach by land, such as remote or mountainous areas. Kenya, for example, exports cut flowers to Europe by air, and South Africa exports fruit, flowers and high-value goods by air from O. R. Tambo International Airport.

Disadvantages. Air transport is by far the most expensive mode per tonne. Aircraft carry relatively small loads. It uses a great deal of fuel and produces high carbon emissions per tonne-kilometre, contributing to climate change. It needs airports, which are expensive and take up large areas of land, and goods must be taken to and from airports by road. Weather can cause delays.

Road transport moves goods and people in trucks, vans, buses, minibus taxis and cars on roads.

Advantages. Road transport is the most flexible mode. It can deliver door-to-door, from a farm or factory directly to a shop or home, without changing mode. Routes and schedules can be changed easily. It is good for short and medium distances, for smaller loads and for areas without railways. In South Africa, most freight is carried by road, and trucks connect farms, factories, ports and towns across the country. The N3 highway between Durban and Gauteng is one of the busiest freight routes in Africa.

Disadvantages. Road transport is more expensive per tonne than rail and sea over long distances. Trucks carry smaller loads than trains. Heavy trucks damage roads, which are expensive to repair. Roads become congested, causing delays. Road transport produces air pollution and carbon emissions, and road accidents kill many people every year. When too much freight moves by road instead of rail, roads deteriorate faster and become more dangerous.

Passengers also use these modes. Air travel connects South Africa's major cities and the world, and supports tourism, an important source of foreign income. Road transport carries most passengers within South Africa: long-distance buses, minibus taxis and private cars link towns and cities, and the minibus taxi industry carries millions of commuters every day, which you will study later this term.

## Subtopic: Rail and Pipelines

Rail transport moves goods and passengers in trains on railway lines.

Advantages. Rail is very efficient for carrying heavy, bulky goods over long distances on land. A single freight train can carry as much as many trucks, with fewer drivers and less fuel per tonne, so rail is cheaper than road for long-distance bulk freight and produces fewer emissions per tonne. Trains are not slowed by road traffic and can run on fixed schedules. Rail is safer than road for dangerous goods.

South Africa has some of the most impressive heavy-haul railways in the world. The Sishen to Saldanha line carries iron ore from the Northern Cape to the port of Saldanha Bay, about eight hundred and sixty kilometres, on very long trains. The coal line from the Mpumalanga coalfields to Richards Bay carries coal for export. Passenger rail includes Metrorail commuter trains in the major cities, and the Gautrain, a rapid rail link opened in 2010 between Johannesburg, Pretoria and O. R. Tambo airport.

Disadvantages. Railways are very expensive to build and maintain, and they need flat or gently sloping routes, so mountains and valleys make them more costly. Trains can only go where the tracks go, so goods usually need trucks at each end, which adds handling costs and time. Rail is less flexible than road. If railways are poorly maintained, or if equipment such as cables is stolen, services become slow and unreliable, and freight moves to the roads. In recent years, South Africa's freight rail has faced such problems, and reforms aim to improve it and allow more operators.

Pipelines carry liquids and gases, such as crude oil, refined fuels, natural gas and water, through pipes, usually buried underground.

Advantages. Once built, pipelines are a cheap, continuous and reliable way to move large volumes of liquids and gases over long distances. They need few workers, are not affected by traffic or weather, take up little land when buried, and reduce the number of fuel tankers on the roads. In South Africa, a multi-product pipeline carries petrol, diesel and jet fuel more than five hundred kilometres from Durban to Gauteng, and gas pipelines bring natural gas from Mozambique. The Lesotho Highlands Water Project transfers water through tunnels from dams in Lesotho to the Vaal River system, which supplies Gauteng.

Disadvantages. Pipelines are very expensive to build. They can only carry liquids and gases, and only along fixed routes. Leaks can cause pollution, fires and explosions, and pipelines can be damaged or tapped illegally. They are not flexible: if demand moves, the pipeline cannot.

Africa has seen major new railway projects in recent years, such as the standard gauge railway from Mombasa to Nairobi in Kenya, opened in 2017, and the railway from Addis Ababa in Ethiopia to the port of Djibouti, opened in 2016 and 2018. Older colonial lines, such as the Benguela Railway in Angola, which links the copper mines of Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the Atlantic, have been rebuilt.

## Subtopic: Choosing a Mode and the Error Museum

To choose the best mode of transport, consider five questions. What are the goods: heavy or light, bulky or small, valuable or cheap, perishable or durable, solid, liquid or gas? How far must they travel? How quickly must they arrive? How much can be spent? Where are the starting point and destination, and what infrastructure connects them?

A calculation compares rail and road for bulk goods. Suppose, as an example for the method, a freight train has one hundred wagons, each carrying sixty tonnes. One hundred times sixty is six thousand tonnes. A large truck carries about thirty-four tonnes. Six thousand divided by thirty-four is about one hundred and seventy-six point five, so it would take about one hundred and seventy-seven trucks to carry the same load. Those trucks would need one hundred and seventy-seven drivers, far more fuel, and would add heavy traffic to the roads. This is why bulk minerals such as iron ore and coal are moved by rail.

Many journeys combine several modes, called intermodal or multimodal transport. For example, oranges from a farm in Limpopo travel by truck to a packhouse, by truck or train in a refrigerated container to the port of Durban, by ship to Europe, and by truck to a supermarket. Containers make this easy because they can be moved between modes without unpacking.

Transport choices also affect the environment. Per tonne-kilometre, sea and rail usually produce the lowest carbon emissions, road more, and air by far the most. Moving more freight from road to rail and using cleaner fuels can reduce pollution and climate change.

The error museum, five exhibits. One: saying air is always the best because it is fastest. It is too expensive for most goods. Two: saying road transport is always cheapest. It is cheapest for short distances and small loads, but not for long-distance bulk. Three: saying pipelines can carry any goods. They carry only liquids and gases. Four: forgetting that most journeys need more than one mode. Five: ignoring the environmental impact of each mode.

Layout for marks in a transport question: name the mode, give two advantages and two disadvantages, give a South African or African example, and explain why it suits the goods in the question.

The questions for this section are with you now: choosing a mode, the train and truck calculation, intermodal transport and the error museum.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same lesson again, plain words, same facts: ships, planes and trucks, and trains and pipes.

## Subtopic: Ships

Ships carry most of the world's goods by weight. Container ships carry boxes of goods. Bulk carriers carry loose things like coal and iron ore. Tankers carry oil. Car ships let cars drive on and off.

Ships are slow but very cheap for big, heavy loads. They need ports, and trucks or trains to bring goods to the port.

Picture a giant ship stacked with thousands of coloured boxes.

Keep that ship in mind and try the questions for this part: sea transport.

## Subtopic: Planes and Trucks

Planes are the fastest. They carry things that are light, valuable or that spoil quickly: flowers, fresh fish, medicines, diamonds. But planes are very expensive and cause a lot of pollution.

Trucks can go door-to-door, from the farm straight to the shop. They are flexible and good for short trips. But they carry smaller loads, damage roads, cause traffic jams and pollution.

Keep the plane and the truck in mind and try the questions for this part: air and road.

## Subtopic: Trains and Pipes

Trains carry heavy things like coal and iron ore over long distances, cheaply. One long train can carry as much as about one hundred and seventy-seven trucks. But trains only go where the tracks go.

Pipelines carry liquids and gases: petrol from Durban to Gauteng, gas from Mozambique, water from Lesotho. They are cheap to run but very expensive to build.

Pocket summary of the lesson. Sea transport is cheapest for heavy, bulky goods over long distances but slow and needs ports. Air is fastest and suits light, valuable, perishable or urgent goods but is the most expensive and polluting. Road is flexible and door-to-door but costly over long distances and damages roads. Rail is efficient for heavy bulk over long distances but expensive to build and fixed to tracks. Pipelines move liquids and gases cheaply once built. Most journeys combine several modes.

Keep all five modes in mind, and take the final questions of the lesson: modes of transport.
