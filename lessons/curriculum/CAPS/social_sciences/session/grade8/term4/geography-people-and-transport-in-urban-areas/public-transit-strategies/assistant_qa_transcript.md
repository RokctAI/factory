### subtopic_1 — Rapid Transport Systems
**Assistant:** Why do some drivers dislike bus lanes?

**Tutor:** Because a bus lane takes away a lane that cars could use, so the remaining car lanes can become more crowded, at least at first. A driver stuck in traffic sees a nearly empty bus lane and feels that space is wasted. But a lane should be judged by how many people it moves, not how many vehicles. A bus lane with frequent full buses can move about twice as many people as a car lane. Over time, if the buses are fast and reliable, some drivers switch to the bus, which helps everyone. Planners therefore try to explain the benefits and make the bus service good enough that people want to use it.

### subtopic_2 — Subsidies and Making Public Transport Affordable
**Assistant:** Why has BRT caused conflict with taxi operators?

**Tutor:** BRT routes often run along the same main roads that taxis already served. When a new bus system starts, taxi owners fear losing passengers and income, because their business depends on fares. In some cities this led to protests and even violence. To reduce conflict, cities have negotiated with taxi associations, compensated owners whose routes were affected, and given taxi operators shares in the companies that run BRT buses, so that they become part of the new system rather than being pushed out. Including the taxi industry in planning is an important lesson for future projects.

### subtopic_3 — Bus Lanes, Cycle Lanes, Park and Ride and Car-Free Zones
**Assistant:** Is park and ride useful in South Africa?

**Tutor:** It can be, where there is a fast and reliable public transport link from the parking area into the city, such as the Gautrain. Drivers can avoid the worst congestion and parking costs in the city centre. However, park and ride only helps people who own cars. Most low-income commuters do not have a car, so they need good feeder buses and taxi links rather than parking. It is one useful tool among many, not a complete answer.

### subtopic_4 — Evaluating Strategies, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I evaluate a transport strategy in an exam?

**Tutor:** Describe the strategy briefly and give a South African example. Then give advantages, such as less congestion, lower cost or cleaner air, and disadvantages, such as cost to the public, loss of road space or limited coverage. Ask who benefits and who might be left out, especially low-income commuters. Finish with a judgement, for example that a strategy works well on a busy corridor but must be combined with other measures. Use numbers where you can, such as people moved per hour.
