Picture the M1 highway into Johannesburg or the N2 into Cape Town at seven in the morning. Lanes of cars crawl bumper to bumper, many carrying only the driver. Minibus taxis weave between them, buses wait in the same queue, and a brown haze hangs over the city. Meanwhile, workers who left home before dawn are still on the road, paying fares that eat into their wages. Today we study the problems of urban transport: the cost to commuters, traffic congestion and pollution.
