### subtopic_1 — The Cost of Commuting
**Assistant:** Why do taxi fares go up when the petrol price rises?

**Tutor:** Because fuel is one of the biggest costs of running a taxi. A minibus taxi drives many kilometres every day, and most taxis are not subsidised by the government in the way that many buses and trains are. The owner must pay for fuel, maintenance, insurance, the driver and repayments on the vehicle from the fares collected. When fuel becomes more expensive, the owner's costs rise, and the taxi association usually agrees on a fare increase to cover them. This affects millions of commuters who depend on taxis, which is why changes in the fuel price are important news in South Africa.

### subtopic_2 — Traffic Congestion
**Assistant:** What is a temperature inversion?

**Tutor:** Normally, air near the ground is warmer than the air above it, so warm air, carrying pollution, rises and spreads out. On cold, still winter nights on the highveld, the ground loses heat quickly and cools the air touching it. A layer of warmer air can then lie above the cold air near the ground. This upside-down arrangement is called a temperature inversion. The warm layer acts like a lid, so smoke and exhaust cannot rise and are trapped near the ground. That is why the air in Gauteng can look hazy and smell of smoke on winter mornings, until the sun warms the ground and the inversion breaks up.

### subtopic_3 — Air Pollution, Noise and Health
**Assistant:** Why does congestion affect taxi and bus passengers who do not drive?

**Tutor:** Because they share the same roads. Unless buses have their own lanes, they wait in the same queues as private cars. A bus carrying sixty people takes up about as much road space as three cars carrying perhaps four people in total, yet all of them move at the same slow speed. So public transport passengers lose time because of traffic caused mostly by private vehicles. This is one reason why cities create dedicated bus lanes: to let the people who use road space most efficiently move faster.

### subtopic_4 — Linking the Problems, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I answer a question on urban transport problems?

**Tutor:** Name the problem clearly, then give causes and effects separately, with specific examples. For cost, mention fares, fuel and time, and explain why the poorest are hit hardest. For congestion, mention peak hours, single-occupant cars and the effects on time, fuel, business and emergency services. For pollution, name some pollutants, explain smog and give health effects. Show how the problems link together. If there is a calculation, write each step, for example distance times trips times days, then fuel per hundred kilometres, then the price.
