In 1913, a woman named Emily Wilding Davison ran onto the racecourse at the Epsom Derby and was struck by the king's horse. She died four days later. Around the same time, women were smashing shop windows in London, setting fire to empty buildings, and refusing to eat in prison, where they were held down and force-fed through tubes. Their goal was simple: the right to vote. Leading many of them was Emmeline Pankhurst. Then, in 1914, war broke out, and everything changed. Today we study the campaign for women's votes in Britain, Emmeline Pankhurst's role, and how the First World War affected the outcome.
