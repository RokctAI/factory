### subtopic_1 — Suffragists: The Peaceful Campaign
**Assistant:** Were the suffragettes right to use violence?

**Tutor:** This is a question of judgement, and people have disagreed since the time. Supporters argue that decades of peaceful campaigning had failed, that the suffragettes mostly attacked property rather than people, and that their actions forced the issue into the headlines. Critics argue that violence, especially arson, put lives at risk, turned some politicians and members of the public against the cause and gave opponents an excuse to delay reform. Many historians think the peaceful and militant campaigns together kept up pressure in different ways. In an exam, explain both views with evidence before giving your own reasoned judgement.

### subtopic_2 — Emmeline Pankhurst and the Suffragettes
**Assistant:** Why was the voting age for women set at 30 in 1918?

**Tutor:** Politicians were worried about the effect of suddenly giving the vote to millions of women. So many men had been killed in the war that, if women had been given the vote at 21 like men, women would have been a majority of voters. Setting the age at 30 and adding a property qualification limited the number of women voters to a minority of the electorate. This shows that, even in 1918, many politicians did not yet accept full equality between men and women. The difference was removed in 1928.

### subtopic_3 — The War and the Vote
**Assistant:** What is the difference between a suffragist and a suffragette?

**Tutor:** Both campaigned for women's right to vote. Suffragists, mainly in the NUWSS led by Millicent Fawcett, used peaceful and legal methods, such as petitions and meetings. Suffragettes, members of the WSPU led by Emmeline Pankhurst, used direct action and militant methods, such as window smashing and arson. The name suffragette was first used by the Daily Mail in 1906 to mock the WSPU, but its members adopted it with pride. In exams, using the two terms correctly shows that you understand the different approaches.

### subtopic_4 — Weighing the Causes, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I answer a question on why women got the vote?

**Tutor:** Identify several factors: the long peaceful campaign, the suffragette campaign, women's war work, the need to reform votes for soldiers, and political change. For each, explain how it helped, with evidence. Then weigh them, perhaps arguing that the war was the trigger but that decades of campaigning had laid the foundations. Remember to say exactly which women got the vote in 1918 and that equal voting came in 1928. A comparison with South Africa, where white women voted from 1930 and all adults from 1994, can strengthen your answer.
