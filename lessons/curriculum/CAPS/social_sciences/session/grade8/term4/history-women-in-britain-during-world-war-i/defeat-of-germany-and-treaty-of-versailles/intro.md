At eleven o'clock in the morning on 11 November 1918, the guns on the Western Front fell silent. In a railway carriage in a forest near Compiègne in France, German representatives had signed an armistice a few hours earlier. Seven months later, on 28 June 1919, exactly five years after the shots at Sarajevo, German delegates were brought into the glittering Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles to sign a peace treaty they had not been allowed to negotiate. Among the signatories were two South Africans, Louis Botha and Jan Smuts. Today we study how Germany was defeated, and what the Treaty of Versailles demanded.
