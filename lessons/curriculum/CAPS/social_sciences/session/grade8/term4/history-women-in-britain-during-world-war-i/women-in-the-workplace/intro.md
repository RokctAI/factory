In 1914 a young woman in a British town might work as a maid in a wealthy household, as a seamstress or in a textile mill, or not work for wages at all. By 1917 she might be filling artillery shells with explosives in a munitions factory, driving a bus, working the land, or serving in uniform in France. Her skin might even have turned yellow from handling chemicals. The war took millions of men away from their jobs, and women stepped in. Today we study how the First World War changed women's work in Britain, what women achieved, the dangers and unfairness they faced, and what happened when the war ended.
