### subtopic_1 — The Defeat of Germany in 1918
**Assistant:** Why did many Germans believe they had not really lost the war?

**Tutor:** In November 1918, German armies were still in France and Belgium, and no Allied soldiers had fought on German soil. Wartime propaganda and censorship had hidden how badly the war was going, so the sudden request for an armistice came as a shock. When the new democratic government signed the armistice and later the treaty, some nationalists and army leaders claimed that the army had been betrayed by politicians, socialists, Jews and others at home: the so-called stab-in-the-back myth. Historians reject this myth: the German army was being defeated on the Western Front, and its leaders, Hindenburg and Ludendorff, had themselves told the government that an armistice was necessary. But the myth was believed by many Germans and was later used by Hitler.

### subtopic_2 — The Peace Conference and the Big Three
**Assistant:** Why did South Africa get German South West Africa?

**Tutor:** South African forces had conquered German South West Africa in 1915, and South African leaders, especially Smuts, argued that the territory should be joined to South Africa. Wilson opposed simply annexing colonies, so the mandate system was created as a compromise: South Africa would govern the territory on behalf of the League of Nations and was supposed to promote the well-being of its people. In practice, South Africa treated it much like a fifth province and later applied apartheid policies there. The territory gained independence as Namibia only in 1990, after a long struggle.

### subtopic_3 — The Terms of the Treaty of Versailles
**Assistant:** What were reparations, and could Germany pay them?

**Tutor:** Reparations were payments that Germany had to make to the Allies to compensate for the damage caused by the war, especially in France and Belgium. In 1921 the total was set at 132 billion gold marks. Germany paid part of the sum, but it struggled, and in 1923 when it fell behind, French and Belgian troops occupied the Ruhr, Germany's main industrial area. This contributed to severe inflation in Germany. Payments were later reduced and rescheduled, and they were effectively stopped in the early 1930s. Historians debate whether Germany could have paid more if it had wanted to, and whether reparations were the main cause of its economic problems.

### subtopic_4 — Judging the Treaty, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I evaluate the Treaty of Versailles in an exam?

**Tutor:** First, describe the main terms under clear headings: guilt, reparations, armed forces, territory and colonies, and the League. Then explain different views: Germans saw it as a harsh Diktat; Clemenceau and many French people thought it too lenient; Keynes thought reparations too harsh; and some historians argue it was a reasonable compromise that was poorly enforced. Use evidence for each view, such as the loss of about thirteen percent of territory, the one hundred thousand army limit and comparison with Brest-Litovsk. Finish with your own reasoned judgement.
