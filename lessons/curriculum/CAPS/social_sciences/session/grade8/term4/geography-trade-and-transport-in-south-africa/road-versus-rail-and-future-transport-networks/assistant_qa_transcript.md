### subtopic_1 — Road Transport: Advantages and Disadvantages
**Assistant:** If trains are cheaper per tonne, why do companies still use trucks?

**Tutor:** Because cost per tonne is not the only thing a company cares about. A business also needs its goods to arrive on time, in good condition and at the right place. A truck can collect from the factory and deliver straight to the customer, leave as soon as the load is ready and change route if there is a problem. A train needs the goods to be taken to a terminal, loaded, carried, unloaded and then usually put on a truck anyway. If the train is also late, or a locomotive breaks down, the cheaper price is lost in delays. In South Africa, years of unreliable rail service pushed many customers to trucks. So trucks often win for reliability and flexibility, even when rail would be cheaper for the actual distance travelled.

### subtopic_2 — Rail Transport: Advantages and Disadvantages
**Assistant:** What is cable theft and why does it affect trains?

**Tutor:** Cable theft is the stealing of the copper cables that carry electricity and signals along railway lines. Copper can be sold as scrap metal, so criminals cut out lengths of cable. Electric trains need the overhead power lines to run, and all trains need working signals to run safely. When cables are stolen, trains stop or must run very slowly under manual control, causing delays and cancellations for commuters and freight. Repairing the damage costs a great deal of money. That is why protecting railway infrastructure is one of the requirements for a reliable future network.

### subtopic_3 — Comparing Modes on the Durban to Gauteng Corridor
**Assistant:** Does building more roads fix traffic jams?

**Tutor:** Usually only for a short time. When a new lane or road is opened, travel becomes easier, so more people decide to drive and some trucks switch from other routes. After a while, the new road can be as crowded as the old one. Planners call this induced demand. Roads are still needed, and some new roads are important, but a lasting solution also includes good public transport, moving bulk freight to rail, and planning cities so that people live closer to work and services.

### subtopic_4 — Future Networks, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How should I answer a road versus rail question?

**Tutor:** Start by naming the goods, the distance and what matters most, such as cost, speed or flexibility. Then give advantages and disadvantages of each mode that relate to that situation, rather than a general list. Use an example from South Africa, such as coal by rail to Richards Bay or fresh produce by road to a local market. End with a clear conclusion that says which mode, or which combination, is best and why. If the question asks about the future, mention reliability, integration, safety, sustainability and access for all.
