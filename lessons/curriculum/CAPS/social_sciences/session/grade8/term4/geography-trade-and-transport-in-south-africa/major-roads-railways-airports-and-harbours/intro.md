Open a road map of South Africa and trace the red lines of the national roads. The N1 runs from Cape Town through Bloemfontein and Johannesburg to Beitbridge on the Zimbabwe border. The N2 follows the coast from Cape Town through George, Gqeberha, East London and Durban. The N3 climbs from Durban over the Drakensberg escarpment to Johannesburg. Now add the railways, the airports and the eight commercial harbours. A pattern appears: lines that run from the coast to Gauteng, the economic heart of the country, and on to the neighbouring countries. Today we study South Africa's transport network on a map, and explain why it has the shape it has.
