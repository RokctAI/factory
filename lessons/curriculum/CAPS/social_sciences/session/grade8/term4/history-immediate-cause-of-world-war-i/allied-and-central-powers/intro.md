By the end of 1914, two great coalitions faced each other across Europe and beyond. On one side stood Britain and its empire, France and Russia, soon joined by others. On the other stood Germany and Austria-Hungary, joined by the Ottoman Empire. Over the next four years, the war spread from the fields of Belgium to the mountains of Italy, the beaches of Gallipoli, the deserts of the Middle East, the bush of East Africa and the oceans of the world. Today we identify the Allied Powers and the Central Powers, find them on a map of Europe, and see how the war became a world war, including for South Africa.
