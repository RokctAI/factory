### subtopic_1 — Background: Bosnia, Serbia and Austria-Hungary
**Assistant:** Why did a murder in one city start a world war?

**Tutor:** Because Europe was already divided and tense, the assassination set off a chain of reactions. Austria-Hungary saw it as a chance to crush Serbian nationalism. Germany backed Austria-Hungary with the blank cheque. Russia backed Serbia, France was allied to Russia, and Germany's war plan meant attacking France through Belgium, which brought in Britain. Each government made choices, often expecting the others to back down. The long-term causes of militarism, alliances, imperialism and nationalism made it much harder to keep the crisis local, so the murder became the trigger for a much bigger war.

### subtopic_2 — The Assassination: 28 June 1914
**Assistant:** Was the Serbian government behind the assassination?

**Tutor:** The conspirators were helped by members of the Black Hand, including Serbian army officers led by Colonel Apis, the head of military intelligence, and their weapons came from Serbia. However, the Black Hand was a secret society, not the government itself, and it was often in conflict with the prime minister, Nikola Pašić. Evidence suggests that Pašić had some warning that armed men had crossed into Bosnia, and that any warning passed to Vienna was vague. Historians therefore usually say that elements within Serbia, rather than the government as a whole, organised the plot, while the government failed to stop it.

### subtopic_3 — The July Crisis and the Road to War
**Assistant:** What is an ultimatum?

**Tutor:** An ultimatum is a final set of demands, usually with a deadline, which warns that serious action, often war, will follow if the demands are not met. Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia on 23 July 1914 gave Serbia 48 hours to reply. Britain later sent an ultimatum to Germany demanding that it respect Belgian neutrality. Ultimatums leave little time for negotiation, which is one reason why the July Crisis moved so quickly.

### subtopic_4 — Immediate and Long-Term Causes, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I sequence the events of 1914 in an exam?

**Tutor:** Learn the key dates in order: 28 June, assassination at Sarajevo; 5 and 6 July, the blank cheque; 23 July, ultimatum to Serbia; 28 July, Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia; 30 July, Russian general mobilisation; 1 August, Germany declares war on Russia; 3 August, Germany declares war on France; 4 August, Germany invades Belgium and Britain declares war on Germany. Draw a timeline or a chain of arrows, and for each step explain why it led to the next. Link each step to a long-term cause where you can.
