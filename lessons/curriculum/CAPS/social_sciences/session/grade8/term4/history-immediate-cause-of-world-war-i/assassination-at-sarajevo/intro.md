On Sunday 28 June 1914, an open car carrying Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary, and his wife Sophie took a wrong turn in the streets of Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia. As the driver stopped to reverse, a nineteen-year-old Bosnian Serb named Gavrilo Princip stepped forward and fired two shots. Within an hour, both the archduke and his wife were dead. Within six weeks, the great powers of Europe were at war. Today we study the immediate cause of the First World War: the assassination at Sarajevo and the crisis that followed.
