### subtopic_1 — Volunteers: Answering the Call in 1914
**Assistant:** Why did so many men volunteer in 1914?

**Tutor:** There were many reasons, and different men had different motives. Many felt a strong sense of patriotism and duty to defend Britain and the empire. Reports of the German invasion of Belgium made people angry. Many believed the war would be short and exciting, and saw it as a chance for adventure and travel. Friends joined together in Pals battalions, and there was strong pressure from families, employers, newspapers, posters and even strangers with white feathers. For some poorer men, the army offered regular pay, food and clothing. Historians use letters, diaries and recruitment records to understand these motives, and they show that the reasons were often mixed.

### subtopic_2 — Conscription in Britain
**Assistant:** Is all propaganda lies?

**Tutor:** No. Propaganda can contain true information, half-truths, exaggerations or outright lies. What makes it propaganda is its purpose: to influence people's thoughts and actions in a particular direction, usually by appealing to emotions and by leaving out information that does not suit the message. For example, German soldiers did kill civilians in Belgium in 1914, which is true, but some British stories about German atrocities were invented or exaggerated. So when you study propaganda, you must check its claims against other evidence, and ask what the makers wanted their audience to believe.

### subtopic_3 — Propaganda and Censorship
**Assistant:** Were conscientious objectors brave or cowardly?

**Tutor:** At the time, many people called them cowards, and they were treated harshly. Today historians and many members of the public see the question differently. Many objectors showed great courage: some served as stretcher-bearers under fire, and absolutists endured prison, hard labour and public hatred rather than act against their beliefs. Others served the country in civilian work. It is fair to say that objectors held a sincere moral position that most people around them did not accept. A balanced answer explains why they were seen as cowards then, and why many see them as people of principle now.

### subtopic_4 — Conscientious Objectors, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I analyse a recruitment poster in an exam?

**Tutor:** Describe what you see: the images, words, colours and people shown. Identify the message, for example that every man must join, or that those who stay home should feel ashamed. Explain the techniques used, such as pointing directly at the viewer, guilt, pride, fear or hatred of the enemy. Identify the intended audience, such as young men, women or children. Consider when and why it was made: posters from 1914 and 1915 were trying to increase volunteering before conscription. Finally, say what the poster shows a historian about the war, and what it does not show.
