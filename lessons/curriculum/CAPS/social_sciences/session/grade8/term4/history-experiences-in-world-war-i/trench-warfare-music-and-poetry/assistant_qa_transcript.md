### subtopic_1 — Why the Trenches? Stalemate on the Western Front
**Assistant:** Why didn't generals just stop attacking?

**Tutor:** Generals were under great pressure to win the war, and they believed that the war could only be won by defeating the enemy army, which meant attacking. Their allies also demanded action: for example, the Somme offensive of 1916 was partly intended to relieve pressure on the French at Verdun. Generals also hoped that new methods, such as bigger bombardments, gas or tanks, would finally break through. Over time, they did learn, and by 1918 the Allies combined artillery, tanks, aircraft and infantry much more effectively. Historians still debate how much the generals deserve blame for the huge casualties, and how much they were trapped by the weapons and conditions of the time.

### subtopic_2 — Life in the Trenches
**Assistant:** What was the Christmas truce?

**Tutor:** At Christmas 1914, in several places along the Western Front, British and German soldiers stopped fighting without official permission. They sang carols to each other across no man's land, and in some places they climbed out of their trenches, shook hands, exchanged gifts such as cigarettes and food, buried their dead and, according to some accounts, played informal games of football. Commanders disapproved, and there was no similar truce on that scale in later years. The truce shows that ordinary soldiers on both sides often did not hate each other as propaganda suggested.

### subtopic_3 — Songs and Poetry as Sources
**Assistant:** Why is Dulce et Decorum Est so famous?

**Tutor:** Because it describes the reality of war in vivid, shocking detail and directly challenges the idea that dying for one's country is sweet and glorious. Owen uses powerful images, such as soldiers bent double like old beggars, the panic of a gas attack, and a dying man's face, to make readers feel the horror. He wrote it as a soldier who had fought and been treated for shell shock, which gives it authority. It is often contrasted with patriotic poems written earlier, and it is widely studied as both literature and a historical source.

### subtopic_4 — Analysing a Source, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I compare two war poems in an exam?

**Tutor:** Start by identifying each poem's author, date and the author's experience of war. Then describe the attitude of each poem to war, with a short quotation or reference as evidence. Explain the language and images each poet uses to create that attitude. Then explain why the attitudes differ, for example because one was written early in the war before the poet saw combat, and the other later by a soldier who had suffered. Finish by saying what the two poems together show a historian about how attitudes to the war changed.
