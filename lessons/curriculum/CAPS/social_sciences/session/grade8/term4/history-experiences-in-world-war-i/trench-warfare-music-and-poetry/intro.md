Imagine a ditch about two metres deep, its floor covered with wooden boards sinking into mud. Rats run along the walls, lice crawl in your clothes, and your feet have been wet for days. A few hundred metres away, behind tangles of barbed wire, another army waits in its own ditch. When the whistle blows, you must climb out and walk towards machine guns. This was life for millions of soldiers on the Western Front. Yet in the same trenches, men sang songs and wrote some of the most powerful poetry in the English language. Today we study trench warfare and how soldiers' experiences were expressed in music and poetry.
