### subtopic_1 — South Africans on the Western Front
**Assistant:** Why were SANLC members kept in closed compounds in France?

**Tutor:** The South African government wanted to control the black labourers and to prevent them from mixing freely with French civilians and soldiers from other countries. In France, there was less racial segregation than in South Africa, and the government feared that black South Africans would experience more equal treatment, form new ideas about their rights and bring those ideas home. So the men were housed in compounds, similar to those used on South African mines, and their movement was restricted. Many of the men resented this treatment, and it shows how segregation was carried even to the battlefields of Europe.

### subtopic_2 — The Battle of Delville Wood, July 1916
**Assistant:** Did the Darro's captain deliberately let the men drown?

**Tutor:** The inquiry did not find that he deliberately intended them to die, but it found him to blame for travelling too fast in thick fog without proper signals, and for failing to help the survivors in the water even though his ship stayed afloat. Why he did not lower boats is not fully known; he may have feared for his own damaged ship, or been confused in the dark. Whatever his reasons, his failure to help was seen as a serious wrong, and many South Africans believed that his punishment, a one-year suspension of his licence, would have been much heavier if the victims had been white.

### subtopic_3 — The Sinking of the SS Mendi, February 1917
**Assistant:** Why is it important to remember the Mendi today?

**Tutor:** Remembering the Mendi honours the men who served and died, and their families. It also corrects an unfair historical memory in which the contribution of black South Africans was ignored for most of the twentieth century. The story of dignity in the face of death has become a symbol of courage and unity for many South Africans. By studying how the Mendi was forgotten and then remembered, we learn that history is not only about what happened, but also about who decides which stories are told.

### subtopic_4 — Memory and Forgetting, Calculation and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How do I write about remembrance in an exam?

**Tutor:** First, describe each event briefly with dates and facts. Then describe how each was commemorated over time, giving specific examples such as the 1926 memorial at Delville Wood, Mendi Day, the 1995 Soweto memorial, the Order of Mendi and the 2016 rededication. Explain why the memory differed, linking it to segregation and white minority rule before 1994. End with a judgement about what this shows, for example that how a society remembers its past reflects who holds power and what that society values.
