A stern face with a bushy moustache stares out of a poster, finger pointing straight at you: Britons, Lord Kitchener wants you. Join your country's army. In 1914 hundreds of thousands of young men answered the call, queuing outside recruiting offices in cities and villages. By 1916 volunteers were no longer enough, and the British government passed a law forcing men to serve. Some refused, saying that their conscience would not let them kill. Today we study how governments raised armies and won support during the First World War, through volunteering, conscription and propaganda, and how conscientious objectors were treated.
