Picture a single farmhouse in the Karoo, with a windmill, a dam and sheep spread across kilometres of veld. Now picture a cluster of a few homesteads on a hillside in the Eastern Cape, and then a village on the West Coast, with a harbour, fishing boats, a church, a school and a small shop. All three are rural settlements, places where people live in the countryside and most work is connected to the land or the sea. Today we study the kinds of rural settlement, from the isolated farmstead to the hamlet and the village, the reasons they are where they are, and the economic activities that support them: farming, mining, forestry and fishing. We also set up the settlement project you will complete later this term.
