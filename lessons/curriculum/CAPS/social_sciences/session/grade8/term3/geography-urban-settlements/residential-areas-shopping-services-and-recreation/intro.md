Drive across any South African city and the houses change around you. In one suburb, large houses stand on big plots behind high walls, with gardens, pools and quiet, tree-lined streets. In another, neat rows of small houses stand close together, with a shop on the corner. In another, small brick houses and shacks are packed tightly together, with narrow paths between them and a communal tap at the end of the row. These are residential zones, the places where people live, and they take up more land than any other use in a city. Today we study high-, middle- and low-income residential areas, and the shopping centres, services and recreation areas that serve them. We also look at why the pattern in South African cities is so unequal.
