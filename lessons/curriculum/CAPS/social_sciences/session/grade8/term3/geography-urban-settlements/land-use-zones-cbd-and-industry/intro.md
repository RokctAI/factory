Stand on the top of the Carlton Centre in Johannesburg, or on Signal Hill above Cape Town, and look down at the city. In the middle, tall office towers crowd together. Further out, there are flat-roofed warehouses and workshops beside the roads and railways, and further still, factories with chimneys, tanks and railway sidings. Each part of the city is used for something different. Geographers call these parts land use zones. Today we study what urban settlements are and how their land is divided into zones, focusing on the central business district and on light and heavy industry. We also learn why each zone is where it is, and how to recognise each one on a map.
