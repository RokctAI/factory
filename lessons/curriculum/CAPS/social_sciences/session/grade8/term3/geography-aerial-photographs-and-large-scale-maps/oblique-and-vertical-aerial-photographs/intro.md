Imagine you are in a small aircraft flying over Durban. If you look out of the side window at an angle, you see the beachfront hotels standing tall, their sides and roofs, the harbour in the distance and the horizon beyond. If a camera under the aircraft points straight down, you see only the tops of things: flat roof shapes, the lines of streets, the white edge of the surf, the green squares of parks, like a map without labels. These are the two kinds of aerial photograph, oblique and vertical. Today we study what each one looks like, how to read them, and how to identify natural and constructed features on them. We also calculate the scale of a vertical photograph.
