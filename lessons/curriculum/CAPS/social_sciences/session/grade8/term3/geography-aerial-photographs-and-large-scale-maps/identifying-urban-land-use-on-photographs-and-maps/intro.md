In a Geography examination, you may be handed a topographic map and an orthophoto map of the same town and asked: Where is the central business district? Where is the heavy industry? Which residential area is high-income, and how do you know? To answer, you need to combine everything you have learned this term: the land use zones of a city, the clues for reading aerial photographs, and the symbols and scale of maps. Today we practise identifying urban land uses on vertical aerial photographs and on large-scale maps, and we learn how to give evidence for every answer. We also measure distances and areas on maps at one to ten thousand and one to fifty thousand.
