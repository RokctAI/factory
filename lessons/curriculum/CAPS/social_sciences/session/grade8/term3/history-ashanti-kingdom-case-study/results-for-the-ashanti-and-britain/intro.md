In 1879, a blacksmith named Tetteh Quarshie returned to the Gold Coast from the island of Fernando Po with a few cocoa beans and planted them at Mampong in the hills north of Accra. Within about thirty years, African farmers, many of them in the forests of Asante, had made the Gold Coast the largest producer of cocoa in the world. At the same time, a British company was digging gold from deep mines at Obuasi, and a railway carried gold and cocoa from Kumasi to the sea. Today we complete the Asante case study by examining the results of colonisation for the Asante kingdom and for Britain: political, economic and social, and the road from colony to the independent nation of Ghana in 1957.
