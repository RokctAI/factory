According to Asante tradition, around the year 1700, at a great gathering of chiefs, the priest Okomfo Anokye called down from the sky a stool covered in gold, which came to rest on the knees of Osei Tutu. This Golden Stool, the Sika Dwa Kofi, was said to contain the soul of the Asante nation. It united the Asante chiefdoms into one kingdom, with Osei Tutu as its first king, the Asantehene, and Kumasi as its capital. For two centuries, the Asante kingdom, also written Ashanti, was one of the most powerful states in West Africa. Today we begin a case study of the Asante: how the kingdom was built, how it was governed, and how it traded and dealt with the Europeans who arrived on the Gold Coast.
