When a city grows by thousands of people every month, everything it provides comes under pressure. A school built for eight hundred learners finds itself with fourteen hundred. A clinic that once saw sixty patients a day sees two hundred, with a queue that starts before sunrise. New shacks appear on the edge of the settlement every week, and a single tap may serve dozens of families. Today we study the social issues caused by rapid urban growth in South Africa and Africa: housing, the provision of services such as water, sanitation and electricity, and access to health care and education. We also look at the ways governments and communities are trying to respond.
