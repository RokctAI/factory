In the 1980s, a photographer followed workers from KwaNdebele, a homeland north-east of Pretoria. They woke before three in the morning, walked to the bus stop in the dark, and rode for hours to their jobs in the city, then rode for hours back again late at night. They were not allowed to live near their work. Their journey was the result of laws that tried to control where black South Africans could live and how many could stay in the cities. Today we study how South Africa urbanised, from the mining towns of the nineteenth century to the cities of today, and how segregation and apartheid laws tried to hold back and redirect African urbanisation, and what happened when those laws were removed.
