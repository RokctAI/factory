On 2 September 1898, at Omdurman in Sudan, a British-led army faced the forces of the Mahdist state. The Sudanese soldiers were brave and numerous, but the British had Maxim machine guns and artillery. In a few hours, about ten thousand Sudanese soldiers were killed, and the British-led force lost fewer than fifty men. Two years earlier, at Adwa in Ethiopia, an Italian army had been crushed by the forces of Emperor Menelik II, who had bought modern rifles and artillery and united his kingdom. Today we study the patterns of colonisation, how Europeans moved from the coasts into the interior, and why European powers were able to colonise most of Africa so quickly, but not all of it.
