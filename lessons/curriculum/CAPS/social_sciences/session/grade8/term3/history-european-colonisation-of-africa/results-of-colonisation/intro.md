Look at a modern map of Africa and you will see many borders drawn as straight lines across deserts and forests, lines that no African people chose. Listen to the news from Dakar, Lagos, Luanda or Nairobi and you will hear French, English and Portuguese alongside African languages. Follow the railways of many African countries and you will find that they run from mines and plantations to the coast, not between neighbouring countries. These are some of the results of colonisation, and they still shape Africa today. In this lesson we study the political, economic and social results of colonisation, for Africa and for Europe, and we practise weighing arguments about whether colonisation brought any benefits, and at what cost.
