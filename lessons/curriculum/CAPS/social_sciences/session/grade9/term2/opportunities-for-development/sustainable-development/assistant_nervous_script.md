Hey — sustainable development sounds like a big phrase, but it fits in three pictures: a savings account, a three-strand rope, and a city that nearly ran dry.

A savings account. The 1987 Brundtland Report defined sustainable development as meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Live on nature's interest, not its savings. Humanity uses nature as if we had about 1,7 Earths.

A rope. Three strands: economic, social and environmental. If one snaps, the rope is weak. In 2015 the world adopted 17 Sustainable Development Goals for 2030, for all countries. South Africa's Constitution, Section 24, protects the environment for present and future generations.

A city. In 2018 Cape Town faced Day Zero. Residents were limited to 50 litres a day, so a family of four had 200 litres. Water use roughly halved, and the taps stayed on. South Africa also aims for a just transition from coal to sun and wind.
