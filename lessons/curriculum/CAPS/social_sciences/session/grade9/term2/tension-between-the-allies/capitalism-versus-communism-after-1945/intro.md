In May 1945 American and Soviet soldiers shook hands on the Elbe River in Germany, allies who had defeated Hitler together. Within two years they were rivals in a conflict that would shape the world for more than four decades. This lesson explains why. We compare the two opposing systems, capitalism in the United States and the West and communism in the Soviet Union, and trace the growing mistrust from the wartime conferences at Yalta and Potsdam, through Soviet control of Eastern Europe and Churchill's Iron Curtain speech of 1946, to the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan of 1947. This lesson leads directly into the Cold War and to Grade 12 History, which begins with the origins of the Cold War.
