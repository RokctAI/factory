Hey — this is how friends became rivals, and it fits in three pictures: two ways to share a harvest, partners who did not trust each other, and a curtain across Europe.

Two ways. Capitalism means private ownership, free markets and, in the United States, democracy. Communism, based on Karl Marx's ideas, meant state ownership and central planning in the Soviet Union, with a one-party state under Stalin.

Partners. They became allies in 1941 to defeat Hitler, but old mistrust remained. At Yalta in February 1945 and Potsdam in July 1945, they agreed to divide Germany into zones but argued about Poland and reparations. Truman now had the atomic bomb.

A curtain. The Soviet Union lost about 27 million people and wanted a buffer of friendly states. Communist governments took over Eastern Europe. On 5 March 1946 Churchill said an iron curtain had descended across Europe. America chose containment: the Truman Doctrine in March 1947 and the Marshall Plan, about 13 billion dollars to rebuild Western Europe.
