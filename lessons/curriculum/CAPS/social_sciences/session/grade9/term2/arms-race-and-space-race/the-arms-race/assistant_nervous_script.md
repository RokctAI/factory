Hey — the arms race fits in three pictures: bigger and bigger dogs, children under desks, and thirteen days in October.

Bigger dogs. Two rivals keep buying bigger weapons because each fears the other. The Soviet Union tested an atomic bomb in 1949. America tested a hydrogen bomb in 1952, about 700 times the Hiroshima bomb. The Soviet Tsar Bomba of 1961 was more than 3 000 times as powerful. Bombers, then intercontinental missiles from 1957, then submarines. By about 1986 there were about 70 000 nuclear weapons.

Under the desk. Deterrence meant having enough weapons to strike back even after an attack. Children practised duck and cover. The race cost a fortune, and atmospheric tests spread radioactive fallout.

Thirteen days. In October 1962, Soviet missiles were found in Cuba. Kennedy blockaded the island, and on 28 October Khrushchev agreed to remove them. Then came treaties: the 1963 test ban, the 1968 Non-Proliferation Treaty and the 1987 INF Treaty.
