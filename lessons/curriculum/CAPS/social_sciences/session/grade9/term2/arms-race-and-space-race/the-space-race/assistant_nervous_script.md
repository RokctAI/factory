Hey — the space race fits in three pictures: a beeping ball in the sky, a race of firsts, and footprints in grey dust.

A beeping ball. On 4 October 1957 the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first satellite. It went round the Earth every 96 minutes, about 15 times a day. Americans were shocked, because a rocket that reaches space can also carry a nuclear bomb. America launched Explorer 1 in 1958 and created NASA.

A race of firsts. Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space on 12 April 1961. Valentina Tereshkova was the first woman in space in 1963, and Alexei Leonov made the first spacewalk in 1965. In 1961 Kennedy set the goal of landing on the Moon before 1970.

Footprints. On 20 July 1969, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed on the Moon in Apollo 11. In 1975 American and Soviet crews shook hands in orbit. South Africa's Hartebeesthoek station helped track spacecraft.
