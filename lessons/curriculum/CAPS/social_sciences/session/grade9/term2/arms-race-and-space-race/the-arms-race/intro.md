In 1945 one country had atomic bombs. By the mid-1980s the world held about 70 000 nuclear weapons, many of them thousands of times more powerful than the bomb that destroyed Hiroshima. This lesson explains the nuclear arms race, the competition between the United States and the Soviet Union to build more, bigger and faster weapons. We trace its stages, from the Soviet atomic bomb of 1949 and the hydrogen bomb to intercontinental missiles and submarines, see how close the world came to nuclear war during the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962, and follow the treaties that slowed and then reversed the race. We also ask what it cost. Grade 12 History studies Cold War crises in depth.
