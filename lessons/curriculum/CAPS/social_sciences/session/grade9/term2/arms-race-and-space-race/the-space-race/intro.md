On 4 October 1957 a small metal ball about the size of a beach ball, beeping a simple radio signal, circled the Earth every 96 minutes. It was Sputnik, the first artificial satellite, launched by the Soviet Union, and it started the space race. This lesson explains how the two superpowers competed to achieve firsts in space, and why it mattered so much: rockets that could reach orbit could also carry nuclear weapons, and every success was propaganda for capitalism or communism. We follow the Soviet lead with Yuri Gagarin, the American response with NASA and President Kennedy's Moon challenge, the Apollo 11 landing of 1969, and the cooperation that followed. We also discover South Africa's role in tracking spacecraft.
