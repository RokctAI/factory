Hey — this topic is about an idea, and it fits in three pictures: a family's life getting better, a three-legged pot, and a map with a wavy line.

A family. Development is the process by which people's quality of life improves: health, education, safety, a decent home, clean water, enough food and the freedom to choose. Standard of living is narrower: income and possessions. Development can also go backwards, through war, drought or disease.

A pot. Development has three legs. Economic: money, jobs, roads, electricity. Social: health, education, housing, rights. Environmental: clean air, water and soil, and resources left for the future. If one leg breaks, the pot falls over.

A map. Countries are often called developed or developing. The North-South line drawn in the 1980s is about wealth, not hemispheres. South Africa has two faces: rich suburbs beside informal settlements, partly because of colonialism and apartheid.

Remember: an average can hide big differences, so always ask who is better off and who is left behind. Open the practice set when you are ready. You've got this!
