This lesson opens the Grade 9 Geography study of development issues by asking a deceptively simple question: what does it mean for a country, a town or a household to develop? We separate economic development, which is about income, jobs and production, from social development, which is about health, education, housing and quality of life, and environmental development, which is about using resources without destroying them. We compare the terms developed and developing countries, and the newer language of the Global North and Global South, and look at why South Africa contains both high and low levels of development side by side. These ideas prepare you for measuring development in the next lesson and for Grade 11 Geography's study of development.
