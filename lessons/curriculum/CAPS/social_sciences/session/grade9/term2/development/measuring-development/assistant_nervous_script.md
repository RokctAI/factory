Hey — measuring development sounds technical, but it fits in three pictures: a report card, a cake, and the things a calculator cannot count.

A report card. Indicators are measurable facts about development: money, health, education, services. Like a school report, one good mark does not mean everything is fine, so we always use several.

A cake. GDP is the total value of everything a country produces in a year. Divide it by the population to get GDP per capita: 380 billion dollars shared by 62 million people gives about 6 100 dollars each. But slices are not equal. The Gini coefficient measures inequality; South Africa's is about 0,63, among the highest in the world.

What a calculator cannot count. Life expectancy shows how long people live. Infant mortality counts baby deaths per 1 000 births: 1 200 out of 48 000 is 25 per 1 000. Lower is better. Literacy shows how many adults can read. But averages hide differences, and unpaid care work is left out.
