Hey — the HDI looks like maths, but it fits in three pictures: a three-part test, a recipe for one score, and a league table.

A test. The Human Development Index was created in 1990 by Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen for the United Nations. It asks three questions: do people live long and healthy lives (life expectancy), do they gain knowledge (years of schooling), and do they have a decent standard of living (income per person, adjusted for prices)?

A recipe. Each answer becomes a score from 0 to 1. For life expectancy of 65 years: 65 minus 20 is 45, divided by 85 minus 20, which is 65, gives about 0,69. The three scores are multiplied and cube-rooted, so one weak area pulls the total down.

A league table. Very high is 0,800 and above; high is 0,700 to 0,799; medium 0,550 to 0,699; low below 0,550. South Africa scores about 0,72, which is high, but the score drops sharply when adjusted for inequality.
