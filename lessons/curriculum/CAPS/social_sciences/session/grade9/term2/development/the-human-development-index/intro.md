The Human Development Index, or HDI, is the world's best-known measure of development, published every year by the United Nations Development Programme. Instead of ranking countries only by income, it combines three things that matter to people: a long and healthy life, knowledge, and a decent standard of living. In this lesson we see why the HDI was created in 1990, how its three dimensions are measured, and how the index is calculated step by step, using a worked example close to South Africa's own figures. We read HDI tables and maps, compare South Africa with other countries, and examine what the HDI leaves out, including inequality. These skills are used again in Grade 11 Geography's development studies.
