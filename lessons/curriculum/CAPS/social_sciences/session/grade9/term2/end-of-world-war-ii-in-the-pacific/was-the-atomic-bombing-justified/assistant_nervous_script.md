Hey — this lesson is a debate, and it fits in three pictures: a set of scales, scientists who said wait, and you as the judge.

The scales. For the bombings: Japan's generals refused to surrender, Okinawa showed how bloody an invasion would be, and leaders believed the bomb would end the war quickly and save lives, including prisoners of war. Against: the bombs deliberately hit cities full of civilians, Japan was already badly beaten, and some Japanese leaders were seeking peace.

Voices that said wait. The Franck Report of June 1945 asked for a demonstration on an empty area first. Leo Szilard's petition asked the president not to use the bomb without warning. Some officials suggested promising that the emperor could stay. Nagasaki was bombed only three days after Hiroshima. Some historians think impressing the Soviet Union was also a motive.

You are the judge. Historians agree on the facts but disagree on motives and morality. Stimson's 1947 claim of a million casualties was written to defend the decision. Choose a line of argument, support it with evidence, and show the other side.
