Was the United States right to drop atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki? For eighty years this has been one of the most debated questions in history. In this lesson you become the historian. We examine why President Truman and his advisers decided to use the bomb, and the arguments that it saved lives by ending the war without an invasion. We then examine the counter-arguments: that Japan was close to surrender, that alternatives existed, that civilians were deliberately targeted, and that the bomb was also aimed at impressing the Soviet Union. We work with primary sources such as the Franck Report and Stimson's 1947 article, and learn to build a balanced argument for the 20-mark History essay.
