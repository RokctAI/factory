On the evening of 25 December 1991, the red flag with its hammer and sickle was lowered over the Kremlin in Moscow for the last time. A country that had been a superpower for almost half a century, covering about one sixth of the world's land, simply stopped existing. This lesson explains, briefly and clearly, why the Soviet Union collapsed. We look at the fifteen republics that made up the Union and the nationalist movements that grew inside them, the failed economic reforms, the dramatic attempted coup of August 1991, and the final break-up in December 1991. We also consider what the collapse meant for the world, for Russia and its neighbours, and for South Africa's own move towards democracy.
