On the night of 9 November 1989, crowds of East Berliners walked through the checkpoints of the Berlin Wall, and strangers from East and West hugged, cried and climbed on the concrete together. This lesson explains how a confrontation that had shaped the world for more than forty years came to an end. We look at why the Soviet Union could no longer afford the Cold War, how Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms of glasnost and perestroika changed the relationship with the United States, how peaceful revolutions swept through Eastern Europe in 1989, and how a confused announcement at a press conference opened the Berlin Wall. We also see how the end of the Cold War helped open the way to negotiations in South Africa.
