The previous lesson looked at history, trade and technology. This lesson turns to three human factors that help explain why development differs between countries: health and welfare, education, and political stability. We see how diseases such as malaria, HIV and tuberculosis reduce people's ability to work and learn, and how social grants and clinics protect households. We examine why education, especially for girls, is one of the most powerful drivers of development, and why the quality of learning matters as much as the number of years in school. Finally we compare countries such as Botswana and Rwanda with countries trapped in conflict, and ask why peace and good governance matter. Grade 11 Geography develops these ideas further.
