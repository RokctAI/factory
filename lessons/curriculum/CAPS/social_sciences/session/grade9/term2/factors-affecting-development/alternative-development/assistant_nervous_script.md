Hey — this lesson is about different roads to development, and it fits in three pictures: two ways to build a village, a small loan, and land kept wild.

Two ways. Top-down development is planned by governments or big companies, like the Katse Dam in Lesotho. Bottom-up development is planned by communities, like a village borehole. Both matter, and projects work best when people have a say. Factories are not the only road.

A small loan. Muhammad Yunus started the Grameen Bank in Bangladesh, lending small amounts to poor women; he won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2006. In South Africa, stokvels help people save together. Appropriate technology means affordable tools that fit local needs: treadle pumps, solar lamps, the Hippo Water Roller.

Land kept wild. The Makuleke community won back land in the Kruger Park in 1998 and earns money from tourism. M-Pesa in Kenya shows leapfrogging. Working for Water clears alien trees and creates jobs.

Remember: more than one road leads to development. Open the practice set when you are ready. You've got this!
