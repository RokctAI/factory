Hey — this lesson answers a big question, why some countries are richer, and it fits in three pictures: an unfair start, beans that become chocolate elsewhere, and a machine that does the work of a hundred hands.

An unfair start. At the Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885, European powers divided Africa without Africans present. By 1914 only Ethiopia and Liberia were free. Colonies exported raw materials, railways ran from mines to ports, and few schools were built.

Beans and chocolate. Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire grow about 60 per cent of the world's cocoa, but most chocolate profit is made elsewhere. If a 60 000-dollar tractor costs 20 tonnes of coffee at 3 000 dollars a tonne, it costs 30 tonnes when coffee falls to 2 000. That is worsening terms of trade.

A machine. The Industrial Revolution began in Britain around the 1760s. Early industrialisers became rich. South Africa's path began with diamonds in 1867 and gold in 1886. Today's gap is also digital.
