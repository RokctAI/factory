Hey — if all the dates on the road to war felt like a blur, here is the calm version: three aims, a fence, a crocodile, two teams.

Three aims: tear up Versailles, bring all German speakers into one Germany, take living space in the east.

A fence. Hitler pushed and watched. 1933: left the League of Nations. 1935: a big army and an air force. March 1936: troops into the Rhineland. Nobody stopped him.

A crocodile. March 1938: Austria joined to Germany. September 1938: the Munich Agreement gave Hitler the Sudetenland; Czechoslovakia was not invited. Giving in to avoid war is appeasement, chosen because of memories of World War I, unready armies and a sense that Versailles was unfair. March 1939: Hitler took the rest of Czechoslovakia, and Britain and France promised to defend Poland.

Two teams. 23 August 1939: the Nazi-Soviet Pact, with a secret deal to split Poland. 1 September 1939: Germany invades Poland. 3 September: Britain and France declare war. Axis: Germany, Italy, Japan. Allies: Britain, France, the Commonwealth, later the Soviet Union and the United States. South Africa joined after a vote of 80 to 67.
