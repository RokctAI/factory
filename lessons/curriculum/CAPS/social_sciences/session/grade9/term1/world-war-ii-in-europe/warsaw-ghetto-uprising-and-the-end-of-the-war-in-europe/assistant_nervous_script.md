Hey — big story, three short scenes: a wall, a turning tide, a final day.

A wall. In 1940 the Germans locked more than 400 000 Jews of Warsaw behind a wall. Tens of thousands starved. In 1942 about 265 000 were sent by train to be murdered at Treblinka. On 19 April 1943, young fighters led by Mordechaj Anielewicz, aged 23, rose up with pistols and petrol bombs. The Germans burned the ghetto down, but the fighters held out for almost four weeks. They could not win; they chose to resist.

A turning tide. The Germans surrendered at Stalingrad in February 1943. South Africans helped win at El Alamein in 1942 and fought through Italy. About 334 000 South Africans volunteered. On D-Day, 6 June 1944, the Allies landed in France.

A final day. Hitler killed himself on 30 April 1945. Germany surrendered, and 8 May 1945 became VE Day. Between 70 and 85 million people died in the war.

Do not mix up the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of 1943 with the Polish Warsaw Uprising of 1944. Open the practice set when you are ready. You've got this!
