Hey — this was the hardest lesson of the year, and it is okay to feel heavy after it. Here is the calm, careful version: walls, a meeting, a diary.

Walls. The Holocaust, or Shoah, was the murder of about six million Jewish people by Nazi Germany and its helpers. It began with persecution and laws. After 1939, Jews in Poland were forced into ghettos, walled-off areas with little food, and made to wear a yellow star. From 1941, killing squads shot Jewish families in the Soviet Union; at Babi Yar more than 33 000 in two days. Destroying a whole group is called genocide.

A meeting. On 20 January 1942, at Wannsee in Berlin, Nazi officials organised the Final Solution, the plan to murder all Europe's Jews. Trains carried families to death camps in Poland: Treblinka, Sobibór, Auschwitz-Birkenau and others. About 1,1 million were murdered at Auschwitz. Roma and Sinti, Soviet prisoners, Poles and disabled people were murdered too.

A diary. Anne Frank hid for two years and died aged fifteen. Rescuers like Irena Sendler saved thousands. Auschwitz was liberated on 27 January 1945. Nazi leaders were tried at Nuremberg.
