Hey — if Hitler's rise felt like a lot of names and numbers, here is the calm version: a speaker, a failed putsch, a crash.

A speaker. Hitler was born in Austria in 1889, failed as an artist, fought for Germany in World War I and believed the stab-in-the-back lie. In Munich he joined a tiny party and turned out to be a gifted, angry speaker. By 1921 he led it: the Nazi Party, with the swastika, the brownshirt SA, and a programme against Versailles and against Jewish people.

A failed putsch. In November 1923 he tried to seize power in Munich. Police fired, it collapsed, and he went to prison for about nine months, where he wrote Mein Kampf: false race ideas, hatred of Jews, living space in the east, one all-powerful leader. Lesson learned: win votes, then destroy democracy from inside. In 1928, only 2,6 percent voted Nazi.

A crash. October 1929, Wall Street crashes. America recalls its loans. By early 1932 about 6 million Germans are unemployed. Brüning rules by emergency decree. In 1930 the Nazi vote leaps to 18,3 percent.

Open the practice set and put these in order: 1923, 1928, 1929, 1930. You've got this!
