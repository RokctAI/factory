In 1928 the Nazi Party won less than 3 percent of the vote in Germany. Four years later it was the largest party in the Reichstag. Today we trace the first half of that rise. We meet Adolf Hitler, the Austrian-born former soldier who took over a tiny Munich party in 1921, and the Nazi programme of nationalism, hatred of the Treaty of Versailles and antisemitism. We follow the failed Beer Hall Putsch of 1923, the prison book Mein Kampf, and the decision to win power through elections. Then the Wall Street Crash of October 1929 sets off the Great Depression, American loans are recalled, and German unemployment climbs towards six million. We see why desperate voters turned to the extremes. In Grade 11 History, the Nazi case study begins from exactly this point.
