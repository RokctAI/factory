Persecution in Nazi Germany did not begin with gas chambers. It began with laws, signs, boycotts and the quiet removal of rights, one step at a time. Today we follow the persecution of Jewish people in Germany from the boycott of April 1933 to the Nuremberg Laws of September 1935, which took away their citizenship and banned marriage between Jews and other Germans, and on to the violence of Kristallnacht in November 1938. We see how other groups were also targeted: Roma and Sinti, people with disabilities, gay men, Black Germans, Jehovah's Witnesses and political opponents. We also look at the refugees, and at South Africa's own decision to close its door in 1937. Grade 11 History returns to these events in its case study of Nazi Germany and the Holocaust.
