Hey — if treaties and inflation felt heavy today, here is the calm version: a new team, LAMB, and a wheelbarrow.

A new team. In November 1918 the Kaiser ran away and Germany became a democracy, the Weimar Republic, named after the town where it began. Everyone over 20 could vote. Lots of small parties meant shaky coalition governments, and Article 48 let the president rule alone in an emergency. Communists and nationalists both tried to overthrow it.

LAMB. The Treaty of Versailles, signed 28 June 1919, was given to Germany to sign. Land: about 13 percent of its European land and all its colonies, including South West Africa, which South Africa then ran. Army: only 100 000 men, no tanks, no air force, no soldiers in the Rhineland. Money: 132 billion gold marks in reparations. Blame: Article 231, the War Guilt Clause.

Germans were furious and many believed the stab-in-the-back lie, that politicians and Jews had betrayed the army. The generals had actually admitted defeat.

A wheelbarrow. In 1923 the government printed money and prices exploded: bread cost about 200 billion marks. Stresemann fixed the currency with American loans.
