What kind of state was Nazi Germany, and how did it differ from the democracy South Africans live in today? Today we define fascism, the political system first built by Mussolini in Italy in 1922, and list its features: extreme nationalism, one leader who cannot be questioned, one party, terror, propaganda, control of young people and a drive to war. We see each feature at work in Nazi Germany, through the SS and Gestapo, Goebbels's propaganda ministry, the Hitler Youth and the economy of rearmament. Then we set fascism side by side with democracy: free elections, many parties, the rule of law, independent courts, a free press and protected human rights, using South Africa's Constitution of 1996 as our example. Grade 11 History and Life Orientation both return to this comparison.
