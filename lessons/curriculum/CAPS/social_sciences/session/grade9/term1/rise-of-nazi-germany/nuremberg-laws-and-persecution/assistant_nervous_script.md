Hey — if this lesson felt heavy, it should; here is the calm version to hold it steady: small steps, a law, the triangles.

Small steps. In 1933 Jewish people were less than one percent of Germany. The Nazis spread the lie that they were a dangerous race. April 1933: a boycott of Jewish shops. A week later: Jewish government workers sacked. Then limits on Jewish students, doctors and lawyers, and books burned.

A law. The Nuremberg Laws, 15 September 1935. Law one: only people of so-called German blood could be citizens, so Jewish Germans lost citizenship and the vote. Law two: no marriage or relationships between Jews and other Germans. Who counted as Jewish was decided by grandparents' religion, proof the race idea was false. November 1938, Kristallnacht: synagogues burned, about 7 500 shops smashed, about 30 000 men sent to camps. Many tried to flee, but most countries, South Africa included, restricted entry.

The triangles. Others were persecuted too: Roma and Sinti, disabled people, gay men, Black Germans, Jehovah's Witnesses and political opponents.
