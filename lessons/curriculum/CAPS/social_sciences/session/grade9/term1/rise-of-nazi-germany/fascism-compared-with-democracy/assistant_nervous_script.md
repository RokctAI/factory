Hey — if fascism and democracy felt like big abstract words today, here is the calm version: one captain, fear and a loudspeaker, a rule book.

One captain. Fascism began with Mussolini in Italy in 1922. Its pattern: the nation above everything, one leader who cannot be questioned, one party, opponents locked up, all media controlled, war celebrated, communism and democracy hated. Nazi Germany was Germany's version, with racism at its centre.

Fear and a loudspeaker. Fear came from the SS and the Gestapo, who could arrest anyone without trial, helped by neighbours reporting each other. The loudspeaker was Goebbels's propaganda: newspapers, radio, films and giant rallies. Boys joined the Hitler Youth and girls the League of German Girls, compulsory by 1939. Women were told: children, kitchen, church. Unemployment fell through roads and rearmament.

A rule book. Democracy is rule by the people: free elections, many parties, power shared, courts that can stop the government, a free press and rights for everyone. South Africa's Constitution of 1996 and its Bill of Rights are the rule book, and the Constitutional Court is the referee.

Open the practice set and compare the two side by side. You've got this!
