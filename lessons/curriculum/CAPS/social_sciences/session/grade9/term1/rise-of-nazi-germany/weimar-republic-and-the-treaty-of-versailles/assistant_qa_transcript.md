### subtopic_1 — Germany in Defeat and the Birth of the Weimar Republic
**Assistant:** Why was Germany not allowed to take part in the peace talks?

**Tutor:** Because the victorious Allies regarded Germany as the defeated aggressor and wanted to settle the peace among themselves before presenting it, and because the three main powers could barely agree with each other, let alone with Germany. The peace conference was a conference of the victors: thirty-two countries took part, but the key decisions were made by the leaders of the United States, Britain and France, with Italy at first in the room. Their aims clashed. Clemenceau wanted security for France through a weak Germany; Wilson wanted a just peace based on self-determination and a League of Nations; Lloyd George was caught between a British public that had been promised Germany would pay and his own belief that a ruined Germany would be bad for trade and might turn communist. The treaty was a compromise between these three, hammered out over five months. Allowing Germany to negotiate would have reopened every compromise. The German delegation was given the draft in May 1919, allowed to submit written objections, which led to only minor changes, and then given an ultimatum. The German government briefly resigned rather than sign, but the army told it that resistance was impossible, and the National Assembly voted to accept. Being excluded is why Germans called it a Diktat, and it made the treaty easier for nationalists to reject as something imposed by enemies rather than agreed. For the essay skills of Grade 10 History, this is a good example of how the way a decision is made can matter as much as what is decided.

### subtopic_2 — The Treaty of Versailles: Land, Army, Money and Blame
**Assistant:** How did South Africa end up running South West Africa after the war?

**Tutor:** Through the mandate system of the League of Nations, which Jan Smuts helped to design, and which handed Germany's former colonies to the victors to govern on the League's behalf. South African forces had conquered German South West Africa in 1915. At the peace conference the victorious powers did not want to annex Germany's colonies openly, which would have contradicted Wilson's ideals, so the colonies became mandates: territories governed by a member of the League, which was supposed to promote the wellbeing of the inhabitants and report to the League each year. South West Africa was classed as a C mandate, the category for territories regarded as suitable to be administered as part of the mandatory power's own territory, and it was given to the Union of South Africa in 1920. In practice South Africa ran it almost as a fifth province, settled white farmers there and applied segregation, and later apartheid, laws to its people. Earlier, between 1904 and 1908, the German colonial army had carried out a genocide against the Herero and Nama peoples, killing tens of thousands, which is often described as the first genocide of the twentieth century. After 1945 South Africa refused to place the territory under the United Nations, and the dispute lasted decades, ending only with Namibia's independence on 21 March 1990 after a long liberation war led by SWAPO. So a clause of the Treaty of Versailles shaped southern African history for seventy years.

### subtopic_3 — German Reactions and the Weaknesses of the Republic
**Assistant:** Was the stab-in-the-back myth completely false?

**Tutor:** Yes, as an explanation of Germany's defeat it was false, although it drew on real events that made it easy to believe. The decisive evidence is that on 29 September 1918 the German army's supreme command, led by Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff, told the Kaiser and the government that the war was lost and that an armistice must be sought immediately. The army had been pushed back since August 1918, it was outnumbered by the arrival of American troops, its allies were collapsing, and its reserves were exhausted. Ludendorff then arranged for a new civilian government to make the request for peace, so that the politicians, not the generals, would be seen to have surrendered. The myth used real elements: there were strikes in Germany in 1918, sailors did mutiny at Kiel in November, and revolution did break out, but these came after the army had already admitted defeat and were partly caused by the defeat. The myth blamed socialists, democrats and, falsely and dangerously, Jews, even though about 100 000 German Jews had served in the army and about 12 000 had been killed. Hindenburg repeated the myth before a parliamentary inquiry in 1919, which gave it authority. It mattered because it allowed nationalists to argue that the republic itself was born of treachery, and the Nazis made it central to their propaganda. Evaluating a claim against evidence like this is one of the core skills of source-based questions in Grade 10 and Grade 11 History.

### subtopic_4 — The Crisis of 1923, Recovery and the Error Museum
**Assistant:** How can printing money make prices rise so fast?

**Tutor:** Because the value of money depends on how many goods it can buy, and if the amount of money grows much faster than the amount of goods, each note is worth less and prices rise. In 1923 the German government had little income: industry in the Ruhr had stopped because of passive resistance, and it still owed reparations. It covered its costs by printing money, more and faster, with printing works running day and night. More marks were chasing the same or fewer goods, so sellers raised prices. Once people expected prices to keep rising, they spent their money the moment they received it, and sellers raised prices even faster, a spiral that fed itself. By late 1923 prices were doubling every few days. The mark fell from about 4 to the dollar in 1914 to 4,2 trillion to the dollar in November 1923. Those with debts, including the government and some industrialists, gained because they could repay them in worthless marks, while those with savings, fixed pensions or fixed salaries lost almost everything. The middle classes, the backbone of support for a stable republic, felt robbed by it. Stresemann's new currency worked because its supply was strictly limited and it was backed by a mortgage on German land and industry, which restored confidence. Hyperinflation is studied in economics as well as history, and in Grade 10 Economic and Management Sciences and FET Economics you will meet inflation measured by the consumer price index.
