Hey — if the elections and laws of 1932 to 1934 felt tangled, here is the calm version: a scoreboard, a back door, an off switch.

A scoreboard. July 1932: Nazis 37,3 percent, the biggest party, but never a majority in a free election. November 1932: down to 33,1 percent. Their voters: farmers, shopkeepers, office workers, young people, and many scared of communism.

A back door. Conservatives around President Hindenburg thought they could use Hitler's voters and control him. On 30 January 1933 Hitler was made chancellor with only three Nazis in the cabinet. On 27 February the Reichstag burned; the next day an emergency decree cancelled basic rights, and thousands of opponents were arrested. March 1933 election: 43,9 percent, still not half.

An off switch. The Enabling Act, 23 March 1933, let Hitler make laws without parliament. Communists were excluded, brownshirts lined the walls, and it passed 444 to 94; only the Social Democrats voted no. Then trade unions were banned, all other parties banned by July 1933, the SA leaders murdered in June 1934, and in August 1934 Hitler became Führer.
