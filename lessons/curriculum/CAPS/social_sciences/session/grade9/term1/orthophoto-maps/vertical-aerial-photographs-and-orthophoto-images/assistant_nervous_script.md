Hey — if photographs and orthophotos blurred together today, here is the calm version: straight down, straightened out, seven questions.

Straight down. A vertical aerial photo is taken with the camera pointing straight down, like a drone above your street. You see roofs and treetops. A slanted photo is oblique: high oblique shows the horizon, low oblique does not. Measure only on straight-down photos.

Straightened out. A raw photo is slightly wobbly: hills look bigger and tall things lean. A computer corrects it so every part has the same scale. That is an orthophoto. With names, 5 metre contours and a grid printed on, it is the 1:10 000 orthophoto map: 1 centimetre is 100 metres.

Seven questions to identify anything: dark or light, smooth or rough, what shape, how big, what pattern, what shadow, what is next to it. Neat rows of houses: planned suburb. Tiny roofs packed close: informal settlement. Perfect green circles on a farm: centre-pivot irrigation. In South Africa, midday shadows point south.
