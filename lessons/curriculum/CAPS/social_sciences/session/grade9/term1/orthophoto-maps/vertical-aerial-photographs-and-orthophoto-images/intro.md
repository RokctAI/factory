In Grade 8 you met the aerial photograph, the earth photographed from the sky. Today we revise it and take it a step further, into the 1:10 000 orthophoto map. We review vertical and oblique photographs and how they are taken in overlapping strips, then learn why a raw vertical photograph cannot be measured like a map and how it is corrected into an orthophoto with a uniform scale. Then we read the image: tone, texture, shape, size, pattern, shadow and association, the seven clues that turn grey and green patches into fields, factories, informal settlements and centre-pivot irrigation circles. In the southern hemisphere, shadows even tell you which way is north. By the end you will identify land use on an orthophoto and confirm it on the topographic map. Grade 10 Geography uses these skills from its first week.
