A photograph from directly above flattens the land: hills and valleys look the same height. So how does an orthophoto map show height? Today we learn the answer: contour lines printed over the image at a 5 metre interval, together with spot heights and trigonometrical stations. We read the height of points on the orthophoto, use contour spacing to judge steep and gentle slopes, and recognise valleys, spurs, hills and ridges from contour patterns drawn over real fields and roads. Then we put the photograph and the contours together to identify features with confidence: a dam wall across a valley, a road cutting through a spur, eroded gullies on a steep slope. We finish with a gradient calculation at 1:10 000 and a comparison with the topographic map. Grade 10 Geography mapwork starts exactly here.
