Hey — if contours on top of a photograph felt busy today, here is the calm version: count in fives, read the V's, trust the bath.

Count in fives. A photo from straight above hides the hills, so the orthophoto prints contour lines over it every 5 metres of height. Find a labelled line and count up or down in fives. Every fourth line matches a 20 metre line on the topographic map. Contours run smoothly and never cross; fences turn sharp corners.

Read the V's. Close lines steep, far lines gentle. V's pointing upstream with a dark line of trees through them: a valley with a river. V's pointing downhill: a spur. Closed loops: a hill. Sharp V's on a bare, pale slope with no river: dongas.

Trust the bath. Still water is flat, so a dam's edge follows a contour, and the wall sits where the valley is narrow. A road where the contours bunch in on both sides is in a cutting.

Gradient on the orthophoto: 1 centimetre is 100 metres. Up 35, along 250, about 1:7.
