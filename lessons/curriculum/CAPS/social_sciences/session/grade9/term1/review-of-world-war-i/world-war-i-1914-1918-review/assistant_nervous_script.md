Hey — if a whole war in one lesson felt like a lot, here is the calm version: MAIN and a spark, a ditch that would not move, two South African stories.

MAIN and a spark. Four deep causes: militarism, building up armies; alliances, two groups promising to back each other; imperialism, grabbing colonies; nationalism, fierce national pride. The spark: on 28 June 1914 Gavrilo Princip shot Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo. Promises pulled everyone in within five weeks: Britain, France and Russia against Germany and Austria-Hungary.

A ditch that would not move. Trenches 700 kilometres long on the Western Front. Machine guns, barbed wire, poison gas, tanks. On the first day of the Somme in 1916, about 57 000 British casualties.

Two South African stories. Delville Wood, July 1916: about 3 000 South Africans held the wood, fewer than 800 answered the roll call. The SS Mendi, 21 February 1917: more than 600 men of the Native Labour Corps died when it sank. Black South Africans were not allowed guns.

The war ended at 11 o'clock on 11 November 1918. About 9 million soldiers dead, four empires gone.
