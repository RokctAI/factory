This lesson looks at the treatment of prisoners of war held by Japan during World War II, one of the harshest experiences of the whole conflict. After Japan's rapid victories in 1942, about 140 000 Allied soldiers and many more Asian labourers and civilians came under Japanese control. We examine why their treatment was so brutal, the Bataan Death March, camps such as Changi, the hell ships, and the Burma-Thailand Railway, built at the cost of tens of thousands of lives. We also meet a South African prisoner, Laurens van der Post, and ask how justice and reconciliation were pursued after 1945. This lesson connects to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in Term 3 and to Grade 11 History.
