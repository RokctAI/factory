This lesson examines a wartime injustice inside a democracy. After Pearl Harbour, fear and long-standing racism led the United States government to force about 120 000 people of Japanese ancestry, most of them American citizens, out of their homes on the West Coast and into guarded camps. We follow Executive Order 9066 of February 1942, life in camps such as Manzanar, the loyalty questionnaire, the Japanese American soldiers who fought for a country that imprisoned their families, and the court cases and protests that challenged the policy. We end with the official apology of 1988. This lesson connects to forced removals in South Africa in Term 3, and to Grade 11 History's study of ideas of race and of the United States.
