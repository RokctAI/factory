Hey — this is a hard topic, but you can hold it in three pictures: a rule about surrender, a long hungry march, a railway through the jungle.

A rule. Japanese soldiers were taught that surrender was shameful. Japan captured about 140 000 Western Allied soldiers in 1942 and had not ratified the Geneva Convention, so prisoners got very little food or medicine.

A march. On the Bataan Death March in the Philippines in 1942, about 76 000 prisoners walked about 100 kilometres in heat, and thousands died. At Changi in Singapore, prisoners grew food and held classes to survive. Hell ships carried prisoners by sea. About one in four Western prisoners held by Japan died, compared with about one in twenty-five held by Germany.

A railway. About 60 000 prisoners and more than 200 000 Asian labourers built the 415-kilometre Burma-Thailand Railway. About 12 000 to 13 000 prisoners and 80 000 to 100 000 Asian labourers died.
