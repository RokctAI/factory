Hey — this is a story about fairness, and it fits in three things: a notice on a pole, a room in a barracks, a letter of apology.

A notice. After Pearl Harbour, many Americans blamed people of Japanese ancestry, even those born in America. On 19 February 1942, President Roosevelt signed Executive Order 9066. Families had about a week to pack only what they could carry. Farms and shops were sold cheaply or lost.

A barracks. About 120 000 people, two thirds of them American citizens, were sent to ten camps behind barbed wire. A family shared one small room. In 1943 a loyalty questionnaire asked whether they would fight for America. Thousands of young men served in the 442nd unit, one of the most decorated in American history.

An apology. Fred Korematsu refused to leave; the Supreme Court ruled against him in 1944. No Japanese American was ever convicted of spying. In 1983 a commission blamed race prejudice, war hysteria and a failure of political leadership. In 1988 the government apologised and paid each survivor 20 000 dollars.
