How does a flat sheet of paper show a mountain? Today we learn the height clues on a topographic map: spot heights, trigonometrical stations, benchmarks and, above all, contour lines, the brown lines that join points of the same height above sea level. We learn the rules contour lines always obey, how to read the height of any point between them, and how their spacing shows whether a slope is steep or gentle. Then we put a number on steepness: gradient, calculated as the vertical interval divided by the horizontal equivalent and written in the exam format of one to x. By the end you will be able to say whether you could cycle up a hill before you ever see it. Gradient is used throughout Grade 10 Geography mapwork and in every Paper 2 calculation question.
