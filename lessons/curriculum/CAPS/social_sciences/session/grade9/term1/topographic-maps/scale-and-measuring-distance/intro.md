Every map is a shrunken copy of the earth, and the scale tells you exactly how much it was shrunk. Today we learn the three ways scale is written, as a word scale, a ratio scale and a line scale, how to convert between them, and why the line scale survives a photocopier when the ratio scale does not. Then we measure: straight-line distances with a ruler, curved distances along roads and rivers with the edge of a sheet of paper or a piece of string, and the conversion of map centimetres into real metres and kilometres in the exam layout. We finish by turning distance into travel time. By the end you will measure any distance on a 1:50 000 or 1:10 000 map with full working. In Grade 10 Geography, distance and area calculations are among the first mapwork skills tested.
