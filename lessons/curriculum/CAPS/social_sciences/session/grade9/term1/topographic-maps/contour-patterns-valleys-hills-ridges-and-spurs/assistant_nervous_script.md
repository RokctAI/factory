Hey — if all those brown squiggles looked the same today, here is the calm version: rings, sausages, a waist, and a V that points the right way.

Rings inside rings: a hill. The smallest ring is the top, often with a trig station. Bigger, higher, rings packed closer: a mountain. If the rings have little ticks pointing in and the numbers go down, it is a hollow, not a hill.

Long thin rings, like sausages: a ridge, long narrow high ground. Rain on each side runs into a different valley, so the ridge is a watershed.

A pinched waist between two tops: a saddle, or nek. The lowest crossing, so roads go over there.

The V. Both valleys and spurs make V's. Valleys point to the source, spurs point to the sea. A valley's V points uphill, with a river running through it. A spur's V points downhill, a finger of high ground. Check by reading the numbers: higher towards the point means valley; lower towards the point means spur.
