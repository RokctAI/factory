Hey — if the key with all those little symbols looked like a foreign alphabet, here is the calm version: three shapes, five colours, two piles, and clues read together.

Three shapes. Dots and small shapes for things at one place, like a house or a windmill. Lines for long things, like rivers, roads and railways. Patches for areas, like a dam, a forest or a field.

Five colours. Blue is water. Brown is the shape of the land. Green is trees. Red is main roads. Black is buildings, railways and writing.

Two piles. Nature's things: rivers, marshes, pans, cliffs, dongas, natural forest. A solid blue line flows all year; a broken blue line flows only after rain. People's things: roads, railways, buildings, furrows, windmills, fields, plantations, and the dam, because people built the wall.

Clues read together. Furrow plus fields beside a river means irrigation. Many windmills and few fields means stock farming in a dry area. Mine dump plus excavation means mining. Name the symbol, say where it is, say what it means.

Open the practice set and sort a few symbols into the two piles. You've got this!
