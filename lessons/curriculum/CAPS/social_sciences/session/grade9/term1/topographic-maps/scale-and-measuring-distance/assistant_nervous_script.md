Hey — if scale felt like a pile of zeros today, here is the calm version: one toy, three ways, two tools, one sum.

One toy. A map is the land shrunk like a toy car. 1:50 000 means 1 centimetre on the map is 50 000 centimetres on the ground, which is 500 metres. So 2 centimetres is 1 kilometre. On the orthophoto, 1:10 000, 1 centimetre is 100 metres.

Three ways to write a scale: in words, as a ratio, and as a little line ruler printed on the map. If the map has been photocopied smaller or bigger, trust the line.

Two tools. Ruler for straight distance, as the crow flies. String or the edge of a paper for curved distance along a road or river. Measure from the middle of one symbol to the middle of the other.

One sum. Real distance equals map distance times the scale. 6,3 centimetres times 50 000 is 315 000 centimetres, which is 3,15 kilometres. Then time equals distance divided by speed: 4,8 kilometres at 5 kilometres an hour is 0,96 hours, about 58 minutes.

Open the practice set and do one straight and one curved distance. You've got this!
