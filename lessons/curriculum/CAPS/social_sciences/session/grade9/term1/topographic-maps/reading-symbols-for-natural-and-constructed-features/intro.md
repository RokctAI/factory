A topographic map speaks in symbols, and today we learn its alphabet. Every 1:50 000 sheet carries a key in its margin that explains about a hundred conventional signs, drawn as points, lines and areas in five colours. We sort them into natural features, such as perennial and non-perennial rivers, marshes, pans and cliffs, and constructed features, such as roads, railways, power lines, dams, buildings and cultivated land. Then we go a step further than naming symbols: we read them together, the way a geographer does, to say what people do in an area, how they make a living and why they settled where they did. A farm with a furrow, a windmill and a dam tells a story. In Grade 10 Geography, map interpretation of land use and settlement builds directly on this lesson.
