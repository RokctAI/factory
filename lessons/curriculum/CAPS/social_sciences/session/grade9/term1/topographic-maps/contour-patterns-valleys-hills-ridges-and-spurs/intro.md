Contour lines do more than give heights: their patterns draw the landforms. Today we learn to recognise five of them on a topographic map. A hill is a set of closed, roughly circular contours rising to a summit; a mountain is the same pattern on a larger, higher and steeper scale. A ridge is a long, narrow stretch of high ground, shown by elongated closed contours. A river valley and a spur both make V-shaped contour patterns, and the whole skill is telling them apart: in a valley the V points uphill, towards higher ground, and in a spur the V points downhill. We will use rivers, labels and spot heights to decide, and add the saddle between two hills. In Grade 10 Geography these landform patterns are the starting point for cross-sections and landscape interpretation.
