Hey — if contours and gradient felt like a wall of brown lines and numbers, here is the calm version: slices, spacing, and up over along.

Slices. A contour line joins places of the same height above sea level. Cut a hill into flat slices every 20 metres and draw the edges: those are the contours. On the topographic map they are 20 metres apart in height; on the orthophoto, 5 metres. They never cross, and the smallest ring is the top. Spot heights, trig stations and benchmarks give exact heights.

Spacing. Close lines mean steep. Far apart means gentle. No lines means flat. Close at the bottom and far at the top is convex; far at the bottom and close at the top is concave.

Up over along. Gradient equals vertical interval over horizontal equivalent. Subtract the two heights for the up. Measure on the map and change to metres for the along: on the 1:50 000 map, centimetres times 500. Divide both by the up to get 1:x. A spot height of 1 420 to a trig station of 1 680, 4 centimetres apart: 260 over 2 000, which is 1:7,7. The smaller x, the steeper.
