Every point on earth has an address made of two numbers: its latitude and its longitude. Today we use that address on the 1:50 000 topographic map. We review the global grid from Grade 8, the equator and the prime meridian, degrees and minutes, and South Africa's place south of the equator and east of Greenwich. Then we learn the exam method for giving the exact position of a feature in degrees, minutes and seconds: read the degrees and minutes from the frame of the map, measure the point's distance into its minute block, and turn that fraction of a minute into seconds. We also work in reverse, finding the feature that stands at given co-ordinates. By the end you will write a full co-ordinate such as 25 degrees 45 minutes 24 seconds south. In Grade 10 Geography, co-ordinates appear in almost every mapwork question.
