Hey — if the first day of maps felt like a pile of new words and numbers, here is the calm version: two maps, two scales, one rule about zoom.

Geography this year is four topics: maps, development, rivers and erosion, and resources. Every test gives you maps, pictures or graphs and asks you to read them. Answer what is asked, show the working, give the unit.

Map one: the 1:50 000 topographic map. A drawing from above with symbols. One centimetre is 500 metres, so two centimetres is one kilometre. Brown contour lines every 20 metres show the hills. One sheet is about 25 by 28 kilometres, numbered from latitude and longitude, like 2628AB.

Map two: the 1:10 000 orthophoto map. A corrected aerial photograph, straight down, with contours every 5 metres, names and a grid printed on top. One centimetre is 100 metres. Twenty-five of them fit in one topographic sheet.

The zoom rule: the orthophoto is zoomed in, so it is the larger scale, smaller area, more detail. The topographic map is zoomed out: smaller scale, bigger area, less detail.

Open the practice set and say it once: two centimetres, one kilometre. You've got this!
