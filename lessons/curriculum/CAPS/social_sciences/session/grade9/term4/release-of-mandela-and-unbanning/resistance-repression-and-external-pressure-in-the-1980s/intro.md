In 1983, the government opened a new Parliament with three houses: one for white people, one for coloured people and one for Indian people. There was no house for the African majority. Within weeks, hundreds of organisations had come together to form the United Democratic Front, and by 1985 townships across the country were in revolt. The government declared states of emergency and detained tens of thousands of people, while banks, companies and governments around the world turned against apartheid. This lesson explains, in broad outline, the internal resistance, repression and external pressure of the 1980s that brought South Africa to a crisis by 1989 and set the scene for the dramatic changes of 1990.
