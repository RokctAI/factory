Hey, this topic fits in three pictures: a parliament with three doors, a township on fire and a bank that said no.
The parliament. P.W. Botha's total strategy combined reform and force. The 1983 constitution created a Tricameral Parliament for white, coloured and Indian voters, but none for Africans. Turnout in 1984 was low. The UDF was launched on 20 August 1983 at Mitchells Plain with about 560 organisations. COSATU followed on 1 December 1985.
The fire. On 3 September 1984, the Vaal uprising began over rents. Boycotts, stay-aways and the call to make townships ungovernable followed. There was also violence: necklacing, which many leaders condemned, and the UDF and Inkatha conflict. States of emergency came in July 1985 and June 1986, with tens of thousands detained. The Langa shootings near Uitenhage, the Cradock Four, Vlakplaas, the CCB and cross-border raids followed.
The bank. After the Rubicon speech of August 1985, Chase Manhattan refused loans. The US anti-apartheid law of 1986 and disinvestment followed. Cuito Cuanavale, Namibia and the Berlin Wall also mattered. Mandela met Botha in 1989, and De Klerk took over.
