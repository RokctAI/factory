Hey, this topic fits in three pictures: a long table, a country holding its breath and a queue that stretched for kilometres.
The table. The National Peace Accord came in September 1991, then CODESA 1 at Kempton Park in December 1991 with nineteen parties; the CP, PAC and AZAPO stayed away. In the March 1992 white referendum, about sixty-nine percent voted yes. CODESA 2 deadlocked in May 1992. Then came the Record of Understanding in September 1992, Slovo's sunset clauses, the 1993 Multi-Party talks and the interim constitution in November 1993.
The breath. About fourteen thousand died in political violence. There was ANC and Inkatha fighting, third force claims and Inkathagate. Boipatong on 17 June 1992 left about forty-five dead. At Bisho on 7 September 1992, twenty-eight marchers were killed. Chris Hani was killed on 10 April 1993, and Mandela called for calm. The AWB stormed Kempton Park, St James Church was attacked, and Bophuthatswana and Shell House followed in 1994. The IFP joined on 19 April.
The queue. Voting ran from 26 to 29 April 1994, with the ANC on 62.65, the NP 20.39 and the IFP 10.54. Mandela was inaugurated on 10 May, with a GNU. 27 April is Freedom Day.
