Between 1990 and 1994, South Africa walked a tightrope. Around negotiating tables in Kempton Park, the government, the ANC and other parties argued about how a new democracy should work. Outside, the country was torn by violence: massacres in Boipatong and Bisho, the assassination of Chris Hani, attacks by right-wing groups and bitter fighting between supporters of the ANC and Inkatha. Several times, it seemed that the talks would collapse. Yet on 27 April 1994, millions of South Africans stood in long queues to vote together for the first time. This lesson explains, in broad outline, the negotiations and violence of 1990 to 1994, and the democratic election that brought Nelson Mandela to power.
