The uprising of 1976 was crushed within about a year, but its effects lasted until the end of apartheid. Thousands of young people slipped across the borders to join the liberation movements in exile, and many came back as trained guerrillas. In September 1977, the Black Consciousness leader Steve Biko died after being assaulted in police custody, and weeks later the government banned nineteen organisations. The United Nations imposed a mandatory arms embargo, and the government began reforms that it hoped would calm the country. This lesson explains the longer-term consequences of 1976 for resistance and repression: exile and the armed struggle, harsher repression, international pressure, reform, and a new generation of activists who would shape the 1980s.
