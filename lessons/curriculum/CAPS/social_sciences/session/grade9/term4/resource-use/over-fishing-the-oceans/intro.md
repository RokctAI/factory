For almost five hundred years, the seas off Newfoundland in Canada were so full of cod that sailors said you could catch them by lowering a basket into the water. Then, in 1992, the cod were gone, and the Canadian government closed the fishery, putting tens of thousands of people out of work in a single year. The cod had been caught faster than they could breed. This lesson explains how fishing works as a resource, what over-fishing is and why it happens, the effects of unwise use of the oceans on fish, ecosystems and people, and South African examples, from the West Coast rock lobster to the poaching of abalone. It shows how a renewable resource can be pushed to collapse.
