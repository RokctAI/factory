Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a full shop and an empty pocket, a four-legged table and a magic seed with a price tag.
The shop. The 1996 World Food Summit defined food security as all people, at all times, having physical and economic access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food. South Africa produces enough food and exports a lot, but about one in five households has inadequate access, and about 27 percent of young children were stunted in 2016. The main cause is lack of access: unemployment, prices and land dispossession.
The table. Four pillars: availability, access, utilisation and stability. Support includes the constitutional right to food, the Child Support Grant, school meals and food gardens.
The seed. Science and technology raised output: mechanisation, fertilisers, pesticides, irrigation, breeding and hybrids. The Green Revolution, led by Norman Borlaug, brought high-yield wheat and rice; India became self-sufficient in wheat, and Borlaug won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970. Costs: eutrophication, pesticide harm and resistance, salinisation, monoculture, fewer jobs, debt and inequality, and less success in Africa. Sustainable intensification aims to fix this.
