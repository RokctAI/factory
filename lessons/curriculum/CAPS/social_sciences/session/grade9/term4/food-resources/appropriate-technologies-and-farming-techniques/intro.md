In the dry north of Burkina Faso, a farmer named Yacouba Sawadogo spent decades digging small pits in hard, bare ground, filling them with manure and planting seeds in them. The pits caught the rare rain and fed the soil, and a forest grew where there had been desert. He became known as the man who stopped the desert, using nothing more than a hoe, manure and knowledge. This lesson explains appropriate technology, which means tools and methods suited to the needs, skills and resources of the people who use them. It looks at appropriate technologies for water, soil and pest control, at farming techniques such as crop rotation, intercropping and conservation agriculture, and at how small-scale farmers and food gardens in South Africa use them to improve food security.
