In 1948, two very different documents shaped South Africa's future. In Paris, the nations of the world adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, which declared that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. In the same year, in South Africa, the National Party won the general election and began to build apartheid, a system that denied those rights to the majority of the population. This lesson explains why the Universal Declaration was written after World War II, who drafted it and what its thirty articles say, how South Africa responded to it, and how its ideas finally became part of South Africa's own Constitution and Bill of Rights in 1996.
