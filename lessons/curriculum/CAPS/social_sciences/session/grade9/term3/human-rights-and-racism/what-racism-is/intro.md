For most of the twentieth century, South Africa's laws were based on the idea that people could be sorted into races and that some races deserved more rights than others. Modern science has shown that this idea is false: all human beings belong to one species and share almost all of their genetic material. Yet racism shaped where people could live, learn, work and vote, and its effects are still visible today. This lesson gives a brief definition and explanation of racism. It distinguishes prejudice, stereotyping, discrimination and racism, explains how so-called scientific racism was used to justify slavery, colonialism, Nazism and apartheid, describes the difference between personal and institutional racism, and looks at how South Africa's Constitution and ordinary people can challenge racism today.
