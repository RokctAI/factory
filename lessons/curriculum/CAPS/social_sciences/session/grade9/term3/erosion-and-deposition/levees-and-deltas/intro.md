The ancient Egyptian civilisation grew up on a narrow ribbon of fertile land along the Nile and on the great fan of land where the river meets the Mediterranean Sea. That fan, the Nile Delta, was built entirely from mud carried by the river. This lesson explains two landforms created by river deposition in the lower course: levees, the natural raised banks that form along rivers when they flood, and deltas, the areas of new land built where rivers enter the sea or a lake. We look at how and why they form, the different shapes deltas take, including Botswana's remarkable inland Okavango Delta, and why so many people live on these fertile but flood-prone lands, with lessons from New Orleans and Bangladesh.
