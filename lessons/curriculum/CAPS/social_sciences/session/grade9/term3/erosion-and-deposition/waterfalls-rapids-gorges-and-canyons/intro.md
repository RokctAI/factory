At Augrabies Falls in the Northern Cape, the Orange River thunders over a granite ledge and plunges into a deep, narrow gorge that stretches for kilometres downstream. The Khoekhoe name for the place is often translated as "place of great noise". This lesson explains how rivers create some of the most spectacular landforms on earth through erosion: waterfalls, rapids, gorges and canyons. We look at why waterfalls form where hard rock lies over softer rock, how they slowly move upstream and leave gorges behind them, how rapids form, and why canyons such as the Blyde River Canyon and Namibia's Fish River Canyon are so deep. We also look at the value of these landforms for tourism, water and energy.
