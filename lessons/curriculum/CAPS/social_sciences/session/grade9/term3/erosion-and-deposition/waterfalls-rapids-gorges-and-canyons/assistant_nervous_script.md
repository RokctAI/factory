Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a river with a saw, a crumbling ledge and a deep slot in the land.

The saw. In the upper course the river is steep and fast and erodes downwards by hydraulic action and abrasion, making a narrow V-shaped valley. Where hard rock lies over soft rock, differential erosion creates a step.

The ledge. Water falling over the step digs a plunge pool. Spray undercuts the soft rock, leaving an overhang of hard rock, which collapses. The waterfall retreats upstream. Examples: Tugela Falls, about 948 metres in five drops; Howick Falls, about 95 metres; Augrabies, about 56 metres; Victoria Falls, about 108 metres high. Rapids are fast, turbulent stretches over bands of hard and soft rock.

The slot. A retreating waterfall leaves a gorge, such as the 18-kilometre Augrabies gorge. A canyon is a very deep, long gorge, often in dry areas: the Fish River Canyon and the Blyde River Canyon. They matter for tourism, dams and hydroelectric power.
