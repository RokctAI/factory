Look down from an aeroplane at a river crossing flat land, and you will see it looping back and forth like a ribbon dropped on the floor, with curved lakes lying beside it like pieces cut off the ribbon. These loops are called meanders, and the curved lakes are oxbow lakes. This lesson explains how rivers in their middle and lower courses erode on one side of a bend while depositing on the other, so that bends grow, migrate and sometimes get cut off completely. We look at river cliffs and slip-off slopes, how a meander neck is cut through during a flood, and why oxbow lakes are important wetlands, with examples from the Pongola flood plain in KwaZulu-Natal and other rivers in southern Africa.
