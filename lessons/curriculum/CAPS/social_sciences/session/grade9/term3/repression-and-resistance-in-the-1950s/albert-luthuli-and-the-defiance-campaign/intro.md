On 26 June 1952, small groups of volunteers across South Africa walked calmly through entrances marked "Europeans only", sat on benches reserved for whites and broke curfew and pass regulations, then waited quietly to be arrested. It was the start of the Defiance Campaign against Unjust Laws, the largest campaign of non-violent civil disobedience South Africa had yet seen. More than eight thousand people went to prison. This lesson explains how the campaign drew on the ideas of Mahatma Gandhi, who had developed non-violent resistance in South Africa decades earlier, how it was organised and what happened, and how Chief Albert Luthuli emerged from it to lead the ANC and later become the first African to win the Nobel Peace Prize.
