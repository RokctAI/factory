On 9 August 1956, about twenty thousand women of all races filled the amphitheatre of the Union Buildings in Pretoria, the seat of the apartheid government. They had come from all over the country to deliver petitions against the extension of pass laws to women. Their leaders knocked on the Prime Minister's door, left the petitions in his office, and returned to stand with the crowd in thirty minutes of complete silence. Then the women sang a song with the words "wathint' abafazi, wathint' imbokodo": you strike a woman, you strike a rock. This lesson explains why passes for women were so feared, how the Federation of South African Women organised the march, what happened that day, and the lives and roles of two of its leaders, Lilian Ngoyi and Helen Joseph.
