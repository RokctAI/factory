Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a little book that could break a family, twenty thousand women in silence and a rock that would not crack.
The book. Women successfully resisted passes in Bloemfontein in 1913. The 1952 Act provided for passes for women, issued from 1956. Women feared influx control, separation from families, arrest leaving children alone, and loss of income. They faced double oppression of race and gender.
The silence. FEDSAW was formed on 17 April 1954, non-racial, and adopted the Women's Charter. On 9 August 1956, about twenty thousand women gathered at the Union Buildings. Ngoyi, Joseph, Moosa and Williams left petitions with over one hundred thousand signatures at Strijdom's office. Thirty minutes of silence, then "wathint' abafazi, wathint' imbokodo". Passes became compulsory for women by 1963, but 9 August is National Women's Day.
The rock. Lilian Ngoyi, garment worker and powerful speaker, first woman on the ANC NEC, Treason trialist, detained and banned, died 1980. Helen Joseph, born in England, social worker, COD founder, FEDSAW secretary, first person under house arrest in 1962, died 1992.
