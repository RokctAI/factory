Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a man thrown off a train, an open door marked "Whites only" and a chief who chose his people.
The train. Gandhi came to South Africa in 1893, was thrown off a train at Pietermaritzburg, founded the Natal Indian Congress in 1894, and developed satyagraha against the Black Act from 1906 to 1914: breaking unjust laws openly, non-violently, and accepting punishment. His early views on Africans are debated. The Indian Congress used passive resistance from 1946 to 1948.
The door. The ANC and SAIC Joint Planning Council demanded repeal of six laws. On 26 June 1952 the Defiance Campaign began, with Mandela as volunteer-in-chief. Volunteers used whites-only facilities, broke curfews and pass laws, and over 8 000 were arrested. The 1953 Public Safety and Criminal Law Amendment Acts ended it. No laws were repealed, but ANC membership rose from about 7 000 to about 100 000.
The chief. Albert Luthuli, teacher and chief of Groutville, was dismissed in November 1952, issued "The Road to Freedom is via the Cross", became ANC President-General in December 1952 and won the Nobel Peace Prize for 1960.
