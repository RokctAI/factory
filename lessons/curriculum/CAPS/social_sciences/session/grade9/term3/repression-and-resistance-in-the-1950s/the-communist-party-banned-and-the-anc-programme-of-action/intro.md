In 1949, a group of young members of the African National Congress persuaded their organisation to change direction. For almost forty years, the ANC had protested through petitions, deputations and polite appeals, and the laws had only become harsher. Its new Programme of Action called for boycotts, strikes, civil disobedience and non-cooperation. A year later, the government passed the Suppression of Communism Act, banning the Communist Party and giving itself the power to silence almost any opponent of apartheid. This lesson explains how the ANC changed from cautious petitions to mass action, why the Communist Party was banned and how the Act was used, and how the strikes and protests of 1950 opened a decade of non-violent resistance and growing repression.
