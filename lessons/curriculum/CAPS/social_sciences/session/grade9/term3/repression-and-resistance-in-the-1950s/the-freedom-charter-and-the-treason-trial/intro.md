On 25 and 26 June 1955, about three thousand delegates gathered on a dusty open field at Kliptown, near Johannesburg, for the Congress of the People. Clause by clause, they adopted a document that began with the words: "We, the people of South Africa, declare for all our country and the world to know: that South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white." On the second afternoon, armed police surrounded the meeting. A year and a half later, 156 people were arrested and charged with high treason, partly because of that document. This lesson explains how the Freedom Charter was written, what it says and why it was controversial, and the story of the Treason Trial, which lasted more than four years and ended with every accused found not guilty.
