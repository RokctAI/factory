On 26 May 1948, South Africa held a general election in which almost only white citizens could vote. The National Party, led by Dr D.F. Malan, won a narrow victory with a promise called apartheid, an Afrikaans word meaning separateness. Over the next decade, its governments passed a flood of laws that classified every person by race and controlled where they could live, work, study, travel, and whom they could marry. This lesson explains why the National Party won in 1948, what apartheid meant and how it built on earlier segregation, and the main apartheid laws in broad outline, from the Population Registration Act to Bantu Education. It also explains the difference between petty apartheid and grand apartheid.
