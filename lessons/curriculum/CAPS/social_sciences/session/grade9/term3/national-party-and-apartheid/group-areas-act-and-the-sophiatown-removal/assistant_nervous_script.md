Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a coloured-in map, a street full of music and trucks in the rain.
The map. The Group Areas Act of 1950 let the government declare each area for one racial group. It claimed this reduced friction, but it gave whites the best land, removed traders and mixed communities, and placed townships far away behind buffer zones for control. Those removed were overwhelmingly black, coloured and Indian.
The music. Sophiatown, west of Johannesburg, founded by Herman Tobiansky in 1903, offered rare freehold rights to black owners. Crowded and poor, but vibrant: Kofifi, jazz, Drum writers, Father Trevor Huddleston. The government saw it as a black spot.
The trucks. Western Areas Removal Scheme and the Natives Resettlement Act of 1954. Slogan: "We won't move". On 9 February 1955, about two thousand armed police moved families to Meadowlands; resistance was called off. Sophiatown was bulldozed and rebuilt as Triomf, renamed Sophiatown in 2006. District Six was declared white in 1966. About 3.5 million were removed from 1960 to 1983. The Act was repealed in 1991; restitution followed in 1994.
