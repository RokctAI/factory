Before dawn on 9 February 1955, in heavy rain, about two thousand armed police arrived in Sophiatown, a lively, crowded suburb west of central Johannesburg. They loaded families and their belongings onto trucks and drove them to bare plots at Meadowlands, many kilometres away. Over the next few years, the whole of Sophiatown was bulldozed, and a white suburb was built in its place, named Triomf, meaning triumph. This lesson is a case study of the Group Areas Act of 1950 and forced removals. It explains what the Act did and why the government passed it, what made Sophiatown so special, how its people resisted the removal, and what the destruction of Sophiatown and places like District Six meant for South Africa.
