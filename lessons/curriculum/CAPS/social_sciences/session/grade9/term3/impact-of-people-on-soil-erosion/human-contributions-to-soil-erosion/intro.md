A single centimetre of fertile topsoil can take hundreds of years to form, yet one heavy storm on bare ground can wash it away in an afternoon. Soil erosion is a natural process, but people have made it far faster in many parts of the world, including South Africa, where deep gullies called dongas scar the land in many provinces. This lesson explains what soil is and why topsoil matters, the main types of soil erosion by water and wind, and how three human activities, agriculture, construction and mining, speed it up. It ends with the damage erosion causes, from silted-up dams to lost farmland, and why every South African depends on the thin layer of soil beneath their feet.
