On 14 April 1935, a black wall of dust thousands of metres high rolled across the Great Plains of the United States, turning day into night. The dust was topsoil, blown off farms that had been ploughed and left bare during a long drought. The day became known as Black Sunday, and the disaster as the Dust Bowl. This lesson is a case study of agriculture as a contributor to soil erosion. It explains how crop farming and livestock farming can expose and damage soil, examines the Dust Bowl and examples from South Africa, from the overgrazed Karoo to the dongas of the former homelands, and then looks at the farming methods that protect soil, from contour ploughing and terraces to conservation agriculture.
