On the morning of 21 March 1960, thousands of unarmed people gathered outside the police station in Sharpeville, a township near Vereeniging, answering the Pan Africanist Congress's call to protest against the pass laws. Shortly after one o'clock in the afternoon, the police opened fire. Sixty-nine people were killed, many of them shot in the back as they ran away, and about 180 were wounded. Nine days later, about thirty thousand people marched from Langa into the centre of Cape Town. This lesson explains the causes of the protests, who led them and what happened at Sharpeville and Langa, and the short-term and longer-term consequences, from the state of emergency and the banning of the ANC and PAC to the turn to armed struggle and the world's response.
