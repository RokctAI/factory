On 6 April 1959, in the Orlando Communal Hall in Soweto, a new liberation movement was born. Its members had broken away from the ANC, which they believed had lost its way. They called their organisation the Pan Africanist Congress, chose a softly spoken university lecturer named Robert Mangaliso Sobukwe as their president, and set themselves a bold goal: freedom in South Africa by 1963. Less than a year later, the PAC's campaign against the pass laws would lead to the Sharpeville massacre. This lesson explains the ideas of Africanism and Pan-Africanism, why the Africanists broke away from the ANC, how the PAC was formed and what it stood for, and the life and leadership of Robert Sobukwe.
