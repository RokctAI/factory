Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a book left at home, forty seconds of gunfire and a long walk into the city.
The book. Pass laws were the most hated symbol of apartheid: arrests, fines and endorsement out. Sharpeville had unemployment, higher rents and raids; Cape Town had hostels and the Coloured Labour Preference Policy. The PAC called on men to leave passes at home on 21 March 1960: no bail, no defence, no fine.
The gunfire. At Sharpeville, a crowd of about five to seven thousand gathered at the police station. About three hundred police, Saracens and low-flying jets. After a scuffle at about one o'clock, police fired without warning for about forty seconds: 69 killed, about 180 wounded, many shot in the back. No one was prosecuted.
The walk. At Langa, police killed at least two on 21 March, and workers struck. On 30 March, Philip Kgosana led about thirty thousand marchers to Caledon Square, sent them home on a promise, and was arrested. A state of emergency followed, with over 11 000 detained; the ANC and PAC were banned on 8 April. UN Resolution 134, Umkhonto we Sizwe and Poqo followed. Human Rights Day is 21 March.
