Many of the people who lived through apartheid are still alive today: grandparents, neighbours, teachers and community elders. Their memories are a living archive that no textbook can fully replace. This lesson introduces the Term 3 History project, in which you research one apartheid law and interview a person who was affected by it. It explains what oral history is and why it matters, how to choose an apartheid law and find written sources about it, how to plan and conduct a respectful interview, and how to judge the reliability of memories and combine them with other evidence into a well-referenced research report. By the end, you will know how to turn a conversation into history.
