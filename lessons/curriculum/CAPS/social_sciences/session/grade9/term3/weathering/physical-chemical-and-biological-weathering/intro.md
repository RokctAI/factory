Look closely at an old gravestone, a cracked boulder on a mountain path or a crumbling brick wall, and you will see rock slowly breaking down where it stands. This lesson introduces weathering, the process that breaks down and changes rock at or near the earth's surface. We explore the three types of weathering: physical weathering, in which rock is broken into smaller pieces without changing what it is made of; chemical weathering, in which the minerals in rock are changed into new substances; and biological weathering, in which plants and animals help break rock down. We look at South African examples, from frost-shattered rock in the Drakensberg to the limestone caves near Oudtshoorn, and see how climate decides which type matters most.
