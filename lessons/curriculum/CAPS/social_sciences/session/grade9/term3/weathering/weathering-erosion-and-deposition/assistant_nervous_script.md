Hey — this whole topic fits neatly in three pictures: a crumbling biscuit, a broom and a pile in the corner.

The biscuit is weathering: rock broken down in place. The broom is erosion: material removed and transported. The pile is deposition: material laid down when the agent loses energy. Weathering breaks, erosion takes, deposition drops.

The brooms are the agents of erosion. Running water is the most important in South Africa: rain splash, rills and dongas, and rivers eroding by hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition and solution. Wind works by deflation and abrasion in dry areas. Ice carves valleys. Waves erode coasts. Gravity causes mass movement: rockfalls, landslides, mudflows and soil creep.

The pile: rivers transport by traction, saltation, suspension and solution. When they slow down, they drop the biggest material first. Wind drops sand to form dunes. Diamonds from near Kimberley were carried by the Orange River to the Atlantic and deposited by waves on west coast beaches. Eroded soil also fills dams with silt.
