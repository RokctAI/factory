Diamonds found on the beaches of the Namaqualand coast began their journey hundreds of kilometres inland, locked inside ancient volcanic rock. How did they get to the sea? The answer lies in three processes that work together to shape every landscape on earth: weathering, erosion and deposition. This lesson explains the difference between them clearly. Weathering breaks rock down where it lies; erosion picks up and carries the broken material away, using running water, wind, ice, waves and gravity; and deposition lays the material down again when the moving agent loses energy. We look at how rivers and wind transport material, at mass movements such as landslides, and at how these processes link together into one great cycle that wears down mountains and builds new land.
