Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a hammer, a rusty nail and a stubborn root.

Weathering is the breaking down of rock in place, at or near the surface. Erosion is different: it moves the pieces away.

The hammer is physical weathering. It breaks rock into smaller pieces of the same rock. Frost action: water expands by about nine percent when it freezes, and wedges cracks apart, as on the Drakensberg. Exfoliation: heating and cooling make outer layers peel off, forming domes such as Paarl Rock. Salt crystals also push rock apart.

The rusty nail is chemical weathering. It changes the minerals. Oxidation makes rock and soil red. Carbonation: weak carbonic acid dissolves limestone, forming caves such as the Cango Caves. Hydrolysis turns feldspar in granite into clay.

The root is biological weathering: roots splitting rock, burrowing animals, and lichens making acids.

Climate decides which wins: chemical in hot, wet places; physical in deserts and on freezing mountains.
