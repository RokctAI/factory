Hey — this topic fits in three pictures: a smoky chimney, a blasted hillside and a fingerprint on a painting.

The chimney. Burning coal and fuel releases sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which form sulphuric and nitric acid and fall as acid rain. Natural rain has a pH of about 5,6; acid rain is below 5, and each pH unit lower is ten times more acidic. Acid rain dissolves limestone and marble, rusts metal and harms lakes. Mpumalanga's coal power stations make the Highveld a pollution hotspot, and wind carries pollution far.

The hillside. Mining, quarrying and road building expose and crack fresh rock, so it weathers faster. Crushed rock in mine dumps weathers very fast. On the Witwatersrand, pyrite oxidises to make sulphuric acid: acid mine drainage. Clearing vegetation and irrigation, which causes salinisation, also speed up weathering.

The fingerprint. Touching, wetting and graffiti damage San rock art in the Drakensberg. Solutions: cleaner energy, rehabilitated dumps, careful farming and respect for heritage.
