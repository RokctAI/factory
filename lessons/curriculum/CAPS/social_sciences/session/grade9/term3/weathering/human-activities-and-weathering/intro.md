Some of the stone statues and buildings in the world's great cities are crumbling faster today than they did a hundred years ago, and the reason is in the air. This lesson explores how human activities speed up weathering. We look at how burning coal and fuel produces acid rain that eats into rock and buildings, a serious issue on the Mpumalanga Highveld; how mining, quarrying, road building and construction expose fresh rock to the weather; how the oxidation of minerals in mine dumps on the Witwatersrand produces acid mine drainage; how clearing vegetation and irrigating farmland change weathering; and how people damage precious heritage, such as San rock art in the Drakensberg. We also consider what can be done to reduce the damage.
