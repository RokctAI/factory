## Question (4 marks)

(Refer to an infographic on Tropical Cyclone Filipo: 'The tropical storm made landfall in Mozambique on 12 March after strengthening off the coast of southeast Africa, bringing strong winds and heavy rain to Mozambique. On 13 March, Tropical Storm Filipo moved back over the ocean and developed into a tropical cyclone on 14 March.' A map shows the plotted path: from 10 March 12:00 UTC over the Indian Ocean it tracks west/south-west, crosses the Mozambique coast, passes near Maputo at 06:00 UTC on 13 March, then curves back south-east over the Indian Ocean. An inset synoptic sketch of the mature stage shows closed isobars of 984 and 980 hPa spiralling around the eye. A table gives wind speeds along the path: 10 Mar 12:00 UTC 63 km/h; 11 Mar 12:00 UTC 95 km/h; 11 Mar 18:00 UTC 116 km/h; 12 Mar 06:00 UTC 95 km/h; 12 Mar 18:00 UTC 74 km/h; 13 Mar 06:00 UTC 84 km/h; 13 Mar 18:00 UTC 106 km/h; 14 Mar 06:00 UTC 127 km/h.) Account for the lack of rainfall and clouds in the eye of the tropical cyclone. (2 x 2)

## Method

process explanation

In the eye the air subsides. Sinking air is compressed and warms; warming dries the air and evaporates any moisture; and without rising, cooling air no condensation can take place — so the eye stays clear and rainless.
