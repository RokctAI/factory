## Question (1 mark)

(Refer to an infographic on Tropical Cyclone Filipo: 'The tropical storm made landfall in Mozambique on 12 March after strengthening off the coast of southeast Africa, bringing strong winds and heavy rain to Mozambique. On 13 March, Tropical Storm Filipo moved back over the ocean and developed into a tropical cyclone on 14 March.' A map shows the plotted path: from 10 March 12:00 UTC over the Indian Ocean it tracks west/south-west, crosses the Mozambique coast, passes near Maputo at 06:00 UTC on 13 March, then curves back south-east over the Indian Ocean. An inset synoptic sketch of the mature stage shows closed isobars of 984 and 980 hPa spiralling around the eye. A table gives wind speeds along the path: 10 Mar 12:00 UTC 63 km/h; 11 Mar 12:00 UTC 95 km/h; 11 Mar 18:00 UTC 116 km/h; 12 Mar 06:00 UTC 95 km/h; 12 Mar 18:00 UTC 74 km/h; 13 Mar 06:00 UTC 84 km/h; 13 Mar 18:00 UTC 106 km/h; 14 Mar 06:00 UTC 127 km/h.) State ONE condition that could have led to the development of Tropical Cyclone Filipo. (1 x 1)

## Method

recall conditions

Recall the birth conditions of a tropical cyclone: a warm ocean surface of at least 26,5 °C supplying heat and moisture, converging air in a low-pressure area, enough Coriolis force to start rotation, calm unstable conditions with high evaporation over the ocean, latent-heat release to power the storm, and divergence in the upper air to keep drawing air upward. Any one condition earns the mark.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- Presence of a low-pressure system/convergence of air (1)
- Presence of Coriolis force (1)
- Sea surface temperatures of 26,5 °C or more (1)
- Calm conditions over the oceans (1)
- Unstable conditions/High rates of evaporation over the oceans/high humidity (1)
- Production of latent heat (1)
- Upper air divergence (1)
