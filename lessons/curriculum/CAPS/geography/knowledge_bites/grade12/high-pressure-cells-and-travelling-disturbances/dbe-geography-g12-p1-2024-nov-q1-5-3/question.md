## Question (4 marks)

(Refer to a source on line thunderstorms over South Africa: 'During summer, thunderstorms can form a line that can extend for hundreds of kilometres. These line thunderstorms can persist for many hours and produce damaging winds and hail, which impacts negatively on the natural environment.' A sketch map of South Africa shows a north-west to south-east moisture front across the interior with cumulonimbus clouds along it at A and B. Wind A blows in from a high off the south-west coast towards the moisture front; wind B blows in from a high off the east coast over the Indian Ocean. An inset diagram grades rainfall outward from the moisture front: heavy rain nearest the front, then moderate rain, then light rain furthest away.) Explain why the heaviest rainfall occurs closest to the moisture front, as evident in the source. (2 x 2)

## Method

process explanation

Along the moisture front the contrast between the two airmasses forces the most rapid uplift, so condensation is deepest and a dense line of cumulonimbus clouds builds there. Moving away from the front, uplift weakens, condensation lessens and cloud thins — so rainfall grades from heavy at the front to light far from it. Answer from either side of that contrast, but stay explicitly close to or far from the front.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

- Close to the moisture front: Rapid uplift of air along the moisture front (2); Greater (vertical) condensation (2); (Dense line of) cumulonimbus clouds form (2)
- OR Further from the moisture front: Less uplift of air further from the moisture front (2); Less (vertical) condensation (2); Less clouds form (2)
