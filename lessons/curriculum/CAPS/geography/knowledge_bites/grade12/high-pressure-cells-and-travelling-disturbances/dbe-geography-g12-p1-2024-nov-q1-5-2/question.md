## Question (4 marks)

(Refer to a source on line thunderstorms over South Africa: 'During summer, thunderstorms can form a line that can extend for hundreds of kilometres. These line thunderstorms can persist for many hours and produce damaging winds and hail, which impacts negatively on the natural environment.' A sketch map of South Africa shows a north-west to south-east moisture front across the interior with cumulonimbus clouds along it at A and B. Wind A blows in from a high off the south-west coast towards the moisture front; wind B blows in from a high off the east coast over the Indian Ocean. An inset diagram grades rainfall outward from the moisture front: heavy rain nearest the front, then moderate rain, then light rain furthest away.) Describe the temperature and moisture content of wind A and wind B that led to the formation of the line thunderstorm. (2 x 2)

## Method

diagram interpretation

Wind A blows in from the high off the south-west coast, across the cold Atlantic — so it arrives cold and dry. Wind B blows in from the high over the warm Indian Ocean — so it arrives warm and moist. Where these two contrasting airmasses meet, the moisture front and its line of thunderstorms develop.

- WIND B (north-easterly): warm and moist (2)
