## Question (6 marks)

(Refer to a source on line thunderstorms over South Africa: 'During summer, thunderstorms can form a line that can extend for hundreds of kilometres. These line thunderstorms can persist for many hours and produce damaging winds and hail, which impacts negatively on the natural environment.' A sketch map of South Africa shows a north-west to south-east moisture front across the interior with cumulonimbus clouds along it at A and B. Wind A blows in from a high off the south-west coast towards the moisture front; wind B blows in from a high off the east coast over the Indian Ocean. An inset diagram grades rainfall outward from the moisture front: heavy rain nearest the front, then moderate rain, then light rain furthest away.) Explain the negative physical (natural) impact of line thunderstorms. (3 x 2)

## Method

impact explanation

Keep the focus on the natural environment, not people or property: habitats and ecosystems destroyed and biodiversity reduced; topsoil washed away and soils leached; mass movements triggered on slopes; wildlife displaced or drowned; trees and natural vegetation uprooted; water quality reduced; sinkholes forming; and channels silting up with deposited material. Any three, explained, at two marks each.
