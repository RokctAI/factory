## Question (4 marks)

Explain the main processes that give rise to the difference in the shape of cross-profiles C and D. (2 x 2)  (On the graded longitudinal profile in sketch B, two valley cross-profiles are drawn: C near the source, a narrow deep V-shape, and D near the mouth, a wide shallow flat-floored shape.)

## Method

process explanation

## Memo working

Link each shape to its dominant process. At C, in the upper course, vertical (downward) erosion dominates - the river cuts down into its bed, carving a narrow, deep V-shaped valley (2). At D, in the lower course, deposition dominates as the river loses energy on the gentle gradient, and lateral erosion widens the valley - producing the wide, flat-floored shape (2 for any one of these lower-course processes). Both profiles must be referred to.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Profile C: Vertical/downward erosion is dominant in the upper course (2). Profile D: Deposition is dominant in the lower course (2); Lateral erosion occurs (2) [ANY ONE]. [MUST REFER TO BOTH PROFILE C AND D]
