Hey, it's Mandy — if industry types are blurring into alphabet soup, hold on, because this whole topic runs on one playground question you already know how to ask.

What's more of a pain to move — what the business uses, or what it sells? That's it. That's the tuck-shop test. Cane is heavy and rots, so the sugar mill goes to the cane. Bread goes stale and beer is heavy glass, so bakeries and breweries go to the customers. Software travels down a cable, so those firms go wherever the skilled people live — footloose. Every bakery-in-every-town product is ubiquitous. And factories that camp where ships meet trains — Durban's harbour — are bridge industries. Five types, one question.

Location factors? It's just choosing an address: ingredients, workers, water, power, transport, customers, government sweeteners, competition. And for any real factory, only two or three of them truly matter — a smelter cares about electricity, a clothing factory cares about workers. Saying which ones matter is the whole skill.

The hindering factors you already know from the news: load-shedding, limping trains, jammed ports, scarce skills, strikes, cheap imports — plus the two big ones, over-concentration and pollution.
