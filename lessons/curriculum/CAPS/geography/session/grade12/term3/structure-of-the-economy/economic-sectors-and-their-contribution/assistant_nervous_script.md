Hi, it's Mandy — if "economic geography" sounds like a subject you never signed up for, relax. This whole term is built on one idea you already use every time you buy a bag of chips.

Four kinds of work. Grow it — farms, fishing boats, mines pulling raw stuff from nature. Make it — mills, factories, builders changing raw stuff into products. Move-and-sell it — shops, taxis, banks, teachers, all the services. Think it up — research and IT. Every job in South Africa is one of those four verbs. Test yourself on your own street: the man with the fruit stand, the woman at the salon, your maths teacher. See? You can already do this.

The numbers are just two surprises. Surprise one: services earn about two thirds of South Africa's money, while farming earns only two or three per cent — yet farming still feeds us all, so small share never means unimportant. Surprise two: money and jobs disagree — farming's job share is about double its money share, because farming uses many hands for modest pay, while banks are the opposite.

Data questions are a calm routine, not a trap: read the title and units, say the biggest and smallest slices, name what changed, give one reason from the lesson. Four steps, every time.

Tonight, do one thing: classify ten jobs you saw today into the four verbs. That's the whole foundation of term three, laid in five minutes. You've got this.
