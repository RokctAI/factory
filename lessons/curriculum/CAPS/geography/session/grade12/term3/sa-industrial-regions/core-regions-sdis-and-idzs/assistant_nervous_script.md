Hey, it's Mandy — last big topic of term three, and if it feels like a lot of names and acronyms, let me shrink it to a story you can tell in one minute.

Four kitchens cook for the whole country: Gauteng up on the highveld, Durban–Pinetown, Port Elizabeth–Uitenhage, and the Cape around Table Bay. One sentence explains the map: minerals made the inland kitchen, harbours made the other three.

You only need two kitchens in detail, and each has one unforgettable hook. Gauteng is the giant that shouldn't exist — no port, no river, water piped from Lesotho — but gold built the railways, skills and market, and the giant outlived its gold. PE–Uitenhage is the car town — port, rail, chosen by Volkswagen and Isuzu — brilliant at one thing, and fragile for the same reason.

Then the fix for the crowded map, two tools: an SDI is a line — upgrade a whole corridor, like the Maputo route to the sea. An IDZ is a point — a ready-made industrial estate at a port, like Coega. Line and point. Partly working, not yet map-changing. That's the honest verdict.

Every case study question is just four pockets: why there, what's made, what helps and hurts, who gains and who pays. Tonight, tell the one-minute story out loud once — four kitchens, the giant, the car town, line and point. Do that and you walk into this essay with the whole answer already in your mouth. You've got this.
