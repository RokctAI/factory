Today we are working through valley climates — the smallest weather systems on the syllabus and among the most reliably examined, because everything in them follows from two mechanisms you can reason out in the exam room: which slope faces the sun, and which way air drains at night. We will build the topic in four moves: slope aspect and the temperature difference it creates in South African valleys, the daily rhythm of anabatic and katabatic winds, the night-time trio of inversions, frost pockets and radiation fog with the thermal belt above them, and finally the human geography of the valley. By the end you will be able to explain exactly why the vineyard, the orchard, the farmhouse and the informal settlement each sit where they do.
