Hey, it's Mandy — good news first: urban climates is the friendliest topic in the whole climate section. No fronts, no stages, no synoptic charts. Just one warm bubble, one dirty lid, and reasons you can feel through your own feet.

Seriously — you already know the core fact. Walk barefoot from grass onto tar on a summer evening. The tar is still hot. That is the urban heat island: a city built of tar and concrete soaks up sun all day like a storage heater and breathes it out all night. Add that the city cannot sweat — its rain runs down the drains instead of evaporating — and you have the two big causes without opening a textbook.

The strategies almost write themselves, because every strategy is a cause reversed. Dark surfaces? Paint them light. No sweat glands? Plant trees and keep the parks — remember, the temperature graph DIPS over every park, and that dip is your proof. Sealed ground? Let water soak in. Waste heat? Insulate, share transport.

The pollution dome is one picture: a pot with its lid on. Winter inversion is the lid, the city's smoke fills the pot, calm keeps it a dome, wind smears it into a plume. Strategies: make less smoke, and never burn on a still winter day.
