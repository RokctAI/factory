Today we work through the three subtropical anticyclones that make South Africa's everyday weather — the South Atlantic High, the South Indian High and the Kalahari High — and the travelling disturbances that break their grip: the moisture front with its line thunderstorms, the coastal low and the berg wind. Cyclones make headlines, but this is the system the matric paper leans on for synoptic-chart questions, season identification and mechanism marks. We will name and place each high, follow the inversion lid that switches the interior between dry winters and stormy summers, trace the berg wind's fire-day arithmetic, and finish with the chart signatures that turn it all into marks. Anchor one fact throughout: an anticyclone is sinking, diverging air — and sinking air is dry, stable and cloudless.
