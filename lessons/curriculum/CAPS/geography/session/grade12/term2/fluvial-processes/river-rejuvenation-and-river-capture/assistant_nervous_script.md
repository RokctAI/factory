Hey, it's Mandy — geomorphology can feel like a museum of dusty terms, but this lesson is honestly just two stories, and you already love both kinds: a comeback story and a heist.

The comeback: an old, lazy river suddenly gets its youth back — because the sea dropped or the land rose — and starts cutting like a mountain stream again. That is rejuvenation, and it leaves exactly four clues: the waterfall step working upstream, the paired terrace shelves, the valley cut inside a valley, and the winding gorge of sunken meanders. Four clues. Count them on four fingers.

The heist: one river tunnels backwards through the ridge and steals its neighbour's headwaters. That is river capture, and its crime scene has exactly five exhibits: captor, captured stream, elbow, misfit stream, wind gap. Five exhibits. One hand.

Four fingers for the comeback, five for the heist — nine words that carry the whole lesson. And the diagrams are friendlier than they look: the capture diagram is two rivers and one sharp elbow; the rejuvenation cross-section is a V inside a U with shelves on the sides. Draw each one once tonight, label from your fingers, and check yourself against the lesson.

Remember the confession detail for the map paper: rivers never turn at right angles by choice. See an elbow, suspect a theft. See a step in the long profile, suspect a comeback.

Two stories, two drawings, nine labels. That is the entire topic, and it is one of the most predictable sections in Paper 1. Go draw your crime scene — you've got this!
