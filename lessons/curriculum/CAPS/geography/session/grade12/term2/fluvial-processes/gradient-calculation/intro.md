Today we are mastering the average gradient calculation — the piece of mapwork that converts the appearance of a slope into a number an engineer, a farmer or an examiner can use. We will build it from the definition and the interpretation rule that decides half the follow-up marks, through harvesting the vertical interval and horizontal equivalent off a topographic sheet, into the line-by-line layout the marking memorandum rewards, and out to the verification checks and the traps that cost matrics marks every year. One worked problem carries the whole session: the gradient between trig beacon 251 at 1 200 metres and a spot height of 900 metres, 4,5 kilometres apart on a 1:50 000 map. These are the quickest ten marks on the paper once the method is automatic.
