Hey, it's Mandy — if the urban section feels like a wall of definitions, breathe. You live this topic. You have walked through most of its content in real life without knowing it had names.

Three anchors and the wall falls over. Anchor one: the definitions are all pairs, and each pair is one contrast. Urbanisation is the percentage; urban growth is the number. Level is the snapshot; rate is the speed. Expansion is spreading; sprawl is messy spreading. Learn the contrast, not the paragraph.

Anchor two: towns have job descriptions. Shop-keeper for the district — central place. Cargo changes vehicles — break-of-bulk, Durban. Routes meet — junction, De Aar. Border door — gateway, Musina. Mountain doorway — gap, Worcester. One example each is all the memo wants.

Anchor three: the taxi ride. Tall expensive middle — CBD. Scruffy changing ring — transition zone. Houses sorted by income, with townships pushed to the far edge by apartheid planning. Factories along the railways. A big mall node like Sandton near the highway. That one ride, replayed in your head, answers every "name the zone" question they can print.
