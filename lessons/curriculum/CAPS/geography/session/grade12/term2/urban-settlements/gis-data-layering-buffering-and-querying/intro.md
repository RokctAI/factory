Today we are turning the GIS block at the end of the mapwork paper from a mystery into a vocabulary, because those marks are surrendered every year by candidates who never learned the toolkit. We will build it in order: what a Geographical Information System is and the two kinds of data it stores, how information stacks into layers, what buffering and querying actually do, and how resolution and data manipulation questions are phrased in the examination. One running example carries the session — a town planner deciding where new houses may be built on the edge of a growing town, the exact scenario the examiners love because it forces every GIS tool to earn its keep.
