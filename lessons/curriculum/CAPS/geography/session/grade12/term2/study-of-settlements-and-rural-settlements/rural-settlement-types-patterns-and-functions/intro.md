Today we are building the toolkit that opens every settlement question in Paper 1: what a settlement is, where it sits, and why it took the shape it did. We will work through site and situation as the two location questions, the classification ladder from a single farmstead to a metropolis, the rural patterns and village shapes the examiners describe in words and expect you to name, and the land-use functions that explain why a rural settlement exists at all. The examples stay South African throughout — from the scattered homesteads of the KwaZulu-Natal hills and the lonely Karoo farmstead to the nucleated villages of the former homelands and the plantation towns of Mpumalanga.
