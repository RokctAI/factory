Hey, it's Bianca — last topic of the year, and it looks like a policy textbook: IRPs, carbon taxes, audits, transitions. Breathe out. This one is actually the friendliest topic in the term, because it is organised exactly like the exam question will be: one bucket, three spanners, one wave.

The bucket: South Africa's energy problem is a leaking bucket — plenty poured in, streams lost through draughty buildings, old bulbs, naked geysers, tired motors. Plugging holes beats building new dams: a power station costs hundreds of billions and takes a decade; efficiency costs a fraction and works this year. Memorise the champion sentence: the kilowatt-hour you never use is the cheapest and cleanest one there is — the invisible power station.

The three spanners, exactly as the syllabus assigns them. GOVERNMENT writes the rules: the IRP roadmap, renewable auctions, Africa's first carbon tax, building standards and appliance labels, solar water heaters for poor households, and the just transition money for coal towns. BUSINESS turns the biggest bolts: energy audits that find profitable savings, rooftop solar and wheeling, and cleaning up because foreign buyers now tax the smoke inside products — plus the warning: no greenwashing. INDIVIDUALS are the third spanner, sixty million strong.

And the wave: the evening peak is nothing but millions of kitchen choices landing together, so households move the grid like a stadium wave. Plays in order of size: geyser first — 60 degrees, blanket, timer, solar if possible — then LEDs, lids on pots, seal-and-jersey winters, kill the standby lights, share the lifts.

One bucket, three spanners, one wave, one champion sentence. Walk in with those and this topic is a gift. You've got this!
