If the development and resources half of the year feels like a wall of terms before the paper, breathe — it compresses into four pictures and one definition, and you can carry all five in one pocket.

Picture one: the scoreboard. Growth is total marks; development is whether the learners actually pass. Fix the score three ways: per capita, then the spread — Gini, ours among the world's highest — then follow the money into lives with the HDI's three dimensions: health, knowledge, standard of living. Core-periphery explains the map, Rostow gives the stages — and always quote his critics.

Picture two: the rigged shop. Raw exports buy fewer machines each year — the terms of trade. Escape by beneficiation: sell the jewellery, not just the gold. Aid gets argued both ways in one breath: lifesaving in emergencies, dependency when permanent — bakeries beat bread.

Picture three: the braai fire. Coal is four-fifths of the power: cheap, steady baseload, but carbon, dirty air, thirsty, and acid mine drainage. Koeberg is the steady low-carbon ember; our rivers are too small for big hydro; pumped storage is a dam used as a battery. The free fuels — Northern Cape sun, Cape wind — are now cheapest of all, but intermittent, and the coal towns need the just transition.

Picture four: the bank account. Soil deposits at a teaspoon a year, one storm withdraws by the bucket — splash, sheet, rill, donga. The fixes: cover, contour, terrace, rotate, carrying capacity, gabions.

And the definition that binds them, word for word: meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own. Live off the interest, never the capital. Four pictures, one rule — say them once and walk in steady. You've got this.
