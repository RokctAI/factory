Hey, it's Bianca — solar farms, CSP towers, cube laws, REIPPPP... the alphabet is back. Relax: this whole session is one idea with three machines and three problems, and you can hold all of it in one hand.

The one idea: the fuel is FREE. Sunshine and wind arrive every day at no charge — you only pay for the catching equipment. The catch inside the idea: free fuel keeps its own hours. Free but moody, versus coal's costly but steady — five words that contain the entire modern energy debate.

The three machines. PV panel: light straight to electricity, no moving parts, a silent windowpane — Upington and De Aar, and every load-shedding rooftop. CSP: a field of mirrors playing the magnifying-glass trick, cooking molten salt that keeps a thermos of heat — Redstone near Postmasburg makes supper-time power from lunchtime sun, twelve hours of storage. Wind turbine: a skyscraper pinwheel obeying the cube law — double the wind, eight times the power — at Jeffreys Bay, Cookhouse and Caledon. World name-drops: China builds the most, Denmark runs half its country on wind, Morocco's Noor lights cities after dark.

The three problems, honestly named: the WIRES — best sun and wind sit far from Gauteng and the lines are full, the real bottleneck; the NIGHT — covered by the mix, storage plus wind that blows while solar sleeps; and the COAL TOWNS — ninety thousand jobs needing a JUST transition: retraining, pensions, new industry, international money.

Verdict for any essay: direction settled — cleanest, cheapest, world-class resource; only the speed is argued, and speed depends on wires, storage and justice.

One idea, three machines, three problems, one verdict. Pocket-sized. You've got this!
