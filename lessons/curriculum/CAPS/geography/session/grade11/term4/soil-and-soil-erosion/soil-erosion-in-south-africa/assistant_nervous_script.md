Hey, it's Bianca — soil erosion looks like a topic with a hundred small facts: splash, sheet, rill, gully, gabions, resolutions. Breathe. It is actually one story told three times, and you already know the plot.

The story: soil fills like a bank account at a teaspoon a year, and one storm on bare ground withdraws by the bucket. Everything else follows from that one unfair exchange rate. Why is ground bare? Because something fired the bodyguard — overgrazing, overcultivation, deforestation, trampling — and in South Africa, apartheid's homeland map crowded too many hooves onto too little land, which is why the deepest dongas run through the Eastern Cape. Causes sorted: human, animal, physical, past and present, all in one sentence.

Then the water plot, five acts in order: splash, sheet, rill, donga — with the compacted footpath as the secret first drain. Say the sequence in order and the definition marks are banked.

Fixes come in two piles. PREVENT: keep ground covered — mulch, stubble, cover crops — plough along contours, rotate crops, match herds to carrying capacity. CONTROL: gabions as speed bumps in the gully, replant, fence and rest. Prevention beats cure, and cover beats everything, because it breaks the chain at splash — the first link.

GIS terms, one hook each: spatially referenced means pinned to coordinates; SPATIAL resolution is pixel size, SPECTRAL is which light bands — infrared exposes bare ground; and features store as dot, route, shape — point, line, area.

Teaspoons and buckets, five acts, two piles of fixes, four GIS hooks. That is the whole session. You've got this!
