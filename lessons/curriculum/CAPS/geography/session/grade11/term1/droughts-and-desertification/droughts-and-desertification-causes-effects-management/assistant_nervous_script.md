Hey, it's Bianca — droughts and desertification can feel heavy: four types of drought, a dozen causes, effects everywhere, and a pile of programmes with official names. Take a breath. This whole topic is really just two stories, and you already know both.

Story one is the bank account. Rain is the salary; drought is the salary dropping far below what that place normally lives on — always measured against the LOCAL average, that word carries a mark on its own. Then the failure runs downhill in a fixed order: atmosphere, soil, storage, society — meteorological, agricultural, hydrological, socio-economic. Follow the water down and the order rebuilds itself; you never have to memorise it cold.

Story two is the shaved scalp. Overgrazing, over-ploughing, tree-cutting and salty irrigation shave the vegetation off fragile land; the bare soil bakes, rain bounces off, dongas tear open, wind lifts the dust, and less grass grows back each round. That spiral is desertification — human-made, self-feeding, and separate from drought even though each makes the other worse. Write those two definitions side by side once tonight and they will never blur again.

For the effects and fixes, carry three names into the exam like tools in a pencil case: Day Zero for how a city cuts demand in half, Working for Water for clearing thirsty alien plants while creating jobs, and the Great Green Wall for the Sahel's village-by-village re-greening. Each name with one number or one detail attached is a top-level answer.

Two stories, one order that follows the water, three named tools. That is the entire topic. You've got this!
