Hey, it's Bianca — I know this one looks like the whole universe arrived at once. Angles, tilts, solstices, four ways heat moves, a global budget. Take a breath, because underneath all of that there are only four real moves, and you already understand three of them from everyday life.

Move one: the sun shines equally, but the earth is round, so tropical light lands concentrated and polar light lands smeared. Torch on a wall — straight beam gives a tight circle, tilted beam gives a big dim oval. That single image answers most of the latitude questions on the paper.

Move two: the earth leans 23,5 degrees and never straightens. When our end leans toward the sun it is December; when it leans away it is June. And whenever a question asks why we have seasons, write "axial tilt", never "distance" — we are actually closest to the sun in January, in the middle of our summer. That is a free mark most of the class throws away.

Move three: heat travels in four named ways, and a braai shows you all four. Warmth crossing the gap to your palms is radiation. The hot grid handle is conduction. Smoke rising is convection. Wind blowing that smoke sideways is advection. Sweat cooling you is latent heat. Learn the braai, and you never have to memorise the definitions cold.

The habit that protects your marks: whenever you meet a number in this topic — 23,5 degrees, 1 367 watts per square metre, 40 degrees for the surplus edge, 2,5 megajoules per kilogram — write the UNIT next to it every single time. Naked numbers lose marks in Geography, and the altitude calculation only works if you keep track of which way you are adding or subtracting the declination.

Four moves, one braai, one torch. That is the whole session, and everything else this term is built on top of it. You've got this!
