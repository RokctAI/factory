Today we go underground, to the massive igneous rocks — magma that pushed into the crust, cooled into batholiths, laccoliths, lopoliths, dykes and sills, and now stands exposed as some of South Africa's most distinctive scenery. One test sorts the whole family for the exam: does the intrusion lie with the strata or cut across them — concordant or discordant. We then watch erosion turn each buried body into a landform, follow pressure release and exfoliation as they peel granite into the smooth domes of Paarl, and finish with the two-stage story of tors, the balanced boulder stacks born underground. From the Bushveld Complex's platinum to the koppies of the Johannesburg Dome, this is the geology the country is literally built on.
