Hey, it's Bianca — geomorphology term has arrived and the word list looks scary: strata, differential erosion, scarp retreat, pediplain. Relax. This whole topic is one birthday cake, and you have seen the real thing from a car window a hundred times.

The cake: soft sponge layers lying flat, hard icing on top. Sponge is shale, icing is dolerite. Weather eats sponge first, always — that is differential erosion, the only process rule in the topic. Where the icing holds, the top stays flat. Where it is breached, the hill rounds off. If you can say "hard cap protects soft layers", you already own half the marks.

The landforms are just the cake at five ages, and the shapes give you the names: table-land, table, stool, traffic cone, flat. Plateau, mesa, butte, conical hill, plain. Mesa wider than tall, butte taller than wide, cone once the cap is gone. Say the countdown out loud twice and it is yours for life.

The process question is the cliff that marches backwards: frost pops blocks off the face, storms gut the soft rock underneath, the cliff rebuilds itself further back at the same angle. Scarp retreat, back wasting — sideways, never downwards, because the cap won't let the summit drop. Each retreating cliff leaves its smooth apron, the pediment, and when the aprons meet you get the great Karoo pediplain with the last buttes standing on it.

For mapwork, two habits protect you: convert the vertical scale to a ratio before dividing — 1 cm to 20 m is 1:2 000, so exaggeration is 50 000 over 2 000, 25 times — and always write gradient as 1 in something. Show the working every time; the working carries the marks.

One cake, five ages, one backwards-marching cliff, two calculations. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
