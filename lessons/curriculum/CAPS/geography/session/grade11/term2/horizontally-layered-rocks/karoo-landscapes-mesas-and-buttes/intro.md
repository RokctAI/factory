Today we are reading landscapes built from horizontal layers of rock — the flat-lying Karoo strata that give South Africa its tabletop hills, and the basalt walls of the Drakensberg. The whole story turns on one rule, differential erosion: soft layers vanish while a hard dolerite cap protects everything beneath it. We follow the landform family from plateau through mesa and butte to conical hill, watch scarps retreat backwards to build the great pediplain, and finish with canyons and the mapwork calculations — vertical exaggeration and gradient — that examiners set on this terrain every year. Learn this session well and every flat-topped koppie you pass becomes a hundred million years of history you can read at a glance.
