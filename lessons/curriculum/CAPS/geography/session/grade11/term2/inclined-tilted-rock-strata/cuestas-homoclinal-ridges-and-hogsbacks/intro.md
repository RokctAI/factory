Today we tilt the rock layers and watch a whole new family of landforms appear: the cuesta, the homoclinal ridge and the hogsback — one ridge at three dip angles. Everything hangs on a single distinction the examiners test relentlessly: the gentle dip slope follows one resistant layer, while the steep scarp slope cuts across the ends of them all. We wrap the same geometry into circles for cuesta basins and domes, with the Magaliesberg and the Vredefort Dome as our home examples, then finish on the map — reading asymmetric contour spacing and calculating gradient on both sides of a ridge to prove which way the strata dip. Master the tilted file and every ridge you drive past introduces itself by name.
