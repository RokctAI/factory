Today we confront why development is so hard to achieve — and the examiner's key word is interrelated: resources, energy, history, trade, population, education and environment come pre-tangled, each factor pulling the others. We then narrow to South Africa's particular burdens — the apartheid spatial legacy, a Gini coefficient near 0,63 and unemployment above thirty per cent — always holding the balance between real post-1994 gains and stubborn strain. Community-based development in rural and urban settings shows what stoep-level projects like Working for Water can and cannot fix, before we close on development's environmental bill and the case for sustainability. Essays on this topic reward the chain of causes, the named case study and the honest limitation — all three are built in this session.
