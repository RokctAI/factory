Hey, it's Bianca — this topic can feel heavy: unemployment, inequality, pollution, history. Take a breath. For the exam it folds into three pictures, and you already own all three.

Picture one: the earphone knot. The causes of poverty come pre-tangled — resources, energy, history, trade, population, education, environment — and pulling one wire tightens another. Never explain one factor alone; write the chain. Drought pulls a child from school, no matric means no job, no income overworks the land, and the next drought bites deeper. Use the exact word INTERRELATED and the marker smiles.

Picture two: the stoep. Community-based development means people plan it, build it and own it — because people maintain what is theirs. Have two names ready: Working for Water, clearing thirsty alien trees for wages and streams; and the Khayelitsha upgrading, where residents co-designed the lit walkways they actually use. Then the honest sentence for the evaluation mark: powerful but small — community hands and state muscle, both.

Picture three: the roof beams. Development that destroys its environment is burning the roof to warm the room — a loan from tomorrow, and the bill lands on the poor first. Acid mine drainage, the Highveld's air, silted dams. But remember the flip side: poverty strips environments too — firewood, overstocking. Close the circle, then land on the term that resolves it: sustainable development.

And for South Africa's own challenges, keep the balance: real gains since 1994 — houses, electricity, grants — against stubborn unemployment above 30 per cent and a Gini near 0,63. Both halves, always.

Knot, stoep, roof beams. Three pictures and the balance sentence — that is the whole session. You've got this!
