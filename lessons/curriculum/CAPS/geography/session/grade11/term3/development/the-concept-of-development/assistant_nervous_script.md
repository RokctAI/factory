Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic feels like alphabet soup. GDP, GNP, HDI, Gini, MEDC, LEDC, NIC. Breathe. Underneath the letters there are only three questions, and you ask them in the same order every time.

Question one: how much does the country produce? That is GDP — inside the borders — or GNP if you follow the owners' nationality instead. Divide by the population and you get per capita, which just means per person. One test case unlocks it forever: the Toyota plant in Durban is South Africa's GDP but Japan's GNP. Where the work happens versus whose company it is.

Question two: how is it shared? That is the Gini coefficient — 0 means everyone equal, 1 means one person owns the lot, and South Africa's 0,63 is the number to memorise, because it explains how a middle-income country can hold so much poverty.

Question three: what are people's lives actually like? That is the HDI — health, education and income averaged into one score out of 1. South Africa sits around 0,71. And the sharpest little indicator of all is infant mortality, because keeping babies alive needs the whole system working at once.

The habit that protects your marks: never argue development with feelings — argue it with indicators. When the essay asks whether South Africa is developed, quote the numbers, show both faces of the country, and conclude "a developing NIC with pockets of both". That balanced sentence is where the top marks live.

Three questions: how much, how shared, how lived. Everything else in this topic hangs off those hooks. You've got this!
