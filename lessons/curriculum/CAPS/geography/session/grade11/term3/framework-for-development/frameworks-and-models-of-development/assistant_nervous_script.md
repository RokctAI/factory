Hey, it's Bianca — two models, five aspects, a pile of new words. It looks like a lot, but this whole session is really just two pictures, and once you can draw them you can answer anything.

Picture one: a magnet on a table. The magnet is the core — Gauteng, the city centre, the rich North. The filings sliding inward are the backwash: the periphery's people, money and minerals draining toward the centre, making the magnet stronger. The little trickles flowing back out — remittances, branch factories, government projects like Coega — are the spread effects. And the magic move is zoom: draw the same magnet at world scale, country scale and city scale, and you have a nine-mark answer from one image.

Picture two: an aeroplane. Parked is traditional society. Ground crew building railways and banks — preconditions. Take-off — factories multiplying, investment past 10 per cent, growth lifting itself. Climb-out is maturity, cruising is mass consumption. Five stages, in order, every time.

Then the marks that separate the top answers: criticise. Rostow assumes one Western runway for everyone, forgets colonialism fuelled some flights by draining others, and parks the plane at a shopping mall with no thought for the planet. Core-periphery, read literally, offers no escape — yet South Korea escaped and Gauteng was once bare veld, so escapes can be engineered.

And keep one sentence word-perfect for sustainability: development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Free marks, every time it appears.

One magnet, one aeroplane, one memorised sentence. That is the entire session. You've got this!
