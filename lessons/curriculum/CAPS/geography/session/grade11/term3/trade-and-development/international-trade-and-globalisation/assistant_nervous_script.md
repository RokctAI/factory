Hey, it's Bianca — trade brings a wall of terms: balance of trade, terms of trade, TNCs, blocs, AGOA, globalisation. Don't panic. The whole session runs on three street-level pictures, and each one carries its own exam terms.

Picture one: the spaza and the supermarket. The spaza sells cheap raw stuff and buys expensive processed stuff — that is a country exporting ore and importing machinery. The lesson in it: the money lives in the PROCESSING, which is why beneficiation and our car factories matter so much.

Picture two: the swap rate. One bag of oranges buys a phone this year; three bags next year. That is TERMS OF TRADE deteriorating, and it happens to raw sellers because phones have patents and brands while anyone can grow oranges. When the exam says trade imbalance, that swap rate is the mechanism to describe.

Picture three: the ladder. South Korea climbed from T-shirts to steel to ships to cars to chips, selling to the world at every rung and banking the earnings into schools. Export-led development, proven real. But always add the warnings: the ladder needs open markets at the top, the bottom rungs are brutal, shocks travel down it, and South Africa climbs with a backpack — load-shedding, distance, skills.

For globalisation questions, keep two columns in your head: gains — investment, jobs, technology, cheaper goods; costs — profits flowing out, the race to the bottom, crushed infant industries like our textiles, and shocks like 2008 arriving uninvited. Two columns, then a verdict: the engine pulls unevenly.

Spaza, swap rate, ladder, two columns. Four hooks and every question in this topic hangs on one of them. You've got this!
