Today we close the development topic with its hardest question: when richer countries send money, goods and expertise to poorer ones, does it actually help? We define development aid and the newer standard of development co-operation, sort aid into its three types — technical, conditional and humanitarian — and then weigh the evidence on both pans of the scale, because the syllabus demands positive and negative cases argued with specifics, from the eradication of smallpox and South Africa's ARV programme to dependency, distortion and the debt trap. The essay-winning verdict is that aid is scaffolding — essential during construction, never mistaken for the house. Build that argument here and the whole development section closes answered like a geographer.
