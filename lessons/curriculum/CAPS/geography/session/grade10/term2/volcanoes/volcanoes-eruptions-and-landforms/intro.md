Today we are studying volcanoes — from magma forming deep underground to the landforms an eruption leaves behind. The single property of viscosity decides why one volcano oozes gentle rivers of lava while another blows its own summit apart, and from it follow the shield, composite, cinder and dome shapes the exam asks you to match to their magma. South Africa has no active volcano on the mainland, yet the Drakensberg's stacked basalt, every dolerite-capped Karoo koppie and the diamond pipes of Kimberley all belong to this topic. We close by weighing the hazards against the fertile soils, geothermal power and minerals that keep millions living on volcanic slopes — and the monitoring that keeps them alive.
