Hey, it's Thandi — plate tectonics arrives with a pile of long words on day one: Pangaea, Gondwana, asthenosphere, palaeomagnetism, subduction. It looks like the biggest topic of the term. It isn't. It's four evidence types and three boundaries, and everything else is decoration.

Start with the evidence, because it's the easiest marks in the topic. Four words: FIT, FOSSILS, ROCKS, ICE. Coastlines fit; fossils match across oceans; the Cape Fold Mountains carry on in Argentina; the Dwyka tillite says ice sat on the Karoo. One example each and you have the whole question.

Then the boundaries — and here's the shortcut that saves you every time. Ask only one thing: are they moving APART, TOGETHER, or PAST each other? Apart makes new crust: ridges under the sea, rift valleys on land. Together destroys crust: trenches, fold mountains, volcanoes. Past makes nothing and destroys nothing: just a straight fault and a very bad earthquake. Three moves, three answers.

The trap everybody falls into is volcanoes. Divergent — yes, volcanoes. Ocean diving under land — yes, volcanoes. Two continents colliding — NO volcanoes, because nothing is sinking to melt. Transform — NO volcanoes, for the same reason. Say that trap out loud twice and you'll never lose the mark.

Habit that protects marks: whenever you name a boundary, name a real place with it. Divergent — East African Rift or Iceland. Ocean-continent — the Andes. Ocean-ocean — Japan. Continent-continent — the Himalayas. Transform — San Andreas. Examiners hand out marks for named examples, and you already know all five.

Four evidence words, three moves, five places. That's the whole lesson in your pocket. You've got this!
