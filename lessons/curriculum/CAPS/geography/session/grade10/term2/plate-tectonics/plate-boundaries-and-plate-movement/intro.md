Today we are studying plate tectonics — the framework on which folding, faulting, earthquakes and volcanoes all rest for the remainder of this term. We start with Wegener's four lines of evidence that the continents were once joined in Pangaea, from the coastline fit to the fossils and glacial rubble South Africa itself supplies. Then we find the engine he lacked — convection currents in the mantle, ridge push and slab pull, proven by the age and magnetic stripes of the sea floor — and finish with the three kinds of plate boundary and the trenches, rifts, island arcs and fold mountains each one manufactures.
