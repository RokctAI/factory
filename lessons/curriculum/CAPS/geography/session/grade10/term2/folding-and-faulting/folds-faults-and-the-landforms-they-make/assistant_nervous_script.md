Hey, it's Thandi — this topic throws a lot of vocabulary at you at once. Anticline, syncline, recumbent, overthrust, graben, horst, hanging wall, throw, heave. If it feels like a spelling test in a foreign language, that's a normal reaction, and the fix is smaller than you think.

Sort everything into two piles first. Pile one: rock BENT. That's folding, and it comes from compression — squeezing. Pile two: rock BROKE and then moved. That's faulting, and it can come from any of the three forces. Every term in the topic belongs to one pile or the other, and half your confusion disappears the moment you ask which pile a word is in.

Now the two words people mix up most. Anticline is the ARCH — think of the "A" as a little pointed roof. Syncline is the SAG — the "s" sound in sag and syncline. Say it once: A for arch, S for sag. That's it. You've now got the two most-asked terms in the topic and you didn't memorise a definition.

For faults, do not learn the pictures — learn the force. Tension pulls, so something drops: normal fault. Compression pushes, so something rides up: reverse fault. Shearing slides, so things shift sideways: shear fault. Pull-drop, push-up, slide-sideways. Then the landforms follow: middle bit drops between two faults, you get a rift valley; middle bit stands up, you get a block mountain.

The habit that protects marks: never answer with just a name. Every description question wants the same four moves in the same order — the force, what the rock did, the shape it made, the landform you see today. Practise that order on the Cape Fold Mountains and you can apply it to anything the paper throws at you.

Two piles, one A-for-arch, three forces, four moves. You've got this!
