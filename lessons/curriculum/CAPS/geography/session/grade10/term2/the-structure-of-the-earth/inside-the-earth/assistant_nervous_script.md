Hey, it's Thandi — discontinuities with impossible names, four layers, two kinds of crust, and waves you cannot see doing things you have to explain. I know it looks like a memory dump. It is not. There are really only three things to hold.

Thing one: the avocado. Skin, flesh, pip — crust, mantle, core. Add the two facts that everyone loses marks on. The mantle is SOLID rock, not a lake of lava; only its soft upper band oozes. And the inner core is solid even though it is the hottest place on earth, because pressure beats heat. If you write those two sentences correctly you are already ahead of most of the room.

Thing two: two kinds of crust, and the heavier one always dives. Continental is thick, light, granite-ish and ancient. Oceanic is thin, dense, basalt and young — nothing on the sea floor is older than about two hundred million years. When they meet, the dense one goes under. That single sentence explains subduction, trenches and half of next week's plate tectonics.

Thing three: how we know. This is the part learners skip and the part markers love. P-waves go through anything. S-waves die in liquid. Beyond a certain distance from an earthquake, S-waves stop arriving anywhere on the far side of the planet — and that silence is the proof that the outer core is molten.

The habit that protects your marks: never name a layer without giving its state and one reason. "The outer core" is nothing. "The outer core, liquid iron and nickel, whose circulation makes the earth's magnetic field" is a full answer. Same structure every time — name, depth, composition, state, feature.

And if the spelling of Mohorovičić frightens you, relax. Everyone calls it the Moho, and so can you.

Three things: avocado, two crusts, the silence. That is the session. You've got this!
