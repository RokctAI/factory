Today we are studying population distribution and density — the two terms geographers use to describe why people are spread so unevenly over the earth, and why confusing them costs marks in almost every exam. We will keep the pattern and the number strictly apart, work the density calculation with correct units, and see why an average of fifty people per square kilometre describes nowhere in South Africa. Then we apply the physical and human factors to our own map: the wet east against the empty west, four great urban clusters including a Gauteng built on gold rather than geography, and the crowded former homelands whose densities are history, not land.
