Hey, it's Thandi — population topics look easy until the exam gives you two words that sound the same and expects you to know which is which. If distribution and density blur together in your head, that is the single most common wobble in this whole section, and it is fixable in one sentence.

Density is a NUMBER. Distribution is a PICTURE. If the question wants people per square kilometre, that is density. If the question wants you to describe where people are — clustered here, empty there, strung along the coast — that is distribution. Read the question, decide which one it wants, and half the marks are already safe.

The calculation is genuinely easy, so do not lose marks on carelessness. People divided by area. Write the unit every time: people per square kilometre. A number floating on the page with no unit is a number the marker cannot reward.

For the long questions, use two headings and never mix them: PHYSICAL factors — relief, climate, water, soil, resources, vegetation and disease, accessibility. HUMAN factors — jobs, transport, services, politics and history, social ties. Examiners are literally counting whether you covered both sides, so a paragraph on each is worth more than a brilliant paragraph on one.

The habit that protects marks: for every factor you name, add a place. Dry and empty — the Karoo and Kalahari. Crowded because of gold — Gauteng. Crowded because of a harbour — Durban, Cape Town, Gqeberha. Crowded because of history — the former homeland areas of the Eastern Cape and Limpopo. Four factors, four places, and you can build a full answer out of them.

Number versus picture, two headings, a place for every factor. That is the whole topic in your pocket. You've got this!
