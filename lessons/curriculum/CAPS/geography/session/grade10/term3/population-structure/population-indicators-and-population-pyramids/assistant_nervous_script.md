Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels like a spreadsheet attacked a graph, take a breath — population indicators and pyramids reduce to five numbers and three shapes, and every exam question is built from those eight pieces.

The five numbers, in plain words. Birth rate: births per thousand people per year. Death rate: deaths per thousand. Birth minus death gives natural increase — the growth speed before migration. Fertility rate: children per woman in a lifetime, and the only threshold you must memorise is 2,1, replacement level. Infant mortality: babies per thousand births who die before turning one. Life expectancy: years a newborn can expect. Say each one WITH its unit and the definition marks are yours.

The three shapes, in plain words. Wide base tapering to a point: expansive — many babies, fast growth, think Niger. Near-vertical tower: stationary — births balance deaths, think Sweden. Pinched at the bottom, fat in the middle: constrictive — below-replacement fertility, ageing, think Japan. South Africa: transitional, between the first two, with a young-adult bulge.

The trap that catches the most people: a high death rate does not mean bad healthcare. Germany's death rate beats Kenya's because Germany is old and Kenya is young. Whenever a rate looks strange, check the age structure before you answer.

For pyramid questions, read like a detective, bottom to top: base width for the birth rate, side steepness for survival, notches for disasters, bulges for booms, the longer female bars at the top. One observation plus one reason per sentence and the marker can only tick.

Five numbers, three shapes, one trap, one reading order. That is the whole topic, and you can hold all of it in one hand. You've got this.
