Today we are tracing world population growth — the J-curve that took ten thousand years to reach one billion people and barely two centuries to reach eight billion — and then confronting the word examiners phrase carefully: overpopulation. You will learn to calculate natural increase and doubling time with the rule of 70, and to argue why the crowded Netherlands is not overpopulated while parts of the near-empty Sahel can be. We finish with the strategies that genuinely slow growth, from the education of girls to the cautionary tale of China's one-child policy, with South Africa's own falling growth rate as the running example. This is a favourite source-based question, so the definitions and calculations here are marks waiting to be collected.
