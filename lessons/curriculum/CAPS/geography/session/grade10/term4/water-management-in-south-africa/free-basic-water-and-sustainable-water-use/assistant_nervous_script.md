Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels like a government report you have to swallow whole, breathe — it is really four small stories, and each one carries its own numbers with it.

Story one, the promise: Section 27 of the constitution, right of access to sufficient water. The policy number: 6 000 litres free per poor household per month — 25 litres per person per day for a household of eight, within 200 metres of home. And the clever pricing: rising block tariffs, where the first block is free and heavy users pay steeply — the pool subsidising the standpipe. One section, three numbers, one phrase.

Story two, why it is hard: split rural from urban and the marks organise themselves. Rural: scattered homes, long pipes, broken pumps, women and children carrying the failure on foot. Urban: informal settlements outgrowing the pipes, households that cannot pay, and the killer figure — a third or more of municipal water lost to leaks and unbilled use.

Story three, the big engineering: Lesotho Highlands — high rain, high mountains, water falling by GRAVITY through tunnels to the Vaal, royalties for Lesotho: Katse, Mohale, Polihali. Its opposite: Tugela-Vaal, PUMPING water up the escarpment into Sterkfontein, the drought reserve. Then the five cleaning steps in kitchen order — sieve, clump, settle, filter, chlorinate. Say the kitchen version in your head, write the technical words on the paper.

Story four, sustainability: two lists, never mixed. Government fixes leaks, polices polluters, clears alien trees with Working for Water, prices in blocks, tries desalination and reuse. Individuals fix drips, shorten showers, harvest rain, reuse greywater, plant indigenous. Proof it works: Cape Town 2018 halved its water and Day Zero never came.

Four stories, each with its own numbers. Tell them in order and the essay writes itself. You've got this.
