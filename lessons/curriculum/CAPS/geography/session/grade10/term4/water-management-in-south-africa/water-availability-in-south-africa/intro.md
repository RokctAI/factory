Today we are establishing why water dominates South African planning: a country among the thirty driest on earth, receiving about 465 millimetres of rain a year against the world's 860, and harvesting only nine per cent of it into rivers. We trace the east-west rainfall gradient, name the rivers from the Orange-Vaal system to the episodic beds of the Karoo, and survey the built answers — more than five hundred large dams — plus the groundwater that keeps the dry west alive. The session closes by assembling the full physical and human factor lists behind water availability, the exact structure the exam question rewards, and the foundation for every management debate in the rest of this term.
