Today we are examining how South Africa turns a constitutional right into water in the hand: the free basic water policy of six thousand litres per poor household per month, and why delivering it is so hard in both rural and urban communities. We then follow government's big engineering — the Lesotho Highlands gravity scheme, the pumped Tugela-Vaal, the dams and the five purification steps — before dividing sustainable water use into government's half and the individual's half. Cape Town's Day Zero near-miss of 2018 closes the session as proof that in the thirtieth driest country on earth, management is not optional; this whole chain, from Section 27 to the rising block tariff, is prime essay territory.
