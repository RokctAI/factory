**Thandi:** A few questions that come up a lot on this topic. First: if the constitution promises water to everyone, why is anyone in South Africa still fetching it from a river?

**Tutor:** Because a right creates an obligation, not a pipeline — and the distance between the two is exactly what this topic studies. Section 27 obliges the state to take reasonable measures, within its available resources, to achieve access to sufficient water progressively; the free basic water policy converts that into six kilolitres per poor household per month, within 200 metres of home. Delivery has genuinely transformed since 1994 — basic access now reaches almost nine households in ten — but the last stretch is the hardest, for reasons the lesson separates by geography. The unserved rural areas are typically the dispersed former-homeland settlements where pipes must cross huge distances to reach small clusters of homes, at a cost per household many times a suburb's, using sources that fail in drought. And access achieved once is not achieved forever: pumps break, municipalities without engineers or revenue cannot repair them, and a village with a certified scheme returns to the river while the statistics still count it as served. So the honest exam answer holds both truths — a massive, real expansion of delivery, and a stubborn remainder explained by dispersal costs, weak municipal capacity and maintenance failure, with the burden of every failure carried in litres, on foot, mostly by women and children.

**Thandi:** Next: the two big transfer schemes confuse people. Why does the Lesotho water flow by itself while the Thukela water must be pumped?

**Tutor:** Altitude decides everything, and holding the country's shape in your mind settles it permanently. Gauteng sits on the highveld plateau, roughly 1 700 metres up. The Lesotho highlands stand far above that — the Maluti peaks exceed 3 000 metres — so water captured at Katse and Mohale starts HIGHER than its destination. Send it through tunnels bored beneath the mountains and physics does the freight: it descends the whole way into the headwaters feeding the Vaal system, no pumping, no power bill. That is why the Lesotho Highlands Water Project is the flagship — the region's heaviest precipitation, captured at the region's greatest height, delivered downhill to the economy that needs it, while Lesotho earns royalties among its largest revenue sources plus hydro-electricity generated by the very descent. The Thukela has the opposite geometry: plenty of water, but flowing at the BOTTOM of the escarpment, below the plateau. Every litre sent to Gauteng must be lifted up that wall into Sterkfontein Dam, and lifting water costs electricity in proportion to the height — which is why the Tugela-Vaal scheme runs as insurance rather than routine: Sterkfontein is kept full as the drought reserve, drawn on when the cheaper sources fall short. One sentence captures both schemes for the exam: water is moved by gravity where the map allows it, and by expensive pumping only where it does not.

**Thandi:** This one is asked every year: do we really have to memorise the five purification steps in order, and is there logic to the order?

**Tutor:** Learn the logic and the order memorises itself, because each step removes what the previous step cannot. Raw water arrives carrying three kinds of passenger: large debris, fine suspended particles, and microorganisms. Screening comes first because it is the coarse work — metal grids catch sticks, leaves and litter that would clog everything downstream. But mud does not screen out; its particles are too fine and too light to sink on their own. So coagulation and flocculation come second: a chemical such as aluminium sulphate makes the fine particles cling together into heavier clumps called flocs. Sedimentation is third because it only works AFTER flocculation — now that the particles are heavy, still settling tanks let them sink, and clear water is drawn off the top. Filtration is fourth as the polishing pass: percolating through sand and gravel traps the last fine particles that escaped settling. And disinfection is deliberately last — chlorine, sometimes with ozone or ultraviolet light, kills the surviving bacteria and viruses, and doing it after the dirt is removed means the chlorine attacks microbes rather than being wasted on mud, with a residual left in the pipes to protect the water on its journey. Coarse to fine to living: sieve, clump, settle, polish, kill. Reconstruct that chain of reasons in the exam and the sequence can never desert you.

**Thandi:** Last one: for the sustainable use essay, how do we avoid just writing shower quicker ten different ways?

**Tutor:** Structure the answer around role-players and mechanisms, which is exactly how the memorandum allocates its marks. Open by defining sustainability — meeting present needs without crippling the future supply — then build two labelled halves. Government's half should show RANGE across mechanisms: reducing losses, through leak repair, metering and pressure management, because non-revenue water of a third or more is the largest single waste in the system; protecting quality, by funding sewage works and enforcing the National Water Act, because polluted water is lost water; restoring supply, through Working for Water clearing alien invasives — always worth citing as the double win of runoff returned plus jobs created; managing demand, through rising block tariffs and staged restrictions; and diversifying sources — aquifer protection, desalination, treated wastewater reuse. The individual's half should also be sorted by mechanism rather than listed randomly: stopping losses, with the fixed drip and the reported street leak; reducing use, with short showers and closed taps; reusing, with greywater on the garden; substituting, with rain tanks and indigenous water-wise planting, watered in the evening so evaporation cannot steal it. Then close with the evaluative point that lifts the essay: Cape Town 2018, where consumption halved and Day Zero never arrived — proof that the two halves together found half a city's supply inside its habits, and that neither perfect pipes nor virtuous households achieves it alone.
