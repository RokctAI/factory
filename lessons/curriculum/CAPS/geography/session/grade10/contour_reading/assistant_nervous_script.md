Hi, it's Thandi — if all those brown squiggles still look like spilled noodles to you, don't worry. Every single geographer started exactly there.

Here's the good news: contour reading runs on ONE idea. Lines close together = steep. Lines far apart = gentle. Everything else — valleys, spurs, cliffs, hills — is just that idea wearing different outfits. And you don't have to memorise the outfits, because you can always cheat honestly: read the numbers on the lines and ask "which way is up?"

The V-thing for rivers? Just remember the V points to where the river comes FROM — the high ground. If you forget, check the contour numbers; water never flows uphill, so the heights sort out any confusion.

Mapwork is Question 3 on every paper till matric, so time spent here pays you back for years. Go try the practice questions now — and if you can, find any 1:50 000 map online and just stare at it for five minutes. I promise the mountains will start popping out of the paper. You've got this!
