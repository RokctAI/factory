Hey, Thandi here — that chart had a LOT going on: circles, feathers, triangles, isobars. If it felt like learning hieroglyphics, honestly, it kind of is — but it's a tiny alphabet, and you nearly know it already.

Count what you actually need: shading = cloud, top-left number = temperature, bottom number = dew point, feathered arrow = wind. Four symbols. Then two big ideas: highs sink and clear, lows rise and rain. And one storyline: warm moist air ahead of the cold front, cold showery clearing air behind it. That's the entire topic.

Best trick I can give you: the weather outside your window right now is on today's real synoptic chart. Look it up after the practice questions — decoding a station near your town makes the symbols stop being homework and start being actual news.

Now go do those practice questions while the symbols are fresh. Wrong answers are just symbols you haven't made friends with yet. You've got this!
