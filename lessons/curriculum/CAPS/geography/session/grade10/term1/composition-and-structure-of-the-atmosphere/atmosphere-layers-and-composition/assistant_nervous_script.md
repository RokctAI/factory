Hey, it's Thandi — four layers, three pauses, a handful of percentages and a whole chemistry story about chlorine. I know it reads like a lot of loose facts with nothing holding them together. There is something holding them together, and once you see it the topic shrinks fast.

The whole structure section runs on ONE question: where is the heater? Troposphere — heater on the floor, so it cools going up. Stratosphere — heater on the ceiling, because ozone sits up there catching ultraviolet, so it warms going up. Mesosphere — no heater, cools again, coldest place in the sky. Thermosphere — fierce heater, almost no air. Say "floor, ceiling, none, fierce" once and you can rebuild the whole graph from scratch, no memorising heights required.

For composition, learn four numbers and one phrase. Seventy-eight nitrogen, twenty-one oxygen, not-quite-one argon, and carbon dioxide as four parts in ten thousand. The phrase is "dry air" — because water vapour changes by the hour and gets left out of the recipe. Examiners love asking why.

The habit that protects marks: never name a layer without giving its reason. "The stratosphere warms with height" is half a mark. "The stratosphere warms with height because ozone there absorbs ultraviolet radiation and converts it to heat" is the full one. Same for ozone questions — write the layer, the cause and the consequence as three separate sentences, because that is usually how the marks are split.

And keep the two big problems apart, because this is the trap of the topic. Ozone hole: too much radiation getting IN through the stratosphere. Greenhouse effect: too much heat trapped DOWN in the troposphere. Different gases, different floor, different mechanism. Get that right and you will out-answer half the room.

Four floors, four numbers, one separation. That is the whole session in your pocket. You've got this!
