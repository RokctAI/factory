Hey, it's Thandi — insolation, albedo, terrestrial radiation, counter-radiation, lapse rates, four transfer processes and five factors. That is a wall of vocabulary, and I know it feels like the topic is mostly words you have to get exactly right or lose the mark.

Here is the thing that shrinks it. The whole session hangs on one sentence: the atmosphere is heated from below. The sun's energy mostly passes THROUGH the air, warms the ground, and the ground then heats the air. Once you hold that, the lapse rate stops being a number to memorise and becomes obvious, the greenhouse effect stops being magic, and convection makes sense on its own.

Only three real moves in the whole topic. Move one: the budget — thirty bounces off, twenty gets absorbed on the way in, fifty reaches the ground; short-wave in, long-wave out. Move two: the four ways heat travels — braai, pot handle, churning pap, sea breeze. Move three: the five factors that set a place's temperature — latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents, and cloud with aspect.

The habit that protects marks in this topic is refusing to answer with a name alone. "Because of latitude" earns almost nothing. "Because at high latitudes the rays strike obliquely, so the same beam is spread over a larger area and passes through more atmosphere" earns the lot. Every factor has a mechanism; write the mechanism, and let the name come along for the ride.

And two things you must keep straight because they get mixed up every single year. First, natural greenhouse effect versus enhanced greenhouse effect — one keeps us alive, one is our doing. Second, warm Agulhas down the EAST coast, cold Benguela up the WEST. Say those two aloud a couple of times and you have protected a whole cluster of marks.

Heated from below. Three moves. Always give the mechanism. You've got this!
