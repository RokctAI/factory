Today we are learning to read contour lines — the language a topographic map uses to describe the shape of the land, and the skill that opens Question 3 on both Geography papers every year. Working on a 1:50 000 extract with a 20 metre interval, we will build from what a contour line actually is, through the rule that line spacing reveals gradient, to the V-shaped signature that separates a river valley from a spur. By the end you will read a whole landscape off the map and defend every claim with the spacing and the heights, exactly the way markers reward.
