Hey, it's Mandy — momentum is the very first topic of matric physics, and I know that makes it feel like a test of whether you belong in Grade 12. Breathe. This topic is two formulas and one habit, and the habit is worth more than the formulas.

The habit: before you touch any numbers, write "let east be positive" — or toward the wall, or the direction the first car moves, whatever fits — and then give every velocity its sign. Momentum is a vector. Almost every lost mark in this topic is a missing minus sign, not missing physics.

Formula one: F net delta t equals m delta v. Impulse equals change in momentum. Use it whenever ONE object speeds up, slows down or bounces, and remember delta v is final minus initial, with signs. A rebound always gives a bigger change than a dead stop — check your answer against that.

Formula two: total momentum before equals total momentum after. Use it whenever TWO objects collide, couple or spring apart, and the question says to ignore friction. Add the momenta with their signs, set the totals equal, solve for the unknown. If they stick together, the mass on the after side is the combined mass. That is the entire method.

Then the classification question, which is free marks if you stay calm: elastic or inelastic? Calculate total kinetic energy before, calculate it after, compare. Same number, elastic; smaller after, inelastic. Never answer from the story — answer from the two numbers, and say where the missing energy went: heat, sound, deformation.

And the safety explanation, three sentences, always the same: the change in momentum is fixed; the airbag or crumple zone or bent knees increase the time over which it happens; therefore the force is smaller. Learn it as a chant.

Signs stated, two formulas, an energy audit, three safety sentences. That is the whole topic, and you have done harder things than this. You've got this!
