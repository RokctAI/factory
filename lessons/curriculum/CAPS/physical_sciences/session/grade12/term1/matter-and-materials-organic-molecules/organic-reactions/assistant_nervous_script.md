Hey, it's Mandy — organic reactions feel like a zoo of arrows, but the whole section runs on one skill: reading conditions. The exam always prints the conditions, and the conditions always print the answer.

Sort any reaction by counting first. Two molecules becoming one, double bond vanishing: addition. One molecule becoming two, double bond appearing: elimination. Two in, two out, saturation untouched: substitution. Count in, count out, compare double bonds — that is the whole classification game.

Then confirm with the passwords. Catalyst and mild: addition — hydrogen with platinum or nickel, bromine on its own, water with an acid. Hot, concentrated, ethanolic sodium hydroxide: elimination, alkene coming. Dilute, aqueous, gently warmed: substitution, alcohol coming. Light on an alkane: substitution, and remember the hydrogen bromide walks away as the second product. Concentrated sulfuric acid with heat on an alcohol: dehydration to the alkene.

Two placement rules, one principle: in addition the incoming hydrogen joins the double-bond carbon that already has MORE hydrogens; in elimination the hydrogen leaves the neighbouring carbon with FEWER. Both build the more internal, more stable double bond — the rich get richer, and the crowd wins.

Esters are pure bookkeeping: alcohol part first ending in Y L, acid part second ending in O A T E, concentrated sulfuric acid as catalyst, water as the forgotten second product. Say all four pieces every time.

Combustion balances carbons, hydrogens, oxygens — in that order, always. Underline the conditions in every question before you write a word, and this section becomes the most predictable marks in Paper 2. You've got this!
