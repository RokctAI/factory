Naming molecules is grammar; making them react is the actual language, and the organic reactions section asks one question over and over: given this molecule and these conditions, what happens and why. Today we cover the full syllabus set — combustion and esterification first, then the three reaction types that dominate the marks: addition across double bonds, elimination to create them, and substitution to swap one group for another. The conditions decide everything: dilute, aqueous and mild favours the swap, while concentrated, ethanolic and hot favours the ejection. By the end of this session the conditions will decide it for you.
