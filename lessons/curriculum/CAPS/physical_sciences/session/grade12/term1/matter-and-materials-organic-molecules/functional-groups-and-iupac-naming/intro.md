Organic chemistry carries more marks than any other chemistry topic in the matric year, and every one of those marks begins with two skills: recognising which family a molecule belongs to, and naming it correctly. Today we build the vocabulary the definitions demand, tour the nine homologous series of the syllabus with their functional groups and suffixes, and set out the IUPAC naming rules as a fixed four-step procedure that works in both directions. We close with the three kinds of structural isomer — chain, positional and functional — and the bent-drawing trap that catches nearly everyone once. Structures are built carbon by carbon, with the four-bond rule as the free error-checker on everything.
