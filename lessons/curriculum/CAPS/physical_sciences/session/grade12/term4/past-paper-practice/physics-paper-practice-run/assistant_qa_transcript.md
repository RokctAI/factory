**Mandy:** Questions from the class about walking a whole paper! First: what does working at examination pace actually mean — is it simply working faster?

**Tutor:** No — it is working in a fixed rhythm so that speed takes care of itself. The rhythm has four beats, and this session drummed it on every question. Beat one: read and classify — name the topic and the tool before touching the calculator. Beat two: plan aloud — free-body diagram for a force question, sign convention for momentum, outside-in for a circuit. Beat three: execute in the memo's own structure — formula, substitution, answer with unit — because that structure is not bureaucracy, it is where the part-marks live; a candidate whose final answer is wrong but whose formula and substitution lines stand still collects most of the marks. Beat four: audit — one glance that costs seconds: branch currents summing to the main current, the approach frequency landing above the source frequency, a positive kinetic energy. Candidates who run out of room at the end of a paper are rarely slow at physics; they are paying for skipped classification with wrong-method detours that cost far more than the planning beat ever would. Rhythm first; pace follows.

***

**Mandy:** Next: the mark allocations you kept naming — formula, substitution, answer. How should a learner actually use mark values printed on a real paper?

**Tutor:** Read them as the examiner's confession of what the solution contains. Two marks on a definition means word-perfect recall, nothing to compute. Three marks on a calculation almost always decodes as formula, substitution, answer with unit — so write those three lines visibly, even when your calculator jumps straight to the number, because an unwritten formula is an unpaid mark. Four or five marks announces a multi-step problem: a resistance to collapse before a current, a photon energy before a kinetic energy — so a one-line answer to a five-mark question is a warning that a step was skipped. The allocations also settle the classic dilemma of a blank mind: a stated principle and a correctly substituted formula earn their marks even when the final arithmetic goes astray, which is why surrendering a question entirely is the only unpayable error. And keep the disciplined habits attached: units on every final line, direction on every vector, decimal comma answers rounded to two places. The marks are a map of the solution — navigate by them.

***

**Mandy:** The audits at the end of each walk — the books balancing, the frequency landing on the right side of 800. Are those worth the extra moments in a real examination?

**Tutor:** They are the cheapest insurance in the room, because each one is a single comparison, not a recalculation. The audits in this paper cost perhaps a breath each: parallel resistance smaller than the smallest branch; branch currents summing to the main current; terminal voltage below the emf while current flows; the approach frequency above the source and the recession below; kinetic energy positive in the photoelectric equation; the landing speed of the projectile matching its launch speed. Each check catches a specific, common error — an inverted parallel formula, a sign slip in the Doppler denominator, a subtraction reversed — and it catches the error while you can still repair it for full marks rather than after the paper is surrendered. There is a psychological dividend too: an audited answer closes the question in your mind, so the nerves do not drag it into the next one. Build the audit into the rhythm as its fourth beat and it stops feeling like extra work; it is simply how a question ends.

***

**Mandy:** Last one: with the whole syllabus walked, how should learners use this practice run in their remaining preparation days?

**Tutor:** Use it as a template, not a souvenir. Each of these seven walks is a pattern the real paper will re-dress in new numbers: the incline with friction, the coupling collision, the vertical throw, the energy-method drag, the passing siren, the network with internal resistance, the photon against the work function. So the drill is reproduction: close the lesson, take each question cold, and rebuild the full solution on paper — plan, marked steps, audit — then compare against the walk as if it were the memo. Where your version differs, you have found a genuine gap; where it matches, move on without rereading, because rereading feels like study and stores almost nothing. Then graduate to an untouched past paper and impose the same rhythm on it under quiet conditions. Keep a single page of personal audit findings — the errors YOUR hands favour — and review that page, not the whole syllabus, on the final evening. The paper is a set of patterns; make your practice the same set, and the examination becomes recognition rather than invention.
