Hey, it's Mandy — the Doppler effect is one equation. One! And you have heard the physics yourself every time an ambulance passed. If your ears already understand it, the marks are closer than you think.

The whole topic obeys a single sentence: moving toward each other, the detected frequency goes UP; moving apart, it goes DOWN. Write that at the top of your rough work. Every sign, every check, every explanation flows out of it.

The routine for calculations, four steps. One: decide from the story — closing in or pulling away? — and write "expect higher" or "expect lower". Two: place the players — listener's speed upstairs in the numerator, source's speed downstairs in the denominator; that placement never changes. Three: pick the plus or minus that makes your prediction come true, substitute with 340 for the speed of sound unless told otherwise, and calculate. Four: check the answer against your prediction. Higher when approaching, lower when receding — if it disagrees, you caught your own error, free of charge.

Reverse questions — given the heard frequency, find the speed — start with one earned mark: compare the two frequencies and SAY whether the source approaches or recedes. Then the same equation, rearranged.

For the explanation questions, keep two words apart: pitch is frequency and belongs to Doppler; loudness is amplitude and belongs to distance. And for the astronomy marks, one chain of three: spectral lines shifted toward the red, so galaxies are receding, so the universe is expanding. Farther galaxies, bigger shift. That chain, in that order, is the full answer.
