Hey, it's Mandy — the photoelectric effect is the shortest big topic in Paper 1: one equation, one story, and a set of concept questions that repeat almost word for word every year. This is a topic you can genuinely finish.

The story in one line: light arrives as photons, each carrying E equals h f, and one photon deals with one electron — the whole packet or nothing. The equation is the budget of that deal: photon energy equals work function plus maximum kinetic energy. Exit fee first, change as speed.

Calculations, three moves. If you are given wavelength, convert to frequency with c over lambda. Multiply by Planck's constant for the photon energy. Subtract the work function for E k max — and always CHECK first that the photon energy beats the work function; if it does not, write that no emission occurs, and take those marks with a smile.

The concept marks live on two dials that never touch each other's jobs. Intensity is photons per second: it sets HOW MANY electrons leave each second. Frequency is energy per photon: it sets WHETHER they leave and HOW FAST. Bright light below threshold does nothing — say it with confidence, because that is the particle-nature evidence they want.

Spectra in two sentences: electrons drop between fixed levels, emitting photons of fixed energies — bright lines. Cool gas steals the same energies from white light — dark lines, same positions, same element.

One equation, two dials, one barcode. Walk in, budget the photon, and collect. You've got this!
