Today we are tackling the photoelectric effect and atomic spectra — the experiment that broke classical physics, earned Einstein his Nobel Prize, and appears in Paper 1 every single year. Light of suitable frequency knocks electrons out of a metal, and the bookkeeping of one photon meeting one electron gives us the photoelectric equation and its calculations. The conceptual heart of the session is keeping intensity and frequency in their separate jobs — one counts photons, the other sizes them — because crossing those two dials is where the marks vanish. We finish with atomic spectra, where the same photon idea turns every element's light into a fingerprint sharp enough to name the elements in a star.
