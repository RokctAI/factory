Today we are studying electrolytic cells — chemistry that happens because you pay for it, with electrical energy forcing reactions that would never run on their own. We will contrast the electrolytic cell with the galvanic cell it reverses, then work the syllabus's model reaction, the decomposition of copper chloride, electrode by electrode. From there the same machinery earns its keep: electroplating a spoon with silver, refining copper to cable purity, and the electrolysis of concentrated brine that turns salt water into chlorine, hydrogen and caustic soda — an entire industry from the cheapest raw material on Earth. Hold one anchor throughout: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode, in every cell, every time.
