Hey, it's Mandy — electrochemistry looks like a chapter full of arrows and jargon, but the whole galvanic section stands on five sentences, and you are about to own all five.

One: oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain — OIL RIG, forever. Two: the anode is where oxidation happens, the cathode where reduction happens, in every cell ever built. Three: the agents cross over — the oxidising agent is itself reduced, the reducing agent is itself oxidised, because agents are named for what they do to the other substance. Four: electrons travel through the wire from anode to cathode, while the salt bridge completes the circuit AND keeps both beakers neutral — write both jobs, always, that is two marks. Five: emf equals cathode minus anode.

For the calculation, the layout saves you: label cathode and anode first — higher potential is the cathode — then substitute with brackets around every negative value before you touch the arithmetic. Cathode minus anode for zinc-copper is 0,34 minus bracket minus 0,76 bracket — 1,10 volts, positive, as a working cell must be. A negative answer just means your labels are swapped: relabel, redo, rescue the marks.

Table questions are one comparison: the higher half-reaction's oxidising agent takes electrons from the lower one's reducing agent. Say who grabs, say who gives, quote the gap.

And if they ask about a flat cell, smile and write: the reaction has reached equilibrium, so the emf is zero. Five sentences, one subtraction, one league table. Walk in and collect what is yours. You've got this!
