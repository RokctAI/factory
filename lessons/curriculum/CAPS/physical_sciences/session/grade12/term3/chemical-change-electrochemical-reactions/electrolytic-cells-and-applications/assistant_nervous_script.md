Hey, it's Mandy — electrolysis is the friendliest half of electrochemistry, because nothing here is spontaneous or sneaky: you can see the battery in the diagram, and the battery tells you everything.

Start every question the same way. Spot the power source — that confirms an electrolytic cell, electrical energy forced into chemistry. Then plant the two anchors that never move: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode, in every cell in the universe. In the electrolytic cell the anode is the POSITIVE electrode, and the ions behave like magnets should: anions to the anode, cations to the cathode. The names rhyme on purpose — use them.

Three applications carry all the marks, and each is one picture. Copper chloride: copper plates the cathode, chlorine bubbles at the anode, blue fades — two half-reactions, learn them as a pair. Electroplating: object at the cathode, bar of plating metal at the anode, bath of that metal's ions — and the bar refills the bath as it dissolves. Refining: impure slab dissolves, pure sheet grows, and the gold and silver drop off as sludge.

For brine, remember the auction: water outbids sodium at the cathode, so hydrogen forms, never sodium metal; concentrated chloride outbids water at the anode, so chlorine forms. Three products — chlorine, hydrogen, sodium hydroxide — and a use for each in your back pocket.

If polarity ever spins your head in the exam, breathe and ask one question: where are electrons being taken away? That is the anode, and the rest unfolds by itself. You've got this!
