Today we are opening electrochemistry with the galvanic cell — a chemical reaction split in half, with the electrons forced to travel through a wire to complete the trade, which is every battery ever made. We will sharpen the redox vocabulary until oxidation, reduction and the two agents are automatic, then build the zinc-copper cell piece by piece, from the electrodes to the salt bridge that keeps both beakers neutral. The table of standard electrode potentials follows, anchored on the hydrogen electrode's zero, and the session closes with cell notation and the emf calculation — cathode minus anode, brackets on the negatives — the most reliable marks in this section of Paper 2.
