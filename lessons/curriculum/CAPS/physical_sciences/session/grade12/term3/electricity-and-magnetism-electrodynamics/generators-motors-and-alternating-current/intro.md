Today we are mastering generators, motors and alternating current — one piece of physics read in two directions, and the reason almost every joule on South Africa's grid began as a spinning coil in a magnetic field. We will build the AC generator part by part, run the story backwards into the motor effect, then argue properly why the grid chose AC: transformers plus the I squared R transmission-loss case. The session closes with rms values, the honest DC-equivalent numbers that let a kettle on a swinging 311-volt wave be billed at a steady 220. Labelling the machine and dividing peaks by root two are marks the examiners hand out every single year.
