Hey, it's Mandy — circuits with internal resistance look like Grade 11 with a trap inside, but the trap is one small resistor hiding in the battery, and once you can see it, it stops catching you.

One equation runs the whole topic: emf equals I times R external plus I times r. Read it as a budget — the battery's promise, split between what the circuit receives and what the battery keeps as heat. Every question in this section is that budget in disguise.

The routine, four steps, every time. One: collapse the outside network — parallel clusters first, product over sum for pairs, then add along the series line. Two: solve the whole loop for the main current, internal resistance included. Three: walk back into the network, splitting voltage across series parts and current between parallel branches. Four: audit — branch currents must add up to the main current, and voltages around the loop must add up to the emf. If the books balance, your marks are safe.

For the explain questions, memorise one chain: parallel branch added — external resistance falls — current rises — lost volts rise — terminal voltage drops. Four links, in order, one mark each. And for the graph: intercept reads the emf, the size of the gradient reads the internal resistance.

Watch the units, keep your decimal commas tidy, and check that every parallel answer is smaller than its smallest branch. This topic rewards the careful, and you have just practised being exactly that. You've got this!
