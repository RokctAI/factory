Today we are answering the question Paper 2 opens its chemistry with: why do atoms bond at all, and what shapes do the resulting molecules take? We start with the energy valley that makes the covalent bond happen, build the Lewis diagrams for the molecules the syllabus names, and then let electron-pair repulsion give us every shape — linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, pyramidal and angular, with the shrinking bond angles the examiners love to compare. The session closes with electronegativity, the number that decides whether a bond shares fairly, unfairly, or gives up sharing altogether — and the crucial twist that a molecule full of polar bonds can still be non-polar if its shape cancels them. Every later chemistry topic stands on this one.
