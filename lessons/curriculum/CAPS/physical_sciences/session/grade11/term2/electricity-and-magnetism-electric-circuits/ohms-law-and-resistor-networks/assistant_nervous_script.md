Hey, it's Bianca — circuit diagrams can look like a plate of noodles: resistors everywhere, numbers everywhere, and one battery expecting you to sort it all out. Here is the secret the top marks already know: every circuit question in this paper is the same four-move walk, and the walk never changes.

Move one, shrink. Fold parallel pairs into single numbers — product over sum for two resistors — then add anything in series. Keep folding until the battery faces one resistance. Move two, total current: battery volts divided by that one resistance. Move three, spend the volts: walk the loop, giving each series resistor its share, current times resistance, and whatever remains lands across the parallel section — the same voltage for every branch in it. Move four, split the amps: each branch takes its shared voltage divided by its own resistance.

Then audit yourself, because the audits catch every slip. Branch currents must add up to the current that arrived. Voltages around the loop must add up to the battery. Parallel totals must come out SMALLER than the smallest branch — if yours did not, you forgot to flip the fraction at the end. Thirty seconds of checking turns a shaky answer into a safe one.

Keep the two sharing rules on a card in your head: series shares current, parallel shares voltage. Half the theory questions are that one sentence.

And for the money questions, three conversions before any multiplying: watts become kilowatts, minutes become hours, then kilowatts times hours times tariff. If a kettle costs more than a school uniform, a conversion went missing — go back one line.

Shrink, total, spend, split, audit. Say the five words at the top of every circuit question and write them as your headings. The circuit stops being noodles the moment you walk it in order. You've got this!
