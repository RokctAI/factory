Hey, it's Bianca — quantitative chemistry plus acids and bases in one revision sweep sounds like a lot, but the whole thing runs on three pictures you already understand from ordinary life.

Picture one: the currency exchange. Grams, solution volumes, gas volumes — none of them talk to each other directly. Everything exchanges through one currency, the mole, and the rates are printed on what you are given: molar mass from the periodic table, c equals n over V for solutions, 22,4 cubic decimetres per mole for any gas at STP. Into moles, do the chemistry, out of moles. That one discipline is most of Paper 2.

Picture two: the pancake recipe. Limiting reagent is not a monster — it is checking which ingredient runs out first. Pick a reactant, ask how much partner it demands through the balanced equation, compare with the shelf. Whichever runs short does ALL the talking afterwards, and the excess just sits there like spare flour.

Picture three: the rugby pass. Acid passes the proton, base receives it, and the conjugate pair is just the same player one ball lighter or heavier. Strong versus weak is how completely the ball is released; concentrated versus dilute is how many players are on the field. Keep those two axes apart and half the theory marks are already yours.

And the traps have names now: kelvin not Celsius, cubic decimetres not cubic centimetres, the ratio step in every titration, and never calculating from the excess reagent. You have run all of this before — this year, for marks. Revision is reminding your hands, not learning fresh. Open the practice set, name the picture, run the check, and stack the wins. You've got this!
