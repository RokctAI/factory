Hey, it's Bianca — a whole year of physics folded into one revision lesson looks like a mountain, so let me show you it is actually four small hills with good paths.

Hill one: forces. One method solves everything — dot on the page, honest arrows only, components if anything slants, F net equals m a along each axis. Most force marks are lost to smuggled arrows, not hard physics, so let the lonely dot keep you honest.

Hill two: the inverse-square twins. Gravitation and Coulomb are the same skeleton with different constants: multiply the two sources, divide by the distance SQUARED. Double the distance, quarter the force — that one reflex answers a whole family of questions before your calculator wakes up.

Hill three: circuits. Collapse, solve, expand. Fold the network into one resistor, pull the total current from V equals I R, then unfold and let the two audits certify you: branch currents sum at the junction, and the power ledger closes to the battery's output. When the ledger closes, you KNOW you are right — certainty you can carry to the next question.

Hill four: electromagnetism. Say the chain: currents make fields, fields count as flux, CHANGING flux makes emf. Faraday only pays for change — steady field, zero emf — and Lenz always pushes back.

You have climbed every one of these hills already, this year, for marks. Revision is just walking the paths again until your feet know them. Open the practice set, name the hill each question lives on, run its audit, and stack the small wins. You've got this!
