Hey, it's Bianca — energy diagrams, delta H, activation energy, activated complexes... it sounds like a physics topic that wandered into chemistry. Breathe. This whole topic is one picture, and once you can draw that picture, every question is just reading numbers off it.

The picture: two valleys and a ridge. Reactants live in the left valley, products in the right, and every reaction must climb the ridge between them. That is the entire theory. Now the three readings, always in the same order.

Reading one: compare the two valley floors. Right valley lower than left? The reaction pays out energy — exothermic, delta H negative. Right valley higher? The reaction absorbs — endothermic, delta H positive. Delta H is always valley-to-valley, and never, ever involves the peak.

Reading two: the climb from the LEFT valley floor to the top of the ridge. That is the activation energy — the entry fee the reaction must pay before anything happens. It exists for every reaction, even the downhill ones; that is why petrol needs a spark and a braai needs a match.

Reading three: the very top of the ridge is the activated complex — the unstable halfway arrangement, old bonds half broken, new bonds half formed. Label it on every diagram; the label itself earns marks.

Two guards for the exam. Guard one: attach the sign of delta H to the CHEMICALS, not to your hand — a hot flask means the chemicals lost energy, so negative. Guard two: when a catalyst enters the question, only one thing on your drawing moves — the peak drops. Both valleys stay put, so delta H is untouched. Faster, not more energy.

If a calculation appears, it is subtraction: peak minus start for activation energy, end minus start for delta H, and the reverse reaction just reads the same picture from the right-hand side.

One picture, three readings, two guards. Sketch the valleys before you answer anything and the topic does the work for you. You've got this!
