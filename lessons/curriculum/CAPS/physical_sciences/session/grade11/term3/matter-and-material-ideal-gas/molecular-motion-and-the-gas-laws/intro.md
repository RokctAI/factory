A gas is a crowd of molecules in ceaseless random motion, and everything a gas does — the pressure in a tyre, the swelling of a hot balloon, the hiss of a deodorant can — follows from that crowd's behaviour. Today we build the kinetic model of gases, define the ideal gas and the two conditions where real gases stray from it, and establish Boyle's law from the prescribed practical, constant product and all three graphs. We finish with the general gas equation and the ideal gas law, p V equals n R T — the single equation that contains the whole topic and counts invisible molecules with a pressure gauge and a thermometer. Kelvin discipline throughout, because every gas equation eats kelvin and nothing else.
