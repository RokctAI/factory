Hey, it's Bianca — gas laws, kinetic theory, two different equations with letters everywhere, and a lecture about "ideal" gases that apparently don't even exist. Deep breath. This whole topic is one picture and two equations, and you already own the picture.

The picture: a sealed room full of bouncy balls that never slow down. Pressure is the drumroll of balls hitting the walls. Temperature is how fast the crowd is moving on average. Every single theory question in this topic is answered by describing that room. Squeeze the room smaller: same balls hit the walls more often, pressure up — that is Boyle. Heat the room: faster balls hit harder, pressure up. There is no theory beyond the room.

Now the two equations, and the test that picks between them. Count the states in the question. Gas described BEFORE and AFTER a change — two states? Use p1 V1 over T1 equals p2 V2 over T2, and if temperature is constant it shrinks to Boyle's see-saw, p1 V1 equals p2 V2. Only ONE state, but the question asks how many moles or what mass? That is p V equals n R T, the equation with the amount in it.

Two unit tollgates, and they are where all the lost marks live. Tollgate one: kelvin, always — add 273 to every Celsius before it enters any equation. Write the conversion as its own line. Tollgate two, for p V equals n R T only: pascals and cubic metres — kilopascals times 1 000, cubic decimetres divided by 1 000. Three little conversion lines at the top of your answer, then the formula, then substitute.

Sanity check at the end: everyday pressures sit near 100 kilopascals. An answer wildly far from that is asking you to find a missing thousand.

One room, two equations, two tollgates. Read the question, count the states, convert the units, and the topic rolls over for you. You've got this!
