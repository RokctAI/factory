Today we promote the Grade 10 mole-ratio journey to full Grade 11 stoichiometry, with the three complications industry lives with daily: the reactant that runs out first, the product that falls short of the prediction, and the raw material that arrives less than pure. We work the limiting reagent test in full, then the percentage yield and percentage purity calculations, and finish inside a car airbag, where a soap-bar of sodium azide becomes sixty-seven cubic decimetres of nitrogen gas in milliseconds. One thread ties it all together: the balanced equation promises in moles, and every complication is measured against that promise.
