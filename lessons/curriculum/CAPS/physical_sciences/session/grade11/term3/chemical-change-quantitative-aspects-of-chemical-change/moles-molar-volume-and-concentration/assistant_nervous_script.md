Hey, it's Bianca — this topic looks like four different chapters stapled together: moles, gases, solutions, formulae. It is actually one idea wearing four outfits, and once you see the one idea, the whole thing folds flat.

The idea: the mole is just a counting box, and every question is asking how many boxes you have. That is all. The only thing that changes from question to question is which conversion gets you to boxes. Given a mass? Divide by molar mass. Given a gas volume at STP? Divide by 22,4. Given a particle count? Divide by 6,02 times 10 to the 23. Given a solution? Moles equal concentration times volume. Four doors, one room.

So here is your exam routine, and it never varies. Read the question and underline what you are GIVEN — mass, gas volume, particle count or solution. That tells you which door you enter through. Then underline what is ASKED — that tells you which door you leave through. Write the formula, substitute with units, answer. Two doors and a formula each time; you never need to hold the whole chapter in your head at once, just the two doors of this question.

Three small guards while you work. Guard one: build molar masses out loud, subscript by subscript — the three oxygens in CaCO3 are worth 48, and skipping one is the commonest slip in the paper. Guard two: volumes into cubic decimetres before anything else — 250 cubic centimetres is 0,25, and that conversion is worth writing as its own line. Guard three: 22,4 is for gases at STP only; never let it near a solid or a solution.

And if a formulae question appears, remember the pair: empirical is the simplified ratio, molecular is the real count, and the molar mass is the bridge between them.

One idea, four doors, three guards. Work slowly through the first two questions and the rest will feel like repetition, because it is. You've got this!
