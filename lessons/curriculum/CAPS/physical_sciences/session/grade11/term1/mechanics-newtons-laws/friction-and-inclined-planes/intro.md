Today we are pinning down the two forces that cause almost all the trouble in Grade 11 mechanics: the normal force, which is not always equal to the weight, and friction, which comes in a self-adjusting static kind and a constant kinetic kind. We will build the free-body diagram discipline the markers reward, work the classic does-it-move test on a level floor, and then rotate the whole problem onto an inclined plane, where the weight splits into a component along the slope and a component into the slope. The session ends with the full incline calculations — sliding, held, and pushed — plus the elegant tangent test that decides slipping without any mass at all. Master this and the rest of mechanics becomes bookkeeping.
