Hey, it's Bianca — Coulomb's law questions look busy: microcoulombs, centimetres, three charges, arrows everywhere. But strip the costume off and this is a formula you already know how to drive, because it is gravitation with the labels changed. Multiply the two quantities on top, divide by the distance squared. You have done this dance before.

The whole topic is three habits.

Habit one: convert before anything else. Micro means times 10 to the minus 6. Centimetres become metres. Do the conversions as line one of every solution, before the formula appears, so the numbers enter clean. Nearly every wrong answer in this topic died in the units, not in the physics.

Habit two: magnitudes in the formula, signs outside it. The formula gives you a SIZE — always positive. The plus and minus signs answer one question only: pull or push? Opposites pull, likes push. Write the size, then write the direction in words. When two or more forces act, draw an arrow for each one first, then add arrows that agree and subtract arrows that fight. Arrows before arithmetic, every single time.

Habit three: each pair keeps its own distance. When three charges sit in a line, the middle one feels its left neighbour at the left distance and its right neighbour at the right distance — two separate little calculations. Never let the far-end distance sneak into the wrong pair.

And if the two arrows are at right angles, do not panic and do not just add them — that is your vectors topic paying you a visit. Square, add, root for the size; inverse tangent for the angle. You practised that for weeks.

Convert, arrows, own distances. Three habits and the marks are yours. You've got this!
