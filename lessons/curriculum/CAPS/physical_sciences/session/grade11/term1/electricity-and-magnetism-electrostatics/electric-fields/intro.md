Today we are studying the electric field — the answer to how a charge pushes or pulls something it never touches. We will define the field in the exact wording the examiners mark, master the field-line patterns and their four rules, and then work the topic's two calculations: E equals F over q where a force is measured, and E equals k Q over r squared near a known source charge. The session closes by superimposing the fields of two charges at a point, where the arrow-first vector discipline from Coulomb's law earns its keep again. Get the direction rule right — the force on a positive test charge — and every arrow in this topic follows from it.
