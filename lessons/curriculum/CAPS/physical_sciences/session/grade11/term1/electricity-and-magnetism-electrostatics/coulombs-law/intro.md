Today we are working with Coulomb's law — the equation that turns "charges attract and repel" from Grade 10 into an exact number of newtons. Every electrostatics question in Paper 1 leans on it, and the marks are won or lost in the discipline: convert microcoulombs and centimetres first, substitute magnitudes only, and state the direction in words. We will drill the standard substitution, then build the two arrangements the exam loves — three charges in a straight line and three charges at a right angle — before finishing with touching spheres and the traps that catch unprepared candidates. By the end, forces between charges will add exactly like the vectors you have already mastered.
