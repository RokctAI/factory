Hey, it's Thandi — I remember when volts, amps, ohms, emf and terminal-whatever all sounded like the same word wearing different hats. If they're blurring for you right now, that's the normal starting point, not a warning sign.

Here's the trick that untangles it: every quantity answers exactly one question. Amps: how fast is charge passing? Volts: how much energy does each coulomb carry? Ohms: how hard is the wire fighting back? When a problem lands, first ask "which question is this?", and the right formula picks itself — I equals Q over delta t, V equals W over Q, R equals V over I. Three tiny formulas, that's the entire toolbox.

The meters trip everyone once, so let the rhyme carry you: current THROUGH, so ammeter in the loop; volts ACROSS, so voltmeter over the gap. Say it while you draw and the marks stay yours.

And keep the water loop in your head whenever things wobble — pump, pipes, clogs, flow. Every hard question turns friendly inside that picture. Now go do the practice set: name the question, pick the formula, mind your units, and watch how quickly this topic becomes free marks. You've got this!
