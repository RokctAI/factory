Hey, it's Thandi — after a year of formulas, a topic with almost none can feel weirdly slippery. Nothing to calculate, so what do they even ask? If that's the worry, relax: this is the topic where you get to sound like a scientist talking about the real world, and the marks are for clear thinking, not algebra.

Anchor yourself on three pictures. The bucket: ten litres of planet water, teaspoons actually drinkable — that one image answers every "why does water matter" question. The round trip: one drop going ocean, cloud, rain, rock, river, plant, person, sea — narrate it and you have covered evaporation, condensation, dissolving and transpiration in a single story. And the river with two banks: every dam question is benefits, costs, then a weighing sentence at the end. Never just one side.

The chemistry part is friendlier than it looks because you already know it: the ion tests are the same acid-silver-barium drill from aqueous solutions, just aimed at a water sample. And keep the purification order as a story — settle it, filter it, kill the microbes — plus the twist that dissolved salt survives everything except distillation.

Have a go at the practice questions and let yourself write full sentences — this topic rewards them. You've got this!
