Hey, it's Thandi — pendulums, roller coasters, ramps, vertical throws... it can feel like four different topics wearing one name. Deep breath: it is ONE equation applied four times, and you already met the equation last lesson.

Every single problem is the same ritual. Pick two points. Put zero height at the lower one. Count both energies at each point — write the zeros in, they're doing real work. Then solve for the one thing you don't know. Swing, slide, coaster, throw: the ritual never changes, only the scenery does.

Two little guardians will protect your marks. Guardian one: if the object is already moving at your first point, its kinetic energy goes in the sum — that sneaky 900 joules on the coaster catches everyone once. Let it catch you in practice, not in the exam. Guardian two: the words "ignore friction" are your permission slip; if a question says "rough", the equation is off the table and they want words, not numbers — energy decreased, it became heat.

The path-doesn't-matter idea feels wrong the first time, so test it yourself on the practice set: steep ramp, gentle ramp, same drop — watch the same speed appear. Once that clicks, this becomes the topic where you finish early and check twice. You've got this!
