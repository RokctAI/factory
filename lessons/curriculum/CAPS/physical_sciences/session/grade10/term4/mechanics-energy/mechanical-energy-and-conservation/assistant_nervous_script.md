Here's why: there is only ONE idea. The total stays the same. Everything else — the two formulas, the big equation — is just counting where that total is sitting right now. Height money or speed money, two pockets, one sealed wallet. You already do this kind of accounting with actual pocket money.

When you practise, start every problem the same way: pick your zero for height, count both energies at the starting point, and write the total down. That total is your anchor — every later position must add up to it, so your own working checks itself as you go. No memorising trajectories, no equations of motion, just one number that refuses to change.

And keep g at 9,8, square the speed, and don't lose the half in half m v squared. Those three habits carry basically all the marks. Now go run the practice set — count the wallet at the top, track the coins, and let the total do the heavy lifting. You've got this!
