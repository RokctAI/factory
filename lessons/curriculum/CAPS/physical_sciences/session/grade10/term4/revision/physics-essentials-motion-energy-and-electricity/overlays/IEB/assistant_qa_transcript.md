**Thandi:** Revision brings the same worries every year, so let's face them directly. First: three equations of motion, one question — how do we pick the right equation quickly, without trying all three?

**Tutor:** Let the MISSING quantity choose for you, because each equation omits exactly one of the movement quantities. Start every motion question by listing the knowns down the margin — initial velocity, acceleration, time, displacement, final velocity — marking what is given and what is wanted. Then read the gaps. Displacement absent from the question entirely? Use v equals u plus a t. Final velocity absent? Use delta x equals u t plus a half a t squared. Time absent? Use v squared equals u squared plus 2 a delta x. The selection is mechanical — no judgement, no guessing. The margin list also blocks the two classic substitution slips: "pulls away from rest" means u is zero and must be written as zero, and whichever direction you declare positive at the start binds every quantity to the end. Learners who bleed motion marks are rarely weak at algebra — they substitute before listing, pick the wrong equation, and pay triple the time the list would have cost. Thirty seconds of margin discipline buys the whole question.

***

**Thandi:** Next, the energy ledger: during a fall the potential energy keeps changing and the kinetic energy keeps changing — so what exactly are we allowed to trust?

**Tutor:** Trust the sum, and nothing but the sum — that is the entire principle in one sentence. At every instant of a frictionless fall, the joules leaving the height account arrive in the speed account simultaneously, so the mechanical total is one fixed number that you compute ONCE, at whichever position makes it easiest. That is why the method always opens at the top: a 5-kilogram ball at rest 8 metres up holds 392 joules, and that number is locked for the whole journey. Every later question — kinetic energy at 4 metres, speed at the ground — is answered by asking where the 392 joules are currently sitting, never by starting fresh. And guard the licence: the phrase "ignore air resistance" is what seals the total. If a question declares a rough surface instead, the mechanical total is no longer constant — the correct statement becomes that mechanical energy decreased, the difference transferring to heat, while total energy stayed conserved. Reading for that licence phrase, and quoting it back, is reliably worth a mark. Sum sealed, licence checked: those two habits are the whole energy toolkit.

***

**Thandi:** The circuit rules are mirrored pairs, and under pressure they swap themselves. Same current or same potential difference — how do we keep series and parallel permanently straight?

**Tutor:** Anchor each rule to its physical REASON, because the reasons are not symmetric and cannot swap. Series is one unbroken loop: charge has a single path, cannot pile up anywhere and cannot escape, so the current — the flow rate — must be identical at every point of the loop; what gets divided is the battery's energy, so the potential differences add up to the emf. Parallel is a junction: each branch connects directly across the battery's terminals, so each branch must feel the full emf — same potential difference — while the FLOW is what divides, and the branch currents recombine at the junction. Notice the underlying pattern: the quantity that is the same is the one the geometry forces; the quantity that adds is the one being shared out. Then close every circuit with its audit, both ways: in series, sum the potential differences and confirm they return the emf — 6 plus 12 gives back the 18. In parallel, sum the branch currents and confirm they return the battery current — 6 plus 3 gives back the 9. A failed audit means a wrong number upstream, caught free of charge while you can still fix it.

**Thandi:** Last one: any advice for structuring physics revision itself in these final weeks — what actually moves marks?

**Tutor:** Three shifts, in order of payoff. First, revise by solving, not rereading. A worked solution read through feels like progress and stores almost nothing; the same solution reproduced cold, book closed, is what installs the method. Set yourself a timed mixed exercise across mechanics and electricity, work it without notes, then mark yourself honestly against the solutions — the gaps that surface are your true revision list, and no amount of highlighting finds them. Second, drill the traps by NAME, because in the final examination the physics is familiar but the traps are freshly set: g at 9,8 not 10, the unsquared speed in kinetic energy, the swapped circuit rule, the vector quoted without direction, the ammeter wired across instead of through. Write all five on one page with a single example each and rehearse recognising them — recognition is the entire defence. Third, end every solution with its second-route check: displacement against the graph area, speed against the wallet audit, series potential differences against the emf, parallel currents against the battery. The check converts an uncertain answer into a certain one while you are still in the room, and that certainty steadies every question that follows. Solve cold, name the traps, check by second route — those three carry more marks than any volume of note-reading.
