Hey, it's Thandi — a full year of chemistry compressed into one revision lesson can feel like the periodic table fell on you, so let me stack it back into three small piles.

Pile one: matter. The atom is two numbers — protons for identity, protons plus neutrons for mass — and isotopes just change the second number. The periodic table is a catalogue sorted by behaviour: same column, same valence electrons, same chemistry. Read the column and you already know the element.

Pile two: bonding. Three clicks, chosen by the table's two sides. Metal with non-metal swaps an electron: ionic. Non-metal with non-metal shares a pair: covalent. Metal with metal pools a sea: metallic. Name the click and the properties recite themselves — that is a whole exam question answered by one word.

Pile three: reactions and counting. Balance with coefficients only — subscripts are identity, never touch them — and audit every element on both sides. Then the mole: it is just counting by weighing, like eggs by the crate. Grams to moles with n equals m over M, moles to concentration with c equals n over V, moles to gas volume with 22,4 at STP. Into moles, do the chemistry, out of moles. One currency, every exchange through it.

You have done all of this before — this year, successfully, for marks. Revision is reminding your hands, not learning. So open the practice set, say which pile each question lives in, and audit every answer before moving on. Every audit is a small win, and small wins stack. You've got this!
