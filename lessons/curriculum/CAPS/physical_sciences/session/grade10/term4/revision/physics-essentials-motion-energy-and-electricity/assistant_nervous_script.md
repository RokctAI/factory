Hey, it's Thandi — a whole year of physics in one revision lesson sounds heavy, so let me shrink it down to the three pictures that carry everything.

Picture one, the logbook: motion. Odometer is distance, straight arrow home-to-destination is displacement, and acceleration is just the speedometer needle moving. Three equations, and the question picks the equation for you — list your knowns and the missing quantity points at the right one.

Picture two, the wallet: energy. Height pocket m g h, speed pocket half m v squared, and if nothing rubs, the wallet is sealed — the total never changes, it only moves between pockets. Count it at the top, count it again lower down, and any arithmetic slip shows itself because the pockets stop adding up.

Picture three, the pipes: electricity. Battery is the pump, current is the flow, resistance is the narrow bit, and potential difference is the pressure drop. Series is one loop — same current, pressure bites add. Parallel is a fork — same pressure on each branch, flows add. One question sorts every circuit: is there a junction?

And the traps have names now: g is 9,8 not 10, the speed gets squared, the ammeter sits in the loop and the voltmeter across. You have solved every one of these problems before — this term, for marks. Revision is just reminding your hands. Open the practice set, name the picture out loud for each question, and check every answer by its second route. You've got this!
