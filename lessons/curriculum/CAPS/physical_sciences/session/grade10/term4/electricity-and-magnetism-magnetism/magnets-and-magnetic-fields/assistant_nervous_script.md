Hey, it's Thandi — magnetism is the topic with no formulas, and weirdly that scares people MORE. Nothing to calculate means it's all words and drawings, and suddenly everyone worries their words aren't good enough. Breathe: the words are short, and there are only a handful of them.

Here's your whole survival kit. One definition: a field is a region in space where a magnet or ferromagnetic material feels a force. One rule: poles come in pairs, always — cut forever, never a lone pole. Four drawing laws: north to south outside the magnet, arrows on every line, close lines mean strong field, lines never cross. That's honestly most of the topic.

For the Earth part, keep the three-step argument in your pocket: the needle's north end points north, opposites attract, so the pole up there acts like a magnetic south. Say it in that order and it can never tangle on you.

When you practise, draw. Bar magnet, two magnets attracting, two repelling, the Earth — four sketches, arrows on, done twice each. Your hand will learn the patterns faster than your eyes ever will. Then the exam drawing is just your hand repeating itself. You've got this!
