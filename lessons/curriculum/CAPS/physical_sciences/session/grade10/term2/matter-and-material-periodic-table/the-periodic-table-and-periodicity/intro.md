Today we treat the periodic table not as a poster of facts but as a machine for predicting chemistry. Building on electron configurations, we read how the table is organised — why a row tells you the number of energy levels and a column the number of valence electrons — then locate the metals, non-metals and metalloids, and follow the four great trends that run from lithium to argon. We close with the family chemistry of groups 1, 2, 17 and 18, the reactivity stories every exam draws on. Learn to read an element's address both ways and half the periodic-table marks in Paper 2 are already yours.
