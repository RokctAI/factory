Today we are building the two circuit patterns that every electricity question for the next three years is assembled from: series and parallel. We take one 12-volt battery with a 4-ohm and an 8-ohm resistor, wire them first in a single loop and then side by side, and calculate every current, every potential difference and every total resistance in both arrangements. The rules themselves are short — what costs marks is mixing them up, so we will drill exactly which quantity stays the same and which one divides in each pattern. By the end you will also see why the plugs in your own home are wired in parallel, and what that choice costs.
