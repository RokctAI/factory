Today we map the space that decides every formula, reaction and periodic-table trend for the next three years: the inside of the atom. We meet the nucleus and its two occupants, learn the notation that identifies an element and its isotopes, and compute the weighted average behind the masses printed on the periodic table — including exactly where chlorine's 35,5 comes from. Then we arrange the electrons by the strict filling rules — energy levels, orbitals, Aufbau diagrams and sp notation — because the outermost electrons that arrangement produces are the ones the whole of chemistry will spend.
