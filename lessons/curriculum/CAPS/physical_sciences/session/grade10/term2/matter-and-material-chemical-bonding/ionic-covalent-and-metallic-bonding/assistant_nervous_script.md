Hey, it's Thandi — if covalent, ionic and metallic are blurring into one soup of bond words, take a breath — this whole topic is one goal and three ways of reaching it.

The goal: every atom wants a full outer shelf of electrons, like the noble gases who need nothing. That is the only reason any bond forms. Keep that sentence and every diagram in the topic makes sense.

The three ways, as three home situations. Two non-metals share, like friends splitting one data bundle both count as theirs — covalent, and the shared pairs make molecules. A metal and a non-metal swap: the metal gives its unwanted electron away, both end up charged, and the plus-minus attraction is the bond — ionic, stacking into that giant salt grid. Metals alone pool everything into an electron sea washing around positive ions — metallic, and the sea is the whole reason metals bend, shine and conduct.

Deciding which is which is just geography: find the elements on the table. Two upper-right neighbours share. Left side plus right side swap. Metal alone, sea.

Two habits protect all the calculation marks. Ionic formulas: balance charges to zero — calcium's plus two needs two minus-one chlorides, hence CaCl two. Formula masses: read the inventory out loud first — "one calcium, TWO chlorines" — then multiply, then add: 40 plus 71 is 111. The subscript you say out loud is the subscript you never forget.

One goal, three ways, two habits. Run the practice set with those in hand and this topic becomes the steadiest marks in Paper 2. You've got this!
