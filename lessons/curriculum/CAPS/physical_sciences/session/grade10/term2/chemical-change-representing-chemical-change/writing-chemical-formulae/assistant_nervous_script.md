Hey, it's Thandi — ions, crossovers, brackets, Roman numerals... if formula-writing feels like learning to text in a new alphabet, breathe. It's smaller than it looks.

Here's the whole game in one line: positives and negatives must cancel to zero. That's it. Every formula you'll ever write is just picking the ratio of ions that lands on zero — the crossover method is that game played at speed, and the brackets are just the honest way to multiply a team of atoms.

Even better: the charges aren't a memory test. The periodic table gives you the group charges, your data sheet lists the polyatomic ions, and the Roman numeral in a name literally hands you the metal's charge. The exam brings your cheat sheet for you.

So do the practice questions with the audit habit: after every formula, add up the plusses and minuses out loud. Hitting zero every time isn't just correct — it *feels* good, like a till balancing. Three formulas from now, this is a game you win on autopilot. You've got this!
