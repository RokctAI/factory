Hey, it's Thandi — if balancing felt like spinning plates (fix the carbons, the oxygens wobble; fix those, something else falls), you experienced exactly why the ORDER matters. Good news: the order carries you.

Three habits, that's the entire craft. One: hands off the subscripts — only the big numbers in front may move. Two: for burning things, go C, then H, then O — carbon and hydrogen settle instantly, and oxygen, the busy element, waits politely until the end. Three: audit — count each element on both sides like you're counting change. Match, match, match? Provably done.

That audit is the part I love for you: you never have to *hope* you're right. Ten seconds of counting turns "I think so" into "I proved it" — and that certainty is such a good feeling in a test.

So hit the practice questions now, whispering "C... H... O..." as you go. The first one might take three minutes; by the fourth you'll be at ninety seconds. These become the most reliable marks in your chemistry paper. You've got this!
