**Student:** Why can't I just change H2O to H2O4 or whatever makes the oxygens work? It balances the numbers perfectly!

**Thandi:** The numbers, yes — the chemistry, catastrophically no! Subscripts aren't adjustable knobs; they're the substance's identity card. H2O is water. H2O2 is peroxide — the stuff for cleaning cuts. "H2O4" isn't even a real substance! Your reaction genuinely produces *water*, so water's formula is locked the moment you write it. The only legal knobs are the coefficients out front: 4H2O says "four water molecules" — more water, still water. Think of it like ordering pizza: you can order four pizzas (coefficient), but you can't call a pizza with 42 olives "the same pizza" (subscript). Amounts change; identities don't.

**Student:** I always start with oxygen because it has the most atoms, and I always end up going in circles. Why does that keep happening?

**Thandi:** Because oxygen is the busiest element in the equation — it has fingers in every pie! It appears in BOTH products (CO2 and H2O), so its total depends on the carbon and hydrogen coefficients... which you haven't set yet. Fix oxygen first and every later adjustment re-breaks it — that's your circle. Flip the order: carbon lives in one product, so it settles in one move. Hydrogen, same. THEN count what oxygen must be — and since O2 stands alone on the left, one final coefficient mops it up without disturbing anything. Busy elements last, lone elements last of all. The circle disappears.

**Student:** I got 7/2 O2 for the ethane equation. Fractions feel wrong — did I mess up?

**Thandi:** You didn't mess up — you balanced it correctly and hit a completely normal situation! Odd oxygen demands (seven atoms) meet a supply that comes in pairs (O2), so a half shows up: 7/2. The equation is genuinely balanced with that fraction. Grade 10's convention just asks for whole numbers, and the fix is elegant: double EVERYTHING. 2C2H6 + 7O2 → 4CO2 + 6H2O. Doubling every coefficient keeps all the ratios identical — like doubling a recipe — so the balance survives. Fraction appears → double the whole line → audit again. Three beats, no panic.
