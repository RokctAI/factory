Hey, it's Thandi — if physical versus chemical feels like a list of fifty examples to memorise, put the list down — this entire topic is one question asked over and over.

The question: is there a new substance? That is it. Ice melts — still water — physical. Wood burns — ash and gases, wood gone forever — chemical. Every example in every exam is just that question wearing a disguise. You never memorise the fifty examples; you interrogate each one fresh.

A helper question when you are stuck: can I get the original back by simple means — cooling, evaporating, sieving, a magnet? Salt comes back out of seawater with nothing but sunshine, so dissolving is physical. No freezer ever un-fries an egg, so cooking is chemical.

Then the detective kit for spotting chemistry: unexpected bubbles, a solid appearing from two clear liquids, a colour that will not come back, heat or cold nobody added, light from the change itself. Five clues — but remember, clues only raise suspicion. Bubbles alone might just be boiling. The conviction always returns to the one question: new substance or not.

Two traps to step around. Energy is not the test — melting absorbs heat and stays physical; rust makes no heat you can feel and is deeply chemical. And mass is never lost in either kind of change — the burnt braai wood's missing mass left as invisible gases, every atom intact.

One question, one helper, five clues, two traps. Try the candle in the practice set — melting at the top, burning at the wick, both at once — and feel how the whole topic fits in one hand. You've got this!
