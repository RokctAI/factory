Today we build chemistry's counting unit: the mole, the bridge between the invisible world of molecules and the laboratory balance. One problem drives the whole session — 8,8 grams of carbon dioxide, converted first to moles with n equals m over M, then to molecules with Avogadro's number, and finally to litres at STP with the molar volume of 22,4 cubic decimetres. Along the way we assemble molar mass subscript by subscript, sense-check every division, and draw the mole map whose one rule — inward divide, outward multiply — runs every quantitative chemistry question from here to matric.
