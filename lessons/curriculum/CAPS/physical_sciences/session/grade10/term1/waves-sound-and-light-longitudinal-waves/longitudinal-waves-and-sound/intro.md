Today we turn from waves that shake the medium sideways to the family that squeezes it along the line of travel: longitudinal waves, with sound as the great example. This session defines compressions and rarefactions, explains why sound needs a medium and dies in a vacuum, and ranks solids, liquids and gases by the speed they carry it. We then separate pitch, loudness and quality onto their own dials — frequency, amplitude and waveform — and finish with echo and sonar calculations, where the sound travels there and back and every answer must be halved. The factor of two and the kilohertz conversion are where these marks are won or lost.
