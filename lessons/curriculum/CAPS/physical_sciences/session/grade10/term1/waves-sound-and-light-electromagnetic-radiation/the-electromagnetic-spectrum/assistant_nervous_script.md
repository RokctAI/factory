Hey, it's Thandi — powers of ten everywhere, a Greek letter, a constant with thirty-four zeros hiding in it, and seven bands to memorise. I know exactly how that looks on the page. But this topic is one of the kindest in Paper 1 once you see how little there actually is.

Start with the anchor fact, because it fixes half the confusion: every single one of them travels at the same speed in space. Three times ten to the eight metres per second, radio and gamma alike. Gamma rays are not faster. They are just more energetic. If you remember only one sentence from the whole topic, make it that one.

Then the order, and you already half know it. Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma. Anchor yourself on visible light in the middle, because its two neighbours are named after it — infra-red sits just below red, ultra-violet just beyond violet. Long and lazy on the radio side, short and sharp on the gamma side, and energy climbing steadily as you walk to the right.

The habit that saves your marks: convert prefixes on the page, in a line of their own, before you touch the calculator. Write "250 nm = 2,5 × 10⁻⁷ m" as its own step. Write "100 MHz = 1 × 10⁸ Hz" as its own step. Almost every lost mark in this topic is a prefix that never got converted, not a formula that was misunderstood.

Only two formulas exist here. c equals f lambda — speed of light equals frequency times wavelength — and E equals h f — photon energy equals Planck's constant times frequency. Frequency is the hinge between them, so if a question gives you a wavelength and asks for energy, find the frequency in the middle and walk across. Energy answers come out in joules, always, and they will be tiny numbers with big negative powers. That is normal. Do not panic and move the decimal.

One order, two formulas, one conversion habit. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
