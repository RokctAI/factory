Today we build kinetic energy — the energy an object carries because it is moving — and the formula that prices it: E k equals a half m v squared. One exam problem carries everything: a 1 000 kilogram car at 20 metres per second, holding 200 000 joules, and then the same car with its speed doubled to 40. That second answer is the most important road-safety fact in the whole physics course, because the square in the formula means double the speed is four times the energy — four times the braking distance, four times the wreckage. Master the square and both the marks and the message are yours.
