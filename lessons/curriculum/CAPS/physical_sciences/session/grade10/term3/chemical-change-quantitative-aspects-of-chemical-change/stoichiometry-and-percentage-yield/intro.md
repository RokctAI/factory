Today we build stoichiometry — the art of predicting exactly how much product a reaction can deliver — and percentage yield, the honest comparison between that prediction and what the laboratory actually collects. One exam problem carries everything: 50 grams of calcium carbonate heated until it decomposes into calcium oxide and carbon dioxide, first predicting the maximum 28 grams the atoms allow, then judging the 25,2 grams a learner really collects. The method is a three-leg journey — mass to moles, ratio across the equation, moles back to mass — and it is the engine room of the biggest block of marks in the matric chemistry paper.
