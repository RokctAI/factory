Today we put the molar gas volume to work inside stoichiometry — measuring chemistry not with a balance but with the space a gas fills. One exam problem drives everything: 10 grams of calcium carbonate reacting completely with excess hydrochloric acid, and the volume of carbon dioxide it produces at STP. Underneath it sits Avogadro's astonishing law — one mole of any gas fills the same 22,4 cubic decimetres at standard conditions — and beside it runs the examiners' favourite shortcut, reading gas volumes straight off a balanced equation with almost no arithmetic at all.
