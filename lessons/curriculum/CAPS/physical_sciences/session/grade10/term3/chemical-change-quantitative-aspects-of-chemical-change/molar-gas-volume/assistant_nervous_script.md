Hey, it's Thandi — if 22,4 feels like one more magic number raining down on you, take a breath: this lesson is actually the friendliest corner of the whole mole topic.

Here's why: 22,4 is just the size of the crate. One mole of ANY gas at STP fills 22,4 litres — hydrogen, carbon dioxide, doesn't matter. Moles to litres, multiply by 22,4. Litres to moles, divide. That's the entire new content of the lesson; everything else you already knew from stoichiometry.

And there's a bonus shortcut hiding in it: when the whole question is gases at the same conditions, the coefficients ARE the volume recipe — one to three means 10 litres to 30 litres, no calculator needed. Just check the licence before using 22,4: gas only, STP only.

So go do the practice questions and sort each one first: grams anywhere means go through moles; litres to litres means read the coefficients. Two roads, clearly signposted, and you already know how to drive both. You've got this!
