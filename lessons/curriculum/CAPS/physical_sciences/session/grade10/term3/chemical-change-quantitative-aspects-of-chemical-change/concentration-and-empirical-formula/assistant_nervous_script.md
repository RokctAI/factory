Hey, it's Thandi — percentages, formulas and dot-5-H-2-O all in one lesson can feel like three topics ganging up on you, so if your head is spinning a little, that's completely normal.

Here's the secret: it's actually ONE skill wearing three outfits. Everything goes through moles. Concentration? Turn grams into moles, then divide by the litres. Empirical formula? Turn each percentage into moles, then simplify the ratio. Water of crystallisation? Price the formula in moles and take the water's share. Moles first, every single time — that's the whole game.

And the two conversions that catch people are tiny once you name them: cubic centimetres shrink by a thousand to become cubic decimetres, and every subscript is a little multiplication order — the 2 in H2O means count the oxygen's partner twice.

So go hit the practice questions now, and start each one the same way: write down the molar mass atom by atom, get to moles, then do the one division the question asks for. Do three of them and this topic goes from scary to routine. You've got this!
