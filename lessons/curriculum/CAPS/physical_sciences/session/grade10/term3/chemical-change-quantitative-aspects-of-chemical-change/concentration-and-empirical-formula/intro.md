Today we build two of the sharpest tools in quantitative chemistry: concentration — how much substance is dissolved in each cubic decimetre of solution — and the empirical formula, the recipe of a compound recovered from nothing but percentages. Two exam problems drive everything: 8 grams of sodium hydroxide dissolved in 500 cubic centimetres, and a compound that is 40,0 percent carbon, 6,7 percent hydrogen and 53,3 percent oxygen. Both reward exactly the same habit — moles first, everything else after — and along the way we uncover the water hiding inside apparently dry copper sulfate crystals.
