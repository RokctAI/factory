Today we build the equations of motion — the four-equation toolkit that describes anything moving with constant acceleration in a straight line — and, more importantly, the rule that selects the right equation first time. Each of the four equations is missing exactly one of the five motion quantities, so the winning habit is to find the quantity that is neither given nor wanted and pick the equation that excludes it. One exam problem carries the hour: a car accelerating uniformly from 10 to 25 metres per second over 300 metres, where we choose the equation, calculate the acceleration, and then find the time by two independent roads that must agree.
