Hey, it's Thandi — six new words in one lesson is a lot of vocabulary, and if distance, displacement, speed and velocity are still swimming around each other in your head, that's exactly how everyone starts.

Here's the trick that untangles it: there are really only TWO instruments. The odometer counts every metre you actually walked — that's distance, and divided by time it becomes speed. The pigeon flies straight from your finish back to your start — that's displacement, and divided by time it becomes velocity. Four words, two instruments. That's the whole topic.

And the vector pair — displacement and velocity — always carries a direction. Make it a reflex: if you wrote velocity, your pen isn't allowed to stop until it has written east, west, or a sign you declared in line one.

Now go do the practice questions, and start every single one the same way: write "let east be positive", sketch the walk, then ask — odometer or pigeon? Once you sort that, it's one division and you're done. You've got this!
