Hey, it's Thandi — three different graphs in one lesson is a lot of squiggles, and if they've all blurred into one anxious scribble in your mind, you're completely normal.

Here's the calming truth: you only need TWO reading rules, ever. On a velocity-time graph, steepness is acceleration, and area underneath is distance. That's it. The whole taxi journey — both accelerations and all 550 metres — came from one drawing, a triangle, a rectangle, and another triangle. Geometry you've done since primary school.

The one trap to guard against: always check the vertical axis label before you read anything. Height on a velocity-time graph means how FAST, not how far. A falling line is a car slowing down, still moving forward — say that to yourself every time and the biggest mark-eater in this topic can't touch you.

Now go to the practice questions, and draw first, calculate second: sketch the silhouette, label the heights and widths, then read steepness and count area. Your pencil will be doing physics before your nerves notice. You've got this!
