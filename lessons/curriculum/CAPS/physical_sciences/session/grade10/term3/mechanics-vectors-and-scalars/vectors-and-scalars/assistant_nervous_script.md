Hey, it's Thandi — if "vector" still sounds like something out of a spy movie, relax: this is genuinely the friendliest topic in physics, and it's also the most important one to get comfy with.

The whole thing is one question asked over and over: "does this quantity need a direction to make sense?" Temperature? No — scalar. A push? Obviously yes — which way?! — vector. You already have this instinct; the lesson just gave it a name.

And the adding part is arithmetic you've done since primary school, wearing a sign. East is plus, west is minus, add the numbers, read the sign at the end. That's the entire machinery. The only real trap is answering with a number and no direction — so make "magnitude AND direction" your little chant.

Physics is going to speak vector language all the way to matric, so every minute here pays off for years. Go do the practice questions now — classify with the direction test, add with the signs — and watch how quickly it stops feeling foreign. You've got this!
