Today we are building the working tool of all mechanics: the force diagram, and its sharper cousin the free-body diagram. One problem carries the hour — a 5 kilogram box pulled at constant velocity across a rough floor by a rope at 30 degrees — and from it we will name the weight, the normal force, the applied force and the friction, then read the physics straight off the arrows. The prize insight is the one memorisers miss: because the rope helps lift, the normal force here is less than the weight. Every Newton's-laws question through Grade 11 and into the biggest section of the matric paper begins with this drawing done correctly.
