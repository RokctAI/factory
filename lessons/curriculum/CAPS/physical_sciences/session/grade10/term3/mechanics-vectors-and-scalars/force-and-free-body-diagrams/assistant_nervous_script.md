Hey, it's Thandi — if you're worried your diagram will be missing an arrow or have one too many, that's actually the perfect worry to have. It means you get what the game is.

Here's your safety routine: hunt in order, every time. Gravity first — it's ALWAYS there, pulling straight down. Then just ask "what is physically touching my object?" The floor? That's a normal force (and maybe friction). A rope? Tension along the rope. A hand? Applied force. Nothing else gets an arrow — if you can't point at the object responsible, the force isn't real.

And that dot-and-arrows drawing isn't baby physics — it's exactly what engineers sketch before designing bridges. Four clean, labelled arrows from one dot is a professional move.

So grab scrap paper and draw our box problem from memory: dot, weight down, normal up, rope at 30 degrees, friction backward. Then hit the practice questions. Each diagram you draw makes the next one twice as fast — that's a promise. You've got this!
