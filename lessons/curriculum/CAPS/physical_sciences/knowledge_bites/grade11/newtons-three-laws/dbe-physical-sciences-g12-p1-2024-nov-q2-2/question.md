## Question (2 marks)

(Context: a crate of mass 8,5 kg lies stationary on a rough horizontal table, connected by a light inextensible string over a frictionless pulley to a hanging mass hanger.) Draw a labelled free-body diagram showing ALL the HORIZONTAL forces acting on the crate JUST BEFORE it starts moving.

## Method

free body diagram

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) Just before the crate starts moving, only two horizontal forces act on it: the tension T in the string, pulling it towards the pulley, and static friction fs, acting along the surface in the opposite direction, resisting the tendency to move. On the free-body diagram the crate is a dot, with one labelled arrow for T pointing towards the pulley side and one labelled arrow for fs pointing the opposite way. The two arrows are equal and opposite in effect here because the crate is still in equilibrium.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

[Free-body diagram: dot with static friction fs to the left and tension T to the right (directions may be mirrored).] Accepted labels: fs = static friction/f/Ff; T = FT/Fstring/tension. Do not penalise if vertical forces (w, N) are shown; mark awarded for label and arrow; if arrows do not touch the dot max 1/2; any additional force except w and N max 1/2; correct but no arrows max 1/2.
