## Question (4 marks)

(Context: a crate of mass 8,5 kg on a rough horizontal table is connected by a light inextensible string over a frictionless pulley to a hanging mass; the hanging mass is increased until the crate starts to move.) Calculate the coefficient of static friction (µs). [From the graph the crate starts to move when the hanging mass is 4,2 kg.]

## Method

static friction coefficient

## Memo working

The crate is on the point of moving, so both the crate and the hanging mass are still in equilibrium and static friction is at its maximum value. Step one, the hanging mass: with zero acceleration, Fnet = ma gives mg – T = 0, so the tension equals the hanging weight, T = mg = 4,2(9,8) = 41,16 N. Step two, the crate: horizontally, maximum static friction balances the tension, fs(max) = T. Using fs(max) = µsN with the normal force N equal to the crate's weight on a horizontal table: µs(8,5)(9,8) = 41,16, so µs = 0,49. (Memo option 2: equate fs(max) directly to the hanging weight, µs(8,5)(9,8) = 4,2(9,8), which cancels g to µs(8,5) = 4,2, giving µs = 0,49. Note µs has no unit.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

µs = 0,49
