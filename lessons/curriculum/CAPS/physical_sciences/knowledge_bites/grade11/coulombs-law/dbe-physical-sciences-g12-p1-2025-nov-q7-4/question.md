## Question (6 marks)

Sphere X, carrying a charge of +3 x 10⁻⁷ C, is at point A on a horizontal surface. Sphere Y, carrying a charge of −5 x 10⁻⁷ C, is fixed at point P, which is 0,05 m to the left of X, and sphere Z, carrying a charge of −4 x 10⁻⁷ C, is fixed 0,04 m to the right of sphere X. The NET FORCE acting on sphere X is 0,0427 N at point A. Is the surface frictionless? Choose from YES or NO. Explain the answer by means of a calculation.

## Method

coulombs law net force

## Memo working

Force of Y on X, using Coulomb's law: FXY = kQXQY/r² = (9 x 10⁹)(3 x 10⁻⁷)(5 x 10⁻⁷)/(0,05)² = 0,54 N, directed towards Y on the left (opposite charges attract).

Force of Z on X: FXZ = kQXQZ/r² = (9 x 10⁹)(3 x 10⁻⁷)(4 x 10⁻⁷)/(0,04)² = 0,68 N, directed towards Z on the right.

These two forces act in opposite directions, so the net electrostatic force on X is 0,68 − 0,54 = 0,14 N. Since 0,14 N is not equal to the given net force of 0,0427 N, another horizontal force must act on X: friction. From 0,0427 = 0,14 − f, the friction is f = 0,0923 N. (Memo note: ignore if negative charges are substituted.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

No. The net electrostatic force is FXZ − FXY = 0,68 − 0,54 = 0,14 N, which is greater than / not equal to the given net force of 0,0427 N (so friction of 0,0923 N acts).
