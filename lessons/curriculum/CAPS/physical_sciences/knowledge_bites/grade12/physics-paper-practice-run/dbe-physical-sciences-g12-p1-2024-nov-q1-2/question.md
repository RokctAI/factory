## Question (2 marks)

Multiple choice: A stone thrown vertically downwards from the top of a building takes t seconds to strike the ground. Consider the acceleration-time graph for the motion of the stone. The effects of air friction are ignored. [Graph: acceleration (m·s⁻²) constant at 9,8 from 0 to t seconds; the area under the graph between 0 and t is shaded.] What does the shaded area between 0 and t seconds, shown in the graph, represent? A The final velocity of the stone B The change in position of the stone C The constant velocity of the stone D The change in velocity of the stone

## Method

multiple choice

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) The area under an acceleration-time graph equals acceleration multiplied by time, and a·Δt is the change in velocity, Δv. Here the area is 9,8 x t, the amount the stone's velocity changes during the fall. It is not the final velocity, because the stone already had a non-zero starting velocity when it was thrown downwards; the area gives only the change, not the final value. Change in position would come from the area under a velocity-time graph, not an acceleration-time graph.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

D
