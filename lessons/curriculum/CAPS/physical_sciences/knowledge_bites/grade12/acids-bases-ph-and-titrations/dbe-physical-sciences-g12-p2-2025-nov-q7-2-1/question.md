## Question (4 marks)

Barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)2, dissolves in water according to the balanced equation: Ba(OH)2(s) → Ba2+(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq). A 100 cm³ solution is prepared by dissolving an unknown amount of Ba(OH)2 at 25 °C. 25 cm³ of this Ba(OH)2 solution is reacted with 15 cm³ of a 0,2 mol·dm⁻³ HCℓ solution in a flask, according to the balanced equation: Ba(OH)2(aq) + 2HCℓ(aq) → BaCℓ2(aq) + 2H2O(ℓ). The final pH of the solution is 12,62 at 25 °C. Calculate the final concentration of the hydroxide ions in the flask.

## Method

ph to hydroxide concentration

## Memo working

Step 1 — from pH to hydronium concentration: pH = −log[H3O+]; 12,62 = −log[H3O+]; [H3O+] = 2,4 x 10⁻¹³ mol·dm⁻³.

Step 2 — from the ion product of water to hydroxide concentration: [H3O+][OH⁻] = 10⁻¹⁴; (2,4 x 10⁻¹³)[OH⁻] = 1 x 10⁻¹⁴; [OH⁻] = 0,0417 mol·dm⁻³.

(Memo option 2: pH + pOH = 14; pOH = 1,38; pOH = −log[OH⁻]; [OH⁻] = 0,042 mol·dm⁻³.)

Marking criteria: (a) any correct formula; (b) substitute 12,62; (c) substitute the calculated [H3O+] in [H3O+][OH⁻] (or 1,38 in pOH = −log[OH⁻]); (d) final answer.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

[OH⁻] = 0,04 mol·dm⁻³ [accepted RANGE: 0,04 - 0,042 mol·dm⁻³]
