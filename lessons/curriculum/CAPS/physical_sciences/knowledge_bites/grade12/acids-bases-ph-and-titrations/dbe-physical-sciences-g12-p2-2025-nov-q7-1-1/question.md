## Question (2 marks)

Phosphoric acid, H3PO4(aq), is an example of an acid that can ionise in three steps, as shown below: H3PO4(aq) + H2O(ℓ) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + H2PO4⁻(aq), Ka = 6,9 x 10⁻³; H2PO4⁻(aq) + H2O(ℓ) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + HPO4²⁻(aq), Ka = 6,2 x 10⁻⁸; HPO4²⁻(aq) + H2O(ℓ) ⇌ H3O+(aq) + PO4³⁻(aq), Ka = 4,8 x 10⁻¹³. Which one is the stronger acid, H2PO4⁻ or HPO4²⁻? Give a reason for the answer by referring to the data above.
