## Question (8 marks)

Calculate the number of moles of Ba(OH)2 used to prepare the 100 cm³ solution. (Context from Q7.2.1: a 100 cm³ Ba(OH)2 solution is prepared at 25 °C; 25 cm³ of it is reacted with 15 cm³ of a 0,2 mol·dm⁻³ HCℓ solution according to Ba(OH)2(aq) + 2HCℓ(aq) → BaCℓ2(aq) + 2H2O(ℓ); the final pH in the flask is 12,62, which gave [OH⁻] = 0,0417 mol·dm⁻³ in Q7.2.1.)

## Method

titration excess base calculation

## Memo working

POSITIVE MARKING FROM QUESTION 7.2.1.

Step 1 — moles of acid added: n(HCℓ) = cV = (0,2)(0,015) = 3 x 10⁻³ mol.

Step 2 — moles of hydroxide neutralised: n(OH⁻) reacted = n(HCℓ) = 3 x 10⁻³ mol (1:1).

Step 3 — moles of hydroxide left over after the reaction: the total volume in the flask is 25 + 15 = 40 cm³, so n(OH⁻) remaining = cV = (0,0417)(0,04) = 1,67 x 10⁻³ mol.

Step 4 — hydroxide originally in the 25 cm³ sample: n(OH⁻) initial = 3 x 10⁻³ + 1,67 x 10⁻³ = 4,67 x 10⁻³ mol.

Step 5 — each Ba(OH)2 releases two OH⁻: n(Ba(OH)2) = ½ n(OH⁻) = ½(4,67 x 10⁻³) = 2,33 x 10⁻³ mol in 25 cm³.

Step 6 — scale up to the full 100 cm³ solution: (4)(2,33 x 10⁻³) = 9,34 x 10⁻³ mol.

Marking criteria: (a) substitute 0,2 mol·dm⁻³ and 0,015 dm³ in n = cV; (b) use ratio n(OH⁻):n(HCℓ) = 1:1 or n(Ba(OH)2):n(HCℓ) = 1:2; (c) substitute c(OH⁻) and 0,04 in n = cV; (d) n(OH⁻)initial = n(reacted) + n(final); (e) use ratio n(Ba(OH)2):n(OH⁻) = 1:2; (f) multiply by 4 (or divide by 0,025 dm³ and multiply by 0,1 dm³); (g) final answer.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

n(Ba(OH)2) = 9,34 x 10⁻³ mol [accepted RANGE: 9,2 x 10⁻³ - 9,36 x 10⁻³ mol]
