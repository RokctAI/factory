## Question (2 marks)

A stationary listener, standing on the roadside, records the wavelength of the sound emitted by the siren of a police car travelling at a constant velocity (wavelength-time graph: the wavelength recorded by the listener is λL = 0,40 m and the wavelength emitted by the siren is λS = 0,38 m; the speed of sound in air is 343 m·s⁻¹). Is the car moving TOWARDS or AWAY FROM the listener? Give a reason for the answer.

## Method

doppler direction reasoning

## Memo working

Compare the two wavelengths on the graph: the listener records λL = 0,40 m while the siren emits λS = 0,38 m, so the detected wavelength is longer than the emitted wavelength. A source moving away from a listener stretches the wavefronts, lengthening the detected wavelength and lowering the detected frequency.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Away from. Wavelength of sound detected/observed by the listener is longer than wavelength of sound emitted by the source OR frequency detected by the listener is lower than the frequency of the source OR λL > λS OR fL < fS.
