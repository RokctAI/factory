## Question (6 marks)

An ambulance siren produces sound with a constant frequency. The ambulance moves towards a stationary sound detector (Part A) and away from the stationary detector (Part B) at constant velocities. (Graph: detected frequency versus velocity of the ambulance; the Part A line passes through 1 298 Hz at 25 m·s⁻¹ and the Part B line passes through 1 115 Hz at 25 m·s⁻¹.)

Calculate the speed of sound in air.

## Method

doppler equation simultaneous

## Memo working

When the ambulance approaches the detector at 25 m·s⁻¹, the detected frequency is 1 298 Hz. The Doppler equation for a source moving towards a stationary listener is fL = v/(v − vs) × fs, which rearranges to vfs = 1 298(v − 25) ... equation (1).

When the ambulance moves away at 25 m·s⁻¹, the detected frequency is 1 115 Hz. For a source moving away, fL = v/(v + vs) × fs, which rearranges to vfs = 1 115(v + 25) ... equation (2).

The siren frequency fs and the speed of sound v are the same in both parts, so the left-hand sides are equal. Setting equation (1) equal to equation (2): 1 298(v − 25) = 1 115(v + 25). Expanding gives 183v = 60 325, so v = 329,64 m·s⁻¹.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

v = 329,64 m·s⁻¹
