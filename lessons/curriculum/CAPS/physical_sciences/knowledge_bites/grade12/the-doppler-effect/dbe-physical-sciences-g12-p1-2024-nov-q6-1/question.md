## Question (1 mark)

A stationary listener, standing on the roadside, records the wavelength of the sound emitted by the siren of a police car travelling at a constant velocity. In the wavelength-time graph, NOT drawn to scale, λL is the wavelength of the sound recorded by the listener and λS is the wavelength of the sound emitted by the siren. Take the speed of sound in air to be 343 m·s⁻¹. [Graph: two horizontal lines of wavelength versus time; λL = 0,40 m (upper line) and λS = 0,38 m (lower line).] 6.1 Name the phenomenon that explains why the wavelengths shown in the graph differ.

## Method

identify doppler effect

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) The source of the sound (the siren) is moving relative to the stationary listener, so the wavelength the listener records differs from the wavelength the siren emits. The change in observed wavelength or frequency caused by relative motion between a sound source and an observer is the Doppler effect.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Doppler effect
