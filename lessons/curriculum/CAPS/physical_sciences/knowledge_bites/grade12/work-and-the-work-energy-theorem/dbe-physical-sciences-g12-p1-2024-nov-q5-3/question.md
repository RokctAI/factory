## Question (4 marks)

(Context: a constant force F at 30° above the horizontal pulls a 6 kg crate from rest at point A to point B, 1,5 m away on a horizontal surface, against a constant frictional force of 10 N; the speed of the crate at B is 2 m·s⁻¹.) Using ENERGY PRINCIPLES ONLY, calculate the magnitude of force F.

**Note: the memo's marking-criteria block states "Correct final answer: 20,79 N" while all of the memo's worked options give 20,78 N; the published accepted range 20,769 – 20,79 N covers both values. Independent recomputation gives 20,78 N.**

## Method

work energy theorem

## Memo working

Apply the work-energy theorem over the 1,5 m from A to B. Option 2 (net work): Wnet = ΔEk. Only the horizontal component of F does positive work, F(1,5)cos30°, and friction does negative work, 10(1,5)cos180° = –15 J. The kinetic energy changes by ½(6)(2² – 0²) = 12 J. So F(1,5)cos30° – 15 = 12, giving F(1,29904...) = 27 and F = 20,78 N. Option 1 states the same balance as Wnc = ΔEk + ΔEp with ΔEp = 0 on the horizontal surface: F(1,5)cos30° + 10(1,5)cos180° = ½(6)(2² – 0²) + 0, so F = 20,78 N. (Memo options 3 and 4: work with the net horizontal force, (Fcos30° – 10)(1,5)cos0° = 12, either solving directly for F = 20,78 N or first finding Fx = 18 N and then F = 18/cos30° = 20,78 N.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

F = 20,78 N (accepted range 20,769 – 20,79 N)
