## Question (1 mark)

The reaction between sulphur dioxide, SO2(g), and oxygen, O2(g), takes place in a sealed container according to the balanced equation: 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g), ΔH = −196 kJ·mol⁻¹. Curve X is the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curve for the reaction above. (Graph: number of particles versus kinetic energy; single distribution curve labelled X starting at the origin, rising to a peak and tailing off.) More SO2(g) is now added to the container at constant temperature. How will this change affect the heat of the reaction? Choose from INCREASES, DECREASES or REMAINS THE SAME.

## Method

heat of reaction reasoning

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) The heat of reaction, ΔH, is a fixed property of the reaction itself — the energy difference between products and reactants per mole. Adding more of a reactant changes how much reacts, and adding it at constant temperature does not change the energy of the particles, so ΔH stays −196 kJ·mol⁻¹: it remains the same.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Remains the same
