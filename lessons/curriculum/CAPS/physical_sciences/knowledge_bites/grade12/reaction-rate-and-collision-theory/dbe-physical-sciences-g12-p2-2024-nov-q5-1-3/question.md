## Question (4 marks)

Assume that the concentration of the HCℓ(aq) stays constant for the duration of the reaction. Use the collision theory to explain the change in the reaction rate from t = 0 to t = 5 minutes. (Context: a 0,5 g Aℓ strip reacting with HCℓ(aq); the volume-of-H2 curve rises steeply at first and then flattens.)

## Method

collision theory explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) The graph flattens, so the rate is dropping — and with the acid concentration held constant, the cause must be the aluminium. The four mark points form a chain: as the strip reacts away, its surface area (mass/size) decreases; fewer aluminium particles are exposed to the acid; therefore there are fewer effective collisions per unit time between acid particles and the metal surface; and fewer effective collisions per unit time means the reaction rate decreases.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

The surface area/contact area/mass/size of aluminium decreases. Less particles exposed. Less effective collisions per unit time/second (OR lower frequency of effective collisions). Reaction rate decreases / lower reaction rate / reaction slows down.
