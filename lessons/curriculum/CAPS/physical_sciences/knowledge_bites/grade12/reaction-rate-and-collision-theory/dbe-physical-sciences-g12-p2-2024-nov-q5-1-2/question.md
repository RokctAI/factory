## Question (6 marks)

EXPERIMENT I: 1 mol·dm⁻³ HCℓ solution reacts with a 0,5 g Aℓ strip from an aluminium roll at room temperature. (Graph: volume of H2 in dm³ versus time in minutes, not drawn to scale; the curve rises steeply then levels off; the time axis is marked at 5 minutes. Balanced equation: 2Aℓ(s) + 6HCℓ(aq) → 2AℓCℓ3(aq) + 3H2(g).) For the time interval t = 0 to t = 5 minutes, the average reaction rate for the formation of H2(g) is 0,033 dm³·min⁻¹. Calculate the mass of Aℓ present in the container at t = 5 minutes. Take the molar gas volume as 24,5 dm³·mol⁻¹.

## Method

reaction rate stoichiometry

## Memo working

Rate = ΔV(H2)/Δt: 0,033 = ΔV(H2)/5; V(H2) = 0,165 dm³. n(H2) = V/Vm = 0,165/24,5 = 6,74 x 10⁻³ mol. n(Aℓ) = (2/3)n(H2) = (2/3)(6,74 x 10⁻³) = 4,49 x 10⁻³ mol. OPTION 1: m(Aℓ) reacted = nM = (4,49 x 10⁻³)(27) = 0,12 g (0,121); Δm(Aℓ) = 0,5 – 0,12 = 0,38 g. OPTION 2: n(Aℓ)initial = 0,5/27 = 0,0185 mol; Δn(Aℓ) = 0,0185 – 4,49 x 10⁻³ = 0,014 mol; m(Aℓ) = 0,014 x 27 = 0,38 g. Step by step: first use the average rate to find the volume of hydrogen made in the first 5 minutes (rate times time gives 0,165 dm³). Convert that volume to moles with the molar gas volume. Then use the 2:3 mole ratio of Aℓ to H2 from the balanced equation to find the moles of aluminium that reacted, convert to mass with M = 27 g·mol⁻¹, and subtract the reacted mass from the 0,5 g you started with — what is left in the container is the answer.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

0,38 g (0,379 g) (accepted range 0,365 – 0,42 g)
