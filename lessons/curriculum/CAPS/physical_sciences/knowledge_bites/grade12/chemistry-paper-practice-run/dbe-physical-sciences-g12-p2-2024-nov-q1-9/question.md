## Question (2 marks)

Multiple choice: A standard galvanic cell is set up. [Diagram: voltmeter V connecting a Cd electrode in Cd2+(aq) and an Ag electrode in Ag+(aq), with a salt bridge between the half-cells.] Which ONE of the following combinations of the metal used as cathode and the electron flow direction is CORRECT? A Cd; Cd to Ag B Ag; Cd to Ag C Cd; Ag to Cd D Ag; Ag to Cd

## Method

multiple choice

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) Compare standard reduction potentials from the table of standard electrode potentials: E°(Ag+/Ag) = +0,80 V is higher than E°(Cd2+/Cd) = −0,40 V, so Ag+ is reduced and the silver electrode is the cathode, while cadmium is oxidised at the anode. In the external circuit electrons always flow from the anode to the cathode, that is from Cd to Ag. Cathode Ag with electron flow from Cd to Ag — option B.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

B
