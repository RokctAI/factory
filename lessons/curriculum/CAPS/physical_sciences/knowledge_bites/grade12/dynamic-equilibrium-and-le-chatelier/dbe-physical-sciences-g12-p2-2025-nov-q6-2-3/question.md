## Question (8 marks)

Calculate the mass of NH4HS(s) that will be present at equilibrium at 250 °C. (Context: initially 70 g of NH4HS(s) is placed in a sealed 3 dm³ container at 250 °C and the reaction NH4HS(s) ⇌ NH3(g) + H2S(g) reaches equilibrium; Kc = 18 x 10⁻² at 250 °C.)

## Method

kc equilibrium calculation

## Memo working

**Note: the official memo's two solution routes give different final values — the mole-based route (Option 1) obtains 5,1 g while the concentration-based route obtains up to 5,74 g — so the marking guidelines accept the RANGE 4,96 - 5,74 g. The memo's stated answer is 5,61 g.**

Step 1 — initial moles of solid: n(NH4HS) = m/M = 70/51 = 1,37 mol.

Step 2 — Kc expression: NH4HS is a solid, so it is omitted from Kc: Kc = [NH3][H2S].

Step 3 — let x mol of NH4HS decompose (mole ratio NH4HS : NH3 : H2S = 1 : 1 : 1); the container is 3 dm³, so the equilibrium concentrations are [NH3] = [H2S] = x/3.

Step 4 — substitute into Kc: 18 x 10⁻² = (x/3)²; x = 1,27 mol.

Step 5 — moles of solid remaining: n(NH4HS) at equilibrium = 1,37 − 1,27 = 0,1 mol.

Step 6 — mass: m = nM = 0,1 x 51 = 5,1 g.

(Memo option 2, working in concentrations: x = 0,42 mol·dm⁻³ so n(change) = 1,26 mol, m(eq) = 0,11 x 51 = 5,61 g or 70 − 64,26 = 5,74 g; the concentration route gives 4,998 - 5,128 g. Hence the accepted range 4,96 - 5,74 g.)

Marking criteria: (a) n(NH4HS) = 70/51; (b) ratio 1:1:1; (c) equilibrium concentrations (divide equilibrium moles by 3); (d) correct Kc expression; (e) substitute Kc = 18 x 10⁻²; (f) n(NH4HS)eq = n(in) − n(change); (g) substitute 51 in n = m/M; (h) correct final answer. No Kc expression with correct substitution: max 7/8; wrong Kc expression: max 6/8.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

m(NH4HS) at equilibrium ≈ 5,61 g [memo's stated answer; accepted RANGE: 4,96 - 5,74 g; Option 1 of the memo itself obtains 5,1 g]
