## Question (4 marks)

The electrolyte in the aluminium half-cell is prepared by dissolving some aluminium sulphate, Aℓ2(SO4)3(s), in water. Calculate the mass of Aℓ2(SO4)3(s) needed to prepare 250 cm³ of this solution so that the cell operates initially under STANDARD CONDITIONS.

## Method

standard conditions mass calculation

## Memo working

Step 1 — standard conditions mean the ion concentration is 1 mol·dm⁻³: [Aℓ3+] = 1 mol·dm⁻³.

Step 2 — moles of aluminium ions needed in 250 cm³: n(Aℓ3+) = cV = (1)(0,25) = 0,25 mol.

Step 3 — each formula unit of Aℓ2(SO4)3 supplies two Aℓ3+ ions: n(Aℓ2(SO4)3) = ½ n(Aℓ3+) = ½(0,25) = 0,125 mol.

Step 4 — convert to mass with M(Aℓ2(SO4)3) = 342 g·mol⁻¹: m = nM = 0,125 x 342 = 42,75 g.

Marking criteria: (a) calculate n(Aℓ3+); (b) use mole ratio n(Aℓ2(SO4)3) : n(Aℓ3+) = 1 : 2; (c) multiply n(Aℓ2(SO4)3) by M = 342; (d) final correct answer 42,75 g.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

m(Aℓ2(SO4)3) = 42,75 g
