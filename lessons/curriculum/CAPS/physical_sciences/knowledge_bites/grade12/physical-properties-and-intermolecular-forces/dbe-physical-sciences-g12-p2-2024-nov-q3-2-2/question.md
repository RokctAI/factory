## Question (3 marks)

Fully explain your answer to QUESTION 3.2.1. (In QUESTION 3.2.1 the vapour pressure of compound C, 2,2-dimethylpropane, was identified as 146 kPa — the highest of the three isomers A pentane, B 2-methylbutane and C 2,2-dimethylpropane.)

## Method

intermolecular forces explanation

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) The memo awards the three marks for the structure point, the intermolecular-forces point and the energy point listed in the answer below: branching reduces the surface area over which London forces act, weaker forces need less energy to overcome, so more molecules escape to the vapour phase and the vapour pressure is highest for the most branched isomer.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

Comparing compound C/2,2-dimethylpropane with compounds A/pentane and B/2-methylbutane: Structure - compound C is more branched than compounds A and B / shorter chain length / most compact / most spherical / smallest surface area (over which intermolecular forces act). Intermolecular forces - compound C has weaker/fewer intermolecular forces/Van der Waals forces/London forces than A and B. Energy - less energy needed to overcome or break intermolecular forces/Van der Waals forces in compound C than A and B.
