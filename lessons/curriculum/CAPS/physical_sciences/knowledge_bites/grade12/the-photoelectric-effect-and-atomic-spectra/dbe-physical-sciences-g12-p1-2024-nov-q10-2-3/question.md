## Question (5 marks)

Light of different single frequencies is shone on a sheet of potassium. The electrons with a maximum kinetic energy of 2,65 x 10⁻²⁰ J are ejected by light that has a frequency of 5,85 x 10¹⁴ Hz. Calculate the frequency of the light that ejected electrons with a maximum kinetic energy of 6,96 x 10⁻²⁰ J.

## Method

photoelectric equation

## Memo working

OPTION 1: Apply the photoelectric equation E = W0 + Ek(max) twice — the work function of potassium is the same in both cases. First find W0 from the known frequency: hf = W0 + Ek(max), so (6,63 x 10⁻³⁴)(5,85 x 10¹⁴) = W0 + 2,65 x 10⁻²⁰, giving W0 = 3,61 x 10⁻¹⁹ J. Then use W0 with the larger kinetic energy: hf = W0 + Ek(max), so (6,63 x 10⁻³⁴)f = 3,61 x 10⁻¹⁹ + 6,96 x 10⁻²⁰, giving f = 6,5 x 10¹⁴ Hz. (Memo option 2: hf1 − Ek(max)1 = hf2 − Ek(max)2. Memo option 3: via the threshold frequency f0 = 5,45 x 10¹⁴ Hz.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

f = 6,5 x 10¹⁴ Hz
