## Question (5 marks)

Ball A is thrown vertically upwards at 12 m·s⁻¹ from the top of a building. Two seconds after ball A was thrown upwards, ball B is thrown vertically downwards at 5,4 m·s⁻¹ from the top of the same building. Both balls, A and B, strike the ground at time t seconds. Ignore the effects of air friction. [Graph: position-time sketch graphs for both balls; ball A rises from the top of the building (position Z above the ground) to a maximum position Y, then falls to the ground at time t; ball B starts at Z at 2 s and moves down to the ground, also reaching it at time t.] Using EQUATIONS OF MOTION ONLY, calculate the value of t as shown in the graphs.

## Method

equations of motion simultaneous

## Memo working

Both balls start at the same height and land at the same moment, so write each ball's displacement with Δy = viΔt + ½aΔt² and set them equal. Take upwards as positive, so a = –9,8 m·s⁻². Ball A is in the air for the full t seconds with vi = +12: Δy = 12t + ½(–9,8)t² — equation one. Ball B is thrown 2 s later, so it is in the air for (t – 2) seconds with vi = –5,4: Δy = –5,4(t – 2) + ½(–9,8)(t – 2)² — equation two. Both displacements equal the same drop from the roof to the ground, so set them equal: 12t – 4,9t² = –5,4(t – 2) – 4,9(t – 2)². Expanding the right-hand side, the t² terms cancel, leaving 12t = 14,2t – 8,8, so 2,2t = 8,8 and t = 4 s. (Memo also gives the downwards-as-positive equivalent and options substituting x = t – 2 for ball B or splitting ball A's motion at its turning point, t = 2,45 s.)

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

t = 4 s
