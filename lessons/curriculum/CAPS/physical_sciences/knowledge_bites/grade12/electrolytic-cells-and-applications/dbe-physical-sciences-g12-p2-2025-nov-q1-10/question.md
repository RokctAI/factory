## Question (2 marks)

Multiple choice: The set-up for electroplating an iron medal with silver is shown in the simplified diagram below. [Diagram: power source connected to an iron medal (left electrode) and a silver rod (right electrode), both immersed in an electrolyte.] Which ONE of the following combinations for the ANODE and REACTION AT THE CATHODE is CORRECT? A Anode: Iron medal, Reaction at the cathode: Ag+(aq) + e⁻ → Ag(s) B Anode: Silver rod, Reaction at the cathode: Fe2+(aq) + 2e⁻ → Fe(s) C Anode: Iron medal, Reaction at the cathode: Ag(s) → Ag+(aq) + e⁻ D Anode: Silver rod, Reaction at the cathode: Ag+(aq) + e⁻ → Ag(s)

## Method

multiple choice

## Memo working

(see marking guidelines) In electroplating, the object to be plated is always the CATHODE, and the plating metal is the ANODE. Here the iron medal must receive the silver coating, so the medal is the cathode and the silver rod is the anode — that immediately eliminates A and C. At the cathode, reduction occurs: silver ions from the electrolyte gain electrons and deposit as silver metal, Ag+(aq) + e⁻ → Ag(s). Option B wrongly deposits iron; option C writes an oxidation at the cathode. The correct combination is silver rod as anode with Ag+(aq) + e⁻ → Ag(s) at the cathode.

## Answer (per marking guidelines)

D
