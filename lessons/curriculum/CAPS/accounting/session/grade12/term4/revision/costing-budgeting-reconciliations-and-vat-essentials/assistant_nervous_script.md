Hey, it's Mandy — Paper 2 looks like four unrelated topics, but every one of them is just careful sorting, and sorting is a skill you already have.

The production cost statement: five levels, spoken like a lift going up. Direct materials, direct labour, prime cost, factory overheads, total cost of production — then adjust for work-in-progress, opening in, closing out. Before you place any cost, ask it one question: do you live in the factory? Commission does not. Office stationery does not. Carriage on raw materials does — it rides with the materials.

Break-even: one fraction. Fixed costs over contribution per unit, and contribution is selling price minus VARIABLE cost only — never the full unit cost, because the fixed portion is already waiting in the numerator. Compare the answer to units produced and say which side of the line the business sits. That sentence is a guaranteed mark.

The cash budget: one question per item — did money move this month? Depreciation and bad debts never do; loans and share proceeds always do. And check your collection percentages: if they add to less than a hundred, the gap is the bad debts, already handled.

Reconciliations: timing differences wait on the statement side; errors get fixed by whoever made them. Age analysis: compare the ages to the terms, then recommend like a manager — interest, suspension, screening.

VAT: collected minus paid, then two nudges. Drawings give the input claim back — owed goes up. Bad debts take the output back — owed goes down. Excluding means fifteen percent; including means fifteen over one hundred and fifteen.

Sort calmly, show every working, and the marks stack up line by line. You've got this!
