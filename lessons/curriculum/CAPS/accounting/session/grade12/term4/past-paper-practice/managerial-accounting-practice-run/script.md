# Part 1 — Expert

This session is a full practice run of Paper 2, Managerial Accounting, Internal Auditing and Control — one hundred and fifty marks on the internal life of a business: manufacturing, break-even, inventory valuation, budgets, reconciliations, VAT and control. The questions are exam-style, built to the official guideline, and each is walked at marking pace: every step named with the marks it carries, every working spoken in full, because Paper 2 is the workings paper — the marker rewards the visible route as generously as the destination.

## Subtopic: Question One — Concepts and Cost Sorting

The opener is a concepts ladder, a mark per rung. Direct costs can be traced to the product — the leather in the shoe; indirect costs serve the factory as a whole — the supervisor's salary. One mark each. Fixed costs ignore the level of production — factory rent; variable costs ride on every unit — raw materials. One mark each. Perpetual inventory: cost of sales recorded at every sale, the stock account always current; periodic: stock counted at intervals and cost of sales deduced. One mark each, and the deducing is the difference the examiner wants stated.

Then the sorting table, six to eight marks: place each cost in its column. Salesperson's commission — selling and distribution, never factory. Factory cleaner's wages — indirect labour, factory overheads. Carriage on raw materials — direct material cost, riding with the materials. Office internet — administration. Depreciation on delivery vehicles — selling and distribution; depreciation on factory machines — factory overheads. One mark per placement, and each wrong placement usually costs a second mark downstream when the totals inherit it. Sort slowly here; the statement question is about to reuse every answer.

## Subtopic: The Production Cost Statement Question

The main manufacturing question — call it thirty marks — and it opens with the two notes before the statement itself.

Direct material cost, the note the marker reads first. Opening stock of raw materials R60 000, plus purchases R540 000, plus carriage on purchases R12 000 — carriage always follows its materials — minus closing stock R72 000. Issued to production: R540 000. Four components, four marks.

Direct labour cost: factory wages R336 000 plus the employer's contributions of R24 000 that ride on those wages — R360 000, two marks, and omitting the contributions is the classic single-mark leak.

Prime cost: 540 000 plus 360 000 — R900 000, one mark, label and all.

Factory overheads: the given R320 000 plus the factory's share of the building rent. The paper says rent of R40 000 is split seventy percent factory by floor area: R28 000 in, the R12 000 remainder to administration. Overheads: R348 000, two or three marks living in that split.

Total cost of production: R1 248 000 — one mark. Then work-in-progress: add opening R52 000, subtract closing R40 000 — the half-finished goods released a net R12 000 into finished production. Cost of production of finished goods: R1 260 000 — two marks. And the closing line the paper loves: twenty-one thousand units were finished, so unit cost is 1 260 000 over twenty-one thousand — R60 per unit, one mark, and hold it: the interpretation subquestion will ask whether R60 is better or worse than last year's R64, and the answer must name a reason — cheaper cotton, less waste, or simply higher volume spreading the fixed overheads thinner.

## Subtopic: The Break-even Question

Break-even arrives as a table of costs for a shoe factory and four subquestions, roughly fifteen marks.

Contribution first: selling price R240, variable costs per unit R150 — materials, labour, and the selling costs that travel with each pair. Contribution: R90, one mark, and it must use variable costs ONLY — sliding the fixed portion of unit cost in here is the topic's cardinal error.

Break-even: fixed costs of R630 000 over the R90 contribution — seven thousand pairs, two marks: formula, answer.

Comparison, two marks and always asked: the factory made eight thousand two hundred pairs this year, twelve hundred beyond break-even — safely profitable; last year it made seven thousand five hundred against a break-even of seven thousand six hundred — one hundred pairs SHORT, a loss year. Name both positions and the improvement; the marks are for the sentence, not the subtraction.

Profit from the margin: twelve hundred pairs past the line at the full R90 each — R108 000, two marks, and the shortcut is legitimate because fixed costs died at pair seven thousand.

Then the what-if marks: leather rises R15 a pair, contribution thins to R75, break-even climbs to eight thousand four hundred — above current production, and the required response is managerial: raise the price, renegotiate supply, or cut variable cost, each with its trade-off named. Two or three marks for reasoning through contribution, none for rebuilding the whole table.

## Subtopic: The Inventory Valuation Question

The stock question, about twenty marks, hands you a trader's year — school bags. Opening stock five hundred units at R84: R42 000. Purchases: fifteen hundred units at R90, R135 000; then one thousand units at R99, R99 000. Available: three thousand units costing R276 000. Closing count: eight hundred units.

FIFO first — first in, first out, so the units REMAINING are the newest. All eight hundred come from the last batch at R99: closing stock R79 200, two marks. Cost of sales: 276 000 minus 79 200 — R196 800, one mark.

Weighted average: total cost over total units — 276 000 over three thousand — R92 a unit, two marks including the rounding statement. Closing stock: eight hundred at R92 — R73 600, one mark. Cost of sales: R202 400, one mark.

The comparison the paper always demands, three or four marks: prices rose all year, so FIFO's closing stock is R5 600 higher and its cost of sales lower — FIFO reports the HIGHER profit in rising prices, weighted average the steadier one. State the direction and the reason, not just the numbers.

Then stock management: two thousand two hundred units were sold; eight hundred remain — about a hundred and thirty-three days of stock at the current selling rate. If the school season peaks in January, holding four months of bags may be planning; if demand is flat, it is capital sleeping on shelves and a theft risk. Recommend: match orders to the sales rhythm, secure the storeroom, count cyclically. Marks flow to specific, situation-aware sentences.

## Subtopic: The Cash Budget Question

The budget question, twenty-five marks, opens with the debtors' collection schedule — the working the whole question leans on.

Sales: August R180 000, September R200 000, October R220 000; forty percent cash, sixty percent on credit. Credit customers pay fifty percent in the month after the sale and forty-five percent in the second month; five percent turns bad. October's cash from sales, three lines spoken slowly. Cash sales: forty percent of 220 000 — R88 000, one mark. From September's credit sales of R120 000: half — R60 000, two marks. From August's credit sales of R108 000: forty-five percent — R48 600, two marks. Total receipts from sales: R196 600. The five percent appears nowhere — bad debts move no cash, and writing them into a budget is an instant lost mark.

Then the sorting marks: depreciation of R15 000 — projected income statement only. Loan received — budget only. Provisional tax and an interim dividend — budget lines for a company, in the months the money moves.

The question closes with judgement, six-plus marks, and the guideline says companies and their directors are fair game here. Budgeted advertising R10 000, actual R2 500: the underspend likely explains the sales shortfall — advertising is seed, not luxury. Directors' fees rising twenty percent in a year the budget promised five: query the authorisation, name the remuneration committee. A projected overdraft in the equipment-purchase month: shift the purchase, pre-arrange finance, or lease instead. Managers earn these marks; calculators do not.

## Subtopic: The Reconciliation and Age Analysis Question

The control question — around twenty marks — pairs the creditors' reconciliation with the debtors' age analysis.

The reconciliation. Our creditors' ledger account for a supplier shows R66 300; their statement demands R84 500. Work the statement side: our payment of R12 000 made on the last day has not reached them — subtract, timing. An invoice of R8 400 is for goods arriving only next month — subtract; we record goods when received. The statement overcharges R300 on one invoice against the agreed price — subtract, THEIR error, corrected on their side. Statement adjusted: 84 500 minus 12 000 minus 8 400 minus 300 — R63 800. Our side: a debit note for returns of R2 500 was never recorded — subtract from the ledger: R63 800. Agreement, seven or eight marks, one per item plus the totals — and each item's mark is really for choosing the correct SIDE.

Age analysis: the debtors' book of R240 000 on thirty-day terms — current R96 000, forty percent; thirty days R72 000, thirty percent; sixty days R48 000, twenty percent; ninety days and beyond R24 000, ten percent. The reading, two marks: thirty percent of the book is beyond terms, and the oldest tenth is sliding toward write-off. The response, three or four marks, must be operational: interest on arrears, suspend the ninety-day accounts, credit-check new applicants, discount for early settlement — and one control sentence: the person who receives cash must not also keep the debtors' ledger, or shortages can be buried as fake credits.

## Subtopic: The VAT, Internal Control and Ethics Question

The closer, fifteen-odd marks, starts with the VAT settlement. Sales of R720 000 excluding VAT collected output tax of fifteen percent: R108 000, one mark. Input tax on purchases and expenses: R79 500 — given. Base position: R28 500 owed. The nudges, two marks each with direction. The owner took stock costing R6 000 excluding VAT: the input claim on consumed goods returns — add R900. A debtor owing R9 200, VAT INCLUSIVE, was written off: reclaim fifteen over one hundred and fifteen — subtract R1 200. Amount owed to SARS: 28 500 plus 900 minus 1 200 — R28 200, one mark for the total, and the inclusive-versus-exclusive reading carried three of the marks on the way.

Internal control, applied to cash because the guideline names it: division of duties — the till operator does not reconcile the till; daily banking — cash sleeping on the premises is temptation plus risk; documents numbered in sequence, gaps investigated; surprise counts. One mark per named control WITH its reason.

Ethics closes the paper. The scenario: a business runs a second till and lets cash sales bypass the record. Name it: VAT fraud and tax evasion — the fifteen percent collected at that till was held in trust for SARS and is being stolen from the public; the understated sales also cheat any profit-sharing and mislead the bank reading the statements. Consequences: penalties, interest, prosecution, a destroyed audit trail. Two to four marks, earned by naming the trust relationship, the victims and the consequence — not by declaring dishonesty bad. Attempt every part, show every route, and Paper 2's one hundred and fifty marks are walked.
