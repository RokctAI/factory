Hey, it's Mandy — Paper 2 the night before feels like seven different subjects, but it is really one skill worn seven ways: sort carefully, then say what the sorting means. Here is your pocket card.

Manufacturing: five levels, always in order — materials, labour, prime cost, overheads, total cost of production — then work-in-progress, opening in, closing out. Before placing any cost, ask: does it live in the factory? Carriage on raw materials does. Commission never does.

Break-even: one fraction, fixed costs over contribution, and contribution is price minus VARIABLE cost only. Then the sentence that earns the comparison marks: name which side of the line production sits, this year and last.

Stock: FIFO leaves the newest costs on the shelf; weighted average blends everything into one honest number. Rising prices, FIFO shows the higher profit — say the direction and the reason.

The budget: one question per line — did money move this month? Depreciation and bad debts never do. And build the collection schedule from the sales months, letting each month's cohort pay its instalments on schedule.

Reconciliations: timing waits on the statement side, errors get fixed by whoever made them, and your ledger only ever changes for your own mistakes.

VAT: collected minus paid, then the nudges — drawings give the input back, owed goes up; inclusive bad debts reclaim output at fifteen over one-fifteen, owed goes down.

And for every comment question, answer like the manager: name the control, name the reason, name the fix.

Sort, then speak. Marks stack line by line, and you have practised every line. You've got this!
