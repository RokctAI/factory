Hey, it's Mandy — this topic looks like a wall of words, but it is really four short lists and one method, and you already understand the ideas from ordinary life.

List one: the split. Shareholders own — they vote, elect directors, appoint the auditor, ask questions at the annual general meeting. Directors run — in good faith, in the company's interest, declaring any personal interest. That is fiduciary duty in one line: it's not your money.

List two: the King Code's four demands — responsibility, accountability, fairness, transparency. Look after what the company owns; answer for every decision; weigh all stakeholders, not just the loudest; report openly, bad news included. Scenario questions almost always break one of these four — your job is to spot which.

List three: the five ethics principles — integrity, objectivity, competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. And the two share crimes: insider trading is using information the market lacks; manipulation is feeding the market false information. Different directions, same cheat.

List four: the three layers of checking. Internal controls every day, internal audit all year reporting to the audit committee, the independent auditor once a year reporting to shareholders.

Then the method that turns any scenario into marks, three moves: state the problem from the facts, name the principle or duty broken, recommend the remedy. Problem, principle, remedy — write it every single time and the marks line up.

Four lists, three moves. Think of the soccer club whenever a term feels abstract — the treasurer, the quotes, the outside accountant — and the answer is usually common sense wearing a suit. You've got this!
