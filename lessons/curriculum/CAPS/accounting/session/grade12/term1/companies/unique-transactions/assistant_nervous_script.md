Hey, it's Mandy — companies feel like a whole new subject, I know. New words, new accounts, tax with its own timetable. But strip it down and grade 12 adds exactly four new transactions to what you already do, and each one has a rule you can hold in one hand.

Share issue: multiply the shares by the issue price, debit Bank, credit Ordinary Share Capital, done. No par value means the whole price goes to capital — no premium account, nothing clever.

Buy-back: this is the one that scares people, so here is the whole trick. Work out the AVERAGE issue price first — total share capital divided by total shares. The capital account gives up shares-bought-back times that average; whatever you paid ABOVE the average comes out of retained income. Two debits, one credit to Bank. Then cross the shares out — they are cancelled, and they get no more dividends.

Tax: think of a slate at the shop counter. Provisional payments go on one side, the final tax charge on the other, and the balance is what you owe SARS — or what SARS owes you if you overpaid. Read the slate; never assume the direction.

Dividends: cents per share TIMES the shares in issue ON THAT DATE. Say that out loud. If a buy-back or an issue happened between the interim and the final dividend, the share counts differ, and using the wrong count is the single most common error in the whole question.

And the loan with capitalised interest just grows before it shrinks: opening balance plus interest, minus payments. The interest is still an expense even though it never left the bank separately.

Four transactions, four rules. You've got this!
