Hey, it's Mandy — the financial statements question is the biggest one on the paper, and I know the page count alone is frightening. But here is the secret: it is not one giant question. It is a set of small slates, each with its own tiny bit of arithmetic, and they reward you slate by slate.

The income statement is a staircase you already know: sales, minus cost of sales, gross profit; plus other income, minus operating expenses, operating profit; plus interest income, minus interest expense, profit before tax; minus tax, profit after tax. Say the staircase out loud before you start writing and the layout marks are yours.

The balance sheet balances or it doesn't — and if it doesn't, check the two company slates first. SARS: charge on one side, provisional payments on the other, balance owing or refund due. Shareholders for dividends: it is ONLY the final dividend, because the interim was already paid.

The cash flow statement scares everyone, so hold three anchors. Anchor one: depreciation gets added back and never returns; interest gets added back and returns as its own paid line. Anchor two: dividends PAID means last year's final plus this year's interim — the new final dividend has moved no cash. Anchor three: the statement must land exactly on the balance sheet's closing bank figure. If it lands, you are right; if it misses, check the working capital signs — stock up is cash out, debtors up is cash out, creditors up is cash in.

And the audit report is three verdicts: unqualified is clean, qualified means except-for, disclaimer or adverse means run. Addressed to shareholders, always, because the auditor checks the directors on the owners' behalf.

Slate by slate, anchor by anchor. You've got this!
