Hey, it's Mandy — analysis questions look like a wall of formulas, but here is what the markers are actually paying for: three sentences per indicator. Quote the figure. Compare it to something. Judge it with a reason. That pattern, repeated, is the whole question.

The formulas themselves come in pairs that share logic, so learn them as pairs. ROSHE and ROTCE: shareholders' profit over shareholders' money, and everyone's profit over everyone's money. After tax over average equity for the first; before tax plus interest over average capital employed for the second. If you remember that each top line must belong to its bottom line, you can rebuild both under pressure.

EPS and DPS: earned per share and paid per share. Profit after tax over shares; dividends over shares. The gap between them is what the company kept, and DPS over EPS is the pay-out rate.

NAV against the market price: the books' answer against the market's answer. Same units — cents against cents — and the gap is the market's opinion of the future.

Now the three comparisons that unlock the discussion marks. ROTCE against the interest rate on loans decides the gearing argument: earning seventeen and a half while paying eight means borrow more; the gap closing means stop. ROSHE against a fixed deposit decides whether shareholders are paid for their risk. And this year against last year decides direction — always ask for the trend.

One trap before you go: liquidity ratios can look worse while the company is actually managing better — a current ratio falling from a bloated three to a lean two is an improvement. Never judge a ratio by size alone; judge it by what changed and why.

Quote, compare, judge. Three sentences, every indicator. You've got this!
