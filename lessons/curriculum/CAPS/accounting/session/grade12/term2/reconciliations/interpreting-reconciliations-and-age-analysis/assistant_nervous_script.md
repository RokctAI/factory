Hey, it's Mandy — reconciliation questions look fiddly, but they are the most method-driven marks in Paper 2, and method is learnable. One diagnostic question runs the entire topic: whose record has not caught up with reality?

Bank reconciliation: items the BANK has not seen yet — your late deposit, your just-sent payments — ride the reconciliation statement. Items YOUR BOOKS have not heard yet — bank charges, interest, debit orders — get recorded in the books first and never appear on the reconciliation. Books catch up first, then bridge the timing. That order alone earns the structure marks.

Creditors' reconciliation: line up the supplier's statement against your ledger and assign every difference to its owner. Real goods your ledger missed: ledger side. Your payment or return their statement missed: statement side. Their posting error: their side. Both columns must land on the same final figure, and THAT figure is what you owe and what you pay. If the columns miss each other, check for an item on the wrong side — a gap of twice the item is the giveaway.

Control account against the list: journal-total errors hit the control; posting errors hit the individual accounts. Locate, correct, agree.

Age analysis: sort, percentage, and then interpret with the golden sentence — the older the debt, the less likely it pays, so the ninety-day column drives the provision for bad debts. Escalation in order: remind, charge interest if terms allow, suspend credit, collect if worth it.

And the interpretation flags, because they are asked every year: a deposit outstanding for weeks means rolling of cash; recurring month-end reversals mean window-dressing; and the fix is always segregation plus an independent review.

One question, two sides, agree, interpret. You've got this!
