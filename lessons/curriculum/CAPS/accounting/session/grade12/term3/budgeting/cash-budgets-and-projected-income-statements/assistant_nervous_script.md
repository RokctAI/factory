Hey, it's Mandy — budgeting is the friendliest topic on Paper 2, because it is mostly logic you already use at home. Three tools, and you own the question.

Tool one: the boundary test. Two documents, two questions — profit on paper, or money in the bank? When any item appears, ask it: do you move CASH in this month? Depreciation never does — income statement only. A new machine moves all its cash at once — cash budget only, and just its depreciation reaches the income statement. Loans in, capital in, drawings out — cash budget only, because none of them are income or expenses. Bad debts — income statement, and in the cash budget they are simply the money that never arrives.

Tool two: the collection schedule, rows and columns. Rows are each month's credit sales; columns are the months cash lands. Thirty percent same month, sixty next, eight the month after — write each row's landings, then read DOWN the column they asked for. Check: newest sales carry the biggest same-month layer, and the bad-debt percentage appears nowhere, ever. Creditors mirror it — usually next month, times ninety-five percent if there is a settlement discount.

Tool three: the three chained lines. Receipts minus payments is the surplus or shortfall; add the opening balance; land on the closing balance, which becomes next month's opening. A shortfall is not failure — it is early warning, and saying what management should DO about it is the interpretation mark.

And for budget-against-actual: label, explain, judge. Underspent advertising beside fallen sales is a false economy — say the pair, not the line. Overspends are innocent until the explanation convicts them.

Three tools, one pair-reading habit. You've got this!
