Today we point the accounting machinery at the future: the projected income statement, which asks whether the business will be profitable, and the cash budget, which asks whether there will be money in the bank on the days the bills arrive. We will master the debtors' collection schedule — the one hard calculation that appears in almost every budgeting question ever set — assemble a full month's cash budget to the rand, and then do what grade 12 is really examined on: reading the forecast, comparing budget to actual, and judging the decisions the variances reveal. This is where arithmetic becomes management, and where a false economy can be spotted two rows apart on one page.
