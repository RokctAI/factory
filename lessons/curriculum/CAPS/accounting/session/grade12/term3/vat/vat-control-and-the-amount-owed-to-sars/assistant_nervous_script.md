Hey, it's Mandy — VAT questions are gift marks wearing a scary mask. The whole topic is one subtraction, two little formulas, and four nudges. Let me put them in your pocket.

The subtraction: output tax collected on sales, minus input tax paid on purchases, equals the amount owed to SARS. If input wins, SARS owes YOU — read the direction, say the direction, and you have the interpretation mark.

The two formulas. Price WITHOUT VAT: multiply by fifteen percent to find the tax. Price WITH VAT inside: multiply by fifteen, divide by one hundred and fifteen. Before you touch any number, ask it one question — do you already contain the VAT? Fifteen percent of an inclusive price is the single most common error in the topic, so let that question become a reflex.

The four nudges, with directions. Customers return goods: output DOWN. Owner takes stock home: amount owed UP — the business gives back the input claim on goods that were consumed, not sold. Bad debts written off: amount owed DOWN — and careful, the debt is INCLUSIVE, so use fifteen over one-fifteen. Discounts allowed: output down; discounts received: input down.

The control account is the same story in ledger clothes: credit side holds what is owed to SARS — output and drawings; debit side holds what comes back — input, returns, bad debts, and every payment made. Credit balance, liability; debit balance, refund. It must agree with your subtraction — if it does, you have checked your own work like a real vendor.

And if an ethics paragraph is asked: VAT collected was never the vendor's money — it is held in trust for SARS, and hiding sales or inventing invoices is theft from the public. Two sentences, full marks.

One subtraction, two formulas, four nudges. You've got this!
