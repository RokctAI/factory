Hey, it's Thandi — four new adjustments plus a growing loan in one lesson is a LOT of vocabulary: accrued this, prepaid that, received in advance... If the words are swimming, drop the words and keep the grid.

Two questions sort everything. One: is the shop PAYING or COLLECTING? Two: did the money move before or after the thing it pays for? That's it — four corners, and every scary term is just a corner's name tag. You don't memorise four rules; you ask two questions four times.

Then the fix never varies: the report card gets what the year actually USED or EARNED — count the months, that's Grade 4 counting — and the leftover becomes either a sticky note (someone owes someone) or a parcel (value stored for next year). If you can decide "note or parcel, and who's holding it", you have the whole topic, including the marks most learners drop.

The loan one — interest capitalised — has the easiest trick of all: no cash moved, so your only clue is the loan getting bigger. When a question mentions a loan and interest but no payment, that's your bell.

And a quiet superpower: check your work with the symmetry rule — every adjustment moves profit AND creates a balance-sheet item. If you only wrote one half, go back for the other. Practice a few grids and this becomes the most mechanical topic in the book. You've got this!
