Hey, it's Thandi — five formulas, comparison bases, four-move comments... if analysis feels like a new subject bolted onto Accounting, here's the calming truth: the formula sheet is IN the exam. Nobody is testing your memory tonight. They're testing three small skills, and each is practisable.

Skill one: picking the right formula — and the question always tells you. Pricing or mark-up mentioned? Gross percentages. Costs mentioned? Expenses on sales. The whole year's result? Net on sales. Match the word in the question to the gauge; that's the whole trick.

Skill two: substituting the right figures. Label them as you write — "gross profit 20 000, sales 100 000" — because the classic lost mark is a right formula fed a wrong number. Labels are free insurance.

Skill three: the comment — and it's a recipe, not inspiration. What happened, so what, why (from the story they gave you!), now what. Four short sentences. Write them as four lines and you can literally watch yourself collect the four marks.

And keep the hundred-rand picture close: 80 replaces the goods, 20 is margin, 17 runs the shop, 8 stays. If you can tell that little story, you understand profitability better than most adults with bank accounts. The practice questions are the same story with different splits. You've got this!
