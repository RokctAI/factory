Hey, it's Thandi — four more formulas after last lesson's five, and I know the pile feels heavy. So let's do what analysts do: reduce it. Tonight was three QUESTIONS, not four formulas.

Question one: can this year's bills be paid? That's liquidity — and its two ratios are the same question asked twice, once trusting the stock, once not. Current assets over current liabilities; then again without inventories. If you remember "I can't pay the landlord in rice", you remember the acid test forever.

Question two: could everyone be paid, ever, in the end? Total assets over total liabilities. One-to-one is the cliff edge. Everything above it is degrees of comfort.

Question three: was the owner's money in the right place? Profit over the AVERAGE stake — average, because the stake moved during the year; write "(20 000 + 23 900) ÷ 2" and that classic lost mark is banked. Then compare with the bank's safe rate and remember the one wise sentence: risk deserves its wage.

The formula sheet holds every formula, so your job is matching question to gauge: bills → liquidity, survival → solvency, worth-it → return. And in comments, both directions count: too low is danger, too high can be laziness — saying that is what "meaningful comment" means.

Nine gauges now and you can read the whole dashboard. That's a term of real skill. You've got this!
