# Part 1 — Expert

Two statements now stand complete — the year's story and the midnight photograph. But several of their lines are summaries: one figure standing for an assembled family. The NOTES to the financial statements are where those families are unpacked, item by item, for any reader who wants the detail behind the line. Grade 10 prescribes seven notes — interest, fixed assets, inventories, trade and other receivables, cash and cash equivalents, owner's equity, trade and other payables — and today we build each one from our year's figures, then close with the GAAP thread and the indigenous bookkeeping the syllabus weaves through this topic.

## Subtopic: Why Notes Exist

A statement must be readable in a minute — that is its power — so its lines compress. "Trade and other receivables: R4 950" is honest but silent about WHO owes WHAT. The notes restore the detail without cluttering the page: each summarised line carries a note number in its reference column, and the note, on the pages behind, opens the figure up. Line and note must agree to the rand — a marker, like an auditor, travels the reference both ways and expects the same figure at both ends. That cross-reference discipline is the first habit of this lesson: number the note, quote the number on the line, land both on the same total.

Why not simply print everything on the statement? Because detail in the wrong place DESTROYS readability — a statement listing every debtor and every asset would bury its own subtotals; the mother, the banker and the buyer would drown before reaching net profit. The two-layer design serves both readers at once: the summary page for the minute-reader, the notes for the investigator. This is GAAP's disclosure thinking in action: report ALL material information — but organised so that importance shows.

And know which notes Grade 10 owns: interest; fixed assets; inventories; trade and other receivables; cash and cash equivalents; owner's equity; trade and other payables. Seven doors behind the two pages — we now open each.

## Subtopic: The Fixed Assets Note and the Interest Notes

The FIXED ASSETS note is the fullest, because it reconstructs an asset's whole year. Its shape, top to bottom: carrying value at the BEGINNING of the year — shown as cost minus accumulated depreciation; then the year's MOVEMENTS — additions at cost for anything bought, disposals for anything sold, and the year's depreciation charge; then carrying value at the END — again as cost minus accumulated depreciation. Our equipment, bought this year: opening carrying value nil; additions R18 000; depreciation for the year R1 800; closing carrying value R16 200 — presented as cost R18 000 less accumulated depreciation R1 800. Check the note against its two masters: the closing carrying value must equal the statement's fixed-assets line, and the year's depreciation charge must equal the income statement's depreciation expense. One note, two statements, three agreements — and the asset register stands behind it all as the item-level detail.

The INTEREST notes are the simplest pair. Interest income: what interest the business EARNED — ours, none this year; where it exists, the note names the source — a fixed deposit, a savings account. Interest expense: the R1 200 on the loan, named to its source, and reconciling with the loan's movement: opening balance R10 000, plus interest capitalised R1 200, giving the closing R11 200 — the note showing exactly how the debt grew without a payment. Small notes, but they anchor the financing tail of the income statement to the liability on the photograph.

## Subtopic: The Working-Capital Notes

Four notes unpack the current families — quick builds, strict agreement.

INVENTORIES: trading stock R2 250; consumable stores on hand R200; total R2 450 — agreeing with the statement's inventories line. The note separates the sellable from the usable: stock awaits customers; consumables await the office.

TRADE AND OTHER RECEIVABLES: trade debtors R3 850 — the control figure the debtors' list reconciles; accrued income R500 — the tenant's owed month; prepaid expenses R600 — the insurance stored ahead. Total R4 950. The note is precisely the assembly working you bracketed on the statement — now given its own titled home.

CASH AND CASH EQUIVALENTS: bank R15 000; petty cash R300; cash float where one exists. Total R15 300. Equivalents means near-cash — amounts available on demand; a fixed deposit maturing years away would NOT belong here.

TRADE AND OTHER PAYABLES: trade creditors R3 200 — the creditors control the list reconciles; accrued expenses R100 — February's telephone; income received in advance R500 — the tenant's early month. Total R3 800.

Notice what these four notes share: each is a family register — members named, amounts attached, total struck — and each total must land on its statement line to the rand. The adjustment children you placed in term three all live here, by name, where any reader can find them.

## Subtopic: The Owner's Equity Note, GAAP and the Indigenous Thread

The OWNER'S EQUITY note tells the jar's story formally: balance at the beginning of the year R20 000; plus additional capital contributed — none this year; plus net profit for the year R7 900 — the pipe from the income statement, cited to the rand; less drawings R4 000; balance at the end R23 900, agreeing with the statement's equity line. Where an owner contributed capital mid-year — cash, or a vehicle brought into the business — it enters this note as a contribution, never as income: the entity's earnings and the owner's investments must never blur.

Now the GAAP thread, gathered. The statements you built apply the principles as a working set: HISTORICAL COST anchored the equipment at R18 000; GOING CONCERN justified carrying values rather than forced-sale prices; MATCHING shaped every adjusted figure; PRUDENCE booked the deficit and the growing loan without delay; the BUSINESS ENTITY rule kept drawings out of expenses and the owner's household out of the books; and disclosure thinking organised material detail into these very notes. When an exam asks "name and explain the GAAP principle applied when...", the answer is one of these, tied to the line it shaped.

And the syllabus asks one more integration: INDIGENOUS BOOKKEEPING. South Africa's stokvels, burial societies and savings clubs have kept trusted records for generations — member books, contribution registers, payout rosters — often without a debit or credit in sight. Set them beside what you built and see the same architecture: regular recording, member-by-member detail agreeing with a running total — a control account and its list, in another dress — and full transparency to every member at every meeting. The formal statements are one tradition of a universal practice: money held for others must be accounted to them. Trust is the product; the format is the language.

The final questions of this part are with you now: the equity note's movements, the principle-to-line pairings, and the shared architecture of formal and indigenous records.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same notes from the stoep — because every family in the township already knows what a note is: it's the detail behind the headline, the list behind the total, the receipts in the shoebox that back up the story.

## Subtopic: The Page and Its Backpages

Think of the two statements as the front page of a newspaper: the headlines. SHOP WORTH R38 900. PROFIT R7 900. Headlines are honest — but nobody signs a contract on a headline. The full stories run on the inside pages, and that is exactly what the notes are: the inside pages of the financial statements.

Each headline carries a little number — like "see page 3" — and on that page, the headline opens up. "Receivables R4 950" — turn to its note: the customers owe R3 850, the tenant owes a month, the insurer owes six months of cover. Same total, now with faces. And the rule that keeps the newspaper honest: the headline and the story must SAY THE SAME THING. If the front page claims R4 950 and the inside page adds to R4 850, the paper has contradicted itself — and in the exam, that contradiction is exactly what the marker hunts for, travelling from line to note and back.

Why not print everything on the front page? Try reading a front page with two hundred names on it. The genius of the design is the two speeds: a one-minute read for the visitor, the full detail for anyone who wants to dig. Both readers get the truth; each gets it at their own depth.

Quick check before we carry on — questions on the front page and the backpages are coming to you right now: what compresses, what unpacks, and what must always agree.

## Subtopic: Unpacking the Suitcases

Picture the statement's family lines as packed suitcases standing in a row. The notes are the unpacking — open each one on the table.

The INVENTORIES case: unzip — R2 250 of goods waiting for customers, R200 of stationery waiting for the office. Sellable and usable, side by side, R2 450 on the tag outside. The RECEIVABLES case: customers' IOUs R3 850, the tenant's late month R500, the insurance paid ahead R600 — everything on its way IN within the year, tag R4 950. The CASH case: the bank's R15 000 and the petty cash tin's R300 — money already home, tag R15 300. The PAYABLES case: the suppliers' R3 200, February's telephone R100, the tenant's early rent R500 — everything knocking to be paid within the year, tag R3 800.

And the biggest case, FIXED ASSETS, has compartments: what the equipment cost when it arrived — R18 000; how much wearing has been charged so far — R1 800; and what's honestly left in the books — R16 200 on the tag. The note even shows the year's journey: arrived this year, worn by R1 800, standing at R16 200 at the photograph.

The rule for every case: the tag OUTSIDE must equal the contents INSIDE, counted to the rand. That's the whole discipline — pack honestly, tag exactly. When you build notes in the exam, you're just doing the packing in reverse: contents listed, tag totalled, tag matched to the statement's line.

## Subtopic: Trust, in Any Language

Last piece — and it reaches beyond the exam. Why does all this exist? The heading says it plainly enough: statements, notes, cross-references... but the real product is TRUST. And South Africa has been manufacturing that product for generations, in books that never heard the words debit or credit.

Visit a stokvel meeting. The treasurer opens the book: every member's name, every contribution, every payout, every date. The running total on the last line — and any member may check her own page against it, any meeting, any time. Hear the echo? A control total and its member list, agreeing — the exact architecture of your debtors control and its list, your notes and their statement lines. The burial society's register, the savings club's roster — the same invention, made independently, because the problem is universal: when you hold money that belongs to others, you must be able to SHOW them, in detail, at any moment, that it is all there.

GAAP is one language for that trust; the stokvel book is another; both are grammars of the same honesty. And both punish the same sin: the tag that doesn't match the contents, the total that can't survive the member's check.

So carry both prides out of this term: you can now build the formal statements that banks and buyers read — and you come from a tradition that understood accountable record-keeping long before it was a school subject. The suit and the stokvel book are cousins.
