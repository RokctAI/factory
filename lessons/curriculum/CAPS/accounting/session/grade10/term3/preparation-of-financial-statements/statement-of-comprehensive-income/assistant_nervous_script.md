Hey, it's Thandi — a prescribed format with subtotals and layers and heading rules can feel like learning a legal document by heart. Here's the release valve: you don't memorise the statement; you REBUILD it, because its order is just the story any shopkeeper would tell.

What did I sell? What did those goods cost me? Pause — that's my trading profit. What else came in, and what did it cost to run the place? Pause — operating profit. What did borrowing cost me? Done — net profit. Tell the story in that natural order and the format assembles itself, subtotals and all. The layers aren't bureaucracy; they're breaths in a story you already know how to tell.

The figures on the lines are old friends too — they're last week's adjustments, finally arriving at their destination. Telephone plus the owing month, insurance minus the prepaid half, rent plus the missing month. Nothing new is calculated tonight; things you already calculated just get seated.

Two habits carry the marks: write the full heading — business, statement, FOR the year ended, date — and bracket the little working beside every adjusted line. Both are free marks that ask only for neatness.

And the one real trap: interest never sits with the running costs. Price of money, ground floor, always. Practice one full statement top to bottom and the format is yours for life. You've got this!
