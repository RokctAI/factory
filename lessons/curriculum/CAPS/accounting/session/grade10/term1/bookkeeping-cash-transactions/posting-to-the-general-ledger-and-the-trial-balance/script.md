# Part 1 — Expert

The journals have gathered a month of transactions into disciplined columns; today that work arrives home. We post the Cash Receipts Journal and Cash Payments Journal to the general ledger, balance the accounts, and prove the whole month with a trial balance. This is the heart of the accounting cycle — and the month we post is Thabo's Trading's actual month, so every figure will be one you have already met.

## Subtopic: The Ledger's Architecture

The general ledger is the book of ACCOUNTS: one account per item the business tracks, each shaped as a T — debit side left, credit side right. Where the journals answered "what happened this month, in order?", the ledger answers "what is the total story of Bank? Of Sales? Of Wages?" — one account, one item, all its movements gathered.

The ledger is organised in two sections, and the trial balance later respects this order. The BALANCE SHEET SECTION holds the accounts that describe what the business owns and owes and the owner's stake: Capital, Drawings, Land and Buildings, Equipment, Trading Stock, Debtors and Creditors when they arrive, and Bank. The NOMINAL SECTION holds the incomes and expenses — Sales, Cost of Sales, Rent Income, Wages, Rent Expense — the accounts that exist to measure this year's performance and will close into the profit calculation at year end. Assets and equity endure; incomes and expenses reset annually. That is the logic of the two sections.

Every posting carries FOLIO references — the paper trail's grammar. In the ledger account, the folio column records which journal page the amount came from: CRJ 1, CPJ 1. In the journal, the folio records which account received the posting. A marker can follow any amount from document to journal to ledger and back; so can you, and so must you when hunting an error.

One discipline before we post: totals post, not lines. The whole design of the column journals was to deliver monthly TOTALS — one Sales figure, one Bank figure — so the ledger receives a handful of postings instead of a hundred. Only sundry items, named individually in the journals, post individually.

Pause here — questions on the ledger's architecture are with you now: sections, folios, and what posts as a total versus by name.

## Subtopic: Posting the Cash Receipts Journal

Take Thabo's completed CRJ: Bank column R56 900; Sales R4 500; Cost of Sales R3 600; Sundries R52 400 — being Capital R50 000 and Rent Income R2 400. Post it, entry by entry, each with its reasoning.

The Bank total: money came IN — the asset Bank grew. Debit Bank R56 900. The details written in the Bank account read "Total receipts", folio CRJ 1.

The Sales total: sales are income, income grows on the credit side. Credit Sales R4 500, folio CRJ 1. A month of selling, one posting — the column earning its keep.

The Cost of Sales total is the double posting to command: R3 600 posts TWICE. Debit Cost of Sales — the expense that measures what the sold goods had cost; and credit Trading Stock — the shelf gave up R3 600 of goods at cost. This pair is the perpetual system's signature in the ledger: sales income above, the cost of earning it alongside, the stock account staying truthful throughout.

The sundries post by name: Capital R50 000 — the owner's stake grew, equity grows on the credit side: credit Capital. Rent Income R2 400 — income: credit Rent Income.

Now audit the month's double entry as a whole, because the journal guaranteed it: debits posted — Bank R56 900. Credits posted — Sales 4 500, Trading Stock 3 600... no. Stop. Trading Stock's credit partners the Cost of Sales DEBIT, not the Bank figure. Run it properly: debits are Bank 56 900 and Cost of Sales 3 600 — total 60 500. Credits are Sales 4 500, Capital 50 000, Rent Income 2 400, Trading Stock 3 600 — total 60 500. Equal. Every rand of debit found its credit; the CRJ posts in perfect balance, and checking this BEFORE moving on is the professional's habit.

Stop for this section's questions now: destinations, sides, and the double posting of cost of sales — reason each answer from "what grew, what shrank".

## Subtopic: Posting the CPJ and Balancing the Accounts

The CPJ's totals: Bank R22 500; Trading Stock R8 000; Wages R3 000; Sundries — Rent Expense R3 500, Equipment R6 000, Drawings R2 000.

Bank total: money OUT — the asset shrank: credit Bank R22 500, "Total payments", folio CPJ 1. Trading Stock total: the shelf loaded at cost: debit Trading Stock R8 000. Wages: an expense, expenses grow on the debit side: debit Wages R3 000. The sundries by name, all debits: Rent Expense R3 500; Equipment R6 000 — an asset acquired; Drawings R2 000 — equity withdrawn, debited because it reduces the owner's stake. Balance check: credits 22 500; debits 8 000 plus 3 000 plus 3 500 plus 6 000 plus 2 000 — 22 500. Balanced.

Now BALANCE the accounts that carry forward. Bank: debit side 56 900, credit side 22 500. The difference, 34 400, is entered on the credit side as "Balance c/d" to equalise the sides, and brought down on the debit side as "Balance b/d" — R34 400, a debit balance: the business has money. Trading Stock: debits 8 000, credits 3 600 — balance b/d R4 400 debit: stock on the shelf at cost. Equipment: R6 000 debit. Capital: R50 000 credit. Drawings: R2 000 debit.

The NOMINAL accounts — Sales, Cost of Sales, Wages, Rent Expense, Rent Income — are not balanced month to month; they simply accumulate, because their job is to measure the period. They will be closed off at year end into the final accounts. For the trial balance, each shows its total to date.

The questions on this section are in front of you now: post, balance, and state each closing balance with its side — reasoning aloud, asset up debit, asset down credit.

## Subtopic: The Trial Balance — Proving the Month

Now the proof. List every account with its balance or total, debits in one column, credits in the other, balance sheet section first, nominal section after.

Debits: Bank 34 400; Trading Stock 4 400; Equipment 6 000; Drawings 2 000; then the nominal debits — Cost of Sales 3 600; Wages 3 000; Rent Expense 3 500. Total: 56 900. Credits: Capital 50 000; then Sales 4 500; Rent Income 2 400. Total: 56 900. The columns agree, and the month stands proven — every debit posted found its credit, every addition survived checking.

Understand precisely what this proves and what it does not — the distinction is a standing exam question. The trial balance proves ARITHMETIC equality: double entry was maintained and the casting is consistent. It does NOT prove the books are correct. Post the R3 500 rent to Repairs instead of Rent Expense — correct side, wrong account — and the trial balance still balances. Leave an entire transaction out of the journals — nothing posted anywhere — and it balances still. The trial balance is a smoke detector, not a full inspection: silence is necessary, never sufficient.

When it does NOT balance, hunt with method, not panic. Take the difference between the columns. A difference equal to one of your amounts: a posting made once instead of twice. A difference divisible by two: an amount posted to the wrong SIDE — it doubles its error. A difference divisible by nine: digits transposed — 3 500 written as 5 300. Three diagnostic questions before any re-adding, and most errors surrender.

The final questions of this part are with you now: build the trial balance, test it, and diagnose the classic failures by their fingerprints.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Same journey now, but let us walk it with luggage in hand — because posting is really just a month's worth of sorted paper finding its way home to a filing cabinet.

## Subtopic: The Filing Cabinet

Picture Thabo's back room. The journals — his two diaries — are the SORTING ROOM: everything that happened this month, in order, receipts on one page, payments on another. Useful for "what happened on the 15th?" — useless for "how much money do I actually have?" To answer THAT, you would have to re-read the whole month every time.

So the back room gets a filing cabinet: one drawer per THING. A drawer for Bank. A drawer for Sales. A drawer for Wages, for Stock, for the fridge, for Thabo's own stake. Posting is nothing more than the monthly trip from the sorting room to the cabinet: carry each column total to its drawer, drop it on the correct side, note where it came from.

The correct side — you know this dance from the double-entry lesson: each drawer has a left page and a right page, and every kind of drawer has its growing side. Money drawers and possession drawers grow on the left. Drawers about what the business owes, and the owner's-stake drawer, grow on the right. Earning drawers grow right; spending drawers grow left. Nothing new — the drawers just make the habit permanent.

And the little reference codes — CRJ 1, CPJ 1 — written next to every amount? Those are the return tickets. Any number in any drawer can tell you exactly which diary page it travelled from, so a mistake can be chased backwards in seconds. A cabinet without return tickets is a cabinet you can never audit.

Quick check before we carry on — questions on the cabinet are coming to you right now: which drawer, which side, and why.

## Subtopic: One Month Comes Home

Now carry Thabo's actual month to the cabinet, total by total, and watch the drawers fill.

From the money-IN diary: R56 900 arrived — into the Bank drawer, left side, one line. The R4 500 of sales — into the Sales drawer, right side, because Sales is an earning drawer. The cost-of-sales pair moves together, exactly as it did at the till: R3 600 onto the left of the Cost of Sales drawer — what the sold goods had cost — and R3 600 onto the right of the Stock drawer, because the shelf gave those goods up. And the two named visitors go home by name: R50 000 to the right of Capital — Thabo's stake grew; R2 400 to the right of Rent Income.

From the money-OUT diary: R22 500 left — Bank drawer, right side. R8 000 of new stock — Stock drawer, left side; the shelf loaded up. R3 000 of wages — Wages drawer, left. And the visitors: rent R3 500 left of Rent Expense; the fridge R6 000 left of Equipment; Thabo's R2 000 left of Drawings.

Now ask the cabinet the owner's favourite question: how much money do we have? Open the Bank drawer: left page 56 900, right page 22 500. Difference: R34 400, sitting on the left — the business HAS thirty-four thousand four hundred rand. One drawer, one subtraction, instant answer — the very question the sorting room could never answer without re-reading the month. Do the same for Stock: 8 000 in, 3 600 out — R4 400 of goods on the shelf at cost. The cabinet knows everything, drawer by drawer.

Your questions for this part are up now: carry the totals home yourself — drawer, side, and the balance the drawer then shows.

## Subtopic: The Weigh-In

Last move of the month: prove the cabinet. Here is the beautiful fact hiding under everything you have done since the double-entry lesson: every amount that ever travelled went to TWO drawers — a left somewhere, a right somewhere, always equal. So if the month was carried home faithfully, all the left-side balances added together must weigh exactly the same as all the right-side balances.

That weigh-in is the trial balance. List every drawer and its balance: lefts — Bank 34 400, Stock 4 400, Equipment 6 000, Drawings 2 000, Cost of Sales 3 600, Wages 3 000, Rent Expense 3 500. Rights — Capital 50 000, Sales 4 500, Rent Income 2 400. Add each side: 56 900 and 56 900. The scales sit level, and Thabo's first month is proven — not guessed, PROVEN.

Two honest warnings about the scales, so you never over-trust them. Level scales cannot see a filing error: put the rent into the Repairs drawer — wrong drawer, correct side — and the scales stay level, wrong. And they cannot see what never arrived: forget a transaction entirely, both halves missing, and level they remain. The scales test the WEIGHING, not the FILING. That is why the diaries keep their return tickets and the documents keep their numbers — different checks for different errors, layered.

But when the scales DON'T level, they even whisper what went wrong: a gap equal to one of your amounts — something was dropped once; a gap that halves neatly — something sat on the wrong side; a gap divisible by nine — two digits swapped places somewhere. Read the gap before re-adding anything.

And here come the last questions of the lesson, right now: weigh the cabinet, read the gaps, and say what level scales do and do not prove. You have just closed your first full month — sorting room to cabinet to level scales. That is the whole cycle's engine room, and you drove it.
