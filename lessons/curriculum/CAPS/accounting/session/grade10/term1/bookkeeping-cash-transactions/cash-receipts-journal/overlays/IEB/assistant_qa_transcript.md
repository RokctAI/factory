**Thandi:** Time for the class questions! First one, straight from my own notes: if the money is banked anyway, why bother with the analysis column at all?

**Tutor:** Because the analysis column answers a question the Bank column cannot: what arrived, moment by moment, before it was banked? Money lands in a business in pieces through the day; the deposit happens once. The analysis column lists the pieces; the Bank column shows the deposit. Now suppose the pieces add to two thousand rand but the deposit shows one thousand eight hundred — the analysis column has just exposed a two-hundred-rand gap between the counter and the bank, and it names the exact day. Remove the column and that gap becomes invisible. It is the shop's own security camera, written in figures.

***

**Thandi:** Next: for the cost of sales, why do we divide by one comma five instead of just taking fifty percent off the selling price?

**Tutor:** Because the fifty percent was never a slice of the selling price — it was added on top of the COST. Build the price forwards and you see it: cost of one hundred rand, add half of the cost, fifty rand, price is one hundred and fifty. The mark-up is fifty percent OF THE COST, but only a third of the selling price. So to reverse the journey you must undo the multiplication: divide by one comma five. Subtracting fifty percent of the price takes off too much — on our R1 800 sale it gives R900 instead of the true R1 200. Always divide by one plus the mark-up rate, then multiply back to check you land on the selling price again.

***

**Thandi:** Here's one that puzzled the back bench: the cost of sales column sits in a CASH journal, but you said no cash moves for it. Isn't it in the wrong book?

**Tutor:** It rides in this book because it is born at the same instant as the cash. The moment the till takes R1 800, the shelf loses R1 200 of stock — one event, two truths. The perpetual inventory system insists both truths are captured immediately, and the CRJ line is where the event lives, so the cost travels on that line as a passenger. But it keeps its distance from the money arithmetic: it stays out of the cross-cast, and at month end it posts to Cost of Sales and Trading Stock, never to Bank. Same line, separate lane — a stock record hitching a lift in a cash book.

***

**Thandi:** Last question: what actually goes wrong if someone skips the cross-cast and just posts the totals?

**Tutor:** Then any error in the journal is laundered into the ledger wearing a clean shirt. Say a receipt reached the Bank column but its sundry entry was never written. The journal is internally inconsistent — but posting does not know that. The wrong totals land in the ledger, the trial balance may even still balance, and weeks later somebody spends an evening hunting a difference that a ten-second cross-cast would have caught at the source. The rule of the month end: total, prove, and only then post. The cross-cast is the journal's own handshake — never post a book that has not shaken hands with itself.
