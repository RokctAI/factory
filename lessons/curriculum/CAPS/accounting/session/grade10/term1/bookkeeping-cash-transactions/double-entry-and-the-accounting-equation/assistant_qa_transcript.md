**Thandi:** Class questions! The big one first: the owner GAVE the business money — that feels like a positive thing, so why is Capital a credit?

**Tutor:** Discard the idea that debit means good and credit means bad — they are only the left and right sides of an account, nothing more. Then remember whose eyes the books look through: the business's, never the owner's. The business received one hundred thousand rand — its Bank asset grew, and assets grow on the debit side. But the business did not get that money for nothing: it now, in effect, owes its existence to the owner — the owner holds a claim against it. That claim is owner's equity, and equity grows on the credit side. So the deposit is entirely positive AND Capital is credited — the two statements never conflicted; only the good-versus-bad myth made them seem to.

***

**Thandi:** Next: buying the equipment had "no effect on the equation" — so a few of us wrote "no entries needed" and lost the marks. Where is the flaw?

**Tutor:** The flaw is reading "no NET effect" as "no events". Inside the owned pile, two real movements occurred: Equipment grew by thirty thousand rand and Bank shrank by thirty thousand rand. Both must be recorded — debit Equipment, credit Bank — because each ACCOUNT'S story must be true, not merely the total. Skip the entries and the books claim money the business no longer has and omit equipment it certainly owns; every individual balance is wrong while the grand total smiles innocently. The equation staying level is the RESULT of two equal recordings — never a permission to skip them.

***

**Thandi:** This one trips everyone: paying the creditor five thousand rand — we DEBIT Creditors Control? Liabilities are credit accounts!

**Tutor:** Liabilities GROW on the credit side — and shrink on the opposite side, which is the debit. Paying the creditor makes the debt smaller: a liability decreasing, entered on the debit side of Creditors Control. And use the cross-check that never fails: the other account in the transaction is Bank, and money went out, so Bank must be credited. Since every transaction demands one debit and one credit, Creditors Control has no choice but to take the debit. When either side of an entry is certain, the other side is decided for you — let the certain half discipline the doubtful half.

***

**Thandi:** Last one: where do wages and rent fit? They are not things the business owns, and not money it owes — are they a secret fourth pile?

**Tutor:** No fourth pile exists — expenses and incomes live INSIDE owner's equity, as its movements. The owner's stake is not frozen: every income the business earns fattens it, every expense thins it. Wages of two thousand rand: Bank down — credit; and the owner's pocket thins through the Wages expense — debit, because the shrinking side of equity is the debit side. At year end all those small swellings and thinnings gather into the profit, which flows into Capital — the pocket, formally updated. So when you meet any income or expense, translate silently: this is the owner's pocket, growing or shrinking, wearing work clothes.
