Hey, it's Thandi — two journals in one lesson is a proper workout, so if your head is juggling columns and tins and vouchers, let's set them down gently.

The CPJ is just the money-out diary — the mirror twin of the one you already built. Same skeleton: date, proof, who, how much; regulars get lanes, visitors get named; and the handshake at the end proves the month. You didn't learn a new system today — you learned the same system facing the other way.

The one genuinely new muscle is the sorting question: what did the SHOP get? Say it before classifying any payment. Stock or a fridge — possession. Wages or rent — consumed service, expense. The owner's own pocket — drawings, never an expense. One question, three answers, all the marks.

And the petty cash tin? Cash plus slips equals the float, always — the tin does its own auditing; you just count.

Go run the practice questions: sort first, columns second, handshake last. Three habits, and this whole lesson is yours. You've got this!
