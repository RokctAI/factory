**Student:** I keep mixing up the ledger and the trial balance. Aren't they both just lists of accounts?

**Thandi:** I see why they blur together, but they do totally different jobs! The ledger is where the action happens — every account is a little T-shaped battlefield where debits and credits fight it out until you get a balance. The trial balance does no recording at all. It's just a *checklist*: you copy every ledger balance into one page, debits in one column, credits in the other, and check that the two columns are equal. Think of the ledger as doing the work and the trial balance as marking the work.

***

**Student:** Why can't we skip the journals and just write transactions straight into the ledger? It feels like double work.

**Thandi:** Great question — it *does* feel like double work until you picture a real month. Say the shop makes eighty cash sales in June. If you posted each one separately, the Bank account would have eighty entries and eighty chances for a mistake. The journal collects all eighty in one place, you total the column once, and only that single total gets posted. Journals also sort transactions by type, so if something goes missing you know exactly which book to search. Less posting, fewer errors, easier checking.

***

**Student:** The lesson said a balancing trial balance doesn't mean the books are correct. Then what's the point of it?

**Thandi:** It catches a specific family of errors — the arithmetic ones. If you posted only the debit half of an entry, or wrote R450 on one side and R540 on the other, the columns won't agree, and the trial balance waves a red flag before you build statements on broken numbers. What it *can't* see is an entry that's wrong but balanced, like posting rent to the wrong expense account. So it's a smoke detector, not a full inspection — still very worth having!

***

**Student:** Do I do the whole cycle every single day?

**Thandi:** No — and this is a nice thing to realise! The stages run on different clocks. Source documents and journal entries happen daily as transactions occur. Posting to the ledger and the trial balance happen monthly, when you total the journal columns. Final accounts and financial statements happen once a year, at the end of the financial year. So the cycle spins constantly at the front, monthly in the middle, and yearly at the end.
