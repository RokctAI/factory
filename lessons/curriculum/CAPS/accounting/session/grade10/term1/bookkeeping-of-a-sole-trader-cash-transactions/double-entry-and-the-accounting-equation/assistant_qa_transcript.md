**Student:** I don't get why capital is a credit. The owner GAVE money to the business — surely that's a positive thing, so it should be a debit?

**Thandi:** Ah, you're thinking of debit as "good" and credit as "bad" — throw that idea away completely, it will haunt you forever! Debit and credit are just left and right, nothing more. Now remember whose eyes we look through: the *business's*, never the owner's. The business received R100 000 in its bank — Bank (asset) goes up, debit. But the business didn't get that money for free; it now effectively owes its whole existence to the owner. That claim the owner has on the business is owner's equity, and equity lives on the credit side. Positive thing? Absolutely. Credit? Also absolutely.

***

**Student:** How can buying equipment have "no effect" on the equation but still need two entries? That feels like a contradiction.

**Thandi:** It's a sneaky one! "No effect" only means no effect on the *totals*. Inside assets, two very real things happened: Equipment went up R30 000 and Bank went down R30 000. Imagine moving R30 000 from your left pocket to your right pocket — your total money didn't change, but both pockets definitely did. We record what happened to each pocket: debit Equipment, credit Bank. The equation staying still is the *result* of the two entries cancelling, not a reason to skip them.

***

**Student:** When we pay a creditor, why do we DEBIT Creditors Control? I thought liabilities are credit accounts.

**Thandi:** You're halfway there — liabilities *increase* on the credit side, yes. But decreases always go on the *opposite* side to increases. Paying R5 000 makes the debt smaller, so the liability decreases, and that decrease is written on the debit side. Here's a quick sanity check you can always use: the other account in the transaction is Bank, and money went out, so Bank must be credited. Since every transaction needs one debit and one credit, Creditors Control *has* to take the debit. The two entries lock each other in place.

***

**Student:** Where do things like wages and rent fit in? They're not assets, not liabilities... are they a fourth thing?

**Thandi:** Great instinct — they feel like a fourth thing, but they're actually hiding inside O. Owner's equity isn't frozen; it grows when the business earns income and shrinks when the business pays expenses. Wages of R2 000? Owner's equity takes the knock, so the expense is debited (the decrease side of O), and Bank is credited. Come year end, all those little income and expense accounts get gathered up into profit, and the profit flows into Capital. So incomes and expenses are just owner's equity in motion during the year.
