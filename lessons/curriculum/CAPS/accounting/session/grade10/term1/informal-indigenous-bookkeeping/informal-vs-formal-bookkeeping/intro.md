Before a single journal is ruled, Accounting asks a grounding question: how do businesses with no formal books — the spaza shop, the street vendor — still manage money successfully, and what do their methods share with the registered supermarket's ledgers? Today we compare the informal and formal sectors concept by concept: capital, fixed assets, stock, cost and selling price, labour cost, income, expenses and profit — definitions that carry marks all year. We will see that the concepts are identical and only the recording differs, then trace exactly where records bite: proof for the bank, accuracy against leaking expenses, separation of business and owner, and continuity. We close with indigenous practices — the stokvel, cattle wealth, communal grazing rules — and find modern bookkeeping principles living inside every one of them.
