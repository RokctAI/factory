Today we study the rulebook behind every figure in a set of financial statements: the GAAP principles — Generally Accepted Accounting Practice. Grade 10 demands six of them — historical cost, prudence, materiality, the business entity rule, going concern and matching — and the exam almost never asks you to recite a list; it hands you a scenario and asks which principle is at work and why. So for each principle we learn the rule, the reason, and the tell-tale signs that identify it, starting with a piece of land bought for R500 000 that an agent now values at R900 000 yet still sits in the books at R500 000. By the end, matching scenario to principle will feel like recognising a face.
