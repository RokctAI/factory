Hey, it's Thandi — five components, a toolkit of controls, scenario methods... if that felt like a lot of rules to hold, let's shrink it to something you can carry.

The whole topic is five plain questions: Who sets the example? What could go wrong? What rules stop it? Does everyone know? Who checks? That's it. The fancy names — control environment, risk assessment and friends — are just those questions in formal dress. If you can ask the five questions about a tuck shop, you can answer them about any business an exam invents.

And remember the heart of it: internal control isn't about suspecting people. It's the system that PROVED the tuck-shop kids honest when the box balanced. Rules that protect the good ones — that's a system worth understanding.

For the practice questions, lean on the three moves: find the gap, say what could go wrong, recommend who should do what differently. Specific beats vague every single time. Go patrol those scenarios — you've got this!
