**Thandi:** Class questions! First up, the one on half the mini whiteboards: who issues the debit note and who issues the credit note? People keep swapping them.

**Tutor:** Anchor it to whose books are talking. A debit note is issued by the BUYER — Lindi — announcing: I am debiting your account in MY books; I owe you less, and here is the evidence, the damaged box. The credit note is issued by the SELLER — remember last lesson, Lindi issued credit notes to her own customers — announcing: I am crediting your account in MY books; you owe me less. Same event, both sides eventually hold both papers: Lindi's debit note goes out, Coastal's confirming credit note comes back. But each business records from its OWN document on its OWN timeline — that is why the debit note exists at all: the buyer does not sit waiting for the supplier's paperwork before correcting the debt.

***

**Thandi:** Next: why does Coastal's page in our books grow on the credit side, when Mr Sithole's page grew on the debit side? It feels inconsistent.

**Tutor:** It is perfectly consistent — with the equation, not with a habit. Ask what each page represents. Sithole's page is money coming TO us: an asset, and assets grow on the debit side. Coastal's page is money going FROM us: a liability, and liabilities grow on the credit side. The page format looks identical, but the pages stand on opposite sides of A equals O plus L, so their growing sides must be opposite too. Test yourself with the returns: send damaged goods back and Coastal's page is DEBITED — the liability shrinks toward zero. Every side falls out of one question: which way does the debt point?

***

**Thandi:** Here's the sneaky one: when we buy stock on credit, why is there no cost of sales entry? Selling always had two numbers.

**Tutor:** Because cost of sales is born at the moment of SELLING, not the moment of owning. When goods arrive, one thing happens: the shelf gains stock at cost — debit Trading Stock, credit the creditor. One truth, complete. The famous second number only appears when goods LEAVE at a selling price while having cost something different — that gap between price and cost is what the Cost of Sales account measures, and it does not exist on the buying side, where goods come in at cost and, if returned, go back at cost. So enjoy the simplicity: buying writes one number; selling writes two. If you ever find yourself entering cost of sales in the Creditors Journal, stop — you have wandered across the counter.

***

**Thandi:** Last question: paying early sounds responsible — why did the lesson call it a mistake?

**Tutor:** Because credit terms are part of the price, and a smart buyer uses everything that was paid for. If Coastal grants thirty days, the store is entitled to hold its cash for thirty days — cash that buys the month's small stock runs, covers wages, absorbs surprises. Pay on day three and that entire month of free financing evaporates, for zero reward. The discipline is a calendar with two fences: never late — late payment damages the store's name and future terms — and never needlessly early — unless an actual settlement discount makes early payment worth more than the cash. On terms, on time, exactly. The professional pays with a calendar, not with nerves.
