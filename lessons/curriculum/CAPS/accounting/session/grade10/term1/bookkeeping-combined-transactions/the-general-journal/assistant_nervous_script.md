Hey, it's Thandi — the General Journal can feel scarier than the others precisely because it has no columns doing the thinking for you. Blank page, your call. But flip that around: you already OWN the thinking it asks for.

Every GJ entry is just the five-step analysis from week one — which two accounts, what kind, up or down, which sides, does the equation hold — written out in the journal's little two-line shape with a sentence underneath. You've been practising this skill since before the journals existed. The GJ is its graduation, not a new subject.

And the classics come down to three questions you can always ask: Did something die? (Bad debt — asset gone, expense born.) Did the owner take something? (Drawings — at cost, always.) Did lateness earn its price? (Interest — pointing whichever way the slowness went.)

For corrections, breathe and be a surgeon: what WAS done, what SHOULD have been, fix only the broken leg, write the sentence. Never redo the healthy side.

Go take the practice questions slowly — analysis first, entry second, narration last. Three beats per question, full marks per beat. You've got this!
