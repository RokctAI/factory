Hey, it's Thandi — revision time, and if a whole year of bookkeeping feels like too much to hold, breathe: it's five habits, not fifty topics. Sort the slips. Write the diaries. Gather the ledger. Weigh the trial balance. Count the street twice. That's the entire machine, and every exam question is just the machine with new numbers in it.

Two sorting questions place any transaction: did cash move or a promise, and which way did the value travel? Answer those and the journal chooses itself — you never have to memorise which slip goes where again.

For the ledger and trial balance, remember what the level scale can and can't do. Equal totals prove your pairs were equal — they do NOT prove the pairs went to the right accounts. Examiners love asking exactly that, so say it in their words: agreement proves double entry, not correctness.

And control accounts — the trick is sources. Control comes from journal TOTALS, the list from INDIVIDUAL postings. When they disagree, ask which source the error touched, and the correction writes itself.

Run one small month end to end tonight — even the tiny one from the lesson, eight transactions, balancing at twenty thousand a side. Once the machine runs under your own pen, the paper holds no surprises. You've got this!
