Hey, it's Thandi — year-end revision can feel like four exams stacked on top of each other, so let's shrink it to four moves you already know.

Move one: adjustments. Every single one is the same trick — fix the income or expense to the TRUE year figure, and park the leftover on the balance sheet. Owed by us, current liability. Owned by us — prepaid cover, rent still coming — current asset. Depreciation's only twist: straight-line works on COST, diminishing balance on CARRYING VALUE. Say those two words before you touch the calculator.

Move two: the statements. Income statement is a story — sales, gross profit, net profit. Balance sheet is a photo — and if your photo doesn't balance, go back to the adjustments first; that's where photos break.

Move three: the ratios. The formula is half the mark; the COMMENT is the other half, and the comment is always one sentence: figure, comparison, judgement. Two to one, better than last year, healthy. Practise saying it in that rhythm until it's automatic.

Move four: the guards and the small arithmetic. Controls: name the risk, then the fix. Unit cost: everything the batch consumed, divided by the units. Budget: cash only, closing becomes opening.

Four moves, one business, every figure balancing. Do one full past paper this way and the panic turns into a checklist. You've got this!
