Today we open the factory door and ask the maker's first question: what does one unit actually cost to produce? Using a workshop sewing four hundred school shirts a month, we sort every cost twice — direct or indirect by whether it traces to one unit, fixed or variable by how it behaves when output changes — then stack the layers the formula sheet expects: prime cost, factory overheads, total cost of production, and the unit cost that puts a floor under every price. The what-if calculation at a new volume is where markers catch learners scaling fixed costs that stand still, so behaviour discipline is drilled throughout. Master the two sortings and the arithmetic is short; they carry straight into next year's manufacturing accounts and every pricing decision after that.
