Hey, it's Thandi — a whole factory's worth of new words in one lesson: direct, indirect, prime, overheads, fixed, variable, work-in-progress... Deep breath. Under the vocabulary, tonight was TWO questions and ONE division.

Question one: can I point at ONE unit and say "this cost is yours"? Yes — direct. No — it's an overhead, a cost of keeping the kitchen cooking. That single pointing question sorts every cost the exam can list. Fabric? Point at the shirt — direct. Supervisor? She watches everything — overhead. Done.

Question two: if we make MORE, does this cost grow? Yes — variable. No, it stands there like rent — fixed. Same pointing energy, different direction.

Then the one division: stack the direct costs (prime), add the overheads (total), divide by the units — unit cost. Three labelled lines. You've been doing harder arithmetic since term one.

The only real trap: in what-if questions, fixed costs DON'T scale. When output rises from 400 to 500, the fabric bill grows but the rent just... sits there. Write "fixed: R6 000 (unchanged)" as its own line and the trap can't touch you.
