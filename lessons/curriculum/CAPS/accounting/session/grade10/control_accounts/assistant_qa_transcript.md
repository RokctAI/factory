**Student:** How do I remember which side of the Debtors Control account things go on? I keep guessing.

**Thandi:** Stop memorising sides and start asking the magic question: "after this transaction, do debtors owe us more or less?" Debtors are an asset, and assets grow on the debit side. So credit sales — they owe us more — go on the debit side. Payments received, returns, bad debts written off — they owe us less — all go on the credit side. If you ask that question every single time, you'll never need to guess again.

***

**Student:** Why is a bad debt on the credit side? We didn't receive any money, so why does the account go down?

**Thandi:** This one trips up so many people! The credit side isn't "money received" — it's "debtors owe us less". When we write off R900 as a bad debt, we're admitting that debtor is never going to pay. So we stop counting the R900 as an asset. The amount leaves Debtors Control (credit) and lands in Bad Debts, which is an expense — the business absorbs the loss. No cash moved anywhere; we just stopped pretending the money was coming.

***

**Student:** If the control account and the debtors list must always be equal, why do we need both? Isn't one of them a waste of time?

**Thandi:** They're only useful *because* there are two of them! They're built from the same transactions by two different routes — totals into the control account, individual entries into the personal accounts. If someone makes a mistake on one route, the two answers stop matching, and that mismatch is your alarm bell. One record alone can be wrong quietly forever. Two independent records that must agree? That's internal control — the books checking each other.

***

**Student:** For Creditors Control, do I have to learn a whole new set of rules?

**Thandi:** Good news: no! It's a perfect mirror. Creditors are a liability — we owe *them* — so the balance lives on the credit side instead. Credit purchases mean we owe more: credit the account. Payments and returns to suppliers mean we owe less: debit the account. Same magic question, just flipped: "do we owe creditors more or less?" Master debtors, flip the mirror, and creditors comes free.
