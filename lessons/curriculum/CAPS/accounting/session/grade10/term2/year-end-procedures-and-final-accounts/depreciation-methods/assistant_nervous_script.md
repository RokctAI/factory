Hey, it's Thandi — depreciation has a scary reputation, mostly because of that phrase "diminishing balance". But strip the words away and today was three small skills, and you already have all three.

Skill one: keep three numbers apart — receipt, wear, worth. Cost never moves. Accumulated grows. Carrying value is just the subtraction. When a question asks for one, check which one it named before you answer — that alone rescues more marks than any formula.

Skill two: percentages — Grade 8 maths. The only decision is WHICH number gets the percentage. "On cost": the original price, every year, same answer forever. "On carrying value": whatever is left, so write the leftover down first, THEN take the percentage. Say the base out loud before you cut. That's the entire difference between the methods.

Skill three: months. A machine that arrived in September only worked six months, so it only earns six-twelfths of a year's wear. Count on your fingers if you like — the examiner literally cannot see how you counted, only that the fraction is in your working.

The asset register? Not a new system — it's the debtors-list trick again: detail pages that must agree with the ledger's total. You've reconciled before.

The practice questions are years and bases and fractions, over and over. Name the number, slice it, label the answer. You've got this!
