Hey, it's Thandi — year-end procedures, two trial balances, closing transfers... if it felt like the term suddenly stacked everything into one lesson, breathe: the whole thing is a five-step recipe, and the recipe never changes.

Prove the books balance. Tidy them to match reality. Prove they still balance. Squeeze out the profit through the two sieves. Hand the profit to the owner. That's it — every year-end question ever set is some slice of those five steps, in that order.

The adjustments scare people most, so shrink them too: Grade 10 gives you exactly two, and each is one honest sentence. "We didn't USE all the stationery" — so R200 of expense becomes a little asset. "The shelf disagrees with the book" — so the book comes down and the loss gets a name. Each is a normal double entry, both sides, always. Write both sides and the topic's most famous lost mark can't touch you.

And the final accounts aren't new maths — they're term one's closing transfers doing ceremony. Sales and cost meet in Trading: gross profit. Everything else meets in Profit and Loss: net profit. Net profit goes home to Capital.

The practice questions hand you a trial balance and two adjustments. Recipe, in order, both sides of every entry, principle named when asked. You've run whole months before — this is just the year doing the same thing once, with a bow on it. You've got this!
