**Thandi:** Questions from the class before we close! First: if the trial balance already balanced BEFORE the adjustments, why do we still need to adjust? Balanced means correct, doesn't it?

**Tutor:** Balanced means arithmetically equal — never more. The pre-adjustment trial balance can level perfectly while the Stationery account claims R1 000 was consumed when R200 still sits in the cupboard, and while Trading Stock claims R2 400 when the shelf holds R2 250. Those are not double-entry failures; both sides of every original entry were faithfully recorded. They are REALITY failures — the ledger describing the year as documented, while year-end fact has moved on without a document. The trial balance tests the books against themselves; the adjustments test the books against the shop. A statement built on the unadjusted figures would balance beautifully and overstate the profit by every untidied rand — which is why the run tidies first and reports second.

***

**Thandi:** Next: the stationery adjustment. Why does the unused R200 become an ASSET? It's just pens and paper — surely it was an expense the day we paid for it.

**Tutor:** Ask what an asset is: something of value the business owns that will serve FUTURE trading. The R800 that was written on, posted and used up served THIS year — that is this year's expense, and matching insists this year's statement carries it. But the untouched R200 has given the business nothing yet; its service lies entirely in next year, when it will be used. Leave it as a current expense and this year is charged for consumption that never happened, while next year uses the paper free. The adjustment simply moves each rand to the year it serves: R800 stays as the expense of the year that consumed it, R200 crosses the line as Consumable Stores on Hand — a small asset that next year will convert into its own expense, by use. Paid-for does not mean used-up; the expense follows the using, not the paying.

***

**Thandi:** This one had the class divided: why does the physical count of R2 250 override the account's R2 400? The account was built from a whole year of careful perpetual records!

**Tutor:** Because the two figures make different KINDS of claim. The account is a prediction: given everything documented, R2 400 SHOULD be on the shelf. The count is an observation: R2 250 IS on the shelf. When a prediction meets an observation, the observation wins — no document survives contact with an empty shelf. And far from insulting the perpetual system, the count completes it: without the running balance of R2 400 there would be no benchmark, and the missing R150 would have no name, no size, and no chance of being noticed at all. The system predicts, the stocktake tests, and the gap between them becomes a Trading Stock Deficit — recognised now, by prudence, not hoped away. Books that always agreed with the shelf would mean nobody was really counting.

***

**Thandi:** Last one: after all the transfers, the Sales account and every expense account stand at ZERO. Doesn't that erase the year's history?

**Tutor:** The history is not erased — it is PROMOTED. Every rand those accounts held now lives on, summarised: the trading story as the gross profit inside the year's records, the whole year's result as R11 500 sitting permanently in Capital, and the journals and ledgers of the year remain as the audit trail behind it. What the closing transfers actually protect is the MEANING of next year's figures. Nominal accounts measure flows — sales OF A PERIOD, expenses OF A PERIOD — and a measurement of a period must start each period empty, or March's sales would sit on top of last year's and no one could ever say what THIS year earned. Assets, liabilities and capital carry forward, because owning continues across the line; earning starts fresh. Empty pages for the new year are not amnesia — they are the ruler being set back to zero so the next measurement is true.
