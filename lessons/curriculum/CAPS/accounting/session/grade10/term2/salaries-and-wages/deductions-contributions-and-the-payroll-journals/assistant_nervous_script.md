Hey, it's Thandi — five deductions, four contributions, two journals, and then ethics on top... if payroll felt like the busiest lesson of the term, let's shrink it to its skeleton, because it has one.

Everything hangs on ONE sorting question: whose envelope does this amount touch? If it comes OUT of the worker's gross — PAYE, pension, UIF, medical, union — it's a deduction, and it builds the gap between gross and net. If the BOSS pays it on top — the matching pension, matching UIF, matching medical, and the SDL — it's a contribution, and the worker's net never feels it. Sort first, calculate second. Every mark in this topic starts with that sort.

The journal part is less new than it looks: gross is the expense, net goes to the bank, and everything withheld just becomes a small creditor — SARS, the fund, the scheme — paid off through the CPJ days later. You've been making creditors and paying them since term one; these ones just have famous names.

And the numbers themselves? Percentages of gross and fixed amounts — Grade 8 arithmetic wearing a payslip. The practice questions will hand you a gross and a list; sort each item into its envelope, add carefully, and the net, the total cost, and the journal row all fall out of the same little table. One sort, one table. You've got this!
