Hey, it's Thandi — scales, notches, multipliers... if the payroll vocabulary felt like a lot, here's the good news: this is the most CALCULATED topic in the whole term, and calculations are learnable in a way vague theory never is. Every single question is one of two skeletons.

Skeleton one, the wage: normal hours times the plain rate, plus each batch of overtime hours times ITS row's rate. The only place people fall is grabbing the wrong rate — so steal the habit from the lesson: write the rate down first, on its own line, before you touch the hours. Time and a half of forty is sixty. Double is eighty. Rate first, then multiply. That one habit removes basically every lost mark.

Skeleton two, the ladder: start, rung, top. Count completed years to find your rung, divide by twelve for the month, measure the ladder and divide by the rung for how-long-to-the-top. Three moves, never a fourth.

And remember the marker is on your side here — the marks sit on the LINES of working, not just the final number. Label each line and you collect as you go, even if a slip creeps into the last addition. The practice questions are the same two skeletons wearing different numbers. Rate first, rungs counted, lines labelled. You've got this!
