Hey, it's Thandi — if the reconciliation felt like a maze of "correct the control, no wait, correct the list", take a breath, because the whole topic is really ONE question asked over and over: where did the error happen?

That's it. Error in a TOTAL — the control drank the bad number, the pages never did, so fix the control. Error on a PAGE — the pages drank it, the totals never did, so fix the list. Error in the original line — both drank it, fix both. Every finding in every exam scenario answers to that one question, and once it's classified, the arithmetic is just adding and subtracting small amounts you're given.

And the direction part — up or down, debit or credit — you never memorise. You reason it fresh each time: what did the error DO to the balance? Then undo exactly that. On the creditors side everything mirrors, which is exactly why reasoning beats memorising: the memoriser copies the debtors fix and loses the mark; you'll derive it and take the mark.

One more comfort: the check is built in. If your two statements don't end on the same figure, the question itself is telling you to look again — you can't silently get this wrong. The practice cases are small: two starting numbers, three or four findings each. Classify, correct, meet in the middle. You've got this!
