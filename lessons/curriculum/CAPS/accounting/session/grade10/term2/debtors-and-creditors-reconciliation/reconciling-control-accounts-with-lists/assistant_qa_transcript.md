**Thandi:** Questions from the class before we close! First up: when the control account and the list disagree, isn't the control account the "official" one? It's in the general ledger, after all.

**Tutor:** Official in location, yes — but not automatically correct, and that assumption loses marks every year. The control account is built from journal column totals; the list from personal accounts posted line by line. Two separate journeys from the same documents, and an error can happen on either journey — a miscast total corrupts the control while the pages stay right, and a misposted line corrupts a page while the totals stay right. In our own worked case, BOTH records were wrong: control at R7 440, list at R7 130, and the truth was R7 500 — a figure neither of them held. The general ledger's prestige protects nothing; only the evidence decides which record moves.

***

**Thandi:** Next one: how do you actually TELL whether an error belongs to the control side or the list side? In the exam the findings just sit there in a paragraph.

**Tutor:** Ask one question of each finding: where did the error physically happen? If it happened in a TOTAL — a column undercast or overcast, a total posted to the wrong place — then the pages, posted earlier from the correct individual lines, never saw it: control side. If it happened in POSTING TO A PAGE — wrong person's account, transposed digits on the page, a line never carried over — then the totals, struck from correct lines, never saw it: list side. And if it happened in the original journal LINE, before any totalling, both records inherited it: correct both. The wording in the scenario always tells you the location — "the column was undercast" points at totals; "posted to her account as" points at pages. Read for the place, not the amount.

***

**Thandi:** This one caused actual arguments: the overcast creditors column in the CPJ. Half of us corrected the control by debiting it. Why is it a credit?

**Tutor:** Because you must undo what the error DID, and on the creditors side you derive that fresh. The CPJ's creditors column is posted as a DEBIT to Creditors Control — payments shrink what we owe. Overcast that column and control received too LARGE a debit, so its credit balance ended R90 too small — understated. To restore it, reverse the excess: credit Creditors Control R90, lifting it to R5 330. The learners who debited were transplanting a debtors-side habit into the mirror. Never carry a direction across; re-derive it from what the posting actually did to the balance. Examiners set the creditors version precisely to catch the copiers.

***

**Thandi:** Last one: if the trial balance already balances, haven't we proved the books are fine? Why does this reconciliation still find errors?

**Tutor:** Because the trial balance and the reconciliation test different promises. The trial balance tests that total debits equal total credits across the general ledger — and every error in today's case kept that promise. An undercast column posts equally short to both sides; an unposted personal entry never touches the general ledger's equality at all. The reconciliation tests something the trial balance cannot see: that the summary and its detail agree — the control account against the pages that are supposed to explain it. Different guard, different gate. That is why a well-run business needs both, every month: the trial balance catches the double-entry slips, the reconciliation catches the journey slips, and what escapes one is caught by the other.
