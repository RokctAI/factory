# Part 1 — Expert

Term one built six journals, two subsidiary ledgers, a general ledger and a trial balance — each in its own lesson, each in isolation. Real bookkeeping never runs them in isolation: a live month throws cash sales, credit purchases, returns, receipts and write-offs at you in one stream, and the skill the examinations now demand is ROUTING — reading any transaction and sending it through the right door, to the right ledgers, without hesitation. Today we run a full combined month, beginning to trial balance, and integrate the control and ethics thread that examiners attach to it.

## Subtopic: Six Journals, One System

First, the map on one page. Every transaction enters the books through exactly one journal, and the choice is decided by two questions: did MONEY move, and in which DIRECTION — or if no money moved, what happened?

Money received — Cash Receipts Journal, whatever the reason: cash sales, capital, rent income, and now also receipts from debtors, which give the CRJ the Debtors Control column promised in term one. Money paid — Cash Payments Journal: stock, wages, rent, and now payments to creditors, giving the CPJ its Creditors Control column. No money, goods sold on trust — Debtors Journal. No money, goods bought on trust — Creditors Journal. Goods coming back from a debtor — Debtors Allowances Journal; goods going back to a supplier — Creditors Allowances Journal. And no money, no goods — the exceptions: bad debts, interest, corrections — the General Journal.

Drill the routing reflex on quick calls. Received R2 000 from a debtor: money in — CRJ, Debtors Control column, and the debtor's page. Paid Metro Wholesalers R6 000 on account: money out — CPJ, Creditors Control column, Metro's page. Sold goods on credit: DJ. The owner's neighbour pays rent for the back room in cash: CRJ, sundries. A debtor's debt written off: no money moved — GJ.

One warning for the new columns: money received from a debtor touches Bank and the debtor's DEBT — it never touches Sales, because the sale was recorded the day it was earned. Likewise paying a creditor never touches Trading Stock — the stock was recorded the day it arrived. The cash journals' new columns settle DEBTS; the trading columns belong to the original transactions.

Pause here — routing questions are with you now: for each transaction, name the journal, the columns struck, and any personal account touched.

## Subtopic: A Combined Month, Journalised

Now the month itself — Thabo's Trading, mark-up 25 percent on cost, opening with one balance carried from May: S. Naidoo, a debtor, owing R300; capital correspondingly R300, with everything else starting clean for our exercise.

Route each event as it happens. The 1st: owner deposits R20 000 capital — CRJ, sundries: Capital. The 2nd: trading stock bought on credit from Metro, R10 000 — CJ: Creditors Control R10 000, Trading Stock R10 000; Metro's page credited. The 5th: cash sales R6 000 — CRJ: Bank and Sales R6 000, Cost of Sales R4 800. The 8th: credit sale to L. Dube, R3 000 — DJ: Sales R3 000, Cost of Sales R2 400; Dube's page debited. The 10th: rent paid, R3 500 — CPJ, sundries: Rent Expense.

The 12th: Dube returns goods, R500, undamaged — DAJ: Debtors Allowances R500, Cost of Sales R400; her page credited. The 15th: Dube pays R2 000 on account — CRJ, Debtors Control column; her page credited. The 18th: paid Metro R6 000 on account — CPJ, Creditors Control column; Metro's page debited. The 20th: returned damaged stock to Metro, R800 — CAJ: Creditors Control R800, Trading Stock R800; Metro's page debited, debit note issued. The 25th: wages, R1 500 — CPJ, Wages column. The 28th: Naidoo's R300 declared irrecoverable — GJ: Bad Debts debit, Debtors Control credit, narration written; his page closed.

Eleven events, six journals, no exceptions and no doubts — every one routed by the two questions. Notice the month's texture: the same customer appears in three different journals — sale, return, receipt — and her page in the Debtors Ledger quietly accumulates the whole story: 3 000 debit, 500 credit, 2 000 credit — R500 owing.

Stop for this section's questions now — the month's transactions, re-routed: journal, columns, pages, and the reason in one clause.

## Subtopic: Posting the Month and Proving It

Month end: cross-cast every journal, post totals to the general ledger, personal entries having gone daily — then prove everything at once.

The balances, account by account. Bank: receipts 20 000 plus 6 000 plus 2 000, payments 3 500 plus 6 000 plus 1 500 — R17 000 debit. Trading Stock: in 10 000, out 4 800 and 2 400 to cost of sales, back in 400 from the return, out 800 to Metro — R2 400 debit. Debtors Control: opening 300, plus sales 3 000, less allowances 500, less receipts 2 000, less the write-off 300 — R500 debit, and the list agrees: Dube R500, Naidoo closed. Creditors Control: purchases 10 000, less payment 6 000, less return 800 — R3 200 credit; Metro's page says the same. Capital: R20 300 credit. The nominal accounts: Sales 9 000 credit; Debtors Allowances 500 debit; Cost of Sales 4 800 plus 2 400 minus 400 — 6 800 debit; Rent Expense 3 500; Wages 1 500; Bad Debts 300 — all debits.

The trial balance: debits — 17 000, 2 400, 500, 500, 6 800, 3 500, 1 500, 300 — total R32 500. Credits — 20 300, 9 000, 3 200 — total R32 500. Level. And the deeper checks stand too: both control accounts agree with their lists, and every journal cross-cast before posting. Three layers of proof, each catching what the others cannot.

The equation view, because exam questions ask for it per transaction: every event moved A, O and L in balance — the credit purchase raised A and L together; the rent payment dropped A and O together; the receipt from Dube swapped one asset for another inside A. Analyse any of the eleven on demand: accounts, amounts, effect on A equals O plus L.

The questions on this section are in front of you now: balances, the trial balance, and equation effects — computed, not guessed.

## Subtopic: The Control Web Around the Month

Now thread the term-one controls through this living month, because integrated scenarios are where the marks concentrate.

Cash: the R28 000 that flowed through Bank this month was protected by daily intact banking, the CRJ's analysis column exposing any gap between takings and deposits, and the month-end reconciliation against the bank statement — the independent record testing ours. Stock: the perpetual system priced every movement, so the Trading Stock balance of R2 400 is a CLAIM the December stocktake can test — a shortfall now has a name and a size. Debtors: Dube's growing page was governed by a credit limit; her statement went out at month end; and Naidoo's R300 was written off only after documented follow-up — prudence acting on evidence, not mood. Creditors: Metro's invoice was matched to order and delivery note before recording; the damaged goods went back under a numbered debit note; payment followed the terms, not impulse.

And the ethics thread the syllabus names — accountability and transparency — runs through every one of those controls. The bookkeeper who records the write-off with a narration is being accountable: the decision is visible, dated, explained. The owner who banks intact daily is being transparent: the records and the cash agree for anyone entitled to look. Integrated questions will hand you a scenario — takings banked short, a friend's account over its limit and growing — and ask for weakness, risk, recommendation, principle. You hold every tool that answer needs.

The final questions of this part are with you now: integrated scenarios — route the transaction, name the control, cite the principle. The full system, examined as one.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now the same month, from the stoep of the shop — because a combined month is not eleven puzzles; it is one busy shop doing what shops do, with every event knowing its own front door.

## Subtopic: Eleven Slips, Six Doors

Friday evening. Thabo tips the month's papers onto the counter: slips, invoices, a debit note, a sad letter about Naidoo. Eleven pieces of paper. Six doors in the back room. The whole job is: every paper through its right door — and the doors ask their own questions.

Door one asks: did money COME IN? The capital deposit, the cash sales, Dube's R2 000 — in they go. Door two: did money GO OUT? Rent, wages, the R6 000 to Metro — through door two. Door three: goods out on TRUST? Dube's R3 000 sale. Door four: goods in on trust? Metro's R10 000 delivery. Doors five and six: anything coming BACK? Dube's return through five; the damaged box back to Metro through six. And the last paper — Naidoo's dead debt, no money, no goods — slides through the odd-jobs door, with its explaining sentence.

Feel how fast the sorting goes when each paper answers two little questions: money or no money? Which direction? Eleven papers, maybe ninety seconds. That reflex — not memorising eleven procedures, just asking two questions eleven times — is the entire skill the term-two examiner is watching for.

One trap to spot at the doors: Dube's R2 000 goes through the money-in door, but it is NOT a sale — her sale already went through door three, weeks ago. This payment only settles her page. Money that settles old promises never touches the Sales lane. Same on the way out: paying Metro settles his page; the stock came in through door four long ago.

Quick check before we carry on — papers are coming to you right now: two questions each, name the door.

## Subtopic: The Month That Used Everything

Now watch the sorted month flow home and prove itself — the part of bookkeeping that feels, honestly, like magic the first time it works.

The pages tell their small stories. Dube's page: bought 3 000, returned 500, paid 2 000 — owes R500, and she could phone right now and get that answer in five seconds. Metro's page: delivered 10 000, took back 800, received 6 000 — the shop owes him R3 200. Naidoo's page: opened at 300, closed at nothing, with the goodbye written in the odd-jobs book.

The drawers tell the big ones. The Bank drawer: 28 000 in, 11 000 out — R17 000 sitting ready. The stock drawer: loaded with 10 000, gave up 7 200 to sales at cost, took 400 back from Dube, sent 800 back to Metro — R2 400 of goods that SHOULD be on the shelf, checkable any evening Thabo cares to count. Sales drawer: R9 000 earned. And the costs of the month lined up beside it: 6 800 of goods, 3 500 of roof, 1 500 of hands, 300 of goodbye.

Then the weigh-in. All the left-side balances: 32 500. All the right-side balances: 32 500. Level — and think what that levelness certifies: eleven events, six doors, two ledgers of pages, one cabinet of drawers, and not one rand lost or invented anywhere along the way. Both streets counted and agreeing with their lines. That is not luck; that is the system you spent a term building, running whole for the first time.

Your questions for this part are up now: read the pages and drawers, find the balances, and weigh the month yourself.

## Subtopic: The Owner's Friday Walk

Last picture: what all this bookkeeping is FOR. Every Friday at closing, Thabo does a ten-minute walk with the books open — and the month we just ran gives him every stop on the route.

Stop one, the till: count the cash against today's analysis column. Agree? Bank it, intact, tonight. Stop two, the shelf: pick five items, count them, check against what the stock drawer claims. The drawer says R2 400 of goods; the shelf must back it up. Stop three, the names: run a finger down the debtors list — who is near their limit, whose debt is getting old? Dube at R500, fine; had Naidoo's R300 been chased in week one, maybe the goodbye never happens. Stop four, the suppliers: Metro's statement against Metro's page — R3 200, both books, agreed; payment scheduled for the terms date, not before, not after.

Ten minutes, four stops — and notice what makes the walk possible at all: the books. Without them, Friday's questions have no answers to check AGAINST. Records first, control second, trust throughout — the shop watches over itself, and the owner sleeps.

And the ethics thread, in shop language: everything on that walk is visible and explainable to anyone with the right to ask — the partner, the bank, one day a buyer. A shop run like that has nothing to hide and everything to show. That is what accountability and transparency mean when they take off their exam clothes.

And here come the last questions of the lesson, right now: walk the Friday route on a scenario shop — what gets checked, against which record, and what smells wrong. Term one built the machine; you just ran it, whole. From here, everything in Accounting is refinement.
