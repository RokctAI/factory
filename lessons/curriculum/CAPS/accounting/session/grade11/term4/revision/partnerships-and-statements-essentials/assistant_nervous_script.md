Hey, it's Bianca — partnerships pull together half the year's work, so if this revision felt heavy, here's the light version: everything in the topic answers one question — how do two owners share ONE business fairly?

Start with the two piles per partner. Capital: planted money, barely moves. Current: the living account — profit share in, drawings out. Debit balance means the partner overdrew; credit means the business owes them. If you can read a current account, half the theory marks are already yours.

The appropriation account is a recipe, and the order is on the exam paper every time: salaries, then interest on capital, then any bonus, then the remainder in the ratio. Two checks save you: the account must empty EXACTLY — partner totals adding back to the net profit — and salaries plus interest are appropriations, never expenses. Write the word "appropriation" next to them and the examiner knows you know.

Disposals: four steps, always — cost out, accumulated depreciation out, proceeds in, difference is the profit or loss. Chant it.

And indicators: formula is half the mark, comment is the rest — figure, comparison, judgement. Ask whose eye is on the number: creditor wants liquidity, lender wants gearing, partner wants return.

Do one full appropriation with current accounts tonight, balancing to the rand. Once the pot empties cleanly under your pen, this topic is banked. You've got this!
