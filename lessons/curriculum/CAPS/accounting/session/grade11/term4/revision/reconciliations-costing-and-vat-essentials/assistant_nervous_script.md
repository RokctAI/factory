Hey, it's Bianca — four topics in one revision session sounds brutal, but look closer: each one is a single move you repeat until it feels like breathing.

Reconciliations: one move — correct both records toward each other. Your books absorb what the statement knew first; the reconciliation statement lists what you knew first. If the two sides meet, you are done; if not, an error is hiding and the side it belongs to tells you where. Never argue balances — meet in the middle.

Costing: one number — contribution. Selling price minus variable cost. Fixed costs divided by contribution is break-even; units past the line times contribution is profit. Any what-if question — new rent, new price, special order — just recomputes contribution and asks where the line moved.

Stock and budgets: two calendars. The counting formula — opening plus purchases plus carriage minus closing — gives cost of sales; the collection schedule turns planned sales into planned receipts, and the never-pay percentage is left out on purpose.

VAT: one slogan — going up, fifteen on a hundred; coming down, fifteen on one-fifteen. Output minus input is the return, and the money was never yours.

Four moves: meet in the middle, find the line, mind the calendars, pass the tax through. Run one worked example of each tonight and the paper cannot surprise you. You've got this!
