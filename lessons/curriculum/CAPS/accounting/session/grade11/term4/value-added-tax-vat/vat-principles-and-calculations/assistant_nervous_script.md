Hey, it's Bianca — VAT looks like a whole new subject with its own vocabulary, but I promise it's three small things wearing a big name.

Thing one: who pays. Consumers pay, businesses collect. Every confusing sentence in this topic untangles once you hold that. A vendor charges VAT on sales (output), claims back VAT on purchases (input), and pays over the difference. That's it — that's the whole system.

Thing two: the three baskets. Most things are standard-rated. Staples — brown bread, maize meal, rice, samp, beans, milk, eggs, fresh produce, oil, paraffin — plus exports are zero-rated. And a few things are exempt: bank interest, residential rent, bus and train fares, school fees. The only tricky bit is zero-rated versus exempt, and one question settles it: does the seller get its own VAT back? Zero-rated, yes. Exempt, no. Say that question out loud twice and it's yours.

Thing three: the arithmetic — and honestly, one trap. Going UP from an exclusive price, add fifteen percent. Coming DOWN from an inclusive price, it's fifteen over one-fifteen, NOT fifteen percent. If you only remember one thing from tonight, remember that. And in pricing questions: mark up first, THEN add VAT — never the other way round.

The ethics part writes itself once you see VAT as trust money: it's someone else's money in your till. Practise five calculations tonight, both directions, and this becomes free marks. You've got this!
