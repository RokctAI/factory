Hey, it's Bianca — break-even questions look like they need a business degree, but the whole topic is one fraction, and you already understand it from selling anything, ever.

Here is the entire machine: a pile of fixed costs that exists before you sell unit one, and a contribution — selling price minus variable cost — that each unit tosses onto the pile. Break-even is just the pile divided by the toss. R658 000 divided by R47 is 14 000 pairs. That is the formula, the only formula, and everything else in the topic is feeding it correctly.

Feeding it correctly is a stamping exercise. Take every cost in the question and stamp it: FIXED-TOTAL goes on top of the fraction, VARIABLE-PER-UNIT goes into the contribution below. The three classic wrecks are all stamping errors: forgetting the selling-and-distribution R8 because it isn't "factory"; dragging rent or depreciation into the per-unit figure; or dividing by the selling price instead of the contribution. Twenty seconds of stamping beats all three.

Two finishing touches collect the remaining marks. If the division is not exact, round UP — at fourteen comma anything you are still short a stone, so the survival line is the next whole unit. And never leave the answer naked: put it next to planned production and say what the cushion is. Fourteen thousand against a plan of twenty-four thousand is a 41,7 percent margin of safety — that sentence is the interpretation mark, waiting.

What-if changes are the same fraction, re-fed: price cut or cost rise shrinks the toss; new rent grows the pile. Recalculate calmly and compare the new line with the old. One fraction, stamped inputs, rounded up, compared with the plan. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
