Today we move from the business that buys its stock to the business that makes it — manufacturing costs and the ledger accounts that trace them. Every rand in a factory follows one unbroken chain: direct materials and direct labour form prime cost, factory overheads join them in work-in-progress, and the finished goods that emerge carry a unit cost that every pricing and break-even decision stands on. We will sort costs by traceability and behaviour, run the three stock accounts a factory keeps where a trader kept one, and walk Kagiso Footwear's full year from leather in the storeroom to gross profit. Master the chain and the exam's favourite trick — hiding one figure and asking you to recover it — becomes routine marks.
