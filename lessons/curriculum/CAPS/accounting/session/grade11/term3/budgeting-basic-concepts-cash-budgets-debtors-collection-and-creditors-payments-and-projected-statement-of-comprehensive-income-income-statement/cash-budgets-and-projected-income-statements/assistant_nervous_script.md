Hey, it's Bianca — two forecasts, two schedules, percentages landing in three different months... this topic looks like a spreadsheet exploded. Let me hand you the three moves it actually is.

Move one: sort every item into the right document. Ask one question of each line — does money move this month? Then it's in the cash budget. Does the business earn or use up value this month? Then it's in the profit forecast. Some items are in both, in different months; two items are famously in only one — depreciation (profit forecast only, because no rand moves) and drawings, loans and asset purchases (cash budget only, because they move money but aren't profit or loss).

Move two: the collection grid. Don't try to do it in your head — draw sales down the side and months across. Then fill in one month at a time: this month I receive a slice of last month's credit sales, a slice of the month before, a slice of the one before that. The only two traps are the discount (it comes off ONLY the prompt-payer slice) and the bad-debt slice (it never enters the cash budget at all).

Move three: the closing balance chain. Receipts minus payments, plus what you opened with, equals what you close with — and that closing figure opens the next month. Five lines per month, every month.

And the idea worth keeping past the exam: profitable and broke at the same time is not a contradiction, it's a timing gap — and a business that sees it coming in January has cheap options that vanish by March. You've got this!
