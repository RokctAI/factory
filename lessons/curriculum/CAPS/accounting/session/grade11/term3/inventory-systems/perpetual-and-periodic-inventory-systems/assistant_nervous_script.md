Hey, it's Bianca — a second stock system after a whole year of the first one can feel like the ground moving. It isn't. You already know one system inside out; tonight you learned that the other one just refuses to write things down as it goes, and counts at the end instead.

Two sentences hold the whole comparison. Perpetual: record the cost every time stock moves, so you always know. Periodic: record only what you buy, count at the end, and work out what you sold. Everything else — every advantage, every disadvantage — grows out of those two sentences, so you don't need to memorise the lists. Ask "would this business benefit from knowing right now?" and the answer picks the system.

The one thing to learn properly is the periodic formula, and it has a natural order if you say it as a story: what I started with, plus what I bought, plus what it cost to get it here, minus what I sent back, minus what I took home — that's my pile. Then minus what's still standing — that's what went out. Say it as a story, not a list, and it stays.

Watch two little traps. Carriage IN belongs in the sum; carriage OUT is a selling cost and stays out. And drawings of stock come off the pile — the owner didn't sell those.

Last thing, the idea worth carrying past the exam: the periodic system can't see theft. That's not a detail; that's the whole reason control matters. You've got this!
