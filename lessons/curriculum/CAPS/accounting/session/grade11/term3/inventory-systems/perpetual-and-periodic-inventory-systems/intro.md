Today we compare the two ways a business can keep its stock records: the perpetual system, where every sale carries its cost of sales entry with it, and the periodic system, which records only purchases and works cost of sales out backwards from a year-end count. The heart of the session is the periodic formula — opening stock plus purchases and carriage inward, less returns and drawings, less closing stock — and the silent flaw it carries, because losses that are never counted are quietly called sales. We will weigh the advantages and disadvantages, see how the barcode scanner changed the choice, and finish with the stock controls, ethics and audit tests the examiners attach. This topic is set both as a comparison and as a calculation, so we make you fluent in both.
