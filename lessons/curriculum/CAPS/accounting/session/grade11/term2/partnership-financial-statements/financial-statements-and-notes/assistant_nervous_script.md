Hey, it's Bianca — I know the statements feel like a formatting exam stapled to an accounting exam, and one wrong line seems to wreck the whole page. Breathe. The format is fixed, which is the good news: fixed things can be learned once and reused forever.

Start every answer with the heading, and ask the two-word question: period or moment? The income statement covers a period — FOR the year ended. The financial position is a moment — ON the date. That is a mark you can bank before you write a single figure.

Then trust the skeleton. Income statement: sales, less cost of sales, gross profit, plus other income, less operating expenses, operating profit, less interest, net profit. Financial position: two towers — assets on one side, equity and liabilities on the other — and they must weigh the same. If your towers differ, don't stare at the whole page: the difference itself is a clue. Half of it often points at a sign error; the full amount points at a left-out item.

Notes are not extra work — they are where the marks hide. Every big face figure has a note proving it: fixed assets climbing to R620 000, the current accounts closing at R65 800. The face and the note must meet to the rand, and writing the trail out fully is exactly what the marker pays for.

Two last snares. Partners' salaries and interest on capital never touch the income statement — they are appropriations, and they live in the current accounts note. And debtors go on the face NET of the provision — show the honest number, prove it in the note.

Formats reward the prepared more reliably than any other question type. Learn the skeleton cold, and the figures just take their seats. You've got this!
