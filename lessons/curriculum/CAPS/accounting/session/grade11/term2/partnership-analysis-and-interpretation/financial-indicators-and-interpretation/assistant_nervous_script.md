Hey, it's Bianca — I know the ratio lesson felt like a formula avalanche: percentages, periods, rates, ratios, all sliding at once. Stand still for a second. There are only three families, and each family answers one human question.

Family one, profitability: out of every rand of sales, how much survives? Gross margin, expense percentage, net margin — all just slices of the same rand. Family two, tempo: how fast does money lap the business? Stock days, debtor days, creditor days — three clocks on one journey. Family three, structure: whose money is in the business and what did it earn? Debt-equity for the risk, return on average equity for the reward, liquidity ratios for survival. Before touching any formula, ask which family the question lives in — the sorting alone calms the avalanche.

The formulas themselves have one recurring trick: when the top of the fraction is a whole year's flow — cost of sales, credit sales, net profit — the bottom wants an AVERAGE, opening plus closing over two. Snapshot over snapshot, like the current ratio, needs no averaging. That single rule covers every average the paper will ever demand.

And for comments, never freeze — play the four-beat bar. The figure next to its comparison. Whether that is better or worse. A sensible cause. A piece of advice. Four short sentences; the marks are usually laid out one per beat. Remember the marker cannot award a judgement you never wrote down — say "this is an improvement" in actual words.

Last kindness: if your calculated ratio looks odd, comment on the number you calculated and stay consistent — interpretation marks follow your figure. A slip in arithmetic costs one mark; abandoning the comment costs them all. You've got this!
