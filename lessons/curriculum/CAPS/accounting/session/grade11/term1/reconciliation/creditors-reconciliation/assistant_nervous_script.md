Hey, it's Bianca — two columns of numbers from two different businesses, and you are supposed to know which one to believe. I understand why that felt like a trick question. It is not. There is a simple order to it.

Do the work in two stages and never mix them. Stage one: fix YOUR book. Anything you should have known about — an invoice for goods you actually received, interest they charged, a discount they allowed, a credit note you wrote down wrong — those all get corrected in your creditors ledger account, with a proper debit and credit. Stage two: bridge to THEIR book. Only two kinds of thing survive to that stage: money you sent that they have not processed, and mistakes their clerk made. That is it.

The habit that saves marks is a pencil and one letter per line. Go down the question and mark each item M for "my book changes" or T for "theirs still to do". Sort everything first. Only then start adding. Almost every lost mark in this topic comes from doing arithmetic before doing the sorting.

The one direction that trips people up is the credit note. A credit note means you owe LESS. If you recorded it too small, you have been walking around thinking you owe more than you do — so the correction brings your balance DOWN. Say that sentence to yourself once before every question and the trap stops working.

And remember what this is really for. It is not a puzzle invented by examiners. It is the reason a small business does not pay for someone else's delivery. Write the statement out fully, heading and all, brackets on every subtraction — markers pay for that structure even if your total is out. You've got this!
