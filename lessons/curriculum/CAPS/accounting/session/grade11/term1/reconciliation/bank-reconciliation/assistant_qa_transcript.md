**Bianca:** Questions from the class before we close! The big one first: why can we not just accept the bank's balance? The bank is a bank — surely their number is the correct one?

**Tutor:** The bank's number is correct about what the BANK has processed; it is not correct about what the business owns at that date. Two separate facts are involved. Bushveld Hardware banked R28 400 on the twenty-seventh of February: that money is legally the business's, it is in the night safe or in transit through the clearing system, and the shop's own record rightly shows it. The bank simply had not processed it by midnight on the twenty-eighth. Equally, the shop released two transfers totalling R26 950 to suppliers; those obligations have left the business's control, and the shop's record rightly shows the money gone, even though the bank had not yet debited it. If you adopted the bank's balance you would be claiming money you have already spent and denying money you already hold. So we take the bank's record as an independent CHECK, not as the truth — we borrow the items the bank knows and we did not, we correct our own mistakes, and then we prove the remaining gap consists only of timing. The final figure that reaches the Statement of Financial Position is the corrected Bank account balance of R60 800, not the statement's R55 650.

**Bianca:** A learner asked this and it is a fair question: a customer's electronic transfer of R6 800 bounced. Why is that not simply a bad debt?

**Tutor:** Because nothing has been proven about the customer's ability to pay — only about the state of their account on one day. The reversal restores the position to what it always truly was: debit Debtors control, credit Bank. The receipt we recorded was, in substance, never received; the debt was never settled; the customer is back on the debtors list owing R6 800, and the business will phone them tomorrow and very often be paid. Writing it off as a bad debt would do three wrong things at once. It would remove a debt that is still collectible, so the debtors control account and the debtors list would both be understated. It would charge an expense that has not been incurred, so the profit would be understated — a breach of the matching principle, not prudence. And it would destroy the follow-up: once written off, nobody chases it. A bad debt entry belongs only at the end of a proper process — reminders sent, terms exhausted, the account judged irrecoverable, and a partner authorising the write-off in the General Journal. Bounced today, chased tomorrow, written off only when genuinely lost.

**Bianca:** Next one: how do I handle a bank OVERDRAFT? Everything I memorised seems to turn upside down and I panic.

**Tutor:** Nothing turns upside down if you stop memorising and start reasoning. An overdraft simply means the Bank account has a CREDIT balance in our ledger — the bank is a creditor, not an asset — and it is shown in brackets in the Statement of Financial Position, inside current liabilities. The corrections work identically: fees still reduce the balance, interest charged on the overdraft still reduces it, a dishonoured receipt still reduces it, and each one still names its contra account. On the reconciliation statement, the single test survives untouched: what will the bank still do to this balance? An uncleared deposit will still be added by the bank, so it is still added, and it pushes an overdraft toward zero. Outstanding transfers will still be deducted, so they are still deducted, and they push an overdraft deeper. The only real discipline is arithmetic care with negatives — write an overdraft in brackets, keep it in brackets while you add and subtract, and state clearly at the end whether the final figure is favourable or unfavourable. Examiners set overdrafts precisely to separate learners who reason from learners who recite.

**Bianca:** Last one: the reconciliation is described as an internal control, but the person who does it usually already works in the office. How does it actually stop anybody stealing?

**Tutor:** It stops theft because of who the third participant is. Almost every internal check inside a small business relies on one employee watching another, and both of them can be persuaded, tired or complicit. The bank cannot. It keeps a complete, independent, dated record of every rand that entered or left, it has no interest in the business's story, and it cannot be talked into changing an entry. So the moment a cashier pockets a receipt, the business's records and the bank's records stop agreeing, and the disagreement is a fixed size that will not go away. The control only works, though, if three conditions hold. The person who reconciles must not be the person who handles cash or writes up the cash journals, otherwise they are checking their own work and can hide the gap. It must be done monthly, because a difference found four weeks later is investigable and one found a year later is archaeology. And a partner must review and sign it, with long-outstanding items explained rather than carried forward — because the oldest trick in cash theft is a permanent "outstanding deposit" that never quite clears, covering a hole that was made months ago.
