Hey, it's Bianca — the appropriation account looks like a whole new machine, but you already own every part of it. It is just the profit pot from grade 10 with a recipe attached, because now two people are standing at the pot.

Hold the serving order and half the topic is done: interest on capital first, for the money that waited. Salaries and bonus second, for the hands that worked. Whatever remains, split in the agreed ratio, for the risk they both carry. Serve in that order every time, add up what you served, and check it equals the net profit that went in. If your servings and the pot disagree, an appropriation is missing or doubled — find it before moving on.

The trap that costs the most marks is one sentence long: a partner's salary is never an expense. Expenses pay outsiders; appropriations sort out insiders. If a question hides a partner's salary in operating expenses, pull it out, restore the net profit, and appropriate it where it belongs.

Current accounts are just tabs. Earnings on the credit side, drawings on the debit side, and the balance says who owes whom. An opening DEBIT balance only means that partner drew ahead of their earnings last year — put it on the debit side and carry on; it is not a trick, just a starting position.

And for equation questions, one habit: write the double entry first, classify each account, and read the ups and downs off the debits and credits. The story can fool you; the entry cannot. Check that the scale levels at the end — if it doesn't, one label is wrong, and you get to fix it before the marker sees it. You've got this!
