Hey, it's Bianca — disposal questions look terrifying because there are so many numbers moving at once. There aren't. There are four moves, always in the same order, and only the last one ever changes.

Move one, and write this on your hand: catch up the wear. If the asset was sold part-way through the year, it was still being used for those months, so charge that depreciation FIRST. Everybody forgets this, it carries the most marks, and every number after it depends on it.

Moves two and three: put the original cost on the disposal account, and put all the accumulated wear on it. That clears the asset out of both accounts where it was living.

Move four: put whatever you got for it. Cash, a debtor, a trade-in allowance, or drawings if the owner took it home. That's the only step that changes between question types.

Then weigh it — and use the five-second check that saves people constantly: what did the books say it was worth, and what did you actually get? The difference IS your profit or loss. If that doesn't match your long working, you forgot move one. It's almost always move one.

Two traps worth knowing by name. A trade-in: the new asset goes in at its FULL price, not just the cash you paid — the old asset was part of the payment. And a credit sale of an asset creates a once-off debtor, not a trade debtor, because you don't sell vehicles for a living.

Four moves, one check, two traps. You've got this!
