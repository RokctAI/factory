Today we take on the sum and difference of two cubes — the factorisation that looks like the hardest one in Grade 10 and turns out to be the most mechanical of them all. Our anchor task is to factorise x cubed plus eight, two quiet terms that every familiar tool bounces off, and a question type examiners return to year after year because so few learners truly own it. We will build the six-number vocabulary of perfect cubes, state both identities, control every sign with SOAP — Same, Opposite, Always Positive — and finish by pulling a cube out from behind its favourite hiding place, a common factor. One hour of work here pays marks for three years.
