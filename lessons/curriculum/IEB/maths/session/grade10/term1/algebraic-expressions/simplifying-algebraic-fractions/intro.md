Today every factorising technique you have built goes to work on algebraic fractions — fractions whose tops and bottoms are polynomials. One command opens every operation on them: factorise first, cancel second, because only a factor shared by the whole numerator and the whole denominator may ever be cancelled — never a term. We will simplify, multiply, divide, add and subtract our way through four worked expressions, meet the sum of cubes hiding inside a fraction, and defuse the subtraction trap that costs more marks than any other line in the topic. Alongside all of it runs the quiet habit examiners reward: reading the restrictions off the original denominators and writing them down. Get the law and the restrictions right, and this topic becomes some of the most dependable marks you will ever collect.
