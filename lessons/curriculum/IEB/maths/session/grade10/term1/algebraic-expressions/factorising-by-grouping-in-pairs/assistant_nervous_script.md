Move one: sort the four terms into two pairs — the ones that share letters sit together, and if the given order won't cooperate, reshuffle it until it does. Move two: factor each pair down to its own common factor. Move three: the two pairs will now hold the identical bracket — lift it out, and what's left over becomes your second bracket. That's it. That's the whole method.

The only real danger is one sign. When a pair starts with a minus, take out a NEGATIVE factor — the signs inside flip, and your brackets match. Miss that, and the method jams — which is actually a kindness, because mismatched brackets are the maths telling you exactly where to look.

And you always have a lie detector in your pocket: multiply your answer back out. All four original terms return, correct signs and all, when you're right.
