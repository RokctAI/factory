Hey, it's Thandi — that long bracket with its three terms looks like something only a textbook author could dream up, right? Here's the secret: you never dream it up. You assemble it.

Think of the whole technique as a form with three blanks. Blank one: the two cube roots — and there are only six cube numbers to know: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, so the roots practically wave at you. Blank two: the terms — square the first, multiply the pair, square the last, in that order every time. Blank three: the signs, and SOAP fills all three at once — Same, Opposite, Always Positive. Three blanks filled, question done.

And if panic wipes your memory in the exam? Multiply your brackets back out in the margin. When you're right, the middle terms cancel each other perfectly — the maths confirms you on the spot. When a sign is off, the leftovers show you exactly where.

One friendly warning: leave that long bracket alone at the end. It never factorises further, and walking away from it is the correct final move, not laziness.

Now go hit the practice questions, whispering Same, Opposite, Always Positive at every sign. By the third one this will feel like a routine; by the fifth it will feel like free marks. You've got this!
