Today we are factorising four-term expressions by grouping in pairs — the tool that takes over the moment common factors and the identities run out of road. The whole method is three moves: sort the terms into two pairs, factor each pair down to its own common factor, then lift out the bracket both pairs share. Standing guard over move two is the topic's famous trap — the pair that leads with a minus sign and demands a negative common factor — and we will defuse it permanently. Grouping is also the hidden engine of the trinomial method and the finishing move for cubic factorisation in your final year, so the eye you train today keeps earning long after this term.
