Hey, it's Thandi — if that lesson felt like an awful lot of steps just to break one expression apart, let me hand you the short version, because underneath it all there is only ONE hunt.

Two numbers. That's the entire game. They must multiply to the front number times the last number, and add to the middle number. Everything else — the splitting, the grouping, the shared bracket — happens automatically once those two numbers are in your hand. You are never inventing anything; you are following the pair.

And the signs do half your hunting for you. Negative product? One plus, one minus. Negative sum? The bigger number takes the minus. Read those two clues first and your list of suspects shrinks before you even start writing.

Stuck anyway? List factor pairs from one upwards like climbing a ladder — you cannot miss a rung that way. And if no pair passes both tests, breathe: writing "it does not factorise" is a full-marks answer, not a defeat.

Last gift: every answer can be proven. Multiply your brackets back out, and the original trinomial either returns or it doesn't. You carry your own marker.

Now go do the practice set. Two numbers, one hunt, check the change. Three questions from now this will feel like a game you know how to win — because it is. You've got this!
