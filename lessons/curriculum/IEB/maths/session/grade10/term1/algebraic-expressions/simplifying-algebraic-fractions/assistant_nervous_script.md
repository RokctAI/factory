Hey, it's Thandi — I know the exact moment this topic gets scary: the fraction fills the whole line, brackets everywhere, and you're not sure what's allowed to disappear. Breathe. There are only four moves, and you already own three of them from the factorising weeks.

Move one: factorise everything — every top, every bottom — before touching anything. Difference of squares, trinomials, common factors, cubes: that is all it will ever be, and you've done each one. Move two: cancel only whole brackets that appear above AND below. If a loose plus or minus is sitting in your top line, nothing in there cancels — that's the crates-and-loose-bottles rule. Move three: for adding and subtracting, build the smallest common denominator from each different factor once, and put a bracket around any numerator you subtract, so the minus reaches every term. Move four: before you write the final answer, glance back at the ORIGINAL bottoms and write down the x-values that would make them zero. Those restrictions are a free mark waiting for anyone who remembers them.

And here's your safety net: substitute x equals one into the original and into your answer. Same value? You're right, provably.

Now go take the practice questions — factorise, cancel crates, bracket the parcel, ban the zeros. Four moves, over and over, and this topic turns into your most reliable marks. You've got this!
