Today the three trig ratios go to work: we are solving right-angled triangles — every unknown side, every unknown angle — and then pointing that skill at the problems examiners actually set: ladders, guy wires, ramps, water towers and lighthouses, reached through angles of elevation and depression. Four worked problems carry the session, and each one turns on the same two decisions: which ratio the two circled sides demand, and whether the unknown sits on top of the fraction or underneath it. Along the way we bank the sense checks that catch errors before any marker does — the hypotenuse must outrank every other side, the observer's eye height must be added back, and one test of the calculator's degree mode saves an entire question.
