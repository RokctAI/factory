Hey, it's Thandi — towers, wires, lighthouses, ships... I know the word problems feel like a different subject from the tidy triangles in your notes. They're not. Every single one collapses into one right-angled triangle and two small decisions.

Decision one: which ratio? Stand at your angle and name only two sides — the one you know and the one you want. Opposite and hypotenuse is sine. Adjacent and hypotenuse is cosine. Opposite and adjacent is tangent. Everything else in the picture is decoration.

Decision two: where's the unknown sitting? On top of the fraction — multiply. Underneath — divide the known side by the ratio. That's the whole trick, and the hypotenuse gives you a free safety check: it must always be the longest side, so if your answer breaks that, you flipped the fraction.

Three small guards finish the job: check for the D on your calculator screen before anything else; add back the eye height whenever your triangle starts above the ground; and when a depression angle confuses you, walk to the bottom of the picture and use the same angle looking up — they're always equal.

Now go to the practice questions. Sketch first, stand at your angle, two sides, upstairs or downstairs. Four moves per question, every question the same four moves. By the third one you'll be measuring lighthouses like it's nothing. You've got this!
