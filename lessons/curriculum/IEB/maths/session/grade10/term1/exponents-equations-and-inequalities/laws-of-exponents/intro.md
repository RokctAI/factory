Today we install the laws of exponents — the grammar every formula from here to your final exams is written in. Two tasks carry the whole session: simplifying one bracket-heavy expression with a negative exponent lurking inside it, and solving the exponential equation three to the power x plus one equals twenty-seven. Every law will be justified from one plain fact — an exponent counts repeated multiplication — so the rules become common sense instead of memory work. We will also walk the four-exhibit error museum: the coefficient that dodges the bracket's power, exponents added across different bases, the negative exponent misread as a minus, and the base multiplied by its own counter. Meet the traps here, and they never meet you in an exam.
