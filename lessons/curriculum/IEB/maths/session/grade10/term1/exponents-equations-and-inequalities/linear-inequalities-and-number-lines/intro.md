Today we are solving linear inequalities — questions whose answers are whole stretches of the number line rather than a single balancing point. The algebra is the algebra you already own, plus exactly one rule of its own: multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the sign, and we will see precisely why debt makes that obvious. Around the rule sit two presentation skills that earn their own marks — the number-line sketch with its solid and hollow dots, and interval notation with its square and round brackets. Four worked inequalities carry the session, from a one-step solve through a compound inequality trapped between two fences, to a market-stall profit problem where one word decides whether the boundary counts.
