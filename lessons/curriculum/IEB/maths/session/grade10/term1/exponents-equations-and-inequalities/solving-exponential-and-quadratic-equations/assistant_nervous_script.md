Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels like two different subjects fighting over one page, you're not wrong: quadratics and exponentials really are different animals. The good news? Each one stands on exactly ONE idea, and once you can name it, the working is short.

Quadratics stand on zero. Push everything to one side until the other side reads zero, factorise, and read the brackets apart — because a product equal to zero means one of its factors is zero, and zero is the only number that gives you that gift. Brackets equal to eight? Expand and move the eight across. A fraction? Ban the zero denominator in writing, multiply it away, carry on.

Exponentials stand on one base. Translate the plain number into the base's language — sixty-four is two to the six, one over eighty-one is three to the minus four — then equate the counters and solve what's left.

And guard the two famous traps: never divide an equation by anything containing x, because a root goes into the bin with it — factorise instead. And when a story problem hands you a negative width or a negative number of items, reject it in writing, with its reason.

Now go start the practice questions. Ask of each one: where does the unknown live? Downstairs — factorise to zero. Upstairs — same base. Two ideas, six question types, all yours. You've got this!
