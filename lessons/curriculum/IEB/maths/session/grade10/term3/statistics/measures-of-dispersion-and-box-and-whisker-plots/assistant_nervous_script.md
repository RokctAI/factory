Hey, it's Thandi — if quartiles, whiskers and creases are tangling together right now, let's smooth them out for a moment.

The whole lesson lives in three folds of a paper strip. Fold the ordered numbers in the middle — that crease is the median, 13. Fold each half — those creases are the quartiles, 8 and 24. The width between the outer creases is the IQR, and it's the spread measure you can trust, because the wild days at 3 and 33 can't reach it. That's it. Everything else is drawing: walls at the quartiles, a line at the median, whiskers to the extremes.

And when you READ a box plot, one rule does all the work: every section holds a quarter of the data. A long whisker doesn't mean many days — it means that quarter of days is stretched out. Say "long means spread, never many" before every reading question, and comparison questions become two calm sentences: whose median is higher, whose box is longer.

So grab the practice questions now, while your fingers still remember the folds. Order the data first — always — then fold, fold, fold, and draw. Two questions in, this topic will feel like origami you've done before. I'm right here with you.
