Today we take on the midpoint theorem — the hardest-working theorem in Grade 10 geometry, where a single hypothesis about two midpoints pays out two conclusions at once: a parallel line and a half length. We state it precisely, prove it by constructing a clever parallelogram, and then reverse it into its converse, which is tested just as often as the theorem itself. The session closes with the riders where both versions chain together, and with the five quiet errors that empty a page of its marks.
