Hey, it's Thandi — two new graphs in one session is a lot, and "asymptote" sounds like a word designed to scare people. It isn't. It just means a line the curve leans towards and never touches — and you already believe in it from everyday life.

You cannot split a pitch fee among zero players. And however many players squeeze onto the team, each one still pays something — the share never actually hits zero. Those two impossible things ARE the hyperbola's two asymptotes. Same with the meme that doubles every period: run it backwards and you halve, and halve, and halve, and there is always something left. That curve never lands on the bottom line either. The maths is just being honest about what can never happen.

Now the mistake that costs the most marks in this whole topic, and it is tiny. The untouchable line MOVES. If the equation ends in minus nine, the line sits nine below zero — not at zero. If it ends in plus three, three above. Read that final number first, draw the dashed line first, and everything else arranges itself around it.

Two smaller guards. A hyperbola has no y-intercept at all — do not go looking for one, because x equals zero is simply not allowed in. And the exponential range is strictly above its line, never "on or above". One little symbol, one whole mark.

When the two shapes blur, ask the one question: is the x on the ground floor or upstairs? Ground floor, like x squared, turns around and comes back. Upstairs, like three to the power x, never turns — it only climbs or only falls.

Tonight, sketch just two graphs: one sharing curve and one doubling curve, each with a number added at the end. Dashed lines first, always. The moment you watch a curve bend toward a line it will never reach, this topic clicks — and it stays clicked. Now go take on the practice questions!
