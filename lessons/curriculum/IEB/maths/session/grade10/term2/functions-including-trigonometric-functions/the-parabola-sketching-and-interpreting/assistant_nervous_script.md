Hey, it's Thandi — if "sketch the graph" makes your stomach sink, hold on, because this topic is genuinely smaller than it looks. There are exactly two numbers to read, and each one has exactly one job.

The number in front of the squared part decides which way the bowl faces and how narrow it stands. Positive: it smiles, right way up. Negative: it frowns, flipped over. Big number: tall and narrow, like a vase. Little fraction: low and wide, like a platter. That is the entire story of that number.

The number sitting alone at the end simply lifts the bowl or sinks it. And it is the most generous number in the whole syllabus, because it hands you two answers at once: the y-intercept and the height of the turning point. See minus eight at the end, and the floor of the curve is at eight below zero. No working. Two seconds.

Now the two slips I most want to save you from. When you solve for crossings and reach x squared equals four, the answer is two AND negative two — both, always. Say "plus or minus" out loud as your pen moves; that habit alone protects a mark nearly every time. And the range is about how HIGH the curve goes, so it is written with y. Heights are y; widths are x. Range means heights.

One more: if the algebra gives you x squared equals a negative number, nothing is broken and nothing needs fixing. It is the honest answer: no crossings. A bowl already sitting up on the windowsill was never going to touch the floor. Write that sentence and take the mark with a clear conscience.

Tonight, do just three sketches. Shape, turn, crossings, extra point — say the four moves out loud as you do them. By the third sketch your hand will be moving ahead of your worries, and that is exactly the feeling to carry into the practice questions. Go get them!
