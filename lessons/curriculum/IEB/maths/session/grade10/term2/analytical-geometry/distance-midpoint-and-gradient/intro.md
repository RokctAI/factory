Today we open analytical geometry — the topic that places geometric shapes on a coordinate grid so that every claim about them becomes a calculation. Three tools do nearly all the work: the distance formula, which is Pythagoras in disguise; the midpoint formula, the only one of the three that adds; and the gradient, rise over run, with its parallel and perpendicular conditions. We build each one from scratch, rehearse all three on a single pair of points so the numbers become familiar, and then combine them to classify a quadrilateral with a full proof of every property — the skill this topic keeps coming back to in your exams.
