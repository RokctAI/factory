Hey, it's Thandi — if the formulas for boxes, tins and prisms are swirling into one big blur, take a breath, because the truth of this topic is tiny.

There is one volume law. One. Find the face that repeats, work out its area, multiply by how tall the stack is. A box is a stack of rectangles, a tin is a stack of circles, a chocolate-bar prism is a stack of triangles. You have been counting stacked things your whole life — this is that, with units.

Surface area is even more hands-on: unfold the solid flat and add up the panels. And the tin's scary-looking two pi r h? That is just the wrapper sliding off as a flat rectangle — height of the tin, width once around the rim. Peel it in your head and the formula writes itself.

Then just three small habits: halve a diameter before you square anything, keep pi as a symbol until the last line, and check your unit — cubic when you filled, square when you wrapped.

The practice questions are ready, and the first one is a gentle box, promise. Name the repeated face, find its area, count the layers. One law, one net, and you are through this whole topic. You've got this!
