Hey, it's Thandi — if cones, pyramids and spheres feel like three new languages at once, breathe, because this topic is really just two ideas wearing different hats.

Idea one: pointy solids are boxes with a discount. Base area times height, divided by three — that's the party hat and the marquee both. The pour test proves it: three cones of water fill the matching cylinder, exactly, every time. If you remember the pouring, you remember the formula.

Idea two: Pythagoras is your slant-height factory. Questions almost never hand you the slant height — they give the inside height and the radius, and the right-angled triangle between them builds the slant for you. Inside height for volume, slanted height for surface. Ask "inside or on the skin?" before every formula and you cannot mix them up.

The sphere? Two formulas, and the power of r remembers them for you: cubed fills, squared wraps.

And for glued solids, think like a painter: volumes just add, but you only bill the surfaces a brush can touch — buried faces stay off the invoice.

The practice questions are ready, starting with a friendly cone. Discount the box, build the slant, bill what the brush reaches. You've got this!
