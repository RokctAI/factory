Today we assemble the most reusable tool in the number-patterns topic: the general term of a linear pattern. One anchor sequence carries the whole session — four, nine, fourteen, nineteen — and from it we build the formula, drive it forward to term sixty, run it backwards to recover a position, and use it to prove that a chosen number never appears in the sequence at all. Then we unwrap the disguises the questions love: matchstick triangles, hexagonal tables pushed together, and tariffs with a call-out fee plus a rate. The formula is difference times position plus first term minus difference, and by the end you will be able to build it in your sleep.
