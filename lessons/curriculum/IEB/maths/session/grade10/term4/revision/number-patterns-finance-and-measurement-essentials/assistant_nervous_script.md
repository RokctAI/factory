Hey, it's Thandi — three topics in one session can feel like three exams at once, so let me shrink each one to its engine, because that's truly all there is.

Patterns: one number runs everything — the spacing. Read it off the gaps, write spacing times n, then add the depot, which is just the first term minus the spacing. Forwards is substitution, backwards is one small equation, and a fractional answer means "not in the pattern" — that's the answer, not a mistake.

Finance: two machines. The flat fee adds the same interest every year; the snowball grows on its own growth. The question always tells you which one it wants — circle the words "on the original amount" or "compounded" before you touch a number, and the formula picks itself.

Measurement: stack the base for volume, unfold and add for surface. Pointed solids pay a third; the sphere is the only true memory item, and the powers of r remember it for you — cubed fills, squared wraps. And the scale factor just counts dimensions: k, k squared, k cubed.

The practice questions are waiting, and the openers are single-engine warm-ups. Spacing, machine words, stack-and-wrap — three small engines, all yours. You've got this!
