Hey, it's Thandi — four topics in one revision session sounds heavy, I know. But look closer: this lesson is really just three pictures, and you already know how to read pictures.

Picture one: the family tree. Every quadrilateral child keeps the family traits and adds one. Read it downwards for properties — free inheritance. Read it upwards for proofs — pay only the cheapest ticket, like diagonals bisecting each other, and the tree hands you everything else.

Picture two: two circles. All of probability is counting fairly, and the only danger is counting the overlap twice. Fill the overlap first, subtract it once, and check your regions add up to the class size. If they don't, the diagram itself tells you where to look.

Picture three: the box. Order the data — always order first — then five numbers draw the whole story: minimum, quartile, median, quartile, maximum. Box for the middle half, whiskers for the tails, and the lean reads straight off the drawing.

And the coordinate formulas? Just Pythagoras, averages, and rise over run — three old friends in new clothes.

The practice questions are waiting, and the first ones are gentle tree-reading and circle-filling. Three pictures, checked totals, no panic. You've got this!
