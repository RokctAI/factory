Hey, it's Mandy — I know a page full of factorials looks like it is shouting at you, exclamation marks everywhere. The truth is far friendlier: this is the topic where you count outfits and team photos, and everything runs on little dashes.

The one habit that matters: draw a dash for every decision, in order. Above each dash, write how many options are still available at that moment. Multiply along. That is the fundamental counting principle, and it answers most questions all by itself — 5 shirts, 2 jeans, 3 caps: 30 outfits. Multiply along a chain of AND decisions; add only between OR worlds that cannot both happen.

When things get used up — debaters in a photo row, letters of a word — the dash numbers shrink by one each step: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. That product is 5 factorial, 120. The exclamation mark is nothing scarier than shrink-and-multiply written small.

Restrictions have exactly three cures, and every question uses one. Fussy first: a forced seat gets filled before anything else. Glue: people who must sit together become one unit — arrange the units, then times 2 for the swap inside the knot; that times 2 is the step everyone drops, so whisper knot inside to yourself. Complement: for must-not-be-together, count together and subtract from the total.

Probability is the same machine run twice: count the whole world for the bottom, count your wish for the top, divide. A random 5-digit code with all different digits: 30 240 over 100 000, which is 0,3024. Cousins together in the photo: 48 over 120, two fifths. Just make sure top and bottom live in the same world — decide what one outcome is before counting either.

Dashes, shrink, three cures, one fraction. None of this needs talent — only tidy slots. Now go try the practice questions; they will feel smaller than you expect. You've got this!
