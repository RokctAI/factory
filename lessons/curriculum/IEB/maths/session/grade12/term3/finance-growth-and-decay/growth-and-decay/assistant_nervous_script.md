Hi, Mandy here — four formulae, roots, logarithms: finance can look like a wall of machinery. Here is the good news: the whole topic is two pictures and two detective moves, and you already own every tool from earlier in the year.

Picture one: the staircase and the escalator. Simple interest climbs by the same fixed step every year — 7 percent of the ORIGINAL amount, forever. Compound interest is the escalator: the whole current balance, interest included, multiplies by 1,07 again and again. Stairs add, escalators multiply. Decay is the same escalator running downhill — a van keeping 75 percent of its current value each year: times 0,75, times 0,75, never quite reaching zero.

Detective move one — finding a hidden rate: divide the end value by the start value to get the total factor, then root by the number of years. The van that dropped from 320 000 to 135 000 in three years kept the cube root of 0,421875 — that is 0,75 a year, so 25 percent shed. Divide first, root second, always.

Detective move two — finding a hidden time: when the unknown sits up in the exponent, the logarithm brings it down. How long to double at 7,5 percent? Log 2 over log 1,075 — about 9,58 — and then round UP to the end of year 10, because the bank only pays at year ends.

For messy questions — deposits arriving late, rates changing — draw the timeline. Mark every event, grow each amount through each stretch it actually lived through, and the hardest question in the topic becomes neat bookkeeping.

Stairs or escalator, divide-then-root, log-then-round-up, and a timeline for everything messy. That is the entire topic in four moves. Head over to the practice questions and try them one move at a time. You've got this!
