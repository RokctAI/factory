Today we take on the proportionality and similarity theorems — the heart of Grade 12 Euclidean geometry, where the theorem proofs themselves are examinable and return in your final examinations year after year. One parallel line cutting a triangle forces its sides into proportion, and from that single fact we will raise the two similarity theorems and finish by proving the Theorem of Pythagoras using nothing but similar triangles. We will walk the area-based proof line by line, drill the ratio technique that keeps part-to-part strictly apart from part-to-whole, and lock in the letter-ordering discipline that turns every similarity statement into correct ratios.
