Hey, it's Mandy — optimisation questions look like word soup: fences, tins, particles, profits, all demanding that you invent the mathematics yourself. Deep breath. Every single one of them is the same four-step recipe, and you already own every tool it uses.

The recipe: write the thing you want as a formula; spend the fixed total — the roll of fence, the required volume — to get down to one letter; differentiate and set to zero; then check and answer what was actually asked. That is it. The whole topic.

For motion questions, think dashboard. The odometer is position. Differentiate once: the speedometer, velocity. Differentiate again: the shove in your seat, acceleration. The phrase momentarily at rest is just the needle touching zero — write velocity equals zero and solve. Keep one small flag by the word speed: it is velocity with the minus sign stripped off.

Two habits carry most of the marks. Habit one: never differentiate while two letters are still in the formula — the constraint exists precisely to kill one of them. Habit two: rewrite fractions as powers before differentiating, and let the minus sign fall forward with the exponent — the derivative of 432 over x is MINUS 432 over x squared, and that sign is what makes the equation solvable.

At the end, interrogate your candidate like a border official: is it in the sensible domain, is it the right kind of turning point, and is it the number the question asked for — the x, the best value, or something built from them?

Formula, constraint, derivative, interpret. Say it as you work, tick each step, and the word soup turns into four familiar moves. Now go and practise on the questions waiting for you — you've got this!
