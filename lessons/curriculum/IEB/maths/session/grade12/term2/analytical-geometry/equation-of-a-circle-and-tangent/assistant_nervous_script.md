Hey, it's Mandy — brackets, squares, sign reversals, and a word like tangency on top: I know this topic looks like it was designed to intimidate. It wasn't. Underneath there is one goat, one wheel, and two short routines you already have the parts for.

Start with the goat. A circle is every point exactly one rope-length from a peg, and the equation just writes that with the distance formula: x minus a squared, plus y minus b squared, equals r squared. Reading it is two small questions. What makes each bracket zero? That is the centre — so x plus 2 means the peg sits at minus 2, signs reversed. What is the square root of the right side? That is the radius — 25 in the equation means a rope of 5.

If the equation arrives multiplied out and untidy, completing the square repacks it: x terms in one pocket, y terms in another, halve the middle number, square it, and declare every constant the bracket smuggles in on the other side. The declaring is the whole game — check yourself by expanding back.

Then the wheel. A tangent touches at one point, and the spoke to that point stands square to the road, always. Square means the gradients multiply to minus 1. So the tangent routine is four small stops: confirm the point is on the circle, find the radius gradient with rise over run, flip and negate it, and write the line through the point. Finish by substituting the touching point back in and watching both sides agree.

No new machinery anywhere — distance formula, rise over run, perpendicular gradients. Three things you already know, holding hands. Go show the practice questions who's boss!
