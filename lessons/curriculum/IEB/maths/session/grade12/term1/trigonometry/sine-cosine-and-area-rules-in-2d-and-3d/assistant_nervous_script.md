Hey, it's Mandy — if three rules plus a 3D mast felt like too many moving parts, let me hand you the sorting hat, because this topic is really one decision made well.

Look at what the question GIVES you, before touching any formula. Do you hold a complete pair — a side together with the angle directly opposite it? Then the sine rule: side over sine of its angle, all fractions equal. Do you hold two sides with the angle clamped between them, or all three sides? Then the cosine rule: Pythagoras with a hinge — the minus 2 b c cos A term shortens or stretches the far side depending on how the corner leans. Asked for area with two sides and the wedge between them? Half a b sine C, no height needed. That stocktake is the entire choosing skill, and it is worth more than any calculation.

Two safety habits. First, pair sides with their OPPOSITE angles — small a faces capital A — because the commonest zero in this topic is dividing a side by the sine of the wrong angle. Second, sense-check sizes: the biggest angle must face the longest side, so if your answer breaks that, an angle got mispaired.

For 3D, breathe and remember the open book: one flat triangle on the ground, one standing upright, sharing a single spine. Solve the page that holds the data, carry the spine across, finish with tan of the elevation angle. Draw the pages separately if the sketch swims.

Stocktake, pair correctly, find the spine. The practice questions are ready for you now — deal yourself the cards and pick the right tool every time. You've got this!
