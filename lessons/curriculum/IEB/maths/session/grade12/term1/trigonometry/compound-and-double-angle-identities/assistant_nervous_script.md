Hey, it's Mandy — four new identities plus three faces of cos 2 alpha sounds like seven things to memorise, and I watched that maths land heavily. Let me repack it into two hooks and one habit.

Hook one: sine mixes and copies. Sine of a sum blends both functions — sine cos plus cos sine — and the sign in the middle copies the sign in the bracket. Hook two: cosine pairs and flips. Cos cos, then sine sine, and the bracket's plus becomes a minus. Say those two sentences five times and all four compound identities live in your pocket.

The doubles are not new formulas — they are the compounds folded onto themselves. Set both angles equal: sine 2 alpha is 2 sine alpha cos alpha, and cos 2 alpha is cos squared minus sine squared, which the Pythagorean identity re-dresses as 2 cos squared minus 1, or 1 minus 2 sine squared. Three costumes, one actor.

The habit: before touching any proof or equation, ask which costume matches the scene. Holding only cosine? Wear 2 cos squared minus 1. The other term in the equation is a sine? Wear 1 minus 2 sine squared, and watch the equation become an ordinary quadratic you have factorised a hundred times. Quadrant signs are the only ambush left — sketch the little triangle in its quadrant and read the sign off the sketch, never from hope.

Exact values are the same game: split 15 into 45 minus 30, expand with a hook, substitute the surds you already know.

Two hooks, three costumes, one habit. The practice questions are waiting, and every one of them opens with a hook you now own. Go get them!
