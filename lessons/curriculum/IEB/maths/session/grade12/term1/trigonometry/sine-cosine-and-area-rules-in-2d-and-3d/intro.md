Today trigonometry leaves the right angle behind. The sine rule, the cosine rule and the area rule govern every triangle there is, and this session drills the decision procedure that picks the correct one from what the question supplies: a matched pair calls the sine rule, a clamped angle calls the cosine rule, two sides around a wedge call the area rule. Then we climb into three dimensions, where every mast-and-observers problem splits into two flat triangles sharing one bridge side — solve the data-rich triangle first, carry the bridge across, and the height falls out.
