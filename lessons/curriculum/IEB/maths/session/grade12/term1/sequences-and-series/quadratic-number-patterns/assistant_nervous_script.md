Hey, it's Mandy — if that difference table looked like a spreadsheet with a grudge, take a slow breath. A quadratic pattern is just a staircase that steepens by the same extra amount every step, and this whole lesson is three moves you can do half-asleep.

Move one: subtract. Write the terms in a row and take the gaps. Gaps constant? Linear, done. Gaps not constant? Take the gaps of the gaps. If THOSE are constant, the pattern is quadratic. That is the entire verdict — no guessing, no squinting.

Move two: three facts build the formula. Half the second difference gives a. The first gap equals 3 a plus b, which gives b. The first term equals a plus b plus c, which gives c. Three short sentences and T n equals a n squared plus b n plus c stands complete. Then test it on a term you can see — if it reproduces term three, it will not betray you at term fifty.

Move three: reverse questions. A value is named, so set the formula equal to it and solve the quadratic. If n lands on a natural number, that is the position. If n comes out negative, fractional or irrational, the value is simply not in the pattern — write that sentence and collect the mark.

And for the turning patterns, remember the vertex: n equals minus b over 2 a names the position of the smallest term, as long as it is whole; otherwise test the whole numbers either side.

Subtract twice, build from three facts, make n prove itself. The practice questions coming your way now use exactly those moves. Go show them who runs the table!
