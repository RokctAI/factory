Hey, it's Mandy — if the words formal definition made this lesson feel like a legal document, relax. Everything here is one vending machine and three questions about it.

Question one: is the machine honest? An honest machine drops exactly one snack per code. That is the whole definition of a function. On a graph, slide a vertical line across — touch the curve twice at any position and the machine is dishonest. Remember the direction of the promise: one snack per code. Two codes sharing a snack is perfectly fine.

Question two: how do I reverse the machine? Swap x and y, then solve for y again. That is the entire recipe. The reversed machine undoes the original's operations in the opposite order — add back what was subtracted, divide out what was multiplied. Then trial it with one pairing you already know: if the original sent 2 to 1, the inverse must send 1 to 2. Ten seconds, and any slip is caught.

Question three: will the reverse be a function? Check the ORIGINAL with a horizontal line. Touch twice, and the reverse will stall between two possible codes — that is what the plus or minus in y equals plus or minus root x is confessing. The repair is to keep one arm only: restrict the domain, and let the old domain choose the sign of the root, because the old domain becomes the new range.

Honest machine, swap and solve, horizontal line, one arm. Four moves, and every mark in this topic sits inside them. The practice questions are ready for you now — go and run those machines both ways!
