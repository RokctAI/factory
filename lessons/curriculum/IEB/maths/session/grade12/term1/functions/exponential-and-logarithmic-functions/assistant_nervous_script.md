The exponential is a story you already know: something doubles, or triples, or halves, on repeat. Day zero it is 1, so the graph goes through 0 and 1. It can never be zero or negative, so the x axis is a floor it approaches and never lands on. Base bigger than one climbs; base between zero and one falls. That is the entire graph.

The log is the same story asked backwards. Three to the what gives 81? Four — and log base 3 of 81 is just the notation for that four. Whenever a log frightens you, translate it out loud into an exponential sentence: log base b of x means b to the power something equals x. The answer usually falls out before you finish the sentence. Fractions give negative logs — steps in reverse. Zero and negatives give no log at all, because no number of steps reaches them.

For the graphs, remember the mirror: the log is the exponential reflected in the line y equals x. Anchor at 1 and 0, wall up the y axis, never touching it. And for equations, one drill: isolate the power, match bases if you can, call the log if you cannot — then sanity-check the size.
