# Part 1 — Expert

This session is a practice run through a complete exam-style paper on the algebra side of the course — a full paper written specially for this session, covering algebra and equations, number patterns and series, functions with their inverses, finance, differential calculus, and probability. Each question is stated, attacked with a plan, then solved in full at examination pace, and because this practice paper carries its own mark allocation, every mark is named as it is earned so you can see exactly where marks are paid. Work each question yourself before listening to the solution — the pause button is your invigilator.

## Subtopic: Question One — Algebra and Equations

The question, five parts. One point one: solve x times x plus three equals zero, for two marks. One point two: solve two x squared minus seven x plus two equals zero, correct to two decimal places, for four marks. One point three: solve x plus five times x minus three less than or equal to zero, for three marks. One point four: solve the square root of two x plus seven equals x plus two, for four marks. One point five: solve simultaneously, y equals x minus three and x squared plus y squared equals twenty-nine, for six marks. Nineteen marks in total.

The attack plan: this is the routine opener, so the plan is speed with checks. Already-factorised means read the roots. Two decimal places means formula. An inequality means a parabola sketch. A surd means square and then vet. Simultaneous means substitute the linear into the quadratic.

One point one: the product is zero, so x equals nought or x equals minus three — one mark for each answer, and no expanding of brackets, because expanding a factorised equation is undoing a gift.

One point two: the formula. X equals seven plus or minus the square root of, forty-nine minus sixteen, all over four. The substitution into the formula is the first mark. The discriminant, thirty-three, is the second. Then the two rounded answers: three comma one nine, and nought comma three one — a mark each, paid only at two decimals.

One point three: roots minus five and three, parabola opening upward, negative or zero between the roots. One mark for the critical values, one for the sketch or sign analysis, one for the answer: x between minus five and three, endpoints included because of the equal sign in the inequality.

One point four: square both sides — two x plus seven equals x squared plus four x plus four. That line is a mark. Rearrange to x squared plus two x minus three equals zero, factorise as x plus three times x minus one: one more mark. Candidates one and minus three: a mark. Then the vetting: x equals one gives three equals three, valid; x equals minus three gives one equals minus one, rejected. The selection with its check is the final mark.

One point five: substitute — x squared plus, x minus three squared, equals twenty-nine. One mark. Expand and simplify to two x squared minus six x minus twenty equals zero, then x squared minus three x minus ten equals zero: two marks. Factorise, x minus five times x plus two: one mark. The paired answers: x equals five with y equals two, x equals minus two with y equals minus five — two marks, and unpaired answers forfeit them.

## Subtopic: Question Two — the Quadratic Number Pattern

The question: given the pattern five; twelve; twenty-one; thirty-two. Two point one: write the next term, one mark. Two point two: determine the general term T n, four marks. Two point three: which term of the pattern equals one hundred and forty, four marks. Nine marks in total.

The attack plan: differences first, always. First differences seven, nine, eleven — second difference constant at two, so the pattern is quadratic and the machinery is a n squared plus b n plus c.

Two point one: the next first difference is thirteen, so the next term is forty-five. One mark.

Two point two: two a equals the second difference, so a equals one — first mark. Then three a plus b equals the first of the first differences, seven, giving b equals four — second mark. Then a plus b plus c equals the first term, five, giving c equals nought — third mark. The general term: T n equals n squared plus four n, and the final mark is for the formula stated and checked against a known term: four plus eight is twelve, confirmed.

Two point three: set n squared plus four n equal to one hundred and forty. Rearranged: n squared plus four n minus one hundred and forty equals zero — one mark. Factorised: n plus fourteen times n minus ten — one mark. Then n equals ten — one mark — with n equals minus fourteen rejected because term numbers are natural numbers; the rejection sentence carries the final mark.

## Subtopic: Question Three — Geometric Series

The question: consider the series eighty-one plus twenty-seven plus nine and so on. Three point one: explain why the sum to infinity exists, and calculate it, for three marks. Three point two: determine the smallest value of n for which S n exceeds one hundred and twenty-one, for four marks. Three point three: evaluate the sum from k equals one to thirty of four k plus one, for four marks. Eleven marks in total.

The attack plan: identify a and r, quote the convergence condition, and for the inequality let the powers do the arguing. The sigma part is arithmetic wearing sigma clothing — read off the first term and the common difference.

Three point one: a is eighty-one, r is one third, and since r lies strictly between minus one and one the sum converges — that sentence is the first mark. S infinity equals a over one minus r: eighty-one over two thirds — substitution mark — equals one hundred and twenty-one comma five, answer mark.

Three point two: S n equals one hundred and twenty-one comma five times, one minus a third to the n. Setting up the inequality, one hundred and twenty-one comma five times one minus a third to the n, greater than one hundred and twenty-one, is the first mark. Isolating the power gives a third to the n less than one over two hundred and forty-three — second mark. Recognising one over two hundred and forty-three as a third to the fifth: third mark. Since the powers of a third shrink, n must exceed five, so the smallest n is six — final mark, with the check that S six is about one hundred and twenty-one comma three, past the target as promised.

Three point three: the terms run five, nine, thirteen, up to one hundred and twenty-one — arithmetic, a equals five, d equals four, thirty terms; identifying that is one mark, and the count of thirty is a mark of its own, because miscounting the terms is the classic sigma slip. The sum formula: thirty over two, times, two times five plus twenty-nine times four — substitution mark. Fifteen times one hundred and twenty-six: one thousand eight hundred and ninety — answer mark.

## Subtopic: Question Four — Functions and Inverses

The question: given f of x equals three over x plus two, minus one. Four point one: write the equations of the asymptotes, two marks. Four point two: determine the x and y intercepts, three marks. Four point three: sketch f, showing all asymptotes and intercepts, two marks. Four point four: given g of x equals five to the power x, determine g inverse in the form y equals, two marks; and if the point three semicolon one hundred and twenty-five lies on g, write the corresponding point on g inverse, one mark. Ten marks in total.

The attack plan: hyperbola checklist — asymptotes from the structure, intercepts from substitution, sketch last so it inherits everything. For the inverse: swap, solve, and swap the point.

Four point one: vertical asymptote where the denominator is zero, x equals minus two; horizontal from the vertical shift, y equals minus one. A mark each.

Four point two: y-intercept, substitute x equals nought: three over two, minus one, equals a half — one mark. X-intercept, set f to zero: three over x plus two equals one, so x plus two equals three, x equals one — two marks, one for the setup, one for the value.

Four point three: two branches hugging the asymptote cross, passing through nought semicolon a half and one semicolon nought. One mark for correct branch positions with asymptotes drawn, one for intercepts labelled.

Four point four: swap x and y in y equals five to the x, giving x equals five to the y; making y the subject delivers y equals log base five of x — the swap is one mark, the log form the second. The point three semicolon one hundred and twenty-five reflects to one hundred and twenty-five semicolon three — one mark.

## Subtopic: Question Five — Finance, Growth and Decay

The question. Five point one: convert a nominal rate of nine percent per annum, compounded monthly, to the effective annual rate, for three marks. Five point two: a loan of seven hundred and fifty thousand rand is repaid over three hundred equal monthly payments at nine comma six percent per annum compounded monthly, payments starting one month after the loan is granted; calculate the monthly payment, for four marks. Five point three: calculate the total amount repaid over the full term, and the total interest paid, and comment on the comparison, for three marks. Ten marks in total.

The attack plan: part one is the effective rate identity. Part two is the present value annuity formula, with i as the monthly rate. A quick timeline confirms the standard setup: loan today, first payment one month later, three hundred payments.

Five point one: one plus i effective equals, one plus nought comma zero nine over twelve, to the power twelve — writing that identity is two marks. Evaluating: one comma zero zero seven five to the twelfth is one comma zero nine three eight, so the effective rate is nine comma three eight percent — final mark, quoted as a percentage.

Five point two: the formula — P equals x times, one minus one plus i to the minus n, over i. Identifying i as nought comma zero nine six over twelve, which is nought comma zero zero eight, and n as three hundred, earns the first mark. Substituting seven hundred and fifty thousand on the left: second mark. Rearranging for x: third mark. The payment: six thousand six hundred and four rand and ninety-four cents — final mark, sensibly rounded to the cent.

Five point three: total repaid is three hundred times the payment — about one million, nine hundred and eighty-one thousand, five hundred rand — one mark. Interest is that total minus the seven hundred and fifty thousand borrowed: about one comma two three million rand — one mark. The comment: the interest alone exceeds the amount borrowed — say it, because the comparison sentence is where the final mark sits.

## Subtopic: Question Six — Differential Calculus

The question. Six point one: determine f prime of x from first principles if f of x equals two x squared plus three, for five marks. Six point two: determine d y d x if y equals five x to the fourth plus the square root of x minus three over x squared, for four marks. Six point three: given f of x equals x cubed minus twelve x plus sixteen, determine the intercepts and turning points and sketch the graph, for seven marks. Sixteen marks in total.

The attack plan: first principles is a recited ritual — definition, expansion, division, limit. The rules question is a rewriting question in disguise. The cubic is the full anatomy: factorise, differentiate, classify.

Six point one: write the definition — f prime of x equals the limit as h tends to nought of f of x plus h minus f of x over h — one mark for the definition with the limit present. F of x plus h: two x squared plus four x h plus two h squared plus three — one mark for correct expansion. The difference: four x h plus two h squared — one mark. Divide by h: four x plus two h — one mark. Take the limit: f prime of x equals four x — final mark, and the limit symbol must have survived until this line.

Six point two: rewrite — y equals five x to the fourth plus x to the half minus three x to the minus two; the rewrite is the first mark. Differentiate term by term: twenty x cubed — mark; plus a half x to the minus a half — mark; plus six x to the minus three — mark, and watch the sign: minus three times minus two is plus six.

Six point three: factorise f — x equals two is a root, since eight minus twenty-four plus sixteen is nought, and division delivers x minus two squared times x plus four — one mark. Intercepts: x equals two, a touching double root, and x equals minus four; the y-intercept at sixteen — two marks. Derivative: three x squared minus twelve, zero at x equals two and x equals minus two — two marks. Turning points: minus two semicolon thirty-two, a local maximum, and two semicolon nought, a local minimum — one mark with coordinates. The sketch, rising to the maximum at minus two, dipping to touch the axis at two, then rising: one mark for correct shape with all points labelled.

## Subtopic: Question Seven — Probability and Counting

The question. Seven point one: events A and B are independent with P of A equal to nought comma three and P of B equal to nought comma six; calculate P of A and B, and P of A or B, for four marks. Seven point two: how many different arrangements can be made of all the letters of the word SUCCEED, where the two C's are identical and the two E's are identical, for three marks. Seven point three: what is the probability that such an arrangement begins with the letter D, for two marks. Nine marks in total. The seven questions together visit every topic on this side of the course, with the heavier topics carrying the heavier questions.

The attack plan: independence unlocks the product rule; the addition rule then delivers the union. Repeated letters divide the factorial — once for each repeated pair. For the final part, count the favourable arrangements the same way and divide.

Seven point one: independence means P of A and B equals P of A times P of B — stating that meaning is the first mark. The product: nought comma one eight — second mark. The addition rule: P of A or B equals nought comma three plus nought comma six minus nought comma one eight — third mark. The answer: nought comma seven two — fourth mark.

Seven point two: seven letters with two repeated pairs arrange in seven factorial over, two factorial times two factorial, ways — the setup is one mark, and the double division is the second. Five thousand and forty over four: one thousand two hundred and sixty — answer mark.

Seven point three: fix D in front; the remaining six letters, still carrying both repeated pairs, arrange in six factorial over two factorial two factorial ways — one hundred and eighty; that count is one mark. The probability: one hundred and eighty over one thousand two hundred and sixty, which is one seventh — final mark.
