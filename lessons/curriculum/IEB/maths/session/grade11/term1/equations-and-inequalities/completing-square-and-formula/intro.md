Today we take on the quadratics that refuse to factorise. Completing the square solves every quadratic equation without exception, and it delivers something no other method can: a rewritten form from which the turning point of the graph reads off instantly. We build the technique from the perfect-square pattern, tame the leading coefficient when it is not one, then complete the square once on the general equation to derive the quadratic formula from first principles — a derivation you are expected to reproduce on demand. We close with the habits that make formula questions reliable: list a, b and c with their signs before substituting, keep the entire numerator over two a, and round exactly once, at the end.
