Today we master the fastest of the three ways to solve a quadratic equation: factorisation. Everything rests on one property that zero alone possesses — a product can only be zero if one of its factors is — and that is why standard form, with zero on the right, is never optional. We work through the four factorising shapes of the grade, from a simple common factor to the split middle term and the difference of squares, then push the same machinery further: equations with x in a denominator and the restriction rule that guards them, a substitution trick that shrinks a fourth-degree beast into an ordinary trinomial, and a linear equation paired with a quadratic one, where answers only count in matched pairs.
