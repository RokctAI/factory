Today we solve equations whose answers never run out. Because sine, cosine and tangent repeat on every turn of the wheel, an equation like two cosine x minus root three equals zero has infinitely many solutions, and the general solution is the single line that captures them all: a reference angle measured from the positive value, quadrants chosen by the sign, and a counter k adding whole periods — three hundred and sixty degrees for sine and cosine, one hundred and eighty for tangent. From there we harvest the specific answers an interval demands, defend every division before making it, and crack the equation with a quadratic hidden inside it, rejecting in writing any value that sine or cosine could never reach.
