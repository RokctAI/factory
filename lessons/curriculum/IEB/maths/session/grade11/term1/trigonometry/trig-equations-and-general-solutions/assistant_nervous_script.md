Hey, it's Bianca — infinitely many answers sounds like a nightmare, right? An equation you can never finish? Take a breath, because the truth is the opposite: the general solution is a machine for finishing an infinite job in one line. You are not chasing every answer; you are writing the timetable that owns them all.

Here is the whole method in three moves, and it never changes. Move one: get the size. Feed the POSITIVE value into the calculator and take the reference angle. The minus sign never goes into the calculator — the minus is a map instruction, not a size. Move two: place it. The sign of the value picks two quadrants, and the reference angle gets planted in each. Move three: add the loop. Plus k three sixty for sine and cosine, plus k one eighty for tangent, because tangent's taxi runs a half-length route. Size, place, loop. Say it like a chant.

Interval questions are just the timetable plus a fishing permit: run k through minus two, minus one, zero, one, two, and keep only the catches between the markers. And check your count — a wave crosses a middle height twice per turn, so two turns means four answers. If you have three, one swam away; go find it, it is usually hiding at a negative k.

The scary quadratic ones? Nickname trick, same as always. Call sine x by the letter s, factorise the little trinomial from last term, then swap the real thing back. And if the nickname offers you sine x equals two, smile and cross it out in writing — a wheel seat cannot sit outside the wheel, and that written rejection earns you a mark.

Tonight, just practise the chant on two equations: size, place, loop. By the third one, your hand will do it without you. The practice questions are ready for you — go write some timetables!
