Today trigonometry breaks out of the right-angled triangle. With the ratios redefined on x, y and r, any angle becomes legal, and four tools organise everything that follows: the quotient identity and the square identity that bind sine, cosine and tangent together, the CAST rule that hands each quadrant its signs, the reduction formulae that fold one eighty and three sixty angles back onto acute ones, and the co-function rules where ninety degrees swaps sine and cosine outright. We finish with the two set pieces these tools exist for — collapsing a long chain of ratios into one clean expression, and proving an identity, then stating exactly which angles it is allowed to claim.
