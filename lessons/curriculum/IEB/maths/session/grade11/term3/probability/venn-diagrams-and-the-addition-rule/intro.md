Today probability graduates from single events to combinations — this or that, this and that, not this, and the deepest question of all: does one event change the chances of another? We revise the two honest ways of assigning a probability, build the Venn diagram and the addition rule that stops anyone being counted twice, and pin down the vocabulary that decides marks: mutually exclusive, complementary, and independent. By the end you will carry the product rule as a working lie detector for independence — the idea the whole of grade eleven probability stands on.
