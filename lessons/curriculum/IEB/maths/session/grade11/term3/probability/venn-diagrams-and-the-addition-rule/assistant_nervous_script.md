Hey, it's Bianca — if the vocabulary avalanche got you today — exclusive, complementary, independent, addition rule, product rule — let me stack it back into three small piles you can actually carry.

Pile one: where probabilities come from. Symmetric object — coin, die, fair spinner? Count outcomes: favourable over total. Messy real life — late taxis, drawing pins? Count a tally: occurrences over trials. One sentence names your model, and you are safe.

Pile two: the picture. Or-and-and questions are begging for two circles. Fill the OVERLAP first, subtract outwards, and whoever is left stands outside. Then the addition rule is nothing but honesty about double-counting: add the two circles, subtract the overlap once, because the friends in both WhatsApp groups got counted twice. If the circles truly never touch — mutually exclusive — the minus is zero all by itself. And complementary is the strict big sister of exclusive: no overlap AND nothing left outside, both checks, every time.

Pile three: influence. Independent means one event tells you nothing about the other — the coin has no memory, and independent probabilities multiply. When events live in data, run the lie detector: multiply the two separate probabilities, compare with the actual both-probability. Equal means independent; unequal means dependent. And never let exclusive masquerade as independent — exclusive events destroy each other, which is the loudest influence there is.

Three piles: source, picture, influence. Every question today lives in one of them. Go to the practice questions and sort each one into its pile first — the rest is arithmetic. You've got this!
