Today statistics learns to measure scatter. Two classes can share an average and be nothing alike — one steady, one wild — and only measures of spread can tell them apart. We revise the five number summary and the box-and-whisker map it draws, then build grade eleven's precision instruments: variance and the standard deviation, the typical distance of the data from its mean. With centre and spread in hand we diagnose the shape of a dataset — symmetric or skewed, with the mean chasing the tail — and finish by putting suspicious values on trial with the outlier fences, learning when to trust the fragile tools and when to reach for the robust ones.
