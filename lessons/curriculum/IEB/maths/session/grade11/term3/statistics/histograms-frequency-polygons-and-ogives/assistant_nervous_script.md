Hey, it's Bianca — if three different graphs of the same fifty study times felt like two graphs too many, let me hand you the one sentence that organises the entire lesson: each picture answers its own question.

The histogram answers how many in EACH class. Touching buildings, height equals frequency, tallest building is the modal class. Before drawing anything, add the frequencies and check they hit n — that one audit protects every mark that follows.

The frequency polygon answers the same question, but built for comparisons. Dot on each rooftop's centre — the class MIDPOINT — straight lines between dots, and two zeros anchoring the ends to the ground. Midpoints, straight lines, anchors: three words, full marks.

The ogive answers how many SO FAR. Build the running-total column — 4, 13, 30, 42, 50 — and plot each total at the UPPER boundary of its class, because thirteen-under-thirty is only a complete fact at thirty. Start at zero-zero, end at n, and remember the curve never comes downhill, because running totals never shrink.

Then the whole payoff is one ruler move: across from the position, down to the value. Median at position n over two. Quartiles at a quarter and three-quarters. Percentiles at their percent of n. Say approximately every time, and leave your dotted lines on the graph — they are marks in themselves.

That is truly all of it: each class, midpoints joined, so far, across and down. Go run the practice questions with your ruler and watch this topic become the friendliest marks in statistics. You've got this!
