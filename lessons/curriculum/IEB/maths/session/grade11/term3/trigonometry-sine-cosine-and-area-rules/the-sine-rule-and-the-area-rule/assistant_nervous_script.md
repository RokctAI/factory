Hey, it's Bianca — if triangles without right angles feel like lawless territory, take a breath: this lesson is two formulae and one labelling habit, nothing more.

The habit first, because it protects everything: small letters live OPPOSITE their capitals. Draw the triangle yourself, plant the capitals at the corners, then cross the triangle with your pen to place each small letter on the far side. Do that, and both formulae become safe to use without thinking.

Formula one, the area rule: half, times one side, times the other side, times sine of the angle squeezed BETWEEN them. Think of the folding security gate — the two sides are the arms, and sine of the hinge angle is the squash factor. At 90 degrees, no squash, biggest area. The only trap is grabbing an angle that is not at the hinge: put a finger on each given side and use the angle where your fingers meet.

Formula two, the sine rule: side over sine of its facing angle is one fixed exchange rate around the whole triangle. One complete pair — a side with its opposite angle — sets the rate; everything else trades at it. Hunting a side? Write sides on top. Hunting an angle? Flip the fractions. And when an angle comes from inverse sine, check the second suspect at 180 minus your answer against the angle sum — usually it fails, and saying so earns the mark.

Multi-step questions are just the two formulae in a queue: claim the free third angle, build the missing side, then pay the area rule its price. Plan backwards, drive forwards.

Label, squash, rate, suspect. Four words and you own this lesson. The practice questions are ready for you — you've got this!
