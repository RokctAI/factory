Today we repair the sine rule's blind spot. Two sides with the angle pinched between them, or three sides and no angle at all — the sine rule stalls on both, and the cosine rule takes over: Pythagoras generalised to every triangle, with a correction term that measures how far the corner leans from ninety degrees. We prove the rule with one dropped perpendicular, drill both directions — finding sides and hunting angles without ambiguity — and then work the full two-dimensional stories of harbours, bearings and shared sides, where two triangles queue up and one bridge side carries the answer between them.
