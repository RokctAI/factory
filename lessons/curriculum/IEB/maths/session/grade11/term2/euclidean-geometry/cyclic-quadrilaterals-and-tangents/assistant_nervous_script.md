Hey, it's Bianca — that was the deep end of circle geometry, and if the words alone wore you out — cyclic, converse, alternate segment — let your shoulders drop for a minute, because the whole session was really three pictures you have already seen.

Picture one: the kraal wall. Two opposite gate posts watch two arcs that together make the whole wall, so their angles must share one eighty. That is the entire cyclic quadrilateral theorem — and each converse test is just that sentence read backwards to prove a circle exists.

Picture two: the peg and the ropes. The spoke meets the kerb dead square — that is the tangent fact, and it turns every length question into Pythagoras: seventeen paces, radius eight, rope fifteen. And two ropes from one peg are always twins, which plants an isosceles triangle in the diagram: fifty-two at the peg leaves sixty-four and sixty-four at the wall.

Picture three: the echo. The angle between the wall and the chord shouts across the chord and comes back identical from the far arc. Same size, opposite segment — that is the tangent-chord theorem, fully.

Tonight's ten-minute rehearsal: tangents from P touch at A and B, angle APB is fifty-two, C is on the major arc. Walk the chain out loud — twins, then sixty-four and sixty-four, then echo, so angle ACB is sixty-four. Then check it the second way: two nineties and a fifty-two leave one twenty-eight at the centre, and half is sixty-four. Two roads, one answer, zero doubt.

Say the reasons as you go — they are where the marks sleep. Now head to the practice questions and wake a few up. You've got this!
