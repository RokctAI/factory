Hey, it's Bianca — I saw some worried faces when the word "prove" started appearing today. Circle geometry sounds like a new language, but the whole lesson was really just three pictures, and you already own all three.

Picture one: the fold. Fold a paper circle so a chord's ends meet — the crease halves the chord, crosses it at ninety, and runs through the centre. That is all three chord theorems in one crease. And every chord sum is one triangle: half the chord, the distance, one radius. Half the chord — that is the mark everyone drops.

Picture two: the stadium. Every seat on the same arc sees the goal at the same angle. The centre is greedy and always sees double. And if the goal-chord is a diameter, every seat gets a perfect ninety. Centre one twenty, seats sixty — that is the entire doubling theorem.

Picture three: the case file. Darken the radii, hunt the diameter, shade the arc, then collect small certainties one at a time — and write the reason after every claim. Eight little phrases cover every line you will ever write.

Tonight's ten-minute fix: draw a circle, centre O, with angle AOB equal to eighty degrees, C on the major arc. Find angle ACB, then the base angles of triangle AOB. Check yourself: forty, then fifty and fifty. Say the reason out loud for each line — centre is double, radii, base angles, angle sum.

Reasons are not decoration; they are where the marks live, and writing them is a habit, not a talent. Habits are learnable. Now go open the practice questions and build yours — you're closer than you think. You've got this!
