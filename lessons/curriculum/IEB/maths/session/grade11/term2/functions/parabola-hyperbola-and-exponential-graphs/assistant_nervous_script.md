Hey, it's Bianca — three graph families, a dozen controls, fences, tents, tollgates... if your notes look like a construction site right now, give me two minutes, because the whole lesson was secretly one idea wearing three outfits.

The one idea: every equation is a work order on a basic shape. The a is tension — steeper, wider, or flipped. The p pushes sideways, and you never argue with its sign — you just ask what x makes the bracket zero, and that is where the action moved to. The q is the crane, straight up or down. Parabola, hyperbola, exponential: same three knobs, every time.

Then each family adds one personal quirk. The tent touches its lowest point, so its range gets the equals sign. The hyperbola has two fences — where the bottom would divide by zero, and the height the fraction cannot make — and its domain and range are just those fences written out. The exponential has one floor it never lands on, so its range is strict: greater than, no equals.

And the tollgates: average gradient is two substitutions and a division — the taut string. When the gates creep together, the strings sneak up on one number, and that number is the curve's own steepness at the point. That is all of it.
