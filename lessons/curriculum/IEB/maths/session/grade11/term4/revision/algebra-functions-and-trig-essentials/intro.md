Today is the revision sweep for the year's algebra and trigonometry — nothing new, everything sharpened to its exam-day version. We move through quadratic equations in all their disguises with the discriminant as the one-calculation answer to nature-of-roots, then the three function families and the single grammar of shifts that runs them all, and finally the trigonometry engine: identities, reduction, general solutions, and the sine, cosine and area rules. Along the way we visit the error museum — the four traps that cost candidates the same marks every year — so that this year they cost someone else.
