Hey, it's Bianca — building plans look like an architect's secret language, but you already speak it: it is just measuring, times fifty, with a shopping trip at the end.

The plan: roof off, viewed from above. Thick double lines are walls, a gap with a quarter-circle is a door and its swing, triple thin lines are a window. The scale 1 : 50 has the friendliest trick in the book — halve the paper millimetres, then add two zeros, and you have real millimetres. 80 mm halves to 40, add the zeros, 4 000 mm, which is 4,0 m. That is the entire conversion.

Elevations: the four faces of the house, named for the direction they LOOK toward. They carry the heights the floor plan cannot. For matching questions, be a detective: count the openings on each wall of the plan, find the elevation with that exact list — and draw a little arrow of yourself standing outside, because looking at the north face means facing south, and the plan's west lands on your RIGHT. The arrow beats imagination every time.

Costing: three moves, always. Scale to real. Real to quantity — area for floors and paint, perimeter for skirting, minus doors and windows. Quantity to rands through pack sizes, rounding UP because shops sell wholes, with the reason written down.

Packing: the opposite rounding. Divide the box by the can, direction by direction, and round DOWN each time — 2,3 layers is 2 layers, the rest is air. Then multiply the three counts. Never divide volume by volume for a count; melted cans are not cans.

Plan to real, real to quantity, quantity to rands — and round in the direction reality points. You've got this!
