Today we read maps the way careful travellers and careful candidates both must: the number scale that shrinks the world by an exact ratio, the bar scale that survives a photocopier, the grid and compass that find and describe a place, and the chain that turns centimetres on paper into kilometres, minutes and petrol money. We work with two maps of a single journey — a street map for the bus depot and the library, and a road map for the trip to White River — and we finish by deciding, in rands, whether the car or the minibus wins. Every step of measure, multiply, convert and compare carries its own mark.
