Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic looks like four separate mountains, but VAT, UIF, exchange rates and tariffs are honestly one small hill: a percentage or a rate, pointed in the right direction. Direction is everything.

VAT: going up, multiply by 1,15. Coming back down, divide by 1,15 — never subtract fifteen percent, because the inclusive price is 115% of the original, and fifteen percent of the wrong number is the oldest lost mark in the book. Before every VAT question, whisper: is the fifteen already inside this price?

UIF: one percent, the kindest percentage you will ever meet. Slide the comma two places — R9 200 gross becomes R92. The worker pays it, the employer matches it, the fund banks both. Check whether the question wants the worker's side, the employer's side, or the total, because all three numbers will be sitting in the options looking innocent.

Exchange rates: say the rate as a price. One euro costs R19,80. Turning rands into euros? Divide. Turning euros into rands? Multiply. Then run the size check: rands into euros must shrink, euros into rands must grow. If your answer breaks that rule, you chose the wrong direction — fix it before anyone else sees.

Tariffs: two deals, two formulae, test both at the usage given, pick the smaller, and state the saving in one clear sentence. Monthly-fee deals win for heavy users, pay-as-you-go wins for light users, and they swap at the break-even point.

One rate, one direction, one honest sentence at the end. Now go try the practice questions — you've got this!
