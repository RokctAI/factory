Hey, it's Bianca — data handling looks like a mountain of vocabulary, but the whole topic is one story told five times over: ask, collect, sort, squeeze, draw. Walk the story in order and every question knows its place.

Ask: the question must carry a group, a quantity and a unit — rand per learner per week, never "too much snacking". Collect: the population is everyone, the sample is the stand-ins, and the only trustworthy sample is one that chance picked — every eighteenth name on the register, not your circle at break. When a question says "identify the bias", answer in three parts: who got over-counted, why the method did it, and which way the result tilts.

Sort: tally into bins that say "to under" — R20 to under R40, with the next bin opening AT R40 — so nobody owns two addresses. Then add the frequencies and make sure they land on the total. That little addition is a free mark, every single time.

Squeeze: three middles and a spread. Mean is the share-out — total divided by count. Median is the queue — sort first, tap the middle shoulder. Mode is the favourite. Range is biggest minus smallest. And when mean and median argue, point straight at the outlier: the mean shook hands with the R100, the median never met it.

Draw: bars compare, lines trend, scattered dots betray partnerships. Before believing any graph, check where the axis starts — an axis opening at R25 turns a R4 gap into a canyon. Quote the real numbers and the picture loses its magic.

Ask, collect, sort, squeeze, draw — with a reason in words at every step. Now go show the practice questions who's boss. You've got this!
