Hey, it's Bianca — measurement questions look long and scary because they arrive dressed up as an entire building project. Strip the story off and there are only three moves in the whole topic, and you already own two of them.

Move one: perimeter, the walk around the edge. Add all the sides, or for a rectangle add one long and one short and double it. That is what fencing and skirting are bought by. Move two: area, the covering over the top. Length times breadth for a rectangle, half base times height for a triangle, 3,14 times radius times radius for a circle. That is what paint, tiles, paving and grass are bought by. If you can ask yourself "am I buying the edge or the covering?", you have already picked the right formula.

Move three is where the marks separate: turning the answer into a shopping list. The question always gives you the coverage rate and the packet size, and it always expects you to use both. Paint covers so many square metres per litre. Tiles come in boxes. Add roughly 10% wastage for anything that gets cut, then round UP — never down — because eleven boxes on a twelve-box floor means bare floor and a second taxi to the shop.

Your mark-protecting habit here is subtraction. Study the picture for what must come OFF before anything meets a price: a gate off the fence, a door and a window off the walls, a herb bed off the paving. Nearly every question hides one, and it is worth a mark on its own.

And write the units on every line — m for the edge, m² for the covering. Then finish with a sentence: she must buy 12 boxes, costing R3 180.

Edge or covering, subtract the openings, round up, units on. That is the whole topic — now go prove it on the practice questions. You've got this!
