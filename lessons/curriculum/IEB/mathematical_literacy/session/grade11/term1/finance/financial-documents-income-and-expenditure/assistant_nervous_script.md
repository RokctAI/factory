Hey, it's Bianca — I know finance questions look like a mountain of paperwork, but every document in this topic runs on the same three moves, and not one of them is new maths. Multiply, add, fifteen percent. That is the entire engine.

The municipal statement: units used, times the price per unit, plus the fixed basic charge, plus VAT at fifteen percent on the whole lot. Work down the page in that exact order every time and the bill builds itself. If a question hands you the total WITH VAT and asks for the amount before, divide by 1,15 — never subtract fifteen percent, that lands on the wrong number every single time.

Income and expenses: just sort the drawers. Fixed means the amount is promised — salary, rent, stokvel. Variable means the amount moves — wages, groceries, taxi fare. Occasional means no timetable — uniforms, gifts, funerals. Label with a reason, total honestly, and write surplus or deficit in a sentence. That sentence carries a mark.

And simple interest, the one everyone dreads: principal, times the rate as a decimal, times the years. R6 500 at fourteen percent for two years is 6 500 times 0,14 times 2 — R1 820 — and the loan truly costs R8 320. Three numbers multiplied, then added back on. That is everything simple interest has ever been.

Your mark-protecting habit: convert before you multiply. Percent to decimal, months to years. Fourteen percent is 0,14; thirty months is 2,5. Almost every mark lost in this topic is a skipped conversion, not maths misunderstood.

Multiply, add, fifteen percent, and close with a sentence in rands. Now go show those practice questions who's boss — you've got this!
