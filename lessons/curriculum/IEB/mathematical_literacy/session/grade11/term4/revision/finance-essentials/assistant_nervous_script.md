Hey, it's Bianca — a whole year of finance looks like a mountain, but it is really five small machines, and you already know how to drive each one.

The payslip machine: gross minus named deductions equals net. UIF is 1 percent — slide the decimal, R112 on R11 200. Pension 7,5 percent — 0,075 times gross. Show the subtraction line; the visible path is what earns the credit, not just the answer.

The VAT machine has two gears. Forward: times 1,15. Reverse — when the price INCLUDES VAT: divide by 1,15, never subtract 15 percent. And remember the zero-rated staples: brown bread, maize meal, rice — sort them out before applying VAT to a trolley.

The tariff machine climbs steps: first block at its rate, the rest at theirs. 150 units at R1,95 is R292,50; the next 110 at R2,85 is R313,50; total R606. Never one rate times everything.

The exchange machine: the rate is just a price per euro. Multiply to buy euros into rand, divide to turn rand into euros, and check that the rand number is the big one.

The slow-money machine: break-even is fixed cost over contribution — R1 800 over R15 is 120 sales. Compound interest grows year by year in a table — R8 000 at 10 percent runs 8 800, 9 680, 10 648. Inflation is the same table working against your wallet.

Five machines, one habit: end with the sentence that answers the question. Now go drive them through the practice set. You've got this!
