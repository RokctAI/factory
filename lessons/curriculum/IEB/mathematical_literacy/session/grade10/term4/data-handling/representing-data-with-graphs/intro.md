Today we are building the data pictures ourselves — bar graphs, histograms and broken-line graphs — and learning to read the pie chart, which Grade 10 interprets but never draws. All our data comes from one week at a school snack stand: pie counts by type, daily takings with a mystery buried in Wednesday, and a class's grouped science marks. The deepest skill here is matching the data's shape to the right graph — named categories take bars with gaps, grouped numbers take touching histogram bars, time takes a joined line — and then drawing so precisely that the picture cannot be misread. By the end you will know the marker's checklist by heart: title, labels, an even scale starting at nought, and heights audited back against the table.
