Hey, it's Thandi — four graph types can feel like four separate subjects to revise, but they are honestly one skill in four outfits. Let me shrink it for you.

First, name the animal. Separate named groups — piles — get a bar graph with gaps. Numbers scooped into intervals — buckets — get a histogram with touching bars. Something measured over time — a journey — gets a line graph. And the pie chart is just the piles squashed into a tart, already sliced for you: you only ever read it, never draw it.

Then every drawing follows the same four ruler rules: title on top, words and units on both axes, a scale that starts at nought and climbs in EQUAL steps, and heights copied straight from the table. That is the entire marking checklist. Draw with it next to you and the marks are mechanical.

Pie questions are one multiplication: the share times the whole. Amount wanted? Share times the total pies — 40 percent of 150 is 60. Angle wanted? Share times 360 — 40 percent gives 144 degrees. Check that shares total 100 and angles total 360, and printed errors expose themselves.

Interpretation? Three layers: facts with numbers, trend with evidence, reason that fits the dip. If your reason were true, the graph would look exactly as it does — that is the test.

Now go draw the practice graphs, checklist beside you, and read each bar back when you finish. You've got this!
