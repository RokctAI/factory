Hey, it's Thandi — a whole year of finance in one revision session sounds heavy, but here is the secret: it is the same four moves wearing different price tags. Percent. Per unit. Two lists. Fixed plus rate.

Percent: 30 percent off R650 is 0,30 times 650 — R195. Percentage change? The word FROM names the bottom of the fraction: from 20 to 23 is 3 over 20, fifteen percent. And including VAT means divide by 1,15 — never subtract 15 percent from the tag.

Per unit: best-buy questions are settled by division. R230 over 10 kilograms is R23,00 a kilogram; R62,50 over 2,5 is R25,00. Divide both, then say the winner out loud with its numbers — that sentence is a mark.

Two lists: every budget and every profit question is money in, money out, subtract, name the answer. Surplus, deficit, profit, loss — the name is part of the answer.

Fixed plus rate: bank fees and tariffs are one shape — a fixed part, plus a rate times the usage. R3 plus 1,5 percent of R400 is R9. R60 plus R2,20 times 250 units is R610. On a graph, the fixed part is where the line starts and the rate is the slope.

Last thing: reverse every answer. Price before VAT times 1,15 must hit the tag. Surplus plus expenses must hit the income. One line of proof, every time. Go run the practice questions with those four moves. You've got this!
