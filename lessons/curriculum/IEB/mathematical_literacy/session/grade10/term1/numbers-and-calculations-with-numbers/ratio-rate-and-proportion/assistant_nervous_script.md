Hey, it's Thandi — ratio, rate, direct proportion, indirect proportion. Four scary names, but honestly? There are only three moves in this whole topic, and you already use all three at the shops.

Move one is sharing. Any ratio question, however it's dressed up: add the parts, divide the money by the total parts, multiply each share back out. Add the parts, find one part, deal them out. Then re-add your answers and check they land on the original amount — that one check catches nearly every slip before the marker sees it.

Move two is "what does ONE cost, or what does ONE do?" That's a rate, and division takes you there. Rands per kilogram, kilometres per litre, rands per kilolitre. The only ambush is time: if the answer must be per HOUR, turn your minutes into hours first. Forty-five minutes is 0,75 of an hour, not 45.

Move three is deciding whether more means more, or more means less. More muffins cost more money — drop to one, then climb. More workers means fewer days — multiply the pair to size the job, then divide. Before touching the calculator, ask out loud: when this goes up, does that go up or down? Answer that and the method picks itself.

And the habit that guards your marks in all of them: write the substitution down, keep the units on every line, round only at the very end. Nobody can credit a method they cannot see, and "25,00" without "per kg" is only half an answer.

Three moves, one habit, one closing sentence that answers what was asked. That's the whole topic in your pocket. Now go take on the practice questions — you've got this!
