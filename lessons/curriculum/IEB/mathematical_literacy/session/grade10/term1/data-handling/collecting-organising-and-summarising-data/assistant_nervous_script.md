Hey, it's Thandi — data handling looks like a swamp of vocabulary. Population, sample, primary, secondary, discrete, continuous, categorical, tally, frequency, mean, median, mode, range. Thirteen words and a project due. Breathe — the actual work is small, and you have done it before, at home, with the washing.

The whole topic is four moves. One: ask a question a number could answer. Two: collect the answers without only asking the people who will say what you expect. Three: line the values up in order and sort them into piles that never overlap. Four: find the numbers that speak for the pile. That is it. Everything else is vocabulary resting on those four moves.

The four summary numbers each hold one job. Mean is sharing out — total divided by how many. Median is whoever stands in the middle once everyone lines up in order, and you must line them up first, every single time. Mode is the one that shows up most often, and it is the only one that works for words instead of numbers. Range is biggest minus smallest — the stretch, not the centre.

The habit that rescues the most marks: show the division. Write 540 divided by 15, then write 36 minutes. If the calculator slips, the marker still sees the method and credits it. And keep the unit attached — minutes, rands, commuters. A bare number is an unfinished answer.

One more thing that turns a good answer into a full one. When the mean and the median disagree, don't panic and don't pick one at random. Say WHY they disagree — a few very long waits pulled the mean up — and say which one answers the question that was asked. That single sentence is often worth as much as the whole calculation.

Four moves, four numbers, one unit, one sentence of interpretation. Now go try the practice questions — you've got this!
