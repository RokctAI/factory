Hey, it's Thandi — probability has this reputation as the strange topic, the one where answers feel like opinions. They are not. Probability is counting, and you have been counting since before you could tie shoelaces.

The whole subject sits on one short line from 0 to 1. Zero is impossible. One is certain. A half is a tossed coin. Below a half, unlikely; above it, likely. And here is a free gift: if your answer ever comes out negative or bigger than 1, you have not broken mathematics — you have made a slip, and the scale just caught it for you at no charge.

The actual calculation is one fraction: the ways the thing you want can happen, over the ways anything can happen. Fourteen blue sweets out of forty is 14 over 40, which is 0,35, which is 35 percent. Fraction, decimal, percentage — one fact in three outfits, and the question tells you which outfit to dress it in.

Now the place where the marks actually hide, so hear it twice: the BOTTOM of the fraction. "Out of all 160 learners" is a different bottom number from "out of the 100 under-16s". Before you write a single digit, say quietly to yourself: "out of whom?" That one question is worth more in this topic than anything else I could tell you.

Two small rules that carry serious weight. When you see "not" or "at least one", grab the complement — 1 minus what you already have. And for anything with two stages, draw the little tree before calculating, remembering that heads-tails and tails-heads are two separate paths, never one.

You already think this way, you know. You know the taxi is more likely to be full at five in the afternoon than at ten in the morning. We are just putting numbers on instincts you already own. You've got this!
