Hey, it's Thandi — VAT, discounts and interest in one session sounds like three separate monsters, and I promise it is one animal wearing three coats. Every question in this lesson is a percentage question, and a percentage only ever asks one thing: fifteen out of every hundred of WHAT?

That "of what" is the entire game. VAT on a shelf price is already inside the number, so you travel backwards by dividing by 1,15 — never by subtracting 15 percent. A second discount comes off the already-reduced price, not the original. Simple interest is always calculated on the amount you started with, year after year after year. Three situations, one question asked three times.

What actually costs people marks here is not the maths — it is the months. Simple interest wants TIME IN YEARS. Six months is 0,5. Nine months is 9 divided by 12, which is 0,75. Fifteen months is 1,25. Write that little fraction down before your fingers touch the calculator and the mark is yours forever.

Build four habits and this topic pays you back every time: write the substitution line before pressing anything, keep the rand sign and the cents on every money answer, round only at the very end, and finish with a sentence — "the family should buy during the sale because…". Markers give real marks for that sentence.

And remember you already do this maths in the wild. You know a R200 hoodie at 25 percent off is not R25. You know the bread costs what the shelf says, tax and all. We are just teaching your street sense to show its working on paper. You've got this!
