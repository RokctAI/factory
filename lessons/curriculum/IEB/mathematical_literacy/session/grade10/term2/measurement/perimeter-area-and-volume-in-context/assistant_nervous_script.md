Hey, it's Thandi — if measurement looks like a mountain of formulas you were supposed to memorise, breathe out, because here is the secret: memorising formulas is not what this topic tests. Nobody is checking whether you can recite pi r squared h in your sleep.

What is being tested is three small decisions, the same three every single time. Decision one: which question is this — how far around, how much to cover, or how much to fill? Around is perimeter, cover is area, fill is volume. Decision two: are all my units the same yet? Decision three: which way does the rounding go at the end?

That third one is the sneaky mark. Materials go UP — 8,99 litres of paint means two tins, because arriving home short means a second trip and a wall in two shades. Days and whole items go DOWN — even 5,99 days of water is 5, because the sixth day runs dry before it finishes. Ask yourself "must I have enough, or must it be complete?" and the direction chooses itself.

Two small habits that quietly guard your marks. Halve the diameter before any circle formula — everyone forgets, and forgetting makes the tank four times bigger on paper than it is in the yard. And write the unit on every line, not just at the end, because a right number in the wrong unit is simply a wrong answer.

And listen — you already do this maths. You have judged whether one two-litre bottle will stretch around the table. You have eyeballed a wall and guessed at the paint. We are only teaching your hands to show what your head already does. You've got this!
