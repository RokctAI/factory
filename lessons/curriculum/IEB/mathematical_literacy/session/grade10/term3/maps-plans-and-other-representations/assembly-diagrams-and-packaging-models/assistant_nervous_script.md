Hey, it's Thandi — a page of exploded furniture and floating screws can look like chaos, but this is honestly one of the friendliest topics you will meet. No formulas at all. Just reading and small multiplications.

The assembly diagram is a recipe. Parts list equals ingredients — count them first. Numbered steps equal the method — follow them in order. The little zoom circle means "everyone messes this up, look closely," and the crossed-out picture literally shows you the wrong way so you cannot fall for it. Every question is just "say what the picture says, in words."

The quantities chain together like beads: 3 shelves, times 2 brackets each, times 4 screws each — 24 screws. Pull the chain one link at a time and the checking questions, the missing-screws questions, all become one-step sums.

Jars in a box? Egg box. Spots along, spots across, storeys up: 4 times 3 times 1 is 12. Never divide volumes — round jars waste corner space and the volume answer lies. And remember the two rounding worlds: FITTING rounds down, BUYING rounds up. The verb tells you which.

Cheapest box? Divide price by jars for cost per jar — but if the question gives a real order, cost the actual job instead. Two answers, two questions, both yours now.

Go read the practice diagrams slowly, finger on the parts list. You've got this!
