Hey, it's Thandi — no formulae in this topic, promise. If that word "interest" makes you brace for algebra, relax: everything here is a percentage you already know how to take.

Hold onto one picture: interest is rent for money. Borrow money, you pay the rent. Save money, the bank is borrowing from YOU, so the bank pays the rent. That is the whole direction question sorted — who is using whose money?

Then two words that refuse to be the same thing. The RATE is the price tag — a percent. The INTEREST is the actual money — rand. Six percent per year is the tag; R240 is what lands. Check the unit on your answer and half the trick options die instantly.

Multi-year questions? One year at a time, and always write the balance the year STARTS with. R4 000 grows to R4 240, so next year's 6 percent works on 4 240, not on the old 4 000. Balance first, percentage second. That is the only place people slip.

And the mashonisa question — just read the small words. Fifteen percent PER YEAR against twenty-five percent PER MONTH is not close; it is R600 a year against R1 000 a month. Same time period first, then compare.

Go work the practice set: one percentage at a time, units on everything. You've got this!
