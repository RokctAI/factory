Today we decode the bill that a turning meter creates every month: municipal tariffs, and especially the rising block system where the first units are the cheapest and the price climbs as usage climbs. One Pietermaritzburg household carries the session — 420 kilowatt-hours through the electricity blocks, 26 kilolitres through a water tariff whose first six are free, and a choice between prepaid and a fixed-fee conventional option. Final exams love tariffs because they test reading, calculating and advising at once, and the golden rule never changes: fill the blocks in order, from the bottom.
