Hi, Mandy here — VAT questions are honestly one of the friendliest parts of finance, because the entire topic is one number: 1,15. Everything else is knowing which way to point it.

The picture that holds it all: the price before VAT is 100%, the government adds a 15% slice, and the price you pay is 115%. So going forward — quote to final bill — multiply by 1,15. Going backward — till slip to pre-VAT price — divide by 1,15. That is the whole machine.

The trap that eats marks every single year: taking 15% OFF an inclusive price. Never. The 15% was charged on the smaller pre-VAT number, so slicing the bigger number cuts too deep. R391 on the shelf divided by 1,15 is R340 before VAT — and you can prove it, because 340 times 1,15 runs straight back to 391. Always run the check; it is five seconds of certainty.

Need just the VAT hiding inside a price? Fifteen parts out of every 115: multiply the inclusive price by 15, divide by 115. R391 gives R51, done.

And the till slip rule: sort before you slice. Brown bread, maize meal, milk, eggs, fresh fruit and veg — zero-rated, no VAT inside them at all. Only the starred, standard-rated pile contains tax, so the fraction touches that pile only. Fresh potatoes free, frozen chips taxed — know the pairs.

Label every price as 100% or 115% before you calculate, point the 1,15 the right way, and check backwards answers by running them forwards. You've got this!
