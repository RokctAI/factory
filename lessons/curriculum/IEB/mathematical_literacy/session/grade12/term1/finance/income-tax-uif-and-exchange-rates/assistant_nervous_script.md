Hi, Mandy here — tax tables and exchange rates look like the scariest part of finance, but both run on pictures you already understand: a ladder and a price tag.

The ladder first. Your income climbs the tax table rung by rung, and each rung keeps its own rate forever. The table has even pre-calculated the lower rungs for you — that flat amount at the start of your band, like the R42 678, IS the tax on everything below the line. Your only job is the slice above the line: subtract the band's floor, multiply the slice by the band's rate, add the flat amount. Three lines of working. Then the gift at the end: subtract the rebate, R17 235, before you divide by 12 for the monthly PAYE. If you ever catch yourself multiplying your WHOLE salary by 26%, stop — only the slice pays the new rate.

UIF is even kinder: one percent of salary, but the counting stops at the ceiling of R17 712. Below the ceiling, a clean one percent. Above it, everyone pays the same R177,12. Check the ceiling first, then subtract PAYE and UIF from gross to land on net pay — and say the answer as a sentence about what reaches the bank.

Exchange rates: read the rate as a price tag. R20,40 per euro means one euro costs R20,40, like apples per kilogram. Buying euros? Divide your rands by the tag. Turning euros into rands? Multiply. Then the size check: the rand number must always be bigger than the euro number, or the machine ran backwards. Rate goes up, rand weakened, imports cost more — that sentence is a mark.

Ladder, ceiling, price tag. Slice, cap, check the size. You've got this!
