Hi, Mandy here — interest and inflation questions are gentler than they look, because the whole topic runs on one move you already own: multiplying by a growth number like 1,065 or 1,05. Everything else is deciding how many times to press it.

Simple interest first: it only ever looks at the ORIGINAL amount. Rate times principal gives one year's interest, then multiply by the years — R12 000 at 6,5% is R780 a year, R2 340 over three years, straight line, done. Compound interest looks at the CURRENT balance, so build the table: 12 000 becomes 12 780, becomes 13 610,70, becomes 14 495,40 — multiply by 1,065 again each year, and write down every row, because the rows carry marks.

Bank statements are just a story read top to bottom: money in, money out, balance keeping score. Circle the fees — the admin fee and the ATM charges — and then rebuild the closing balance yourself: opening plus credits minus debits. If you land on the bank's number to the cent, everything above it is right.

Inflation is compound growth working against you: prices ride the × 1,05 machine while your note stands still. So compare every rate to inflation before you celebrate — growth above inflation is a real gain, below it is a loss in disguise, and a 3% raise during 5% inflation quietly buys less than last year.

Label the principal, the rate and the years before touching the calculator, build the table year by year, and always finish with a sentence about buying power. You've got this!
