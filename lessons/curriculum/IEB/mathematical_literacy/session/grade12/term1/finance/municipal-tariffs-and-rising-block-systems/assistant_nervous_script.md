Hi, Mandy here — tariff tables look like walls of numbers, but every tariff question in matric is the same three moves: find the blocks, fill them in order, add once. That is the entire topic.

The staircase rule first. Consumption fills the blocks from the bottom, and each block's price touches only the units inside it. The first 150 units at R1,95, the next ones at R2,45 — nobody ever re-prices your cheap units just because you climbed higher. If you catch yourself multiplying the WHOLE consumption by one rate, stop; that is the single biggest mark-loser in this topic.

Second move: turn every range into a capacity before you multiply. A block from 6 to 20 holds 14 units, not 20 — subtract the boundaries. Then check that your block amounts add back to the meter reading. Six plus fourteen plus six must give the 26 on the meter. Ten seconds, and it catches almost every slip.

Third move: subtotal first, VAT once, at the end. All the consumption charges plus any fixed charge, draw a line, then 15% on the lot. And if the total already includes VAT, divide by 1,15 — never subtract 15%.

Comparisons scare people, but they are just the same bill calculated twice. Work out both options at the given consumption, name the cheaper one, say the saving in rands, and give one sentence of advice for that household. Low users suit no-fixed-fee options; heavy users suit flat rates. The crossing point on the graph is break-even — below it one line is cheaper, above it the other.

Blocks in order, capacities not boundaries, VAT once, compare with two full calculations. You've got this!
