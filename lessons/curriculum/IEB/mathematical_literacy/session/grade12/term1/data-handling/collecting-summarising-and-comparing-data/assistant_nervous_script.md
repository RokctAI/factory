Hi, Mandy here — data handling looks like a forest of terms, but nearly every question in this topic falls to one humble move: put the numbers in order and count. No formulas, just a queue.

The queue rule first. Median? Order the values, count to the middle person. Quartiles? Cut each half of the queue in the middle again. IQR? Upper quartile minus lower quartile — the width of the middle half. If you remember nothing else on exam day, remember: order first, count second. The most common zero in this topic is a median taken from an unordered list.

The mean is the only one that calculates: add everything, divide by how many. And know its weakness like you know a friend's — one extreme value drags the mean, but the median will not budge. Outliers in the data? Say the median is safer, and say why. That sentence is marks.

Box plots are friendlier than they look. Five numbers — minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum — and the picture draws itself. Then one rule unlocks every question: each whisker and each half of the box holds exactly a quarter of the data. Q3 at 90? Then a quarter of the patients wait 90 minutes or more. Comparing two plots? Compare medians for the typical value, compare box widths for spread, and write your conclusion in context — patients, minutes, mornings — never just numbers.

And on collecting data: watch for the two poisons — leading questions that tell people what to answer, and samples that reach only convenient people. Name the flaw and its direction.

Order, count, quarters, context. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
