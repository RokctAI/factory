Hey, it's Mandy — maps in Grade 12 look like Grade 11 maps that went to gym: bigger scales, gradients, choosing your own scale. But every question still runs on the same three-step engine you already own: measure, multiply, tidy.

First habit, before the ruler moves: price one centimetre. The scale says 1 : 1 500 000 — breathe, convert once, every centimetre is 15 km. Now the whole map is a times table. Paper to ground, multiply: 33 cm becomes 495 km. Ground to paper, divide: 225 km becomes 15 cm. If your answer says a page distance is thousands of centimetres, you swapped the operations — the sense test catches it free of charge.

Making your own scale is one honest fraction: drawing over real, SAME units, tidied to 1 : something. Netball court drawing 12 cm, real court 3 000 cm — 1 : 250, done. Choosing a scale? Pick the round number that makes the plan as big as the space allows, then SHOW the check: 45 m pool complex at 1 : 100 is 45 cm — fits the 90 cm board. The checking line is where the mark lives.

Directions: compass words belong to the world — Knysna is east of Cape Town, and swapping towns reverses the word. Left and right belong to your body and turn around when you do. Orient the map first, answer second.

And gradient, the new star: climb over distance, written 1 : n. One hundred and twenty metres up over twelve hundred along is 1 : 10. The trap has one cure — say the ratio as a sentence: a metre up every three steps beats a metre up every ten. SMALLER second number, STEEPER path. Picture the 1 : 3 ladder standing almost upright.

Price the centimetre, one honest fraction, orient then answer, say the gradient aloud. Four habits, full marks. You've got this!
