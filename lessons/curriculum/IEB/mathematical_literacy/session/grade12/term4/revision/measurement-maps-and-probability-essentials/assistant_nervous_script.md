Hey, it's Mandy — if Measurement and Maps feel like a pile of formulas, breathe: the practical half of the subject is really five habits wearing different clothes.

Habit one: walk the unit ladder, never jump it. Map says 9 centimetres at 1 to 25 000? Multiply first — 225 000 centimetres — then divide by 100, then by 1 000: 2,25 kilometres. Two written divisions beat one guessed one every time.

Habit two: the clock runs on sixties. 3,3 hours is NOT 3 hours 30 — the decimal times 60 gives 18 minutes. Say times sixty out loud every time hours come out with a comma in them.

Habit three: radius before anything. The drum formula wants radius squared, and questions love handing you the diameter — halve it first, 1,2 across means 0,6 in the formula, and that halving is the first mark. Then cubic metres times 1 000 lands you in litres, where real questions live.

Habit four: plans are people-sized. 88 millimetres at 1 to 50 is 4,4 metres — and rooms run two to eight metres, so a 44-metre bedroom means a dropped zero, caught by you before the marker.

Habit five: probability is three dialects of one number — 1 over 8, 0,125, 12,5 percent — and the complement always tops up to one whole: 40 percent rain means 60 percent dry.

Ladder, sixties, radius, room-sized, dialects. Five habits, and the practical questions start paying you back. You've got this!
