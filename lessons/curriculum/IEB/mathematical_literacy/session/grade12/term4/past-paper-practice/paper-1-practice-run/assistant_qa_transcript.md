**Mandy:** Questions from the class before we close! First: follow-through keeps coming up. If a wrong number can still earn marks, what exactly does the marker check on the later lines?

**Tutor:** The marker checks the OPERATIONS, not the digits. Under follow-through the marking plan asks one question at every stage: did the candidate do the right thing to the number they had? Suppose the electricity bill's block two slipped and you wrote R730,00 instead of R720,00. The subtotal mark is now judged on whether you added YOUR three blocks correctly — 180 plus 730 plus 186 is R1 096,00, and that line pays. The VAT mark is judged on whether you multiplied YOUR subtotal by 1,15 — 1 096 times 1,15 is R1 260,40, and that pays too. One slip, one lost mark, and the chain keeps earning because every later operation was sound. Now see what breaks it. If you abandon the question, the unearned marks stay unearned — blank lines pay nothing under any principle. If you hide your working and show only a final wrong number, the marker cannot see any sound operations to credit. And if the slip makes the answer absurd — a household electricity bill of thirty thousand rand — the reasonableness habit should trigger a quick re-check, because follow-through protects marks but a caught error protects more. So the practical drill: write every stage on its own line, never erase working into invisibility, and when a number feels wrong, finish the chain first, then come back if there is space in your head. Marking in this subject is designed to pay method generously — your job is to leave the method visible.

**Mandy:** Next: in the interest questions, learners ask why the year-by-year route is safer than the formula. Isn't the formula faster?

**Tutor:** Faster, yes — safer only for candidates who never mistype. The year-by-year route wins on three grounds. First, marks are staged: the marking plan for the fixed deposit allocates credit for the multiplier 1,072 built correctly, for interest applied to the NEW balance, and for the final rounding — and the year-by-year layout displays each of those visibly. R9 500 becomes R10 184,00, becomes R10 917,25, becomes R11 703,29: three lines, three chances to bank part-marks even if the last multiplication slips. A single formula line that comes out wrong shows the marker almost nothing creditable. Second, the route IS the concept. Compound interest means interest calculated on a balance that already contains interest, and writing the growing balance three times proves you know that — which is exactly what the follow-up explanation question asks in words. Candidates who only know the formula often cannot write that sentence, and it carries its own marks. Third, the year-by-year route self-checks: each balance must be a little larger than the last by a plausible amount — around seven hundred rand at these rates — so a typo announces itself immediately, where a formula typo hides inside one big number. Use the formula when the term is long and the question allows it, absolutely. But when the term is three years and the marks are staged, three multiplications shown in full is the professional's route, not the beginner's.

**Mandy:** This one came from homework: the question said comment on the coach's claim, and a learner wrote a whole paragraph and got one mark out of three. What went missing?

**Tutor:** Numbers went missing — computed ones. Comment questions are marked on citations, not eloquence: the marking plan lists specific values it expects to see inside the argument, and each cited value with a correct inference is a mark. The claim was that the group did well because the mean is 59. A full three-mark comment makes three moves. Move one, test the mean's honesty: the median is also 59,0, sitting right beside the mean, so no outlier is distorting the centre — one mark, and notice it required quoting BOTH averages. Move two, bring in spread: Q1 is 49, meaning a quarter of the group scored below 49 — the single number 59 hides a struggling quarter, and that is the second mark, earned by a quartile the learner had already calculated two parts earlier. Move three, the verdict tied to evidence: the claim is fair at the centre but incomplete about the spread — third mark. The paragraph that scored one mark said the group was average, some did well and some did not — true, humane, and almost numberless; it earned only the loose gesture at spread. The repair habit is mechanical: before writing any comment, glance back at every value you computed in that question — mean, median, quartiles, range — and force at least two of them into your sentences with the word because attached. If your comment could stand without your calculations, it is not yet a full answer.

**Mandy:** Last one: what should the final read-through hunt for, specifically in a Finance and Data paper?

**Tutor:** Hunt in priority order, because the read-through must be quick. Priority one: form errors on money — every rand answer wants the rand sign, two decimals and the decimal comma, and a scan for naked numbers like 1 248,9 takes seconds while recovering real marks; while you scan, confirm nothing was rounded mid-chain, because a subtotal rounded early poisons every line after it. Priority two: direction errors on the two reversible operations — VAT and percentages. Check each VAT line against its label: exclusive prices should have been multiplied by 1,15, inclusive totals divided; and check each percentage sits on its correct base — mark-up on cost price, tax percentage on the excess above the bracket floor, not the whole income. These direction errors are silent: the arithmetic looks clean and only the label betrays them. Priority three: completeness of staged questions — any question left mid-chain, especially an advise or comment ending without a verdict sentence, is unfinished harvest; even one line with a number and a because can claim the reasoning mark. Priority four: the probability dialect — if the question demanded a percentage and your answer sits as a fraction, convert it. And leave nothing blank, ever: Question One's warm-up items are definitions and single steps, so an attempt is always possible. That order — form, direction, completeness, dialect — collects the most marks per second of checking, which at the end of a long paper is the only currency that matters.
