Hey, it's Mandy — the measurement practice paper is the practical one, and a full run can look scary until you notice the whole paper is built from chains you already know, just wearing overalls.

Question One offers thirty warm-up marks: 4 800 metres is 4,8 kilometres, 0,25 cubic metres is 250 litres, thin double lines on the plan are a window. Take them with the units written on every line, because in this paper a right number in a wrong unit is a lost mark.

The trip question is the sixties trap: 3,1 hours is NOT 3 hours 10 minutes. Decimal times 60 — six minutes. Say times sixty every single time the hours come out with a comma.

The painting question is a story, not a formula: how much wall, minus the doors and windows, times the coats, divided by the tin's promise, rounded UP to whole tins, then times the price. Six questions a real painter would ask, in order — re-enter the story wherever you blank.

The drum question hides its mark in the first line: if they give the diameter, HALVE it before the formula, because feeding in the diameter quadruples the answer. Then cubic metres times 1 000 lands in litres.

And when money sneaks in — fuel, tiles — smile: measurement becomes a quantity, the quantity meets a price, and each exchange is a mark. Round up to whole boxes BEFORE multiplying by the price.

Units on every line, times sixty, the painter's story, halve the diameter, round up then price. Five habits and the practice run turns into rehearsal. You've got this!
