Hi, Mandy here — building plans look like an architect's secret code, and plenty of people freeze at the first pair of double lines. Good news: the code has about ten words, and the maths underneath is the same scale arithmetic you already own from the mapwork block.

Start with the friendliest conversion in the whole subject: at 1 : 100, every centimetre on the plan is a metre of real building. Ruler says the lounge is 50 by 40 millimetres? That's 5 by 4 metres. Then run the sanity check: rooms are people-sized, two to eight metres. If your bathroom comes out at 30 metres, the builders didn't lose their minds — a zero slipped, and you can catch it yourself before anyone else does.

The symbols: fat double lines are walls, the quarter-moon is the door and its swing, thin lines in a wall are a window, WC is the toilet. Ten words, learned over one cup of tea.

Elevations are just the building seen by a neighbour, one wall at a time, named for the direction the wall FACES. For the matching question, don't trust left and right — they flip the moment you face the wall, like the driver of an oncoming taxi appearing on your left. Anchor to the compass instead: east never flips.

And the shopping questions are all one law: shops sell whole things. Fourteen screws, packets of four — four packets. Five hundred and fifty tiles, boxes of twenty — twenty-eight boxes. Nine comma six litres — ten. Divide, then round UP. And packing: count whole boxes along each side — five along, five across, two layers is fifty — and let volume only check you.

Centimetre-is-a-metre, ten symbols, compass not left-right, round up, count whole boxes. Five habits and the plan reads itself. Now go show the practice questions who's boss!
