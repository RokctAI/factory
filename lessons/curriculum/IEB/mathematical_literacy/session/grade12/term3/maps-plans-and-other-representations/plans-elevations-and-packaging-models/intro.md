Today we read a building off a page — and then pack its crates. An architect's floor plan speaks a symbol language of about ten words, and once you can speak it, plan millimetres turn into real metres at 1 : 100 and the flat elevations match themselves to the walls they describe. From there we step into three dimensions: exploded assembly diagrams, crates packed axis by axis, and the material estimates that tell a builder how many tiles and litres of paint to load — always rounded up to what shops actually sell. It is the map skill moved onto a building site: the same scale arithmetic, a smaller territory, and a buying decision at the end of every calculation.
