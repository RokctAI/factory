Today we tackle the rural settlement issue that carries the heaviest marks: people leaving the countryside. We begin with the engine of rural–urban migration — the push factors that shove and the pull factors that tug, acting on one person at one kitchen table. Then we follow the consequences: rural depopulation, the hollowed-out villages it leaves behind, and the strategies that try to break the spiral. We connect today's rural poverty to a century of unequal access to land and resources, and we finish with the three-legged land reform programme — tenure, redistribution and restitution — kept cleanly apart, the way full-mark answers keep them. Our running example follows the thinning villages of northern KwaZulu-Natal and the workers they send to Durban and Johannesburg.
