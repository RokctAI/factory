Today we take on the two great dramas of fluvial geomorphology: the river that recovers its youth, and the river that robs its neighbour. First we build rejuvenation from its foundations — base level, the graded profile, and the two families of causes — and then read its four signatures in the landscape: the migrating knickpoint, paired terraces, the valley-in-a-valley, and incised meanders. Then we investigate river capture: headward erosion as the weapon, the five-piece vocabulary of the capture diagram, and the aftermath for thief and victim alike. We close with what both processes mean for people and with the skills for identifying every clue on profiles, cross-sections and topographic maps.
