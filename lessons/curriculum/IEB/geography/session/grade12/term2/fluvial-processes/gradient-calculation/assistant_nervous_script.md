Hey, it's Mandy — I know map calculations can make your stomach drop, but gradient is honestly the friendliest one in the whole set, because it is the same four lines every single time.

Hold the clinic picture: the staircase and the wheelchair ramp reach the same door — the only difference is how far you walk while climbing. That is all gradient measures: for every one metre up, how many metres along?

Then the routine. Get the up number: high point minus low point — 1 350 minus 1 100 is 250 metres. Get the along number and force it into metres — 5 kilometres becomes 5 000, no exceptions. Then the four lines: formula, the numbers with their units showing, divide both by the top number, and the answer dressed as 1 : 20. Finish with one sentence of meaning — one metre up for every twenty along, properly steep — and a three-second check that 250 times 20 really is 5 000.

Two guards and you are bulletproof: both numbers in metres before you divide, and the 1 always belongs to the climb, never the walk. And remember, the bigger the number after the colon, the gentler the slope — the teaspoon in the jug.

That is the entire skill. It cannot surprise you; it can only repeat itself. Go straight to the practice questions and run the four lines until they feel boring — boring is what full marks feels like the night before. You've got this!
