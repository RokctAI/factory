Today we are mastering the average gradient calculation — the piece of map work that turns the look of a slope into a number that decides where roads, railways and pipelines can go. We will build the skill from the definition and the interpretation rule that decides the follow-up marks, through harvesting the vertical interval and horizontal equivalent off a topographic sheet, into the line-by-line written layout that collects full credit, and out to the verification checks and the classic traps. One worked problem carries the whole session: the gradient between trig beacon 89 at 1 350 metres and a spot height of 1 100 metres, 5 kilometres apart on a 1:50 000 map. Once the method is automatic, few marks in your final exams come faster.
