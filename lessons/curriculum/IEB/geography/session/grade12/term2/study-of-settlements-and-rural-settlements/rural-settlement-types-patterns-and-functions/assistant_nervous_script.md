Hey, it's Mandy — settlement geography looks like a pile of definitions, but tonight it folds into three small tools you already own.

Tool one: the two conversations. Site is the conversation about the plot itself — slope, drainage, water, winter sun. Situation is the conversation about distances — school, clinic, taxi rank, town. Under the floor versus out the window. If a question says site, fence yourself onto the ground; if it says situation, describe everything around it.

Tool two: the beans. Fling beans across the floor — dispersed, one homestead per big private farm. Pour them into one bowl — nucleated, homes together on communal land with the fields around the outside. Scattered means private; one bowl means shared. That ownership link is the reason-mark, every time.

Tool three: the magnet question — what is the village organised around? A kraal in the middle makes a round village. A road or river out front makes a linear village, beads on a string. A junction makes a T or an X, because paused traffic is trade. Then let the land name the function: mealies for crops, kraals for stock, straight pine rows for forestry, rest camps for conservation.

Two conversations, a handful of beans, one magnet question. That is the entire toolkit, and the practice questions are where it becomes automatic — go do them right now while the pictures are sharp. You've got this!
