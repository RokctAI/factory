Picture one: the funnel. Every river drains a funnel of land, and whatever happens anywhere in the funnel ends up in the river. That one sentence unlocks the entire topic — why catchments matter, why nobody manages them properly, and why every fix works upstream rather than at the tap.

Picture two: the fish tank. Over-feed it and it blooms green, goes dark, dies, rots, and suffocates its fish. That is eutrophication in six beats — feed, bloom, dark, die, rot, suffocate — and the killer is the oxygen crash, so make sure the word oxygen lands in your answer. South Africa's green tank is Roodeplaat Dam.

Picture three: the spades. Working for Wetlands — crews plugging drains, stopping gullies with gabions and replanting, so the catchment's sponges work again and rural communities get jobs and training. Name, problem, method, and the two dividends: five sentences, full case study.
