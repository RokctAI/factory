Today we cover the urban half of settlement geography in four moves. We begin with the birth of towns — surplus plus specialisation plus exchange — and the industrial revolution that turned an urban trickle into a majority. We sharpen the vocabulary of urban growth: urbanisation against urban growth, rate against level, expansion against sprawl, and counter-urbanisation. We classify towns by the work they do — central places, break-of-bulk points, junction, gateway and gap towns, and the specialised towns — with South African examples for each. And we finish inside the city itself: the urban hierarchy's see-saw rule and a journey through the land-use zones, from the towering CBD through the transition zone and the residential gradient to the industrial belts and the decentralised nodes.
