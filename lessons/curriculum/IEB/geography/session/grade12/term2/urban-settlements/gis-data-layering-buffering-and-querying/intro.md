Today we turn Geographical Information Systems from a mystery into a vocabulary. We define a GIS by its five verbs — capture, store, manipulate, analyse, display — and separate the two kinds of data it holds: spatial, the drawing, and attribute, the label. We stack information into layers like transparency sheets, then put the three decision tools to work: buffering, which draws protective zones around features; querying, which fetches every feature matching a condition; and the data integration that must happen first. We close with resolution, pixels and the scale manipulation that sharpens an unclear image. One running example carries it all: a municipal planner siting a new clinic in a growing coastal town, by evidence instead of argument.
