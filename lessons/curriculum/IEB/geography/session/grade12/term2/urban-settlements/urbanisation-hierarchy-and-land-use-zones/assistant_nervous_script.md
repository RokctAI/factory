Hi, Mandy here — urban settlement is a big chapter, but tonight it folds into three pictures and a taxi.

Picture one: the recipe. Extra food plus divided jobs plus a marketplace equals a town — and factories later turned towns into cities. Keep the twin pairs clean: urbanisation is the percentage, urban growth is the number; level is the snapshot, rate is the speed. The parked car is the developed world; the climbing car is the developing world.

Picture two: the shopping trip. Bread at the spaza, shoes in town, the specialist in the city — ordinary things are near and everywhere, special things are far and few. That is the whole urban hierarchy. And the towns' job descriptions: the shopkeeper central place, cargo changing vehicles at Richards Bay, routes meeting at De Aar, the border door at Komatipoort, the mountain doorway at Ceres, and the one-trick towns — Welkom mines, Secunda makes fuel, Stellenbosch teaches.

Picture three: the taxi ride. Towers on the priciest, most reachable land — the CBD. Then the scruffy changing ring — transition. Then housing, with apartheid's rearranged seats: townships far out behind buffers and railways. Factory belts along the rail lines, and finally the giant mall node that ate the CBD's lunch.

Ride that taxi once in your head, then open the practice questions — every described zone will announce itself before the sentence ends. You know this city; you live in it. You've got this!
