Hi, Mandy here — GIS scares people because it sounds like computer science, but you already use every one of these tools on your phone, so tonight is just naming things you can do.

The system is a burger: rivers, roads and property lines on separate layers, made separately, stacked in perfect alignment. On every layer live two kinds of information — the drawing, which is spatial, and the label, which is attribute. Where versus what. If you could erase it without moving anything, it was an attribute.

The tools are three. The chalk circle: a buffer draws a measured edge around trouble — the flooding stream, the power line, the wetland — so the inside is treated differently. The search bar: a query fetches every plot matching your conditions, exactly like typing a song into a music app. And integration is loading the library first — nothing can be searched until all the data sits in one system on one grid.

Then the pocket camera: zoom too far into a photo and it turns to blocks — few pixels, low resolution, blurry image. Asked to sharpen an unclear orthophoto, give the answer your thumbs already know: enlarge the scale, zoom in.

Six definitions, one story: layers, where plus what, vector and raster, buffers, queries, integration. Say the story once out loud, then go straight into the practice questions — these are the calmest marks in the whole subject, and they are already yours. You've got this!
