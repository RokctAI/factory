Today we take apart the mid-latitude cyclone — the great winter storm of the South African south coast, known equally as a frontal depression, extra-tropical cyclone or temperate cyclone. We will establish where and why these systems are born on the polar front, follow the three-stage life cycle from the first ripple on the front to the final occluded spiral, walk through the weather a cold front drags across a coastal town scene by scene, and finish with the synoptic-chart and satellite-image skills that let you diagnose any system you are shown in your final exams. Hold one governing picture throughout: two air masses of different density meeting along a boundary south of the country, refusing to mix, and the fight between them winding itself up into a spinning storm.
