Today we shrink the weather down to a single valley — the smallest climate system in the syllabus and one of the most reasonable, because everything follows from two questions: which slope faces the sun, and which way does the air drain at night? We will work through slope aspect and the warm and cool walls it creates in a Southern Hemisphere valley, the daily breathing of anabatic and katabatic winds, the night-time inversion with its frost pockets and radiation fog and the warm thermal belt floating above them, and finally the human layer — why the orchards of a valley like the Langkloof stripe the mid-slope, why the old farmsteads sit just above them, and why housing pushed onto the valley floor pays for that decision every winter morning.
