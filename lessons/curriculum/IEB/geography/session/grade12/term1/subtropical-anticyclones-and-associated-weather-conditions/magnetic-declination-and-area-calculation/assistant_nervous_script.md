Hi, Mandy here — relax your shoulders. These two calculations look technical, but they are the friendliest marks in mapwork, because they never change shape. Same recipe, different numbers, every time.

Declination is a clock problem wearing a compass costume. The needle tells a small lie that grows a few minutes each year. Count the years since the margin note's date, multiply by the yearly change, add it on, and if the minutes hit sixty or more, roll them over into a degree — exactly like seventy-five minutes past ten becoming quarter past eleven. Finish with the magic words: west of true north. That phrase alone is worth a mark.

Area is tiling a floor. Un-shrink the length, un-shrink the breadth, then multiply. Never multiply the paper centimetres first — the shrinking happened to each side separately, so the un-shrinking must too. And check your answer against a rugby field: about seven thousand square metres. If your block is fifteen fields, believable. If it is half a classroom or a whole province, hunt the slip.
