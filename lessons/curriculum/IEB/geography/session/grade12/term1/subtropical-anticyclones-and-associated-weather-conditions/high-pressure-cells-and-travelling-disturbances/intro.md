Today we study the quiet machinery behind South Africa's everyday weather: the three subtropical anticyclones — the South Atlantic High, the South Indian High and the Kalahari High — and the travelling disturbances that break their grip: the moisture front with its line thunderstorms, the coastal low, and the berg wind. We will name and place each high, follow the inversion ceiling that switches the interior between bone-dry winters and stormy summers, work through the descending-air arithmetic that turns a winter's day on the coast into a fire alert, and finish with the synoptic-chart signatures that let you identify every one of these systems in your final exams. Anchor a single fact throughout: an anticyclone is sinking, spreading air — and sinking air is warm, dry, stable and cloudless.
