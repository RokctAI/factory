Hey, it's Mandy — last big topic of the term, and if it looks like a wall of place names and acronyms, let me shrink it into a story you can tell in one minute.

Four engines pull the whole country's train: Gauteng up on the highveld, Durban–Pinetown, Port Elizabeth–Uitenhage, and the Cape around Table Bay. One sentence explains the whole map: minerals built the inland engine, harbours built the other three.

You only need two engines in detail, and each has one unforgettable hook. Gauteng is the giant that shouldn't exist — no port, no big river, water piped in from Lesotho — but gold built the railways, the skills and the market, and the engine outlived its gold. PE–Uitenhage is the car town — port, rail, chosen by Volkswagen and Isuzu — brilliant at one thing and fragile for the very same reason.

Then the fix for the crowded map, two tools. An SDI is a line: upgrade a whole corridor, like the Maputo route to the sea. An IDZ is a point: a ready-made industrial estate at a port, like Coega. Line and point. Partly working, not yet map-changing — that's the honest verdict.

And every case-study question is just four pockets: why there, what's made, what helps and hurts, who gains and who pays.
