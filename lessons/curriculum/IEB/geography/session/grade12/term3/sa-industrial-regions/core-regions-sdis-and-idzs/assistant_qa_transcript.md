**Mandy:** These two case studies feel like mountains of facts. Is there one skeleton I can learn once and use for both Gauteng and PE–Uitenhage?

**Tutor:** One skeleton, four rows — learn it once, fill it twice. Row one, location factors: WHY the region stands where it does. Gauteng: gold, the coal next door, water engineered in from the Vaal and Lesotho, the converging railways, the accumulated skills, the richest market on the continent. PE–Uitenhage: the natural harbour on Algoa Bay, rail into the Karoo's wool country, and the decisive choice of the global car makers to build there. Row two, main activities: WHAT is made. Gauteng: steel at Vanderbijlpark, chemicals at Sasolburg, vehicles at Rosslyn, machinery, with finance stacked on top. PE–Uitenhage: assembly at Volkswagen Kariega and Isuzu, the component belt with its catalytic converters, tyres, port logistics. Row three, favouring and hindering: Gauteng runs on market, skills and agglomeration, and strains against no port, borrowed water, load-shedding and congestion; PE–Uitenhage runs on automotive expertise, export programmes and two harbours, and strains under one-industry dependence, drought and provincial poverty. Row four, impacts: economic muscle beside social division — Sandton against Alexandra; the plant fence against the township. Practise by drawing the empty grid and filling all eight cells from memory in ten minutes. Any question on these regions is only asking you to read out cells you have already filled.

**Mandy:** My essays keep SAYING Gauteng is dominant, but the question asks me to EXPLAIN dominance in a place with no port and no river. How do I turn the contradiction into an answer?

**Tutor:** The contradiction is the answer — the region is studied precisely because it breaks the location rules, and your job is to name the mechanism that overrides them. Start with the historical trigger: gold on the Witwatersrand trumped every disadvantage, because a gold reef cannot be relocated — so capital, labour, railways and coal-fired power all travelled uphill to IT, port or no port. Then name the sustaining mechanism properly: agglomeration and cumulative causation. Once the mines had gathered railways, power stations, skills and a vast wage-earning population, every new factory found its suppliers, workers, customers and banks already waiting — so each arrival made the region more attractive to the next, and growth manufactured its own growth. Meanwhile the missing pieces were engineered around: the Vaal Dam and the Lesotho Highlands scheme piped in the water, and the rail network — now joined by OR Tambo's air freight and the shortcut of the Maputo Corridor — stood in for the missing harbour. Close on the sentence that carries the idea: the gold is nearly gone, but the region it assembled remains — industrial regions outlive their reasons. Then add the top-level footnote: the handicaps were managed, never abolished — the overland haul, the borrowed water, the load-shedding are standing costs, so the dominance is powerful but not unconditional.

**Mandy:** SDIs and IDZs keep merging into one blur of letters. What is the cleanest way to hold them apart and keep the examples straight?

**Tutor:** Hold them apart by their geometry: an SDI is a LINE, an IDZ is a POINT. The Spatial Development Initiative takes an entire route — hundreds of kilometres of road and rail between an inland economy and a harbour — and upgrades the whole artery so farms, factories and tourism sprout along its length. Flagship: the Maputo Development Corridor from Gauteng through Mpumalanga to Maputo's port, with the Lubombo and Wild Coast SDIs as the supporting examples built on tourism and agriculture. The Industrial Development Zone takes one fenced site at one port or airport and furnishes it completely: serviced land, bulk infrastructure, tax incentives, duty-free inputs for exporters, one-stop approvals. Learn the zones as a coastal tour: Richards Bay for minerals and metals; Dube TradePort at King Shaka airport for cargo that flies; East London serving the Mercedes-Benz plant; Coega beside the deep-water port of Ngqura, the largest; and Saldanha Bay for oil, gas and marine engineering. Since the SEZ legislation, IDZs sit inside the wider Special Economic Zones family. Anchors for the memory: line versus point, Maputo versus Coega. And keep one linking sentence ready, because linking earns credit: both instruments attack the same disease — over-concentration in the four core regions — the corridor treating it along a route, the zone treating it at a gate.

**Mandy:** If a question asks whether these policies have actually SUCCEEDED, what does a top-level conclusion look like?

**Tutor:** It looks like a verdict measured against the policy's own goal — spreading industry beyond the four regions — argued with evidence from both sides and closed without cheerleading or despair. Evidence for: Coega is no longer an empty fence — vehicle assembly, agro-processing and energy projects employ thousands, anchored by Ngqura's deep water; East London's zone genuinely serves the Mercedes-Benz export plant; Dube TradePort has carved out an air-freight niche; and the Maputo Corridor moves real freight while giving the port-less inland giant its shorter second route to the sea. Evidence against: the cost per job in the zones runs high; several stand partly filled years after opening; incentives at the gate cannot cancel the national problems — load-shedding, queuing ports, decaying rail — that top every investor's checklist; and the decisive test, the map itself, still shows the four core regions holding the overwhelming share of manufacturing. So conclude in two measured sentences: the policies have built genuine islands of new industry and useful corridors, proving the approach works at specific points; but they have shifted growth at the margin rather than redrawn the map, because site-level sweeteners cannot outweigh economy-wide constraints. And if recommendations are invited, close the loop logically: the zones will only deliver in full once the national basics — power, ports, rail — are repaired, since no fully furnished flat finds tenants in a building where the lifts are broken.
