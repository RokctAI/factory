Hey, it's Mandy — if the industry types are melting into one grey blur, hang on, because this whole topic runs on a single everyday question you already know.

What's more of a pain to move — what the business uses, or what it sells? That's the chip-shop test. Fish spoils and shrinks into a little can, so the cannery goes to the bay. Bread goes stale and cooldrink is heavy glass, so bakeries and bottlers go to the customers. Software travels down a cable, so those firms follow the skilled people — footloose. The product every town buys fresh daily — bakeries, brickworks — is ubiquitous. And plants camped where ships meet trucks, like the car factories at East London's harbour, are bridge industries. Five types, one question.

Location factors are just choosing an address: ingredients, workers, water, power, transport, customers, government sweeteners, competition. For any real factory only two or three genuinely matter — a smelter cares about electricity, a clothing factory cares about workers — and naming which ones matter is the entire skill.

The hindering factors you already know from the news: power cuts, limping trains, jammed ports, scarce skills, strikes, cheap imports — plus the two structural ones, over-concentration and pollution.
