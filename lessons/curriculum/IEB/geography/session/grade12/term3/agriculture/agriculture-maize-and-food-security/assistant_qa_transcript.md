**Mandy:** For the maize case study, how much detail is enough? I never know whether to quote the rainfall figures or just write "adequate rain".

**Tutor:** Quote the figures — specificity is the entire difference between a case-study answer and a general answer, because a named case study exists so that detail can be demanded. Build yourself a fact kit of about eight items and drill it until it recites itself: the Maize Triangle across the Free State, North West and the Mpumalanga highveld, with the Free State the biggest producer; summer rainfall of roughly five hundred to eight hundred millimetres in the growing season; long, hot, frost-free summers; deep fertile soils on gently rolling land built for machinery; the silo-rail-mill support web; white maize as the human staple and yellow as the feed base; drought as the chief hindering factor, trailed by hail, frost and rising input costs; and export to neighbouring countries in surplus years. Then apply the deployment rule: match the fact to the question's verb. "Name the area" wants the triangle and its provinces. "Account for the location" wants the favouring factors — and each earns its mark only when tied to the crop's needs: not "summer rainfall" bare, but "the rain arrives in the growing season, watering the plant exactly when it grows". "Discuss the economic contribution" wants the double role, the processing chain and the exports. A figure like five hundred to eight hundred millimetres, placed correctly, tells the marker in three words that you studied the actual case and not just the general idea of maize.

**Mandy:** Compare questions on the two farming systems keep coming back half-marked because I write two separate lists. What is the repair?

**Tutor:** Compare inside each sentence, not between paragraphs — and the tool is the word "whereas". A list answer says: commercial farms are large, mechanised and market-driven; then, separately, small farms are small and family-run. That forces the marker to assemble the comparison, and marking instructions tell them not to. A comparison answer runs one criterion per sentence with both systems inside it: commercial farms span thousands of hectares, WHEREAS small-scale plots are often under five; commercial farming runs on tractors, irrigation and scientific inputs, WHEREAS small-scale farming runs on hand tools and family labour; commercial output feeds national and export markets, WHEREAS small-scale output mostly feeds the household; commercial land is held under title deed and can secure credit, WHEREAS communal tenure blocks the loan. Four or five whereas-sentences, each anchored to a named criterion — size, capital, labour, market, tenure — and the comparison marks are complete. When the verb rises from compare to evaluate, add the judgement layer: which system achieves which goal — national supply and exports on one side, livelihoods and rural stability on the other — and close with the bridge policy that helps emerging farmers across. The content you already own; the structure is the repair.

**Mandy:** If a source shows hungry households in a settlement right beside a stocked supermarket, what analytical point is the question fishing for?

**Tutor:** It is fishing for the access argument, and the scene is engineered to make it vivid. A full supermarket beside hungry homes is the South African paradox compressed into one image: availability without affordability. Walk the definition through the scene clause by clause. Is food physically present? Yes — metres away, shelves full — so physical access exists and national production is not the failure. Can the households buy enough? No — unemployment and poverty break ECONOMIC access, and the definition demands both kinds. So the settlement is food insecure amid abundance, proving the binding constraint is income, not agriculture. Then work the time clause: a grant-dependent household may eat properly in the first week of the month and poorly in the last — failing "at all times". And the nutrition clause: a tight budget buys cheap calories, heavy on starch and light on protein and vegetables, so even a fed household can fail the "nutritious" test — hidden hunger. Finish with strategies matched to the diagnosis: because the failure is access, the front-line fixes are income-side — grants, school feeding, food gardens, employment — before any production-side measure. That clause-by-clause walk, closed with matched strategies, is exactly the structure a long analytical paragraph is designed to reward.

**Mandy:** Last one: essays on how climate change threatens food security keep sliding into doom-writing. How do I keep mine geographical?

**Tutor:** Chain every claim to a place, a mechanism and a consequence — three links per sentence family, no free-floating alarm. Open with the exposure: South Africa is a dry country — only around twelve per cent of the land is arable and most cropping is rain-fed, not irrigated — so any shift in rainfall strikes a system with no slack. Then run mechanism chains. Chain one: hotter summers and more erratic rainfall over the interior plateau threaten the Maize Triangle — heat stress at pollination cuts yields, and failed thunderstorm seasons deliver drought years; because maize is both the staple and the feed base, a poor harvest lifts the price of pap AND of chicken and eggs, and poor households, who spend the biggest share of income on food, take the hit first. Chain two: the winter-rainfall Western Cape — engine of the fruit and wine export industries — faces drying trends, with the Cape Town water crisis as the warning shot, threatening export earnings and seasonal farm jobs. Chain three if space allows: shrinking grazing forces stock cuts across the Karoo and communal rangelands, and the uninsured small farmer absorbs that loss whole. Then the response paragraph, each measure answering a chain you described: drought-tolerant cultivars, conservation farming, efficient irrigation, early-warning systems, diversified rural incomes. Place, mechanism, consequence, response — that frame converts alarm into analysis, and analysis is where the marks live.
