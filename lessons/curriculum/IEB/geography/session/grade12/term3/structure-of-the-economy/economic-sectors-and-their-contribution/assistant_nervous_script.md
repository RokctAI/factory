Hi, Mandy here — if "economic sectors" sounds like something for accountants, breathe. This whole term stands on one idea you already use every time you buy a loaf of bread.

Four kinds of work. Grow it — farms, fishing boats, mines pulling raw stuff out of nature. Make it — mills, bakeries, factories, builders turning raw stuff into products. Move-and-sell it — shops, taxis, banks, teachers, all the services. Think it up — research and software. Every single job in South Africa is one of those four verbs. Try it on your own day: the person who drove your taxi, the woman at the tuck shop, your geography teacher. See? You already classify sectors without noticing.

The numbers are two surprises, nothing more. Surprise one: services take about two thirds of the country's money while farming takes about three per cent — yet farming feeds all of us, so a small share never means unimportant. Surprise two: money and jobs disagree — farming's jobs share is roughly double its money share because it uses many hands at modest pay, and banks are the exact opposite.

And data questions are a routine, not a trap: read the title and units, name the biggest and smallest slices, say what changed, give one reason from the lesson. Four steps, every single time.

Tonight, do one small thing: take ten jobs you saw today and sort them into the four verbs. That is the foundation of the whole term, laid before supper. Now go tackle the practice questions — you are more ready than you feel.
