Today we are aiming the global circulation at one continent and reading Africa's weather off four moving parts: convergence and subsidence as the master switch between rain and drought, the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone that tows the rainy season up and down the map on a yearly timetable, the Kalahari High whose seasonal breathing locks the plateau dry in winter and opens it to thunderstorms in summer, and the two ocean currents that make Richards Bay lush while Alexander Bay, at nearly the same latitude, stays desert. We close on the synoptic chart and the satellite image, where every one of these ideas appears as a symbol you can read in seconds. Hold on to the single question underneath it all — is the air rising or sinking — and the continent's climate map becomes something you can reconstruct from scratch.
