Hey, it's Bianca — Africa's weather can look like a crowd of separate facts: an ITCZ, three anticyclones, two currents, charts, satellites, El Niño. Breathe out. Every single fact answers to one two-word engine: rising rains, sinking shines.

Say the engine, then hang the continent on it. At the equator air converges and rises, so the Congo is wet all year. Near 30 degrees it comes back down, so the Sahara, Kalahari and Namib sit bone dry. Everything in between waits for the rising band — the ITCZ — to migrate over it: down to us in January, up to the Sahel in July. Count how many times it crosses you and you have your rainy seasons. That is most of the topic in four sentences.

South Africa itself is two pictures. Picture one: the Kalahari High is a gate on the escarpment — bolted below the rim in winter, so the plateau is dry; lifted above the rim in summer, so the storms pour in. Picture two: two pots on the stove — the warm Agulhas simmering under the east coast's air, the cold Benguela icing the west coast's. Gate plus pots answers nearly every explain-the-climate question there is.

For the chart, protect your marks with a fixed reading order: temperature top left, dew point under it, cloud in the circle, wind FROM the shaft, ten knots per feather. Same five reads, same order, every station, then say which pressure cell it sits under.

One engine, one migrating band, a gate and two pots, five reads. Now go and try the practice questions while the pictures are still warm. You've got this!
