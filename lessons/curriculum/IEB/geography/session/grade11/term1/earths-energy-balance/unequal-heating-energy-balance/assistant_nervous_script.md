Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic looks like the whole sky fell on your desk at once. Angles of incidence, parallelism, solstices, five kinds of heat transfer, a planetary budget. Breathe. Underneath it all there are only four moves, and you have met three of them in your own back yard.

Move one: the sun delivers the same beam everywhere, but the round earth spreads it. Hosepipe on the lawn — straight down soaks a small circle, tilted sprays a long weak stripe. That one picture unlocks nearly every latitude question you will ever be asked.

Move two: the earth leans 23,5 degrees and never straightens. Lean toward the sun, December; lean away, June. And when a question asks why seasons happen, the answer is always the tilt, never the distance — we are actually closest to the sun in January, in the middle of our summer. Knowing that saves a mark most people lose.

Move three: heat travels five ways, and your kitchen shows all of them. Oven-door warmth across the gap is radiation. The hot teaspoon in the tea is conduction. The porridge pot rolling over is convection. The breeze carrying the cooking smell down the passage is advection. Washing drying on the line is latent heat. Learn the kitchen and the definitions come free.

The habit that protects your marks: every number in this topic needs its unit — 23,5 degrees, 1 367 watts per square metre, 6,5 degrees per kilometre, 2,5 megajoules per kilogram. And in the sun-angle calculation, always ask first whether your latitude and the overhead sun are on the same side of the equator, because that decides whether you subtract or add.

Four moves, one hosepipe, one kitchen. Now go and try the practice questions — they will feel much friendlier than they looked ten minutes ago. You've got this!
