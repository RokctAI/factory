Hey, it's Bianca — droughts and desertification can feel like the heaviest topic in the term: four stages, five causes, effects spilling everywhere, and a stack of programmes with official names. Breathe. The whole thing is two stories you already understand.

Story one is the prepaid bundle. Rain is the monthly bundle a place loads; drought is the bundle arriving far below what THAT account was built on — always measured against the LOCAL normal, and that one word is worth a mark by itself. Then the failure runs downhill in a fixed order: sky, soil, storage, society — meteorological, agricultural, hydrological, socio-economic. Follow the water down and the order rebuilds itself in the exam; you never memorise it cold.

Story two is the goalmouth. Too many boots, too many hooves, too many axes shave the cover off fragile ground; the bared soil bakes hard, rain sheets off and rips open dongas, wind lifts the dust, and less grass returns each round. That self-feeding spiral is desertification — made by people, exposed by drought, and separate from drought even though each feeds the other. Write the two definitions side by side once tonight and they will never blur again.

For effects and fixes, carry three names like tools: Nelson Mandela Bay for a city fighting toward fifty litres a day, Working for Water for clearing thirsty alien trees while creating jobs, and the Great Green Wall for the Sahel's village-by-village recovery. Each name plus one number or detail is a top answer.

Two stories, one downhill order, three named tools. That is the entire topic — now go and prove it to yourself on the practice questions. You've got this!
