Hey, it's Bianca — if the development-and-resources half of the year feels like a wall of terms the night before you need it, breathe. It folds into four pictures and one definition, and all five fit in one pocket.

Picture one, the scoreboard: growth is total marks; development is whether the learners actually pass. Fix the score three ways — per capita, then the spread with Gini, ours among the world's highest, then follow the money into lives with the HDI's three dimensions: health, knowledge, standard of living. Core-periphery explains the map; Rostow gives the stages — and always quote his critics.

Picture two, the rigged shop: sell raw, buy finished, and your trolley buys less every year — the sliding terms of trade. The escape is beneficiation: sell the jewellery, not the gold. At the charity counter, argue both ways and land on: bakeries beat bread.

Picture three, the braai fire: coal burns steady baseload on the Highveld seams, but the bills — carbon, air, water, acid — keep arriving. The two free fuels, Northern Cape sun and Cape coast wind, are now cheaper than new coal; they just take days off, so the grid needs batteries, water batteries like Ingula, a steady ember, thicker wires — and a JUST transition for the coal towns.

Picture four, the bank account: topsoil fills by teaspoons and empties by buckets — splash, sheet, rill, donga — so cover the ground, plough the contours, rest the veld, pack the gabions.

The definition, word for word: meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own. Live off the interest, never the capital. Now go walk the circle through the practice questions — you carry the whole year in one pocket!
