Hey, it's Bianca — if a whole year of cells, ridges and contour lines feels impossible to hold at once, breathe. The session runs on two machines and one walk, and you already own all three.

Machine one, the sky: unequal heating. Vertical rays at the equator, slanted at the poles — surplus and deficit. Air rises at the equator, sinks at 30 degrees, and that sinking parks the deserts. Three cells, winds bent LEFT down here — trades from the south-east, westerlies from the south-west. Slide the machine with the sun: summer drags the ITCZ south for Highveld thunderstorms; winter parks the Kalahari High's dry lid on the plateau and sends the fronts to the Cape. Drought is rain below what the region expects — four rungs, meteorological to socio-economic, El Niño the usual suspect. Desertification is different: long-term, human-made land damage that drought exposes.

Machine two, the rock: flat strata bake the shrinking cake — plateau, mesa, butte, cone under a dolerite lid. Tilted strata lean like books — cuesta, homoclinal, hogsback; steeper tilt, more equal slopes. Massive granite is the loaf — peeling domes and stacked tors.

The walk: crest, cliff, talus, pediment — then the hill moves: creep, flows, slides and slumps, rockfall, and the trigger is nearly always water or us. On the map, crowded rings mean cliffs, wide rings mean pediment, asymmetry means cuesta, and gradient is just rise over run — one hundred over two thousand is one in twenty.

Two machines, one walk. Now go run the practice questions — you know this ground because you have stood on it all year!
