Hey, it's Bianca — PV, CSP, cube laws, auction programmes: the abbreviations are stacking up again. Breathe. This entire session is one idea, three machines and three fumbles, and it fits in one hand.

The one idea: the fuel is FREE. Sunshine and wind arrive every morning without a mine or an import bill — you only pay for the catching machine. And the catch inside the idea: the free fuel keeps its own diary — sun off at dusk, wind on leave without notice. Five words hold the whole debate: free but moody, costly but steady.

The three machines: the PV panel — light in, current out, no moving parts, king of the farms around De Aar and of load-shedding rooftops. The CSP plant — a field of mirrors cooking molten salt, and the salt is a building-sized thermos: Bokpoort holds about nine hours of heat, so lunchtime sun serves supper. The turbine — a skyscraper pinwheel ruled by the cube law: double the wind, EIGHT times the power; that is why Gibson Bay, Kouga, Gouda and Sere stand where the coast blows hardest.

The three fumbles at the baton pass: the extension cord — the best sun and wind live far from Gauteng and the lines are full; the night shift — covered by the mix: salt, batteries, pumped storage, night wind; and the coal towns — around ninety thousand jobs that need a JUST transition: retraining, pensions, new factories, real money.

Verdict, both columns: environment, sun and wind win by a distance; economy, cheaper power and rural investment against real pain in coal country. Direction settled, speed argued. Now go show the practice questions who owns this topic!
