Hey, it's Bianca — the final topic of the year looks like a stack of policy documents: roadmaps, taxes, audits, partnerships. Exhale. This is secretly the kindest topic in the term, because it is already organised the way a question wants it: one bucket, three spanners, one wave.

The bucket: South Africa's energy problem is a leaking bucket — plenty poured in at the top, streams escaping through draughty rooms, filament bulbs, naked geysers and tired motors. Plugging holes beats building dams: a new station costs hundreds of billions and takes a decade; efficiency costs a fraction and works this season. Carry the champion sentence everywhere: the unit of electricity you never use is the cheapest and cleanest unit there is.

The three spanners: government writes the rules — the IRP roadmap, renewable auctions, the carbon tax, building rules, appliance labels, the just-transition billions. Business turns the big bolts — audits that find paying leaks, rooftops full of panels, wheeling, and the border question about smoke inside products; no greenwashing. Individuals hold the smallest spanner, but sixty million of them.

The wave: the evening peak is just millions of private choices landing at six o'clock. Run the plays by size — geyser first, around forty per cent of the bill: thermostat down, blanket, timer, solar if possible; then LEDs, lids on pots, sealed draughts, standby off at the wall, shared lifts. Honest line for full credit: one home cannot decarbonise a grid, but the peak is the sum of homes, and sums move.

Bucket, spanners, wave — now go take the practice questions. You know this better than you think!
