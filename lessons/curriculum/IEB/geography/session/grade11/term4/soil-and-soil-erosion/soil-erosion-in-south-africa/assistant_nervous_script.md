Hey, it's Bianca — soil erosion looks like a hundred loose facts: splash, sheet, rill, gully, gabions, carrying capacity, resolutions. Slow down. It is one story with three scenes, and you already know the plot.

Scene one, the bank account: soil fills at a teaspoon a year — centuries for a few centimetres — and one storm on bare ground withdraws by the bucket. That unfair exchange rate is why soil counts as non-renewable, and why the stakes are national: only about one plate in ten of South Africa is arable, and sixty million people eat from it.

Scene two, the plot of every donga: something fires the bodyguard — overgrazing, overcultivation, deforestation, trampling — and in our history the homeland map did the firing at scale, crowding too many hooves onto too little land, which is why the deepest dongas cut the former Transkei and Ciskei. Then the staircase runs by itself: splash, sheet, rill, gully. Many canyons started as a cattle path.

Scene three, the stitches: prevent by covering — contour ploughing, mulch blankets, rotation, carrying capacity, rested camps; control by slowing — gabion speed bumps that make floods drop their stolen soil, planted banks, patient fences. And the menders aim from orbit: infrared betrays bare ground, pixel size sets what you can see, and point, line and area layers stack into the map that says where to stitch first.

Account, plot, stitches. Three scenes, one story — now go tell it to the practice questions. You have got this!
