This session opens the resources theme with the resource everything else stands on: soil. We will see why a layer that takes centuries to build and an afternoon to lose must be treated as non-renewable, then sort the causes of erosion into human, animal and physical — reaching from apartheid's homeland map to today's overgrazed camps — read the evidence in dongas, brown rivers and silting dams, and weigh the effects on people and the environment. From there we turn to the fixes: contour ploughing, rotation and carrying capacity to prevent damage, gabions and re-vegetation to control it, and the GIS toolkit — resolutions, points, lines and areas — that tells the menders where to work first. In your final exams this topic rewards ordered sequences and named places, and this session hands you both.
