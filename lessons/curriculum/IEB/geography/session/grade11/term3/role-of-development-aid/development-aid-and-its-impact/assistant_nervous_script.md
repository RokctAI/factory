Hey, it's Bianca — the aid topic can feel like a moral argument with no right answer, and that is stressful before a test. Good news: the whole thing folds into three pictures and one closing sentence.

Picture one: the fish and the fishing lesson. The fish is humanitarian aid — fast, life-saving, but it only feeds today. The lesson is technical aid — spent once, working for thirty years. Timing decides which is right: in mid-flood you need the boat, not the lesson. And if free fish arrives forever, the local boats get beached — that is dependency, the exact word to write.

Picture two: the gift with strings. The neighbour drills your borehole but you must buy his pump and take his budget advice. String one is procurement tying, string two is policy conditionality — with structural adjustment in the eighties and nineties as the case where the cuts landed on clinics and classrooms. The upgrade is co-operation: your plan, their contribution, duties both ways.

Picture three: the scale. Triumphs on one pan — smallpox gone, our ARV programme, malaria beaten back, floods reached in days. Failures on the other — beached farmers, budget-eating debt, money cycling home, governments facing donors instead of voters.

Then the sentence that closes any essay: aid is scaffolding — essential while the building rises, never the house, designed from day one to come down.

Fish, strings, scale, scaffolding. That is the entire topic. Now go do the practice questions — you are carrying more than enough to score well.
