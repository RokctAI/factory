Hey, it's Bianca — if this topic looks like a bowl of letters right now, GDP, GNP, HDI, Gini, MEDC, LEDC, NIC, take a breath. Underneath all of it sit just three questions, asked in the same order every single time.

Question one: how much does the country produce? That is GDP if you follow the soil, GNP if you follow the passport. One pair of examples locks it in forever: BMW at Rosslyn is South Africa's GDP but Germany's GNP, and MTN in Nigeria is Nigeria's GDP but South Africa's GNP. Divide by the population and you have per capita — per person.

Question two: how is it shared? That is the Gini coefficient — 0 means perfectly equal, 1 means one person takes everything, and South Africa's 0,63 is the figure worth memorising, because it explains how a middle-income country can still hold so much poverty.

Question three: what are lives actually like? That is the HDI — health, education and income averaged into one score, with South Africa around 0,71. And the sharpest little truth-teller is infant mortality, because a baby only survives when the whole system works at once.

One habit protects your marks: argue development with indicators, never with feelings. Quote the numbers, show both faces of the country, and land on "a developing NIC with pockets of both."

How much, how shared, how lived. Hang everything on those three hooks, then go try the practice questions — you are more ready than you think.
