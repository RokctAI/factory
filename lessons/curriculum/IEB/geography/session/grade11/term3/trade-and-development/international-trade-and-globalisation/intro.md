Today we follow the money across borders. We define exports, imports and the balance of trade, then hit the distinction that decides development: primary products against manufactured goods, and why the value always settles where the processing happens. We watch commodity prices lurch on world markets, measure the swap rate called the terms of trade and see why it drifts against raw-material sellers, and sort the trading relationships — free trade, protectionism, blocs like SADC and the African Continental Free Trade Area, agreements like AGOA, and fair trade floors. Then globalisation, argued honestly in two columns of gains and costs, and finally the export-led ladder the Asian Tigers climbed from textiles to semiconductors — with every warning on its label, and South Africa's own climb assessed with its heavy backpack. By the end you can argue the world trading system from either side with evidence.
