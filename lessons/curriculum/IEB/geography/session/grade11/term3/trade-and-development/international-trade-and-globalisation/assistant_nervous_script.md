Hey, it's Bianca — trade topics look like economics homework wearing a geography badge, I know. But this whole lesson lives in three street-level pictures, and you already have all three.

Picture one: the spaza and the supermarket. Raw sellers earn thin; processors earn thick, because the value settles where the processing happens. South Africa works both ends — ore out spaza-style, cars from Kariega and Durban out supermarket-style. One line holds the whole strategy: stop selling only the beans, start selling the chocolate.

Picture two: the swap rate. One bag of oranges buys a phone this year; next season the same phone costs four bags. That is deteriorating terms of trade — and it drifts against raw sellers because anyone can grow oranges while patents and brands guard the phone's price. Then the team rules that push back: SADC, the African Continental Free Trade Area, AGOA's gate into America, fair trade floors — and the rich world's farm subsidies as the rule worth arguing about.

Picture three: the ladder. T-shirts, steel, ships, cars, semiconductors — the Tigers climbed it by banking every export dollar and buying schools with it. Just read the warnings: buyers needed at the top, bruising bottom rungs, imported shocks, deferred smog. South Africa climbs too, wearing a heavy backpack.

Spaza, swap, ladder. Ask which picture the question wants and start there. Now go at the practice questions — you have this topic in hand.
