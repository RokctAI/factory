Having learned to measure development, we now learn to explain it. This session covers the five aspects every development plan must address — economic, social, sustainable, appropriate-scale and spatial — including the Brundtland definition worth knowing word for word. Then the two great models. The core-periphery model explains the map: cores that attract like magnets through cumulative causation, peripheries drained by backwash, spread effects trickling outward, and the pattern repeating from the global scale down to a single metro. Rostow's stages of economic growth claim to explain the timeline, from traditional society through take-off to high mass consumption. Both models are tested twice — describe them accurately, then criticise them intelligently — and South Africa, where apartheid engineered core and periphery by law, is the sharpest testing ground for each. This is the session that turns your indicators into arguments.
