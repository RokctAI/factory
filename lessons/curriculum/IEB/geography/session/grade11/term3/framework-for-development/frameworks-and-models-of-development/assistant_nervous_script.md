Hey, it's Bianca — two whole models in one lesson looks like a lot, I know. But you walked out of that session carrying exactly three things, and all three are pictures.

Picture one: the checklist with five stamps. Every development plan must tick the wallet, the people, tomorrow, the fit and the map — economic, social, sustainable, appropriate-scale, spatial. And inside stamp three lives the one sentence to know cold: meet the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Say it in the shower until it says itself.

Picture two: the magnet. The core pulls in the periphery's people and savings — that drain is backwash — while remittances and branch plants trickle outward as spread. Planning is plumbing: widen the spread pipes, narrow the drain. And remember the zoom trick — the same picture works globally, nationally with Gauteng, and inside one city.

Picture three: the aeroplane. Parked, ground crew, take-off at ten per cent investment, climb, cruise. Then the four criticisms that earn the real marks: one runway for everyone, tanks siphoned from colonies, planes held at the gate, and a shopping mall for a destination.

When a question lands, ask which picture it wants — checklist, magnet or aeroplane — and start drawing. The practice questions are waiting for you, and honestly, you are readier than you feel. Go show them.
