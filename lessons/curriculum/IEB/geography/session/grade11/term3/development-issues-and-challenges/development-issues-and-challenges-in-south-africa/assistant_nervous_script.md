Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic feels heavy: unemployment, inequality, pollution, history, all stacked in one lesson. Breathe. It folds down into three pictures you already carry.

Picture one: the earphone knot. The causes of poverty come pre-tangled — resources, energy, history, trade, population, education, environment — and pulling one wire tightens the others. So never explain a single factor alone. Write the chain: thin fish stocks pull a daughter from school, no matric pulls down wages, low wages pull the sea harder. Use the exact word INTERRELATED and the chain does the scoring for you.

Picture two: the meeting on the stoep. Community-based development in one line — people maintain what is THEIRS. Keep two named cases ready: Working for Wetlands healing catchments for wages and skills, and Warwick Junction, where Durban planned with its street traders and a feared interchange became a market. Then the honest limit: stoep projects are small, so community hands and state muscle must work together.

Picture three: the borrowed roof. Development that eats its environment is a family burning its roof beams — warm tonight, drenched later — and the bill lands on the poor first. The fix has names: impact assessments, mine rehabilitation, parks that earn, sun and wind.

The knot, the stoep, the roof. Three pictures, every fact hanging off one of them. Now go take on the practice questions — you know more than you think.
