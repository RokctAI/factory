Today we study mass movement — rock, soil and debris travelling downslope under gravity alone, from soil creep too slow to see to mud flows moving at highway speed. We open with the arm-wrestle between the forces driving a slope to fail and the forces holding it up, and with water's double game inside that contest. Then we sort the six kinds of movement onto a speed-and-water grid so any photograph identifies itself, take the KwaZulu-Natal disaster of April 2022 as our case study — the numbers, and the unequal risk that turned a natural process into a national tragedy — and close with the four families of prevention, from gabions and drains to hazard maps and early warning. By the end you will be able to read a real hillside before the rain comes.
