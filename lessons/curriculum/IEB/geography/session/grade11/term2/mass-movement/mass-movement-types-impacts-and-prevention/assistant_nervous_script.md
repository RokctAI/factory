Hey, it's Bianca — mass movement can feel like six disasters, one tragedy and a hardware store of prevention measures all shouting at once. Deep breath. The whole topic hangs on one arm-wrestle and one starting grid.

The arm-wrestle: gravity strains to pull the slope down; friction, grain-grip and plant roots hold it up. Mass movement is the second the gravity arm wins — material moving downslope under gravity with NO river, glacier or wind carrying it. That definition is two marks by itself; always write both halves. And keep the rusk in mind: a quick dip in the coffee binds it, a long soak collapses it. Soil is the rusk. Nearly every failure arrives with rain.

The starting grid is the six types, slowest to fastest, water rising down the line: creep, solifluction, landslide, slump, rock fall, mud flow. Give each one its picture — leaning fence posts, cold sludge over frozen ground, bricks off the tipper truck, the ice-cream scoop rotating out of the tub, the block dropping into the nets, the boulder milkshake at highway speed — and every photo question sorts itself in seconds.

For the case study, carry April 2022 Durban with three numbers and one sentence: over 300 millimetres in 24 hours, more than 400 lives, billions in damage — and the sentence: same rain, unequal risk, because the least protected homes stood on the steepest slopes. That sentence is the difference between retelling and understanding.

Prevention needs no memorising, because every measure is a cause reversed: too steep — regrade, terrace, gabions; too wet — drains and fixed leaks; too bare — plant it; badly placed — hazard maps, warnings, homes off the red zones. Walk the causes and the answer builds itself.

One arm-wrestle, one six-lane grid, three numbers, one reversal trick. That is the entire topic. Go and show the practice questions exactly who they are dealing with — you've got this!
