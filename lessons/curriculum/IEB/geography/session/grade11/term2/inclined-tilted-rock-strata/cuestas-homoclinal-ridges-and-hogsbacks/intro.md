Today we lean the rock layers over and meet the ridge family they build: the cuesta, the homoclinal ridge and the hogsback — one landform at three angles of dip. The whole topic balances on a single contrast: the gentle dip slope rides down the surface of one resistant layer, while the steep scarp slope cuts across the sawn-off ends of them all. We bend the same geometry into circles for cuesta basins and domes, with the Magaliesberg and the Vredefort Dome as our home examples, then take it to the map — reading unequal contour spacing and proving the dip direction with a gradient on each side of the crest. Learn the tilted sandwich and every leaning ridge you pass will tell you its name.
