Hey, it's Bianca — tilted strata week, and yes, cuesta, homoclinal and hogsback sound like three villains from a fantasy novel. They're not. The whole topic is one sandwich propped up on a matchbox.

Look at the leaning sandwich and both faces appear. The smooth top slice is a ramp — the dip slope, riding down one strong layer. The cut end shows every layer's sawn-off edge — the scarp slope, steep because it slices across the lot. Four words lock it in forever: dip follows, scarp cuts. Those four words label every diagram you will ever be given.

The three names are just three clicks of a car jack under the sandwich. Click one, a gentle lean up to about 25 degrees: cuesta — wheelbarrow ramp, small cliff at the back. Click two, about 25 to 45: homoclinal ridge — think Magaliesberg. Click three, past about 45: the ramp disappears, both faces steep, blade-thin crest — hogsback. Say "about" with every number, keep the order right, and quote the trend: steeper lean, narrower ridge, more equal faces.

Basins and domes trip people only when they memorise. Don't memorise — derive. The dip slope faces wherever the layers lean down. A satellite dish sags to its middle: dips in, scarps out, artesian water waiting underneath. An umbrella leans away from its middle: dips out, scarps in — that is Vredefort. A ten-second sketch beats a memorised list every single time.

On the map, run look, calculate, conclude: compare contour crowding on the two flanks, work a gradient for each side, then state that the rock dips toward the gentle side. Show all the working — the working is the marks.

One sandwich, three clicks, one derivation rule, one three-move map routine. That's everything this topic can throw at you. Now go take on those practice questions — you've got this!
