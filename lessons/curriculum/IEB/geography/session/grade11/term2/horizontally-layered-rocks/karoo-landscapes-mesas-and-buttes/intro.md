Today we read the landscapes built on horizontal strata — the flat-lying layers of the Karoo Supergroup that give the South African interior its tabletop skylines, and the stacked basalts that wall in Lesotho. One rule runs the whole show: differential erosion, where soft layers are stripped away while a hard dolerite cap shields everything beneath it. We follow the landform family from plateau through mesa and butte to conical hill, watch cliffs retreat backwards at full steepness to sweep out the great pediplain, and close with canyon country and the two map calculations — vertical exaggeration and gradient — that let you measure this terrain on paper. Master this session and every flat-topped koppie becomes a story you can read from the roadside.
