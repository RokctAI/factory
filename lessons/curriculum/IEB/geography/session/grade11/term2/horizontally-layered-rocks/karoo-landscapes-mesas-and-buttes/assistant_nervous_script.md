Hey, it's Bianca — I know the geomorphology word list looks brutal: strata, differential erosion, scarp retreat, pediplain. Take a breath. Underneath the vocabulary this whole topic is one picture: a paving slab dropped on a heap of sand, left out in the rain.

Rain strips the bare sand but cannot touch the column under the slab. Sand is the soft shale; the slab is dolerite. Soft goes first, hard survives — that is differential erosion, and it is the only process rule in the topic. Flat top means the slab is still holding; rounded hill means it failed. Say "hard cap protects soft layers" and you are already halfway to full marks.

The landforms are one hill at five ages, and the shapes hand you the names: floor, box, tower, tent, flat. Plateau, mesa, butte, conical hill, plain. Box wider than tall, tower taller than wide, tent once the cap is gone. Run the countdown out loud twice and it sticks.

For the process question, picture the wall that walks away: winter ice pops blocks off the cliff, summer storms dig out the soft rock underneath, and the cliff rebuilds itself one step back at the same steepness. Scarp retreat, back wasting — sideways, never downwards, because the cap refuses to let the summit drop. Each walking wall sweeps out its pediment, and the merged floors are the great Karoo pediplain.

Mapwork holds only two traps: convert the vertical scale to a ratio before dividing — one centimetre to twenty-five metres is 1:2 500, so 50 000 over 2 500 gives 20 times — and always write gradient as 1 in something, showing every line of working.

One slab, five shapes, one walking wall, two calculations. That is the entire topic. Now go show the practice questions who's boss — you've got this!
