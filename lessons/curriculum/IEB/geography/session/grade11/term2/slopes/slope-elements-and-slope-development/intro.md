Today the slope itself takes centre stage. We start with South Africa's three-part build — interior plateau, Great Escarpment, marginal zone — the wedding cake on which every slope in the country stands. Then we assemble the four classic slope elements from summit to plain: convex crest, vertical cliff, straight talus at its angle of repose, and gently concave pediment. We set the profile in motion with parallel slope retreat — the hill that walks backward without changing shape, sweeping out pediplains dotted with inselbergs — and weigh it against the rival idea of slope decline. We close with the digital toolkit: spatial and spectral resolution, vector points, lines and areas with their attributes, and raster grids that turn elevation data into instant slope maps. Old landscapes, new instruments, one lesson.
