Today we go beneath the layers, to the massive igneous rocks — magma that rose into the crust, stalled, and set underground as batholiths, laccoliths, lopoliths, dykes and sills. One two-word test sorts the whole family: does the intrusion lie with the strata or cut across them — concordant or discordant. We follow each buried body as erosion unveils it as a landform, watch pressure release and exfoliation peel granite into smooth self-rounding domes, and end with the two-stage story of tors — boulder stacks rotted into shape underground and then revealed. From the platinum-filled saucer of the Bushveld Complex to the bald granite heads of the Lowveld and the balancing koppies of Limpopo, this is the scenery South Africa is anchored on.
