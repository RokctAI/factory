Today we are learning to read the synoptic weather chart — the symbol-covered map behind every forecast you have ever heard. We will work one complete interpretation from start to finish: isobars and the highs and lows they wrap, with sinking air meaning clear skies and rising air meaning rain; then a station model decoded symbol by symbol — 21 degrees, a dew point of 18, seven-eighths cloud and a north-westerly at 15 knots; then the toothed line of a cold front, identified and proven with evidence from both sides. By the end you will describe the cold, showery, clearing weather behind the front in measured observations, and carry a five-step method you can apply to any chart the weather service publishes.
