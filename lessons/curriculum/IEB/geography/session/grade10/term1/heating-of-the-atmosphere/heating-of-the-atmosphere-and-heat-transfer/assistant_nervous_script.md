Hey, it's Thandi — insolation, albedo, terrestrial radiation, counter-radiation, four transfer processes, five temperature factors. I know exactly how that list looks the night before a test: like a dictionary fell on you. Let me hand you the one sentence that folds the whole thing up.

The atmosphere is heated from below. That is the sentence. Sunshine mostly slips through the air, lands on the ground, and the ground heats the air above it. Hold that and everything else clicks into place on its own: mountains are cold because climbing means walking away from the heater; afternoon storms build because heated air rises; the greenhouse effect works because the energy leaving the ground is a different kind — long-wave — and that is the kind the atmosphere can grab.

The topic only makes three moves. Move one, the budget: a hundred units in, thirty bounced, twenty absorbed on the way, fifty delivered — short-wave in, long-wave out. Move two, the four movers: sun on your face, burnt feet on hot sand, the shimmer over the road, the sea breeze — radiation, conduction, convection, advection. Move three, the five dials that set any town's temperature: angle of the sun, height, distance from the sea, the current offshore, and cloud with aspect.

One habit will carry your marks: never answer with a factor's name alone. "Because of altitude" is a shrug. "Because it lies 1 400 metres up, and air cools about 6,5 degrees per 1 000 metres away from the ground that heats it" — that is an answer.

One sentence, three moves, one habit. Go take the practice questions and watch this topic behave itself. You've got this!
