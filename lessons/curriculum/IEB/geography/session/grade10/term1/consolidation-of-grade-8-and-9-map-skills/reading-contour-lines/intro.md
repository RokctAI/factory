Today we are learning to read contour lines — the code a topographic map uses to describe the shape of the land on flat paper. Working with a 1:50 000 extract and its standard 20 metre contour interval, we start with what a contour line actually is and the rules it can never break, move to the principle that line spacing is a direct picture of slope, and then learn the V-shaped fingerprint that separates a river valley from its dangerous look-alike, the spur. By the end you will read a whole landscape off the sheet — summit, steep face and drainage direction — and back every claim with the spacing and the printed heights, which is the standard a strong mapwork answer must meet.
