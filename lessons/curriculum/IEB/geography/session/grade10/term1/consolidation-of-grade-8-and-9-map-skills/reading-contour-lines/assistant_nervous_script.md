Hey, it's Thandi — I remember opening my first 1:50 000 map and seeing nothing but brown spaghetti. If that is where you are right now, you are exactly on schedule.

Here is the secret: this whole topic runs on one engine. The climb between two lines is always the same — 20 metres on our map — so the only thing the spacing can possibly show you is how far you walk to earn it. Lines squeezed together: short walk, hard climb, steep. Lines spread out: long stroll, easy climb, gentle. Cliffs, ramps, concave, convex — every one of them is just that engine revving at a different speed.

Rivers scare people, but they should not scare you, because you hold a cheat code the shape can never beat: the numbers. The V in the contours points at the high ground the stream comes from, and if you ever doubt yourself, read the values the river crosses — they must drop downstream, because water has never flowed uphill in the history of the planet. Bend pointing at the bigger numbers, valley. Bend pointing away, spur. Done.

When you answer, do it in three beats: name the feature, quote the spacing, read the heights. "This is the steepest slope because the lines are closest together, so the land rises its 20 metres over the shortest distance" — that sentence, adapted, is worth marks over and over, in your final exams and every year after.

So go to the practice questions now, and if you can, pull up any 1:50 000 sheet of your own area tonight and just look at it. Give it five quiet minutes and the koppies will lift right off the paper. You've got this!
