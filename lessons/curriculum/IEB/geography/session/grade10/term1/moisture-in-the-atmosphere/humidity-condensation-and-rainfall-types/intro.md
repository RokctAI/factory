Today we are studying moisture in the atmosphere — how invisible water vapour becomes dew, fog, cloud and finally rain. We start with humidity and the law that warm air carries more vapour than cold, work a relative humidity calculation through to saturation, and define the dew point as the temperature where a shrinking capacity catches up with the moisture already there. From there we follow condensation from a frosty Karoo night to the condensation level that gives every cloud its flat base, learn the four Latin bricks that build every cloud name, and finish with the three lifting mechanisms — mountain, heated ground and cold front — that produce relief, convectional and frontal rainfall across South Africa.
