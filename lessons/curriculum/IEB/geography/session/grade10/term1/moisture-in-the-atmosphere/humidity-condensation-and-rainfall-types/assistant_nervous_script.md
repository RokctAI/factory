Hey, it's Thandi — humidity, dew point, lapse rates, a pile of Latin cloud names and three rainfall types. I know this topic looks like it was designed to be memorised, and memorising is the worst way into it. There are really only two ideas here, and you already know both from ordinary life.

Idea one: warm air is a big sponge, cold air is a small one. Cool air down and the sponge shrinks around the water it is carrying; the moment it is exactly full, that is saturation, and the temperature where it happened is the dew point. Your glasses misting when you walk out of a cool shop, your breath showing on a cold morning — you have been watching this idea your whole life without being told its name.

Idea two: rising air cools. Push air up and it expands and chills, one degree per hundred metres, until it hits its dew point and cloud begins. Every single rainfall type is just a different machine doing the pushing: the ramp — a mountain; the fire — hot summer ground; the wedge — cold air shoving under warm. Ramp, fire, wedge. Region and season are extra marks: mountains for relief rain, Highveld summers for convectional, Cape winters for frontal.

The clouds? Four Latin bricks — cirro high, alto middle, strato flat, cumulo heaped — and nimbus meaning it is raining right now. Build the names instead of memorising them.

When a question comes, name the mechanism first, then the place, then the season. Three short pieces beat one long muddle every time.

Two ideas, three machines, four bricks. That is the whole topic. Go show the practice questions who is in charge — you've got this!
