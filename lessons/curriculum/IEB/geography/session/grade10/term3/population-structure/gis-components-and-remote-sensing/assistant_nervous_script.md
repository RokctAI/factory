Hey, it's Thandi — GIS and remote sensing sound like a computer science course crash-landing in geography, but breathe: this topic is three definitions, one list of five, and one honest limit. You already use all of it on your phone.

Definition one: a GIS captures, stores, analyses, manages and displays georeferenced information — a database where every record knows its position. Your Sunday-night pharmacy search is the whole definition running in your pocket.

Definition two: the layer. One theme per transparent sheet — rivers, roads, homesteads, schools — all to the same coordinates. Stack them and ask questions across the pile: that is overlay analysis, and it is the single most examined idea here.

Definition three: remote sensing — learning about the earth's surface from a distance, no contact, with sensors on satellites and aircraft. Satellite: huge area, returns again and again, shows change. Aircraft: small area, fine detail, gives us the orthophoto maps.

The list of five: hardware, software, data, people, procedures — engine, fuel, steering, service manual. If they ask which matters most, the answer is data, because stale data makes confident nonsense.

And the honest limit, which markers love: the satellite counts roofs, never people. Only the person at the door learns who lives underneath — that is why the census still walks.

Three definitions, five components, one limit. Say the pharmacy, the tracing paper and the roofs, and this topic is yours. Now go take on the practice questions — you are readier than you think!
