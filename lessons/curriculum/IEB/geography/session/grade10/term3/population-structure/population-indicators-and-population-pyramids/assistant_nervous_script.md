Hey, it's Thandi — indicators and pyramids can feel like statistics class broke into geography, but calm down and count what is actually here: five numbers, one graph, three shapes. That is the whole topic.

The five numbers, in plain speech. Births per thousand. Deaths per thousand. Births minus deaths gives natural increase — divide by ten for the percent. Fertility: children per woman, and the one number to tattoo on your memory is 2,1, replacement level — above it grows, below it shrinks. Infant mortality: babies lost before their first birthday per thousand born — the most honest test of development, because a surviving baby means water, food, vaccines and clinics all passed at once. Life expectancy: an average, so lost babies drag it down — a low figure never means there are no grandparents.

The graph: males left, females right, five-year rows, babies at the bottom, elders at the top. Read it with three looks — the base for births, the slope for deaths, and any notch or bulge for history.

The three shapes: triangle means growing fast, column means steady, pinched-at-the-bottom means shrinking and ageing. South Africa sits between triangle and column, with a big bulge of young adults — our one-time chance, if the jobs come.

If a trap appears, it is the Italy-and-Uganda one: old countries have high death rates because they are old, not sick. Ask who is in the population, every time.

Five numbers, three looks, three shapes, one trap. You have the whole topic in one pocket. Now go and show the practice questions who is in charge!
