Today we are learning to read a country the way demographers do — through five numbers and one picture. The numbers are the population indicators: birth rate, death rate, total fertility with its magic threshold of two comma one, infant mortality as the most honest measure of development, and life expectancy, including the fall and recovery written into South Africa's own figure by the Aids epidemic and the treatment programme that answered it. The picture is the population pyramid: paired bars of males and females stacked in five-year cohorts, whose base, slopes, notches and bulges record births, deaths, wars and baby booms. We finish with the three classic shapes — expansive, stationary and constrictive — and place South Africa's transitional pyramid among them, with its youth bulge, its demographic dividend, and the condition on which that dividend pays.
