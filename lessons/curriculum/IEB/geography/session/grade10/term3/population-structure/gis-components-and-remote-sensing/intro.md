Today we are opening the toolbox behind every population map you have used this term: Geographical Information Systems and remote sensing. You will learn the two-part definition of a GIS — a database whose every record knows its position — the five components that make the system work, and the layer principle that lets a computer stack rivers, roads, people and clinics over one piece of ground and answer questions no single map could. Then we go airborne: how sensors on satellites and aircraft read reflected energy, why a satellite's repeat visits turn landscapes into time series, and how South Africa uses exactly these tools — tracking informal settlement growth, preparing the census, mapping the Knysna fires and watching Theewaterskloof Dam shrink. By the end you will also know the tool's honest limit: a satellite counts roofs, but only a person at the door can count people.
