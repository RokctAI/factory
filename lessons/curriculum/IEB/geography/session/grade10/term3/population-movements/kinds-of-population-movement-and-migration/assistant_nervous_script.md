Hey, it's Thandi — migration looks like a wall of terms: emigrant, immigrant, refugee, asylum seeker, oscillating, remittance. Deep breath. The whole topic runs on two questions, one rope and four boxes, and you can own all of them tonight.

First, the gatekeeper definition: migration means the home address changes. If they are back by supper or by Sunday, that is circulation, not migration — write that distinction and you are already scoring.

Then the two sorting questions. Did they cross a border? Inside the country is internal; across a border is international, where one person is an emigrant leaving and an immigrant arriving. Did they choose? Chosen is voluntary; fleeing danger is forced — refugee across the border, asylum seeker waiting for the stamp, internally displaced still inside their own country.

For every why question, pull the rope: push factors at home, pull factors at the destination, obstacles in between, and the too-sunny picture in the migrant's head. Name something from each and your explanation is complete.

For every consequences question, draw the four boxes: origin gains, remittances and returning skills; origin loses, its working-age people; destination gains, workers and businesses; destination strains, housing, services and social peace. Attach the Eastern Cape to Gauteng stream or the Zimbabwean stream as your named example and the essay writes itself.

Two questions, one rope, four boxes, one example. That is the entire topic folded small enough for your pocket. Now go at the practice questions — you are more ready than you know!
