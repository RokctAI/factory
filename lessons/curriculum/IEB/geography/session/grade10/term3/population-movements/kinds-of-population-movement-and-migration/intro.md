Today we are sorting the ways people move and explaining why they migrate. First the vocabulary: what separates a commuter's daily loop from a true change of residence, and the three axes that classify every migration — inside a border or across one, chosen or forced, permanent or a lifetime of oscillating between mine hostel and village. Then the engine: push factors that expel, pull factors that attract, the obstacles in between, and the too-sunny picture in the migrant's head. We apply all of it to two case studies that define this topic in South Africa — the great internal stream from the rural Eastern Cape to Gauteng, and the international streams crossing our borders, above all from Zimbabwe — and finish with the four-box method for arguing consequences at both ends, which is exactly how the top essay answers are built.
