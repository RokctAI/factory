Hey, it's Thandi — billions, percentages, doubling times: this topic loves to look like a maths ambush, but it is actually one story, two small sums, one comparison and one short list. Let's pack them.

The story: flat for ten thousand years, then straight up after 1800. One billion by 1804, eight billion by 2022 — the J-curve. And the whole explosion in one sentence: deaths collapsed while births stayed high. Fewer funerals, not more babies. That sentence answers half of everything in this section.

The sums are gentle. Natural increase: births minus deaths per thousand, divide by ten for the percent. Doubling time: seventy divided by the rate — two comma five percent means twenty-eight years. That is truly all the arithmetic there is.

The comparison is the classic trap, so spring it before it springs you: overpopulation is NOT density. Singapore is jammed and comfortable; the dry south of Madagascar is sparse and struggling. The test is always people against carrying capacity — check the resources before you ever write the word overpopulated.

The list is what slows growth: educate girls, keep babies alive, make family planning reachable, shrink poverty with pensions. For the discussion marks, one contrast does the work — China forced it and inherited an ageing, unbalanced population; Bangladesh and Botswana chose it and kept their balance. And remember our own case: South Africa went from six children per woman to about two comma three entirely by choice.
