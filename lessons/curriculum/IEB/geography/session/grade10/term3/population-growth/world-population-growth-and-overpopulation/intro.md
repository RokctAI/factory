Today we are following the most dramatic line in geography: world population, flat for ten thousand years and then multiplying eightfold in two centuries. You will learn what actually bent that curve — collapsing death rates, not rising births — and master the two calculations that measure growth: natural increase, and doubling time through the rule of 70. Then we take on the word everyone uses loosely and geographers use precisely: overpopulation, which compares people to carrying capacity, which is why ultra-dense Singapore is comfortable while the sparse drylands of southern Madagascar can be under genuine strain. We close with the evidence on slowing growth, weighing China's forced experiment against the voluntary successes of Bangladesh and Botswana, with South Africa's own fall from six children per woman to about two comma three as proof that choice works.
