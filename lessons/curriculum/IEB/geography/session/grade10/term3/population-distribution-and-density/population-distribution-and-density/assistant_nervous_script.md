Hey, it's Thandi — I know this topic looks like two words that mean the same thing, and that is exactly the trap. Nearly everyone wobbles on distribution versus density at first, and the fix takes one line, so let's put it in your pocket right now.

Density is a NUMBER. Distribution is a PICTURE. If the question wants people per square kilometre, do the division and write the unit — that is density. If it wants you to say where people are — bunched here, strung along a river there, empty in the west — that is distribution, and it wants words, not a calculator.

The calculation itself is the friendliest maths in geography: people divided by area. The only way to lose those marks is forgetting the unit, so make writing people per square kilometre a reflex.

For the long answers, keep two headings and never let them blur. PHYSICAL: relief, climate, water, soil, resources, vegetation and disease, accessibility. HUMAN: work, transport, services, government and history, social ties. Cover both sides and your answer is already balanced.

Then one habit that turns lists into marks: pin a place onto every factor. Dry and empty — the Richtersveld and Bushmanland. Crowded from gold — Gauteng. Crowded from a harbour — Durban and Richards Bay. Crowded from history — the former homeland districts. Factor plus place, every single time.

Number versus picture, two headings, a place for every factor. That is the whole topic, and you already have it. Now go try the practice questions — you're readier than you feel!
