Today we are working on population distribution and density — the pattern and the number that geographers keep strictly apart, because muddling them is the most common error in this whole topic. We will prove with two imaginary wards that identical densities can hide opposite patterns, drill the density calculation until the unit comes automatically, and see why South Africa's average of just over fifty people per square kilometre describes nowhere that actually exists. Then we read our own map: a well-watered, well-peopled east against a dry and almost empty west, four great urban clusters crowned by a Gauteng that grew on gold rather than on any gift of the land, and crowded rural districts in the former homelands whose densities were made by law, not by geography.
