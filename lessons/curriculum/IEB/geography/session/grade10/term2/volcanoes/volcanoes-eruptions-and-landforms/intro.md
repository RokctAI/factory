Today we follow molten rock from the mantle to the mountaintop. First the crucial split: why runny, low-silica magma leaks out in glowing rivers while thick, high-silica magma corks its own pipe and then detonates. Then the anatomy of a volcano and its four builds — shield, composite, cinder and dome — followed by the two families of landform: those poured out on the surface, like the stacked basalts of the Drakensberg, and those frozen underground, like the dolerite lids on every Karoo koppie and the diamond pipes at Cullinan. We end with the ledger — pyroclastic flows, ash and lahars against fertile soils, geothermal power and mineral wealth — and the warning signs that let scientists walk whole towns out of danger.
