Hey, it's Thandi — volcanoes hand you a scary word list: pyroclastic, batholith, lopolith, laccolith, caldera, kimberlite. Before you drown in vocabulary, let me give you the two questions that sort every single term in this topic.

Question one: is the melt THIN or THICK? That is the pap pot rule. Thin soup lets its bubbles out — gentle, oozing volcanoes, wide and flat like spilled syrup. Thick pap traps the steam until it spits — explosive volcanoes, tall and steep like a lasagne of ash and lava layers. Nearly every fact about eruption style, shape and danger follows from that one split.

Question two: did the magma get OUT or stay IN? Out means extrusive: cones, lava flows, and the stacked sheets that built the Drakensberg's giant stairs. In means intrusive: the underground shapes — the huge deep blob, the saucer, the blister, the flat sheet between layers, the wall across them, and the frozen pipe. You have seen these: every flat Karoo koppie wears a dolerite lid, and the diamond mines at Cullinan are just old pipes.

For the hazards, rank them by speed: lava walks, ash falls, mud follows the valleys, and the pyroclastic flow runs faster than anything — which is why the four verbs matter: watch, map, plan, practise.

Thin or thick. Out or in. Ask those two questions and the whole topic files itself neatly. Now go tackle the practice questions — you've got this!
