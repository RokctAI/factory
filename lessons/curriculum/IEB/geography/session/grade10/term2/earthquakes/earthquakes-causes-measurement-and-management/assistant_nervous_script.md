Hey, it's Thandi — earthquakes look like a heap of loose facts: waves, scales, depths, disasters, engineering. Let me fold the whole heap into four moves you already understand.

Move one: the branch. Bend it, bend it, CRACK — that is elastic rebound. Strain stores for years in locked rock, then the fault slips and the energy escapes as shaking. Two addresses to attach: the focus is the underground snap point, the epicentre is the spot on the surface straight above it. And shallow snaps are the killers, because their energy arrives fresh.

Move two: the race. Three waves leave together and finish apart — P the fast squeeze, S the sideways whip, surface waves the slow wreckers. The gap between P and S tells each station its distance, and three stations, three circles, one crossing point finds the epicentre — exactly how cell towers find a phone.

Move three: the splash and the ripples. Richter is the splash — one number for the whole event. Mercalli is the ripples — a different reading on every shore. And remember the arithmetic trap: two steps up the Richter scale is a hundred times the shaking and about a thousand times the energy. Never "a bit stronger".

Move four: the buildings. Earthquakes do not kill people; falling buildings do. Chile and Haiti in the same year proved that codes, drills and solid ground beat raw magnitude every time.

Branch, race, splash, buildings. Say those four in order and the topic is yours. Now go crack the practice questions — you've got this!
