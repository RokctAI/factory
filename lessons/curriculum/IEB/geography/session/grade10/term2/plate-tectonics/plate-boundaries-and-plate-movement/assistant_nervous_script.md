Hey, it's Thandi — plate tectonics opens with a wall of long words: Pangaea, Gondwana, palaeomagnetism, lithosphere, subduction. It looks like the heaviest topic of the term. It is actually one photograph, one pot of milk and three moves.

The photograph: the continents are a picture torn in two, and the proof they belong together is that the picture continues across the tear. Four kinds of continuation — the coastline fit, the fossils, the matching mountain ranges, and the ice scratches that only line up when you push the continents back together. Learn one clean example of each, like Mesosaurus in both the Karoo and Brazil, and that whole section is yours.

The milk pot: heat rises in the middle, spreads, cools, sinks — convection — and the skin on top gets dragged around. The skin is the plate. Add the two extra pushes: gravity sliding the plate off the ridge bump, and the sinking edge hauling the rest along like a belt slipping off a table.

The three moves: apart, together, past. Apart builds ridges and rift valleys. Together builds trenches, the Andes, island arcs — and when two continents meet, mountains with no volcanoes, because nothing sinks to melt. Past builds nothing but earthquakes.

If a question ever confuses you, just ask: which move is this, and what is denser than what? Those two questions solve nearly everything in this topic.

Photograph, milk pot, three moves. Now go show the practice questions who's boss — you've got this!
