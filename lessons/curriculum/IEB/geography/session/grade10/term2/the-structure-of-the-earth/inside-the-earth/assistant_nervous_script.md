Hey, it's Thandi — layers with strange names, two boundaries you can barely pronounce, two kinds of crust and invisible waves you somehow have to explain. It looks like a mountain of memorising. It really is not. There are only three ideas to carry.

Idea one: the peach. Skin, flesh, stone — crust, mantle, core. Then bolt on the two sentences that rescue the most marks. The mantle is SOLID rock — only the soft band near the top creeps, like chocolate slumping in a hot car. And the inner core stays solid even though it is the hottest place on earth, because pressure beats heat. Write those two correctly and you are ahead of half the room already.

Idea two: two crusts, and the heavy one always dives. Continental — thick, light, granite, ancient, like Barberton's three-billion-year-old hills. Oceanic — thin, dense, basalt, never older than about two hundred million years. Push them together and the dense one sinks. That one line unlocks subduction and most of next week.

Idea three: the missing waves. P-waves cross anything; S-waves die in liquid. On the far side of every big earthquake, the S-waves simply never arrive — and that silence is the proof of a liquid outer core.

One habit for the exam: never name a layer bare. Give its state and one reason, every time — name, depth, composition, state, feature.

And Mohorovičić? Everyone just says Moho. So can you.

Peach, two crusts, missing waves. That is the whole lesson. Now go try the practice questions — you've got this!
