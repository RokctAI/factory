Hey, it's Thandi — this topic dumps a dictionary on you: anticline, syncline, recumbent, nappe, horst, graben, hanging wall, throw, heave. If it feels like vocabulary soup, breathe. The whole thing sorts into two piles, and you already own both.

Pile one: rock that BENT. That is the dough — squeezed slowly, deep and hot, it humps into arches and troughs. Arch equals anticline, trough equals syncline, and the five squeeze stages just tell the story of pushing harder: even, leaning, overhanging, lying down, torn. One surprise to remember: the arch cracks on top like a rolled magazine's spine, so erosion can turn the arch into the valley — inversion of relief.

Pile two: rock that BROKE. That is the rusk, and the paving slabs outside show every move: one slab sinks — normal fault; one rides up — reverse; two slide past — shear. Middle slab drops between two — rift valley. Middle slab stands proud — block mountain.

For any question, just ask three things. Which force — squeeze, stretch or slide? Did the rock bend or break? And what shape is left standing? Force, response, shape, landform — same order every time.

And the one-glance trick if a picture appears: curves mean folding, ruler-straight lines mean faulting.

Two piles, three questions, one glance. That is the whole lesson — now go take on the practice questions. You've got this!
