Today we are taking apart South Africa's most frequent natural disaster — the flood — and learning to read the instrument that measures it: the flood hydrograph. We separate the physical machinery, from thunderstorm intensity and stalled cut-off lows to slope, soil and catchment shape, from the human amplifiers — the paved city, the lost wetlands, and settlement on the river's own land. Then we assemble the hydrograph in words — base flow, rising limb, peak discharge, lag time, falling limb — and use its three readings for the comparisons that matter: urban against rural, and flash flood against regional flood, anchored in the Jukskei disaster of December 2022 and the Natal floods of September 1987. Learn the three readings and every flood question becomes a story you can tell from a graph.
