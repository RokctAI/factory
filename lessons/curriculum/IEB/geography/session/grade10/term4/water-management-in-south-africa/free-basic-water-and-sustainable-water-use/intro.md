Today we are following South Africa's water promise from the page to the tap. We start with the constitutional right to sufficient water and the free basic allowance of six thousand litres a month — and the arithmetic of twenty-five litres per person per day behind it — then confront why delivery is so hard, from dispersed rural villages and broken pumps to city leaks that lose a third of the supply. We trace the big engineering: the Lesotho Highlands Water Project running downhill by gravity into the Vaal, the pumped Tugela-Vaal reserve at Sterkfontein, and the five purification steps between dam and glass. And we finish with sustainability's two role-players — the state and the citizen — tested by Cape Town's Day Zero and Nelson Mandela Bay's long drought.
