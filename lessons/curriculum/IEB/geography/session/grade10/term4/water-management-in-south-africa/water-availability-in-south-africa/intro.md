Today we are counting South Africa's water honestly. We start with the two numbers that open every strong answer — 465 millimetres of rain a year against the world's 860, and the 9 per cent of it that ever becomes river flow — then follow the rivers across the map from the perennial east to the episodic west, with the Orange carrying Lesotho's mountain water clean through the desert. We ask why this country has almost no natural lakes and how it built more than five hundred large dams instead, from the Gariep to Sterkfontein's strategic reserve, and we go underground to the aquifers that keep the Karoo alive. Finally we assemble the full list of physical and human factors behind water availability — the foundation for every water management debate this term.
