Hi, Mandy here — Doppler questions look scary because of that plus-or-minus sandwich in the equation, but here is the secret: you never have to memorise the signs, because the physics chooses them for you every single time.

One principle runs the whole topic: approach RAISES the detected frequency, recession LOWERS it. So before you touch the equation, write one sentence — "the answer must come out higher than the source frequency" or "lower". That sentence is your compass.

Then place the speeds in their homes. The listener lives upstairs, in the numerator: moving toward the source, add; moving away, subtract. The source lives downstairs, in the denominator: moving toward the listener, subtract — a smaller denominator makes the fraction bigger; moving away, add. If your final number disagrees with your compass sentence, a sign is wrong, and you will catch it before anyone else does.

Reverse problems — given the heard frequency, find the speed — start with the same sentence in reverse: heard higher than emitted means approaching, heard lower means receding. State it, choose the signs, solve.

And keep the two classic explanation marks safe: louder is NOT Doppler, that is just the source getting closer; and the source's own frequency never changes, only the detected one. For red shift, remember the fingerprint: same pattern of spectral lines, slid toward the red, so the galaxy is receding — and the farther the galaxy, the bigger the slide, which is why we say the universe is expanding.

Compass sentence, two homes for the speeds, self-checking answer. Go try the practice questions now and feel how quickly they fall. You've got this!
