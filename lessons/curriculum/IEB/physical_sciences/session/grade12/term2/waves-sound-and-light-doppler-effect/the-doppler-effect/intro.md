Today we take one street-corner observation — the siren that seems to drop in pitch as it passes — and follow it all the way to the edge of the universe. We will define the Doppler effect precisely as a change in the frequency DETECTED by a listener, build the wavefront picture that explains it, and then master the single equation whose plus and minus signs are chosen by pure physics: approach raises the detected frequency, recession lowers it. We will run the calculation forward for moving sources and moving listeners, backward to extract a speed from a shifted frequency, and then let the same idea measure blood flow in an artery and read the red shift that proves the galaxies are receding. One effect, three sizes: the siren, the clinic, the sky.
