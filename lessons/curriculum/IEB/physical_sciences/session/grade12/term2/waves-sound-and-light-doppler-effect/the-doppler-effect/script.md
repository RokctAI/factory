# Part 1 — Expert

A fire engine sweeps past and its siren seems to sag in pitch at the exact moment it passes — yet the siren itself never changed its note. That street-corner observation is the Doppler effect, and it does far more than decorate traffic: it runs the ultrasound machines in every hospital and supplies the evidence that the universe is expanding. This session defines the effect properly, masters the single equation and its signs, works the standard calculations forward and backward, and follows the same physics from a clinic to the edge of the cosmos.

## Subtopic: What the Doppler Effect Is

The definition, stated carefully: the Doppler effect is the change in frequency of the sound detected by a listener, because the sound source and the listener have different velocities relative to the medium in which the sound travels. The load-bearing word is DETECTED. The source vibrates at its own fixed frequency from start to finish; what changes is the frequency arriving at the listener's ear.

Build the mental picture from wavefronts. A stationary siren radiates sound as expanding spheres, crest after crest, evenly spaced in every direction — ripples around a stone dropped into a still pond. Set the siren moving and the pattern deforms: each new crest is emitted from a point slightly ahead of the previous one, so ahead of the moving source the crests crowd together — a shorter wavelength — while behind it they spread apart — a longer one. The speed of sound itself does not budge; it is a property of the air, about 340 metres per second. With the speed fixed and the wavelength squeezed in front, the relation speed equals frequency times wavelength forces the frequency detected ahead to be HIGHER, and the frequency behind to be LOWER.

That is the whole passing-siren story: you stand in front of the source, receiving crowded crests, until the instant it passes — then you stand behind it, receiving stretched ones. Be precise about what changes and what does not. Pitch is the ear's word for frequency, and pitch changes. Loudness is a matter of amplitude and distance — the siren grows louder as it nears simply because it is closer, and that is NOT the Doppler effect. Separating those two observations cleanly is worth a full explanation on its own.

A moving listener creates the same result through a different mechanism: cycle toward a stationary siren and you meet crests more often than someone standing still, so you detect a higher frequency; cycle away and each crest has to catch up to you, so the detected frequency falls.

This section's questions are with you now — the definition word for word, and the wavefront sketch ready in your hand.

## Subtopic: The Equation and Its Signs

One equation carries every case. The frequency detected by the listener equals the speed of sound plus or minus the speed of the listener, divided by the speed of sound plus or minus the speed of the source, multiplied by the frequency of the source. In symbols: f L equals v plus or minus v L, over v plus or minus v s, times f s — with v the speed of sound in the medium, v L the listener's speed and v s the source's speed.

Choose the signs from one physical principle: approach raises the detected frequency, recession lowers it. The listener's speed sits in the NUMERATOR: a listener moving TOWARD the source needs a larger numerator, so add v L; moving away, subtract it. The source's speed sits in the DENOMINATOR: a source moving TOWARD the listener needs a smaller denominator, so subtract v s; moving away, add it. Settle the physics before touching the algebra — should the answer land above or below the source frequency? — then pick signs to force that outcome, and the final number audits itself.

Work the standard case. A fire-engine siren emits 840 hertz while the vehicle approaches a stationary listener at 25 metres per second; take the speed of sound as 340 metres per second. The listener is at rest, so the numerator is simply 340. The source approaches, so the denominator is 340 minus 25, which is 315. Formula: f L equals v over v minus v s, times f s. Substitute: 340 divided by 315, multiplied by 840. Answer: 906,67 hertz — above 840, exactly as approach requires.

The instant the fire engine passes and recedes, the denominator flips to 340 plus 25, which is 365: f L equals 340 divided by 365, multiplied by 840 — 782,47 hertz. The listener hears the note plunge from about 907 to about 782 as the vehicle sweeps by — a drop of more than 124 hertz — while the siren held 840 without a flicker.

Questions on the equation are in front of you now — physics first, signs second, substitute third, and check the direction of the answer last.

## Subtopic: Moving Listeners and Reverse Problems

Now put the listener in motion. A car travels at 15 metres per second toward a stationary factory siren of 600 hertz. The listener approaches, so the numerator grows: 340 plus 15, which is 355; the source stands still, so the denominator stays 340. Formula: f L equals v plus v L, over v, times f s. Substitute: 355 divided by 340, multiplied by 600. Answer: 626,47 hertz. Driving away at the same speed gives 340 minus 15 over 340, times 600 — 573,53 hertz.

The most instructive twist runs the equation in reverse: given the detected frequency, extract the speed. A stationary listener hears a train hooter of 750 hertz as 800 hertz. First sentence of the solution: the detected frequency is higher than the source frequency, so the train is APPROACHING. Then the algebra: 800 equals 340, over 340 minus v s, times 750. Rearrange: 340 minus v s equals 340 multiplied by 750 divided by 800, which is 318,75. So v s equals 21,25 metres per second. Always open a reverse problem by stating the direction of motion from the frequency comparison — that sentence is the anchor of the whole solution.

Now the medical payoff. Ultrasound — sound above 20 kilohertz, beyond the reach of human hearing — is beamed into the body, where it reflects off whatever moves. A moving blood cell acts first as a moving receiver and then as a moving re-transmitter, so the reflected ultrasound comes back with a shifted frequency, and the size of that shift measures the speed of the blood. This is Doppler flow measurement: where an artery narrows, the blood must accelerate through the constriction, and the machine hears the speed-up without a single incision. The same principle carries a foetal heartbeat to waiting parents: the tiny moving heart wall shifts the reflected frequency rhythmically, and the machine renders those shifts as audible beats. Painless, non-invasive, and entirely this equation.

This section's questions arrive now — direction first in every reverse problem, and let approach-raises, recede-lowers audit each answer.

## Subtopic: Red Shifts and the Expanding Universe

The Doppler effect does not stop at sound. Light is a wave as well, and relative motion between a light source and an observer shifts its detected frequency in the same spirit: a source receding from the observer has its light moved toward LOWER frequency — toward the red end of the visible spectrum, a RED SHIFT — while an approaching source shifts toward the blue, a blue shift.

How is such a shift read off starlight? Through a built-in barcode. Every chemical element absorbs and emits light at its own precise set of frequencies — its spectral lines, familiar from atomic spectra. Hydrogen's line pattern is as distinctive as a fingerprint. When astronomers spread out the light of a distant galaxy, they recognise hydrogen's pattern immediately — but the whole pattern sits at lower frequencies than the same lines measured in a laboratory on Earth. The fingerprint is undamaged; it has simply slid toward the red. The light is red-shifted, and the size of the slide states the speed of recession.

Then the observation that rebuilt cosmology: the light from almost EVERY distant galaxy is red-shifted, and the farther away a galaxy lies, the larger its red shift — the faster it recedes. The conclusion to state in full: the galaxies are moving away from us, and away from one another, in every direction — the universe is EXPANDING. Run that film in reverse and all the receding matter converges into an early, hot, dense state, which is the heart of the Big Bang picture.

Keep the claim precise, because precision is what earns credit. Red shift does not crown Earth the centre of anything: in an expanding universe every observer in every galaxy sees the identical recession pattern, like raisins in a rising loaf all watching each other drift apart. And the expansion is a conclusion REASONED from evidence, not an assumption: shifted fingerprints, measured recession, expanding universe — three steps, delivered in that order.

The final expert questions are with you now — red means receding, and nearly the whole sky is receding.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

The same physics again, from the pavement: one siren, one taxi rank, and a story written across the entire sky.

## Subtopic: The Siren That Lies to You

Stand on the pavement while a fire engine howls past, and listen like a physicist. On approach: a high, urgent note. The moment it passes: the note slumps, abruptly deeper, as if the driver had toggled a switch. No switch exists. Sit in the cab beside the driver and the siren sings one steady note for the entire trip. The siren tells the crew the truth all day — it lies only to the people it passes.

The honest version of events: sound is a parade of pressure crests marching out of the siren, evenly spaced. While the engine drives toward you, each new crest sets off from slightly closer to you than the one before, so the crests reach your ear bunched up — more crests per second — and more per second simply IS a higher pitch. After it passes, every crest departs from farther away than the last: the parade stretches, fewer crests per second arrive, and the note sinks.

Notice the two things that never changed: the siren's own note, and the speed of sound through the air. The only thing that changed is the SPACING of what reaches you, because the thing launching the crests was moving. That single sentence is the Doppler effect: motion re-spaces the waves between source and listener.

And hold one distinction steady, because it is regularly probed: the siren also grows LOUDER as it comes near — but nearness is all that is, the way a nearby drum outshouts a distant one. Loudness belongs to distance and amplitude; PITCH belongs to frequency, and only the pitch change is Doppler.

Your first questions are with you now — the note sags because the spacing stretched, never because the siren changed.

## Subtopic: Taxis Leaving Every Ten Minutes

Set sound aside and think about taxis. A rank sends a taxi down the same road every ten minutes, dead on schedule. You wait at a stop along that road. Standing still, one taxi passes you every ten minutes — you receive them at exactly the rate the rank dispatches them.

Now start WALKING toward the rank. You meet each taxi head-on a little earlier than you otherwise would, so they pass you every eight or nine minutes. The rank never touched its timetable; your own motion lifted your personal arrival rate. Turn around and walk away, and every taxi has to chase you down before it can pass: the gaps swell to eleven or twelve minutes. Timetable unchanged, arrival rate down.

That is the moving LISTENER, and it is half of the Doppler equation. The other half is a moving RANK. Picture the entire rank creeping down the road toward you while still dispatching every ten minutes: each taxi begins its trip from nearer to you than the last, so the taxis sit closer together on the road, and everyone downstream receives them more often than the timetable promises. A retreating rank stretches the spacing instead, and the arrival rate falls.

Trade taxis for wave crests and the timetable for the source's frequency, and the physics is complete: approach packs arrivals closer together — higher detected frequency; retreat spreads them out — lower. Better still, the equation's plus-and-minus signs stop being a memory exercise: motion toward each other must always push the detected rate UP, so choose whichever sign forces that. Decide the direction of the answer first, and no sign can ever betray you.

Questions on this section are coming to you now — you cannot edit the timetable, only how often the timetable reaches you.

## Subtopic: From the Maternity Ward to the Whole Sky

Why does a pitch trick deserve this much attention? Because bouncing a wave off a moving thing measures its speed without touching it — and that one idea operates from inside a womb to the far side of the universe.

In the clinic, the machine beams ultrasound — sound pitched far beyond hearing — into the body, where it bounces off whatever is in motion: hurrying blood cells, a tiny beating heart. Reflected off a mover, the wave returns with its pitch shifted, and the size of the shift gives away the mover's speed. That is how a clinic checks blood flow through a narrowed artery — blood squeezed through a constriction must speed up, and the machine hears exactly how much — and it is how parents first hear a heartbeat: the machine converts the rhythmic shifts from the moving heart wall into sound. No needles, no cutting; speed, measured by echo.

Point the same idea at the night sky. Light is a wave, so motion shifts it too: a source rushing away has its waves stretched toward the red end of the spectrum — a red shift. Every element stamps a fingerprint pattern into light, so when astronomers find hydrogen's pattern in a galaxy's glow shifted bodily toward the red, the verdict is immediate: that galaxy is moving away. And the discovery that rewrote the sky: nearly every galaxy is red-shifted, and the more distant the galaxy, the harder it recedes. Everything is leaving — not fleeing from some centre, but carried apart as space itself stretches, like raisins separating in rising bread dough, each one watching all the others drift away.

So one pavement observation scales across the universe: the sagging siren, the racing blood cell, the retreating galaxy — one effect at three sizes. The last questions of the lesson are yours now — bounce a wave off it, read the shift, and anything moving will tell you its speed.
