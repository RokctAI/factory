Hey, it's Mandy — energy conservation looks like a topic with a hundred formulas, but I promise you it is one equation and one question, asked over and over.

The question first: for every force in the problem, ask "banker or toll collector?" Gravity is the honest banker — it charges for height and repays every joule on the way down, whatever route you took. Friction, air resistance, engines and winches are the toll collectors and donors — they take energy out or pay it in, and none of it comes back on its own.

Then the equation: W n c equals delta E k plus delta E p. Four numbers, always the same four — kinetic and potential energy at the start, kinetic and potential at the end. Pick your zero level at the lowest point so the heights stay positive, form the two changes as final minus initial, add them, and read the sign like a bank statement. Negative means friction collected; positive means something paid in; zero means the perfect frictionless trade.

Two distances, and this is the trap the markers love: friction bills the PATH — the metres along the ramp — while gravity bills the HEIGHT — the vertical rise only. Never swap them.

And power is just speed of payment: work divided by time, or force times speed for anything cruising at constant velocity. Same bill, different pace.

That is genuinely the whole topic: one question, four numbers, one sign, two distances. Go and run the practice questions with that checklist and watch it click. You've got this!
