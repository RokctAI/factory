Today we study the chemistry of HOW FAST. We will define reaction rate as change in amount or concentration per unit time and calculate it straight from balance readings, then let collision theory explain every factor that changes a rate — nature of the reactants, surface area, concentration, temperature and catalysts — always ending at effective collisions per second. We will tour the standard measurement techniques, from gas syringes to the vanishing cross, and learn to read any rate graph in three words: steepness for speed, bend for tiring, plateau for extent. The session closes with the molecular energy distribution curve, where temperature swells the reacting tail and a catalyst moves the activation energy line itself — the picture behind this topic's hardest explanations.
