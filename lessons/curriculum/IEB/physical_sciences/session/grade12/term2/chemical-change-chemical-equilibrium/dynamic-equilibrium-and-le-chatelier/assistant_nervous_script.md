Hey, it's Mandy — equilibrium feels slippery because nothing seems to happen, and yet the questions keep asking what WILL happen. Let me hand you the three pictures that make the whole topic solid ground.

Picture one, the shop: shoppers stream in and out at matching rates, so the crowd inside never changes. That is dynamic equilibrium — constant concentrations, non-stop two-way traffic. Two guard rails: constant never means equal, and the reaction has NOT stopped. Say both, every time, and the definition credit is yours.

Picture two, the stubborn see-saw: every disturbance is a passenger landing on one end, and the system always leans back against it — partly, never completely. Extra reactant? Lean toward products. Heat? Write heat into the equation as a chemical — product side if exothermic — and treat it like any other passenger. Pressure squeeze? Count gas moles and lean toward the smaller count. And the catalyst just greases the pivot: balance comes sooner, the tilt never changes, the yield never improves.

Picture three, the referee: K c, products over reactants, coefficients as exponents. The calculation is always the same four lines — in, change, remain, divide by the volume. That last division is the one everybody forgets under pressure, so circle it in your working. Big score, products winning; tiny score, barely started. And the referee listens to temperature alone — nothing else ever moves K c.

Shop, see-saw, scoreboard. Three pictures, and every question in this topic is one of them wearing a costume. Go work through the practice questions and watch the costumes fall off. You've got this!
