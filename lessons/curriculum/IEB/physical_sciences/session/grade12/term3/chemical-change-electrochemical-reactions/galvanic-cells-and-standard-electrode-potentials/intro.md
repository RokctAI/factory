Today we are opening electrochemistry with the galvanic cell — a spontaneous redox reaction sawn in half, with the electrons forced to travel through a wire to complete the trade. That trick is every battery ever built. We will drill the redox vocabulary until oxidation, reduction and the two agents come without thinking, then assemble the zinc-copper cell piece by piece, ending with the salt bridge and its two-clause job: complete the circuit, keep both beakers neutral. From there we learn to read the table of standard electrode potentials against the hydrogen electrode's agreed zero, and we close with cell notation and the emf calculation — cathode minus anode, brackets on every negative — the steadiest marks this topic offers.
