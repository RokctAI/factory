Hi, Mandy here — and if electrolysis feels like a lot, here is the secret: it is the most honest topic in electrochemistry. Nothing hides. The battery is drawn right there in the diagram, and once you spot it, you know exactly what kind of cell you are in.

So make that your opening move every single time: find the power source. Power source present means electrolytic — electricity forcing the chemistry. Then lay down the two rules that never move: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode. In this cell the anode is the positive electrode, and the ions do the obvious thing — anions to the anode, cations to the cathode. The words rhyme so you cannot lose them.

The applications are three little pictures, not three chapters. Copper chloride: copper crusts the negative rod, chlorine stings at the positive one, the blue-green colour drains away. Plating: object on the negative, bar of the coating metal on the positive, bath of its ions — and the bar refills the bath as it dissolves. Refining: dirty slab shrinks, clean sheet grows, treasure drops to the floor.

Brine is one idea: an auction at each electrode, with water bidding at both. Water beats sodium at the cathode — hydrogen, never sodium metal — and concentrated chloride beats water at the anode, giving chlorine. Products: chlorine, hydrogen, sodium hydroxide, one use each.

And if the signs ever swim in front of you, just ask: where are electrons being taken away? That is the anode. Everything else unfolds from there. Now go do the practice questions — you are more ready than you feel!
