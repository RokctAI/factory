Hey, it's Mandy — I know electrochemistry looks like a wall of jargon and signs, but the galvanic section really stands on four small stones, and you can hold all four in one hand.

Stone one: OIL RIG. Oxidation is loss, reduction is gain, and the agents are job titles — the oxidising agent takes electrons and is itself reduced. Say "the agent does the opposite of its name" three times and that trap never catches you again.

Stone two: one picture. The zinc-copper cell — zinc dissolving at the negative anode, copper plating at the positive cathode, electrons walking the wire from zinc to copper, and the salt bridge doing its two-clause job: completing the circuit and keeping both beakers neutral. Draw it once from memory tonight; that single sketch answers half the section.

Stone three: the table. Every number is just a comparison with hydrogen's zero. Higher potential grabs the electrons and takes the cathode — that one sentence decides every "will it react?" question.

Stone four: the formula. Cathode minus anode, with the anode's value in brackets, sign and all. Zinc-copper gives 0,34 minus negative 0,76, which is 1,10 volts. If your answer comes out negative, don't panic — it just means your labels are swapped. Swap them back and move on.

That's genuinely the whole topic: a mnemonic, a picture, a comparison and a subtraction. Go try the practice questions now, one stone at a time — you have everything you need!
