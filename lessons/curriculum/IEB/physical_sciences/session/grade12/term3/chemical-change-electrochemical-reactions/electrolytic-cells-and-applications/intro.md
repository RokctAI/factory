Today we are studying electrolytic cells — the cells where chemistry is conscripted rather than volunteering, with a power source forcing redox reactions that would never run on their own. We will set the electrolytic cell against the galvanic cell it mirrors, then take apart the model demonstration, the electrolysis of copper chloride solution, one electrode at a time. Then the applications: silver-plating an object atom by atom, refining smelted copper to the purity electrical cable demands, and the electrolysis of concentrated brine, which turns salt water into chlorine, hydrogen and caustic soda — three industries from one cheap input. Carry a single compass the whole way: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode, in every cell, without exception.
