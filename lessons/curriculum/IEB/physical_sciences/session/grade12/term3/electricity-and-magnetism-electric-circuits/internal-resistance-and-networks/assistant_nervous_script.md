Hey, it's Mandy — internal resistance looks like Grade 11 circuits wearing a disguise, and honestly, that is exactly what it is. One new idea, one new symbol, and everything else you already know.

The new idea: every battery hides a small resistor inside itself, called r. It charges a toll on every ampere that passes — current times r — and what survives the toll is the terminal voltage. That gives you the only new equation of the topic: emf equals I times R external plus I times r. Say it as a budget: promise equals delivery plus toll. When no current flows there is no toll, so an idle battery shows its full emf. That single sentence answers every open-switch question ever set.

For networks, trust the routine and nothing else. One: collapse — parallel clusters first, product over sum for pairs, then add the series spine. Two: solve the loop with the battery's r included. Three: walk back in, voltages then branch currents. Four: audit — branch currents must add up to the main current. If the audit balances, breathe; your answer is almost certainly right.

Graphs: intercept is the emf, gradient is negative r. Explanations: the four-link chain — resistance down, current up, lost volts up, terminal voltage down — one mark per link, in order.

Sanity checks are your safety net: parallel answers undercut their smallest branch, and budgets must balance to the volt. Now go open the practice questions and run the routine twice — by the second one it will feel mechanical, because it is. You've got this!
