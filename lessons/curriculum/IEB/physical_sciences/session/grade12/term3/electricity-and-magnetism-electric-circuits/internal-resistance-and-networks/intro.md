Today we are working on internal resistance and resistor networks — the physics behind a torch cell that reads full voltage yet barely lights the bulb, and behind headlights that droop the moment a car cranks. Every real battery resists its own current, so the emf its chemistry promises is never quite the terminal voltage it delivers, and one energy-budget equation governs the difference. We will rebuild series and parallel combinations until the collapse of any network is automatic, define emf, internal resistance and lost volts properly, then run the full routine on a mixed circuit: collapse the outside, solve the loop, walk back in branch by branch, and audit the junction. We finish with the straight-line graph whose intercept reads a battery's emf and whose gradient reads its internal resistance — a diagnosis from a single plot.
