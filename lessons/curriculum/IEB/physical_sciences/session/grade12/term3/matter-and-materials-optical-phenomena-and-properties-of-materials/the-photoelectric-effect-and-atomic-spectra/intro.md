Today we are tackling the photoelectric effect and atomic spectra — the experiment that wave physics could not survive, and the discovery that earned Einstein his Nobel Prize. Light of suitable frequency ejects electrons from a metal, and the bookkeeping of one photon paying one electron's exit price gives us the photoelectric equation and its calculations. The conceptual centre of the session is keeping the two dials apart: intensity counts the photons, frequency sizes them, and crossing those jobs is the most expensive mistake in the topic. We finish with atomic spectra, where the same photon idea turns each element's fixed ladder of energy levels into a fingerprint of light — sharp enough to name the elements in a star no one will ever visit.
