Hi, Mandy here — if the photoelectric effect feels intimidating, hold on to this: it is one equation, one club rule, and one pair of dials. That's the entire topic.

The equation: E equals W nought plus E k max. A photon arrives with energy h f, pays the metal's exit price — the work function — and the electron keeps the change as speed. Given a wavelength? Convert to frequency FIRST, with f equals c over lambda, then price the photon, then subtract. Three moves, in that order, every single time. If the photon's energy comes out smaller than the work function, stop right there and write "no emission" — that IS the answer, and it earns the marks.

The club rule: one photon, one electron, no pooling. That single sentence explains the threshold — below it, every photon is individually too poor, and no amount of brightness fixes poverty one customer at a time.

The dials: intensity counts photons, so it controls HOW MANY electrons leave per second. Frequency sizes photons, so it controls HOW FAST each electron leaves. Never let the dials swap jobs in an answer — that swap is where this topic loses most of its marks, and you now know better.

Spectra are the bonus round: fixed rungs on each element's energy ladder make fixed drops, fixed drops make fixed colours, and fixed colours are a fingerprint — bright lines from hot gas, dark lines from cool gas, same positions.

One equation, one rule, two dials, one ladder. Say them out loud once, then go straight into the practice questions while they're fresh — you are far more ready than you feel!
