Today we are mastering generators, motors and alternating current — one arrangement of coil and magnet read in two energy directions, and the origin of nearly every joule the national grid delivers. We will build the AC generator part by part, from armature to slip rings, and see how one swapped component — the split-ring commutator — turns its output into DC and, run backwards, turns the whole machine into a motor. Then the argument every examinable version of "why AC?" requires: transformers change AC voltage easily, and because transmission losses go as current squared, high-voltage low-current transport wins. We close with rms values — the DC-equivalent numbers that let a mains wave peaking at 311 volts be honestly labelled 220 — and the average power calculations built on them.
