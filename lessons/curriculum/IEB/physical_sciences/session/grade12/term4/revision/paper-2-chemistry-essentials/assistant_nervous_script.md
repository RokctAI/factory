Hey, it's Mandy — a whole year of chemistry in one sweep sounds enormous, so let me fold the map down to pocket size.

Organic is three moves in different costumes: read the name tag — longest chain, functional group, numbered branches — then count the bonds to classify the reaction, then quote the conditions, because conditions choose the product. Seats made, filled, or swapped; that question answers almost everything.

Equilibrium is a seesaw with a factory setting. The RICE table runs in moles, then one compulsory line divides by the volume before K c — give that line its own row, every time. And only temperature can change the constant itself; everything else just tilts the plank.

Acids bring the word trap: strong is the fraction ionised, concentrated is the amount dissolved — strangers, not twins — and the pH logs run both ways, acid straight down from the hydronium, base through K w first.

Electrochemistry is one machine with two gears, and the labels never move: an ox, red cat — oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode, in both cells, forever. The potentials table picks which metal wears which label, and the electrons flowing out of an electrode confirm it is the anode.
