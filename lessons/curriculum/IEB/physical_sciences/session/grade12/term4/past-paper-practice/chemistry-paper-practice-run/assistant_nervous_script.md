Hey, it's Mandy — a whole chemistry practice set in one walk is a big meal, so here is the recipe card version.

Organic is recognition: read the functional group, count the bonds, and the reaction names itself — seats swapped, filled, or created. Quote the conditions on every arrow, because conditions choose the product, and number your chains so the important group gets the smallest address.

Stoichiometry is a chain of signposts: volume to moles, ratio from the balanced equation, moles to mass, mass to percentage. Write the signposts before the arithmetic, and check that purity lands under a hundred.

The two sneaky lines that decide whole questions: divide by the volume before Kc — moles in the table, concentrations in the constant — and convert cubic centimetres to cubic decimetres before any titration mole. Give each its own written line, every time, even when it feels unnecessary.

And the cell question at the tired end of any exam runs on rails: higher reduction potential is the cathode, the other metal reverses and is the anode, E cathode minus E anode with the double negative spoken slowly — 0,80 minus, minus 1,66 — and the salt bridge keeps both solutions neutral.
