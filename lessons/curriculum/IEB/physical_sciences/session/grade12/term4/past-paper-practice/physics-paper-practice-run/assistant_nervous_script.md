Hey, it's Mandy — seven physics questions in one sitting sounds heavy, so here is the pocket version to carry with you.

Mechanics runs on one drawing: the dot with its arrows. Weight down, normal force off the surface, friction against the motion, and on a slope split the weight before anything else — sine along, cosine into. Draw it every time, even unasked; it banks marks and catches sign errors while they are still cheap.

Momentum questions are a ledger — before equals after in an isolated system — and elastic or inelastic is decided by computing kinetic energy on both sides, never by feel.

Projectiles: gravity never switches off, not even at the top where the velocity is zero. Declare which way is positive before your first substitution, and give every vector answer a direction.

Circuits go outside-in: collapse the network, include the battery's own resistance in the total, find the main current, then walk back into the branches — and check that they sum to the main current at the end.

And the photoelectric question is a script: minimum in the definition, nanometres to metres, and intensity counts photons while frequency sizes them.

Every walk in this session used physics you have already earned marks with this year. Your final exams re-dress the same patterns with new numbers. Work the practice set now, cold, and let each audit that closes stack another small win. You've got this!
