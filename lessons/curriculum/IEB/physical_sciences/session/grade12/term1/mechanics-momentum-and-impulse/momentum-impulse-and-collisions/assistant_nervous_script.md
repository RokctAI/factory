Hey, it's Mandy — momentum is the opening topic of the final year, and I know it can feel like an entrance exam for whether you belong here. Deep breath. This whole topic is two formulas and one habit, and the habit matters more than either formula.

The habit: before any numbers, write your positive direction — "let east be positive", or toward the wall, or the direction the first trolley moves, whatever suits the question — and then give every single velocity its sign. Momentum is a vector. Nearly every mark lost in this topic is a missing minus sign, not missing understanding.

Formula one: F net delta t equals m delta v. Impulse equals change in momentum. Reach for it whenever ONE object speeds up, slows down or bounces, and remember delta v is final minus initial, signs included. A rebound always beats a dead stop for size of change — use that to sense-check your answer.

Formula two: total momentum before equals total momentum after. Reach for it whenever TWO objects collide, couple or spring apart and the question says to ignore friction. Add the momenta with their signs, set the totals equal, solve. If the objects stick, the after side carries the combined mass. That is the entire method.

Then the classification question, which is calm, free scoring: elastic or inelastic? Compute total kinetic energy before, compute it after, compare. Equal: elastic. Smaller after: inelastic — and say where the missing energy went: heat, sound, deformation. Never judge from the story; judge from the two numbers.

And the safety explanation, three sentences, never varying: the change in momentum is fixed; the airbag or crumple zone or bent knees increase the time over which it happens; therefore the force is smaller. Learn it like a chorus.

Signs declared, two formulas, an energy audit, three safety sentences. That is the whole topic — and you have already survived harder ones. You've got this!
