Organic chemistry is the largest chemistry topic of the final school year, and it all stands on two foundation skills: identifying which family a molecule belongs to, and moving between names and structures without error. Today we anchor the definitions the topic demands, work through the nine homologous series with their functional groups and suffixes, and turn IUPAC naming into a four-step algorithm that runs in both directions. We finish with the three types of structural isomer — chain, positional and functional — and the bent-drawing trap that fools almost everyone exactly once. Every molecule is built carbon by carbon, with the four-bond rule as a built-in error alarm on every structure.
