Knowing a molecule's name is grammar; predicting what it does next is fluency, and the organic reactions section asks one question in a hundred costumes: here is a molecule, here are the conditions — what forms, and why. Today we work the full syllabus set: combustion and esterification first, then the three reaction types that decide most questions — addition across double bonds, elimination to create them, and substitution to trade one group for another. The conditions referee everything: dilute, aqueous and mild calls the swap, while concentrated, ethanolic and hot calls the ejection. By the end of this session you will let the conditions call every reaction for you.
