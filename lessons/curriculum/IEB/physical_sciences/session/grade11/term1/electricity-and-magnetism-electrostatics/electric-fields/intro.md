Today we ask what a single charge does to the empty space around it — and the answer is the electric field, a region where any visiting charge feels a force. We define the field's direction through the shove on a positive test charge, draw the standard field-line patterns under their four rules, and master the topic's two calculations: E equals F over q at a point where a force is measured, and E equals k Q over r squared at a point near a known source, where the test charge cancels out of existence. We finish by letting two charges broadcast at once, adding their contributions like arrows — cooperating between unlike charges, competing between like ones, and cancelling entirely at the famous dead spot.
