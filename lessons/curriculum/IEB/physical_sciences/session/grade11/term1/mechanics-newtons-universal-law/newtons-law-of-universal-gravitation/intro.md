Today one equation explains a falling mango, the Moon's orbit and why a bathroom scale changes its story on another planet. We state Newton's law of universal gravitation in wording worth full marks, feel the feebleness of the constant with two learners in a classroom, master the inverse-square ratio questions where the distance always gets squared, and derive g equals G M over r squared — the line where the object's own mass cancels and every dropped thing falls alike. We close by splitting mass from weight once and for all, and by replacing the myth of zero gravity in space with the truth: astronauts float because they are falling, endlessly, around the planet with their floor falling beside them.
