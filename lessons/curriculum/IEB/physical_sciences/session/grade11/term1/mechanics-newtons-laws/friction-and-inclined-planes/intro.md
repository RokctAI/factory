Today we hunt the two forces that cause the most damage in mechanics answers: the normal force, which equals the weight far less often than people assume, and friction, which comes in a self-adjusting static form with a ceiling and a weaker kinetic form that rules once sliding starts. We then tilt the whole world onto an inclined plane, split the weight into a share pulling down the slope and a share pressing into it, and discover why grip fades as slopes steepen. The session works a full ramp problem three ways — sliding freely, dragged up at constant velocity, and tested on the point of slipping — and closes with a mass-free shortcut: compare the tangent of the angle with the coefficient of static friction.
