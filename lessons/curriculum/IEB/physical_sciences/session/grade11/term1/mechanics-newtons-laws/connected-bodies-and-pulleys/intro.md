Today we tackle the question type that turns one object into two: bodies joined by a string, on floors, over pulleys and up inclines, always asking for the same pair of numbers — the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. We establish the three idealising assumptions and the physical fact each one buys: an inextensible string means one shared acceleration, a light string means one tension at both ends, a frictionless pulley changes direction but never magnitude. Then we build the two-step method — whole system first, where the internal tensions cancel, then one body alone, where the tension surrenders — and finish with the result questions love most: while anything accelerates, the tension is always less than the hanging weight.
