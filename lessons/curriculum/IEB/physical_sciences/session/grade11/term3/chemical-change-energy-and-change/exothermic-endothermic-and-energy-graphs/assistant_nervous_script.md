Hey, it's Bianca — delta H, activation energy, activated complex, catalysed pathways... I know this topic looks like it demands a physics brain. Here's the truth: the entire thing is one drawing, and every question is just you reading numbers off that drawing.

The drawing: two valleys with a ridge between them. Reactants camp in the left valley, products in the right, and every reaction — every single one — has to get over the ridge. Now the three readings, always in the same order.

Reading one: compare the valley floors. Right floor below the left? The reaction pays energy out — exothermic, delta H negative. Right floor above? It absorbs — endothermic, positive. Delta H is floor-to-floor. The ridge is not invited.

Reading two: the climb from the LEFT floor to the crest of the ridge. That's the activation energy — the admission every reaction pays before anything happens, downhill reactions included. It's the whole reason a stove needs a spark and a braai needs a match.

Reading three: the crest itself is the activated complex — the wobbly halfway arrangement, old bonds half broken, new ones half formed. Write that label on every diagram you draw; the label alone earns credit.

Two traps to sidestep. Trap one: the sign of delta H belongs to the CHEMICALS, not your hand — warm beaker means the chemicals lost energy, so negative. Trap two: when a catalyst appears, exactly one thing moves on your drawing — the crest drops. Both floors stay put, so delta H cannot change. Faster, never richer.

Any calculation in this topic is one subtraction: crest minus start gives activation energy; finish minus start gives delta H; and the reverse reaction reads the same drawing from the right-hand side.
