Hey, it's Bianca — limiting reagents, yields, purity, and a steering wheel that explodes politely... it sounds like three new topics stacked on last year's stoichiometry. Truth: it's the SAME journey you already know, with three small checkpoints added, and each checkpoint is one extra written line.

The journey never changes: grams to moles, ratio across the equation, moles back to whatever's asked. Write those three legs for every question and the new material just clips on.

Checkpoint one, limiting reagent — it only appears when a question supplies TWO amounts. Routine: both amounts to moles, then the ratio test. Pick one reactant, calculate how much partner it demands, check the shelf. Whoever can't cover the demand is limiting, and it alone builds the products. Never trust the smaller number: twenty lemons lose to six cups of sugar when the recipe eats lemons four at a time.

Checkpoint two, percentage yield: actual over theoretical, times one hundred. The actual is the measured number the question hands you; the theoretical is the ceiling YOU calculated. Answer above one hundred? Don't celebrate — either you divided upside down, or the question wants you to say "wet or contaminated product".

Checkpoint three, percentage purity: mass of pure substance over mass of sample, times one hundred — where the pure mass comes from working BACKWARDS from a measured product. Impurities never react, so they never enter the moles. That one sentence is the entire trick.

The airbag question? The same journey wearing a seatbelt: equation, grams to moles, two-to-three ratio, times 22,4 for the volume. Nothing in it is new tonight.

Three legs, three checkpoints, and a mass audit at the end when there's time — in equals out, always. Now open the practice questions and take them one written line at a time. You've got this!
