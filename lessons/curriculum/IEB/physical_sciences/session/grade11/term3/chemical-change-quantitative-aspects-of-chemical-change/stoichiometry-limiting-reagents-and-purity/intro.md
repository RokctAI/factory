Today the mole-ratio journey grows up. We keep the three legs you know — mass to moles, ratio across the equation, moles back to mass — and add the three complications real chemistry never escapes: the reactant that runs dry first, the product harvest that undershoots the prediction, and the raw material that arrives adulterated. We work the limiting reagent ratio test in full, master percentage yield and percentage purity, and end inside a car airbag, where a palm-sized pellet of sodium azide becomes fifty cubic decimetres of nitrogen in milliseconds. One thread throughout: the balanced equation promises in moles, and every complication is measured against that promise.
