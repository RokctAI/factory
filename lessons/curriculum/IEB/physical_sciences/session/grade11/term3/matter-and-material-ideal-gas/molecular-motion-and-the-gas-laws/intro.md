A gas is an uncountable crowd of molecules in perpetual random flight, and everything a gas does — the stiffness of a tyre, a balloon swelling in the sun, the sigh of a fizzy drink opening — follows from that crowd's behaviour. Today we assemble the kinetic model, define the ideal gas and the two conditions where real gases abandon it, and extract Boyle's law from the standard practical — constant product, molecular explanation and all three graphs. We finish with the general gas equation and the ideal gas law, p V equals n R T, the one line that counts invisible molecules with a pressure gauge and a thermometer. Kelvin throughout: every gas equation accepts kelvin and nothing else.
