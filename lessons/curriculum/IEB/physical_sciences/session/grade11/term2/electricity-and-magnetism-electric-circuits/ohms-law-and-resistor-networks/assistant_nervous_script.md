Hey, it's Bianca — a circuit diagram can look like a bowl of spaghetti: resistors scattered everywhere, values everywhere, and a single battery expecting you to make sense of it all. Here is what the strongest answers all have in common: every circuit question is the same four-move walk, and the walk never changes.

Move one, shrink. Fold each parallel pair into one number — product over sum for two resistors — then add whatever lies in series. Keep folding until the battery faces a single resistance. Move two, total current: battery volts divided by that single number. Move three, spend the volts: travel the loop, paying each series resistor its share, current times resistance, and whatever survives lands across the parallel section — the same voltage for every branch inside it. Move four, split the amps: each branch takes the shared voltage divided by its own resistance.

Then audit your work, because the audits catch every slip. Branch currents must add up to the current that reached the junction. Voltages around the loop must add up to the battery. A parallel total must come out SMALLER than its smallest branch — if yours did not, you forgot the final flip of the fraction. Half a minute of checking turns a hopeful answer into a certain one.

Keep the two sharing rules on a mental index card: series shares current, parallel shares voltage. An enormous number of theory marks are that one sentence.

For the money questions, convert before you multiply: watts into kilowatts, minutes into hours, then kilowatts times hours times tariff. If a kettle prices out like a plane ticket, a conversion went missing — step back one line and find it.

Shrink, total, spend, split, audit. Say those five words at the top of every circuit question and use them as your headings. The spaghetti untangles the moment you walk it in order. You've got this!
