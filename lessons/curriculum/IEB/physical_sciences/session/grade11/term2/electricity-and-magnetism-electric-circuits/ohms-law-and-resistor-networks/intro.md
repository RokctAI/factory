Today we are joining emf, potential difference and current into the one equation that carries the whole of electricity — Ohm's law — and then setting it loose on complete resistor networks. We will state the law with its constant-temperature condition, meet the glowing filament that escapes it, and lock in the two combination rules: a plain sum for series, the reciprocal sum with its final flip for parallel. The centrepiece is a four-move routine for any mixed circuit — shrink the network, find the total current, spend the volts, split the amps — backed by the balance checks that expose a slip before it costs you. We end with power and energy, turning kilowatts and hours into the rands on a household electricity account.
