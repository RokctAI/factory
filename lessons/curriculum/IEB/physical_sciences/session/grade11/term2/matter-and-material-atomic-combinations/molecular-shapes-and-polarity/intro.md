Today we are answering the question all of chemistry opens with: why do atoms bond in the first place, and what shapes do the resulting molecules take? We begin with the energy hollow that makes the covalent bond worthwhile, build Lewis diagrams for the molecules the syllabus names, and then let electron-pair repulsion hand us every shape — linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, pyramidal and angular, with the shrinking bond angles that make such a rewarding comparison. The session closes with electronegativity, the number that decides whether a bond shares fairly, shares unfairly, or gives up sharing entirely — plus the crucial twist that a molecule full of polar bonds can still be non-polar when its shape cancels them. Every later chemistry topic stands on this one.
