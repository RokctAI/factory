# Part 1 — Expert

This session is the physics revision sweep for the final examination — the year's mechanics, fields and electricity rebuilt as four working toolkits: the free-body method that dismantles every force problem, the two inverse-square laws that run gravity and electrostatics, the resistor network drill, and the electromagnetism chain from field to flux to induced emf. Nothing in it is new. Everything in it is the exam-day version — compressed, put in working order, and drilled through fresh worked examples chosen to spring the classic traps while springing them still costs nothing.

## Subtopic: Vectors and Newton's Laws — the Free-Body Method

Every force problem the year can pose surrenders to the same five-step method, so fix the method before the physics. Step one: draw the free-body diagram — the object shrunk to a dot, every force on it drawn as a labelled arrow. Step two: choose a positive direction and declare it. Step three: resolve any slanted force into components — F x equals F cos theta, F y equals F sin theta; an 80-newton pull at 60 degrees above the horizontal becomes 40 newtons horizontal and 69,3 newtons vertical. Step four: write Newton's second law along each axis: F net equals m a. Step five: solve, and attach units and direction.

The three laws behind the method, stated for the definition marks. First law: an object continues at rest or at constant velocity unless a net force acts on it — that resistance to change is inertia. Second law: when a net force acts, the object accelerates in the direction of the net force, with F net equals m a. Third law: when object A exerts a force on object B, B simultaneously exerts a force on A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction — and the two forces act on DIFFERENT objects, which is why they can never cancel.

Run the method. An 8-kilogram trolley on a rough floor is pulled horizontally with 60 newtons while friction opposes with 12 newtons. Free body: applied force forward, friction backward, weight down, normal force up. The vertical forces balance. Horizontal: F net equals 60 minus 12, which is 48 newtons. Acceleration: a equals F net over m, which is 48 over 8, giving 6 metres per second squared, forward.

And the third-law reading of the same scene: the trolley pulls back on the rope exactly as hard as the rope pulls the trolley. Equal, opposite, different objects — and the trolley still accelerates, because only the forces ON the trolley enter its free-body diagram.

This section's questions are with you now — the five steps in order, the three laws word for word, and the trolley's 6 metres per second squared reproduced.

## Subtopic: The Inverse-Square Twins — Gravitation and Coulomb

Two of the year's laws are one law wearing different constants, and revising them together halves the work. Newton's law of universal gravitation: every particle attracts every other with a force F equals G m one m two over r squared, with G at 6,67 times 10 to the minus 11. Coulomb's law: the force between two point charges is F equals k Q one Q two over r squared, with k at 9 times 10 to the 9.

The shared skeleton: both forces act along the line joining the centres, both multiply the two sources, and both fall away with the SQUARE of the separation. Double the distance and either force drops to a quarter; triple it, a ninth. That inverse-square reflex settles an entire family of ratio questions before the calculator wakes.

The differences hold the other half of the marks. Gravity only attracts; the electrostatic force attracts or repels, because charge comes in two signs and mass in one. And gravity is astonishingly weak — it takes a planet's worth of mass to be felt — while the electric force between modest laboratory charges dominates outright.

Work one of each. Coulomb: charges of 4 microcoulombs and 5 microcoulombs, 0,2 metres apart. F equals 9 times 10 to the 9, times 4 times 10 to the minus 6, times 5 times 10 to the minus 6, all over 0,2 squared. The numerator: 9 times 10 to the 9 times 20 times 10 to the minus 12 is 0,18. Divide by 0,04: 4,5 newtons. Both charges negative, so the force repels, along the line joining them — and by the third law each charge feels the same 4,5 newtons.

Gravitation pays out through weight: the familiar w equals m g is the universal law evaluated at the Earth's surface, where g equals G M over r squared lands at 9,8 metres per second squared. Climb to TWO Earth-radii above the surface and r has tripled, so g falls to a ninth: a 20-kilogram mass weighs 196 newtons on the ground and about 21,8 newtons up there. The mass never changed; the weight did.

Take this section's questions now — both laws stated, the ratio reflex fired, the 4,5 newtons reproduced, and mass held apart from weight.

## Subtopic: Circuits — Ohm's Law and the Network Drill

Ohm's law anchors the topic: V equals I R, at constant temperature. Series resistors add: R s equals R one plus R two. Parallel resistors combine by reciprocals: one over R p equals one over R one plus one over R two — and for exactly two resistors the shortcut is product over sum.

The favourite structure in any final exam is the mixed network, and it falls to a three-pass drill: collapse, solve, expand. Take this year's specimen: a 24-volt battery feeding a 2-ohm resistor in series with a parallel pair of 6 ohms and 3 ohms.

Pass one, collapse. The pair: product over sum is 6 times 3 over 6 plus 3, which is 18 over 9, giving 2 ohms. The circuit is now 2 plus 2 in series: 4 ohms total.

Pass two, solve the collapsed loop. Total current: I equals V over R, which is 24 over 4, giving 6 amperes from the battery, and through the series 2-ohm resistor.

Pass three, expand back. Potential difference across the series resistor: V equals I R, 6 times 2, 12 volts. That leaves 24 minus 12, which is 12 volts across the parallel pair — the same 12 volts across each branch, because parallel branches share their potential difference. Branch currents: 12 over 6 is 2 amperes; 12 over 3 is 4 amperes. Junction audit: 2 plus 4 returns the 6 amperes that entered. Every number found, every rule used exactly once.

Close with power, because power audits the whole circuit. P equals V I, or I squared R. The battery delivers 24 times 6, which is 144 watts. The components dissipate: the series resistor, I squared R, 36 times 2, 72 watts; the 6-ohm branch, 2 squared times 6, which is 24 watts; the 3-ohm branch, 4 squared times 3, which is 48 watts. Sum: 72 plus 24 plus 48 — 144 watts, meeting the battery exactly. Energy in equals energy out, and that one-line ledger certifies every current and potential difference before anyone else checks them.

This section's questions are ready — collapse, solve, expand on the full network, both audits run, and the power ledger closed.

## Subtopic: Electromagnetism — Field, Flux and Faraday

Electromagnetism is a chain of three ideas, and reciting the chain in order IS the revision. Idea one: currents MAKE magnetic fields. Around a straight current-carrying conductor the field lines are circles centred on the wire, and the right-hand rule reads them: right thumb along the conventional current, curled fingers giving the circles' direction. A coil stacks those circles into the field of a bar magnet, with a north face and a south face.

Idea two: fields are counted as FLUX. Magnetic flux is the field threading a surface: phi equals B A cos theta — field strength, times area, times the cosine of the angle between the field and the normal to the surface. Unit: the weber. Face a coil square-on to the field and theta is zero, cosine one, maximum flux: a field of 0,4 tesla through an area of 0,05 square metres gives phi equals 0,4 times 0,05, which is 0,02 webers. Swing the coil edge-on and the flux is zero — same field, same coil, nothing threading through.

Idea three: CHANGING flux makes emf. Faraday's law: the induced emf equals N delta phi over delta t — the number of turns, times the rate of change of flux. Not the flux itself — its CHANGE. A coil parked in the strongest steady field on Earth induces nothing; wave a cheap magnet near a coil and current flows. Work it: a coil of 200 turns threads 0,02 webers, and the magnet is pulled clear in 0,5 seconds. Emf equals 200 times 0,02 over 0,5, which is 200 times 0,04, giving 8 volts. Pull the magnet clear in a quarter of a second instead and the emf doubles to 16 — the speed of the change is everything.

The minus sign in Faraday's law is Lenz's law, and it supplies the direction: the induced current flows so that its own magnetic field OPPOSES the change in flux that produced it. Push a north pole toward a coil and the near face becomes a north to push back; withdraw it and the face flips to south to cling. Energy conservation demands the opposition — an induced current that assisted the change would be a machine for free energy.

The final questions of this part are with you now — the chain recited in order, the flux at three angles, the 8 volts reproduced, and Lenz's opposition argued from energy.

## Subtopic: Invisible Springs and the Thinning Spray

Start with what a force diagram really is: an honesty exercise. The object sits alone on the page, and the only arrows permitted are forces that something ELSE exerts on IT — the Earth pulling down, the floor pushing up, the rope pulling forward, the roughness dragging back. No arrow for "its motion", no arrow for anything it does to other objects. Most force errors are smuggled arrows, and the dot-alone-on-the-page picture is the smuggling check at the border.

Newton's third law loses its spookiness the moment you see every force as a two-ended spring. A spring never pushes at one end only — compress it and BOTH ends push outward; stretch it and both ends pull inward. Every force in nature is such a spring between two objects: the Earth pulls the dropped phone and the phone pulls the Earth, with exactly equal force. The phone does all the visible moving because it is absurdly lighter — same spring, wildly different masses hanging on its two ends.

Now the inverse-square picture. Set a garden sprinkler in the middle of a lawn, spraying equally in all directions. Close in, the grass is drenched; at double the distance the same water crosses four times the area, so the soaking is a quarter as heavy; at triple, a ninth. Gravity and the electric force spread their influence exactly like that spray — the same total through every sphere, thinning with the square of the radius. That is all "inverse square" means, and it is why doubling a distance never merely halves these forces; it quarters them.

The field idea rides the same picture: a field map is the soaking chart of the lawn — at every point, how hard the spray would hit whatever stood there. Gravitational field strength promises newtons per kilogram placed there; electric field strength promises newtons per coulomb. The map exists before any visitor arrives.

Your questions for this section are ready — smuggled arrows caught, both spring ends named, and the spray thinned at two distances.

## Subtopic: The Delivery Network

A circuit is a delivery network, and every electrical quantity holds a job in it. The battery is the depot, and its emf is the energy loaded onto each coulomb of cargo as it leaves. Current is the traffic — coulombs passing per second. Resistors are the delivery stops, where the cargo's energy is unloaded as heat and light. Potential difference across a stop is the energy each coulomb drops there. The trucks are never consumed; the cargo is — which is why charge circulates endlessly while the battery still runs flat.

Series is a single route through every stop in turn. The same traffic passes each stop — same current everywhere. Each stop takes its share of the cargo, and the shares add back to the full load — potential differences sum to the emf. More stops on the one route means slower traffic overall — resistances add.

Parallel is a fork into side streets that rejoin. Each side street runs from depot-side to return-side directly, so every branch is offered the FULL load per coulomb — the same potential difference across each branch. The traffic divides at the fork, more trucks taking the easier street, and merges again beyond it — currents add at the junction. And opening any extra street eases the total flow, however narrow the street — which is why a parallel combination always comes out below its smallest branch.

Power is simply the delivery rate: energy unloaded per second, P equals V I. And the network audit is common sense made rigorous: every joule per second leaving the depot must be dropped at some stop — 144 watts out of the battery, 72 plus 24 plus 48 dropped across the three stops. If the ledger will not close, some number upstream is wrong, and the ledger has just found it for you.

Take this section's questions now — every quantity matched to its job, both route patterns argued, and the delivery ledger closed.

## Subtopic: The Trap Museum and the Last Check

Five exhibits, each labelled with the mark it destroys, each with its antidote.

Exhibit one: mass confused with weight. Mass is the amount of matter, in kilograms, identical everywhere in the universe; weight is the gravitational force on that matter, w equals m g, in NEWTONS, changing with location. Antidote: let the unit police the answer — any weight quoted in kilograms is already wrong.

Exhibit two: the unsquared distance. In both inverse-square laws r is squared; dividing by 0,2 instead of by 0,2 squared inflates the Coulomb answer fivefold. Antidote: square the distance before anything else touches the calculator, and run the sanity ratio — double distance, quarter force — over the final number.

Exhibit three: the prefix slip. Microcoulombs are 10 to the minus 6, nanocoulombs 10 to the minus 9, and one missed prefix shifts an answer by three powers of ten. Antidote: convert every quantity to base units in a written line BEFORE substituting.

Exhibit four: series and parallel formulas swapped — reciprocals applied to a series chain, or a plain sum applied to a parallel pair. Antidote: ask the junction question — no junction, plain sum; a junction, reciprocals — and remember the parallel result must land SMALLER than the smallest branch, which exposes the swap on sight.

Exhibit five: flux confused with change of flux. A coil resting in a steady field induces nothing, however mighty the field; Faraday pays only for delta phi over delta t. Antidote: before writing the formula, name what is changing — the field, the area, or the angle. If nothing changes, the emf is zero and the question was a definition check in disguise.

And the last habit, the one that turns near-marks into full marks: every toolkit in this lesson closes its own books. Free-body answers: confirm the net force's direction matches the acceleration. Inverse-square answers: fire the ratio reflex. Networks: junction currents sum, potential differences rebuild the emf, and the power ledger closes on the battery's output. An answer that survives its own audit is not a hope; it is a certainty, sealed before anyone else reads it.

The final questions of the lesson are with you now — five exhibits dodged, and every answer sealed by its own audit.
