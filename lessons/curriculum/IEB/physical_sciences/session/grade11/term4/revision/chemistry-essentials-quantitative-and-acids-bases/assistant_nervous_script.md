Hey, it's Bianca — quantitative chemistry plus acids and bases in one revision sweep sounds enormous, but the entire thing runs on three pictures you already understand from ordinary life.

Picture one: the currency exchange. Grams, solution volumes, gas volumes — none of them talk to each other directly. Everything trades through one currency, the mole, and the rates are printed on things you are given: molar mass from the periodic table, c equals n over V for solutions, 22,4 cubic decimetres per mole for any gas at STP. Into moles, do the chemistry, out of moles. That one discipline carries most of the calculation marks you will ever be offered.

Picture two: the pancake recipe. The limiting reagent is not a monster — it is checking which ingredient runs out first. Pick a reactant, ask how much partner it demands through the balanced equation, compare with the shelf. Whichever falls short does ALL the talking afterwards, and the excess just sits there like spare flour.

Picture three: the rugby pass. The acid passes the proton, the base receives it, and a conjugate pair is the same player one ball lighter or heavier. Strong versus weak is how completely the ball gets released; concentrated versus dilute is how many players are on the field. Keep those two axes apart and half the theory marks are already banked.

And the traps have names now: kelvin not Celsius, cubic decimetres not cubic centimetres, the ratio step in every titration, and never calculating from the excess reagent. None of this is new — you did all of it this year. Revision is reminding your hands, not learning fresh. Open the practice set, name the picture each question wears, run the check, and stack the wins. You've got this!
