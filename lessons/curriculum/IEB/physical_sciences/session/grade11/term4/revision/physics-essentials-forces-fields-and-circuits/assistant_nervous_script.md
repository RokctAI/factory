Hey, it's Bianca — a whole year of physics squeezed into one revision lesson looks like a cliff face, but walk closer and it is four small hills, each with a well-marked path.

Hill one: forces. One method answers everything — the object as a lonely dot, honest arrows only, components if anything slants, then F net equals m a along each axis. Most force marks are lost to smuggled arrows, not to hard physics, so let the lonely dot keep you honest.

Hill two: the inverse-square twins. Gravitation and Coulomb share one skeleton with different constants: multiply the two sources, divide by the distance SQUARED. Double the distance, quarter the force — that single reflex settles a whole family of questions before your calculator is even awake.

Hill three: circuits. Collapse, solve, expand. Fold the network down to one resistor, pull the total current from V equals I R, then unfold — and let the two audits certify you: branch currents summing at the junction, and the power ledger closing on the battery's output. When the ledger closes, you KNOW you are right, and that certainty travels with you to the next question.

Hill four: electromagnetism. Say the chain out loud: currents make fields, fields count as flux, CHANGING flux makes emf. Faraday only pays for change — a steady field earns zero — and Lenz always pushes back against the change that made it.

Every one of these hills is ground you have already covered this year. Revision is walking the paths again until your feet remember them without you. Open the practice set, name the hill each question sits on, run its audit, and stack the small wins one after another. You've got this!
