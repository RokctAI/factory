The chemistry year closes by completing both of its great reaction families. Acid-base chemistry trades protons, and we finish that story with the deliberate design of salts from their acid and base parents. Then we open the family that powers next year's electrochemistry — batteries, electrolysis and corrosion — the reactions that trade electrons. We build the accounting system called oxidation numbers, define oxidation and reduction exactly, name the agents without inverting them, and close with the half-reaction method for balancing any redox equation from the table of standard reduction potentials. Learn the bookkeeping now, and next year's electrochemistry becomes arithmetic you already own.
