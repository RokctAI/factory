Hey, it's Thandi — if series and parallel feel like two rule books that keep swapping pages, let me staple them down for you, because this topic is honestly just traffic.

Charge is taxis. Volts are the energy loaded into each taxi at the rank — eighteen joules per coulomb in our lesson. Ohms are roughness on the road. That is the whole vocabulary.

One road — series: every taxi drives the same route, so the traffic is the SAME everywhere; the energy gets shared out at the rough patches, biggest patch biggest share; and roughness simply adds. Three plus six is nine ohms, eighteen volts gives two amps, and the volts split six and twelve.

Two roads — parallel: every route runs between the same two corners, so every route drops the FULL eighteen volts; the traffic splits instead, with the easy road busier — six amps and three amps; and the roughness sum works upside down: a third plus a sixth is a half, then FLIP it back to two ohms. If your parallel answer ever comes out bigger than the smallest branch, you forgot the flip.

One anchor sentence kills the classic mix-up: same path, same current; same pair of junctions, same volts. Say it before every question.

And the checks are free: series volts must add back to the emf, parallel amps must add back to the total.

Now go run the 18-volt circuit in the practice questions both ways — the numbers are friendly, and the rules will finally sit still. You've got this!
