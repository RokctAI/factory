Hey, it's Thandi — if the periodic table feels like a hundred squares of stuff to memorise, here is the secret: you never memorise the table. You learn to read it, and it does the remembering for you.

Three readings cover almost everything. Reading one — the address: your row says how many floors of electrons your house has; your column says how many electrons sit on the top floor. Sulphur at row 3, column 16 means three floors, six upstairs. It works backwards too — a configuration hands you the address.

Reading two — the staircase: left of it live the metals, shiny benders and conductors that shed electrons; upper right the non-metals, dull insulators that grab electrons; on the line, the metalloids like silicon, the semiconductors inside every phone.

Reading three — the tug-of-war: every trend question is one game. Across a row, more protons pull on the same rope, so atoms shrink and grip electrons harder. Down a column, the rope lengthens and inner floors muffle the pull, so atoms grow and let go easily. Any trend answer is just "more pull" or "longer rope".

Then the four families introduce themselves: the one-marble kids of column 1, wilder downward; the careful two-marble givers of column 2; the sticker collectors of column 17, tamer downward; and the content noble gases, whose full album everyone else is chasing.

Go try the practice questions — read the address, place the staircase, feel the rope. The table will answer for you. You've got this!
