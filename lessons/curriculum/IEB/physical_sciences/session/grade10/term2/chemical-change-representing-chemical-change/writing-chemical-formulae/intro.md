Today we turn names into formulae. One law runs the whole craft — a compound must be electrically neutral, its charges summing to exactly zero — and three compounds teach every technique that serves the law: calcium chloride for the crossover method and its safety rules, ammonium sulphate for polyatomic ions and the bracket that multiplies a whole team, and iron three oxide for the Roman numeral that hides a transition metal's charge inside its name. You leave with a five-step method, a charge audit that proves every answer, and formulae solid enough to carry the equations you will balance next.
