Today we make equations tell the truth. A chemical equation with correct formulae can still lie about the atoms — claiming carbons vanished and oxygens appeared from nowhere — and balancing is the craft of fixing the counts until the equation obeys the law of conservation of mass. We work the combustion of propane from first census to final audit, learn the one commandment — change coefficients, never subscripts — the C, H, O order that keeps the awkward element for last, the doubling trick that clears fractions, and the smallest-set rule that finishes every answer. The coefficients you write today become the mole ratios that power stoichiometry next term.
