Today we answer one question three ways: how do atoms hold on to each other? Salt, wax and copper behave utterly differently because their atoms bond differently — non-metals share electron pairs to build molecules, metals hand electrons to non-metals and stack into charged crystal lattices, and metal atoms alone pool their electrons into a delocalised sea. We draw each story in Lewis diagrams, predict ion charges straight off the periodic table, balance every ionic formula to zero, explain melting points, brittleness, conduction and shine from structure alone, and close with the formula-mass arithmetic that turns any recipe of atoms into a single number the mole will soon convert to grams.
