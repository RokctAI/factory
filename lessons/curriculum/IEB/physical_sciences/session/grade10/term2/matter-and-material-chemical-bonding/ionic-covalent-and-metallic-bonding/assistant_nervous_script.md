Hey, it's Thandi — if three bond types feel like three separate chapters to memorise, relax: it is one story with two questions.

Question one: what does every atom want? A full outer shelf — eight electrons for most, two for the tiny ones. Every bond in this topic is just a plan for filling that shelf.

Question two: who is in the room? That decides the plan. Two non-metals — neither will give electrons up, so they SHARE pairs: covalent, and you get molecules like water and methane. A metal with a non-metal — one wants to dump electrons, one wants to grab, so they SWAP: ionic, and the plus and minus ions stack into a giant grid like salt. Metals alone — nobody to swap with, so everyone pools electrons into the SEA: metallic, and that loose sea is the whole reason copper conducts, bends and shines.

The properties then come free. Grid: hard to melt, shatters, conducts only when melted or dissolved. Molecules: soft, low melting, never conducts. Sea: conducts solid, bends, shines.

Two little skills finish the topic. Balance every ionic formula to zero — magnesium's plus two needs two chlorides, and sodium oxide puts the little two on the metal: Na2O. And weigh recipes with the three beats: inventory, multiply, add — so MgCl2 is twenty-four plus seventy-one, ninety-five.

One story, two questions, three pictures. Go meet the practice questions and watch every substance sort itself. You've got this!
