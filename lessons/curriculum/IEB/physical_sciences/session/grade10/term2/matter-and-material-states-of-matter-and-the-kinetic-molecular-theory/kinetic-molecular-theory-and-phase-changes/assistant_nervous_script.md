Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels like a pile of graphs and vocabulary, breathe: it is one picture, and you have been standing inside it your whole school life.

Everything is tiny particles that never stop jiggling, with little pulls between them. Assembly is a solid — everyone in rows, fidgeting on the spot. The corridor after assembly is a liquid — still packed, but sliding past each other. Break time on the field is a gas — far apart, fast, filling all the space. Which picture you get is a tug-of-war: pull winning, solid; even, liquid; jiggle winning, gas. Heating just turns the jiggle up.

The scary graph is only a staircase. Temperature climbs, then sticks flat at zero while the ice melts, climbs again, sticks flat at one hundred while the water boils, then climbs once more. The flat parts are the whole exam story: energy is still pouring in, but it is busy prising particles apart — paying off the debt of separation — not speeding them up. And the boiling step is longer because setting particles completely free is the bigger job.

Three traps, three shields. Never say "no energy is added" on a plateau — say the energy overcomes the forces. Never confuse heat with temperature — one is money moving, the other is average wealth. Never mix evaporation with boiling — surface at any temperature versus everywhere at the boiling point.

Now go walk the staircase in the practice questions, one step at a time. You've got this!
