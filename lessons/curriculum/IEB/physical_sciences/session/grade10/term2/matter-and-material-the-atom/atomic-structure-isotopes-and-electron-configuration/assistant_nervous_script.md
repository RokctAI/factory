Hey, it's Thandi — if the atom feels like a swarm of particles and rules, hold three pictures and the whole topic organises itself.

Picture one: the stadium and the pea. The atom is a stadium; the nucleus is a pea on the centre spot holding all the mass and all the plus charge; the stands are nearly empty space where the featherweight electrons live. Two numbers label any atom: Z counts the protons — the identity — and A counts protons plus neutrons, so neutrons are just A minus Z. Ions are easy bookkeeping: only electrons ever move, so run protons minus electrons and the charge falls out.

Picture two: the twins with backpacks. Isotopes are the same element wearing different neutron loads — boron ten and boron eleven, identical chemistry, different mass. The table's odd 10,8 is the crowd's weighted average, done exactly like school marks: twenty percent of ten plus eighty percent of eleven. If the average leans toward one twin, that twin outnumbers the other.

Picture three: the hotel. Electrons check in cheapest room first — 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, then the bargain 4s — two per room with opposite spins, spreading out across the p rooms before sharing. Write the register, add the little numbers, and you must get Z back. That free check catches every slip.

A pea, a pair of twins, a hotel register. Take them into the practice questions and unpack a few atoms — it turns into pure bookkeeping faster than you expect. You've got this!
