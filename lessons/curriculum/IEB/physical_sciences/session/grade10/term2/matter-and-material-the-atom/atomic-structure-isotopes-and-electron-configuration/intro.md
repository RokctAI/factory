Today we map the space where all of chemistry is decided: the atom. We meet the three particles and the experiment that found the nucleus, learn the two numbers and the notation that identify any atom, and see how ions form when electrons — never protons — are lost or gained. Isotopes explain the strange fractions printed on the periodic table through a weighted average you can compute like school marks, and the electron hotel — energy levels, s and p orbitals, the Aufbau order with its 4s quirk, and Hund's spreading rule — hands you the configuration of every element up to calcium, ending at the valence electrons that run the periodic table and bonding topics ahead.
