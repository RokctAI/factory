Hey, it's Thandi — if physical versus chemical feels like an endless list of examples to memorise, breathe out and put the list away. This whole topic is one question, asked over and over.

The question: is there a new substance? That is the entire test. Chocolate melts in your pocket — still chocolate — physical. A match burns — smoke and a black stump, the match gone for good — chemical. Every example you will ever be shown is that one question wearing a disguise, so you never memorise examples; you interrogate each one fresh.

When you are stuck, use the helper: can I get the original back by simple means — cooling, evaporating, filtering, sieving? Epsom salts climb back out of the basin when the water dries, so dissolving is physical. Nothing un-toasts blackened bread, so toasting is chemical.

Then carry the detective kit: unexpected foam, a solid appearing from two clear liquids, a colour that will not come back, heat or cold nobody added, light from the change itself. Five clues — but clues only raise suspicion. A fizzy drink bubbles and it is still physical, because that gas was already in there. The conviction always comes back to the one question.

Two traps to sidestep: energy is not the test — melting absorbs heat and stays physical — and mass is never lost, because atoms only regroup; burnt steel wool even gets heavier by borrowing oxygen from the air.

One question, one helper, five clues, two traps. Now go try the candle in the practice questions — melting at the top, burning at the wick, both at once — and feel the whole topic click into place. You've got this!
