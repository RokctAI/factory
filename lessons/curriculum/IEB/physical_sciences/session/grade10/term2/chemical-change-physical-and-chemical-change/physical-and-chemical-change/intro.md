Today we draw the line that runs through the whole of chemistry: the border between physical and chemical change, decided by one question — is a new substance formed? We work both sides of that border at particle level: melting, dissolving and grating, where the particles come through untouched, against burning, rusting and baking, where bonds break and atoms regroup into something new. You leave with the five observable signs of a chemical change, the conservation laws of mass and energy that hold on both sides, and the tricky cases that catch a memorised answer — electrolysis, the fizzy drink versus the fizzing tablet, sugar in the pan, and a candle running both changes at once.
