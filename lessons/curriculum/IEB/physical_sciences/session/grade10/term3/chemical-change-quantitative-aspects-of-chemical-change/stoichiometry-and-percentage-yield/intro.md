Today we build stoichiometry — the craft of predicting exactly how much product a reaction can deliver — and percentage yield, the honest comparison between that prediction and what the laboratory actually collects. One problem carries everything: 42 grams of magnesium carbonate heated until it decomposes into magnesium oxide and carbon dioxide, first predicting the maximum 20 grams the atoms allow, then judging the 17 grams a learner really collects. The method is a three-leg journey — mass to moles, ratio across the equation, moles back to mass — and it is the engine room of quantitative chemistry all the way to the end of Grade 12.
