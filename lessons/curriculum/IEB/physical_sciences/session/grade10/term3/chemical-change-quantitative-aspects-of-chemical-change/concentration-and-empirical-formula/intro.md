Today we build two of the sharpest tools in quantitative chemistry: concentration — how much substance is dissolved in each cubic decimetre of solution — and the empirical formula, a compound's recipe recovered from nothing but percentages. Two problems drive everything: 10,6 grams of sodium carbonate dissolved to make 200 cubic centimetres, and a compound that is 52,2 percent carbon, 13,0 percent hydrogen and 34,8 percent oxygen. Both reward exactly the same habit — moles first, everything else after — and along the way we uncover the water hiding inside an apparently dry box of washing soda crystals.
