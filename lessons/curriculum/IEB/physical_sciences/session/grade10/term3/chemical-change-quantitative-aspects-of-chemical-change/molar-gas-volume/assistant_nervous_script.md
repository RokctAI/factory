Hey, it's Thandi — if gas calculations feel like a whole new subject, here's the relief: you already know the mole journey, and this lesson only added one new door at the end of it.

The picture to keep is the courier crate: at STP, every mole of every gas — light, heavy, whatever — fills the same 22,4 litres. Multiply by 22,4 to pack moles into litres; divide by 22,4 to unpack litres back into moles. That's the entire new skill.

So the zinc problem was really the old three-step dance: 13 grams over 65 gives 0,2 moles, the equation's one-to-one ratio carries it across, and 0,2 times 22,4 gives 4,48 litres of hydrogen. Mass to moles, ratio across, crates at the exit.

And when EVERYTHING in a question is a gas, you get the lazy shortcut: read the coefficients as litres directly. Two of this plus one of that gives two of the other — done.

Head into the practice questions with two checks: who's in excess, and did the 22,4 stay at the last step where it belongs. The crates never change size, and neither does the method. You've got this!
