Today we put the molar gas volume to work inside stoichiometry — measuring chemistry not with a balance but with the space a gas fills. One problem drives everything: 13 grams of zinc reacting completely with excess hydrochloric acid, and the volume of hydrogen it releases at STP. Underneath it sits Avogadro's astonishing law — one mole of any gas fills the same 22,4 cubic decimetres at standard conditions — and beside it runs the favourite shortcut of every assessor: reading gas volumes straight off a balanced equation with almost no arithmetic at all.
