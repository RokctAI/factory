Today we build kinetic energy — the energy an object carries because it is moving — and the formula that prices it: E k equals a half m v squared. One problem carries everything: an 800 kilogram bakkie at 15 metres per second, holding 90 000 joules, and then the same bakkie with its speed doubled to 30. That second answer is the most important road-safety fact in the whole physics course, because the square in the formula means double the speed is four times the energy — four times the braking distance, four times the wreckage. Master the square and both the marks and the message are yours.
