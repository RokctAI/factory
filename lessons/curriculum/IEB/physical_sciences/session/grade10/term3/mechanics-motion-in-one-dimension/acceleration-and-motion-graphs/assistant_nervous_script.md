Hey, it's Thandi — if graphs make your brain fog up, breathe: this whole topic is honestly just two reading rules, and you already know the geometry.

Rule one: steepness. On a velocity-time graph, the slope is the acceleration — climbing means speeding up, level means cruising, falling means braking. You can literally read the driver's feet off the shape. Rule two: area. The space under the graph is the distance — triangles and rectangles, half-base-times-height and length-times-width. That's Grade 4 maths cashing in Grade 10 marks.

And keep the golden correction taped to your brain: height on that graph means how FAST, never how far. A falling line is a bus slowing down, not rolling backwards.

Here's your move for the practice questions: sketch the silhouette first, every time — ramp, plateau, ramp. Label the numbers, read the slopes, slice the areas. Our bus gave 2,5, zero, minus 1,5, and 570 metres, all from one drawing. Yours will surrender just as easily. Go draw your way to the marks — you've got this!
