Hey, it's Thandi — if six new words in one lesson felt like a lot, here's the secret: it's really only two pictures doing all the work.

Picture one: the odometer. It counts every metre you actually cover — that's distance, and divided by time it's speed. Picture two: the pigeon. It flies straight from your finish back to your start — that's displacement, and divided by time it's velocity. Every single question in this topic is just asking "odometer or pigeon?" That's it. Two instruments, and you already understand both.

The other habit is one sentence long: write "let north be positive" before you touch a number. Five seconds, and every plus and minus afterwards behaves itself.

Try this right now: our walk was 60 metres north, 20 metres south, 80 seconds. Say the four answers out loud — 80 metres, 40 metres north, 1,0 metres per second, 0,5 metres per second north. Felt the pattern? Then you're ready for the practice questions. Go collect those marks — you've got this!
