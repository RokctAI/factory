Today we build the working instrument of all mechanics: the force diagram, and its sharper cousin the free-body diagram. One problem anchors the hour — an 8 kilogram box dragged at constant velocity across a rough floor by a strap at 25 degrees — and from it we will name the weight, the normal force, the applied force and the friction, then read the physics straight off the arrows. The prize insight is the one memorisers miss: because the strap helps lift, the normal force here is less than the weight of 78,4 newtons. Every Newton's-laws question through Grade 11 and beyond begins with this drawing done correctly.
