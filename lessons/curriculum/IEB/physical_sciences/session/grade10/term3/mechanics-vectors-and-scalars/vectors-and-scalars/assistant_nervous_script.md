Hey, it's Thandi — if the word "vector" is making your stomach drop, take a breath: this is honestly the kindest topic in the whole physics course, and the one that pays you back the longest.

Everything reduces to a single question you already ask in real life: "does this need a which-way to make sense?" The price of airtime? No — scalar. A shove on a gate? Obviously yes — which way?! — vector. You have used that instinct your entire life; the lesson only gave it a name.

The adding part is primary-school arithmetic wearing signs. Pick a direction, call it plus, make the opposite minus, add, and read the sign at the end. That is the entire machine. The one genuine trap is writing a number without its direction — so keep the little chant going: magnitude AND direction, every time.

Physics speaks vector language from here to the end of Grade 12, so every minute you spend now earns interest for years. Head into the practice questions — classify with the direction test, add with the signs — and feel how fast it stops being scary. You've got this!
