Today we build the equations of motion — the four-equation toolkit that describes anything moving with constant acceleration in a straight line — and, more importantly, the rule that selects the right equation first time. Each of the four equations is missing exactly one of the five motion quantities, so the winning habit is to find the quantity that is neither given nor wanted and pick the equation that excludes it. One problem carries the hour: a bakkie accelerating uniformly from 8 to 20 metres per second over 210 metres, where we choose the equation, calculate the acceleration of 0,8 metres per second squared, and then find the time by two independent roads that must agree.
