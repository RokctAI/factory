Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels blurry because it is "not really chemistry, not really geography", let me hand you the map. There are only four stations, and you have already visited three of them this year.

Station one: the inventory. One bucket, the whole planet — nearly all salty, most of the fresh part frozen, tablespoons left to share. Know the three states, know where groundwater and permafrost hide, and know that under one percent is actually reachable.

Station two: the round trip. One drop — evaporates, condenses, rains, soaks, dissolves rock underground, gets drunk by a plant or a person, returns to the sea carrying salts. That single story answers almost every process question, including why the sea is salty.

Station three: the dam debate. Never pick a side and shout. Benefits with reasons, costs with reasons, then weigh them — and name the study methods: interview the elders, read the literature, compare upstream with downstream.

Station four: the chemistry checkpoint. Settle, filter, disinfect — in that order, big to small to alive — and remember the one thing nothing removes: dissolved salts. The ion tests are the same drill you already know from mystery bottles: acid, silver, barium.

That is the entire topic — four stations, one drop of water. Walk the loop once out loud, then go do the practice questions. You know more of this than you think. You've got this!
