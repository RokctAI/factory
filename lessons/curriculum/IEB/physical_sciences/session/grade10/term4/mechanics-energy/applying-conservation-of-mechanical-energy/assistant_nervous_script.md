Hey, it's Thandi — four applications in one lesson sounds like four things to fear, so here is the secret: it is ONE tool in four costumes. Two points, one equality: kinetic plus potential at the first equals kinetic plus potential at the second. That's it. That's the whole topic.

Your job in every problem is just the zero-hunt. Dropped from rest? Kinetic starts at zero. Peak of a throw? Kinetic is zero for that one instant. Bottom of the swing, on your reference level? Potential is zero. Write all four terms, write the zeros proudly with their reasons, solve for the survivor.

Two guardian habits. First: if the question GIVES you a starting speed — like a coaster crossing the crest at 4 metres per second — that kinetic energy is alive and must go in the sum. Circle the speed when you read it; forgetting it produces a wrong answer that looks scarily right. Second: check for the magic words "ignore friction and air resistance". They are your licence. Present: use the equality. Absent, with a rough surface: don't calculate — just say mechanical energy decreased and went to heat.

And enjoy the superpower: the path never matters. Steep slide, lazy flume, corkscrew — same height, same arrival speed. Only the drop counts.

Now go to the practice questions and run the zero-hunt on each one. You have ridden every example in this lesson in real life — you already know the answers in your stomach. You've got this!
