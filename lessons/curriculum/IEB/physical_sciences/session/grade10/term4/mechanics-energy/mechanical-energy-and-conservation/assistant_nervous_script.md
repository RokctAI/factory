Hey, it's Thandi — if this topic feels like formula soup, let me hand you the spoon. There is only ONE idea here, and you have used it your whole life: a wallet with two pockets.

Height pocket: m g h. Speed pocket: half m v squared. The wallet's total is mechanical energy — and while nothing rubs, the wallet is sealed. That is the entire principle. Every problem is just: count the wallet at one spot, count it at another, set the totals equal, solve for the one thing you don't know.

Keep three habits and the marks follow. One: pick your floor — where height is zero — say it out loud, and never move it. Two: write the zeros in — "at rest, so E k equals zero" is a mark, not an apology. Three: end with the audit line: top, halfway, bottom — the two pockets must always add to the same total, and if they don't, you've caught your own slip before anyone else sees it.

And the two facts that feel wrong until they feel wonderful: mass cancels, so heavy and light land at the same speed — run the numbers once and believe your own arithmetic; and friction never destroys energy, it just moves it out of the wallet as heat — touch a bike brake after stopping and you've literally felt the answer.

Now open the practice questions. Count coins, not worries — you already know how wallets work. You've got this!
