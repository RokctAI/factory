Today we assemble the most powerful bookkeeping tool in Grade 10 physics: mechanical energy — the sum of gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy — and the principle that seals its total while friction and air resistance stay away. One worked problem carries the whole method: a 4-kilogram ball leaving a rooftop 15 metres up, whose energy account we will read at the top, at halfway and at the ground without ever touching an equation of motion. Along the way we separate the universal law of energy conservation from its mechanical special case, learn why landing speed ignores mass, and collect the habits — one reference point, explicit zeros, an audit line — that bank the marks in your final exams.
