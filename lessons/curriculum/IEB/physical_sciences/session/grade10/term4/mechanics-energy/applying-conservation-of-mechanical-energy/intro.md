Today the principle of conservation of mechanical energy goes to work on its four classic playgrounds: the vertical throw, the swinging pendulum, the roller coaster, and the inclined plane. One equality — kinetic plus potential at the first point equals kinetic plus potential at the second — will lift a thrown ball to exactly 10 metres, clock a pendulum at the bottom of its arc, and tell us how fast a 500-kilogram coaster car crossing a 20-metre crest at 4 metres per second is moving at the base, all without touching a single equation of motion. Along the way we earn the topic's superpower — the path can be ignored — and learn precisely where the tool's licence expires the moment friction joins the problem.
