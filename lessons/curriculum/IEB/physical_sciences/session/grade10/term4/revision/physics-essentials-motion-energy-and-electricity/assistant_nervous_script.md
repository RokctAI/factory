Hey, it's Thandi — a whole year of physics in one revision lesson sounds heavy, so let me shrink it to the three pictures that carry everything.

Picture one, the logbook: motion. Odometer is distance, the straight home-to-destination arrow is displacement, and acceleration is just the speedometer needle moving. Three equations — and you never choose between them: list your knowns, find the missing quantity, and the equation picks itself.

Picture two, the wallet: energy. Height pocket m g h, speed pocket half m v squared, and if nothing rubs, the wallet is sealed — the total never changes, it only moves between pockets. Count it at the top, count it again lower down, and any slip exposes itself when the pockets stop adding up.

Picture three, the pipes: electricity. Battery is the pump, current is the flow, resistance is the narrow bit, potential difference is the pressure drop. Series is one loop — same current, pressure bites add. Parallel is a fork — same pressure on every branch, flows add. One question sorts every circuit ever printed: is there a junction?

And the traps now have names: g is 9,8 not 10, the speed gets squared, the ammeter sits in the loop and the voltmeter stands across. You have solved every one of these problems before — this is reminding your hands, not learning. Open the practice set, say the right picture out loud for each question, and check every answer by its second route. You've got this!
