Hey, it's Thandi — a whole year of chemistry in one revision lesson sounds impossible, so let me shrink it to something you can hold: bricks, clicks, sentences and a scale.

Bricks: atoms are bricks, and the periodic table is the parts catalogue, sorted by behaviour. A brick's column tells you its connection points — the valence electrons — and that one number drives everything else.

Clicks: there are only three ways bricks join. Swap an electron — ionic, metal plus non-metal. Share a pair — covalent, non-metal plus non-metal. Pool into a sea — metallic. Name the click and the properties recite themselves.

Sentences: equations are just sentences that must balance. Coefficients move, subscripts never — subscripts are the substance's name. Balance, then audit out loud: every element, both sides. The audit is free marks.

The scale: the mole is counting by weighing, exactly like eggs. Into moles first, do the chemistry, out of moles last — and convert those cubic centimetres to cubic decimetres before touching c equals n over V.

And keep the trap list on one page: no subscript editing, remember the diatomic seven, fix the volume units, never feed grams into ratios, and dissolving is physical — no new substance, no chemical change.

You have done every one of these skills before. Tonight is just reminding your hands. Open the practice questions, work them cold, audit every answer — and watch how much you actually know. You've got this!
