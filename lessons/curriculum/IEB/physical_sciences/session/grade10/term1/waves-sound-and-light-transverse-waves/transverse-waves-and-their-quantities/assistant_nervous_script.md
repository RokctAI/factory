Hey, it's Thandi — I saw the list of new words too. Crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency, in phase, out of phase, and a Greek letter on top. It looks like a mountain, but here is the secret: it is one picture and one multiplication, and everything else is just labels.

The picture is a wiggly line crossing a flat rest line. Four labels live on it. Height of a hump from the flat line: amplitude. Hump-top to the next hump-top: wavelength. How long one hump takes to pass: period. How many humps pass per second: frequency. That is the entire vocabulary — and period and frequency are the same fact flipped over, like saying five humps a second or 0,2 seconds each.

The multiplication is even friendlier: humps per second, times the length of each hump, gives you how far the wave travels in a second. That is v equals f lambda. You could have invented it yourself.

The traps are only bookkeeping. Turn centimetres into metres before you multiply. Measure amplitude from the flat line, never top to bottom. Write the unit next to every number so a period can never sneak in dressed as a frequency. And read the bottom axis of any graph before measuring — distance means wavelength, time means period.

One picture, four labels, one multiplication, four small habits. That is the whole topic. Go into the practice questions now while the picture is still bright in your head — you are far more ready than you feel. You've got this!
