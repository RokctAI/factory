Hey, it's Thandi — if compression and rarefaction sound like words invented to frighten you, relax. They mean squashed and stretched. That is all. This whole topic is a queue being bumped, and you have stood in that queue.

Picture it: someone at the back nudges forward, the squash travels up the line, and every person rocks and settles back into their spot. The squash is a compression. The stretched-out gap behind it is a rarefaction. People moving along the same line the squash travels — that is longitudinal. Swap people for air molecules and you have sound. One picture, whole topic.

The facts hang off that picture by themselves. No particles, no squash — so a vacuum is silent and space battles are lies. Tight queue, fast message — so sound is quickest in solids, then liquids, then gases. And your ear has three dials: how often the squashes arrive is pitch, how hard they arrive is loudness, and the special shape of a voice is quality. How often, how hard, whose shape. Say it twice and it is yours.

The only calculation trap is the echo: sound goes there AND comes back, so halve the distance before you write it down. A clap that returns after 0,9 seconds travelled 306 metres in total — the wall is at 153. Half, every time.

That is genuinely the whole lesson: one queue, three dials, one halving. Head into the practice questions now while the queue is still fresh in your mind — you are more ready than you think. You've got this!
