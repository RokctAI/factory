Hey, it's Thandi — if current, potential difference, emf and resistance have blurred into one big electric fog, stand still for a second. This topic is four ideas, and every one of them already lives somewhere in your ordinary week.

Current is the turnstile at the stadium: how many coulombs click through per second. Forty coulombs in eight seconds is five per second — 5 amps. That is the whole definition. And nobody vanishes inside a turnstile, so current is never used up — the same count flows into a bulb and out of it.

Potential difference is the ticket price: how many joules each coulomb pays as it goes through a component. Joules per coulomb — that is literally what a volt is. The battery is the pump that loads every coulomb with its parcel of energy, and emf versus terminal pd is just the pump's promise on the shelf versus its slightly smaller delivery when working, because a little energy is spent inside the battery itself.

Resistance is the crowded corridor at break: electrons bumping through a jittery crowd of ions, losing energy as heat with every bump. Longer corridor, harder. Narrower, harder. Jitterier — hotter — harder. Long, thin and hot means high resistance; short, thick and cool means low.

And the meters follow their own questions: the ammeter counts, so it stands in the line; the voltmeter compares two points, so it reaches across the component. Counter in the line, gauge across the ends — never swapped.

Four pictures: turnstile, ticket, pump, corridor. Walk into the practice questions with those four and watch this topic turn friendly. You've got this!
