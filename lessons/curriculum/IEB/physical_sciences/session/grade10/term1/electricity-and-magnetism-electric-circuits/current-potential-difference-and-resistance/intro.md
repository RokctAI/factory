Today charge starts moving, and four foundations get laid for every circuit to come. Current: the rate of flow of charge, coulombs per second, with the conventional arrow running positive to negative outside the battery while electrons drift the other way. Potential difference: the work done per coulomb, joules per coulomb, with emf as the battery's open-circuit promise and terminal pd its slightly smaller delivery under load. The meters: ammeter in series with low resistance, voltmeter in parallel with high resistance — never swapped. And resistance: electrons colliding with a vibrating lattice, turning electrical energy into heat, growing with length and temperature and shrinking with thickness. Charge is never used up; only the energy it carries is spent — hold that line and the whole topic falls into place.
