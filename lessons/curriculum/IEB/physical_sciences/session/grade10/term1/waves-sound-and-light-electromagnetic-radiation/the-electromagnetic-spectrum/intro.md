Today we meet the family of waves that needs nothing to travel through: electromagnetic radiation, born from accelerating charges as two fields that regenerate each other across empty space. Every member crosses a vacuum at the same three times ten to the eight metres per second, so the seven bands — radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma — differ only in frequency and wavelength. Two equations run all the calculations: c equals f lambda trades wavelength against frequency on a see-saw, and E equals h f prices the punch of a single photon. That photon energy, rising along the spectrum, is what separates the harmless bands from the ionising ones — and it explains every use and every danger, from the TV remote to the lead apron.
