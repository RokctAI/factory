Hey, it's Thandi — if this lesson felt like sonar, bats, dolphins AND hospitals all at once, here is the calming truth: it is one equation and one question, wearing four different costumes.

The equation is the one you already own: v equals f lambda. The only new thing is knowing who controls what. The medium owns the speed — 340 in air, about 1 500 in water. The source owns the frequency. The wavelength is just the division at the end. That is why sound crossing into water keeps its frequency but stretches its wavelength — the count cannot change, the spacing can.

The question is even simpler: did the sound come back to where it started? Ping to the seabed and back, squeak to the moth and back, clap to the cliff and back — round trip, so halve the distance. Thunder rolling from the storm to your ears — one way, halve nothing. Ask the question out loud before every calculation and the famous factor of two can never trick you.

Everything else in the lesson is those two ideas shaking hands. The bat squeaks high because high frequency buys short waves, and short waves bounce off small moths. The scanner is a bat in a hospital coat — clicks in, echoes timed, depths halved, picture built. Safe for the baby because sound is a gentle push, not the harsh cell-damaging kind of ray.

So walk in with your checklist: convert the kilohertz, ask the round-trip question, halve only journeys, never wavelengths. Now go straight to the practice questions while the checklist is warm — four costumes, same friendly physics underneath. You've got this!
