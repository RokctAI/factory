Today the sound theory goes to work. We give each symbol in v equals f lambda its owner — the medium owns the speed, the source owns the frequency, and the wavelength is whatever the division produces — and we settle what happens when sound crosses from air into water: frequency stays, speed and wavelength stretch together. Then the applications: sonar, where echo timing and the wave equation solve the same problem with two different logics; bats and dolphins, whose ultrasound wavelengths are sized to match their prey; and the hospital scanner, which sees an unborn baby safely because ultrasound is not ionising radiation. One rule stitches it all together — an echo is a round trip, so halve it; a one-way sound is not, so leave it whole.
