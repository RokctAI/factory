Hey, it's Thandi — electrostatics has a scary costume: powers of ten everywhere and a constant with nine zeros marching behind it. But strip the costume off and there are only three ideas underneath, and you can hold all three in one hand.

Idea one: only electrons move. Ever. Every single explanation in this topic is just electron traffic — gained them and you are negative, lost them and you are positive. If a sentence has protons travelling, the sentence is wrong. That one rule writes your answers for friction, contact and induction all by itself.

Idea two: charge is only ever moved, never made. So when two identical balls touch, pool the charges WITH their signs and split the pool: minus 5 and plus 1 pool to minus 4, so minus 2 each. Adding the bare sizes invents charge out of nowhere — and you can catch yourself every time by checking that your answers still add up to the starting total.

Idea three: close is strong. Bigger charges push harder, and distance is squared, so doubling the gap drops the force to a quarter — like stepping back from a braai and feeling the heat vanish faster than the step deserved.

The sums have two guard rails: turn centimetres into metres BEFORE you square, and keep the minus signs out of the formula — get your newtons, then say "attraction" or "repulsion" in words.

Three ideas, two guard rails. That is the entire topic. Go straight into the practice questions and let the first few prove it to you — this one is far friendlier than its costume. You've got this!
