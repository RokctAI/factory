Today electricity begins, with the property it all grows from: charge. We establish the two kinds of charge and its unit, the coulomb, and the two unbreakable laws — conservation, which says charge is only ever moved, never made, and quantisation, which says it moves in whole electrons of 1,6 times ten to the minus nineteen coulombs each. Then the three charging methods — friction, contact and induction — every one explained purely as electron traffic, and the sharing rule for identical spheres, where charges are added with their signs before halving. We finish with Coulomb's law, turning attraction and repulsion into newtons, and its inverse square: double the distance and the force falls to a quarter. Convert to metres, square the distance, keep the signs out of the sum — that is where this topic's marks live.
