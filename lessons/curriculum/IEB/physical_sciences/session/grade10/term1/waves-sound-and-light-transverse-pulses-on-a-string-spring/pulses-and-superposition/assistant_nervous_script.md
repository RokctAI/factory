Hey, it's Thandi — if this lesson felt like a pile of new words, pulses and superposition and two kinds of interference, take a slow breath. Underneath the vocabulary there are only two ideas, and you already own both of them.

Idea one: a pulse is a single bump running along a spring or a rope, and the rope itself stays home. Every piece just hops up and drops back while the bump and its energy travel past. One sentence covers it: energy makes the journey, the medium does not.

Idea two is literally primary-school adding, just with signs. Up-bumps are plus, down-bumps are minus, and wherever two pulses meet, the rope adds the numbers. Plus 25 and plus 15 make plus 40 — that is constructive. Plus 30 and minus 10 make plus 20 — destructive. Plus 20 and minus 20 make zero — a flat rope for one instant. That is the entire principle of superposition. No formula sheet needed.

And the two traps are easy to sidestep once you have seen them. A harder flick never makes a faster pulse — only a tighter rope does that, because speed belongs to the medium. And pulses are never destroyed by meeting — they pass through each other and come out unchanged, even after a perfect cancel. Flat is not gone.

So your answer recipe is short: write each displacement with its sign, add them for the overlap instant, then say both pulses continue unchanged. Same signs build, opposite signs cancel, everybody survives.

Now go do the practice questions while the pictures are fresh — you will be surprised how quickly they fall. You've got this!
