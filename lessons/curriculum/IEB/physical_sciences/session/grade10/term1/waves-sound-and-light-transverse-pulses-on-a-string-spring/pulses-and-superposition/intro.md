Today we take the smallest building block of the whole waves course — the pulse, a single disturbance travelling through a medium — and learn everything it can teach us. We define the transverse pulse, where the medium moves at right angles to the travel, and settle the fact that trips up half the class: the medium stays home while only the energy makes the journey. We measure amplitude properly, from the rest position, and then meet the principle of superposition — adding displacements with their signs wherever pulses overlap, building up constructively or cancelling destructively. And we hold on to the golden rule: pulses pass through each other unchanged, so even a spring that looks momentarily flat still carries both of them.
