Today we point the demand and supply machinery you already command at a wider truth: no market lives alone. A price change in one market drags demand around in another — rice and maize meal, butter and margarine, smart TVs and streaming. What happens at the tills decides what happens to jobs, through the idea of derived demand and the loop between product markets and factor markets. And the number of sellers inside a market — from forty spinach stalls at a farmers' market to the single set of water pipes under your street — decides who gets to write the price at all. Three relationships, three reliable sources of exam marks, and by the end you will read every price change as a message travelling between markets.
