Hey, Bianca here — a formula, five zones, a pile of determinants, revenue rules... if elasticity feels like a warehouse of machinery, here's the secret: it is one idea wearing four different jackets.

The one idea: when the price moves, how far do the buyers move? That's the whole thing. Stones barely move — maize meal, the ride to work, prepaid electricity. Feathers fly — ice lollies, one brand of energy drink, anything with an easy way out.

The number is just that idea measured: buying-change percent divided by price-change percent. Below one, stone. Above one, feather. And percentage change always divides by the original number — the number things started at. That one habit rescues more marks than any other in this topic.

The revenue rule? Don't memorise it cold — rebuild it from Zanele's spaza. Raise the maize meal price: everyone still buys, tin fills up. Raise the ice-lolly price: buyers drift off, tin runs down. Stone, push the price up. Feather, don't.

So open the practice questions and work the bus one and the car-wash one with your own calculator, slowly, once. After that the whole topic is the same five lines of working on repeat. You've got this.
