Today we take on globalisation: what it means for the world's economies to operate as components of a single system, the technology and policy forces that opened the two doors, and the double-edged scorecard of consequences — from the vehicle plants of Kariega to the silent footwear works of Pietermaritzburg. Then we dig beneath the scorecard to the fracture globalisation has not closed: the divide between the global North and the global South, driven by raw-for-made trade, dollar debt and unequal voice in the rooms where the rules are written. South Africa lives on both sides of that divide at once, so the evidence stays close to home — and so do the marks.
