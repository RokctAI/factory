Hey, Bianca here — systems, factors, three GDP methods, elasticity AND two policies in one sitting? Breathe. The whole session folds into four pocket-sized keys.

Key one: who reads the queue. Free market — the queue decides the menu. Planned — a far-away memo decides, and the memo can't see the queue. Mixed — queue plus inspector plus soup pot, which is every real country. Ours gets the extra word: dualistic, the restaurant strip and the paraffin stove sharing one flag. Then four envelopes: rent, wages, interest, profit — and profit is the leftover that can come up empty, which is exactly why it exists.

Key two: one event, three photographs. Every loaf sold is value produced, income earned and money spent at once — so the three GDP methods must agree. Watch the traps: grants are not G, machines are I, imports come off.

Key three: the stretch test. Chips bounce — rivals on the shelf, elastic. Taxi fare doesn't — no substitute, inelastic. The stretch predicts the till, and the vetkoek stall adds the stopping rule: fry while the next one costs less than fifteen rand, stop when it doesn't. That's marginal revenue equals marginal cost, fried golden.

Key four: quick pedal, slow ship. The Reserve Bank's repo pedal moves in an afternoon chasing three to six percent inflation; the Treasury's budget ship turns once a year carrying spending and tax. Which driver, which lever, which speed, which target — that's the whole comparison.

Four keys, and every practice question below fits one of them. Go try the locks right now — you'll hear them click.
