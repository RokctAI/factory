Today we put the environment on an economist's balance sheet: a stock of real wealth that markets treat as free and therefore spend as if it were worthless. We diagnose deterioration as precise market failure — the externality's unposted bill, the tragedy of the unowned commons, and future generations who cast no vote in today's markets — then sort the four families of tools governments use in response, from emission limits to the carbon tax. We define sustainability through the Brundtland definition and the three pillars, and end at home: a water-scarce, coal-powered South Africa that pollutes heavily, floods and dries early, and is attempting one of the world's most watched energy transitions. Here, sustainability is not sentiment but solvency.
