Today we hold two questions in one hand: how does a country produce more, and who ends up with what gets produced? That is economic growth and the distribution of income and wealth — measured honestly with real GDP per capita on one side, and with the Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient on the other. South Africa is the sharpest case study on Earth for both: a decade of growth slower than population growth, and the deepest inequality any major economy has recorded. We finish with what can be done about it — the four redistribution methods government actually uses, and why growth and sharing have to feed each other rather than fight.
