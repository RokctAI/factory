Hey, it's Bianca — I know, that lesson was enormous: measurement, six characteristics, three plans, and a whole knowledge system at the end. Nobody holds all of that at once, so don't try. Hold three anchors instead.

Anchor one: the pencil mark. Growth is the mark on the wall — more produced, easy to measure. Development is everything the mark can't see — living longer, learning more, a tap that runs. And South Africa in one line: richer than we are healthy or educated. That single line unlocks the HDI question every time.

Anchor two: the street. Don't recite six characteristics — replay six scenes. Food that stretches at month-end, the seamstress without a machine, one pension feeding eight, CVs printed for the fortieth time, coal out and tools in, the substation tripping. See the scene, name the sign.

Anchor three: three plans, three bets. RDP bet on delivery. GEAR bet on stability. NDP bets on capability. Verdict in five words: basics delivered, jobs still missed.

And IKS is simply the reminder that the street built its own systems first — stokvels, healers, drought crops — and that knowledge is property worth protecting.

Now go open the practice questions. Take them one scene at a time, and start each answer by asking: is this about the mark on the wall, or about the life? You know more than you think you do.
