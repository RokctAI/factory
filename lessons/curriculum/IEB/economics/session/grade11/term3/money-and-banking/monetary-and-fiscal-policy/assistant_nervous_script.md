Hey, it's Bianca — two policies, five links, taps, wheels... I promise this topic is smaller than it feels. It is really one question asked over and over.

The question: whose hand is on the lever? If it's the Reserve Bank's hand, it's monetary policy, and honestly the Bank only has one lever that matters — the repo rate, guarding the three-to-six percent band. If it's the government's hand through the Budget, it's fiscal policy, and there are only two taps — spending and taxes. That's the entire map. Three levers, two hands.

Then one story: repo up, prime up, repayments up, spending down, price rises slow down. Don't memorise it — follow the money downhill from the Reserve Bank to the shops, and the order builds itself.

And one comparison to finish: monetary is fast but blunt — same-day, but it hits every borrower alike. Fiscal is slow but aimed — once a year, but it can point at one tax or one province.

If a question ever says the Minister moved the repo rate, smile — that's the trap, and you already know the answer: wrong hand.

Now go try the practice questions. Start every single one with 'whose hand?' and let the rest unfold. You're more ready than you feel.
