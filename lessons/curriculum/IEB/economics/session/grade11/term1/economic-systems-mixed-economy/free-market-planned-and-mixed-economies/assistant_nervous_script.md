Hey, it's Bianca — I know this topic feels slippery. It reads like a debate, so people write debate-club answers full of feelings, and feelings score nothing. Let me hand you the frame that holds it all steady.

The entire topic is a grid: three questions across the top — what, how, for whom — and three systems down the side — market, planned, mixed. Nine cells. Market row: consumers decide what, producers pick the cheapest how, income decides for whom. Planned row: the planning authority fills all three cells itself. Mixed row: markets answer first, government corrects and redistributes. If you can sketch those nine cells from memory, you can face any question in this topic without flinching.

Then guard the golden word: mechanism. Never write "the market is unfair" — write "for whom is answered by ability to pay, so someone with no assets receives little". Never write "planning fails" — write "no office can gather the millions of facts that prices collect automatically, and fixed prices cannot signal the errors". Same opinion, completely different marks, because one is a feeling and the other is machinery.

And keep the twins apart. Mixed is about WHO DECIDES — market plus state. Dualistic is about WHAT EXISTS — formal economy plus informal economy. South Africa is both, but they are two separate claims, and swapping them is the most common slip in this whole topic.

My routine for you tonight: draw the nine-cell grid once, from memory, before you touch the practice questions. Thirty seconds of drawing buys you an hour of confidence. You've got this!
