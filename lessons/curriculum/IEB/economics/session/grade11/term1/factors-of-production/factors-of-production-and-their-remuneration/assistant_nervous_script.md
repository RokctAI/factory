Hey, it's Bianca — if this topic feels like a double lesson stapled together, you read it right: four factors with four incomes on one side, and a parade of acronyms on the other. Neither half bites once you shrink it.

Half one is a matching game. Ground earns rent. People earn wages. Machines earn interest. The risk-taker earns profit. If it helps, walk through the salon: the ground under the container, the stylists, the clippers, the owner. Four stations, four payments. Then just two guard rails on top: money is not capital — the clippers the money bought are — and profit is not a salary; it is whatever survives to month-end, and some months nothing survives.

The calculation is friendlier than it looks. Takings minus everything bought from other businesses gives value added — the pot. Pay wages, rent, interest and the wear-and-tear set-aside out of the pot, and the scraps are profit. Then use the free self-check: add your five pieces back together, and if they equal the pot, you are right. That add-back has rescued more marks than any other habit in this topic.

Half two is one sentence wearing four badges: South Africa builds machinery to widen who can reach the factors. The plan is the IDP. The walls are collateral, information, transport and networks. The voice is the ward committee. The money is tender points — most for price, a fixed handful for empowerment. Learn the sentence, then hang the badges on it.

And everywhere, give the mechanism, not the mood: never "people struggle" — always "no title deed means no collateral, so no loan". That single upgrade transforms an average answer.

Four pairs, one pot, one add-back check, one sentence with badges. Now go take it out on the practice questions. You've got this!
