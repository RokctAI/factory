Hey, Bianca here — if this lesson felt like a geography test, a history test and an economics test all at once, slow down, because it folds into something small enough to carry.

Honestly, the whole topic answers just two questions. First: who makes what? Use the verbs. Take from nature — primary. Turn into goods — secondary. Serve people — tertiary. Every business ever invented is doing one of those three verbs, and when a company seems to do all three, you classify each verb separately. That single trick answers half the questions in this topic.

Second: what does everything stand on? The shared stuff — wires, rails, roads, harbours, pipes, plus schools and clinics. Hold one image: when the power dies on a trading corner, the barber, the butcher and the café all lose money in the same minute, and nobody got worse at their job. Shared thing breaks, everyone pays at once. And the flip side: if the shared thing never reached your street at all, you pay every single day. That image IS the infrastructure section.

The numbers only need their shape, not their decimals: services about two thirds, factories about a fifth, mines and farms under a tenth together, household electricity around ninety percent, and manufacturing down from over twenty to about thirteen. Rough and confident beats precise and terrified every time.

Now go open the practice questions. Ask the verb, picture the corner, and watch how many of them fall over on the first push. You've got this!
