This session draws the map of how South Africa earns its living. We sort all production into the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors and anchor each with its rough share of GDP — revealing a services economy that happens to own famous mines — then follow the century-long shift from mines and farms through the factory decades to services, including the premature deindustrialisation you must be able to name and explain. From there we examine infrastructure, the economy's skeleton: the economic-versus-social split, South Africa's impressive endowment, and the strain of load shedding and freight bottlenecks that taxes every producer at once. We finish with service access, building the argument in full: access converts infrastructure into opportunity, so unequal access means unequal opportunity.
