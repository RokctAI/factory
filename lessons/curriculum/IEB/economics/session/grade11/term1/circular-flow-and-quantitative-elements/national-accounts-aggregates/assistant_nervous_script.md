Hey, it's Bianca — deep breath. I know this topic looks like somebody spilled a tin of abbreviations across the page. C, G, I, GNE, GVA, GDP, GNI. But here is the secret: underneath the letters there are only three moves, and you already know all three from ordinary life.

Move one is sorting. Every purchase in the country lands on one giant till slip, and you sort it into piles: what households bought, what government bought for its daily work, and what anyone bought to keep and use for years. Sorting — that is literally it. Add the piles, adjust for stock on shelves, and GNE is done.

Move two is correcting. Trade correction: add what foreigners bought from us, remove what we bought from them — now you have what the country produced, GDP. Price-tag correction: peel the VAT and levies off the shelf price to see the maker's price — that is GVA. Ownership correction: subtract the profit and interest owed to owners abroad, add what our people earn out there — that is GNI, what we keep.

Move three is refusing to count anything twice. The grant is not in G because it gets counted when it is spent. The bottler's sugar is not counted because it is already inside the cold drink. One rule, worn two ways.

And my favourite safety net: after every calculation, run it backwards. If your GNI does not climb back up to your GDP, you have found your own mistake before anyone else does — that is the best feeling in an exam.

Sort, correct, never double count. Now go show those practice questions who is in charge. You've got this!
