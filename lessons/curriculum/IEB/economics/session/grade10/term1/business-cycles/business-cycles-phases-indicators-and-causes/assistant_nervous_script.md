Hey, it's Thandi — four phases, three kinds of indicators, four movements in a time series, two families of causes... I watched that list grow and felt my own pulse spike, so let's shrink it back down together.

Everything in this lesson hangs off ONE picture: a wave rolling around a rising line. The rising line is the trend; the wave is the cycle. The four phases are just the wave's positions: going up, at the top, going down, at the bottom — upswing, peak, downswing, trough. Say them while drawing the wave in the air and they stick.

Indicators? Use the car. Headlights see ahead — building plans, new orders: leading. Speedometer reads now — this month's output and sales: coincident. Mirror shows what's passed — unemployment, always late: lagging. If a question names a series, just ask: is this a decision about tomorrow, a reading of today, or a leftover of yesterday?

The four movements trick is one question: WHICH movement am I looking at? Decades-long climb — trend. Multi-year wave — cycle. Same jump every December — season. Once-off flood — shock. Never let a December or a drought fool you into shouting "upswing" or "collapse".

And causes: outside stones — drought, wars, world prices — versus inside sloshing — mood and credit. Real answers usually say: stone first, sloshing after.

One wave, one car, one question, stones and sloshing. That's the whole lesson — now go take it out on the practice questions!
