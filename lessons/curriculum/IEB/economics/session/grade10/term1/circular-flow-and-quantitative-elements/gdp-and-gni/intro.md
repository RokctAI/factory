Today we attach a meter to the circular flow and read the size of the whole economy. Two national measures do the work: Gross Domestic Product — the market value of all final goods and services produced within our borders in a year — and Gross National Income, which follows the factor incomes home to our residents wherever in the world they were earned. We will dissect both definitions phrase by phrase, dodge the double-counting trap with value added, cross the bridge between GDP and GNI in both directions, correct totals per capita, and finish with the honest list of everything the big number cannot see. Precision with these definitions pays for itself all the way to your final exams.
