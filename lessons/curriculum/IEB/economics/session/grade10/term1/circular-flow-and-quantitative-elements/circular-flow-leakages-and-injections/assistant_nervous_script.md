Hey, Thandi here — I know that diagram kept growing: two participants, then four, arrows everywhere, six new terms. Breathe. The whole thing folds down into one picture you already own: money circling your own neighbourhood on a month-end Friday, with three drains and three taps.

Build it in layers, exactly like the lesson did. Layer one: families and businesses, work going one way, money coming back — that's the skeleton, and it never changes. Layer two: the bank door — savings drain out, loans to businesses pour back as investment. Layer three: the government door — taxes drain, teachers' salaries and tarred roads pour back. Layer four: the border door — imports drain, exports pour back. Never memorise six floating words; memorise THREE DOORS, each with an out and an in. Bank, government, border. That's it.

The favourite trap: thinking drains are bad. Say it with me — direction, not virtue. Granny's deposit IS the bakery's future mixer.

And the level question is pure common sense: drains beating taps, the water drops — quiet tills. Taps beating drains, it rises. Equal — steady, and the fancy word is equilibrium.

Three doors, two lanes, one water level. Draw it once from memory, then go straight into the practice questions while it's fresh — you're more ready than you feel!
