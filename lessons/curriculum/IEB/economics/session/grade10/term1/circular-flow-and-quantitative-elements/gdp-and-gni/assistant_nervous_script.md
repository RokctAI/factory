Hey, it's Thandi — GDP, GNI, final goods, value added, the bridge, per capita, four blind spots... I saw the acronyms stacking up and felt the panic from here. Let's flatten them.

The whole lesson is two questions about one street. Question one: what was made HERE? That's GDP — the where-rule. Question two: what did OUR people earn, wherever they were? That's GNI — the who-rule. The outsider's bottle store: counts in GDP, mostly not in GNI. The daughter working in Cape Town: skips GDP, counts in GNI. Every bridge calculation is just moving between those two questions: minus what leaves with outsiders, plus what our people send home.

The double-counting trap has a one-word password: NEW. The R400 of parts already lives inside the R1 500 repair — count the final job once, or count each person's added slice. Old bakkie resold? Nothing new — out. Grant arriving? Support, not production — out.

And the blind spots aren't extra facts to cram — they're one sentence: the number is a speedometer, not a report card. It reads how fast, never how well, who got it, or what got used up. Say that sentence in any limitations answer and then name the four: sharing, unpaid work, how life feels, the environment.

Two questions, one password, one sentence. That's the entire lesson folded small. The practice questions are waiting — go collect them!
