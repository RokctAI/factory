Hey, it's Thandi — first Economics lesson done, and if the vocabulary felt like a downpour — needs, wants, four factors, micro, macro, positive, normative, ceteris paribus — take a breath, because it all hangs off ONE hook: not enough for everybody, so choices everywhere. Every fancy term is just a label for some corner of that single idea.

The two places people slip, and the fix for each. Slip one: the factor-reward pairs. Don't learn eight loose words — learn four little stories: the land is RENTED, the hands are WAGED, the tied-up money earns INTEREST, the risk-taker keeps the PROFIT. Say the story, and the pair comes with it. Slip two: micro versus macro. Use the gate test: if you could point at it from your front gate — one shop, one price, one hiring — it's micro. If you'd need a drone over the whole country — inflation, unemployment, growth — it's macro.

And positive versus normative is just measure versus wish: "fare went up two rand" is a measurement; "fares are unfair" is a wish. Label the sentence first, argue second.

One hook, four little stories, the gate test, and measure-versus-wish. That's genuinely the whole lesson. Now go try the practice questions — you'll surprise yourself with how much stuck!
