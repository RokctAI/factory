Hey, it's Thandi — scarcity, shortage, opportunity cost, three questions, three systems, externalities... that vocabulary list looks scary until you spot the trick: it's ONE story in stages. Not enough → so choose → every choice costs its runner-up → whole societies face the same three choices → different systems answer differently → and people and the planet feel the results.

Two spots where the marks usually leak, and the plug for each. Leak one: scarcity versus shortage. Ask one question: can the delivery truck fix it? Truck fixes it — shortage. Nothing fixes it — scarcity. Leak two: writing opportunity cost in RANDS. The answer wants the runner-up ITSELF — "one gym month", "an afternoon of revision" — never "R300". Real things, not money; the money was leaving your pocket either way.

The three questions — what, how, for whom — are just a family planning supper: what gets cooked, do we cook or buy ready-made, and who gets the biggest plate? Systems are simply who decides: prices, planners, or a mix of both — which is us.

And the rights-and-environment section is a habit, not extra content: every scenario gets TWO columns — promoting and violating — both filled, fixes named at the bottom. Balanced columns, full credit.

One story, two little tests, one two-column habit. That's the entire lesson in your pocket. Go show the practice questions who's boss!
