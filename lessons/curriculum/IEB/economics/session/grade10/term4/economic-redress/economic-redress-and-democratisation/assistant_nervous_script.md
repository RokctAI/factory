Hey, it's Thandi — final topic of the year, and I know this one can feel like walking into a family argument with a pen in your hand. Breathe. The exam never asks for your politics; it asks for structure, mechanisms and honesty on both sides — and this topic hands you the structure for free.

Here it is: everything hangs on the four factors from the very first week of the year. A PLACE — restitution, redistribution, tenure, always with the support attached, because a plot with no buyer is not yet a business. SKILLS — learnerships and trades funded by the levy, and employment equity opening jobs all the way up to management. MONEY — ownership, procurement, and finance for people banks won't look at. THE CHANCE — mentorship, easy registration, a first guaranteed order. For each one, say what was denied, name the instrument, add the support it needs. That three-step sentence, four times over, is most of the marks.

Then the decision side: workers get a chair through labour law, residents through hearings, and NEDLAC seats four — government, business, labour, community — before big laws reach Parliament. Know the four chairs cold.

And the golden rule for any evaluation question: both columns, mechanisms attached. Real gains — services, grants, a professional class. Real shortfalls — unemployment, inequality, slow land reform, narrow benefits. Write both and you sound like an economist, not a supporter.

You know this material — now go prove it on the practice questions. That's the whole year done, and you finished it strong.
