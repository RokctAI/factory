Hey, it's Thandi — I know a whole half-year folded into one lesson feels like too much to hold. So don't hold pages. Hold four structures — that's all this sweep actually was.

Structure one: the gap. The wallet never covers the list — that's scarcity, and it's permanent. Every choice then has one true cost: the single next best thing you walked away from, never the whole list. The picture is the curve: on it means everything working, inside it means idle machines and idle hands, beyond it means not yet — until the whole curve moves out, and that move is growth.

Structure two: the circle. Households and firms passing money in a loop, with three leaks — saving, taxes, imports — and three refills — investment, government spending, exports. Then two counting rules: GDP counts WHERE things are made, GNI counts WHO earns the income, and only finished goods enter the total.

Structure three: the crossing. Demand falls, supply climbs, and where they cross there's no queue and no pile. The sentence that earns the most marks all year: the product's own price moves you ALONG a curve; everything else moves the curve itself. Then any shock is four steps — which curve, which way, which excess, which new crossing.

Structure four: the breath. Recovery, prosperity, recession, depression — fixed order — read through headlights, speedometer and rearview mirror.

That's it. Four structures, and every question in the practice set opens with one of them. Go take them one at a time — you're readier than you feel.
