Today we study labour relations — the system South Africa designed to turn the old confrontation between employers and workers into negotiation governed by rules. We begin with the imbalance that makes labour law necessary, then shelve the three pillar statutes by the jobs they do: the Labour Relations Act for the rules of engagement, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act for the floor beneath every job, and COIDA for the safety net after harm. From there we tour the machinery of collective bargaining — unions, bargaining councils and workplace forums — and finish on the CCMA's ladder of conciliation, mediation and arbitration, with the labour courts above it. The skill this topic rewards is matching: for every institution, know its function, its level, and the reason it exists.
