Every economy begins with its people, and today we learn to count them the way economists do. We follow populations through the four stages of the demographic cycle, watch migration redraw the map, and then pin down the definitions that carry the marks — exactly who belongs to the labour force, and why South Africa publishes both a strict and an expanded measure of unemployment. From there we treat the labour market as a true market: households supplying hours and skill, firms demanding them because of the goods their customers want, and the wage forming where the two sides meet. Hold onto today's definitions tightly — the coming lessons on labour relations and unemployment are built directly on top of them.
