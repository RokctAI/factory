Hey, it's Thandi — I know that felt like a dictionary fell on you: demographic cycles, strict versus expanded, derived demand, participation rates. Breathe. The whole lesson folds into three pictures you already own.

Picture one: a street. Cradles minus funerals, plus moved-in minus moved-away — that's all population change is. Then four chapters: cradles and funerals balancing, the clinic arrives and the street swells, families plan smaller and it slows, then it stands still. Attach each chapter to its cause — clinics cut deaths, education cuts births — and you can retell the cycle without memorising a word.

Picture two: a Sunday table. Sort the family: too old or too young, off the sheet. Working — on the field. Searching — on the bench, and BENCH still counts as on the team. The full-time learner: off the sheet. The one who stopped searching: off the STRICT sheet, on the EXPANDED one. Those three tricky seats are the whole exam. And always divide the benched by the TEAM, never by the table.

Picture three: two gates at dawn. Families sell hours, firms buy them — but only when their own customers are buying: no orders, no shifts. Short queue at the artisans' gate, long queue at the general gate, and a certificate is the boarding pass between them.

Street, table, gates. Walk in with those three pictures and the questions will feel like they were written for you. Now go show the practice questions who's boss!
