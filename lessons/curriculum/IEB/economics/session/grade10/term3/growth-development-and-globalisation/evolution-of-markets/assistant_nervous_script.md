Hey, it's Thandi — ten thousand years in one lesson sounds terrifying until you notice the trick: there is not a single date to memorise. Not one. What gets tested is the ORDER and the WHY — and the order is a story where every stage fixes the mess the previous stage left. Nobody forgets a story.

Tell it to yourself like this. No extra, no trade — a family that does everything does nothing well. Then farming makes EXTRA, and extra feeds the first specialists. Specialists must trade, and swapping brings four miseries. Copper — money — kills all four in a day, and the market bursts its banks. A bigger market needs roads, rules and shared risk — transport, law, companies. The market needs what no trader builds alone, so the chief arrives: regulate, tax, build. And when hands hit their limit, the machines arrive: industry. Seven links. Practise saying the chain out loud once — if you can tell it to a friend at break, you can answer anything on it.

Two lists deserve word-perfect status, and they are secretly one list: barter's four defects — double coincidence, indivisibility, no common measure, no store of value — and money's matching answers — medium of exchange, divisibility, unit of account, store of value. Learn them as pairs that hold hands.

And for South Africa, one sentence with two columns: our industrial age came out of the mines — huge gains, heavy costs, always both.

A story, a set of pairs, a balanced sentence. That's everything. Off you go to the practice questions — you're readier than you feel!
