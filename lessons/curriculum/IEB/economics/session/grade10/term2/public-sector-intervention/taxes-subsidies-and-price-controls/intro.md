Markets set prices — and society regularly overrules them. This session covers the public sector's toolbox for stepping into markets: indirect taxes that make harmful goods dearer, subsidies that make essential goods cheaper, welfare transfers that bypass prices entirely, and the price controls that overrule the crossing point directly — ceilings that cap prices below equilibrium, floors that hold them above it, and the minimum wage as a floor under the price of work itself. Each instrument is traced honestly on the diagram: the shifted curves, the shortages under ceilings, the surpluses on floors, and the wage debate argued both ways. One habit carries the whole topic: ask what the intervention was for, what the diagram predicts, and what else follows.
