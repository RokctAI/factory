Hey, it's Thandi — taxes, subsidies, ceilings, floors, wage debates... I know this one feels like five topics stacked in a trench coat. Good news: it's actually ONE skill worn five ways.

Here's the skeleton. The state has four moves: make it dearer (tax), make it cheaper (subsidy), freeze the price (ceiling or floor), or skip the market completely (grants and free services). And for every single one, you ask the same three questions: what was it FOR? What does the diagram predict? And what ELSE follows? That's it — that's the entire topic. Every question you'll ever get is one of the four moves plus the three questions.

And the diagrams? Two pictures, total. Taxes and subsidies SHIFT the supply curve — left for a tax, right for a subsidy. Ceilings and floors DON'T shift anything — they freeze the price and let the gap show: a shortage under a ceiling, a surplus on a floor. If you remember "queues under ceilings, piles on floors," you already have the punchline.

So try this: pick any intervention from this week's news — a levy, a grant, a capped price — and run the three questions on it out loud. Then hit the practice questions straight away. If you blank on a diagram, just ask "did the state move a curve, or freeze the price?" — the answer draws itself from there. You've absolutely got this. Go!
