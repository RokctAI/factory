Hi, it's Thandi — I saw some worried faces when the definitions started stacking up: utility, value in use, value in exchange, monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition... it sounds like a whole dictionary. Let me shrink it for you.

This lesson is really just three ideas wearing fancy names. Idea one: worth lives in PEOPLE, not things — that's why the same water bottle costs R9 at the café and R30 inside the festival. You already understood that before you knew the word utility. Idea two: scarce things trade dear, plentiful things trade cheap — that's the whole water-and-diamonds riddle in one line. Idea three: you can sort any market with three quick questions — how many sellers, same product or dressed-up different, and how easy is it to join in?

Here's what I want you to try: on your next trip through town or through your feeds, classify three markets you actually see. The produce stalls, the banks, the coffee shops, even the sellers on your socials. Say the three questions out loud and place each one. It turns the scary vocabulary into a game — and once you've played it three times, the definitions stick by themselves.

Then go straight into the practice questions while it's warm. If you mix up oligopoly and monopolistic competition, just count the sellers again — that fixes it nine times out of ten. You're closer than you think. Go!
