Before demand and supply can carry their full weight, three foundations must be poured: what value actually is and why utility lives in the person rather than the product; how markets are classified, from the perfect tomato rows at dawn to monopolies, oligopolies and the coffee-shop street; and the five jobs every market performs for an economy — forming prices that then allocate, inform, ration and connect. Along the way we crack the old water-and-diamonds riddle with scarcity, watch a thirty-rand festival water bottle prove the point, and audit what phones and electronic platforms have done to markets — wider, faster, better informed, but not automatically fairer.
