Scarcity and opportunity cost were defined in words last term; today they get their picture. The production possibility curve draws the outer edge of what an economy can produce with everything it has — and one diagram then displays scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, efficiency, unemployment and growth all at once. We build the curve from a wheat-and-wool schedule, learn to read points on it, inside it and beyond it, sort out what moves the whole boundary, and count the real cost of producing inside the line — before shrinking the entire model into a sewing workshop with one room of machines and two uniforms to sew. By the end, the difference between walking along the curve, walking back to it, and moving it will be second nature.
