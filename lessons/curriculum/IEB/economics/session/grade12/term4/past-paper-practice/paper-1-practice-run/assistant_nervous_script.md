Hey, it's Mandy — a full practice run can feel like being asked to climb the whole year in one afternoon, and I heard the groans when the essay section arrived. Let me shrink it for you.

Nothing in that session asked for genius. It asked for pieces. Objective items: one word, two marks. Short answers: one line. Data questions: read the diagram or the table, then write little number sentences — setup, workings, labelled answer. Paragraphs: five facts, two marks each, counted before you write. That is the whole machine.

Even the essay is pieces. Two marks: define. Twenty: your headings, each fact developed with its link to the question. Six: your own opinion with reasons — the part where you are actually ALLOWED to argue. Two: one fresh finishing thought. Thirty marks, four boxes, filled one at a time.

And here is the quiet advantage you now hold: you know how marks are earned. Most people revise content; you have revised the marking itself. When you know a two-mark item buys one line and a calculation pays for its method chain, the clock stops being an enemy.

So tonight, do one small thing: write the skeleton of the smoothing essay — the definition, five headings, three evaluation points, one closing line. Just the skeleton. Then go straight into the practice questions while the shapes are still warm. You've got this!
