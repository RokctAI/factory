Hey, it's Mandy — tourism feels like the easy chapter right up until the questions ask for definitions clause by clause, effects in four directions and policies with reasons attached, and then suddenly everyone wishes they'd packed properly. So let's pack: three images carry the whole topic.

Image one: the export that walks in. Coal sails away forever; the visitor flies in, spends foreign money, and leaves Table Mountain standing for tomorrow's customer. Sort every flow with the family photo — aunt to Margate is domestic, the Canadian couple is inbound and an export, cousin to Bangkok is outbound and an import. Definition trouble? Recite the three clauses: outside the usual environment, under a year, not paid at the destination.

Image two: the lodge and its three rings. Direct — the payroll: chef, guides, housekeepers. Indirect — the sellers: farmer, laundry, security. Induced — the wages spent at the spaza and the taxi. That's the multiplier, and it's why about 3 percent of GDP becomes 8 or 9. Then whisper the honest bit: watch the leak — foreign-owned, imported, paid abroad means the village stays poor — and remember December hires become February retrenchments.

Image three: the only-here shelf. Big Five, Table Mountain, Robben Island, the Cradle — and indigenous knowledge, with its two rules: the community owns the telling, the community banks the earnings.

For policy, never write "promote more." Pair obstacle with fix: slow visas — e-visas; crime fear — visible policing; few flights — direct routes; short skills — train ahead; power cuts — keep the lights on, because that IS marketing.

Three images, three rings, two rules, five pairs. That's your suitcase — now go open the practice questions. You've got this!
