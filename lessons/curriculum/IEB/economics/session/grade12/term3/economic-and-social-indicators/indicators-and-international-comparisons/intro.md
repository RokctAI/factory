This session builds the instrument panel economists use to judge a country. We start by classifying indicators — leading, coincident and lagging, levels against rates, nominal against real — then walk the economic gauges one by one: GDP and GDP per capita, the price indices, the money aggregates, interest rates, the external measures, unemployment on both definitions, and productivity. From there we cross to the social side — life expectancy, infant mortality, education, services, the HDI and the Gini coefficient — and finish with the craft of comparing countries honestly through per-capita and purchasing-power corrections, plus the IMF and the World Bank kept cleanly apart. This panel is the raw material of data questions and essay introductions alike in your final exams.
