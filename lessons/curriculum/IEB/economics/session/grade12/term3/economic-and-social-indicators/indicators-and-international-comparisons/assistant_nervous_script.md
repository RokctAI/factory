Hey, it's Mandy — I know this topic looks like someone emptied a statistics yearbook onto your desk and said learn it all. You don't need the yearbook. You need one bus and three rules, and you already rode the bus in the lesson.

The bus sorts every timing question. Headlights see ahead: building plans, job adverts, factory orders — leading. The speedometer reads now: tills, production, GDP — coincident. The mirror looks back: jobs, inventories, insolvencies — lagging, because bosses retrench late and rehire late. If a question names an indicator, just ask: promise, reading, or confirmation? Seat it in the bus and the mark is yours.

Two habits protect your calculations. Size is not speed — a huge economy can crawl. And rands are not kilometres — strip inflation out before you call anything growth: nominal 7,5 minus inflation 5,5 leaves real growth of 2.

For the social side, walk the passengers: how long do people live, do babies reach one, are learners learning, is there a tap and a switch. Then the two summaries — HDI squeezes health, school and income into one score; Gini asks how the money is shared, and our 0,63 answers loudly.

And for comparisons, three honest rules: divide by the people, price money where it lives, and remember the spaza the meter never sees. IMF the paramedic, World Bank the builder — one line each.

One bus, four passengers, three rules, two institutions. That's the entire topic. Head into the practice questions and watch them sort themselves. You've got this!
