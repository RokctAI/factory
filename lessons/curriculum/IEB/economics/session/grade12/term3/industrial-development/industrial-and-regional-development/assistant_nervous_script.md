Hey, it's Mandy — I know this topic reads like a government directory: frameworks, programmes, corporations, zones, all in capital letters. Nobody can love a directory, so don't memorise it as one. Hang everything on three pictures instead.

Picture one: the dock. A bag of ore earns a few hundred rand; a bakkie earns hundreds of thousands, with every maker's wage and tax folded inside. That picture IS value added and beneficiation — sell the making, not just the digging. Behind every factory stand its suppliers, in front stand its dealers: linkages, both directions. And tape the warning on: help needs a finish line, or the infant never grows up.

Picture two: the fence at Coega. The state builds the port and the serviced land, then sweetens the sums — 15 percent tax, hiring breaks, duty-free inputs for exporters. East London does it for cars, Dube TradePort beside the runway, Richards Bay for metals. The Maputo Corridor is the same idea shaped like a road. And the homelands are the warning photo: paid to arrive, dead when the paying stopped — a factory only stays where the business works.

Picture three: the marker's memo, four moves. State the test — exports, temporary, foundations. Wins by name — cars, Coega, Dube, the N4. Failures by name — the share down from a fifth to an eighth, clothing jobs, empty zones. Then the bottleneck line that earns the top marks: no sweetener outbids a power cut.

Three pictures, four moves, one verdict. The acronyms will attach themselves once the pictures are up. Go do the practice questions — you're more ready than you think. You've got this!
