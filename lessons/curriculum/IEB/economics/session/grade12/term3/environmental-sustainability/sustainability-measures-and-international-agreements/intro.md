This closing topic asks the course's own question one level higher: what happens when the scarce resource is the planet itself? We begin with the Brundtland definition of sustainable development and the three market failures — externalities, public goods and the tragedy of the commons — that explain why prices fail the environment. We then survey the state of the environment globally and at home, from a coal-heavy grid to stressed rivers and Highveld air, before opening the policy toolkit: command-and-control rules, the carbon tax and levies, subsidies, tradable permits and property rights. We finish by climbing the ladder of international agreements — Montreal, Rio, Kyoto, Johannesburg, Paris and the summits since — and land on South Africa's own just energy transition. Definitions, tools and treaties here convert directly into marks in your final exams.
