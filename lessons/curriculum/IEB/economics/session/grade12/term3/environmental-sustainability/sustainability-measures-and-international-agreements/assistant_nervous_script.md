Hey, it's Mandy — this topic can feel like three subjects stapled together: science, law and economics, each with its own vocabulary. Breathe. Underneath the stapling it is ONE economics idea told four times: the price tag is missing, so put it back.

Start every answer at the river. The kiln's pipe pays nothing, the village pays everything — that's an externality, a real cost outside the polluter's books. Nobody owns the river, so nobody defends it — that's the commons. Fresh air can't be sold to paying customers only, so everyone waits for someone else to pay — that's free riding. Three failures, one diagnosis: the damage is priced at zero.

Then the four repairs, in the same order every time: the RULE that bans and inspects, the TAX that makes the firm's own bookkeeper police the pipe, the REWARD that pulls in solar and wind, and the OWNER — the antelope that pays the village school fees is the antelope everybody guards. Close with the mixing rule: ban what kills, tax what harms, own what can be owned.

For the treaties, you need one line per rung: Montreal worked and proves cooperation is possible. Rio wrote the constitution. Kyoto bound only the rich and the curve ignored it. Paris got everyone pledging, voluntarily — strength and weakness in one sentence. Then the recent three: coal phase-down, loss-and-damage fund, global stocktake. And ours: 8,5 billion dollars, one condition — the transition must be JUST.

One river, three failures, four tags, one ladder. That's the whole chapter. The practice questions are waiting, and you're readier than you feel. You've got this!
