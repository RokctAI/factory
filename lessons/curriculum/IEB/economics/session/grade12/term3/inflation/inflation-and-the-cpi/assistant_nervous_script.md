Hey, it's Mandy — I know inflation feels like a topic that mixes a memory test with a maths test, and that double demand is exactly what makes people panic. So let's cut it down to the four things that actually earn the marks.

First, the definition is three checkpoints: SUSTAINED, GENERAL, OVER TIME. One fuel-levy jump fails the first. Dearer shoes alone fails the second. No comparison year fails the third. Tick all three and the definition marks are yours before any calculation starts.

Second, one formula: new index minus old index, divided by the OLD index, times 100. The CPI goes 108 to 114,5 — that is 6,5 over 108, about 6 percent. The two traps are cheap to avoid once named. Never divide by the new index — a bus ticket going from R20 to R25 rose 25 percent on the twenty you used to pay. And index points are never percentages — 6,5 points is not 6,5 percent. Write the formula line first and the marker rewards your working even if a digit slips.

Third, the sorting rule for the essay: ask where the pressure starts. At the till, with busy shops and easy credit — demand-pull. At the loading bay, with diesel, tariffs and a weak rand — cost-push. Then add the sentence that lifts your answer: the repo rate fights demand-pull well and cost-push badly, because an interest rate cannot make diesel cheaper.

Last, if consequences blank out, picture four faces: the pensioner loses, the saver loses, the borrower quietly wins, and the Reserve Bank holds it all between 3 and 6 percent so everyone can still plan.

Three checkpoints, one formula, two fires, four faces. Now go take the practice questions — they will feel much smaller than they did an hour ago. You've got this!
