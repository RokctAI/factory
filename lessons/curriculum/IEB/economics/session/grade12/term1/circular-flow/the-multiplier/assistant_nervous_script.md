Hi, Mandy here — the multiplier is one of the rare corners of Economics where actual calculation shows up, and I know the sight of a formula makes some of us want to close the book. Don't. This is one formula, one substitution and one sentence, and then it's over.

The formula: k equals one over one minus the MPC — which is the same thing as one over the MPS, because the two propensities always add to one. Write the formula down before you touch any numbers. The written formula earns its own credit even if your arithmetic stumbles afterwards.

Then run the three-step drill every single time. Step one, state the formula. Step two, substitute. Step three, write ONE interpretation sentence: "national income rises by forty billion rand, five times the injection, because the spending is earned and re-spent round after round." That closing sentence is where a mark hides, and it is the step everyone skips.

Two traps to spot from a distance. Trap one is the backwards question: you're given the change in income and the injection, and asked for the MPC. Divide income change by injection to find k, then unwind — if k is two comma five, one minus MPC is nought comma four, so the MPC is nought comma six. Trap two is the open economy: the moment a question breathes the words tax or imports, the denominator becomes all three leakages added together — MPS plus MPT plus MPM. Add first, divide second. Three leakages of nought comma one five, nought comma two and nought comma one five give a multiplier of two, not five.

For the discussion marks, remember the multiplier is conditional: it needs idle workers and machines, it needs months to unwind, and it runs backwards just as hard — that is what a recession is.

One formula, three steps, two traps, one caveat. The practice questions are waiting — go show them who's in charge.
