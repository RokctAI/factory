Hi, Mandy here — the public sector chapter looks like a warehouse of lists, and the panic move is to cram them all the night before and pray they stay in order. There's a better way: the entire topic is four questions asked in sequence, and every list answers exactly one of them.

Question one: why does the state exist inside a market economy? Answer in pairs — each market failure clipped to its state role. Public goods, so the state provides: your example is the night patrol, non-rivalrous and non-excludable, sunk by the free-rider standoff. Externalities, so the state taxes the harms and funds merit goods like schooling and immunisation. Monopoly, so the state regulates. Need-blind incomes, so the state redistributes through progressive tax and grants. Four pairs, and they score every time they appear.

Question two: why is providing hard? Five problems — accountability, efficiency, needs, pricing, parastatals. If your mind goes blank, rebuild the list from one street: the office with no daily election, the department guessing how many houses, the stepped electricity tariff, and Eskom pulled between villages and balance sheets. The story regenerates the list.

Question three: how does the budget work? In, out, gap. In: income tax, VAT, company tax, excise — and sort them direct on income, indirect on spending. Out: grants, education, health, and the interest line that grows fastest of all. Gap: the deficit, financed by bonds. The Laffer curve is just a hump — zero collected at zero percent, zero at a hundred, a crest somewhere between — plus the golden extra sentence: nobody knows where the crest is.

Question four: what happens when the state fails? Causes — skills, apathy, bureaucracy, politics, corruption — then effects, always finishing with who suffers most: the people who cannot buy private substitutes.

And close every essay with the balance: markets fail, so the state exists; states fail, so the state must be watched. Four questions, four answers, one closing line. Now go prove it to yourself on the practice questions.
