Hi, Mandy here — business cycles looks like four separate lists to memorise: phases, schools, cycle types, indicators. Breathe. It is actually ONE drawing, and every list hangs off a corner of it.

Sketch it mentally. Time along the bottom, real GDP up the side, a gentle rising slope for the trend, and a wave weaving around the slope. Label the wave clockwise: trough at the bottom, upswing rising, peak at the top, downswing falling. Peak-to-peak is length; slope-to-peak is amplitude. That is the entire anatomy, and the diagram question becomes calm, free marks.

The two schools are one contrast sentence. Monetarists: the cause is OUTSIDE the market — mostly government blundering — so keep policy stable and hands off. Keynesians: the cause is INSIDE — confidence, multiplier, accelerator — so government must lean against the wave. Outside means hands off; inside means hands on. Add 2020 as your outside shock and confidence swings as your inside engine, and you have an evaluation paragraph ready.

Cycle types go smallest to biggest: Kitchin stock-rooms, Juglar machines, Kuznets buildings, Kondratieff technology. Three-to-five, seven-to-eleven, fifteen-to-twenty, forty-five-to-sixty.

Policy is just a thermostat: cold economy, repo down, spending up, taxes down; overheating, flip everything. Attach the real years — the 3,5 percent low of 2020, the hikes of 2022 and 2023 — because dated examples are what make an answer sound in charge.

Indicators: leading move first, like orders and building plans; coincident move with, like retail sales; lagging move last, like unemployment. First, with, last.

One drawing, one contrast, one size-order, one thermostat, one first-with-last. Now go do the practice questions — you will surprise yourself.
