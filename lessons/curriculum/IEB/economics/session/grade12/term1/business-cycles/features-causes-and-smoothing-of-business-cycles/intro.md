Today we are studying business cycles — the repeating waves of expansion and contraction that every real economy rides around its long-term growth trend. The topic asks four things of you at once: label the wave, explain why it exists, operate the monetary and fiscal levers that smooth it, and read the indicators analysts use to spot the next turn before it arrives. We build the labelled diagram first — trend line, trough, upswing, peak, downswing, with length and amplitude — then set the monetarists against the Keynesians, put South Africa's own repo-rate history to work, and close with leading, coincident and lagging indicators. South Africa has lived through every phase of this chapter in recent memory, so every idea here can be pinned to a real year and a real event.
