Today we take the perfect market apart and study the three structures that grow where its assumptions fail: monopoly, where one seller behind blocked entry makes the price; oligopoly, where a few giants watch each other so closely that prices freeze and rivalry turns to advertising — or to illegal collusion; and monopolistic competition, where many slightly different sellers each hold a sliver of pricing power that free entry steadily melts away. We will draw each firm's diagram with the same tools as last lesson — MR equals MC, price off the demand curve, the verdict from average cost — and finish with the four structures lined up side by side. By the end you should see that only two dials separate them: the slope of the demand curve and the ease of entry. In your final exams, that comparison is where the marks concentrate.
