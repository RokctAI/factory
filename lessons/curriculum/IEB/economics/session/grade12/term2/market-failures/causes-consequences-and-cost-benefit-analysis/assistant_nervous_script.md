Hey, it's Mandy — market failure is the topic where the lists breed: six causes, two diagrams, seven instruments, five steps, four limits. Take a breath, because underneath every list sits one single idea, and once you hold it, the lists rebuild themselves on demand: prices sometimes lie, and a lying price produces the wrong quantity.

The six causes? Six ways a price can lie. It leaves out a cost that lands on bystanders — externalities. It cannot exist at all — public goods, the market that never forms. It is bent by power — imperfect competition. It rests on ignorance — imperfect information. Resources cannot answer it — immobility. Or it hears only money and never need — inequality. One sentence each, and cause plus mechanism is exactly what the marks want.

The two diagrams are mirror twins, so learn one and flip it. External cost: draw the social cost curve parallel ABOVE private cost; the market lands too far right — over-production. External benefit: social benefit parallel ABOVE private demand; the market lands too far left — under-production. Say the twin sentence and you frame either essay: unsent bills make too much; unsent thank-yous make too little.

The instruments match the lies one for one: tax the hidden cost, subsidise the hidden benefit, provide what cannot be billed, regulate what must not be priced, cap or floor prices knowing the side effects, redistribute for the inequality lie. And always add each tool's own cost — that is the evaluation mark, every time.

Cost-benefit analysis is five verbs: list, list, value, shrink, compare. Then the four limits: contested values, the moral discount dial, optimism bias, and totals that never ask whose rands.

One idea, then the lists. You know this better than you think — go prove it on the practice questions right now.
