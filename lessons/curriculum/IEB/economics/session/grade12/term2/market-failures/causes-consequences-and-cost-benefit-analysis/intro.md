Today we confront the situations where the price mechanism itself gets the answer wrong: market failure, when prices left alone produce too much of some things, too little of others, and none at all of a few. We define the failure precisely — wrong quantities, not high prices — and work through its six causes, from externalities and missing markets to immobile factors and inequality. Then we build the topic's two great tools: the private-against-social cost and benefit diagrams that show over- and under-production on paper, and cost-benefit analysis, the five-step method governments use to judge dams, highways and power plants that no market can price. Along the way we match every corrective instrument — taxes, subsidies, provision, regulation, price controls — to the specific failure it targets, and we weigh each one's own cost. Keep asking the topic's one question throughout: is this price telling the truth?
