Today we settle into the oldest argument in trade policy: how open should a country's borders be? We arm ourselves first with the instruments of protection — tariffs, quotas, subsidies, exchange control, standards and the special cases of dumping and infant industries — then weigh the case for shelter against the case for open trade, argument and rebuttal in matched pairs. From there we follow the two grand strategies a developing country can run, import substitution and export promotion, through South Africa's own history with both, and finish with the machinery of globalisation: the world trade rulebook and the ladder of economic integration that Africa is climbing through the AfCFTA. Keep one number in mind throughout — protection's gains are always concentrated and visible, its costs thin and everywhere — because in your final exams, the marks in this topic live in the weighing.
