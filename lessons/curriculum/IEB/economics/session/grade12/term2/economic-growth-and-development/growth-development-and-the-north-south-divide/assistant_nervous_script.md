Hey, it's Mandy — the growth and development essay is the giant of the year, and I know it feels like it demands everything you've ever learnt. Deep breath: it does, and that is actually the good news, because you are not learning new material tonight — you are connecting material you already own.

Anchor yourself with the two questions. Is the country producing more? That's growth: real GDP, inflation stripped out, then divided by population — and say that dividing sentence early, because real growth below population growth means the average person is getting poorer, and markers reward students who spot it in the data. Are the people living better? That's development: the HDI's three subjects — long healthy life, knowledge, decent standard of living. Payslip and report card. Ask both of every table of figures you're given.

Then the two toolboxes, and the kitchen picture holds them: the fire is demand-side — grants, public works, lower rates — fast, real, and stopped by four walls: inflation, imports, debt, structure. The stove is supply-side — power, ports, schools, skills, certainty — slow, permanent, and dependent on a state that can actually build. The conclusion that earns the evaluation marks is proportion, never victory: fire for this winter, stove for all winters.

For South Africa, keep the procession in order with one clause each — RDP, basic needs; GEAR, discipline; ASGISA, constraints named; NDP, the 2030 destination — and give the verdict with both hands: stability and the social wage achieved; growth behind population and the same constraints still standing.

And the north-south divide is four mechanisms — terms of trade, debt service, tariff escalation, brain drain — plus the counter-currents and the corner house we live in.

Two questions, two toolboxes, one procession, four mechanisms. The whole giant fits on one page. Now go take it apart in the practice questions — you are more ready than you feel.
