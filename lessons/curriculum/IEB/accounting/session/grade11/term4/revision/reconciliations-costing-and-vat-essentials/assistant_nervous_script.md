Hey, it's Bianca — four topics packed into one revision session sounds cruel, but look again: each one is a single move you repeat until it feels like breathing.

Reconciliations: one move — correct both records toward each other. Your books absorb what the statement knew first; the reconciliation statement lists what you knew first. If the two sides meet, you are finished; if not, an error is hiding, and the side it belongs to points at the culprit. Never argue balances — meet in the middle.

Costing: one number — contribution. Selling price minus variable cost. Fixed costs divided by contribution is break-even; units past the line times contribution is profit. Every what-if — new pitch fee, new price, special order — just recomputes contribution and asks where the line moved.

Stock and budgets: two calendars. The counting formula — opening plus purchases plus carriage minus closing — hands you cost of sales; the collection schedule turns planned sales into planned receipts, and the never-pay percentage is left out on purpose.

VAT: one chant — going up, fifteen on a hundred; coming down, fifteen on one-fifteen. Output minus input is the return, and the money was never yours.

Four moves: meet in the middle, find the line, mind the calendars, pass the tax through. Work one example of each tonight and no question can ambush you. You've got this!
