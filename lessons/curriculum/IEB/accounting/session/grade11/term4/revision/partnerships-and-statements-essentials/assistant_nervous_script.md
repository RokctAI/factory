Hey, it's Bianca — partnerships bundle up half the year's work, so if this revision felt like a mountain, here's the footpath: the entire topic answers one question — how do two owners share ONE business fairly?

Begin with the two piles per partner. Capital: planted money, hardly moves. Current: the breathing account — profit share in, drawings out. A debit balance means the partner overdrew; a credit means the business owes them. Read a current account confidently and half the theory marks are already in your pocket.

The appropriation account is a recipe, and the order never changes: salaries, then interest on capital, then any bonus, then the remainder in the ratio. Two checks rescue you every time: the account must empty EXACTLY — the partner totals adding back to the net profit — and salaries plus interest are appropriations, never expenses. Write the word "appropriation" beside them and your understanding is on the page.

Disposals: four steps, always — cost out, accumulated depreciation out, proceeds in, difference is the profit or loss. Say it like a rhyme.

And indicators: the formula earns half the mark, the comment earns the rest — figure, comparison, judgement. Ask whose eye is on each number: creditor wants liquidity, lender wants gearing, partner wants return.

Do one complete appropriation with current accounts tonight, balanced to the rand. The first time the pot empties cleanly under your own pen, this topic is yours for good. You've got this!
