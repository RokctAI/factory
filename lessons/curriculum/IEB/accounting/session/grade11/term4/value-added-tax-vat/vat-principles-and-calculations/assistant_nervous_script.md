Hey, it's Bianca — VAT arrives dressed up in scary vocabulary, but underneath it's three small ideas holding a trench coat.

Idea one: who pays. Consumers pay, businesses collect. Every tangled sentence in the topic straightens out once you hold that. A vendor charges VAT on its sales — output — recovers VAT on its purchases — input — and pays over the difference. That is genuinely the entire system.

Idea two: the three baskets. Most things are standard-rated. The staples — brown bread, maize meal, rice, samp, beans, milk, eggs, fresh produce, cooking oil, paraffin — plus exports are zero-rated. A short list is exempt: bank interest, the rent on your home, bus and train fares, school fees. The only genuinely tricky pair is zero-rated versus exempt, and one question settles it forever: does the seller get its own VAT back? Zero-rated, yes. Exempt, no. Whisper that question twice and it belongs to you.

Idea three: the arithmetic — really just one trap. Going UP from an exclusive price, add fifteen percent. Coming DOWN from an inclusive price, it is fifteen over one-fifteen, NEVER fifteen percent of the total. If one thing sticks from tonight, make it that. And in pricing questions: mark up first, THEN add VAT — never in reverse.

The ethics section practically writes itself once you see VAT as trust money — someone else's money resting in the till. Run five calculations tonight, both directions, and this topic turns into free marks. You've got this!
