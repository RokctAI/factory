Hey, it's Bianca — two columns of numbers from two different businesses, and somehow you are meant to know which one to trust. I get why that felt like a trick question. It is not. There is a simple order to it.

Do the work in two stages and never mix them. Stage one: fix YOUR book. Anything you should have known about — an invoice for goods you actually received, interest they charged, a discount they allowed, a credit note you wrote down wrong — all of that gets corrected in your creditors ledger account, with a proper debit and credit. Stage two: bridge to THEIR book. Only two kinds of thing survive into that stage: money you sent that they have not processed yet, and mistakes their clerk made. That is the whole list.

The habit that saves marks costs one pencil stroke per line. Go down the question and mark each item M for "my book changes" or T for "theirs still to do". Sort everything first. Only then start adding. Almost every lost mark in this topic comes from doing arithmetic before doing the sorting.

The one direction that trips people up is the credit note. A credit note means you owe LESS. If you recorded it too small, you have been walking around believing you owe more than you really do — so the correction brings your balance DOWN. Say that sentence to yourself once before every question and the trap stops working.

And remember what this is actually for. It is not a puzzle somebody invented to torment you. It is the reason a small business never pays for someone else's delivery. Write the statement out fully, heading and all, brackets on every subtraction — markers pay for that structure even when your total is out. You've got this!
