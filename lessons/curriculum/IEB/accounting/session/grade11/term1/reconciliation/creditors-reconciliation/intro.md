Today the reconciliation discipline turns from the bank to the supplier. Every month a creditor posts a statement of what its books say we owe, while our own creditors ledger says something different, and neither record is automatically right. We learn to read a supplier's statement as a claim to be tested, sort every difference into timing, omission or error, correct our own ledger account for Kei Steel & Roofing Wholesalers, and then build the Creditors Reconciliation Statement that bridges the supplier's balance to ours. The twist that separates this from the bank topic is that the outsider here is another ordinary business — so their statement can be wrong too — and the lesson closes with the control practices that stop a business quietly paying for goods it never received.
