Today we set the two ways of keeping stock records side by side: the perpetual system, where every sale carries its cost of sales entry with it, and the periodic system, which records only purchases and reasons cost of sales out backwards from a year-end count. The heart of the session is the periodic formula — opening stock plus purchases and carriage inward, less returns and drawings, less closing stock — and the silent flaw it carries, because losses that are never counted get quietly labelled as sales. We weigh the advantages and disadvantages, see how the barcode scanner rewrote the choice between the systems, and close with the stock controls, ethics and audit tests that complete the topic. It is set both as a comparison and as a calculation, so we make you fluent in both.
