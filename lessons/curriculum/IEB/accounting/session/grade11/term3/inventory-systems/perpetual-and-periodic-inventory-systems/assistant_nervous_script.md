Hey, it's Bianca — meeting a second stock system after a whole year inside the first one can feel like the floor shifting. It shouldn't. You already know one system inside out; tonight you learned that the other one simply refuses to write things down as it goes, and counts at the end instead.

Two sentences carry the entire comparison. Perpetual: record the cost every time stock moves, so you always know. Periodic: record only what you buy, count at the end, and work out what you sold. Every advantage and every disadvantage grows out of those two sentences, so there is nothing to memorise. Ask "would this business benefit from knowing right now?" and the answer chooses the system for you.

The one thing to learn cold is the periodic formula, and it has a natural order when you tell it as a story: what I started with, plus what I bought, plus what it cost to get it here, minus what I sent back, minus what I took home — that's my pile. Then minus what's still standing — that's what went out. Tell it as a story, not a list, and it sticks.

Watch two small traps. Carriage IN belongs in the sum; carriage OUT is a selling cost and stays out. And drawings of stock come off the pile — the owner never sold those.

Last thing, the idea worth carrying past the exam: the periodic system cannot see theft. That is not a footnote; that is the whole reason control exists. You've got this!
