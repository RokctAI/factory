Hey, it's Bianca — break-even questions look like they belong in a business degree, but the entire topic is one fraction, and you already understand it from selling anything, ever.

Here is the whole machine: a wall of fixed costs that exists before you sell unit one, and a contribution — selling price minus variable cost — that every unit throws at the wall. Break-even is simply the wall divided by the throw. R560 000 divided by R56 is 10 000 bags. That is the formula, the only formula; everything else in the topic is feeding it correctly.

Feeding it correctly is a stamping exercise. Take every cost in the question and stamp it: FIXED-TOTAL goes on top of the fraction, VARIABLE-PER-UNIT goes into the contribution underneath. The three classic wrecks are all stamping errors: dropping the selling-and-distribution R8 because it isn't "factory"; dragging rent or depreciation into the per-unit figure; or dividing by the selling price instead of the contribution. Twenty seconds of stamping defeats all three.

Two finishing touches gather the remaining marks. If the division isn't exact, round UP — at ten thousand comma anything you are still a stone short, so the survival line is the next whole unit. And never hand in a naked answer: set it beside planned production and name the cushion. Ten thousand against a plan of twenty thousand is a fifty percent margin of safety — that one sentence is the interpretation mark, sitting there waiting.

What-if changes are the same fraction, re-fed: a price cut or cost rise shrinks the throw; new rent grows the wall. Recalculate calmly and compare the new line with the old. One fraction, stamped inputs, rounded up, compared with the plan. That is the whole topic. You've got this!
