Hey, it's Bianca — manufacturing looks like a whole new subject: new accounts, new vocabulary, costs flying in every direction. It isn't. It is one path, walked by one rand, and you already know the arithmetic of every step from trading stock.

Draw the path before you touch a single number: shelves, floor, store, customer. Raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods, cost of sales. Every figure in a manufacturing question lives at one of those four stations, and each station runs the grammar you have used since grade 10: what was there, plus what arrived, minus what remains, equals what moved on. Four stations, one grammar — that is the entire ledger.

Sorting costs is two quick questions, asked separately. Question one: can I point at this cost inside the product? Yes — direct: canvas, machinists' wages. No, but it keeps the factory running — overhead: supervisor, rent, machine depreciation, oil and needles. Not factory at all — office is admin, reaching customers is selling, and neither ever enters production cost. Question two: does it grow when output grows? That is variable or fixed — a separate question entirely, so never let "fixed" trick you into saying "not production".

When a figure is missing — one always is — don't guess. Write the station's grammar with the three figures you have and the fourth falls out on its own. Questions hide figures precisely because the grammar recovers them; that recovery is the skill being marked.

And keep the unit cost close: cost of finished production divided by units completed. It is the factory's heartbeat, and half the interpretation marks come from noticing it creep and naming a suspect. You've got this!
