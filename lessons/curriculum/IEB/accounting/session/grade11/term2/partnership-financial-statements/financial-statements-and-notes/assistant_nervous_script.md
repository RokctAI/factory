Hey, it's Bianca — I know the statements can feel like a formatting test stapled on top of an accounting test, and one misplaced line seems to sink the whole page. Take a breath. The format is fixed, and that is actually the good news: a fixed thing can be learned once and reused forever.

Open every answer with the heading, and ask the two-word question: period or moment? The income statement covers a period — FOR the year ended. The financial position is a moment — ON the date. That mark is yours before a single figure is written.

Then lean on the skeleton. Income statement: sales, less cost of sales, gross profit, plus other income, less operating expenses, operating profit, less interest, net profit. Financial position: two towers — assets on one side, equity and liabilities on the other — and they must weigh the same. If your towers disagree, don't panic-stare at the whole page: the difference is itself a clue. Half the difference often points to a sign error; the full amount points to something left out.

The notes are not extra admin — they are where marks hide. Every big face figure has a note proving it: fixed assets climbing to R755 000, the current accounts closing at R89 800. Face and note must meet to the rand, and writing that trail out in full is exactly what gets rewarded.

Two last snares. Partners' salaries and interest on capital never enter the income statement — they are appropriations, living in the current accounts note. And debtors go on the face NET of the provision — show the honest number, prove it in the note.

Format questions repay preparation more reliably than any other kind. Learn the skeleton cold, and the figures simply take their seats. You've got this!
