Hey, it's Bianca — I know the ratio lesson can feel like a formula landslide: percentages, periods, rates, ratios, all coming down at once. Stand still for a moment. There are only three families, and each family answers one human question.

Family one, profitability: out of every rand of sales, how much survives? Gross margin, expense percentage, net margin — all slices of the same rand. Family two, tempo: how fast does money lap the business? Stock days, debtor days, creditor days — three stopwatches on one journey. Family three, structure: whose money is in the business and what did it earn? Debt-equity for the risk, return on average equity for the reward, the liquidity ratios for survival. Before touching any formula, ask which family the question lives in — the sorting alone stops the landslide.

The formulas share one recurring trick: when the top of the fraction is a whole year's flow — cost of sales, credit sales, net profit — the bottom wants an AVERAGE, opening plus closing over two. Snapshot over snapshot, like the current ratio, needs no averaging. That single rule covers every average you will ever be asked for.

For comments, never freeze — play the four-beat bar. The figure next to its comparison. Whether that is better or worse. A sensible cause. A piece of advice. Four short sentences, and the marks usually sit one per beat. Remember that nobody can reward a judgement you never wrote — say "this is an improvement" in actual words.

One last kindness: if your calculated ratio looks strange, comment on the number you calculated and stay consistent — interpretation marks follow your own figure. An arithmetic slip costs one mark; abandoning the comment costs all of them. You've got this!
