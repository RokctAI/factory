Today the business starts trusting its customers: goods now, money at month end. Selling on credit brings two new books of first entry — the Debtors Journal for credit sales and the Debtors Allowances Journal for goods returned and allowances granted — plus a second ledger with a personal page for every customer. We run a full credit month at a general store: invoices that give birth to debts, cost of sales riding along at a 50 percent mark-up, a returned shirt that reverses both the money story and the shelf story, and a scratched kettle where only the price moves. We finish with the beautiful lock at the heart of the system: the list of debtors' pages must always equal the one control line in the general ledger — the same promises, counted two ways.
