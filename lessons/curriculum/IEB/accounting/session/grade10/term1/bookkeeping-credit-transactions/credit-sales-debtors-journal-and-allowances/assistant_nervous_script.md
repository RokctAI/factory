Credit sales feel like double the work — two journals, two ledgers, invoices, credit notes — but there are really only two ideas hiding in all of it. First: every credit sale is the same two truths you already know from the till, except the money truth says "owed" instead of "received". Second: every promise is counted twice — once on the customer's own page, once in the street's one control line — and those two counts must always agree.

For returns and allowances, don't memorise rules; just follow the goods. Did the goods come back? Then the cost comes back too — stock in, cost of sales out. Did the customer keep the goods? Then only the price moves, and the cost column stays empty.

Take the practice questions one line at a time: name first, amount second, cost third. And if a question asks about control measures, think like a shopkeeper: limits before trust, statements every month, chase what's overdue. You've got this — go show that book of names who's boss.
