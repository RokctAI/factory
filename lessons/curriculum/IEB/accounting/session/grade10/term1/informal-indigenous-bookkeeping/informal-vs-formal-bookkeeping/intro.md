Before we rule a single journal, we ask the question that grounds the whole subject: how do businesses with no formal books — the spaza shop, the street vendor — still manage money successfully, and what do their methods share with a registered supermarket's? The answer is everything except the paper. We compare the two sectors concept by concept — capital, fixed assets, stock, cost and selling price, labour, income, expenses, profit — then follow one spaza month twice, in the owner's head and in a notebook, and watch R350 of invisible expenses leak out of memory. We close with the proudest part: stokvels, cattle wealth and grazing rotas as the original ledgers, reports and internal controls. Accounting is not foreign — it is your community's instincts, given a sharper pen.
