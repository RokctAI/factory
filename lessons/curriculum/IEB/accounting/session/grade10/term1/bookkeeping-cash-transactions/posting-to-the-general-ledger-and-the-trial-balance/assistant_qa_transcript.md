**Thandi:** Question time! First from the class: why do we bother balancing the Bank account but leave Sales just sitting there unbalanced?

**Tutor:** Because the two accounts answer different questions on different clocks. Bank is a balance-sheet account: it describes something the business HAS, and the owner needs that answer continuously — so each month we balance it and carry the figure forward; the story never ends, it rolls on. Sales is a nominal account: it MEASURES performance for a period. Balancing it monthly would serve nobody — its job is to accumulate the year's selling until year end, when it closes into the final accounts and starts the new year at nil. So remember the split: balance-sheet accounts balance and carry forward; nominal accounts accumulate and close. Two sections, two habits, one tidy system.

***

**Thandi:** Next: the cost-of-sales double posting keeps catching people. Why does Trading Stock get the credit and not Bank?

**Tutor:** Follow what physically happened. When goods walked out with customers, no money left the business — money came IN, and the Bank debit from the receipts side already records that. What LEFT was stock: three thousand rand of goods, at cost, off the shelf. The credit must therefore land on Trading Stock, the account that watches the shelf, and its partner is the Cost of Sales debit, the expense measuring what those goods had cost. Pair them in your mind permanently: debit Cost of Sales, credit Trading Stock, joined at the hip. The moment you try to credit Bank for cost of sales, ask yourself — did the business pay anyone when the goods sold? No. Then Bank stays out of it.

***

**Thandi:** Here's the one everyone argued about at break: if the trial balance balances, the month is correct — we proved it, right?

**Tutor:** You proved something real but narrow: that every posted debit had an equal posted credit, and the additions are consistent. Arithmetic equality — nothing more. Now line up the errors that sail straight through: rent posted to Repairs, correct side, wrong account — balances. A whole transaction never journalised — both halves absent equally — balances. A sale captured twice — both halves doubled — balances. The trial balance is one guard on one gate. The other gates have their own guards: folio references to chase filed amounts, numbered documents to expose missing transactions, and the bank statement reconciliation testing your Bank account against the bank's own record. Level scales are necessary. They are never the whole proof.

***

**Thandi:** Last one: when the columns disagree, where should we actually start looking — just re-add everything?

**Tutor:** Re-adding is the last resort, not the first. Start by taking the exact difference between the columns — the gap is a fingerprint. Gap equal to one of your amounts? That amount was posted once instead of twice; find its lonely half. Gap divisible by two? Halve it and hunt that amount on the wrong side — a side error counts double. Gap divisible by nine? Digits transposed: four thousand two hundred captured as two thousand four hundred leaves exactly one thousand eight hundred, which nine divides. Only when the gap answers none of the three questions do you re-cast the columns and re-check the balances one by one. Method before muscle — the gap usually names its own culprit.
