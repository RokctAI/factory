Today the theory becomes a working book: the Cash Receipts Journal, the daily record of every rand a business receives. We follow a month at a small general store — cash sales at a 50 percent mark-up on cost, the owner's capital deposit, the tenant's rent — and learn where every figure lands: the analysis column that tracks what arrives, the Bank column that tracks what gets deposited, dedicated columns for the daily regulars and a sundry column for the named visitors. We strip mark-ups by division to find cost of sales, and we close with the cross-cast, the month-end handshake that proves the journal before a single total is posted. One line, two truths, one self-checking book.
