Money out demands tighter discipline than money in, and today we build the book that enforces it: the Cash Payments Journal, where every rand leaving the bank account is named, numbered and explained. We work through a full month at a general store — stock from the wholesaler, weekly wages, rent, new shelving, and the owner paying herself — and we meet the trap that catches more learners than any other: telling drawings apart from expenses by asking what the business actually received. Then the small money gets its own machine: the petty cash tin on the imprest system, where cash plus vouchers must always equal the fixed float, so the tin audits itself every time the lid opens.
