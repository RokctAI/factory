An owner tells his bookkeeper to leave the Saturday cash sales out of the books "so the tax comes down" — and in that one sentence, several promises break at once. This lesson names them. We study the code of ethics, the formal statement of values binding every party in the financial environment, and the seven characteristics this topic runs on: leadership, discipline, transparency, accountability, fairness, sustainability and responsible management. Financial information only has value if it can be trusted, so each characteristic is defined for marks, tested against real scenarios — a hardware store hiding sales, a repair shop fitting second-hand parts, a car wash and a storm drain — and then rebuilt through pictures you already know: a team captain, a till slip, a tuck-shop bargain that costs a reputation. A four-move method turns it all into full-sentence answers that hold up under marking.
