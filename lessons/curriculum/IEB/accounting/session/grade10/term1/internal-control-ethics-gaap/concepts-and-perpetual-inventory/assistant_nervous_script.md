Yes, there were a lot of words today. But almost every one of them is just the formal name for something you could explain to a friend in a sentence. Assets: what the business has. Liabilities: what it owes. Equity: the owner's stake, always moving. Debit and credit: literally left and right. If a definition question comes up, answer in plain words first, then dress it up.

The two spots where marks hide: the discounts and the perpetual system. For discounts, ask when the reduction happened — before the sale was recorded, it's trade discount and invisible; after, on an existing debt, it's cash discount and it gets recorded. For the perpetual system, remember the fridge counter: every sale writes TWO things — the money in at selling price, the cost out of stock. Two truths, always.
