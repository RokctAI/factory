One clerk at Zwane Building Supplies orders the cement, signs for the deliveries, and pays the suppliers — and nobody would ever know if a load never arrived. That gap has a name, and this lesson teaches you to see it anywhere. We define internal control and its four objectives, build the five components — control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, monitoring — and stock the working toolkit, from division of duties to numbered documents and surprise counts. Then the whole system is rebuilt from a school tuck shop where one learner runs everything and the float comes up short, five plain questions replace the formal names, and three patrols train your eye to find the weakness, state the risk, and recommend the fix — the three-move answer this topic always demands.
