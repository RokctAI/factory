Six principles sounds like six things to memorise, but here's the trick: you're not memorising rules, you're recognising faces. The grandfather who only quotes what he paid — historical cost. The grandmother who never counts unhatched chickens — prudence. The uncle who won't fuss over a R60 calculator — materiality. The aunt with two purses — business entity. The father planting trees for future years — going concern. The cousin with the calendar — matching. Say the family out loud twice and the list is done.

Then the questions become a game of "who just spoke?" Read the scenario, underline what the accountant did or refused to do, and ask which relative would say that. If a story has three parts, split it — each part gets its own relative; never smear one principle over the whole thing.

Go play the matching game in the practice questions. You've met these six people your whole life — now they just have accounting names. You've got this.
