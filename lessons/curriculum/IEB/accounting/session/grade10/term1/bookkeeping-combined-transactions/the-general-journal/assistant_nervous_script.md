The General Journal looks intimidating because there are no columns to catch you — you have to name both sides yourself. But that's actually good news: it's just the five-step analysis you've been doing for weeks, written out in full. Which two accounts? Which direction each? Does the equation hold? Then one honest sentence underneath saying why. That's a complete entry.

The classics all follow patterns you already know. Bad debt: an asset dies, an expense is born — no cash anywhere. Drawings of stock: the owner's pocket again, just in goods, and always at cost. Interest: whoever is late, their debt grows — income if it's owed to us, expense if we're the slow ones.

And for corrections, remember the surgeon's rule: fix only the broken leg. Work out what WAS done, what SHOULD have been done, and write only the entry that bridges the two. Head into the practice questions now — narrate every answer like you're explaining it to a friend, and the marks will follow. You've got this.
