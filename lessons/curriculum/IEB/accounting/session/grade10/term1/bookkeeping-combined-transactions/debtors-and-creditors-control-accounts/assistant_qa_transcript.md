**Thandi:** Class questions! The first one is very honest: if the personal accounts already hold everything, why keep a control account at all — isn't it pure duplication?

**Tutor:** It is duplication, and that is precisely the point — but there are two reasons, not one. The convenience reason: the owner's most common question is not about one customer, it is about all of them at once, and the control account holds that answer permanently ready — one balance, no forty-page addition. The control reason, which gives the account its name: because the two records are built independently — pages line by line daily, control by monthly journal totals — they must agree at month end, and that forced agreement is a monthly audit costing nothing extra. A single record can be quietly wrong for a year. Two independent records that must reconcile expose each other's errors while they are fresh.

***

**Thandi:** Next: the bad debt confuses everyone. If we never got the money, how can we take it OUT of debtors? It feels like we're deleting evidence.

**Tutor:** Think about what the debtors balance claims. A debit balance says: this money is an asset — it is coming. When a debtor genuinely cannot pay, that claim becomes a lie, and prudence forbids the books from telling it. So the write-off does two honest things at once: it credits Debtors Control, removing the dead claim from the asset, and it debits Bad Debts, admitting the loss as an expense of the period. Nothing is deleted — the whole story is preserved: we sold, we tried to collect, we failed, and the failure cost us R1 200. What we refuse to do is keep the corpse on the books dressed as an asset. Overstated debtors flatter the business today and ambush it tomorrow.

***

**Thandi:** Here's the sharp one: at month end the list gives R31 800 and control gives R30 600. Which one is right?

**Tutor:** Neither — yet. That is the crucial discipline: a disagreement convicts the BOOKS, not a side. Either record could hold the error: a line missed on a page, a journal column miscast into control, a receipt posted twice on one road only. So you investigate, and the gap is your first witness. Twelve hundred rand — scan the month for that figure. The bad debt was exactly R1 200, so check both roads it should have travelled: it reached the journal totals, but the customer's page was never credited. The page is wrong; correct it, recount, and both records land on R30 600. The rule: never adjust either side to force agreement — find the passenger that fell off, and put it back where it belongs.

***

**Thandi:** Last question: do we really have to learn the creditors control account separately, or is it honestly just the debtors one flipped?

**Tutor:** It is honestly the mirror — if you understand rather than memorise. The machinery is identical: personal pages in a subsidiary ledger, one summary line in the general ledger, daily detail, monthly totals, month-end agreement with the list. Only the direction of the debt flips, and every side flips with it: creditors are a liability, so the balance lives on the credit side; credit purchases make us owe more — credit; payments to suppliers and returns make us owe less — debit. So do not learn two accounts. Learn one question with two costumes: for debtors, do they owe us more or less? For creditors, do we owe them more or less? Answer that, and both mirrors show you the correct side every time.
