Every month produces events that fit none of the specialised journals: a debtor who vanishes owing R1 100, interest charged on overdue accounts in both directions, the owner carrying R480 of stock home for supper, and a filing error that must be fixed without ever touching an eraser. Today we open the book built for exactly these moments — the General Journal, where you name both sides of every entry yourself and defend it with a one-sentence narration. We work the classic entries as analyses rather than recipes, learn the surgeon's rule for correcting errors in pen, run the control columns to their month-end posting, and prove the whole journal balances. Six journals complete the system: every transaction now has exactly one front door.
