Hey, it's Thandi — depreciation has a scary reputation, but did you notice what today actually asked of you? Three numbers and one question. That's the whole topic.

The three numbers: what it COST — written in ink on day one, never moves. What it has LOST — the running total of wear. What it's still WORTH — cost minus the wear. Receipt, wear, worth. Every question is asking for exactly one of them, so read the question twice and pick the right one.

The one question: WHICH number am I slicing? Straight-line always slices the original cost — thirty thousand off the bakkie, every year, level. Diminishing balance slices what's LEFT — thirty, then twenty-four, then nineteen two. Say the base out loud before you cut and the year-two trap can never touch you.

And mid-year arrivals? Just count the months on your fingers. The computer worked four months, so it gets four-twelfths of a year's wear — R1 200 instead of R3 600. Assets pay for the months they worked, like anyone else.

The practice questions are waiting: name the method, write the base, count the months, label the answer. Four small habits, and the biggest adjustment in the book becomes routine. You've got this!
