Twelve months of routine bookkeeping end today, and the yearly clock strikes: the bridge from trial balance to financial statements. The bridge has a fixed sequence — pre-adjustment trial balance, adjustments, post-adjustment trial balance, final accounts, then the statements — and we walk it in full at Zanele's Traders. Two adjustments bring the books level with reality: three hundred rand of unused stationery becomes an asset under the matching principle, and a stocktake that counts less than the books claim forces a trading stock deficit under prudence. Then the Trading account isolates a gross profit of fifty thousand, Profit and Loss nets it down to thirty-seven thousand six hundred, and the profit lands where it has always belonged — in Capital. A fixed order, a principle at every step, and the year's one essential number computed inside the ledger itself.
