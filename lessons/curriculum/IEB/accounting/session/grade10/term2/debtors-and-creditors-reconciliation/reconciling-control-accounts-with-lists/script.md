# Part 1 — Expert

Since the control accounts first appeared, one law has stood over them: the control account and its list of personal balances must agree. Today is about the month when they refuse to — and about the disciplined procedure that hunts down every error, corrects each record on its own side, and walks both to the single true figure. Reconciliation stays with you in every grade from here to matric, and the method you build today is the same one you will still be using in your final exams.

## Subtopic: Two Records, Two Kinds of Error

Hold the architecture clearly, because the entire method hangs from it. Debtors Control in the general ledger is assembled from JOURNAL COLUMN TOTALS, posted once a month. The list of debtors is assembled from the PERSONAL PAGES, which receive their entries line by line, every day. Two completely independent journeys, both starting from the same source documents — which is exactly why agreement certifies both records, and why disagreement proves an error on at least one of the journeys.

So the diagnostic question for every error you uncover is simply: WHERE did it happen — on the totals' journey, or on the pages' journey? An error inside a journal TOTAL — a column added up short or long, a total posted incorrectly to control — poisons the CONTROL account, while the pages, built from the correct individual lines, stay healthy. So you correct the control. An error in POSTING TO A PAGE — a line landed on the wrong person, an amount transposed on the page, a line never carried across at all — poisons the LIST, while control, fed from correct totals, stays healthy. So you correct the list. And the third family: an error made in the ORIGINAL LINE, before any totalling — a wrong amount written into the journal entry itself — flows down BOTH journeys and must be repaired in both records.

That classification — totals-side, pages-side, or both — is the whole intellectual content of reconciliation. Everything that follows it is careful arithmetic.

One discipline before we start: neither record outranks the other. The instinct is to trust the control account and bend the list toward it. Resist that — either record can carry the fault, and only the evidence decides. The true figure is the one where both records, each corrected independently, finally MEET.

Pause here — questions on the two journeys and the three error families are with you now. Classify each one by asking where the error physically happened.

## Subtopic: A Worked Reconciliation — the Debtors Side

Month end at Zanele's Traders. Debtors Control stands at nine thousand eight hundred and sixty rand debit. The list of debtors adds to nine thousand three hundred and fifty. Five hundred and ten rand apart — so an investigation begins. It produces four findings; classify and treat each one.

Finding one: the Debtors Journal's sales column was UNDERCAST by three hundred rand — the individual lines add to three hundred more than the total that was struck. Where did it happen? On the totals' journey: the personal pages were posted from the correct lines, but control received a total three hundred rand too small. Treatment: correct the CONTROL — debit Debtors Control three hundred. Control moves to ten thousand one hundred and sixty.

Finding two: a bad debt of two hundred and sixty rand was written off in the General Journal and duly posted to the debtor's personal page — but the control column of the GJ was overlooked, so Debtors Control never heard the news. Totals' journey again: control still carries a debt the pages have already buried. Treatment: credit CONTROL two hundred and sixty. Control moves to nine thousand nine hundred.

Finding three: an invoice for six hundred and forty rand to M. Pillay was correctly entered in the DJ — so it reached the totals and control — but was never carried to his personal page. Pages' journey: the list is missing a debt. Treatment: correct the LIST — add six hundred and forty to Pillay's balance. The list moves to nine thousand nine hundred and ninety.

Finding four: an invoice of four hundred and fifty rand to S. Daniels was written on her page as five hundred and forty — two digits swapped places on the page only; the journal line and the totals were right. Pages' journey. Treatment: reduce the list by ninety rand. The list moves to nine thousand nine hundred.

And the meeting: control, corrected, nine thousand nine hundred. List, corrected, nine thousand nine hundred. Agreement — and notice that neither original figure was the truth; the truth only appeared when the two corrected records met. Set your working out in exactly this shape under exam conditions: one correction statement for control starting at nine thousand eight hundred and sixty, one for the list starting at nine thousand three hundred and fifty, every adjustment named, both ending on the same final line.

Stop for this section's questions now — the four findings return to you: classify, treat, and track both running totals to the meeting point.

## Subtopic: The Creditors Mirror and the Supplier's Statement

The creditors side runs the identical method reflected in a mirror. Creditors Control is fed by the CJ, CAJ, CPJ and GJ totals; the list of creditors by the personal pages. The same three error families apply, the same classification question, the same rule: repair each record on its own side and meet in the middle. Only the orientation flips: balances sit on the credit side, and the standing question is what WE owe.

One worked touch in the mirror. Creditors Control shows six thousand one hundred and eighty rand credit; the list totals five thousand eight hundred and ninety. The investigation finds the CPJ's creditors column overcast by one hundred and twenty rand. Reason the direction from first principles: payments to creditors are DEBITED to control; an overcast column means too large a debit went in, which shrank the credit balance too far — so restore it by crediting Creditors Control one hundred and twenty. Control moves to six thousand three hundred. The second finding: an invoice from K. Naidu for four hundred and ten rand was captured in the CJ but never posted to her page — the list rises by four hundred and ten to six thousand three hundred. The meeting is made. Notice what the mirror demanded: every correction's direction re-derived from scratch, never copied across from the debtors side — questions are built precisely to punish the copier.

And the creditors side carries an extra, external lock: the supplier's monthly STATEMENT — their record of our account — laid against their page in our books. Differences come from timing, such as our payment still in transit or their latest invoice not yet in our post, and from genuine errors on either side. That statement reconciliation grows into a major skill in the senior grades; meet it today as the same principle stretched further — independent records, compared on a schedule, every difference explained item by item.

The questions on this section are in front of you now — mirror thinking throughout: derive each correction's direction fresh; transplant nothing.

## Subtopic: The Method, Compressed — and Why It Matters

The full procedure, ready for any question. One: state both uncorrected figures and the difference between them. Two: classify every finding — totals journey, pages journey, or original line — by asking where the error physically happened. Three: correct the control account for totals-side errors in a labelled statement, and correct the list for pages-side errors in another. Four: the two statements must end on the SAME figure — the true balance; if they refuse to, a finding was misclassified or an arithmetic slip crept in, and you re-examine before going anywhere. Five: only that corrected, agreed figure flows onward to the trial balance and, later, the financial statements.

Bring the error fingerprints along too: a difference exactly matching one amount points to an unposted item; a difference divisible by nine hints at transposed digits; divisible by two, a right amount on the wrong side. Read the difference before you hunt — it often names its own culprit.

And why does this topic carry weight in every grade? Because reconciliation is internal control turned into a monthly habit. Errors are inevitable in any live system; the honesty of the bookkeeping lies not in never erring, but in CATCHING every error within the month, while documents are fresh and memories are young. A business that reconciles its debtors, its creditors and — from next year — its bank account every month is a business whose books can be trusted between audits. And it makes a beautiful question: every scenario is a difference, a list of findings, and a demand for classified, correctly-directed corrections.

The final questions of this part are with you now: full reconciliations on both sides — classify, correct, meet, and state the one true figure.

# Part 2 — Simplifier

Now run the same investigation as a story — because a reconciliation really is a small detective case: two witnesses, two different answers, and the truth waiting for whoever asks the right questions in the right order.

## Subtopic: Two Witnesses, Different Stories

Month end. Zanele asks her books one question, two ways. The summary line — the control account — answers: your customers owe you nine thousand eight hundred and sixty rand. The pages, added one by one, answer: nine thousand three hundred and fifty. Five hundred and ten rand between two records that are supposed to be twins. Somebody is wrong.

First discipline of the detective: do not choose a favourite witness. The control line is not "more official" than the pages, and the pages are not "closer to the ground" than the line. Each was built along its own road — the line from monthly totals, the pages from daily entries — and either road can hide a pothole. The five hundred and ten proves that at least one error exists; it says nothing about where.

Second discipline: treat the difference as a CLUE, not just a nuisance. Five hundred and ten is an awkward number — probably not one clean slip but a few of them, tangled together. So the detective does the unglamorous thing: back through the month's paperwork, item by item, looking for anything that travelled one road but not the other, or travelled with the wrong number on its back.

And know in advance how the case must end — not with one witness surrendering to the other, but with BOTH stories corrected until, independently, they tell the same truth. That meeting point is the only number worth writing down.

Quick check before we carry on — questions on the setup are with you right now: two witnesses, two roads, and what the difference does and does not prove.

## Subtopic: Four Potholes, Each on Its Own Road

The investigation turns up four potholes. For each, one question only: WHICH ROAD has the hole — the totals road that feeds the line, or the daily road that feeds the pages?

Pothole one: the Debtors Journal's sales column was added up three hundred rand short. The pages never felt this — they were written up from the correct lines before the faulty total was ever struck. The totals road is holed: the LINE is three hundred too small. Patch the line: up three hundred, to ten thousand one hundred and sixty.

Pothole two: a customer's two hundred and sixty rand debt was written off and his page duly closed — but the entry never reached the control totals. The page has buried the debt; the line still expects the money. Totals road again: line down two hundred and sixty, to nine thousand nine hundred.

Pothole three: Pillay's six hundred and forty rand invoice went into the journal — the line knows all about it — but nobody carried it to his page. The daily road is holed: the pages are six hundred and forty light. Patch the pages: Pillay up six hundred and forty, running total nine thousand nine hundred and ninety.

Pothole four: Daniels bought for four hundred and fifty, but her page reads five hundred and forty — two digits swapped in the copying. The journal line was right, so the line is untouched; only her page exaggerates. Pages down ninety — nine thousand nine hundred.

Now look at the ending. The line, patched only where its own road was holed, reads nine thousand nine hundred. The pages, patched only on theirs, read nine thousand nine hundred. Neither witness was bullied into agreement — each was corrected exactly where it was wrong, and the truth appeared at the meeting: R9 900, a number that neither original answer contained.

Your questions for this part are up now: four fresh potholes — name the road, patch the right record, and keep both running totals alive as you go.

## Subtopic: The Meeting in the Middle

Why must the case close on ONE number? Because tomorrow the shop acts on it. The trial balance wants the debtors figure; statements go out to customers; credit-limit decisions lean on the total. No business can act on "either nine-eight-six-oh or nine-three-five-oh, we're not sure." The reconciliation turns two unreliable answers into one reliable one — and it documents HOW, finding by finding, so that anyone can retrace the case a year later.

And underneath the loud lesson, hear the quiet one: every error in this case was CAUGHT, inside the month, by routine — not by luck, not by an angry customer, not by an auditor long afterwards. That is the purpose of the whole exercise. Mistakes are not the enemy; undiscovered mistakes are. A shop that reconciles monthly gives every mistake a lifespan of thirty days at most.

The creditors side is the same case reflected: the line of what WE owe against the pages of our suppliers — the same potholes possible on both roads, the same patching rule, the same meeting in the middle. Just re-derive every direction inside the mirror: creditors' balances live on the credit side, so "too much" and "too little" swap their consequences. Never carry a debtors fix across unexamined; think each one fresh.

And once a month a third witness arrives in the post: the supplier's own statement of your account, built entirely inside THEIR office. Compare it against their page in your books and explain every difference by name: your payment still travelling, their invoice not yet arrived, or a true error on one side or the other. Three independent records of one trading relationship — that is how business partners stay friends.

And here come the last questions of the lesson, right now: run a complete case — two witnesses, several potholes, both roads patched, one meeting point stated and carried forward. Close the file the way a detective would: every rand of difference explained, nothing left dangling. Case closed is month closed.
