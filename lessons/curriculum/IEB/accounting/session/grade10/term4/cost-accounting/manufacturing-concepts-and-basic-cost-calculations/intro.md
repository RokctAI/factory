Today the workshop door opens: what changes when a business makes what it sells? Cost accounting starts with two sortings. The first asks where a cost attaches — timber and the carpenter's wage traceable to one stool are direct; glue, the foreman and the workshop rent serve every stool and gather as factory overheads. The second asks how a cost behaves — variables scale with output while fixed costs stand still, which is why busy months make each unit cheaper and quiet months quietly eat the margin. Between the sortings sit the layered calculations: prime cost, total cost of production, and the unit cost that puts a floor under every price, with work-in-progress holding the half-made units at month-end. By the end you will classify on both axes, build the cost statement, rerun it at a new volume, and advise like a decision-maker.
