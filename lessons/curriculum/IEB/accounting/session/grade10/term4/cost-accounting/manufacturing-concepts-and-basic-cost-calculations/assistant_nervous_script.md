Hey, it's Thandi — new topic, new vocabulary, and if the bins feel like a lot, breathe: the whole thing runs on ONE pointing question. Can I point at ONE stool — or one samoosa — and say "this cost is yours"? Yes: direct. No, it serves the whole floor: overhead. Timber and the carpenter — direct. Glue, the foreman, the rent — overheads. And anything outside the workshop, like the sales assistant, isn't a making cost at all. That's the first sorting, done.

The calculation is three layers, always in the same order: direct materials plus direct labour — prime cost; add the overheads — total cost of production; divide by the units — unit cost. For the workshop: 18 000 plus 9 000 is 27 000; plus 8 400 is 35 400; over 300 stools — R118 each. Write the three labels and the marks stack up with the layers.

Second sorting, one sentence each: variables scale with the work but stay the same per unit; fixed costs stand still in total but spread thinner per unit when you make more. That's the whole reason busy months are cheap per stool and quiet months are expensive — same rent, different number of shoulders carrying it.

In the what-if question, write V or F next to every cost first — V-costs multiply, F-costs copy across. Do that and the classic trap can't touch you. One pointing question, three layers, two letters. Go run the practice order — you've got this!
