Hey, it's Thandi — revision time, and if the whole year feels like too much to hold, here's the secret: it was never ten topics. It's ONE machine with five stations, and you already know every station. Sort the slips. Write the diaries. Gather the ledger. Weigh the trial balance. Count the street twice. That's it — that's the year.

Under pressure, lean on the two sorting questions: did cash move, or a promise? And which way did the value travel? Those two questions choose the journal every single time — no memorising eight transaction types, just two questions asked calmly. Then the compass for the pairs: assets, expenses and drawings grow on the debit side; equity, liabilities and income grow on the credit side. Which two accounts, which one increases — ask it and the entry writes itself.

And when the trial balance or the control accounts don't agree in a question, that's not the exam going wrong — that IS the question. The difference gives directions: journal-total errors live in the control, page errors live in the list. Send each error to its address and watch the counts meet. That meeting is you proving your own answer before anyone marks it.

Run the practice month through the machine once, slowly, and you'll feel the stations click. The exam is the same machine with different slips. You've got this!
