Hey, it's Thandi — four more formulas on top of last lesson's five, and I know the pile looks heavy. So do what analysts do: shrink it. Tonight was three QUESTIONS, not four formulas.

Question one: can this year's bills be paid? That's liquidity — and its two ratios are one question asked twice, once trusting the stock, once not. Current assets over current liabilities; then again with the inventories set aside. If you remember "I can't pay the rent with tins of beans", the acid test is yours for life.

Question two: could everyone be paid, ever, in the end? Total assets over total liabilities. One-to-one is the cliff edge. Everything above it is just degrees of comfort.

Question three: was the owner's money in the right place? Profit over the AVERAGE stake — average, because the stake moved during the year; write "(22 000 + 28 000) ÷ 2" and that classic lost mark is safely banked. Then set it against the bank's safe rate and keep the one wise sentence: risk deserves its wage.

Your formula summary holds every formula, so your real job is matching question to gauge: bills → liquidity, survival → solvency, worth-it → return. And in comments, both directions count: too low is danger, too high can be laziness — saying so is exactly what "meaningful comment" means.

Nine gauges now, and you can read the whole dashboard. That's a term of real skill. Go show the practice questions who's boss — you've got this!
