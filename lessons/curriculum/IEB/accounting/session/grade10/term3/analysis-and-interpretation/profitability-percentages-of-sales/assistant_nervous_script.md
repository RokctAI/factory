Hey, it's Thandi — five formulas, comparison bases, four-move comments... if analysis feels like a whole new subject bolted onto Accounting, here's the calming truth: nobody is testing your memory tonight. Your formula summary carries every formula. What's being tested is three small skills, and each one is practisable.

Skill one: picking the right formula — and the question always tells you which. Pricing or mark-up mentioned? Gross percentages. Costs mentioned? Expenses on sales. The whole year's result? Net on sales. Match the word in the question to the gauge; that's the entire trick.

Skill two: substituting the right figures. Label them as you write — "gross profit 20 000, sales 60 000" — because the classic lost mark is a right formula fed a wrong number. Labels are free insurance.

Skill three: the comment — and it's a recipe, not inspiration. What happened, so what, why — from the story they gave you! — now what. Four short sentences. Write them as four lines and you can literally watch yourself collect the marks one by one.

And keep the hundred-rand picture close: sixty-seven replaces the goods, thirty-three is margin, twenty-five runs the shop, seventeen-fifty stays. If you can tell that little story, you understand profitability better than most adults with bank accounts. The practice questions are the same story with different splits. You've got this!
