Hi, Mandy here — I know the company chapter feels like moving to a new school. Different vocabulary, a tax office in the middle of your ledger, and everyone whispering about buy-backs. But look closer: it is four new transactions, and each one runs on a single rule you can memorise tonight.

Share issue: shares times issue price, debit Bank, credit Ordinary Share Capital. No par value means the whole price goes into capital — there is no premium account to worry about, ever.

Buy-back: before you touch the entry, calculate the average issue price — total share capital divided by total shares in issue. Capital gives up the shares bought back times that average; the amount you paid above the average comes out of retained income; Bank gets the full credit. Then cancel the shares and lower your share count.

Tax: provisional payments on one side, the final charge at 27 percent on the other. Whichever side is bigger tells you who owes whom — work it out, never guess the direction.

Dividends: cents per share times the shares in issue ON THE DECLARATION DATE. If shares were issued or bought back during the year, the interim and final counts will differ — checking the date is the mark everyone else drops.

And the capitalised loan? Opening balance, plus interest, minus instalments. The interest is an expense even though it never left the bank on its own.

Four rules. Say each one out loud once, then go prove it to yourself in the practice questions — they are waiting for you now.
