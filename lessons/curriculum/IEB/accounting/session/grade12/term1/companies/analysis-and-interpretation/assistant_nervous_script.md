Hi, Mandy here — analysis looks like a wall of formulas, but let me tell you the secret the strong candidates all know: nobody passes this topic by memorising fractions. They pass by asking the six questions behind the fractions.

Can it pay this month? Current ratio and acid-test. Is the money moving? Stock turnover, debtors' days, creditors' days — read as one sentence, not three. Would it survive a storm? Solvency. What did the owners earn? ROSHE, against the fixed deposit. What does the whole engine earn? ROTCE, against the cost of the loans. And what is one share earning, receiving, and worth? EPS, DPS, NAV.

That is the whole lesson. Every formula is just one of those questions written in arithmetic.

Then the move that actually collects the marks: figure, comparison, judgement. Quote your number. Put another number next to it — last year, the norm, the interest rate, the share price. Say which is bigger and what that means. Three sentences. If you catch yourself writing a ratio with no comparison after it, stop — you have done the half that earns the fewest marks and skipped the half that earns the most.

One more comfort: the numbers in these questions are chosen to work out. If your ROSHE comes to something wild, check the average equity and try again calmly.

Six questions, three sentences each. Go run the practice set now and time how quickly the pattern starts repeating — it will surprise you.
