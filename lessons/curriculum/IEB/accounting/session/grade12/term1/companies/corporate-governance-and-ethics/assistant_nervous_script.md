Hi, Mandy here — governance and ethics is the topic people underestimate, then love, because it is the one part of accounting with no arithmetic to slip on. It is pure structure, and the structure fits on a postcard.

One split starts everything: owners are not runners. Shareholders keep the votes, the AGM, and the right to appoint the auditor; directors get the daily running and owe fiduciary duty in return. Every rule in the topic grows out of that split.

Four demands to know like your own name: responsibility, accountability, fairness, transparency. Practise turning each into a question — who took charge, who answered, who was left out, what was hidden?

Five personal principles: integrity, objectivity, competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. And the two share offences — insider trading is dealing on secret information; manipulation is manufacturing a false price.

Three layers of checking: controls every day, internal audit testing all year for the audit committee, the independent auditor reporting to the shareholders once a year. Follow the reporting lines — every arrow points at the owners.

Then the move that turns knowledge into marks, for every scenario: problem, principle, remedy. Say what happened, name what it broke, prescribe what fixes it. Three sentences, in that order, every single time.

That is the whole topic. Go to the practice questions now and run problem-principle-remedy on each scenario — by the third one it will feel automatic, because it is.
