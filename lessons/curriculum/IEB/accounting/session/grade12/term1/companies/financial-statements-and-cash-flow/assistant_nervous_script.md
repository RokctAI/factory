Hi, Mandy here — a full set of company statements looks like a mountain, but climb it in the order the books climb it and every step is one you already know.

Start where the year starts: adjustments. Matching, nothing else — this year's income, this year's expenses. The provision for bad debts moves by its movement, and last year's accruals get reversed. That is grade 11 skill wearing a new blazer.

The income statement is a staircase with three landings: gross profit, operating profit, net profit after tax. Learn the landings and the steps between them fill themselves in. And keep dividends off the staircase completely — they belong in the retained income note.

The balance sheet is a photograph that must balance. Build assets top to bottom, then equity and liabilities, and let the two company liabilities anchor you: shareholders for dividends is just the unpaid final dividend, and SARS is a slate — charge on one side, provisional payments on the other.

The cash flow statement scares everyone, so hold on to its one secret: it must land on the balance sheet's cash figure. Start at profit before tax, add back depreciation and interest, read the working capital changes, subtract what was truly paid, then the investing and financing sections — and when your last line equals the cash in the photograph, you KNOW you are right. It is the only statement that marks itself.

Three documents, one company, every figure connected. Now go to the practice questions and make one set of statements balance from top to bottom — after the first time, the mountain is just a hill.
