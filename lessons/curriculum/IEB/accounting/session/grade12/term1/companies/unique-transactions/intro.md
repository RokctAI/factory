Grade 12 accounting now takes on the company — a legal person in its own right, financed by shareholders who may never visit the business and managed by directors who may own almost none of it. That separation creates a family of transactions found nowhere else: no par value shares issued at whatever price they fetch and bought back at the average issue price, provisional tax paid on estimates against a final charge computed at 27 percent, dividends that only exist once the directors declare them in cents per share, and loans whose capitalised interest grows the balance before instalments shrink it. We trace every entry through one business — Naledi Limited, a building-supplies retailer — because the financial statements, cash flow and analysis ahead all stand on these entries.
