Today we assemble the manufacturer's full grade 12 toolkit: the cost language of the factory, the production cost statement with its notes, the income statement that follows it, and the break-even point that tells management how many units stand between the business and a loss. The statement's signature move is the work-in-progress adjustment — money spent is not the same as production finished — and we work Khanya Footwear line by line until a whole year of spending filters down to a unit cost of twenty-five rand a pair. It is one of the great set-piece calculations of the year, and the same machinery then reappears at street-corner scale, where it becomes impossible to forget. We close with the ethics and internal controls of the factory floor, which every strong answer weaves into the figures.
