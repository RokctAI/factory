Hi, Mandy here — this topic is two skills stapled together, and neither one bites once you see its shape.

Skill one: the fixed asset note. Five beats, always: begin, buy, sell, wear, end. Opening carrying value, plus additions at COST, minus disposals at CARRYING VALUE, minus the year's depreciation, equals closing carrying value. A question will blank one cell — treat it as algebra, because the five beats are one equation and any missing number falls out of the others. And keep the golden habit: things arrive at what they cost, things leave at what the books still carried. The selling price stays out of the note; it belongs to the disposal working and the profit-or-loss line.

Skill two: reading the note. Age is accumulated depreciation divided by the annual depreciation charge — R720 000 gathered at R120 000 a year means six years old. Subtract from the lifespan for the countdown, then SAY the management sentence: replacement must be planned and financed within the remaining four years. Compare additions to depreciation for the health check — adding more than you wear means a renewing fleet; the reverse, year after year, is a business quietly eating itself.

Then the audit half, which is pure recall marks. Evidence, strongest first: see it, read the document, watch the process, ask, recalculate. Sampling: random for fairness, systematic for coverage, stratified so the big rands are always tested. Findings in three moves: the gap, the risk in rands, the fix.

And the contrast table you must own cold: internal auditor — employee, appointed by management, reports to management and the audit committee, all year, improves controls. External auditor — independent, appointed by shareholders, reports to shareholders, year-end, opinion on fair presentation.

Five beats, one division, three moves, one table. You've got this!
