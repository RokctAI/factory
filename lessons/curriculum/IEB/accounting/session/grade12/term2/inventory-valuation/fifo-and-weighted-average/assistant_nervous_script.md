Hi, Mandy here — stock valuation questions look like a blizzard of units and prices, but every single one is the same three-line machine. Units available, rands available, units left. Get those three and the rest is division.

Start every question with the availability table, spoken or scribbled: opening stock plus each purchase, units and rands in two columns, totalled. Radebe's was one thousand two hundred and fifty units, fifty-seven thousand three hundred rand. That total is your anchor — closing stock plus cost of sales MUST rebuild it, whichever method you use. If they don't, stop and find the leak before moving on.

FIFO: value what REMAINS, from the newest delivery backwards. Last delivery first, then the one before, until the remaining units are used up. Say it like stacking: the leftover units are the top of the pile, and the top of the pile arrived last. Never count forward from opening stock — that is the error markers see every year.

Weighted average: total rands divided by total units — weight by UNITS, never average the price list. Then closing stock is units left times that average, and cost of sales is units sold times the same average.

The interpretation sentence, ready-made: in a year of rising prices, FIFO shows the higher closing stock and the higher gross profit, because the cheap old costs went to cost of sales. Falling prices flip every word. One conditional sentence — that is the discussion mark.

And if the question mentions a physical count that disagrees with the records, the missing units at COST become a trading stock deficit, an expense. Count, compare, write off, and say what the deficit might mean.

Three lines, one anchor, one sentence. You've got this!
