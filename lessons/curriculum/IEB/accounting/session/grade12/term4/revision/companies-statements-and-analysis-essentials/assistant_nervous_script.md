Hey, it's Mandy — company statements revision can feel like four topics fighting for space in your head, but they are really one story told four ways, and here is the pocket version.

The income statement is a chain: gross profit, operating profit, net profit before tax, net profit after tax. Learn the six links as a spine and the adjustments become decoration, not danger. And circle the tax line: twenty-seven percent of net profit before tax, every time. It is the easiest mark in the whole topic and the most donated.

Shares: one account, full proceeds, no par value. The average issue price — total share capital over total shares — is the key you carry into every buy-back. Then three lines: capital gives back the average, retained income surrenders the extra, bank pays the whole price. Add the first two; they must equal the third. That check is a free self-mark.

Cash flow: paid is not charged. For tax and for dividends, the working is the same shape — this year's charge, plus what was owed at the start, minus what is still owed at the end. Say plus opening, minus closing quietly to yourself and the working writes itself. And disposals go in at carrying value, always.

Analysis: three sentences per indicator. Quote it, compare it, judge it. The comparisons that unlock discussion marks: return on equity against a fixed deposit, return on total capital against the interest rate on loans, price against net asset value in the same units. If you remember nothing else, remember that a ratio alone earns half the marks and the sentence after it earns the rest.

Breathe. You have built every one of these statements before. Tomorrow you are not learning — you are remembering. You've got this!
