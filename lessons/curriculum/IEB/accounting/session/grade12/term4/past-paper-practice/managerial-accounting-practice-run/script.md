# Part 1 — Expert

This session is a full practice set on managerial accounting, internal auditing and control — one hundred and fifty marks on the internal life of a business: manufacturing, break-even, inventory valuation, budgets, reconciliations, VAT and control. The tasks are written for this session, and each is walked at marking pace: every step named with the marks I have allocated to it, every working spoken in full, because managerial accounting is workings territory — the marker rewards the visible route as generously as the destination.

## Subtopic: Warm-Up — Concepts and Cost Sorting

The opener is a concepts ladder, a mark per rung. Direct costs can be traced to the product — the timber in the desk; indirect costs serve the factory as a whole — the supervisor's salary. One mark each. Fixed costs ignore the level of production — factory rent; variable costs ride on every unit — raw materials. One mark each. Perpetual inventory: cost of sales recorded at every sale, the stock account always current; periodic: stock counted at intervals and cost of sales deduced. One mark each, and the deducing is the difference that must be stated.

Then the sorting table, six to eight marks: place each cost in its column. Salesperson's commission — selling and distribution, never factory. Factory cleaner's wages — indirect labour, factory overheads. Carriage on raw materials — direct material cost, riding with the materials. Office internet — administration. Depreciation on delivery vehicles — selling and distribution; depreciation on factory machines — factory overheads. One mark per placement, and each wrong placement usually costs a second mark downstream when the totals inherit it. Sort slowly here; the statement task is about to reuse every answer.

## Subtopic: The Production Cost Statement Task

The main manufacturing task — thirty marks in this set — and it opens with the two notes before the statement itself. The business is Mopani Woodworks, a maker of school desks.

Direct material cost, the note the marker reads first. Opening stock of raw materials R84 000, plus purchases R612 000, plus carriage on purchases R18 000 — carriage always follows its materials — minus closing stock R90 000. Issued to production: R624 000. Four components, four marks.

Direct labour cost: factory wages R390 000 plus the employer's contributions of R30 000 that ride on those wages — R420 000, two marks, and omitting the contributions is the classic single-mark leak.

Prime cost: 624 000 plus 420 000 — R1 044 000, one mark, label and all.

Factory overheads: the given R296 000 plus the factory's share of the building rent. The information says rent of R60 000 is split sixty percent factory by floor area: R36 000 in, the R24 000 remainder to administration. Overheads: R332 000, two or three marks living in that split.

Total cost of production: R1 376 000 — one mark. Then work-in-progress: add opening R64 000, subtract closing R48 000 — the half-finished desks released a net R16 000 into finished production. Cost of production of finished goods: R1 392 000 — two marks. And the closing line this task loves: twenty-four thousand desks were finished, so unit cost is 1 392 000 over twenty-four thousand — R58 per desk, one mark, and hold it: the interpretation subquestion will ask whether R58 is better or worse than last year's R62, and the answer must name a reason — cheaper timber, less waste, or simply higher volume spreading the fixed overheads thinner.

## Subtopic: The Break-even Task

Break-even arrives as a table of costs for a factory making ceramic mugs and four subquestions, fifteen marks in this set.

Contribution first: selling price R80, variable costs per unit R56 — clay, glaze, labour, and the selling costs that travel with each mug. Contribution: R24, one mark, and it must use variable costs ONLY — sliding the fixed portion of unit cost in here is the topic's cardinal error.

Break-even: fixed costs of R432 000 over the R24 contribution — eighteen thousand mugs, two marks: formula, answer.

Comparison, two marks and always asked: the factory made twenty-one thousand mugs this year, three thousand beyond break-even — safely profitable; last year it made seventeen thousand five hundred against a break-even of seventeen thousand eight hundred — three hundred mugs SHORT, a loss year. Name both positions and the improvement; the marks are for the sentence, not the subtraction.

Profit from the margin: three thousand mugs past the line at the full R24 each — R72 000, two marks, and the shortcut is legitimate because fixed costs died at mug eighteen thousand.

Then the what-if marks: clay rises R6 a mug, contribution thins to R18, break-even climbs to twenty-four thousand — above current production, and the required response is managerial: raise the price, renegotiate supply, or cut variable cost, each with its trade-off named. Two or three marks for reasoning through contribution, none for rebuilding the whole table.

## Subtopic: The Inventory Valuation Task

The stock task, twenty marks here, hands you a trader's year — soccer balls. Opening stock four hundred units at R60: R24 000. Purchases: twelve hundred units at R70, R84 000; then nine hundred units at R80, R72 000. Available: two thousand five hundred units costing R180 000. Closing count: six hundred units.

FIFO first — first in, first out, so the units REMAINING are the newest. All six hundred come from the last batch at R80: closing stock R48 000, two marks. Cost of sales: 180 000 minus 48 000 — R132 000, one mark.

Weighted average: total cost over total units — 180 000 over two thousand five hundred — R72 a unit, two marks including the rounding statement. Closing stock: six hundred at R72 — R43 200, one mark. Cost of sales: R136 800, one mark.

The comparison this task always demands, three or four marks: prices rose all year, so FIFO's closing stock is R4 800 higher and its cost of sales lower — FIFO reports the HIGHER profit in rising prices, weighted average the steadier one. State the direction and the reason, not just the numbers.

Then stock management: one thousand nine hundred units were sold; six hundred remain — about a hundred and fifteen days of stock at the current selling rate. If the soccer season peaks in a few months, holding nearly four months of balls may be planning; if demand is flat, it is capital sleeping on shelves and a theft risk. Recommend: match orders to the sales rhythm, secure the storeroom, count cyclically. Marks flow to specific, situation-aware sentences.

## Subtopic: The Cash Budget Task

The budget task, twenty-five marks in this set, opens with the debtors' collection schedule — the working the whole task leans on.

Sales: January R150 000, February R190 000, March R210 000; thirty percent cash, seventy percent on credit. Credit customers pay sixty percent in the month after the sale and thirty-five percent in the second month; five percent turns bad. March's cash from sales, three lines spoken slowly. Cash sales: thirty percent of 210 000 — R63 000, one mark. From February's credit sales of R133 000: sixty percent — R79 800, two marks. From January's credit sales of R105 000: thirty-five percent — R36 750, two marks. Total receipts from sales: R179 550. The five percent appears nowhere — bad debts move no cash, and writing them into a budget is an instant lost mark.

Then the sorting marks: depreciation of R12 000 — projected income statement only. Loan received — budget only. Provisional tax and an interim dividend — budget lines for a company, in the months the money moves.

The task closes with judgement, six-plus marks, and in this set companies and their directors are fair game here. Budgeted advertising R12 000, actual R5 000: the underspend likely explains the sales shortfall — advertising is seed, not luxury. Directors' fees rising eighteen percent in a year the budget promised six: query the authorisation, name the remuneration committee. A projected overdraft in the machine-purchase month: shift the purchase, pre-arrange finance, or lease instead. Managers earn these marks; calculators do not.

## Subtopic: The Reconciliation and Age Analysis Task

The control task — twenty marks in this set — pairs the creditors' reconciliation with the debtors' age analysis.

The reconciliation. Our creditors' ledger account for a supplier shows R64 550; their statement demands R78 400. Work the statement side: our payment of R9 800 made on the last day has not reached them — subtract, timing. An invoice of R5 600 is for goods arriving only next month — subtract; we record goods when received. The statement overcharges R450 on one invoice against the agreed price — subtract, THEIR error, corrected on their side. Statement adjusted: 78 400 minus 9 800 minus 5 600 minus 450 — R62 550. Our side: a debit note for returns of R2 000 was never recorded — subtract from the ledger: R62 550. Agreement, seven or eight marks, one per item plus the totals — and each item's mark is really for choosing the correct SIDE.

Age analysis: the debtors' book of R180 000 on thirty-day terms — current R81 000, forty-five percent; thirty days R45 000, twenty-five percent; sixty days R36 000, twenty percent; ninety days and beyond R18 000, ten percent. The reading, two marks: thirty percent of the book is beyond terms, and the oldest tenth is sliding toward write-off. The response, three or four marks, must be operational: interest on arrears, suspend the ninety-day accounts, credit-check new applicants, discount for early settlement — and one control sentence: the person who receives cash must not also keep the debtors' ledger, or shortages can be buried as fake credits.

## Subtopic: The VAT, Internal Control and Ethics Task

The closer, fifteen marks in this set, starts with the VAT settlement. Sales of R560 000 excluding VAT collected output tax of fifteen percent: R84 000, one mark. Input tax on purchases and expenses: R61 500 — given. Base position: R22 500 owed. The nudges, two marks each with direction. The owner took stock costing R4 000 excluding VAT: the input claim on consumed goods returns — add R600. A debtor owing R6 900, VAT INCLUSIVE, was written off: reclaim fifteen over one hundred and fifteen — subtract R900. Amount owed to SARS: 22 500 plus 600 minus 900 — R22 200, one mark for the total, and the inclusive-versus-exclusive reading carried three of the marks on the way.

Internal control, applied to cash because this set names it: division of duties — the till operator does not reconcile the till; daily banking — cash sleeping on the premises is temptation plus risk; documents numbered in sequence, gaps investigated; surprise counts. One mark per named control WITH its reason.

Ethics closes the set. The scenario: a business records its weekend cash sales in a private notebook and leaves them off the official record. Name it: VAT fraud and tax evasion — the fifteen percent collected on those sales was held in trust for SARS and is being stolen from the public; the understated sales also cheat any profit-sharing and mislead the bank reading the statements. Consequences: penalties, interest, prosecution, a destroyed audit trail. Two to four marks, earned by naming the trust relationship, the victims and the consequence — not by declaring dishonesty bad. Attempt every part, show every route, and the set's one hundred and fifty marks are walked.
