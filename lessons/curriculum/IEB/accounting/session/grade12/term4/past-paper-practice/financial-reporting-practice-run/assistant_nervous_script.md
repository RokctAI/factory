Hey, it's Mandy — a full financial-reporting practice set can look like a mountain the night before, but you have just walked every path on it, so let me shrink it to a card you can hold.

The set is five stories, always. A warm-up of concepts and one asset working. A big income statement with adjustments. The two notes that track shares and retained profit. A balance sheet or cash flow that tests whether your adjustments landed on both sides. And analysis plus audit at the end, where sentences earn what calculations earn everywhere else.

Three reflexes carry you through the recording tasks. Time-apportion every depreciation figure — ask how many months the asset actually worked. Tax is twenty-seven percent of net profit before tax, one multiplication, never skipped. And the buy-back triangle: capital portion at the average price, premium to retained income, full price out of bank — add the first two and they must equal the third.

For cash flow, whisper plus opening, minus closing over tax and dividends, and solve fixed assets with the carrying-value equation written out, because the marker pays for the equation even when the answer slips.

For analysis, three sentences per indicator: quote, compare, judge. Return on capital against the loan rate settles gearing; return on equity against a fixed deposit settles the shareholders; price against net asset value settles the market's mood.

And everywhere: show workings, carry your figures forward boldly, and let the marks tell you how much to write — a two-mark part wants two things, not a paragraph.
