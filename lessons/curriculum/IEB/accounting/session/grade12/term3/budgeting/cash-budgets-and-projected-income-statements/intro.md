Today the accounting machinery faces forward: the projected income statement, which asks whether the business will be profitable, and the cash budget, which asks whether there will be money in the bank on the days the bills fall due. We will master the debtors' collection schedule — the one demanding calculation that anchors almost every budgeting question — assemble a full month's cash budget to the rand, and then practise what grade 12 is really assessed on: reading the forecast, comparing budget to actual, and judging the decisions the variances reveal. This is where arithmetic becomes management, and where a false economy can be spotted two rows apart on a single page.
