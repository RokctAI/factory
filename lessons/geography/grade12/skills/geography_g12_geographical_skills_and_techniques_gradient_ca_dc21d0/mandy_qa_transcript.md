**Student:** I keep getting the division part mixed up. Do I divide the big number by the small number, or the other way around?

**Mandy:** Oh, that's such a common mistake! Don't think of it as 'big number' or 'small number'. Always go back to the formula: **VI / HE**.
The Vertical Interval (the height difference) is always on top. The Horizontal Equivalent (the flat distance) is always on the bottom.
So, you always put the height difference in your calculator first, then press divide, and then put in the ground distance. And remember, when you are doing the fraction bit at the end to get your 1:X answer, you divide *both* the top and bottom by whatever number is on the top.

**Student:** Why do we even need the answer to look like '1:15'? Why can't I just give the answer as a fraction?

**Mandy:** Good question! Writing it as a ratio like '1:15' makes it standard so everyone can quickly understand what it means in the real world.
When a geographer or an engineer sees 1:15, they instantly know "Okay, for every 15 meters I walk forward, I'm going up 1 meter." It's just a universal language for steepness. If you leave it as 300/4500, they have to do the math in their head to picture the slope! Plus, it's the required format for the NSC exams, so you definitely want those easy marks.

**Student:** What if the horizontal distance isn't given in kilometers? What if I have to measure it on the map?

**Mandy:** Then you just have one extra little step before you convert to meters!
If you measure it with your ruler on a 1:50 000 map, let's say you get 3 cm. You take that 3 cm and multiply it by 50,000 (because 1 cm on the map is 50,000 cm in real life).
So, 3 x 50,000 = 150,000 cm.
Then, you just convert those centimeters into meters by dividing by 100.
150,000 cm / 100 = 1,500 meters.
And boom, you have your HE in meters, ready to use with your VI!
