## Post-Session Q&A with Mandy

**Student:** Why did we multiply by 50 000 instead of divide when using the formula?
**Mandy:** Good question! The map is a shrunken-down version of reality. To get back to the *real* size, we have to make the number bigger. Multiplying by 50 000 takes our tiny map measurement and blows it up to real-world size.

**Student:** I always forget if I should multiply or divide by 100 000 to get kilometres. Help!
**Mandy:** It's super common to mix those up! Think of it like this: a kilometre is a massive unit, so you only need a few of them to describe a distance. A centimetre is a tiny unit, so you need millions of them. If you are changing *to* a massive unit like kilometres, your number needs to get smaller. So, you divide!

**Student:** Does the 0,5 shortcut work on every map in the exam?
**Mandy:** Nope, be very careful there! That shortcut *only* works if the map scale is exactly 1:50 000, and you are trying to find the answer in kilometres. If they give you a 1:10 000 orthophoto map, you can't use 0,5. That's why Ranger Themba showed us the long way first—it works on any map, anytime.

**Student:** What if they ask for the answer in metres instead of kilometres?
**Mandy:** If they want metres, you don't use 100 000. Remember, there are only 100 centimetres in a metre. So, you would take your big centimetre answer and divide it by 100 instead.
