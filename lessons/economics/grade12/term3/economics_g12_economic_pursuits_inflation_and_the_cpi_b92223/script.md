## Subtopic: The Taxi Rank Reality (What is Inflation?)

[Bra Sipho standing at the whiteboard, chalk in hand, looking directly at the camera.]

Sharp, sharp. It’s Bra Sipho here. The Taxi Rank Economist.

Let me ask you something. You know that feeling when you go to the rank, you've got your usual R20 coin ready for your trip to town. You step up to the sliding door, and the queue marshal says, "Chief, it's R22 now."

You check your pockets. The coin hasn't changed. It's still a Mandela R5 and maybe some silver. But suddenly, your money is too small. That trip you took yesterday? Today it costs more.

That, my friend, is what we call INFLATION.

Let’s define it properly, CAPS style, so you get your marks. Inflation is the sustained and significant increase in the general price level of goods and services in an economy over a period of time.

Let me break that down. "Sustained" means it's not just a weekend special that ended. The price goes up and it stays up. "General price level" means it’s not just the taxi fare. It's the bread at the spaza, the petrol at the garage, the school shoes. Everything is moving up together.

When inflation happens, your money loses its muscle. The purchasing power of your Rand drops. That R100 note in your pocket buys fewer things today than it did last year. That’s the reality on the streets.

## Subtopic: The Spaza Shop Basket (The Consumer Price Index - CPI)

Now, the big question. How does the government, or the South African Reserve Bank—the SARB—know exactly how much prices are going up? They can't check every single loose cigarette or packet of chips in the country.

They use something called the CONSUMER PRICE INDEX. We call it the CPI.

Imagine you take a wire basket at the supermarket. Every month, you put the exact same things in it. A loaf of brown bread, a bag of mealie meal, taxi fare, electricity, maybe some airtime. Stats SA—the people who measure things in our country—they have a giant imaginary basket just like this. It's filled with the typical goods and services that an average South African household buys.

Every month, they check the total price of that basket.

If the basket cost R1000 last year, and this year that exact same basket costs R1060, they know prices have gone up. But they don't use the Rand value directly. They turn it into an index number.

Remember index numbers? We use a base year. We say the base year is 100. It makes the math easy. If the index number goes up from 100 to 106, it means the price of the basket has gone up.

The CPI is our thermometer. It tells us how hot the prices are getting.

## Subtopic: The Math on the Streets (Calculating Inflation Rate)

Alright, time to get our hands dirty with the numbers. Because at the end of the day, numbers never lie. We need to calculate the actual inflation rate. The percentage.

Here is a problem they love asking in the exam:
"Calculate the inflation rate if the consumer price index rises from 120 to 126 over one year."

Let’s solve this together. You know percentage change already. The formula for the inflation rate is exactly that:

Inflation Rate = ((New CPI - Old CPI) / Old CPI) × 100

Let’s do the substitution.
The "New CPI" is 126. That's where we are now.
The "Old CPI" is 120. That's where we started.

So, our formula looks like this:
((126 - 120) / 120) × 100

First, find the difference. 126 minus 120 gives us 6. The index went up by 6 points.
Now, divide that 6 by the old CPI, which is 120.
6 divided by 120 is 0.05.

Finally, multiply by 100 to get the percentage.
0.05 × 100 equals 5.

The answer is 5%.

Don't forget the percentage sign! That’s your unit. So, the inflation rate is 5%. This means, on average, the prices of the goods in that CPI basket went up by 5% over the year.

Next time the taxi fare goes up, you can calculate the percentage change yourself. You don't need a textbook, you just need to understand the streets. Keep hustling, and keep calculating. Bra Sipho, out.
