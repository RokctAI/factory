# Mandy Q&A Transcript: Inflation and the CPI

**Student:** Why do we divide by the Old CPI in the formula? Why not the New CPI?
**Mandy:** That's a great question! Think about it like growth. If you want to know how much you grew this year, you compare your new height to where you *started*. The Old CPI is our starting point. We always calculate change relative to where we began.

**Student:** What if the final answer in the formula is a negative number?
**Mandy:** If it's negative, that means prices went *down*. That's called deflation. It doesn't happen often, but if you get a negative number, don't panic! It just means the New CPI was lower than the Old CPI.

**Student:** Do I really have to write the "%" sign in the exam?
**Mandy:** Yes, yes, yes! Bra Sipho said it: numbers never lie, but they need their units. If you just write "5", the examiner doesn't know if you mean 5 Rand, 5 Index points, or 5 percent. The formula gives you a percentage, so always include the sign to get your full marks.
