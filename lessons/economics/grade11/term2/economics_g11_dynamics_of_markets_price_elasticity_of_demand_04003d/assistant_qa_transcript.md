**Student:** Hey Mandy, I'm a bit confused. Why did Bra Sipho say we drop the negative sign when calculating the change in quantity?

**Mandy:** Great question! It can seem a bit strange at first. So, because of the Law of Demand, price and quantity demanded always move in opposite directions. If price goes up (positive change), quantity goes down (negative change). If you keep the negative sign, your final PED answer will always be negative. But economists really just care about the *size* of the reaction, not the direction, because we already know they move opposite to each other. So, we just drop the negative sign to make it easier to compare the numbers—like 1.5 vs 0.5. Does that make sense?

**Student:** Oh, okay! That actually makes it easier. But I still keep messing up the formula. Is it price over quantity or quantity over price?

**Mandy:** I completely understand, it’s easy to mix them up! Remember Bra Sipho's rule: **"Quantity is always on top."** Think of it this way: you want to measure the *reaction* (which is the change in quantity) caused by the *change in price*. We always put the reaction on top in this formula. So it’s Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded divided by Percentage Change in Price. Quantity on top, always!

**Mandy:** If the answer is greater than 1, it is **Price Elastic**. That means the reaction was bigger than the price change. People are very sensitive to the price! 1.5 is definitely greater than 1, so it's elastic. If it was 0.5 (less than 1), it would be inelastic. You're doing great!
