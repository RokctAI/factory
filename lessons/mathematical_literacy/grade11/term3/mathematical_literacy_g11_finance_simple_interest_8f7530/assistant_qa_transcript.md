# Mandy Q&A Transcript: Simple Interest

**Student:** Mandy, I put 6,5 into the calculator for the interest rate and my answer is huge! What did I do wrong?
**Mandy:** Good catch! Remember what Uncle Joe said: "per annum" means each year, but 6,5 is a percentage. A percentage means "out of 100". You have to divide 6,5 by 100 first, which gives you 0,065. Use 0,065 in your calculation, and your answer will look much better!

**Student:** Why do we add the interest back at the end? Isn't the interest the final answer?
**Mandy:** The interest is just the *extra* money the bank is paying you. If the question asks for "simple interest earned", then yes, that's your final answer. But if they ask for the "total value of the investment" at the end of the time period, they want to know how much money you can withdraw in total. You don't just want the extra money; you want your original starting money back too! That's why you add them together.

**Student:** What if the time isn't in full years, like 18 months?
**Mandy:** Great question! The interest rate is "per annum", which means per year. So, the time (`n`) must also be in years. If they give you months, you have to convert it. There are 12 months in a year, so you just divide the months by 12. For example, 18 months divided by 12 is 1,5 years. Then you use 1,5 for `n`.
