# Lesson: Simple Interest
## Subtopic: The Money in Your Hand: What is Interest?

Hello, everyone. I'm Uncle Joe. The cashier gave you wrong change last week and you didn't notice. Let's fix that, because that's exactly what Maths Lit is about. It's about the money in your hand, the numbers on your till slip, and making sure nobody takes you for a ride.

Today, we're talking about interest. Not the kind of interest you have in a new pair of sneakers, but the kind of interest that banks use. Now, if you borrow money, the bank charges you for using their money. That's called interest. But if you put your money into a bank, if you invest it, they pay *you* for using your money. That's also interest. It's like renting out your cash.

Let's look at a real-world situation. Imagine you've saved up some money. Maybe you worked over the holidays, or you got some birthday cash. You have 8,000 rand. Now, you could hide that 8,000 rand under your mattress. But what happens if you leave it there for three years? It just sits there. It's still 8,000 rand. It doesn't grow. In fact, things get more expensive, so that 8,000 rand buys you less.

But what if you take that 8,000 rand and put it in a savings account or an investment? The bank looks at your money and says, "Thank you, Uncle Joe. We're going to use this for a bit, and as a thank you, we're going to pay you a percentage of it every year." That is simple interest.

Simple interest is exactly what it sounds like: it's simple. They calculate the interest on the original amount you put in, and they just pay you that exact same amount of interest every single year. Nothing complicated. Just straight, simple cash added to your pile.

## Subtopic: The Till Slip Calculation: Finding the Interest

Alright, let's get down to the till slip calculation. The pavement-level road to get exactly the right answer. We have our example: we are investing R8 000 at an interest rate of 6,5% per annum for 3 years.

Before we write down any formulas, let's just estimate. Let's think like we're standing at the counter.
10% of R8 000 would be R800.
So 5% is half of that, which is R400.
Our rate is 6,5%, which is a bit more than 5%. So we expect the interest to be maybe R500 or R600 a year. If we leave it for 3 years, we should get around R1 500 or R1 800 in total interest. See? Just a quick estimate so we know if our calculator is lying to us later.

Now for the exact calculation. The formula they give you is:
Simple Interest = P × i × n

Let's break that down into plain English.
'P' stands for Principal. That's the starting money. The money you walk into the bank with. In our case, that is R8 000.
'i' stands for the interest rate. Now, the bank says 6,5% per annum. "Per annum" is just fancy bank talk for "each year". But remember, we can't just put "6,5" into our calculation. We have to use the percentage. 6,5% means 6,5 divided by 100. Let's do that right now: 6,5 ÷ 100 = 0,065. That is our 'i'.
'n' stands for the number of years. How long are we leaving the money there? 3 years. So 'n' is 3.

Let's put the numbers in our till slip formula:
Interest = R8 000 × 0,065 × 3

Take out your calculator. Type it exactly like that.
8000 multiplied by 0,065 multiplied by 3.
You should get 1560.
But remember, we are talking about Rands and cents. We always format money correctly. It is R1 560,00.

So, the simple interest earned over the 3 years is R1 560,00. And look at our estimate from earlier: we guessed it would be around R1 500 or R1 800. Our answer of R1 560 is right in that pocket. We know we're on the right track.

## Subtopic: The Final Count: Total Investment Value

We know how much extra money the bank paid us. They paid us R1 560,00 just for leaving our R8 000 with them for three years. That's not bad for doing nothing, right?

But the final question is: what is the total value of the investment at the end of the 3 years? If you walk into the bank after three years and say, "I want all my money, please," how much are they going to hand over?

Think about it. Are they just going to give you the R1 560 interest? No, you'd be furious! They still have your original money too.

The total value, which we sometimes call 'A' for final Amount, is simply your starting money plus the interest it earned.
A = Principal + Interest
A = P + I

We already know the numbers.
Our starting money, the Principal, was R8 000.
The interest we just calculated was R1 560.

Let's add them up.
A = R8 000 + R1 560

Do it in your head or use the calculator. R8 000 plus R1 000 is R9 000. Plus the R560.
That gives us R9 560.
But again, let's write it down like a proper business person.
The total value of the investment is R9 560,00.

And there you have it. You started with R8 000. You left it alone, and it grew into R9 560,00. That is the power of simple interest. We figured it out step by step, just like checking your change at the till.

Always start with what you know. Always make a little estimate so you don't get fooled. And always remember to add the interest back onto your original money if they ask for the total value.
