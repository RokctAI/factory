## Subtopic: Reading the Municipal Bill

You know, just last week, my neighbour, Mr. Dlamini, came over to my house. He was holding this piece of paper, shaking his head. It was his municipal bill for the month. He looked at me and said, "Joe, I don't get it. I switched off the geyser, I told the kids to turn off the lights, but this bill... it's still eating my money!"

Now, I look at the paper. It's a standard municipal electricity bill. And I tell him, "My friend, before you start yelling at the kids, we need to understand what this paper is actually saying."

You see, that's what Mathematical Literacy is all about. It's about taking the money in your hand—your hard-earned rands and cents—and knowing exactly where it's going. Today, we're going to look at municipal tariff calculations. We are going to look at a real bill and figure out exactly how the municipality calculates what you owe them.

Let's look at a typical example. A municipality charges a basic fee of R120 per month, plus R1,85 per kWh of electricity. And we want to calculate the total bill for a household that used 450 kWh in a month.

Let's start by breaking down what these words mean. First, the "basic fee". What is a basic fee? Well, think of it like taking a taxi. Before the taxi even moves, you have to pay your fare just to sit in the seat. The municipality does the same thing. They charge you a fixed amount just to be connected to the grid, even if you don't switch on a single light bulb. In our example, that basic fee is R120. It's a flat rate. You pay it every single month, no matter what.

Then we have the "usage". This is the part you can actually control. The bill says R1,85 per kWh. kWh stands for kilowatt-hour. It's just a way of measuring how much electricity you use. Think of it like buying petrol. You pay a certain amount per litre. With electricity, you pay a certain amount per kilowatt-hour. Here, it costs R1,85 for every single kWh you use.

And in this month, our household used 450 kWh.

Now, before we jump into the exact calculation, let's do what we always do. Let's make an estimate. Let's get a feel for the money.

If we are paying about R1,85 for a unit, that's close to R2,00, right? And we used 450 units. If it was exactly R2,00 for 450 units, that would be 2 times 450. Well, 2 times 45 is 90, so 2 times 450 is 900. That means the usage part is going to be somewhere around 900 Rands. But remember, we rounded up from R1,85 to R2,00, so our real answer should be a bit less than R900. Let's say, maybe around R800.

Then, we also have to add that basic fee of R120. So, R800 plus R120... our final bill should be somewhere around R900 or R950.

That's our estimate. It's our anchor. If our final answer comes out to R50, we know we messed up. If it comes out to R5000, we know we messed up. Now that we have a feel for the numbers, let's get down to the exact calculation.

## Subtopic: Calculating the Usage Cost

Alright, let's do the exact math for the usage. We need to figure out exactly how much those 450 kWh are going to cost us.

The rule here is simple. We take the amount of electricity used, and we multiply it by the tariff rate. The tariff rate is the price per unit.

So, we write it down:
Usage cost = Electricity used × Tariff rate

Let's substitute our numbers. We look at our context. The electricity used is 450 kWh. The tariff rate is R1,85 per kWh.

So, our sum is:
450 × R1,85

Now, when you put this in your calculator, take your time. 450 multiplied by 1.85.

What does the calculator give us? It gives us 832.5.

But wait. We are working with money here. Rands and cents. In South Africa, our money always has two decimal places. You don't go to the shop and ask for change of point five. You ask for fifty cents.

So, we must write this correctly. We add a zero to make it two decimal places.
It becomes R832,50.

Let's pause and look at this number. R832,50. This is just the cost for the electricity that was used. Remember our estimate from earlier? We guessed the usage would be a bit less than R900, maybe around R800. And look at that! R832,50 is exactly in that ballpark. We are on the right track. The maths is making sense.

This step right here—multiplying the usage by the tariff—is usually where people get confused. They look at the R1,85 and they look at the 450, and they sometimes try to add them, or they divide them. Always remember: "per" means multiply. If it's R1,85 *per* kWh, and you have 450 of them, you must multiply them together. It's like buying 450 loaves of bread at R1,85 each. You multiply to find the total.

So, we have our usage cost. R832,50. Are we finished? Can Mr. Dlamini go to the municipality and just pay R832,50?

No. If he does that, he's going to get a letter telling him he is in arrears. He forgot the first part of the bill. He forgot the taxi fare. He forgot the basic fee.

## Subtopic: Calculating the Total Bill

Now for the final step. We need to calculate the total bill. This is where we bring everything together.

A municipality bill is like a puzzle with two pieces. The first piece is the basic fee. The second piece is the usage cost. To get the total bill, you just add the two pieces together.

Let's write down the formula so we can see it clearly:
Total Bill = Basic Fee + Usage Cost

We already know both of these numbers. Let's substitute them in.
The basic fee, from our original problem, is R120.00.
The usage cost, which we just calculated, is R832,50.

So, our sum is:
Total Bill = R120,00 + R832,50

Let's line them up and add them. Or use your calculator, that's what it's there for.
120 plus 832.50.
That gives us 952.5.

Again, remember our rounding rules for money. We need two decimal places. So that 952.5 becomes R952,50.

Let's check back with our estimate. Right at the beginning, we guessed the total bill would be somewhere around R900 or R950. Our exact answer is R952,50. Our estimate was spot on.

This is the beauty of doing an estimate first. When you get to the end of a long exam question, and your brain is tired, your estimate is your safety net. It tells you that your final answer is reasonable.

So, our final answer, fully interpreted in context, is that the total municipal bill for a household using 450 kWh in a month is R952,50.

Always state your final answer clearly, with the units. The unit here is Rands. Don't just write down "952.50". Write "R952,50". In Maths Lit, the units matter just as much as the numbers. The numbers tell you *how much*, but the units tell you *what it is*.

And that's it! We took a scary-looking municipal problem, broke it down into the basic fee and the usage, calculated the usage by multiplying the rate by the units used, and then added the basic fee to get the final total.

Next time you look at a municipal bill, whether it's yours or your parents', you'll know exactly how those numbers are put together. No more guessing. Just solid, everyday maths. Keep practicing your decimals, keep remembering those basic fees, and you'll be sorting out bills in no time.

Stay sharp, and always count your change.
