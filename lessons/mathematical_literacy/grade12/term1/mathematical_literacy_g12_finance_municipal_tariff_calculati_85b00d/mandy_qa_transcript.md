## Q&A with Mandy

**Student:** I'm a bit confused. Why do we add a zero when the calculator says 832.5? Can't I just leave it like that?
**Mandy:** Great question! In everyday life, saying 832.5 might make sense, but in Mathematical Literacy, we always have to think about the real world. Since we are dealing with South African Rands, we have to show the cents. Cents always have two decimal places. So, 832.5 isn't just a number, it's 832 Rands and 50 cents. If you write 832.5, you might lose marks for not showing that it's money! Always add that zero: R832,50.

**Student:** Uncle Joe talked about an estimate. Do I *have* to do that in the exam? It takes extra time.
**Mandy:** You don't *have* to write down your estimate in the exam unless the question specifically asks for it. But doing a quick mental check, like Uncle Joe did, is a superpower! If you make a mistake on your calculator and get R50,000 for a monthly electricity bill, your quick estimate will immediately tell you something went wrong before you move on to the next question. It saves you from losing easy marks.

**Student:** Sometimes these bills have VAT on them. What do I do then?
**Mandy:** That's a super important point. In this specific lesson, we were just looking at a basic bill *without* worrying about VAT yet. But you are right, in a real exam question, they might ask you to add 15% VAT at the very end. If they do, you just calculate the total bill exactly like we did here (Basic Fee + Usage Cost), and *then* calculate 15% of that total and add it on. We have another whole lesson just on VAT, so don't stress too much about it right now!
