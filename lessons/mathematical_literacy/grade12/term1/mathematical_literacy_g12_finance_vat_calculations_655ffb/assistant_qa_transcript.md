# Mandy Q&A Transcript - VAT Calculations

**Student:** Why can't I just calculate 15% of the final price and subtract it? It feels like it should be the same thing!

**Mandy:** I completely understand why it feels like it should work! It's the most common mistake everyone makes with VAT. But think of it this way: 15% of a *small* number is a small amount, right? But 15% of a *big* number is a bigger amount.

The VAT is calculated on the *smaller* starting price (the shop's price). If you take the *bigger* final price (which already has VAT added in) and calculate 15% of that, you are calculating a piece of a bigger pie. You'll end up with a VAT amount that is too high! That's why we have to divide by 1,15 to work backwards properly.

**Student:** Okay, I get the dividing by 1,15 thing. But what if the question asks me to add VAT instead of taking it away?

**Mandy:** Great question! If a question gives you the "Price Excluding VAT" (the starting price) and asks you to add the VAT, it's actually much easier. Because you are starting at 100%, you can just calculate 15% of that price, and then add it on.
Or, a quick shortcut: you can just take that starting price and multiply it by 1,15. That will give you the final "Price Including VAT" in one step!

**Student:** I always mess up the rounding. I got R899,8695 on my calculator. Why is it R899,87 and not R899,86?

**Mandy:** Rounding with money is super important in Maths Lit! We only ever want two decimal places, because we only have rands and cents.

When you get R899,8695, draw an imaginary line after the first two decimals (the 8 and the 6). Then, look at the very next number. It's a 9. The rule is: if that next number is 5 or more, you must give the number before it a little push up. Because 9 is bigger than 5, we push the 6 up to a 7. So it becomes R899,87!
