# Mathematical Literacy Grade 12: Finance - VAT calculations

## Subtopic: What is VAT?

Right! Gather round. Let's get straight to business.

Have you ever walked into a shop, picked out a nice pair of sneakers, looked at the price tag, and just paid exactly what it says? Of course you have. It doesn't all go to the shop owner. Nope. A piece of it goes straight to the government. We call that piece VAT. Value Added Tax.

VAT is the government's way of collecting money to run the country, build roads, pay teachers, all of that. And they do it by adding a little extra charge onto almost everything we buy. In South Africa, that extra charge is 15%.

Think of it like this. You have the price the shop wants for the item. That's what we call the "Price Excluding VAT". Then, the government says, "Hey, add 15% on top of that for us." Once you add that 15%, you get the final price you see on the shelf. The "Price Including VAT".

When you look at a till slip, especially for a big item, you'll see it broken down. Let's imagine you're helping your aunt buy a brand new television. She's saved up, she walks into the electronics store, and there's a beautiful TV. The big shiny sticker on the box says it costs R6 899. And underneath in small letters, it says "Including VAT at 15%".

That R6 899 is the money leaving your aunt's pocket. It's the total. It includes the TV's price AND the government's VAT.

## Subtopic: The Golden Rule of VAT

This is the Golden Rule of VAT.

The shop's price—the Price Excluding VAT—is the base. It is the starting point. It is 100%.

The VAT is 15% of that base.

So, what happens when you add them together? The final Price Including VAT is the 100% (the shop's money) PLUS the 15% (the government's money).

That means the Price Including VAT is equal to 115% of the original price.

Let me say that again, because if you get this, you win at VAT calculations.
Price Excl VAT = 100%
Price Incl VAT = 115%

A big mistake students make is they take the final price—our R6 899 TV—and try to just minus 15% off it. No, no, no! That 15% VAT is calculated on the *smaller* starting price, not the *bigger* final price. If you just subtract 15% from the final price, you are taking a piece of a bigger pie. It won't work out.

We need to work backwards from 115% to get back to 100%.

## Subtopic: Calculating Price Excluding VAT

Let's look at our television again. The problem says: "A television is priced at R6 899 including VAT at 15%. Calculate the price excluding VAT and the VAT amount."

We know the Price Including VAT is R6 899.
We know that this amount represents 115%.

We need to find the Price Excluding VAT, which represents 100%.

To do this, we use a very simple rearranging trick. We want to find the starting price, right? Let's write out the relationship.
Price Excluding VAT multiplied by 115% equals Price Including VAT.

Remember that 115% is the same as writing 115 divided by 100. And 115 divided by 100 is just 1,15 as a decimal.

So, Price Excluding VAT multiplied by 1,15 equals R6 899.

If I want to get the Price Excluding VAT by itself, I need to do the opposite of multiplying. I need to divide!

So, I take the final price of R6 899, and I divide it by 1,15.
Let's punch that into the calculator.
R6 899 divided by 1,15... gives us exactly R5 999,130434...

Now, stop. Think. This is money. In Mathematical Literacy, what do we do with money? We round to two decimal places. The third number after the comma is a 0, so we keep the second number as it is.

Our Price Excluding VAT is R5 999,13.

That right there is the money the shop gets to keep for the TV. R5 999,13.

## Subtopic: Calculating the VAT Amount

Okay, we've found the shop's price. The TV without the tax is R5 999,13.
But the problem also asks us to calculate the VAT amount. The actual rands and cents that go to the government.

Think about the real world. Your aunt paid R6 899 at the till. We just worked out that the shop keeps R5 999,13.
Where did the rest of the money go? It went to VAT!

So, to find the VAT amount, all we have to do is take the total amount she paid, and minus the amount the shop kept. The difference is the tax.

Let's write it down.
VAT amount equals Price Including VAT minus Price Excluding VAT.
VAT amount = R6 899,00 minus R5 999,13.

Grab your calculator. R6 899 minus R5 999,13.
That gives us R899,87.

So, the VAT amount is R899,87. That's the piece the government gets.

## Subtopic: Checking Our Work

Now, I said there were two ways to find the VAT amount. And I always tell my students, it's good to check your work using the second way. It proves you know what's going on.

Remember the Golden Rule? The VAT is 15% of the *starting* price. The Price Excluding VAT.

So, if we take our Price Excluding VAT, which is R5 999,13, and we calculate 15% of that, it should give us our VAT amount. Let's see if it works.

We want 15% of R5 999,13.
That means 15 divided by 100, multiplied by R5 999,13.
Or, 0,15 multiplied by R5 999,13.

Let's punch it in. 0,15 times 5999,13...
The calculator says 899,8695.
Again, this is money, so we round to two decimal places. The third number is a 9, which is 5 or more, so we round the second number up. The 6 becomes a 7.
That gives us R899,87!

Look at that! It's exactly the same amount we got when we subtracted. R899,87.

We did it. We took a real-life situation, a TV on a shop floor. We took the total price of R6 899, and we stripped the VAT out of it by dividing by 1,15. We found the shop's price was R5 999,13. And we found the VAT amount was R899,87.

Remember, never just minus 15% from the final price. Always work backwards from 115%. Keep it real, keep it simple.
