# Maths Grade 12: Finance, Growth and Decay - Compound growth

## Subtopic: The Logic of Growth on Growth

You know, I once had a Grade 5 learner come to me. She was struggling, looking at these big Grade 12 maths problems, thinking she could never do it. But I taught her this exact topic, and she passed. Because we didn't start with letters and formulas. We started with logic.

Today we are talking about compound growth. Now, if you know how to calculate a basic percentage, and you know how simple growth works, you are already halfway there.

Let me paint a picture for you. Imagine you have a little snowball at the top of a snowy hill. You roll it down. As it rolls, it picks up snow. But here is the trick: as it gets bigger, the *new* snow it picks up also picks up more snow. The snowball doesn't just grow; the *growth* grows.

Or think about farming. You plant a few seeds, you get some mealies. You keep the mealies and plant their seeds next season. Now you have even more mealies. Your harvest is growing on top of your previous harvest.

That is compound growth. It's growth on top of growth. It's making your money work for you, and then making the new money work for you too.

## Subtopic: Year 1: The First Step

Let's look at a real problem. R5 000 is invested at 8% per annum, compounded annually. We need to calculate the investment's value after 3 years.

Now, don't rush to try and find a formula. Let's just use our everyday logic.

You take R5 000 and you put it in the bank. The bank says, "Thank you very much, we will give you 8% extra every year just for leaving it with us."

So, let's look at Year 1.
You start with R5 000.
What is 8% of R5 000?
Well, 8% is just 8 over 100. Or 0,08.
Let's multiply: 5000 times 0,08.
That gives us R400.

So the bank gives you R400 as a thank you. That is your interest.
How much money do you have at the end of Year 1?
You have your original R5 000, plus the R400.
That is R5 400.

Look at that. You've just done the first step. You already understand the logic. Celebrate that small win. You are doing Grade 12 maths.

## Subtopic: Year 2 and 3: The Snowball Effect

Now, here is where the magic of compound growth happens.

In simple growth, the bank would just give you another R400 in Year 2. But this is compound growth. This is the snowball getting bigger.

In Year 2, the bank doesn't look at your original R5 000. They look at what you have *now*. You have R5 400 in the bank. So they pay you 8% on that R5 400! Your interest is earning interest.

Let's calculate it.
What is 8% of R5 400?
5400 times 0,08.
That is R432.
See? Last year you earned R400. This year you earned R432. Your money is working harder.

So, what is your new total at the end of Year 2?
It is R5 400 plus the new R432 interest.
That gives us R5 832.

We are almost there. We just need to do Year 3.
By now you see the pattern. We don't go back to the start. We take our new total.
Year 3 starts with R5 832.
The bank gives us 8% on that.
What is 8% of R5 832?
5832 times 0,08.
That gives us R466,56.

Add that to what we started the year with.
R5 832 plus R466,56.
That gives us a final total of R6 298,56.

That is your answer. You just solved a Grade 12 compound growth problem using nothing but Grade 5 logic. Step by step.

## Subtopic: The Fast Way: The Formula

Now, what we did is beautiful, because it shows we understand what is actually happening. But imagine if the bank asked you to calculate this for 20 years. Doing it step by step would take all day.

We need a shortcut. And in maths, a shortcut is just called a formula. We only name the formula last, after we understand the logic.

The formula for compound growth is:
A = P(1 + i)^n

Let me show you how this is exactly what we just did.
'A' is our final Amount. That is what we are looking for.
'P' is our Principal amount. That is the money we started with. The R5 000.
'i' is our interest rate. We had 8%, which we write as a decimal: 0,08.
And 'n' is the number of years. In our case, 3 years.

Let's put our numbers into the shortcut:
A = 5000(1 + 0,08)^3
A = 5000(1,08)^3

If you type that exactly into your calculator... 5000, open bracket, 1,08, close bracket, to the power of 3... what do you get?
You get R6 298,56.

Exactly the same answer! But in one step instead of three. The formula isn't some magic spell. It is just a fast way of doing the step-by-step logic we already figured out.

That's how you get a distinction.
