**Student:** I tried doing T2 / T1, but then for T3 and T2, I just subtracted them because it was easier. My answer was totally wrong. Why can't I mix them?

**Mandy:** Oh, that's a super common trap! Remember what Mr. Zulu said right at the beginning: a geometric sequence is all about a *constant ratio*. That means it has to be multiplication or division the whole way through. If you divide T2 by T1 to get your ratio, you *must* also divide T3 by T2 to check it. If you subtract, you're treating it like an arithmetic sequence, which changes the whole rule! Pick one method based on the sequence and stick to it.

**Student:** My calculator gave me a syntax error when I typed in `2 * 3 ^ 10 - 1`. What did I do wrong?

**Mandy:** Ah, calculators can be tricky with exponents! When you type `3 ^ 10 - 1` without brackets, the calculator thinks you want to do `3 to the power of 10`, and *then* subtract 1 from that huge answer. But you actually want `3 to the power of 9`. You need to put the `10 - 1` in brackets, like `3 ^ (10 - 1)`. Or even better, just do the `10 - 1` in your head first, and type `3 ^ 9`.
