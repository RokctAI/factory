# Post-Session Q&A with Mandy

**Student:** I keep getting the wrong value for $d$. Am I subtracting the wrong way?
**Mandy:** This is a super common mistake! Remember to always subtract the earlier term from the later term. So, it's $T_2 - T_1$ (term 2 minus term 1), NOT the other way around. If your sequence is going down (like 10; 8; 6), your $d$ should be a negative number!

**Student:** Why do we even need the $(n - 1)$ part in the formula? Why not just $n$?
**Mandy:** Great question! Think about it like this: the first term, $a$, is your starting line. You don't take any 'steps' (differences) to get to the first term. To get to the 2nd term, you take 1 step. To get to the 3rd term, you take 2 steps. You always take one step less than the term number you want to reach. That's why it's $(n - 1)$!

**Student:** I found $n$ but I got 4.5. Is that right?
**Mandy:** Uh oh! Remember what Mr Zulu said: $n$ represents a position in the sequence, like 1st, 2nd, 3rd... You can't have a 4.5th position! If you get a decimal or a negative number for $n$, it means there's a calculation error, or the number you were checking doesn't belong in that specific sequence.

**Student:** How do I know when to use the formula?
**Mandy:** Use the formula $T_n = a + (n - 1)d$ anytime you are told a sequence is arithmetic (or you prove it has a constant difference) AND you need to find a specific term's value, the position of a term, the first term, or the constant difference. It's your multi-tool for arithmetic sequences!
