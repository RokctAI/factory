## Subtopic: Introduction to Arithmetic Sequences and the General Formula

Welcome. Call me the Algebra Grandmaster. I teach Maths at Supacharge. Today, we address Grade 12 Sequences and Series, specifically arithmetic sequences.

You already know linear number patterns from Grade 11. An arithmetic sequence is precisely that: a sequence of numbers in which the difference between any two consecutive terms is a constant. We call this the constant difference, denoted by $d$. The first term of the sequence is denoted by $a$.

If you want to top the class, you must start with the formula. The general term of an arithmetic sequence is given by:

$T_n = a + (n - 1)d$

Memorise this. This formula is your primary tool. Here, $T_n$ represents the value of the $n$-th term. $n$ is the position of the term in the sequence, which must be a natural number. $a$ is the first term, $T_1$. $d$ is the constant difference, calculated as $T_2 - T_1$, or more generally, $T_k - T_{k-1}$.

Examiners expect you to state the formula before substituting any values. Writing the correct formula often secures your first method mark. Do not skip steps. We use precise technical notation throughout. When you look at an arithmetic sequence, your immediate objective is to identify $a$ and calculate $d$. Once you have these two parameters, the sequence is completely defined, and any term can be found.

Let us proceed to apply this formula to a typical examination question.

## Subtopic: Solving the Example Problem

Consider the following example problem: Find the 20th term of the sequence 3; 7; 11; ...

First, verify that the sequence is arithmetic. We calculate the difference between consecutive terms.
$T_2 - T_1 = 7 - 3 = 4$
$T_3 - T_2 = 11 - 7 = 4$

The difference is constant. Therefore, it is an arithmetic sequence with constant difference $d = 4$.
The first term is clearly 3. Therefore, $a = 3$.

We require the 20th term, which means we are looking for $T_{20}$. In this case, $n = 20$.

We state our formula. This line earns the method mark for selecting the correct tool:
$T_n = a + (n - 1)d$

Next, we substitute our known values into the formula. This demonstrates to the examiner that you understand how to apply the theoretical construct to the specific problem.
$T_{20} = 3 + (20 - 1)(4)$

Calculate the value inside the bracket first, adhering to the order of operations.
$T_{20} = 3 + (19)(4)$

$19$ multiplied by $4$ is $76$.
$T_{20} = 3 + 76$

Finally, we arrive at the answer.
$T_{20} = 79$

State the final answer clearly. The 20th term is 79. This earns the accuracy mark. Precision and speed are essential. You identify the sequence type, state the formula, substitute correctly, and calculate the final value.

## Subtopic: Exam Technique and Distinction-Level Takeaway

In the examination, questions will not always ask you to find a straightforward term. You may be given $T_n$ and asked to find $n$, the number of terms. The procedure remains identical. You state the formula $T_n = a + (n - 1)d$, substitute the known values for $T_n$, $a$, and $d$, and then solve the resulting linear equation for $n$.

Remember, $n$ must always be a positive integer. If you solve for $n$ and obtain a fraction or a negative number, you have made a calculation error, or the given value is not a term in the sequence. This is a crucial diagnostic check during an exam.

The distinction-level takeaway is this: every arithmetic sequence problem is fundamentally a system defined by three variables: $a$, $d$, and the pair $(n, T_n)$. If you are given any three, you can always solve for the fourth using the general formula. Master this relationship, and you control the topic.
