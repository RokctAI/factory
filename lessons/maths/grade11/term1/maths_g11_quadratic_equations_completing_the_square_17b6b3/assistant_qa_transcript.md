# Mandy Q&A Transcript - Completing the Square

**Student:** Why do we even need to complete the square? Can't we just use the quadratic formula?

**Mandy:** That's a great question! Yes, the quadratic formula will always work. But here's the secret: completing the square is actually where the quadratic formula *comes from*! Plus, examiners often specifically ask you to "solve by completing the square" in tests. If you just use the formula, you won't get the method marks. The Grandmaster loves those method marks!

**Student:** I keep forgetting what number to add to both sides.

**Mandy:** It happens! Try to remember this little phrase: "Halve it, then square it." You always look at the number attached to the regular 'x'. Cut it in half, then multiply it by itself. That's your magic number. So if it's 8x, half is 4, squared is 16. You add 16.

**Student:** In step 5, why is there a plus and minus sign (\(\pm\))?

**Mandy:** Think about taking the square root of 9. What number multiplied by itself gives you 9? Well, \(3 \times 3 = 9\), but also \(-3 \times -3 = 9\)! So when we take a square root to solve an equation, we have to account for both the positive and the negative possibility. If you forget it, you lose a whole answer, which means losing marks!

**Student:** What if the number in front of the \(x^2\) isn't a 1? Like \(2x^2 + 8x - 4 = 0\)?

**Mandy:** The Grandmaster briefly mentioned this in Step 1. You have to get rid of that number first! You do that by dividing the *entire equation* by that number. So in your example, you'd divide everything by 2. It becomes \(x^2 + 4x - 2 = 0\). Then you proceed with the normal steps.
