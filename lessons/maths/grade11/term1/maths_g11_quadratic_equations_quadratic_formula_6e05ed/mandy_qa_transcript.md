**Student:** I tried to do a problem and I got a negative number under the square root. Did I mess up?

**Mandy:** Oh, that's a classic! Not necessarily. If you double-checked your signs and your arithmetic is correct, and you *still* have a negative under the square root, it just means the equation has no real roots. Remember what the Grandmaster said about the discriminant? If $b^2 - 4ac$ is negative, it means the graph of that quadratic doesn't cross the x-axis at all. So, you didn't mess up, you just found an equation with no real solutions! Just write "no real roots" as your final answer.

**Student:** I always forget the negative sign on the 'b' at the start of the formula. Does it matter?

**Mandy:** It definitely matters! That negative sign flips whatever sign your 'b' currently has. If your $b$ is 5, you put in $-5$. If your $b$ is $-2$, the formula makes it $-(-2)$, which becomes a positive $2$. Dropping that sign changes the whole sum, and like the Grandmaster said, "it's the mark of an amateur." Don't be an amateur! Put brackets around your numbers when you substitute them in; it really helps prevent those silly sign errors.

**Student:** Why is there a plus AND a minus? Can I just pick one?

**Mandy:** Haha, nope, you can't just pick one! A quadratic curve (a parabola) usually crosses the x-axis in two places. The plus and the minus give you those two different points. You have to calculate the whole thing using the plus sign to get the first answer, and then calculate it *again* using the minus sign to get the second answer. That's why quadratics usually have two solutions!
