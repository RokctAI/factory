**Student:** I keep mixing up what goes on the top and bottom of the formula in the exam!
**Mandy:** I used to do that all the time. Think of it like a seesaw. The Fixed Cost is the heavy, big number (like R60,000 rent). The heavy guy always sits on top to squash the smaller numbers. So, Fixed Cost on top, divided by (Selling Price - Variable Cost) on the bottom.

**Student:** Why do we subtract the variable cost from the selling price first? Why can't I just divide the fixed cost by the selling price?
**Mandy:** Because you didn't get the cool drink for free! If you sell it for R50, you don't have R50 to pay the rent. You had to pay the wholesaler R30 first. You only have R20 left over to actually pay the rent. That R20 is what *contributes* to paying the bills.

**Student:** If the answer is 3000.5 units, what do I write?
**Mandy:** You must always round UP in break-even! If you sell 3000 units, you are still a little bit short of breaking even. So you have to sell that extra unit. You would write 3001 units. You can't sell half a cool drink!

**Student:** What if they don't give me the variable cost, but they give me the total variable cost and the number of units?
**Mandy:** Good question! They love doing this in the exam. You just have to find the cost for *one* unit first. Take the total variable cost and divide it by the number of units. That gives you the variable cost per unit. Then you plug that into your normal formula.
