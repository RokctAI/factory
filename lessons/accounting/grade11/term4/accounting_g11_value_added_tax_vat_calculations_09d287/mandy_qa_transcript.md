# Mandy Q&A Transcript: VAT Calculations

**Student:** Mandy, I'm so confused. If VAT is 15%, why did Aunty Grace divide by 115? Why can't I just say R2 875 × 15% on my calculator?
**Mandy:** I know, it feels so weird the first time! But think about what the R2 875 actually is. It is the inclusive amount. That means the 15% has *already* been added to the original price. The original price was 100%, and SARS added 15%. That means the R2 875 is actually 115% of the original price. If you just multiply by 15%, you're calculating tax on top of tax! To find the exact tax slice, you use the formula: Amount × (Want / Have). You *want* the 15%, and you *have* the 115%. So, R2 875 × (15 / 115).

**Student:** Oh, okay! And what if I want to find the exclusive amount directly?
**Mandy:** Great question. You can use the exact same formula! Amount × (Want / Have). What do you *want* now? You want the exclusive amount, which is 100%. What do you *have*? The 115% inclusive amount. So you would do R2 875 × (100 / 115). Try it on your calculator! You'll get R2 500.

**Student:** Why do we put VAT in the VAT Control account? Why not just call it an expense?
**Mandy:** Because it’s not an expense for the business. The business is just acting like a middle-man, collecting the money on behalf of SARS. Since the money doesn't belong to the business, and they owe it to the government, it is a liability. That’s why we credit the VAT Control account when we collect it—liabilities increase on the credit side!
