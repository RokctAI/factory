Today we prepare the bank reconciliation statement — the formal proof that your records and the bank's records tell one story. The numbers never lie, but they do arrive at different times, and the reconciliation statement is where every timing difference is accounted for in the prescribed format. One case runs through the whole session: a bank statement showing a favourable balance of R5 320, an outstanding deposit of R2 100, and two outstanding cheques — number 411 for R1 480 and number 415 for R650. By the end you will set out the statement in the exact layout the exam marks, post every figure in the marked order, and arrive at the bank account balance with every rand traceable.
