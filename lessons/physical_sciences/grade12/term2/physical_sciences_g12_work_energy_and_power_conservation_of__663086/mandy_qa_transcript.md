# Mandy Q&A Transcript

**Student:** Ms Dlamini said "isolated system". What does that actually mean? Do I have to prove it's isolated in my exam?

**Mandy:** Great question! An isolated system just means there are no outside forces messing with our object's energy—like friction or air resistance. Usually, in exam questions for Conservation of Mechanical Energy, the question will say something like "ignore air resistance" or "a smooth surface". When you see that, it's your green light! You don't have to write a proof; you just state the formula.

**Student:** Why did we make the final height zero? Can't the ground be a negative height?

**Mandy:** You can technically set your "zero height" wherever you want, but making the lowest point (the ground) equal to zero is a super helpful trick! If you set the ground as $h = 0$, then final potential energy ($mgh$) just becomes zero, which makes your math way easier. Ms Dlamini did this so the equation simplifies nicely.

**Student:** I always forget the square root at the end. I got 98 and stopped. Do I lose all my marks?

**Mandy:** You won't lose *all* your marks, but you'll definitely lose the final answer mark! The formula has $v^2$, so when you calculate 98, that's velocity *squared*. Remember, velocity has to be in m·s⁻¹. A velocity of 98 m/s for a dropped ball is crazy fast! Always look at your final number and ask, "Does this make sense, or did I forget to square root?"

**Student:** Do I have to write the formula exactly like $(E_k + E_p)_i = (E_k + E_p)_f$? Can I just write $E_{initial} = E_{final}$?

**Mandy:** Ms Dlamini is strict about this for a reason! The CAPS markers want to see the exact formula from your data sheet. While $E_i = E_f$ makes logical sense, it might not get you the formula mark if the memo specifically requires the expanded version. Stick to the data sheet format, exactly as the Science Queen showed you, to guarantee that mark!
