I know stoichiometry can look like a giant wall of scary maths and weird symbols. It used to totally overwhelm me. But here is the secret: it's just a recipe.

Think about baking a cake. If the recipe says 2 cups of flour make 1 cake, you know exactly how to make 2 cakes. You just double it. Stoichiometry is exactly that!

The balanced equation is just the recipe. It tells you the exact ratio of ingredients. But the recipe uses "moles" instead of cups. So, all you are doing when you divide the mass by the molar mass is figuring out how many "cups" of ingredients you have.

Once you have the moles, you just look at the recipe—the balanced equation—to see how much cake you can make. And then, you turn those moles back into grams so you can actually weigh it in the real world.

Don't panic when you see the long questions. Just remember:
1. Balance the recipe.
2. Turn grams into moles (find out what you have).
3. Use the recipe ratio.
4. Turn moles back into grams.

You've got this. Just take it one step at a time, exactly like Ms Dlamini showed you.
