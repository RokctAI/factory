# Lesson Script: Resistors in Parallel

## Subtopic: Introduction to Parallel Circuits

Welcome, Grade 11s. I am Ms Dlamini, and today we are addressing a concept where many students needlessly forfeit marks: resistors in parallel. In Physical Sciences, precision is paramount. We do not guess; we calculate based on established principles.

Before we look at the mathematics, let us establish the principle. When resistors are connected in parallel, the current from the source has multiple paths it can take. Think of it as a river splitting into several channels. The total current is the sum of the currents in each parallel branch.

However, the potential difference—the voltage—across each parallel branch remains exactly the same. The energy transferred per unit charge is identical regardless of the path taken. This is a fundamental property of parallel circuits.

Many students assume that adding more resistors always increases the total resistance. That is true for series circuits. But in parallel, by providing more pathways for the current, we are actually *decreasing* the total resistance of the circuit. The total resistance will always be less than the smallest individual resistor in that parallel combination. This is a vital check for your final answers.

## Subtopic: The Parallel Resistance Formula

Now, how do we quantify this? The National Senior Certificate data sheet provides the formula we must use. It is written as:

1 over R-parallel equals 1 over R-one plus 1 over R-two plus 1 over R-three, and so on.

Let us be absolutely clear on exam technique here. The mark sheet allocates a mark for stating the correct formula exactly as it appears on the data sheet. Do not start manipulating the formula before you have written it down. Write the principle, then write the formula.

Another common pitfall: students calculate the value for '1 over R-parallel' and stop there, presenting it as their final answer. This is incorrect. You must invert your final result to find R-parallel. If you forget to flip the fraction, you lose the final answer mark, and any subsequent calculations using that incorrect resistance will also be flawed. Precision, Grade 11s. Every step matters.

## Subtopic: Worked Example - Total Resistance

Let us apply this to a formal problem. We are given the following: Three resistors of 2 ohms, 3 ohms, and 6 ohms are connected in parallel across a 12-volt battery. We need to calculate the total current supplied by the battery.

This is a two-step problem. First, we must find the total equivalent resistance of the parallel combination. Second, we will use Ohm's Law to find the total current.

Step one. We state our formula:
1 over R-parallel = 1 over R-one + 1 over R-two + 1 over R-three

Now, we substitute. This is where you earn your substitution mark.
1 over R-parallel = 1 over 2 + 1 over 3 + 1 over 6

We find a common denominator, which is 6.
1 over R-parallel = 3 over 6 + 2 over 6 + 1 over 6
1 over R-parallel = 6 over 6
1 over R-parallel = 1

Remember our rule? We must invert. In this specific case, the inverse of 1 is still 1. Therefore, our total parallel resistance, R-parallel, is exactly 1 ohm.

Notice our check: Is 1 ohm less than the smallest resistor, which was 2 ohms? Yes, it is. We can proceed with confidence.

## Subtopic: Worked Example - Total Current

We now have the total resistance, R-parallel = 1 ohm, and we know the potential difference provided by the battery, V = 12 volts. We are asked for the total current, I.

We turn to Ohm's Law. State the formula exactly as it is given:
R = V over I

We substitute our known values:
1 = 12 over I

Rearranging to solve for I, we find:
I = 12 divided by 1
I = 12

Finally, we must state our answer with the correct SI unit and sensible significant figures. The unit for current is the ampere.

Therefore, the total current supplied by the battery is 12 amperes, or 12 A.

That is how you secure full marks. Principle first, formula from the data sheet, explicit substitution, and the final answer with units.

Do not let these foundational calculations cost you your distinction. Master the structure, and the physics will follow. I am Ms Dlamini. Thank you for your focus.
