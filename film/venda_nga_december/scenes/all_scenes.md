# Scene 01 — Opening: The Chest

## Runtime
0:00 – 2:00

## Thread Position
Before the title. Before the name. Before anything.
The film begins with what never moved.

## What Happens

Black screen.

A chest. Close. The tattoo fills the frame.
**VENDA NGA DECEMBER.**

No name. No title. No context.
The audience reads it before they know who it belongs to.

Hold for three full seconds.
The audience is already inside the question.

Then — the title sequence. Three lines.
Each appearing over the chest. One at a time.

*AGAINST ALL ODDS*
**VENDA NGA DECEMBER**
*The King of VenRap*

Hold all three together on the chest.
Three seconds.
Cut to black.

Then — his face. The dark room. The film begins.

## Visual Notes

- The chest shot is the only image in these two minutes
  apart from the title text
- No music under the title — silence, or the very faint
  ambient sound of the dark room
- The tattoo must be sharp. This is the only shot
  in the film where a close-up of the tattoo is held
  long enough to read every word clearly
- Lighting on the chest: single hard source from one side —
  the tattoo has texture and shadow, it is not flat

## Bubbles
None. This section is sacred.
No information. No context. No interruption.
Just the chest and what is written on it.

## Ad Break Bumper
This scene provides the ad break bumper image:
the chest, the tattoo, VENDA NGA DECEMBER.
Used at every break throughout the film.

# Scene 02 — Act One: Thohoyandou Made Me

## Runtime
2:00 – 7:00

## Thread Position
Before the industry. Before the dreads question.
Before the national stage.
Who was this man when he was just a man from Maniini Block J?

## What Happens

Mizo Phyll in the dark room for the first time.
Full face. Full light. Unhurried.

He speaks about where he came from.
Not as nostalgia — as foundation.
What Thohoyandou is. What VenRap is and where it grew from.
What it meant to be a young man from the north
with a sound that had no genre name yet.

Midway through — Tman Gavin in the same chair.
He speaks about the same streets.
The same early years. The same starting point.

Then Nicodemic — briefly — the third voice
confirming the same origin from a different angle.

Then DJ Davic — the producer who believed first.
What did he see? What made him say yes?

By the end of this act the audience understands:
**this man had no reason to succeed.
Every reason not to.**

## Dramatic Reconstruction

One reconstruction sequence in this act:
A young man performing in a yard.
People walking away. Someone laughing.
Shot warm, slightly overexposed — reconstruction signal.
Silent. Mizo's voice narrates over the top.

This is not staged to embarrass anyone.
It is staged to show the distance he traveled.

# Scene 03 — The Witnesses

## Runtime
Woven through Act One and Act Two — not a standalone scene
but a structural device used throughout.

## Thread Position
Every time Mizo makes a claim about his own story —
a witness responds from the same chair.
The audience never sees them in the same room.
The edit does the work.

## What It Is

Tman Gavin and Nicodemic are not interviewed
as separate segments with their own titles.
They are woven into the fabric of the film —
appearing when their testimony is needed
to confirm, contradict, or deepen what Mizo has said.

**The structural rule:**
After Mizo establishes something — a memory, a feeling,
a version of events — we cut to a witness.
The witness was asked the same question
without knowing what Mizo said.

Their answers will do one of three things:
1. Confirm — the audience believes Mizo more deeply
2. Contradict — the audience leans forward
3. Complicate — the audience sits with genuine uncertainty

All three outcomes are valuable.
None of them require editorial comment.

# Scene 04 — Act Two: The Faith and The Dreads

## Runtime
7:00 – 13:00

## Thread Position
The film's most spiritually complex chapter.
This is where the tattoo and the dreads
occupy the same space for the first time —
and the audience begins to understand
what was negotiable and what was not.

## What Happens

Mizo speaks about the Rastafari faith.
What it meant. What it demanded.
What the dreads represented — his words, not anyone's label.

Then — the record company.
The condition. The dreads that came off.

The faith witness in the same chair.
Not hostile. Honest.
What did it mean to the community?
Was it betrayal or was it something harder to name?

The wife — from the same dark room chair.
What did she see from the closest possible distance?

The central question of this act is never asked directly.
It hangs in the air between every answer:

*What are you willing to give up —
and what are you not?*

The tattoo has never moved. The dreads are gone.
The audience holds that.

## Archive Bubbles — This Scene

Archive bubbles in this scene are deliberately minimal.
The emotional content must breathe without information
cluttering the space.

One bubble only — when Mizo first mentions the record deal:

> **Venda Nga December EP**
> *Produced by Soundslucid*
> *The title was always a declaration*

## No Bubble During The Dreads Moment

When he speaks about the dreads coming off —
no bubble appears.
That moment gets silence and his face only.

# Scene 05 — Ricky Rick

## Runtime
13:00 – 15:00

## Thread Position
The national stage. The evidence of distance traveled.
The chapter that cannot be revisited.

## What Happens

Mizo speaks about Ricky Rick.
Not the collaboration first — the person.
What he was like. What that relationship gave him.
What is different now that he is gone.

One photograph appears behind him in the dark room.
Not cutting away — floating in the frame
the way a memory surfaces.

If the pre-production archive search found
Ricky Rick's public words about Mizo Phyll —
they surface as a Type 2 Cut Floor Bubble
at the right moment in this section.
His own words. From the archive.
The audience reads what he said.

Then silence.
The photograph fades.
The film moves forward — the way he had to.

## Duration Rule

Two minutes maximum. Possibly less.
This section does not overstay.
Grief is honoured by restraint, not by dwelling.

## Bubbles — This Scene

**No archive bubble during this section.**

The only possible bubble is the Type 2 Cut Floor Bubble
containing Ricky Rick's own archived words — if found.

Everything else in this section is silence and Mizo's face.

# Scene 06 — Act Three and Four: Venda Nga December

## Runtime
15:00 – 21:00

## Thread Position
The tattoo chapter. The permanent thing.
What stayed when everything else was negotiable.

## What Happens

**Act Three — The National Stage (15:00–18:00)**

Mizo speaks about what the music career became.
The stages. The recognition. The VenRap platform
he built so others from Venda could follow.

Archive photographs and clips float into the frame
behind him as he speaks — not cutting away,
accompanying him.

Tman Gavin responds from his chair —
asked the same question about the national stage.
His answer lives next to Mizo's in the edit.
The distance between them is never stated.
The audience measures it themselves.

**Act Four — The Tattoo (18:00–21:00)**

The tattoo chapter.

When did he get it. Why.
What he knew about himself when he made it permanent
before the world agreed.

The wife speaks about the tattoo
from the place only she can speak from.
She lives with it every day.
What do those three words mean
from inside the life, not outside it?

Mizo's final words before the performance.
What Thohoyandou means to him now.
What he would say to the young man
in the yard who nobody was listening to yet.

# Scene 07 — The Closing Performance

## Runtime
21:00 – 23:00

## Thread Position
The end of the film. The tattoo's final argument.
Everything that was said — now shown.

## What Happens

All testimony is complete.
The dark room falls quiet.

He stands. The coat goes on.
The cameras that have watched him speak
now watch him perform.

He raps. One or two verses.
Alone in the room. No audience.
No stage. No production.
Just the dark room that held everyone's truth —
and the King of VenRap doing what he was
always going to do regardless of what anyone thought.

## The Bubble Sequence

As he performs — the dark spaces around him begin to fill.

**Phase 1 — Community Bubbles (first 60 seconds)**
Fan testimony floats up from the darkness.
Messages. Reactions. Facebook comments.
The community that watched him,
doubted him, claimed him — speaking.

They drift in organically. One at a time.
Occasionally two overlapping briefly.
Never competing. Never crowding.
The room filling slowly with Venda's voice.

See: `bubbles/community_archive.md` for sourced material.

**Phase 2 — The Transition (final 30 seconds)**
He finishes. Silence.

The chest. The tattoo.
**VENDA NGA DECEMBER.**

One final community bubble holds
longer than the others. Then fades.

A beat of complete darkness.

**Phase 3 — Credits as Bubbles**
The first credit bubble surfaces.
Same visual language as the archive bubble.
Same calm. Same format.
But the content has transformed.

Credits arrive one at a time.
The chest and tattoo remain visible throughout.

Final credit:

> **AGAINST ALL ODDS**
>
> # VENDA NGA DECEMBER
>
> **The King of VenRap**
>
> *A ROKCT INTELLIGENCE (PTY) LTD*
> *t/a ROKCT STUDIOS Production*
> *Thohoyandou, Limpopo, South Africa*

Hold. Fade to black.

## The Rule For This Scene

Do not direct his performance.
Do not tell him what to rap or how long to go.
Whatever he does in that room on that day —
that is the ending.

The cameras are locked. The room is his.
The director's only job in this scene
is to not be in the way.
