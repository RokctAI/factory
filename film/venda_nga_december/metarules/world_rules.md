## The Master Thread

```
A man from Thohoyandou tattooed his dream on his chest
before anyone believed in him.
        ↓
He carried a faith that had rules about permanence.
The dreads were a vow. The vow had a cost.
        ↓
The industry found him.
The industry had conditions.
        ↓
The dreads came off.
The tattoo did not move.
        ↓
The ones who doubted him now drive taxis.
The ones who loved him watch from home.
The one who discovered him still answers his phone.
        ↓
Ricky Rick is gone.
The collaboration is still on record.
        ↓
He stands alone in a dark room.
He raps.
        ↓
The tattoo is still there.
VENDA NGA DECEMBER.
It was always going to end here.
```

## The Central Question
This film never asks it directly.
It is felt in every interview, every bubble, every silence.

> *When you build a career on who you are —
> what happens when who you are becomes the obstacle?
> And what do you hold onto when everything else is negotiable?*

---

## What This Film Is Not

- It is not a career documentary
- It is not a timeline
- It is not a music video
- It is not a tourism piece about Thohoyandou
- It is not a tribute to Ricky Rick
- It is not an exposé of the music industry
- It is not a verdict on the dreads

---

## What This Film Is

It is a document of permanence.
Everything in it is asked against one question:
*What stayed?*

The tattoo stayed.
Venda stayed.
The producer's phone number stayed.
The taxi drivers stayed — just not where anyone expected.
The wife stayed.
The music stayed.

Everything that stayed is this film.

---

## The Visual World

One room. One light. Four characters. Three bubble types. One tattoo.

The dark room is not a budget decision.
The dark room is a theological statement.
Truth emerges in controlled darkness.
The indumba. The confessional. The recording booth.
All the same room.

The tattoo is the film's only constant image.
It opens the film. It closes the film.
It appears at every ad break.
It is the film's heartbeat.

---

## The Bubble Layer

The film has two simultaneous tracks:
- What is said on screen
- What the character files know

The bubble layer is where those two tracks meet the audience.

Three types. One visual language. Distinct enough to read. Consistent enough to trust.

See: `bubbles/bubble_design_rules.md`

---

## The Closing Sequence

He stands. The coat goes on.
He performs alone in the dark room.

The dark spaces fill — not with light but with **words.**
Fan testimony. Community voice. Ricky Rick's own words from the archive.

Then the words transform.
Same format. Same visual language.
But now they are credits.

The credits are not the end of the film.
The credits are the film's last argument:
*These people were also real.
These names were also permanent.*

---

## Audit Standard

Every scene file is audited against the master thread.
A scene that cannot be located on the thread has failed.
A bubble that does not deepen what is on screen has failed.
A character file that does not serve the spine has failed.

The film is 23 minutes.
Every second earns its place or it is cut.
