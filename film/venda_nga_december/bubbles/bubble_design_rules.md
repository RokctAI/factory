# Bubble Design Rules — Venda Nga December

## What Bubbles Are

The bubble layer is the film's second voice.
It does not narrate. It does not explain. It deepens.

A bubble that explains what is already on screen has failed.
A bubble that arrives before the audience is ready has succeeded.

---

## The Three Types

---

### Type 1 — The Archive Bubble
*What the character file knows.*

**When it appears:**
When a subject mentions a name, a record, a place, a collaboration,
a year, an award — and the character file has verified context
that the audience would not otherwise know.

**What it contains:**
A single fact. Never more than two lines.
No opinion. No interpretation. Just the record.

**Examples:**
> **Musengavhadzi Productions**
> *Where both Tman Gavin and Mizo Phyll were first recorded*

> **Tshikandeledzi (2009)**
> *Nominated — Best Tshivenda Hip-Hop Single, SATMA*

> **My African Dream**
> *First artist to win Best Tshivenda Hip-Hop, Tshivenda Music Awards (2012)*

> **DJ Davic**
> *Producer on early Mizo Phyll and Tman Gavin recordings*

> **Venda Nga December EP**
> *Produced by Soundslucid. Title track became a platform for Venda artists.*

**Visual language:**
- Clean. Small. Left-aligned or lower-third placement.
- White text on transparent dark background.
- No border. No drop shadow. No animation gimmick.
- Appears: slow fade in (0.5 seconds).
- Stays: 3–4 seconds maximum.
- Disappears: same slow fade out.
- Never overlaps a subject's face.
- Never appears during emotional peaks — only during informational moments.

**Source:**
`characters/{name}.md` → Archive Bubbles section

---

### Type 2 — The Cut Floor Bubble
*What we were told but didn't make it to screen.*

**When it appears:**
When what is on screen is the surface of something deeper —
and the cut floor has the rest.
When a subject mentioned something extraordinary that the 23-minute
structure could not hold.
When Ricky Rick said something publicly about Mizo Phyll
that should be in this film but has no other place.

**What it contains:**
A quote. A voice. Always attributed.
Never paraphrased. Always the actual words.

**Examples:**
> *"He called me before the session.
> Just to check I was okay.
> That's not industry. That's character."*
> — Mizo Phyll, interview footage (unaired)

> *[Ricky Rick's actual public words about Mizo Phyll
> — sourced from social media archive pre-production]*
> — Ricky Rick

**Visual language:**
- Italic text signals it is a voice, not a fact.
- Faint quotation mark at the open.
- Slightly warmer background — not cold white like the archive bubble.
  A very faint amber or warm grey tint on the background.
- Attribution always present. Never anonymous.
- Same fade behaviour as archive bubble.
- Slightly longer on screen — 4–5 seconds — because it is a thought,
  not a fact. The audience needs a beat to receive it.
- Never appears over b-roll. Always over interview or dark room footage.

**Source:**
`characters/{name}.md` → Cut Floor Bubbles section
*Populated after shoot days. Not pre-written.*

---

### Type 3 — The Community Bubble
*What Venda sent in. Facebook doesn't forget.*

**When it appears:**
During the closing performance only.
He is rapping alone in the dark room.
The dark spaces around him begin to fill.

These are not facts. These are not quotes.
These are testimony.
The community that made him, watched him, doubted him,
claimed him — speaking in their own words.

**What it contains:**
Short. One or two lines maximum.
Fan comments. Reactions. Public posts.
Collected during pre-production from public social media archives.
Attributed with first name or handle only — never full names without consent.

**Examples:**
> *"First artist to make me proud to say I'm from
> Thohoyandou at a Joburg party"*
> — Facebook comment, 2019

> *"Ndi dzi king. We always knew."*
> — Twitter, 2021

> *"My father played Venda nga December at my
> lobola. Said it was the only song that sounded
> like home."*
> — Instagram comment

**Visual language:**
- Slightly less formal than archive or cut floor bubbles.
- Placement is organic — not perfectly lower-third aligned.
  They float up from different areas of the dark frame.
  Some left. Some right. Some centre.
  Like messages arriving from different directions simultaneously.
- Slightly smaller text than the other two types.
- Warmer. More human. Less documentary, more community.
- They do not all appear at once. They drift in,
  hold briefly, drift out. A continuous flow.
- Never compete with each other — one visible at a time,
  maximum two overlapping briefly during the peak.

**Source:**
`bubbles/community_archive.md`
*Pre-production research task. Collect before shoot.*

## The Credits Transition

### How It Works

He finishes rapping. Silence. The chest. The tattoo. VENDA NGA DECEMBER.

The community bubbles slow. One final fan message holds longer than the others. Then it fades.

A beat of complete darkness.

Then — the first credit bubble surfaces. Same visual language as the archive bubble. Same format. Same calm. But the content has transformed.

> **Directed by**
> *[Director name]*
> ROKCT STUDIOS

> **Produced by**
> *[Producer name]*
> ROKCT INTELLIGENCE (PTY) LTD

> **Original album artwork (2009)**
> *[Your name]*
> Musina, Limpopo

> **Executive Producer**
> *[Name]*

> **Principal subject**
> *Livhuwani Aubrey Ratshiungo*
> *Known as Mizo Phyll*
> *The King of VenRap*

The credits arrive in bubbles.
The same visual language that carried facts throughout the film
now carries names.

The message is clear without being stated:
*These names are also facts.
These people were also permanent.*

### Rules for Credits Bubbles

- Same format as archive bubbles — white text, dark background, fade in/out.
- One credit at a time. Never stacked.
- The chest and tattoo remain visible as background throughout.
- Final credit bubble is always the title:

> **AGAINST ALL ODDS**
>
> # VENDA NGA DECEMBER
>
> **The King of VenRap**
>
> *A ROKCT STUDIOS Production*
> *Thohoyandou, Limpopo, South Africa*

Hold. Fade to black. Done.

---

## What Bubbles Must Never Do

- Never appear during silence that is meant to be felt
- Never overlap a subject's face
- Never arrive more than once every 45 seconds during interview sections
- Never editorialize — a bubble that passes judgment has failed
- Never appear during the Ricky Rick section
  (that space is sacred — silence and one photograph only)
- Never be more than two lines
- Never use exclamation marks
- Never feel like a trivia game
- Always feel like the film leaning in to tell you something it almost kept to itself
