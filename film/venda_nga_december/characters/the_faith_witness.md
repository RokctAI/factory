# The Faith Witness

## Status

## Role
Someone from the Rastafari faith community in Thohoyandou
who knew Mizo Phyll as a committed member of the faith
before the music career consumed everything.

Someone who understood what the dreads represented —
not as fashion, not as identity politics —
as genuine spiritual commitment.

When the dreads came off, this person felt it.
Not necessarily as betrayal.
Perhaps as grief. Perhaps as understanding.
Perhaps as something more complicated than either.

They sit in the dark room and speak about
what faith looks like when it meets ambition.

## On the Title "Priest"

The word "priest" may or may not apply to Mizo Phyll's
specific role within his faith community.

In Rastafari, formal titles vary by house and tradition.
The Bobo Shanti and Nyabinghi Order have distinct
priest/elder roles. Other expressions are less formal.

**We do not label his role for him.**
Mizo Phyll will name his own position in the faith
during his interview. The faith witness will
confirm or contextualise from their own perspective.

What matters is not the title.
What matters is the weight of the commitment
and what it cost to change it.

## Interview Questions

1. Tell me about who Mizo Phyll was within the faith.
   Not the artist — the person inside the community.

2. What did the dreads mean in your shared context?
   What did carrying them represent?

3. When the dreads came off —
   what did you feel?
   What did the community feel?

4. Did you speak to him about it?
   What was said?

5. Is there a way to understand that decision
   within the faith — or does the faith simply
   call it what it is?

6. What do you make of his music —
   of what he was building —
   alongside the faith?
   Were they ever in tension for you?

7. What do you think the tattoo means
   from the perspective of someone
   who understood his vows?
   Is VENDA NGA DECEMBER a spiritual statement
   or a music industry statement —
   or is there no difference?
