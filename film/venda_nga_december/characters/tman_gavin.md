# Tman Gavin

## Status
TO CONFIRM — Supporting Witness

## Role
He was there at the beginning.
He doubted. He watched. He was wrong about what Mizo would become.
He is now driving a taxi.

He is not in this film to generate sympathy for himself.
He is in this film because he saw something before it was anything —
and the fact that he is driving a taxi while Mizo is the King of VenRap
is not a tragedy to be pitied.
It is a mirror to be looked into.

## Why He Is The Film's Most Honest Voice

Everyone else in this film has a stake in how Mizo is remembered.

The wife loves him.
The producer invested in him.
The faith witness has theological skin in the game.

Tman Gavin has nothing to protect.
He drove the same road Mizo drove and stopped somewhere different.
That gives him a kind of truth the others cannot access.

He will say things about the early years —
about what it actually looked like,
about whether anyone really believed,
about what the streets of Thohoyandou said behind closed doors —
that Mizo himself cannot say without sounding self-congratulatory.

Tman Gavin is the film's permission to be honest.

## Interview Questions

1. Take me back to when you first heard Mizo making music.
   What did you think?

2. Did you believe he would go where he went?
   Be honest.

3. What was the scene like in Thohoyandou back then —
   what did VenRap feel like before it had a name?

4. When did you realise his path and your path
   were going in different directions?

5. What does it feel like to watch someone you started with
   become what he became?

6. What do you think the difference was?
   Between his path and yours?
   Not the easy answer. The real one.

7. What does Venda nga December mean to you
   as someone who was there before those words
   meant anything to anyone else?
