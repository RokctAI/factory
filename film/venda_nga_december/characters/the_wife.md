# The Wife

## Status
TO CONFIRM — Supporting Witness — Most Intimate Voice

## Role
She is the only person in this film who does not see
the King of VenRap when she looks at him.
She sees the man.

Every other character in this film knew him as an artist first,
or a peer, or a faith brother, or a producer's investment.

She knows what he looks like before breakfast.
She knows the tattoo from the other side.
She has slept beside VENDA NGA DECEMBER
for years without it being a symbol.
To her it is just his chest.

That is the most intimate thing this film can show.

## The Jealousy Thread

This is not gossip. This is the film's most honest human moment.

When your husband becomes someone the country knows —
when photos circulate of him with fans,
some of them taken at a certain angle,
when his phone carries numbers that didn't come from family —
what does that feel like to the person
who chose him before the country did?

This is never exploitative in the film.
It is never salacious.
It is simply honest about what public life costs
the people who live closest to it.

She is not asked about specific incidents.
She is asked about the feeling.
The difference matters enormously.

## Interview Questions

These questions require the most care of any in the film.
They are intimate. They are not personal attacks.
They are invitations to be honest about love under pressure.

1. When did you first realise the person you chose
   was also someone the world was choosing?

2. What does the tattoo mean to you?
   You see it every day. What does VENDA NGA DECEMBER
   mean from the inside of the life, not the outside?

3. What is the thing about his public life
   that required the most from you privately?

4. Fans love him. Some of them love him in ways
   that aren't just about the music.
   How do you hold that?

5. Was there a moment — one moment —
   where you looked at him and thought:
   I don't fully recognise what you've become?

6. What does he carry home that nobody else sees?

7. What would you want people to know about him
   that the music doesn't show?

## A Note on Handling This Interview

This is the most emotionally demanding interview in the film.

If she becomes emotional — hold the space.
Do not redirect. Do not comfort prematurely.
The camera holds.

If she says something that is clearly not for broadcast —
stop the camera. Honour the confidence.
She trusts this film with something real.
That trust is more important than any scene.
